ABSTRACT
Generating novel molecules with desired properties is a funda-
mental problem in modern drug discovery. This is a challenging
problem because it requires the optimization of the given objectives
while obeying the rules of chemical valence. An effective approach
is to incorporate the molecular graph with deep generative mod-
els. However, recent generative models with high-performance are
still computationally expensive. In this paper, we propose GF-VAE,
a flow-based variational autoencoder (VAE) model for molecular
graph generation. Specifically, the model equips VAE a lightweight
flow model as its decoder, in which, the encoder aims to accelerate
the training process of the decoder, while the decoder in turns to
optimize the performance of the encoder. Thanks to the invertibility
of flow model, the generation process is easily accomplished by re-
versing the decoder. Additionally, the final generated molecules are
processed by validity correction. Therefore, our GF-VAE inherits
the advantages of both VAE and flow-based methods. We validate
our model on molecule generation and reconstruction, smooth-
ness of learned latent space, property optimization and constrained
property optimization. The results show that our model achieves
state-of-the-arts performance on these tasks. Moreover, the time
performance of GF-VAE on two classical datasets can achieve 31.3%
and62.9%improvements separately than the state-of-the-art model.

1 INTRODUCTION
Finding new molecules with desired properties is a fundamental
problem in chemical biology and drug discovery. The discovery
paradigm involves investments both in organic synthesis and in
biological testing of large compound collections. According to the
estimation of chemists, there are 1060drug-like compounds that
could be made [ 24]. Moreover, for each drug in market, there can
be millions of compounds that are chemically almost identical to
it, some of which may work better than the approved drug by just
slightly changing the compound structure with an extra hydrogen
or double bound [ 24]. Computer algorithms with scripted instruc-
tions have been used to screen millions of compounds for finding
the top-ranked alternative candidates [ 22]. Recently, machine learn-
ing based algorithms are designed to study the less-explored regions
of chemical space and generate novel compounds with desirable
property profiles from scratch [4].
The basic idea of molecule generation based on machine learn-
ing is to approximate one data distribution, which could capture
as much as possible the patterns that are presented in the given
data set. In recent years, different generative models have been
developed to learn data distribution, such as generative adversarial
networks (GANs) [ 11], Variational autoencoders (VAEs) [ 17] and
flow-based models [ 7,8]. They have been applied to molecule gen-
eration resulting in, for example, MolGAN [ 6] and GCPN [ 32] based
on GAN, CVAE [ 10] and JT-VAE [ 14] based on VAE, GraphNVP
[21] and MoFlow [33] based on flow-based models.
Current VAE-based molecule generation models, e.g., GVAE [ 18]
and GraphVAE [ 30], first use the encoder 𝑓to map a molecular
from its source space to a latent space vector z, and then use the
decoder𝑔hoping to map it back. The mapping functions 𝑓and
𝑔can be deep networks learned through optimizing a variational
lower bound on the log-likelihood of the data, such that the gener-
ator𝑔approximately inverts the encoder 𝑓. Although enjoying the
flexibility of the forms of 𝑓and𝑔, the optimization of a lower bound
criterion might yield a suboptimal solution with respect to the true
log-likelihood [ 7]. The failure to optimize the decoder variance in
typical implementations of VAE is a major cause of poor quality
of generated samples [ 5]. In contrast, the flow-based models seek
for a perfect auto-encoder pair, i.e., 𝑔=𝑓−1. The invertible map-
ping allows exact and tractable likelihood estimation for training,
ensures to reconstruct all the training samples, and supports for
various applications such as interpolations between samples and
meaningful modifications of existing samples [ 15]. The flow-based
models thus have potential capability of deeper exploration of the

huge chemical space, which could produce more valid and novel
molecules. However, in order to capture a better invertible mapping
function for flow models, a large number of coupling layers are
required, since the mapping function is often implemented by com-
posing a series of easily invertible modules (e.g., additive coupling
layers). For example, Glow [ 15] has 192 layers. Therefore, the flow
models are often heavyweight and require a long training process.
This shortcoming of flow models has been investigated in image
domain [ 23], not yet for molecular domain. Unlike image genera-
tion, molecular generation suffers from very low fault tolerance and
is very sensitive to noise. Even one wrong generation of valence
will lead to failure of molecule generation. Therefore, molecular
generation naturally benefits from the attribute of exact density
evaluation of flow models. It is thus essential to design light-weight
flow-based molecular generator.
In this paper, we propose GF-VAE which is short for GraphFlow
-Variational AutoEncoder to generate molecular graphs. The key
idea behind it is to attach a VAE-encoder with a flow-generator,
which plays the role of decoder. During the training process, the
encoder learns a mapping from original molecular space to a Gauss-
ian space, and the decoder will also learn this mapping as well as
optimize it, guiding the encoder to learn more meaningful high
level molecule representation (shown in experiment 5.3). This is
a perfect marriage between VAE model and normalization flow
model, which combines the advantages of both, while alleviating
or even eliminating their shortcomings. On the side of flow models,
the model weight is lightened because its mapping target 𝑝(z)is a
Gaussian distribution from VAE-encoder rather than a typical tar-
get which is a standard Gaussian distribution without any original
data distribution information. i.e., 𝑓is not necessarily implemented
by a large number of additive coupling layers any more. On the
other side of VAE-based models, since the poor performance of
typical VAE is due to the failure to optimize the decoder variance
[26], and [ 16] pointed out that the posterior be made more flexi-
ble via the use of normalizing flows. Therefore, attaching the flow
model to VAE-encoder, providing a tighter, modified variational
lower bound, which enables the approximate posterior of VAE can
achieve a better fit to the true posterior.
The flow-generator in GF-VAE is designed with the coopera-
tion of two sub-flow modules followed by a validity check module.
The generator learns the molecular graph structure (bond) and
molecular atom assignments separately based on the global feature
from VAE-encoder. This design allows the generator to generate
molecules in one-shot. GF-VAE is thus more efficient than the se-
quential generation models that generate atoms and bonds of a
molecule one by one [ 29]. It’s also not restricted by the limitation
that the sequential model requires a predefined sequence to specify
the generation process (usually in accordance with the SMILES
sequence). In addition, due to the reversibility of flow-generator,
GF-VAE bypasses the reconstruction loss that usually involves in
computationally demanding graph matching process [30].
In summary, our contributions in this work are as follows:
•We propose GF-VAE, a seamless combination of VAE and nor-
malizing flow model, for one-shot molecule graph generation.
GF-VAE uses a flow-generator to guide a VAE-encoder to
learn more meaningful high-level molecule representations,while it adopts a VAE-encoder to make the flow-generator
lightened with less weights.
•Experimental results on two benchmark datasets show that
GF-VAE outperforms the state-of-the-art models on all eval-
uation metrics, such as validity, novelty, uniqueness and
reconstruction. The flow-generator effectively guides the
learning of the encoder. The meaningfulness of the molecule
embedding and the smoothness of the learned latent space
are demonstrated.
•The lightweight GF-VAE is around two times faster than the
state-of-the-art MoFlow model in training. Our extensive
evaluation also shows that a lightweight GF-VAE can have
better performance than heavier-weight MoFlow.
2 RELATED WORK
A variety of deep generative models have been proposed for de-novo
molecule generation. Some models take molecular SMILES codes
[31] as input, and others directly deal with the molecular graph
structure. Models working with SMILES strings address molecule
generation as a text generation problem, which has been extensively
studied in natural language processing field. However, the linear
string description of molecular structures in SMILES causes two
difficulties in molecule generation. First, the SMILES representation
cannot well capture the structural information of molecules, e.g.,
two structurally similar molecules may be encoded into notable
different SMILES strings, making the distribution complicated to
learn [ 14]. Second, the models tend to learn the specific grammar
rules of SMILES strings, rather than the molecular features [ 3]. We
thus focus on learning from molecular graphs, and discuss next the
related work using VAE and flow-based models.
In the group of VAE-based models, from our best knowledge,
CVAE [ 10] is the first work using VAE to encode and generate
molecular SMILES. GVAE [ 18] parses SMILES as a tree from a
context-free grammar and employs VAE model for encoding and
decoding these parsed trees, ensuring the generated outputs are
always valid. Unlike these SMILES-oriented models, GraphVAE
[30] is a pioneering work using VAE for molecular graph gener-
ation, applying VAE to realize graph-to-graph autoencoding. By
transforming a constrained optimization problem to a regularized
unconstrained one, RVAE [ 20] presents a regularization framework
for VAE-based molecule generation as a step toward semantic valid-
ity. In stead of generating atoms one by one, JT-VAE [ 14] generates
scaffold of chemical substructure components step by step.
In the group of flow-based models, GraphNVP [ 21] is the first
work, generating molecular graphs by invertible flow model in a
one-shot manner. GRF [ 12] improves GraphNVP by using resid-
ual flows. GraphAF [ 29] is an autoregressive flow-based model
which generates molecular graphs in a sequential manner with
validity check when adding any new atom or bond. MoFlow [ 33]
is a recent model achieving state-of-the-art performance in molec-
ular graph generation. The model consists of a variant of Glow
model [ 15] for generating bonds, a novel graph conditional flow for
generating atoms given bonds, and then combining them with va-
lidity corrections. To enable the invertible mapping function to map
complicated molecular structures to a simple standard Gaussian
distribution, one-shot flow generative models (such as GraphNVP
and MoFlow) stack around 50 coupling layers (more than 100 basic

3.1 Problem Definition

node of a molecular graph pictures an atom while each edge repre-

Our main goal is to learn a generative model 𝑝G(𝐺)from a given

Different from the traditional reject-sampling category, we use
a post-hoc validity correction method from [ 33]. The core idea is
to always delete one order for the bond with the largest order until
invalid molecular becomes valid and return its largest connected
component. In detail, for an entered molecular graph 𝐺, we first
check whether its largest connected component violates the valency
constraints or not. If it is valid, then we output it as final molecular
and terminate the algorithm. Otherwise we choose the bond with
the largest order. If the bond is a triple bond or double bond, we
delete one order and change it to a double bond or a single bond.
If it is single bond, we drop it. Then we get a new graph 𝐺′. We
repeat the above process for 𝐺′until we get a valid molecular or
an atom since all bonds are dropped.
4 EXPERIMENTS
In this section, we conduct extensive experiments that evaluate
the performance and time cost of molecular generation, the op-
timization of decoder to encoder, the smoothness of the learned
latent space, and the constrained property optimization to show
the effectiveness of GF-VAE.
4.1 Experiment Setting
Benchmark Datasets. We choose two commonly used chemical
molecular datasets, QM9 [ 25] and ZINC-250K[ 13] for evaluation.
QM9 contains 134k molecules with maximum 9 atoms in 4 different
types, and ZINC-250K is made of 250k molecules with maximum
38 atoms in 9 different types. We apply the chemical libraries rdkit
(https://github.com/rdkit/rdkit) to kekulize the molecules and re-
move hydrogen atoms from them. The resulting molecules containonly single, double, and triple bonds. Then we encode each atom
and bond by one-hot encoding, pad the molecule tensors which
have less than the maximum number of atoms with a virtual atom,
and add a virtual edge channel for adjacency tensor, representing no
bonds between atoms. Thus, an adjacency tensor consists of 𝑅=4
adjacency matrices stacked together, each corresponding to the
existence of a bond type (single, double, triple, and virtual bonds)
between the atoms. The feature matrix represents the type of each
atom (e.g., oxygen, fluorine, etc). For the input of VAE-encoder part,
we use adjacency tensor directly, for the input of flow-generator
part, we dequantize the discrete one-hot-encoded data by adding
uniform random noise 𝑈[0,0.6]for each dimension.
Baselines. We choose the following two groups of baselines:
a) VAE-based models :RVAE [20] and JT-VAE [14] are the recent
state-of-the-art VAE-based baseline models. The RVAE model fo-
cuses on the matrix representation and formulates penalty terms
that address validity constraints. The JT-VAE model captures the
chemical validity by encoding and decoding a tree-structured scaf-
fold of molecular graphs. We also choose three additional VAE
models for comparison: CVAE [10],GVAE [18] and GraphVAE
[30]. The first two models only learn from string (SMILES) infor-
mation, while the third model is the first VAE model learning from
molecular graphs.
b) Flow-based models: GraphAF [29] is the state-of-the-art flow-
based model which generates molecular in a sequential way. MoFlow
[33] is another state-of-the-art flow-based model which generates
molecule in one-shot way. We also choose two more flow-based
models: GraphNVP [21] and GRF [12]. GraphNVP is the first work
to apply normalizing flow model to molecular generation task, and
GRF uses residual flow to optimize GraphNVP. We do not compare
GF-VAE with other sequential generative models such as GCPN
[32], since our model generates molecular in one shot.
Evaluation Metrics. We evaluate the performance of our model
on the generation and reconstruction of real molecules by the fol-
lowing widely-used metrics: Validity (V)- the percentage of chem-
ically valid molecules among all generated molecules; Novelty (N)
- the percentage of generated valid molecules not presenting in
the training set; Uniqueness (U)- the percentage of unique valid
molecules out of all generated molecules; and Reconstruction (R)
- the percentage of the molecules that can be reconstructed from
their own latent vectors. However, it is hard to compare models
in two cases, one has a high Novelty but low Uniqueness (generat-
ing many new molecules, but most of them are the same) and the
other has a high Uniqueness but low Novelty (generating different
molecules, but most of them have already appeared in training set).
Therefore, we adopt another metric Score (S), which is the product
ofValidity, Novelty andUniqueness in order to compromise these
basic metrics.

4.5 Molecule Optimization
Molecule optimization has two branches generally. One is property
optimization, which generates novel molecules with the best prop-
erty score. The other is constrained property optimization, which
means to find molecules similar to a given molecule, but possessing
a better chemical property. We solve these problems in three steps.
1) train a GF-VAE model and fix it; 2) train a Multilayer Percep-
tron (MLP) as a regressor on the latent space of molecules with
target chemical property; and 3) search the molecules with the best
property score by the gradient ascend method between the output
and the input of the learned regressor. For constrained property
optimization, we additionally need to add similarity constrain 𝛿
between the original molecule and the generated novel molecule.
The optimization process fails when the similarity score is smaller
then𝛿or the property score is not improved within limited steps.
We choose two widely used metrics as the target property: Quan-
titative Estimate of Druglikeness (QED) [ 2] and penalized logP
(plogp) [ 9]. QED quantifies how likely a molecule is to be a po-
tential drug, while plogp is defined as the penalized logarithm of
the ratio of the concentrations between two solvents of a solute,
which is a relative complex metric. Molecular similarity is measured
by Tanimoto similarity of Morgan fingerprint [27].

5 CONCLUSION
In this paper, we propose GF-VAE, a flow-based VAE model for
molecule graph generation. It equips VAE a lightweight flow model
as its decoder. The model enables the encoder to speeds up the
learning process of the decoder, and the decoder in turns to opti-
mizes the representation ability of the encoder. GF-VAE inherits the
advantages of both VAE and flow-based methods. Extensive results
show that our model outperforms other state-of-the-art models on
molecule generation and optimization, provides a smooth latent
space and guarantees 100% reconstruction rate due to the flexibil-
ity of normalizing flow. Moreover, the training of GF-VAE is very
efficient, which is around two times faster than the MoFlow model.
