People have been suffering from air pollution for a decade in China, especially from PM 2.5(particulate
matter with a diameter of less than 2.5 mm). Accurate prediction of air quality has great practical sig-
niﬁcance. In this paper, we propose a data-driven model, called as long short-term memory - fully
connected (LSTM-FC) neural network, to predict PM 2.5contamination of a speci ﬁc air quality monitoring
station over 48 h using historical air quality data, meteorological data, weather forecast data, and the day
of the week. Our predictive model consists of two components: (1) Using a long short-term memory(LSTM)-based temporal simulator to model the local variation of PM
2.5contamination and (2) Using a
neural network-based spatial combinatory to capture spatial dependencies between the PM 2.5
contamination of central station and that of neighbor stations. We evaluate our model on a datasetcontaining records of 36 air quality monitoring stations in Beijing from 2014/05/01 to 2015/04/30 andcompare it with arti ﬁcial neural network (ANN) and long short-term memory (LSTM) models on the
same dataset. The results show that our LSTM-FC neural network model gives a better predictive
performance.

1. Introduction
The air pollution problem, especially PM 2.5, has long plagued the
urban population. In the past decade, the air pollution caused by
PM2.5has become increasingly serious in China. The growth rate of
total PM 2.5emissions from 2005 to 2014 is extremely high in some
provinces, for example, Inner Mongolia, Ningxia, Xinjiang, and
Qinghai are the four most serious provinces with a growth rate over
200% ( Jin et al. (2017) ). According to the Air Condition Monthly
Report of 74 cities in China in December 2015, the average ratio of
polluted days (the ratio of the number of days polluted by PM 2.5to
365) in 74 cities was up to 44.4% and there were 39 cities whosepolluted days ratio exceeded 50% ( Fang et al. (2016) ).
It is signi ﬁcant to provide urban residents with air quality
forecasts because of its negative effects for human. The PM 2.5
concentrations of different places are correlated with each other intemporal and spatial perspective, so we can use the temporal
dependence and the spatial dependence of PM
2.5concentration to
predict the air quality of a certain place. For example, the PM 2.5
concentration of a certain place tends to be low if the PM 2.5con-
centration of the past few hours are also low; the PM 2.5concen-
tration of a place is likely to be high if the PM 2.5concentration of
many of the neighboring places is high.
PM2.5concentration prediction methods are mainly divided into
two categories: physical-model-based method and data-drivenmethod. Physical-model-based methods are widely used in envi-
ronmental community, such as operational street canyon models
(Hertel and Berkowicz (2015) ), Gaussian plume models ( Carruthers
et al. (1992) ) and reduced-scale models ( Baker and Hargreaves*Corresponding author. Postal address: 5 South Zhongguancun Street, Haidian

that they can give sights into physical and chemical processes that
govern the dispersion and transformation of atmospheric pollut-

based on a number of empirical assumptions and parameters that
might not be applicable to all urban environments. Physical-model-

to different cities ( Zheng et al. (2013) ). We instead explore a data-
driven method to predict urban air quality, which has the advan-
tage of automatically adjusting the parameters and giving the same
or even more accurate prediction result as the physical-model-
based methods.
Recent evolutions in sensor technology and big data have pro-
vided the environmental community with continuously expanding
resources for data collection. Many data-driven methods, especially
neural network methods, have been used by scholars to predict gas
and particulate matter pollution ( Vitolo et al. (2015) ;Li et al. (2011) ;
Li et al. (2016) ).Kukkonen et al. (2003) considered hourly predic-
tion of the concentration of NO2and PM10on the dataset of two

network models to provide reliable predictions of PM

diction performances of neural networks were superior to other
models. However most of the neural networks in these works
aerely based on multilayer neural networks, which were not spe-
ciﬁcally designed for time series model, so it was meaningful to
explore the use of neural networks designed speci ﬁcally for timeseries to predict air quality, like LSTM. In recent years, Some more
sophisticated data-driven methods had been proposed. Zheng et al.
(2015) proposed a data-driven method to forecast the air quality
over 48 h by considering meteorological data, weather forecasts,
and the air quality data of monitoring stations. Deng et al. (2017)
proposed a cellular automata (CA) model based on a multivariate
regression model and several physical models to analyze the gen-
eration and diffusion of PM 2.5.
The above papers were very helpful for PM 2.5prediction. How-
ever, these papers could not capture the long-term features and
short-term features at the same time, and could not learn both
temporal and spatial properties in one model. To handle this multi-
dimensional multi-step prediction problem, we proposed a data-
driven model, named Long Short-Term Memory - Fully Connected
Neural Network, to predict PM 2.5contamination of a speci ﬁc air
quality monitoring station over 48 h using historical air quality data,
meteorological data, weather forecast data, and the day of the week
when the observation came from (also called time stamp data). First,
we feed all local historical data into an LSTM model, which simulates
the variation of a monitoring station's PM 2.5records, and gives a pre-
prediction, then we de ﬁne the nearest ﬁve monitors of a speci ﬁc
monitor (central monitor) as its neighbor monitor, and use a fully-
connected neural network-based spatial combinatory to integrate
the pre-predictions of neighbor monitors and give the ﬁnal PM 2.5
prediction of the central station. A linear interpolation method is
used to ﬁlled the missing data in the dataset. The experiments on a
real dataset illustrate the effectiveness of our methods.

2. Method
In this section, we introduce the details of our LSTM-FC model.
The goal of our model is to forecast the PM 2.5over 48 h, spe-
ciﬁcally, for the next 1 e6 h, we predict the real-valued concentra-
tion of PM 2.5, and with respect to 7 e12, 13e24, 25e48 h, we predict
the maximum and minimum concentration for each time interval
respectively, as seen in Fig. 1 (a). To make a forecast, some related
data in the past hours is entered into the model, so our model is a
multi-step-ahead forecasting model as seen in Fig. 1 (b).
2.1. LSTM-FC neural network
Fig. 2 presents the framework of the LSTM-FC model, which is
comprised of two main components: an LSTM-based temporal
simulator, and a neural network-based spatial combinatory.
The bottom part of Fig. 2 shows the input feature vectors Xt/C01;/
;Xt/C0Rmeasured in the past Rhours, including the district-level
meteorological data, air quality data of the given station and
neighbor stations in the past Rhours, and the day of the week.
Meteorological data contains weather, temperature, pressure, hu-
midity, wind speed, and wind direction information, all of whichhave strong in ﬂuences on air quality ( Su et al. (2016) ). Air quality
data is the concentration of six main pollutants, PM2:5,PM10,NO2,
CO,O3, and SO2, measured by the monitoring stations. The red star
stands for the station we want to predict, i.e., the central station.
The blue stars are neighbor stations, whose reading implies
geographical dependence.
The input vectors are ﬁrst entered into the temporal simulator
component, as depicted in the middle of Fig. 2 , which simulates the
variation of PM 2.5concentration readings of each station. Note that
the LSTM-based temporal simulator only exploits the temporal
information. Then the predictions of the central station and sur-
rounding stations given by LSTMs are entered into the fully-
connected neural network-based spatial combinatory. The ﬁnal
output is the ﬁnal prediction of the central station.
The innovation of our model is two-fold: (1) The temporal
simulator and spatial combinatory handled temporal information
and spatial information, respectively, leading to an effective and
interpretable prediction result. The LSTM-based temporal simu-
lator fed with all local historical data which could handle the long-
range dependencies. Meanwhile, the spatial combinatory com-
bined the predictions of the surrounding temporal simulators. (2)We combined the spatial information of surrounding stations ac-
cording to geographical distance, instead of using data from all
stations in Beijing, which averted over- ﬁtting and mitigated the
computational challenge.

Note that the latest progress in Li et al. (2017) is somewhat
similar to this paper in that they proposed a long short-term
memory neural network extended model (LSTME) to predict
PM2.5. The difference is that Li et al. (2017) fed LSTMs with historical
PM2.5concentration data from different stations and combined
auxiliary data with a neural network. Correspondingly, in the
LSTM-FC neural network, all local features of a station were fed into
a single LSTM, including both PM 2.5concentration data and auxil-
iary data. The spatial information was handled by a neural network.
Consequently, LSTM-FC model made better use of the geographic
information of auxiliary data.
2.2. LSTM-based temporal simulator
For general-purpose time series modeling and prediction, the
long short-term memory model has proven its excellent ability to
memorize long-range dependencies in many studies ( Xu and Niu
(2018) ). In this subsection, we build a LSTM-based model to simu-
late the variation of PM 2.5, which follows the standard LSTM struc-
ture. As illustrated in Fig. 3 , each LSTM block consists of a memory
cell and three gates: the input gate, forget gate, and output gate.
Serving as an accumulator of status information, the memory
cell preserves the hidden information in the time series so that the
LSTM can exploit long-range temporal context. The three gates
with a sigmoid activation function control changes in cell status.

”forgotten ”if the forget gate ftis off. The output gate determines

3. Results and discussion
We evaluate our model on a real-world air quality dataset, and
compare the prediction results with existing state-of-the-art
methods.
3.1. Settings
In this subsection, we introduce the details of the dataset, the
prediction format and metrics.
The datasets used in this paper are collected by the Urban Air
project of Microsoft Research ( Zheng et al. (2013 ,2015) ). We select
part of the datasets related to Beijing from 2014/05/01 to 2015/04/30 to evaluate the LSTM-FC neural network. The datasets consists of
278 023 hourly air quality records, 116 867 meteorology records,
and 390 702 weather forecast records. Air quality records are
collected from 36 in-city stations (shown in Fig. 5 (a)). Each air
quality instance has six pollutants: PM
2:5,PM10,NO2,CO,O3, and
SO2. Each meteorology record contains seven items: time, weather,
temperature, pressure, humidity, wind speed, and wind direction.
The missing values are ﬁlled with linear interpolation according to
the data before and after. The distribution of PM 2.5in Beijing is
shown in Fig. 5 (b), and the average, median, and standard devia-
tion are 84.73 mg/m3,6 1 . 0 0 mg/m3and 81.23 mg/m3, respectively.
The standard deviation is large, indicating that the data is widely
distributed and forecasting is a dif ﬁcult task.
In all experiments, we use 12-folder cross-validation to evaluate
the performance. Speci ﬁcally, we segment the dataset into 13
subset without changing the order of samples, and each subsetapproximately contains a month of data. As shown in Table 1 ,a
subset beginning from the second is used as a test set in each of
twelve validation experiments, and all subsets before the test set
are used as training sets.

used in the ensuing experiments: the mean absolute error (MAE)

north wind is severe sometimes so that the air quality changes
signiﬁcantly.

4. Conclusion
In this paper, we propose an LSTM-FC neural network model to
predict PM 2.5concentrations over 48 h, based on historical airquality data, meteorological data, weather forecast data. The LSTM-
FC model could memorize long-range temporal dependence and
integrate spatial information. To evaluate the performance of the
LSTM-FC model, we predict PM 2.5concentration of 36 air quality
stations in Beijing. Compared with ANN and LSTM, our proposed
model gives better predictive results based on RMSE and MAE
metrics. In the future, we will try to construct a data-driven model
to predict sudden changes in air pollution.
