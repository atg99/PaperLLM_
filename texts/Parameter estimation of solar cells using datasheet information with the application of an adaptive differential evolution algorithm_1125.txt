Abstract:  A solar cell or photovoltaic (PV) module is electrically represented by an appropriate circuit 10 
model with certain defined parameters. The parameters are required to be correctly computed  from solar 11 
cell characteristic and/or a set of experimental data for simulation and control of the PV system. However, 12 
experimental data or accurate characteristic data  (i.e. current -voltage or I -V curve)  of a PV module  may 13 
not be readily available. The manufacturer of a PV system usually provides relevant information on open 14 
circuit, short circuit and maximum power points. Therefore, a n alternate approach is to estimate the PV 15 
system parameters by utilizing  the I-V characteristi c data at these three major points. The process involves 16 
formulation and solution of complex non -linear equations from an adopted solar cell  model . This paper 17 
proposes  an application of an advanced adaptive differential evolution algorithm on the problem of PV 18 
module parameter estimation using minimum available information from the manufacturer datasheet  by 19 
implementing  single -diode and double -diode models. Linear population size reduction technique of 20 
success history based adaptive differential evolution (L -SHADE) algorithm is implemented to minimize 21 
the error of  current -voltage relationships at the above -mentioned three important points defining the I -V 22 
characteristic . The algorithm facilitates evol ution of solution s that result in almost zero error (<10−12) 23 
at these three major  points.  All relevant parameters of the PV cell are optimized by the algorithm without 24 
an assumption or predetermination of any parameter. It is observed that a  set of feasible solutions 25 
(parameters) is obtained for the PV module from multiple runs of the algorithm.  The fact of attaining 26 
several probable solutions from datasheet information using  few other  metaheuristics is also discussed in 27 
this work . 28

1. Introduction  33 
 34 
Depletion of fossil fuels, environment al regulation and carbon tax imposition in many countries force mankind to 35 
look for clean, replenishable sources of energy. Solar energy is a popular form of renewable energy as it is abundant 36 
and almost ubiquitous. The energy can be directly  converted  to electricity by photovoltaic (PV) system. The unit of 37 
a PV system is the PV cell. Normally, many PV cells are grouped together to form a PV module . The array of PV 38 
modules  can directly feed small DC loads. However, converter is necessary for larger  application of the electricity 39 
generated by the PV system. For effective utilization, the PV device is needed to be operated at maximum power 40 
point (MPP). Therefore,  a designer must know the appropriate  model  of a PV cell for the detailed study, simulatio n 41 
and analysis . 42 
  43 
A PV cell is a p -n junction diode , typically represented with an equivalent electrical circuit [ 1]. Depending on the 44 
accuracy sought, the designer can assume a model with one, two or even more diodes. Single -diode model has been 45 
the most popular model due to its simplicity coupled with an acceptable level of accuracy  in representing the I -V 46 
characteri stic. Double -diode model accounts for the loss in the depletion region caused by the recombination of 47 
carriers [ 2]. Though three -diode model has been studied in literature [ 3-4], the model has not been so popular as 48 
increment in accuracy with this model is  not high enough considering the escalation in complexity due to addition 49 
of one more diode  [5]. No matter whatever circuit model is adopted for the PV system, finding the optimum circuit 50 
parameters requires solution of transcendental equations relating I -V characteristic of the PV device . For a single - 51 
diode model, the parameters to be found are the diode ideality factor ( 𝑎 ), the photocurrent ( 𝐼𝑃𝑉 ), the reverse 52 
saturation curren t of the diode ( 𝐼𝑂), the series resistance ( 𝑅𝑆) and the parallel resistance ( 𝑅𝑃). Population based 53 
metaheuristic methods have been very effective in solving non -linear, multimodal optimization problems. In recent 54 
years, several evolutionary algorithm s (EAs) have been applied in finding optimal parameters of a PV cell.  Usually 55 
an error function between the sets of experimental and the simulated I-V data of the PV system  is minimized by the 56 
EAs to provide optimal cell parameters. Variants of particle swarm optimization (PSO) such as time varying 57

 acceleration coefficients PSO (TV ACPSO) [ 6] and enhanced leader PSO (ELPSO) [ 7] were applied to the problem 1 
of finding PV cell parameters. Flower pollination algorithm (FPA) [ 8] and hybrid [9] of FPA and Nelder -Mead 2 
simplex method s showed promising results in minimizing the error function. An improved version of shuffled 3 
complex evolution algorithm (ISCE) that could enhance exploration and exploitation capability of the original 4 
algorithm was proposed in [ 10] and the same was implemented to PV cell parameter estimation problem. A self - 5 
adaptive weight was incorporated into JAYA algorithm [ 11] in attempting the same problem. Certain adjustment s 6 
on control parameters of differential evolution (DE) w ere observed in [ 12-13] and the modified adaptive DE was 7 
applied to error minimization objective. Chaotic maps were infused in chaotic whale optimization algorithm 8 
(CWOA) [ 14] to compute and automatically adapt the internal parameters of the algorithm in solving the PV cell 9 
problem. The literature [ 6-14] utilized the experimental data of selected solar cell s provided in [ 15] at various points  10 
of the I -V curves  adopting single and/or double -diode models . Synthesized I -V data of various PV modules were 11 
applied to evaluate the performance of penalty -based DE proposed in [ 16]. Experimental I -V data of various PV 12 
modules  under different temperatures and irradiance levels were used to find the cell parameters adopting DE with 13 
integrated mutation strategy [ 17] and bacterial foraging optimization (BFO) [ 18] algorithm.  Power -voltage curve of 14 
the PV system was exploited in [ 19] using imperialist competitive (ICA) algorithm.  In all these publications , a 15 
reasonably large set of data w as needed for parameter estimation of the PV module.  16 
 17 
In general, the PV module manufacturer s provide limited information on the system. A few articles  proposed useful 18 
methods to estimate parameter s based on datasheet  information . Ref. [ 20] presented comparison of parameter 19 
extraction techniques of crystalline and thin -film solar -cell modules  using classical Gauss -Siedel method and 20 
analytical met hod. A comparative study using the iterative method and Lambert W function was exhibited in [ 21]. 21 
Literature [ 22] suggested  a detail ed mathematical model of the PV module  focusing mainly on three major points 22 
of open circuit, short circuit and maximum power on I -V curve. The combination of analytical and metaheuristic 23 
methods was discovered in [ 23-24]. Some of the parameters of the PV system were found analytically while t he 24 
remaining parameters were estimated using particle swarm optimization  (PSO)  [23] and genetic algorithm (GA) 25 
[24]. The nameplate data of solar cell were utilized in [ 25] for parameter extraction  applying bacterial foraging 26 
algorithm . This paper presents PV module parameter extraction using datasheet information at three major points 27 
of I-V characteristic curve for monocrystalline, polycrystalline and thin -film solar cells. A few parameters have been 28 
identified analytically, while the remaining parameters are optimized using linear population size reduction 29 
technique of success history based adaptive differential evolution (L -SHADE) algorithm  [26]. Similar  approach  in 30 
[23] considered  only single -diode model.  Again, Villalva et. al.  [22] used datasheet information with analytical 31 
derivation of only the series resistance ( 𝑅𝑆) and parallel resistance ( 𝑅𝑃) values in single -diode model assuming 32 
certain values for diode ideality factor ( 𝑎 ). This study includes both single and double -diode models without  33 
assum ing any parameter for the solar cell s. A new error function considering I -V characteristic at the three major 34 
points is formulated such that the optimization algorithm comes up with multiple probable  feasible solutions from 35 
different trial runs of the optimization algorithm. The literature [ 22-25] that used datasheet information (only on 36 
three major points) for parameter extraction did not enlighten on the possibility of multiple optimal solutions during 37 
optimization even under a defin ed condition of operating temperature and solar irradiance.  The simple error function 38 
together with the algorithm proposed in this work could ensure zero error at all trial runs  under standard test 39 
condition (STC) . SHADE [27] is a powerful variant of DE algorithm. Classical DE requires 3 -control parameters 40 
to be user defined: scale factor ( 𝐹), crossover rate ( 𝐶𝑅) and population size ( 𝑁𝑝). In SHADE, 𝐹 and 𝐶𝑅 values 41 
are automatically adapted based on the success history of earlier generations. In L-SHADE, population size ( 𝑁𝑝) is 42 
also dynamic  and it is observed that a reasonable population size generates good solutions. In summary, the 43 
performance of L-SHADE is not so much dependent on user -defined inputs. Further, SHADE and its variant L - 44 
SHADE have  shown very promising results for optimization of discrete [ 28] and continuous variables [ 29-30]. 45 
Inspired by the growing application in power domain and its competitive performance, we employ L-SHADE 46 
algorithm for the PV cell parameter estimation.  Finally, the problem with same objective ( error function ) is 47 
optimized employing some  other  well-known algorithms such as genetic algorithm (GA) [ 31], particle swarm 48 
optimization (PSO) [ 32], artificial bee colony (ABC) [ 33], moth swarm algorithm (MSA) [ 34] and grey wolf 49 
optimizer (GWO) [ 35]. The contributions of this work can be summarized as below:  50 
 51 
• An error function utilizing datasheet information on three major points of the I -V characteristic of a PV cell is 52 
proposed.  53 
• A combination of analytical and me taheuristic methods is implemented to single and double -diode cell model s. 54 
• All PV cell parameters are optimized for both single and double -diode models without presumption of any cell 55 
parameter.  56 
• L-SHADE algorithm is applied to optimize the error function for different types of PV cells . A set of feasible 57 
parameters is obtained for a cell from multiple independent runs of the algorithm.  58 
• The results are analyzed in detail and a comparison of L -SHADE algorithm with other equivalent metaheuristics 59 
is carried out on the problem of PV cell parameter estimation.  60

7. Conclusion  4 
This paper presents parameter estimation of PV cell s by applying some selected evolutionary  algorithm s with the 5 
use of datasheet information at three  major points of I -V characteristic: open circuit, short circuit and maximum 6 
power points. Parameter optimizations adopting single -diode and double -diode models for different types of PV 7 
cells are performed in this study.  Detailed circuit analys es are presented for both single -diode and double -diode 8 
models , and parameters are extracted partly by analytical method and partly by the optimization algorithm s. While 9 
application of L -SHADE algorithm is presented in detail, the results obtained using  algorithms such as GA, PSO, 10 
ABC, MSA and GWO are succinctly exhibited . It is substantiated with simulation and analysis that a unique I -V 11 
characteristic cannot be attained with limited datasheet information of only on the three major points. Different set s 12 
of probable optimal parameters can satisfy the I -V relationship s at the three points. Hence, importance only on all 13 
or one of the 3 -points allows the designer to choose any optimal set of parameters  from the pool of optimized 14 
solutions . In order to obtain a unique and accurate I-V characteristic of a PV cell, experimental data on more points 15 
(definitely more than 3 -major points) are necessary. Although the study is performed at STC condition for the PV 16 
cells, the conclusion is valid for  other temperatures and irradiance levels also. At any other temperature or irradiance 17 
level, multiple sets of optimal parameters can be obtained at different trial runs and each set of parameters can 18 
conform to the I-V relationships at the three major  points. 19
