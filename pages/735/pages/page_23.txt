Although it may look simple, the LQR presents some challenges. First, the system can
become easily unstable, as the control matrix Khas to be such that the matrix (A+BK)has
eigenvalues of magnitude smaller than one. Therefore, policy updates cannot be too large, inorder to prevent divergence. Second, the reward is unbounded and the expected return canbe very large, especially at the beginning with an initial random policy. As a consequence,the initial TD error can be very large as well. Third, states and actions are unbounded andcannot be normalized in [0,1], a common practice in RL.
However, we can compute in closed form both the expected return and the Q-function,
being able to easily assess the quality of the evaluated algorithms. More speciﬁcally, theQ-function is quadratic in the state and in the action, i.e.,
