4.3 Neighborhood-based Approaches
Numerous neighborhood-based approaches have been proposed
in the literature (e.g., see [ 23,24] and references therein). While
model-based approaches were found to achieve be/t_ter ranking ac-
curacy on some data sets, neighborhood-based approaches domi-
nated on others, e.g., the Million Song Data Competition on Kaggle
[1,14]. Essentially, the co-occurrence matrix (or some modi/f_ied
variant) is typically used as item-item or user-user similarity matrix
in neighborhood-based methods. /T_hese approaches are usually
heuristics, as the similarity matrix is not learned by optimizing
an objective function (loss function or likelihood). More impor-
tantly, the closed-form solution derived in Eqs. 4 and 8 reveals
that the inverse of the data Gram matrix is the conceptually correct
similarity matrix,3see Section 3.2 for more details. /T_his is in con-
trast to the typical neighborhood-based approaches, which use the
data Gram-matrix without inversion. /T_he use of the conceptually
correct, inverse matrix in /e.sc/a.sc/s.sc/e.sc/r.scmay explain the improvement ob-
served in Table 2 compared to the heuristics used by state-of-the-art
neighborhood approaches.
