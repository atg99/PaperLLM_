In general, the PV module manufacturer s provide limited information on the system. A few articles  proposed useful 18 
methods to estimate parameter s based on datasheet  information . Ref. [ 20] presented comparison of parameter 19 
extraction techniques of crystalline and thin -film solar -cell modules  using classical Gauss -Siedel method and 20

Literature [ 22] suggested  a detail ed mathematical model of the PV module  focusing mainly on three major points 22 
of open circuit, short circuit and maximum power on I -V curve. The combination of analytical and metaheuristic 23 
methods was discovered in [ 23-24]. Some of the parameters of the PV system were found analytically while t he 24 
remaining parameters were estimated using particle swarm optimization  (PSO)  [23] and genetic algorithm (GA) 25 
[24]. The nameplate data of solar cell were utilized in [ 25] for parameter extraction  applying bacterial foraging 26 
algorithm . This paper presents PV module parameter extraction using datasheet information at three major points 27 
of I-V characteristic curve for monocrystalline, polycrystalline and thin -film solar cells. A few parameters have been 28 
identified analytically, while the remaining parameters are optimized using linear population size reduction 29 
technique of success history based adaptive differential evolution (L -SHADE) algorithm  [26]. Similar  approach  in 30 
[23] considered  only single -diode model.  Again, Villalva et. al.  [22] used datasheet information with analytical 31 
derivation of only the series resistance ( 𝑅𝑆) and parallel resistance ( 𝑅𝑃) values in single -diode model assuming 32 
certain values for diode ideality factor ( 𝑎 ). This study includes both single and double -diode models without  33 
assum ing any parameter for the solar cell s. A new error function considering I -V characteristic at the three major 34 
points is formulated such that the optimization algorithm comes up with multiple probable  feasible solutions from 35 
different trial runs of the optimization algorithm. The literature [ 22-25] that used datasheet information (only on 36 
three major points) for parameter extraction did not enlighten on the possibility of multiple optimal solutions during 37 
optimization even under a defin ed condition of operating temperature and solar irradiance.  The simple error function 38 
together with the algorithm proposed in this work could ensure zero error at all trial runs  under standard test 39 
condition (STC) . SHADE [27] is a powerful variant of DE algorithm. Classical DE requires 3 -control parameters 40 
to be user defined: scale factor ( 𝐹), crossover rate ( 𝐶𝑅) and population size ( 𝑁𝑝). In SHADE, 𝐹 and 𝐶𝑅 values 41 
are automatically adapted based on the success history of earlier generations. In L-SHADE, population size ( 𝑁𝑝) is 42 
also dynamic  and it is observed that a reasonable population size generates good solutions. In summary, the 43 
performance of L-SHADE is not so much dependent on user -defined inputs. Further, SHADE and its variant L - 44 
SHADE have  shown very promising results for optimization of discrete [ 28] and continuous variables [ 29-30]. 45 
Inspired by the growing application in power domain and its competitive performance, we employ L-SHADE 46
