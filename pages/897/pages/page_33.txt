Deiss et al. (2018) suggested swapping right and left electrodes to double the size of the dataset. They utilized a dataset of brain monitoring in an intensive care unit (ICU) for 5-way classification (Seizure, Lateralized Periodic Discharges (LPD), Generalized Periodic Discharges (GPD), Generalized Rhythmic Delta Activity (GRDA), Lateralized Rhythmic Delta Activity (LRDA)), and the last one corresponds to Other/Artifacts (O/A))on 155 patients [113]. The most challenging issue in their experiment was to make the model learn how to generalize to new patients. To simulate different patients, they kept three reference electrodes in the middle of the scalp unchanged and left/right flipped the remaining electrodes. Swapping electrodes in this manner doubles the amount of data. The authors reported that this DA method did not affect classification for tasks with symmetrical signals between the brain hemispheres (The authors did not report the accuracies before DA). Shovon et al. (2019) applied STFT on EEG signals to transform signal to images for binary classification of motor-imagery signals [114]. They used rotation, flipping, zoom in and zoom out as DA techniques to overcome the overfitting problem in their proposed CNN model. Additional 1000 augmented images increased the average accuracy to 89.19% (no accuracy before DA was reported). Freer et al.
