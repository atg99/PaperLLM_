Adversarial pretraining has been shown to be highly effective in boosting performance in general-domain
applications [ 37]. We thus conducted adversarial pretraining in PubMedBERT and compared its performance
with standard pretraining (Table 11). Surprisingly, adversarial pretraining generally leads to a slight degradation
in performance, with some exceptions such as sentence similarity (BIOSSES). We hypothesize that the reason
may be similar to what we observe in pretraining with full texts. Namely, adversarial training is most useful if
the pretraining corpus is more diverse and relatively out-domain compared to the application tasks. We leave a
more thorough evaluation of adversarial pretraining to future work.
