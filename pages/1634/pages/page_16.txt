A.2. Experiment 2: Robustness to initial step-sizes
In addition to the experiment on Reacher environment (see Figure 1), we report the sensitivity of the studied methods
to initial step-sizes on another two tasks: Walker2d andHumanoid . For these tasks, we observe a similar trend, which
shows that HARPG can be faster than Vanilla-PG , which happens in the small step-size regime, see Figure 3. On the other
hand, the normalized methods tolerate larger step-sizes and N-PG-IGT , in particular, demonstrates more robustness to
step-size choice. Overall, we observe that, naturally, normalized and non-normalized methods have different range of
optimal step-sizes, which makes it necessary to extensively tune the step-sizes for comparing between these two types of
algorithms. Another observation is that for the tested environments, the variance reduced HARPG method performs better
than Vanilla-PG only for small non-tuned step-sizes. Therefore, in the next experiment, we compare these methods in the
small step-size regime.

A.3. Experiment 3: Comparison of Vanilla-PG and HARPG with small untuned step-sizes
In this experiment, we demonstrate that the variance reduced HARPG method can outperform Vanilla-PG for a fixed small
initial step-size 𝛾0, see Figure 4.7Our observations here are consistent with those reported in the previous work for other
variance reduced policy gradient algorithms, e.g., (Shen et al., 2019; Huang et al., 2020; Salehkaleybar et al., 2022). However,
when the step-sizes are tuned, Vanilla-PG preforms consistently better than variance reduced algorithms, see also Experiment
4 (Figure 5) for additional comparison to other methods with tuned step-sizes.
