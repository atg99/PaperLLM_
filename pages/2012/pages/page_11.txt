CONCLUSIONS  5 
As well as other road des ign features, lane width has been found to impact the disengagement 6 
probability of driving autonom ation  systems.  The authors have explored how this factor affects the 7 
reliability of a level 2 vehicle  throughout 81 lanes located at tangent  sections  of an urban arterial. Results 8 
show that lane width thresholds for human and automated lateral control can be clearly def ined.  9 
Three new lane width concepts are therefore proposed : human lane width  – maximum width for 10 
which human is always requested  for the steering control ; automatic lane width  – minimum width for 11 
which the steering control can always be performed autonomously ; and the critical lane width , which 12 
presents  the same probability of human and automatic  lateral control.  13 
For the tested system, h uman lane width was determined as 2.50 m, automated lane width was 14 
2.75 m, while the critical lane width was established at 2.72 m. Note  the very narrow range that shifts 15 
from complete manual to complete automated lateral control  (0.25 m) . 16 
It is important to highlight that these thresholds were determined for a single LKA system . Other 17 
LKA/LCA systems are based on a variety of technologies and will present different limitations. The 18 
methodology proposed in this paper establishes a framework to test the capabil ities of the different 19 
systems, without the need to know the underlying technology.  Hence, this information could be used as a 20 
factor to define clear Operating Design Domains  and therefore rapidly determine if any width -related 21 
disengagement is expected al ong a certain corridor . 22 
In order to get some insight about the limitations of current marketed systems, a further research 23 
will include more observations with other makes and models, including heavy vehicles . These tests will 24 
be carried out in different Operating Design Domains  and different geometries (such as horizontal and 25 
crest vertical curves), and different speeds. Adverse weather, night driving and sun glaring conditions 26 
should also be tested, to check how robust is a certain driving auto mation system technology to different 27 
ODD variations.  28 
New parameters should also be considered in the future, such as the lateral position of the vehicle, 29 
to determine if it has some influence on the disengagement event. If so, it is expected a slight redu ction of 30 
these thresholds, as well as a possible reduction of the range between manual and automatic lane widths.  31 
Every vehicle should be measured with and without mirrors, considering the outer boundary of the 32 
vehicle.  33
