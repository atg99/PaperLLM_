There have been debates about automation v ersus autonomy. Kaber (2018 ) believes that 
recent human -automation interaction research has confused concepts of automation and 
autonomy , which has led to inappropriate expectation for design and misdirected criticism of 
design methods. The situation is not new and has origins in historical human factors research. For 
example, Sheridan and Verplank  (1978) developed a taxonomy of 10 levels of automation to 
provide a basis for automated systems design. Parasuraman, Sheridan, and Wickens (2000) 
identified various aspects of automation to be considered in human – machine systems design as 
well as a space of possible human -automation combinations for complex systems control (e.g., 
initial function allocation decisions). O’Neill  et al. (2020)  defines the difference between 
automation and autonomy is a matter of degree and both exist on the level of automatio n 
continuum as defined by Sheridan and Verplank (1978).
