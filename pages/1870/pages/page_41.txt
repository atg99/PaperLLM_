Other researchers also advocate human -controlled autonomy. For instance, Endsley (2017) 
believes that in an autonomous system, with the improvement of the "automation" level of 
individual functions, and as the autonomy of the overall system increase s, the operator's attention 
to these functions and situation awareness will decrease  in emergency situations , so that the 
possibility of the “out-of-the-loop" effect will also increase. Fatal accidents involving autonomous 
vehicles  that have occurred in recent years have showed th ese typical human factors issues  as 
seen in automation (Navarro, 2018; NHTSA, 2017; Ends ley, 2017, 2018).  Also, AI-based 
autonomous systems ’ human -like intelligent abilities (e.g., learning ) will continue to evolve as 
they are used in different environments. The uncertainty of the operational results of autonomous 
systems means that the system may develop behaviors in unexpected ways . Therefore, initial 
research shows that autonomy may give operators a stronger shock than "automation surprise" 
(Prada & Paiva, 2014;  Shively  et al., 2017 ). These effects may further amplify the impact of the  
“automation surpri se” issue , which is called the "lumberjack" effect (Onnasch et al. , 2014).  
There is extensive research work carried out on automation awareness (e.g., Sarter &  
Woods, 1995; Endsley, 2017; Xu, 2007) and levels of automation (e.g., Sheridan & Verplank, 
1978; Parasuraman  et al., 2000; Onnasch et al., 2014), advocating human -centered automation 
(Billings , 1997). Also, there are debates about the relationship between automation and 
autonomy. We believe that automation and autonomy are not differentiated in terms of level of 
automation, instead, the presence or otherwise of a certain level of potential human -like cognitive 
and autonomous capabilities (e.g., learning, se lf-governance, self -independence, and self -
adaption) is the essential difference between the two (Kaber, 2018; Xu, 2021).
