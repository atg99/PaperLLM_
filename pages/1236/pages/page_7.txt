We note that the proof for the proximity-weighted
classiﬁcation problem requires that the trees in the forest
are grown until the terminal nodes are pure. We show
empirically, however, that RF-GAP proximity predictions
more closely match the random forest’s predictions than the
other proximities even when trees are pruned. See Figure 2
for a comparison. Here we compared proximity-weighted
predictions using minimum node sizes of 1, 5, 10, 20, and
50. For regression problems, RF-GAP proximity predictions
match those of the random forest perfectly, while this isn’t
always the case for classiﬁcation. The difference increases
with node size as ties become more likely. We also note
that the random forest and proximity-weighted predictions
produce higher error rates with the increase in minimum
node size (Figure A.4).
