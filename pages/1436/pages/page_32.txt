Model unit selection is a very important area in E2E modeling. While most
units (characters [74, 80], word-piece [37, 101, 244, 308, 322], words [7, 154,
259], or even phrases [67]) are purely derived based on text and word-piece
unit is the dominating one, there are also studies working on phoneme units
[112, 234, 293, 345]. As ASR is unique with the underlying acoustic signal, it
should be better to build units bridging between the text and phoneme worlds.
To that end, the pronunciation guided unit [207, 309, 346] is worth studying.
Trained with paired speech-text data, E2E models are more data hungry
than hybrid models. If the training data size is small, the performance of E2E
models drops significantly. There are lots of ways to address the challenge
of building E2E models for low-resource languages. First, regularization
technologies such as dropout [264] can be used to prevent E2E models from
overfitting to limited training data [348]. Data augmentation methods such
as SpecAugment [209] and speed perturbation [133] are also very helpful. In
addition, there are also methods such as adversarial learning [60] and meta-
learning [96]. The most popular solution is to first pre-train E2E models
either with multilingual data or with self-supervised learning (SSL), and then
fine-tune with the low-resource labeled data. A multilingual E2E model already
captures lots of information across languages, which makes the transfer learning
using the target language data very effective [38, 49, 108, 114, 229]. SSL is
even more powerful because it does not need any labeled data for pre-training,
naturally solving the low-resource challenge. Therefore, SSL is becoming a new
