3.2 Conversion Model of Residual Network
Different from linear structures employed in previous methods, the topology of residual network is a
directed acyclic graph. This makes normalisation methods in previous literature incompatible. When
we convert residual network to its spiking version, new synaptic weights are introduced in shortcuts
as SNN creates synaptic connections between any two neighboured layers of neurons. Although
the original weights in shortcuts are set to 1 to perform identity mapping, those weights should also
be scaled accordingly as weights in convolutional synapses being normalised. Otherwise, if we
normalise stacked layers only, i.e., convolutional synapses, ﬁring rates of spiking neurons would
notably deviate from their corresponding activations. In Figure 2, we demonstrated an example of
mismatch between ﬁring rates and activations with the shortcuts unnormalised.
