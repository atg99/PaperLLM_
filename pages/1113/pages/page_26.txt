Kuipers et al.(2022) have shown that when the data is noisy or scarce, the MAP graph
may include a lot of edges with low posterior probability, many of which turn out to be false
positives. We perform model averaging based on a sample of DAGs obtained by partition
MCMC and remove the edges with a posterior less than 0.5. As we have seen in Section 5, this
approach can help to remove false-positive edges, while keeping most of the true positives.
Figure 11 depicts differences between MAP and consensus graphs for the KIRP cohort. Eight
edges out of ten passed the posterior threshold of 0.5. In the KIRC cohort, similarly, 12 out
of 18 edges of MAP CPDAG passed the threshold of 0.5.
Figure 12 visualizes the consensus models for KIRP and KIRC in one graph. Many of the
discovered edges correspond to those found previously by Kuipers et al.(2018b) for the
respective cancer subtypes. Although we have followed the analysis steps from Kuipers et al.
(2018b) on the same set of tumors, we used an updated prior and genes that are specific
to analyzed cohorts. Consequently, the discovered networks display some differences. For
example, we discovered an edge between CCBL2 and R3HDM1, but the gene CCLB2 was
not included in the list of genes analyzed by Kuipers et al.(2018b).
