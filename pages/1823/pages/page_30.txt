7.4 Flexibility analysis  598 
7.4.1 Performance across different types of codes and standards  599 
To test the flexibility of the proposed method, i.e., test its performance across different regulatory 600 
documents, the proposed method was used to generate t hree different types of intelligent requirements: 601 
using MFS requirement hierarchies developed based on IBC, IECC, and ADA Standards. This is 602 
important to evaluate, because requirements in different codes/standards typically have different semantic 603 
and syntactic structures (e.g., terminology, vocabulary, and sentence length). As shown in Table 5, the 604 
proposed method achieved consistently  high performance across all three types of codes/standards, in 605 
terms of both natural -language requirement comprehensibility (i.e., over 80.0% for all BLEU and 606 
ROUGE  scores ) and semantic linking (i.e., over 88% for precision, recall, and F1 measure), indic ating 607 
that the approach has a good level of flexibility in dealing with different types of codes/requirements.  608
