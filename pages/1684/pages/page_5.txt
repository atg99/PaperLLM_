3. Meta-Features
A fundamental MtL question is: how to extract suitable information to characterize spe-
cic tasks? Researchers have been trying to answer this question by looking for dataset
properties that can aect learning algorithm performance, measuring this performance out-
right [8, 59], investigating alternatives [39, 74] and adapting/creating new measures based on
existing ones [17, 75].
In all cases, the meta-features were always organized in groups. These groups are subsets of
data characterization measures [13] that share similarities among them. However, the frontiers
between them are not always clear and strictly delimited. The fact that two studies mentioned
using the same group of measures does not mean that they used exactly the same measures [73].
Additionally, dierent names have been used to describe groups of the same measures. In this
work, we propose the organization of the measures in six groups:
Simple: measures that are easily extracted from data [65], commonly known, and do not re-
quire signicant computational resources [62]. They are also called general measures [17].
Statistical: measures that capture the statistical properties of the data [65]. These measures
capture data distribution indicators, such as average, standard deviation, correlation and
kurtosis. They only characterize numerical attributes [17].
Information-theoretic: measures from the information theory eld [17]. These measures
are based on entropy [70], capturing the amount of information in the data and their
complexity [73]. They can be used to characterize discrete attributes.
Model-based: measures extracted from a model induced using the training data [65]. They
are often based on properties of decision tree (DT) models [8, 58], when they are referred
to as decision-tree-based meta-features [8]. Properties extracted from other models have
also been used [22].
Landmarking: measures that use the performance of simple and fast learning algorithms to
characterize datasets [73]. The algorithms must have dierent biases and should capture
relevant information with a low computational cost. Dierent approaches have been
investigated [23, 74].
