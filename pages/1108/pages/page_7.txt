The DBN is often seen as a generalization of other temporal reasoning developments, such as the hidden Markov 177 
model (HMM) and the Kalman filter model (KFM)  (Hulst 2006 ). These models , which can be expressed in a 178 
compact form,  are popular for their fast learnin g and fast inference techniques . In fact, DBNs generalize HMMs 179 
by expressing the state space in not only a single discrete random variable but also in a factored form.  180 
Temporal plate and contemporal nodes  181 
The temporal plate is the area within the DBN model that includes temporal information (i.e., information that 182 
changes from a time step to another) . The temporal plate includes the variables that evolve over time . These 183 
variables are  the part of the DBN that can be unrolled. However,  nodes that have a constant value at every time 184 
step are considered a waste of memory and computational power  if copied in each time step . Therefore, i t is 185 
wise to introduce the se nodes outsid e the temporal plates. T he collection of these nodes is called the contemporal 186 
space , and the nodes are called c ontemporal nodes  (Murphy and Russell 2002 ). 187 
Anchor and terminal nodes  188 
One extension of the original DBN formalism was established  in (Hulst 2006 ), where the author  introduced 189 
nodes that are only c onnected to the first and last t ime slices  of the DBN. Such varia bles do not affect the 190 
intermediate time slices like the contemporal nodes.  191 
 Anchor node (A): a node that is outside the temporal plate but has at least one child  node  inside the 192 
temporal plate  in the first time slice of the unrolled DBN.  193 
 Terminal node (T) : a node that is outside the temporal plate and has at least one par ent inside the 194 
temporal plate in  the last time slice of the unrolled DBN.  195
