Scenario 1  554 
Figure 15 shows the  result of the first scenario. The states of the damage and recovery variables are set according 555 
to Table 4: the damage variables are set to negative impact ( i.e., H is set to “High”  while R 1 and R2 are set to 556 
“Low” ) while the recovery variables are set to positive impact (i.e., R3 and R4 are set to “High”). To discuss the 557 
analysis result s, we will focus on the node “Performance” (i.e., node in Blue color). The result is presented as a 558 
probability variati on for  each of  the three states of the variable . From Figure 15, we can see that the probability 559 
for the node “Performance” being “High” starts very low then it increases rapidly to reach a stable state. This 560 
result is expected since our initial input for the damage is set to negative impact , which caused the probability 561 
for the system’s performance  of being  “High”  to be low in the beginning. On the other hand, t he recovery 562 
variables have been set to positive impact , and this  caused the probability of the system’s performance of being  563 
“High” to incre ase rapidly over time . The probability does not reach 1 because of the uncertainties introduced 564 
in the static indicators, which have been transmitted throughout the network. As complementary, the probability 565 
of being “Low” starts relatively high then it re duce s over time  with the same rate .  566
