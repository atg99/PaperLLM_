Research on disaster resilience has recently been fostered  due to the noticeable increase in the  number of  natural 23 
and human -caused disasters. Resilience has been  defined differently depending o n the field of study  (Doorn et 24 
al. 2018 ; Hosseini et al. 2016b ; Manyena 2006 ). In engineering, resilience  is the ability to withstand a 25 
disturbance caused by an external agent and recover quickly if damage occurs  (Cimellaro et al. 2016 ). Current 26 
scientific  contribution s are  aimed at understanding disaster resilience and find ing new wa ys to measure it, 27 
quantitatively  or qualitatively . Several solutions exist in the literature  to measure  resilience at the  community 28 
level (Cutter et al. 2014 ; Kammouh and Cimellaro 2018b ; Kammouh et al. 2018c ) and at the  infrastructure level 29 
(Gardoni 20 18; Liu et al. 2017 ; Sharma et al. 2018 ); however, r esearch on  resilience  quantification  is still in the 30 
early stage  of development  and n o universal  method exists so far. Among others,  resilience indicators are an 31 
effective tool to compute the resilience of engineering systems because they allow modeling complex systems  32 
easily and effectively  (Cutter et al. 2008 ). Several authors have proposed  indicator -based methodolo gies to 33 
evaluate the resilie nce of engineering systems ; some are deterministic  (Henry and Ramirez -Marquez 2012 ; 34 
Kammouh et al. 2017b ; Kammouh et al. 2019b ) and the others are probabilistic (Cockburn and Tesfamariam 35 
2012 ; De Iuliis et al. 2019b ; Kammouh et al. 2018b ; Kammouh et al. 2018d ; Shahriar et al. 2012 ). Nevertheless , 36 
developing a  standardized indicator -based methodology  to quantify resilience is still challenging.  The m ain 37 
challenge  is the presence of uncertainty in the resilience model  and its input s. The interdependency and the 38 
weighting factors  distribution among the variables are also other issue s that cannot be handled in a deterministic 39 
way. Therefore, probabilistic methods are usually preferred  over deterministic methods . Probabilistic models 40 
are more powerful to model uncertainties and interdependencies  and they are more ap propriate to represent 41 
reality. In fact,  deterministic models are considered a particular case of probabilistic model s (Pourret et al. 42 
2008 ).  43 
While probabilistic models solve the gap of modeling capability that deterministic models suffer from, not all 44 
probabilistic approaches are suitable to model the behavior of engineering systems, especially in cases where 45 
past data is not  readily  available  (Cimellaro 2016 ). One way to properly model the behavior of a system in a 46 
probabilistic manner  is through the use of Fuzzy analysis and Bayesian Networks (BNs).  Fuzzy theory  47 
represents a method for solving problems that are related to uncertainty and vagueness . It has been implemented 48 
in some  research works to study the res ilience of infrastructure systems (De Iuliis et al. 2019a ; Kammouh et al. 49 
2018e ; Kammouh et al. 2018f ). Fuzzy theory is  usually  implemented in cases with no data , which  is replaced 50 
with expert judgments. The output of fuzzy -based methods is heavily subjective  given that they are based on 51 
opinions rather than data . On the other hand, BN is a Directed Acyclic Graph where the nodes represent 52 
variables of interest and the links between them indicate causal dependencies. BNs are widely used for 53 
knowledge representation and reasoning under uncertainty , especially in the context of partial information . They 54

a single framework. In addition , BN provides probabilistic relationships among the variables, which allows 56 
mode ling the interdependencies  among them . In the literature, there is a limited number of research 57
