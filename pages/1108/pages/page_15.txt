Figure 6 shows a Bayesian network applied to the data of the country “Brazil” (row 26 in Appendix ). The 348 
analysis is done using the GeNIe modeler, a graphical user interface that allows for interactive model building 349 
and learning based on the Bayes’ inference theory  (BayesFusion 2016 ). The top level of the network presents 350 
the main activities to be performed at the national and local levels, the intermediate level includes the three 351 
resilience pillars, and the bottom level node is the output of the network (i.e., resilience).  As can be noticed in  352 
Figure 6, the three resilience pillars have a probability distribution for their different states despite that the 353 
indicators are deterministic. This is caused by the CPTs as well as the characteristics of the Bayesian inference 354 
adopte d in the study. The final output of resilience presents a range of uncertainty (16% High , 28% Good , 31% 355 
Moderate , 19% Low, and 7% Vulnerable) (Note: the sum is 101 instead of 100 because the tool used in the 356 
analysis rounds the values to the nearest whole number). In the analyzed scenario, the resilience state of the 357 
country Brazil is most likely to be “Moderate ” given that this state has obtained the highest probability.  358 
The Bayesian network can also be employed in a backward analysis. A deterministic resi lience state can be set 359 
(for instance “Good ”) and the output would be the levels of the indicators required to achieve the assumed 360 
resilience state. This is rather useful in case of system design or system improvement.  361
