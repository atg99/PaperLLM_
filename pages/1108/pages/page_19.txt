5. Time -dependent resilience analysis using Dynamic Bayesian Networks  395 
In general, the r esilience of a system tends to be  a process rather than  a state; thus, accounting for the 396 
performance variation of a system can be  important. Ordinary Bayesian Networks are unable to account for  the 397 
time dimension in the analysis as they are limited to static systems. In this section , we propose a new 398 
methodology to assess the resilience of engineering systems in a dynamic manner.  399 
5.1 Dynamic resilience model  400 
The resilience model used in the dynamic resilience analysis is based on the resilience definition by Bruneau 401 
and Reinhorn (2007)  who describe the resilience of a system using four components, also called the four R ’s of 402 
resilienc e (4R’s): 403 
 Robustness  (R1): refers to the ability of a system to stand a certain level of stress preserving its 404 
functionality;  405 
 Redundancy  (R2): indicates the alternative resources in the recovery stage when the primary ones are 406 
inadequate;  407 
 Rapidity  (R3): the capacity to contain losses and avoid future disruption. It represents the slope of the 408 
functionality curve during the recovery phase ; 409 
 Resourcefulness  (R4): considers the human factor and the capacity to move needed resources . 410
