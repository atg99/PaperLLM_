Resilience can be an outcome (static)  or a process (dynamic)  (Cutter et al. 2008 ). While most of the 87 
abovementioned research es focused on  analyzing engineering resilience from a static point of view , there is a 88 
significant gap in assessing  the dynamic nature of resilience  through q uantitative approaches.  In fact,  BN does 89 
not capture the behavior of dynamic system s and the interdependencies among  the system ’s components and 90 
variables. BN is a snapshot of the system, which implies that the restoration process, which is inherently time - 91 
dependent, cannot be modeled.  Moreover , a feedback loop is not allowed in a BN model ; thus, it cannot be used 92 
to model a cyclic relationship. Although  there are some research works that use the DBN as an inference tool 93 
to express resilience in a dynamic manner, the resilience model s adopted in those researches and the transitional 94 
model from  one step to another in the time -space was not clearly defined.   95
