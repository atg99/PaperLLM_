V. I LLUSTRATIVE QAOA I MPLEMENTATIONS
In this section, we demonstrate how the QAOA can be
used as a computational intelligence tool in its own right.
First, following the history of the field we describe a QAOA
implementation for the MaxCut problem. Then, illustrating
the expanded power of the operator ansatz generalization, we
modify this for the maximum bisection (MaxBis) problem.
MaxBis is the problem of finding the maximum cut with
equally sized partitions. Here we follow Farhi et al. [6]
for MaxCut and Golden et al. [26] for MaxBis. Next we
look at the traveling salesperson problem (TSP), which is
a central problem in computational intelligence used as a
benchmark for optimization algorithms [72]. We use it as an
example to illustrate the process of parameter selection and
implementation. Application of the QAOA to the TSP was
studied by Hadfield et al. [8], which we follow closely in
this subsection, and B ¨artschi and Eidenbenz [44]. The TSP
also provides a good jumping off point for exploring the
connections between QAOA and CI since Ghimire et al. [62]
apply parallel ant-colony optimization to TSP QAOA. For
more information about this work, see Sec. IV-A.
We print quantum circuit figures as Qiskit circuit dia-
grams [15] to help readers unfamiliar with quantum computing
toolkits verify their code. The horizontal lines represent qubits,
initialized to the |0⟩state. Geometric figures connecting qubits
represent quantum gates, which are applied in series from left
to right until control reaches the end of the diagram. These
gates in this section commute, allowing for the implementation
trick exp(A+B) = exp( A) exp( B)given by the commutative
case of Trotterization—see Sec. III-B for more information.
A. Two Graph Cut Maximization Problems
Recall that a cuton a graph G= (V, E)is a partition of V
into two complementary subsets V=V1⊔V2. The cost of a
cut is the number of edges connecting the two subsets
C(V1, V2) = #{(v, w)∈V2:v∈V1, w∈V2},
which we want to maximize. This problem is NP-complete
and has been approached with computational intelligencetechniques such as ant colony optimization [73] and genetic
algorithms [74]. Throughout this section, we set n=|V|for
convenience. In Sec. V-A1, we present the original QAOA
algorithm for unconstrained maxcut, then in Sec. V-A2, we use
the operator ansatz framework to solve the constrained variant
called maximum bisection for graphs with an even number
of nodes, which restricts the search space to graph bisections,
which correspond to Hamming weight n/2bitstrings.

2) The Maximum Bisection Problem: The maximum bi-
section problem for graphs with an even number of nodes
modifies MaxCut by restricting the search space to graph
bisections, which are partitions into complementary sets of
equal size. In terms of the problem encoding, this corresponds
to the set Sof Hamming weight n/2strings of length n. As
this has the same objective function as MaxCut, the phase
separator stays the same. But we need to start and stay
in the feasible subspace between QAOA rounds, so change
the plus state to the superposition of Hamming weight n/2
strings of length n. Such a superposition of equal Hamming-
weight strings is called a Dicke state . There is a linear-
