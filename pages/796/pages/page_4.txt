the ground truth label of each input image is unknown to the selector in Figure 2 , with
Section 4 focusing on the new CNN topology. Section 5 is devoted to the proposed selector
maintaining classiﬁcation accuracy while achieving signiﬁcant reduction in bits. Finally,
Section 6 concludes the paper.
2. Motivation: Case Study
This section motivates our approach to Question 1 as illustrated in Figure 2. Let us
ﬁrst reproduce results which lead people to the conventional understanding that JPEG
compression generally degrades classiﬁcation performance of DNNs.
The conventional understanding is based on the approach shown in Figure 1, where a
constant QF is used to compress all images in a whole set of images. This approach can be
dubbed as “one QF vs. all images”. For Inception V3 and ResNet-50 V2 pre-trained with
the original images in the training set of the ILSVRC 2012 dataset, Figure 3 shows their
respective curves of the Top 1 classiﬁcation accuracy and Top 5 classiﬁcation accuracy on
the whole ImageNet validation dataset vs. the constant QF as the value of the constant QF
in Figure 1 decreases from 100 to 10 with a step size of 10. From the results in Figure 3, it is
clear that classiﬁcation performance deteriorates as the value of the constant QF decreases,
hereby reconﬁrming the conventional understanding.
