Next, we considered the convergence of the three evolutionary algorithms TCaS, BLA and
MPSO. They were performed on a dataset of 120 tasks over 500 generations with coefﬁcient balance
a=0.5. We observed the population every 20 generations. The ﬁtness values of all individuals were
synthesized, as shown in Figure 7. The middle point in line indicates the average ﬁtness value of whole
population while the bottom and top of the error bar present the ﬁtness value of the worst and best
individual, respectively. It was observed that BLA and MPSO expressed fast convergences after 100
generations, however, the found solutions had small ﬁtness values at 0.69 and 0.72 compared with 0.84
achieved by TCaS algorithm. In some ﬁrst generations, individuals of TCaS algorithm were distributed
with a wide range of ﬁtness value. The best solution was found in 240th generation; afterwards,
the ﬁtness range narrowed, meaning the population gathered around the extreme. TCaS converged
after 300 generations. The mechanism of BLA that all drones perform crossover operator with only
one queen each generation made the population easily fall into local extreme. Similarly, in MPSO,
the leader had a great inﬂuence on every particle in the population, thus there was high probability
that the population fell into local extreme when the leader was stuck. Consequently, both algorithms
converged quickly with low ﬁtness values. Meanwhile, the proposed TCaS algorithm maintained
population diversity, thus could reach more optimal solution.
