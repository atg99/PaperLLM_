The paper discusses the optimization of molecular properties, specifically focusing on the Quantitative Estimation of Drug-likeness (QED) while maintaining similarity to the original molecules. Figure 3a illustrates that as the weight on similarity increases, the optimized molecules exhibit greater similarity to the starting molecules, although a larger fraction of these optimized molecules demonstrates lower QED values than their original counterparts. An experiment involving 20 randomly selected molecules from the ChEMBL database was conducted, where the relative improvement in QED was quantified and depicted in Figure 3b. The relative improvement is defined as the ratio of the actual improvement in QED to the maximum possible improvement. The empirical distribution of absolute QED improvements is presented in Figure S6. Overall, the findings suggest that while optimization can enhance similarity, it may not always lead to improved QED scores. The study emphasizes the balance between molecular similarity and desired property enhancement in multi-objective optimization tasks.
