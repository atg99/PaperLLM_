This paper presents a study on the optimization of molecular properties using a reinforcement learning framework, specifically through the Graph Convolutional Policy Network (GCPN) and a novel approach called MolDQN. The primary focus is on single property optimization, targeting penalized logP and Quantitative Estimate of Druglikeness (QED). The penalized logP is computed as the logP value adjusted for synthetic accessibility and long cycles, while QED represents a measure of drug-likeness.

The optimization process involves setting up a reward system based on the chosen property score, with distinct methodologies for logP and QED optimization. For logP, an empty initial molecule is used, and for QED, a two-step approach begins with an empty molecule followed by five high-QED candidates from the first step. The study compares the performance of the proposed MolDQN model against various baselines, including random, greedy, and ε-greedy strategies, as well as previously published models like ORGAN, JT-V AE, and GCPN.

Results indicate that the bootstrapped DQN variant of MolDQN achieves higher QED scores compared to naive DQN, highlighting its exploration efficiency. Notably, the generated molecules for penalized logP optimization are not drug-like, emphasizing the necessity of a well-designed reward mechanism that considers multiple objectives in medicinal chemistry.

The paper includes a detailed comparison of the top three property scores from each method, illustrating the effectiveness of the MolDQN approaches in both optimization tasks. The findings underscore the importance of reinforcement learning in molecular design, though caution is advised regarding the single-objective reward systems used.
