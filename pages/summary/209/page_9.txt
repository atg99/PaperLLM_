In this paper, the authors present the MolDQN model, which integrates advanced deep reinforcement learning techniques with chemical domain knowledge to optimize molecular design. The study demonstrates that MolDQN achieves comparable or superior results to established algorithms in generating molecules with desired properties. Additionally, the authors introduce a visualization method to elucidate the model's decision-making process, aiding in the development of optimization strategies.

The paper also critiques current evaluation metrics for generative models, highlighting the inadequacies of commonly used metrics like logP and QED. The authors argue that these metrics often fail to provide meaningful comparisons in real-world optimization tasks and suggest prioritizing therapeutically relevant properties that can be experimentally verified. They emphasize the need for predictive models that incorporate experimental data into generative decision-making, advocating for a combination of predictive validation and experimental testing.

The authors conclude that while the MolDQN model shows promise in assisting chemists with molecular design, significant work remains to develop valid assessment methods for generative models to enhance their applicability in drug discovery. The datasets used in the study are publicly accessible, and the code for the MolDQN model is available on GitHub.
