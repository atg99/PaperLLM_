The paper presents a novel approach to molecule modification by framing it as a Markov decision process (MDP) and employing deep reinforcement learning, specifically Deep Q-Networks (DQN), to optimize molecular properties. This method stands out in three significant ways: 

1. It utilizes value function learning instead of the commonly used policy gradient methods, which tend to have high variance in gradient estimation. Value function learning offers greater stability and sample efficiency in relevant applications.
  
2. Unlike most existing algorithms that rely on pre-training with datasets, potentially limiting the exploration of chemical space, this model learns from scratch. This allows for the discovery of novel molecules with potentially superior properties, as the model is not constrained by prior datasets.

3. The model is tailored for multi-objective reinforcement learning, enabling users to prioritize different objectives according to their needs.

The methodology involves a stepwise modification of molecules categorized into three actions: atom addition, bond addition, and bond removal. The MDP is structured to ensure that only valid chemical actions are considered, thus maintaining the integrity of the generated molecules. Key features of this MDP include a defined limit on the number of modification steps and the exclusion of chemically invalid actions. The state space consists of valid molecules and the number of steps taken, while the action space encompasses valid modifications based on valence constraints. Overall, this approach provides a robust framework for exploring the vast chemical space through automated and intelligent molecule optimization.
