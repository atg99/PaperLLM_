The paper discusses a multi-objective reinforcement learning framework for optimizing molecular properties, particularly in drug development. It employs the Huber loss function to minimize errors in predictions. The authors outline a scalarized reward mechanism, which incorporates a user-defined weight vector to balance multiple optimization objectives, allowing the model to maximize cumulative rewards effectively.

A significant focus is on the exploration-exploitation dilemma inherent in reinforcement learning. The ε-greedy strategy is employed to balance these competing goals, allowing the model to sometimes select the best-known action while also exploring new actions. To enhance exploration, the authors utilize bootstrapped deep Q-learning, implementing multiple independent Q-functions to promote deeper exploration of the state space.

The deep Q-learning model is based on Mnih et al.'s framework, incorporating improvements such as double Q-learning. The model processes molecular inputs as Morgan fingerprints, and the architecture includes a four-layer fully connected network. The training is conducted across 5,000 episodes, utilizing the Adam optimizer and a piecewise annealing strategy for the exploration parameter ε.

The results demonstrate the framework's effectiveness in optimizing molecular properties compared to existing state-of-the-art models, such as the Junction Tree Variational Autoencoder and Objective-Reinforced Generative Adversarial Networks, showcasing the potential of the proposed method in drug optimization tasks.
