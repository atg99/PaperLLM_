The study evaluates the effectiveness of different models for optimizing molecular properties, particularly focusing on the logP metric in drug discovery. It highlights the limitations of using unconstrained logP maximization, which can lead to a simplistic and greedy policy of merely adding carbon atoms. The authors suggest that performance should be assessed under specific constraints, such as limiting the number of atoms or targeting a specific logP range, which better reflects real-world drug design needs.

The paper compares the performance of two models, MolDQN and GCPN, finding that MolDQN performs better in logP optimization while demonstrating similar results in optimizing Quantitative Estimation of Drug-likeness (QED). The study emphasizes the importance of constrained optimization, where the goal is to enhance a molecule's properties while maintaining similarity to a reference molecule, using the Tanimoto similarity metric based on Morgan fingerprints.

Experimental results show that both variants of MolDQN significantly outperform GCPN across various similarity thresholds, with a high success rate for maintaining similarity constraints. Additionally, the research explores multi-objective optimization, balancing QED and similarity scores, which is essential for retaining the molecular scaffold during optimization.

The findings underscore the necessity of constrained and multi-objective approaches in molecular optimization to ensure practical applicability in drug discovery. The performance metrics and statistical significance of the results are thoroughly documented, supporting the conclusions drawn from the experiments.
