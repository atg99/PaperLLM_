This paper investigates the effectiveness of deep learning in generating novel document layouts, focusing on the relationships between elements in a layout. Traditional methods often rely on handcrafted rules to define layout distributions, which can be subjective and not generalizable. In contrast, this work employs an attention-based architecture within the Variational Autoencoder (VAE) framework, allowing the model to learn these relationships in an unsupervised manner.

The study addresses a gap in layout generation literature by detailing various design choices, such as the use of autoregressive versus non-autoregressive decoders and learned versus non-learned priors. The proposed generative model aims to capture high-level relationships among numerous layout elements without requiring annotations. The paper presents significant contributions, including:

1. A novel generative model for layout generation with an inductive bias towards understanding relationships among elements.
2. Exploration of strategies to manage variational bottlenecks in sequences of varying lengths.

Related work in layout synthesis is examined, highlighting the limitations of existing models, such as LayoutGAN and LayoutVAE, which either focus on simpler layouts or rely on heuristics that may not generalize well. Unlike these approaches, the proposed model does not depend on heuristic labels and learns the layout distribution end-to-end, enhancing its applicability across diverse datasets. Overall, this research demonstrates state-of-the-art performance in layout generation tasks, emphasizing the potential of deep learning techniques in this domain.
