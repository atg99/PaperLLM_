The paper discusses methods for evaluating the diversity of synthesized layouts compared to real layouts, utilizing the DocSim metric as a similarity measure. It highlights the challenges in rigorously computing Wasserstein distance for evaluating diversity, but approximates it for discrete class distributions and continuous bounding box distributions. 

The authors compare their approach to existing methods such as LayoutVAE and LayoutGAN, noting difficulties in reproducing prior results due to a lack of public implementations. They reimplement LayoutVAE and Gupta et al.'s method, finding that while autoregressive decoding slightly reduces diversity, it enhances perceptual quality. The optimal configuration identified combines an autoregressive decoder with a non-learned prior, balancing diversity and perceptual quality.

Quantitative results show that the proposed method generates a significant number of distinct layouts with alignment metrics comparable to real data, outperforming LayoutVAE and demonstrating improved diversity relative to Gupta et al.'s approach. The method also excels in discovering relationships in data with minimal supervision.

Qualitative results further support the quantitative findings, illustrating that the proposed method effectively captures relationships among layout elements, particularly in complex configurations. The authors also explore the application of their approach for data augmentation in document understanding tasks, emphasizing the importance of layout comprehension for tasks beyond OCR. 

Overall, the paper presents a robust evaluation of layout generation methods, emphasizing the importance of diversity and perceptual quality in synthesized layouts while demonstrating superior performance of the proposed approach.
