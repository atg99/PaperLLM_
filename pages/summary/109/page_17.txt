The paper discusses a feature selection method (RFP) for improving classification performance in network intrusion detection datasets, specifically NSL-KDD, UNSW_NB15, and CIC-IDS2017. It presents a heat map and correlation index table highlighting the relationships among various features, emphasizing the retention of important features based on their correlation and importance indices. 

In the feature selection process, features exhibiting strong linear correlations are retained, while those with weak correlations and low importance indices are eliminated. The final datasets consist of 28 features for NSL-KDD and UNSW_NB15, and 52 features for CIC-IDS2017.

To assess the effectiveness of the proposed RFP method, the paper compares it with established techniques such as PCA and AE under identical conditions. The datasets are pre-processed and balanced using the ADRDB algorithm before feature reduction. The classification results demonstrate that RFP outperforms PCA and AE across all datasets in terms of accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score. For instance, in the NSL-KDD dataset, RFP achieves an accuracy of 99.69%, significantly higher than PCA's 98.26% and AE's 97.61%. Similar trends are observed in the UNSW_NB15 and CIC-IDS2017 datasets, reinforcing the proposed method's effectiveness in feature selection for intrusion detection tasks.
