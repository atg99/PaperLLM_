The paper introduces a hybrid sampling method known as ADRDB, which combines ADASYN and RENN techniques to balance minority and majority class sample sets. The algorithm begins by determining the ratio of minority to majority samples. If this ratio is below a specified threshold, the method calculates the number of synthetic samples needed based on the difference between the majority and minority class sizes. It then identifies neighbors for each minority sample, computes a relative importance score, and generates new synthetic samples accordingly. 

After generating the new minority samples, the algorithm proceeds to adjust the majority class by removing samples based on their neighborhood characteristics and a specified threshold of positive samples. This process is repeated until a balanced majority set is achieved, followed by noise removal using the DBSCAN algorithm.

In addition to the sampling method, the paper proposes a feature selection algorithm aimed at reducing data redundancy. This algorithm employs the Random Forest technique to assess the importance of features, ranking them based on their contribution to the model. It further analyzes feature correlations using Pearson's index before integrating these findings to select the most relevant features. The approach highlights the utility of Random Forest in identifying significant features by evaluating the error rates of out-of-bag samples with and without disturbances, ultimately aiming to enhance model performance by focusing on non-redundant features.
