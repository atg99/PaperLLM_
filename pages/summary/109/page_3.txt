The paper discusses advancements in network security intrusion detection, highlighting various models employed in this field, including Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs), and hybrid approaches. The authors note that traditional CNNs are effective in image-related tasks but have also been successfully adapted for intrusion detection. RNNs excel in processing time series data, making them suitable for this application as well.

To enhance detection accuracy, several studies are referenced. For instance, Tama et al. utilized a combination of particle swarm optimization, ant colony algorithms, and genetic algorithms for feature selection, followed by a secondary classification method. Bu et al. integrated CNNs with traditional classifiers to improve anomaly detection, while Le et al. employed the SFSDT model for feature selection before using RNNs for classification. Hassan et al. developed an intrusion detection system leveraging CNNs and long short-term memory networks for improved results.

In addressing class imbalance, Louk et al. found that the EasyEnsemble method outperformed other sampling techniques. Liu et al. used a combination of edited nearest neighbors and K-Means to balance datasets, while Yan et al. utilized an improved density peak clustering algorithm for accurate anomaly detection.

The paper includes a table summarizing recent works in network intrusion detection, detailing datasets, methods, evaluation metrics, and accuracy results, showcasing the effectiveness of various optimization, deep learning, and machine learning algorithms in achieving high detection rates.
