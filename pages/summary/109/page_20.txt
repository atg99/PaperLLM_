The paper presents a comparative analysis of different pooling methods and their impact on classification performance across various datasets, specifically focusing on accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score. The results indicate that the combination of Average pooling and Max pooling significantly enhances the model's ability to learn features, leading to improved classification outcomes across all datasets examined, including NSL-KDD, UNSW_NB15, and CIC-IDS2017.

Additionally, the paper evaluates the run-time performance of the proposed model against several scenarios, including different preprocessing methods and model architectures. The findings reveal that while the proposed model takes more time to train than simpler models like CNN and GRU, it achieves higher accuracy. This increased training time is attributed to the use of the ADRDB algorithm, which generates additional samples for training, while the RFP algorithm reduces the number of features and consequently the training time.

Overall, the study concludes that although the proposed model is more time-consuming, its enhanced accuracy justifies the additional computational effort.
