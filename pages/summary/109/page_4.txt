The paper discusses various advanced methods for intrusion detection systems (IDS) and their effectiveness in improving detection accuracy. 

Abdullah Alharbi et al. introduced a local-global optimal neural network Bat algorithm (LGBA-NN) which achieved a detection accuracy of up to 90% on the N-BaIoT dataset, outperforming traditional methods like particle swarm optimization. Jevgenijus Toldinas et al. transformed network data into four-channel images and classified them using the ResNet50 model, demonstrating high accuracy in detecting anomalies on the UNSW_NB15 and BOUN_Ddos datasets.

Muhammad Ashfaq Khan proposed a hybrid intrusion detection system (HRCNNIDS) that combines convolutional and recurrent neural networks, yielding a high detection rate for malicious attacks based on the CSE-CIC-IDS2018 dataset. Guo Pu et al. developed an unsupervised anomaly detection method using subspace clustering and support vector machines, achieving improved performance on the NSL-KDD dataset.

Gia Nhu Nguyen et al. incorporated blockchain technology into IDS, enhancing data security and detection efficiency through deep belief networks, with successful evaluations on NSL-KDD 2015 and CIDDS-001 datasets. Ranjit Panigrahi et al. addressed imbalanced datasets by proposing a host-based IDS algorithm that generates balanced samples and employs a multi-class feature selection mechanism, resulting in high detection accuracy.

Lastly, Mohammad Noor Injadat et al. examined the effects of sampling techniques on model performance and proposed a multi-stage optimization approach that includes SMOTE sampling and feature selection, leading to significant improvements in detection accuracy while reducing the training dataset size on the UNSW_NB15 and CIC-IDS2017 datasets.
