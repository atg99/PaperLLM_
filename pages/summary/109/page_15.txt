The paper presents an analysis of various metrics used to evaluate the performance of classification models, specifically in the context of intrusion detection. Key metrics discussed include accuracy, precision, recall, and F1 score, with formulas provided for each. Accuracy measures the ratio of correctly predicted samples to the total, precision focuses on the correctness of positive predictions, recall assesses the model's ability to identify actual positives, and the F1 score represents the harmonic mean of precision and recall, highlighting the importance of both metrics.

The authors conducted extensive experiments to validate the effectiveness of their proposed model, structured into several sections. The experiments include feature selection analysis, comparison of different feature selection methods, evaluation of single versus hybrid models, and assessments of various sampling and pooling methods. Additionally, runtime performance and convergence analysis of the CNN-GRU model were explored, along with a statistical test to evaluate the proposed intrusion detection method.

Focusing on the feature selection analysis, the authors utilized public datasets such as UNSW_NB15, NSL-KDD, and CIC-IDS2017, with a detailed examination of the UNSW_NB15 dataset. The significance of each feature was calculated using the Random Forest algorithm, revealing a wide variance in feature importance. For instance, the feature "sbytes" showed an importance of 0.115, while features like "is_ftp_login" and "ct_ftp_cmd" had an importance value of zero, indicating that certain features are more critical for model performance than others.
