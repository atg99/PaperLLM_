The paper presents a novel network intrusion detection model that integrates Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN) and Gated Recurrent Units (GRU) to enhance detection accuracy, particularly in scenarios involving multiple classifications and imbalanced data. The authors address the challenge of sample imbalance by employing a hybrid sampling algorithm that combines Adaptive Synthetic Sampling (ADASYN) and Repeated Edited Nearest Neighbors (RENN). To mitigate feature redundancy, they utilize a combination of the Random Forest algorithm and Pearson correlation analysis for feature selection.

The model extracts spatial features through CNN, enhancing them further using a fusion of Averagepooling and Maxpooling along with an attention mechanism to optimize feature weights, thereby improving performance and reducing computational overhead. The GRU component is utilized to capture long-distance dependencies in the data for comprehensive feature learning. Classification is performed using a softmax function.

Evaluation of the model on the UNSW_NB15, NSL-KDD, and CIC-IDS2017 datasets yielded classification accuracies of 86.25%, 99.69%, and 99.65%, respectively, which demonstrate improvements of 1.95%, 0.47%, and 0.12% over similar CNN-GRU models. The results indicate that the proposed model effectively addresses issues of low classification accuracy and class imbalance, positioning it as a robust option for network intrusion detection.
