The paper presents a series of experimental simulations to evaluate a network intrusion detection method that integrates Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN) and Gated Recurrent Units (GRU). The experiments are organized into six key categories: feature selection analysis, comparison of feature selection methods, evaluation of sampling methods, comparison between single and hybrid models, assessment of different pooling methods, and overall performance comparisons. 

The experimental setup utilized a 64-bit Windows system with an Intel Core i7-7700HQ CPU and an Nvidia GeForce GTX 1050 GPU, employing Python’s TensorFlow library for model implementation. For the evaluation of the proposed model, three datasets were selected: UNSW-NB15, NSL-KDD, and CIC-IDS2017. These datasets are recognized in the field of intrusion detection, with NSL-KDD being a refined version of the earlier KDD99 dataset, aimed at reducing redundancy. It contains 41 features and classifies data into five categories: Normal, Probe, Dos, R2L, and U2R.
