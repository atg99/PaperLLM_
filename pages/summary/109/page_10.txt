The paper discusses the Convolutional Block Attention Module (CBAM), a lightweight attention mechanism aimed at enhancing the representational power of models by assigning greater importance to significant features while downplaying less relevant ones. Proposed by Woo et al. in 2018, CBAM consists of two primary components: the channel attention module and the spatial attention module.

The channel attention module generates a channel attention map by analyzing the relationships among features across channels. This is accomplished through the aggregation of spatial information using Averagepooling and Maxpooling, resulting in two distinct spatial information sources. These sources are processed by a shared multi-layer perceptron, which produces the channel attention graph, thereby refining the feature vector through element-wise summation.

The spatial attention module, on the other hand, focuses on the spatial relationships between features to create a spatial attention map. It similarly employs Averagepooling and Maxpooling to generate two 2D feature maps, which are then fused using standard convolution to yield the final spatial attention output.

Overall, CBAM effectively enhances feature representation in neural networks by utilizing both channel and spatial attention mechanisms, facilitating improved performance in various applications.
