This paper discusses a feature selection algorithm aimed at enhancing the performance of machine learning models. The algorithm employs a process that includes calculating the importance of features, utilizing the Pearson correlation coefficient to assess the relationship between variables, and filtering out less important features. The Pearson correlation coefficient, which ranges from -1 to 1, indicates the strength of linear relationships between features, with values close to ±1 suggesting high correlation and values near zero indicating no linear relationship.

The proposed feature selection algorithm, referred to as RFP, consists of several steps: calculating errors from out-of-bag data, introducing random disturbances, determining feature importance, ranking features, and performing correlation analysis. The output is a processed dataset that has undergone balancing and feature selection, visualized as grayscale maps for various categories.

Additionally, the paper introduces the Convolutional Block Attention Module (CBAM), a lightweight attention mechanism designed to enhance the model's representation power by assigning greater weights to important features. CBAM comprises two components: the channel attention module and the spatial attention module. The channel attention module generates a channel attention map by analyzing feature relationships across channels, while the spatial attention module derives spatial attention maps based on spatial relationships among features. Overall, the integration of these attention mechanisms aims to improve the model's performance by focusing on significant features.
