This paper presents a performance analysis of various intrusion detection models, specifically focusing on a proposed CNN-GRU model. The comparison is based on run-time performance and accuracy across different datasets, including NSL-KDD, UNSW_NB15, and CIC-IDS2017. The proposed model outperforms others in terms of accuracy, achieving 99.69% on NSL-KDD, 86.25% on UNSW_NB15, and 99.65% on CIC-IDS2017, while also demonstrating competitive run-time performance.

The analysis includes a graphical representation of classification accuracy and loss over iteration steps, indicating good convergence for the CNN-GRU model. Furthermore, the paper compares this model against traditional machine learning methods such as random forest and decision trees, highlighting its ability to learn features through neural networks. This results in a more abstract representation of data, effectively enhancing intrusion detection accuracy by addressing feature redundancy and class imbalance while extracting both spatial and temporal features.

In conclusion, the proposed CNN-GRU model demonstrates superior performance in intrusion detection tasks, making it a valuable contribution to the field.
