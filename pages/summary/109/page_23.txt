The paper presents a comprehensive evaluation of various models for intrusion detection using three different datasets: NSL-KDD, UNSW_NB15, and CIC-IDS2017. The performance of these models is measured using accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score. The proposed model outperforms several existing models, achieving the highest accuracy of 99.69%, precision of 99.65%, recall of 99.69%, and F1-score of 99.70% on the NSL-KDD dataset. Similar trends are observed in the other datasets, with the proposed model consistently achieving superior results compared to traditional methods like Random Forest and K-means, as well as advanced models like CNN-GRU and CNN-BiLSTM.

Additionally, the paper employs a two-tailed t-test to assess the statistical significance of the proposed model's performance. Using the UNSW_NB15 dataset as a case study, the average accuracy and standard deviation of the proposed method are calculated, leading to a critical value that supports the robustness of the results. The findings suggest that the proposed intrusion detection method is statistically significant and not a result of chance, reinforcing its effectiveness in real-world applications.
