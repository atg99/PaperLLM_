The paper evaluates a proposed intrusion detection method using the CNN-GRU algorithm, focusing on the UNSW_NB15 dataset. It begins with feature selection analysis, employing the Random Forest algorithm to calculate and rank the importance of each feature. The results indicate significant variation in feature importance, with some features, like sbytes, showing a value of 0.115, while others, such as is_ftp_login and ct_ftp_cmd, have a value of zero. 

Recognizing that feature importance alone is insufficient, the study integrates Pearson correlation analysis to enhance feature selection. A correlation diagram illustrates the relationships among the 42 features, leading to the identification of pairs with correlation indices above 0.9 or below -0.9 for further analysis.

The feature correlation index table reveals strong correlations among several features, such as a correlation of 0.996 between sbytes and sloss. The paper outlines a systematic approach for retaining important features, including elimination criteria based on correlation strength and importance indices. Ultimately, the analysis results in 28-dimensional features remaining for both the NSL-KDD and UNSW_NB15 datasets and 52 features for the CIC-IDS2017 dataset. This thorough feature selection process aims to enhance the classification performance of the proposed algorithm in intrusion detection contexts.
