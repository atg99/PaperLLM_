The paper discusses advancements in intrusion detection systems, highlighting the use of various neural network architectures such as CNN, RNN, and deep belief networks. It reviews existing literature, noting the successful application of CNNs for image-based traffic classification, LSTM networks for parameter selection, and hierarchical systems that integrate spatial and temporal feature learning. However, it identifies persistent challenges in feature redundancy and sample imbalance that can affect detection accuracy and increase false alarm rates.

To tackle these issues, the authors propose a novel intrusion detection model that combines CNN and Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) networks. The model introduces a feature selection algorithm (RFP) leveraging random forest analysis and Pearson correlation to reduce feature redundancy. Additionally, it presents a hybrid sampling algorithm (ADRDB) that combines ADASYN and RENN to address sample imbalance, complemented by DBSCAN for noise rejection. The model’s architecture employs CNN for extracting spatial features while GRU captures temporal dependencies, augmented by an attention mechanism to prioritize significant features. These innovations aim to enhance detection accuracy and reduce false alarms in intrusion detection systems.
