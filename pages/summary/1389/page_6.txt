The paper presents a comprehensive evaluation of ShelfNet, a semantic segmentation model, particularly in comparison to various state-of-the-art models. The authors focus on the inference speed and accuracy of ShelfNet with different backbone networks including ResNet18, ResNet50, and ResNet101, highlighting the impact of computational efficiency through the use of a shelf-like structure in feature extraction.

In the results section, the authors first analyze performance on the PASCAL VOC 2012 dataset. They report that ShelfNet models achieve mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) scores of 74.0%, 79.0%, and 81.1% for ResNet18, ResNet50, and ResNet101, respectively, when trained solely on the augmented PASCAL training set. When trained on both MS COCO and PASCAL datasets, the mIoU scores improve to 79.3%, 82.8%, and 84.2%. Comparatively, ShelfNet demonstrates a significant speed advantage, achieving 59 FPS for ShelfNet50 and 42 FPS for ShelfNet101, compared to slower models like PSPNet and DeepLabv2, which achieve only 11 FPS and 12 FPS respectively, despite similar mIoU scores.

The paper also compares ShelfNet with real-time models such as Lightweight-RefineNet (LWRF). It finds that ShelfNet50 achieves a higher accuracy of 82.8% compared to LWRF's 82.7% with a ResNet152 backbone, demonstrating the effectiveness of ShelfNet's architecture. The authors attribute the efficiency of ShelfNet to a reduced channel number achieved through 1x1 convolutions, leading to a significant reduction in computational load.

Additionally, the authors showcase results on the PASCAL-Context dataset, where ShelfNet achieves competitive mIoU scores of 45.6% and 48.4% with ResNet50 and ResNet101 backbones, respectively, while maintaining faster inference speeds (59 FPS and 42 FPS) compared to other models.

Overall, the findings indicate that ShelfNet not only matches the accuracy of other leading segmentation models but also enhances inference speed, confirming the advantages of its architectural design and computational efficiency.
