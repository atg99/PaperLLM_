This paper presents a series of experiments evaluating various deep learning models for semantic segmentation on the PASCAL VOC dataset, focusing on the performance metrics of mean Intersection over Union (mIoU), frames per second (FPS), and the number of parameters. The analysis includes models such as FCN, DeepLabv2, CRF-RNN, and the proposed ShelfNet, which is tested with different ResNet backbones (ResNet18, ResNet50, and ResNet101).

Key findings indicate that ShelfNet models achieve competitive mIoU scores while maintaining a high inference speed, significantly faster than other models like EncNet, which, despite achieving a higher mIoU, sacrifices speed. For instance, ShelfNet50 and ShelfNet101 yield mIoU scores of 82.8% and 84.2% respectively, with FPS values of 59 and 42, demonstrating a balance between accuracy and efficiency.

Additionally, results from the PASCAL-Context dataset and Cityscapes show similar trends, with ShelfNet models demonstrating strong performance in both mIoU and FPS. The paper emphasizes the efficacy of ShelfNet in achieving high segmentation accuracy with reduced computational overhead, making it suitable for real-time applications. Overall, the results suggest that ShelfNet is a promising approach in the domain of semantic segmentation, achieving high accuracy and fast inference times, which is critical for practical deployment.
