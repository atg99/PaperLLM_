The paper discusses a method for converting closing prices into structured data, specifically transforming daily closing prices into 50x50 pixel images. This transformation allows for more efficient processing using a Deep Belief Network (DBN) model, which is particularly effective for analyzing financial time series data.

Data splitting was conducted into training, validation, and testing sets, comprising 5000, 3000, and 2000 samples respectively. The DBN model was trained on the training set and subsequently validated and tested to ensure accuracy and reliability.

The trained DBN model was applied to financial market data from 2012 to 2014 using a sliding window approach, which allowed for continuous validation of the model's performance over time. This approach not only enabled the analysis of complex financial data but also facilitated the identification of critical patterns and trends for financial decision-making.

The training of the DBN involved a three-layer architecture, with the input layer containing 50x50 nodes and two hidden layers of 3000 nodes each. The output layer had three nodes representing upward, downward, or unclear trends. The training process included layer-wise training, GPU acceleration for efficiency, fine-tuning with validation and testing data, and the addition of a classifier to enhance precision.

Overall, the study illustrates the effectiveness of the DBN model in converting unstructured financial data into structured formats for improved predictive accuracy in financial markets. The detailed training process and the model's architecture are essential for understanding its capabilities in handling complex datasets.
