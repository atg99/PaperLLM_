The paper discusses the architecture of the SpMM engine within the AWB-GCN framework, focusing on three rebalancing techniques: distribution smoothing, remote switching, and evil row remapping. The architecture consists of groups of processing elements (PEs), specifically Super-PEs and Labor-PEs, which can be customized in number.

Evil row remapping is introduced to tackle the issue of evil-row clustering by redistributing workloads from overloaded PEs to under-loaded ones. This process is initiated based on demand at the end of each computation round, using an autotuner that assesses utilization gaps between PEs. When the gaps exceed a certain threshold, row remapping occurs, allowing for a more efficient distribution of workloads, especially for rows with a high number of non-zero entries.

The Super-PE plays a critical role as it identifies evil rows and orchestrates the remapping process. It utilizes additional hardware components, such as a parallel sorting circuit and a non-zero counter, to enhance its profiling capabilities. The results of remapped workloads are aggregated in an ACC buffer, ensuring that even if Labor-PEs are initially overloaded, they do not become the new bottlenecks post-remapping.

The evaluation of AWB-GCNs is conducted using an Intel D5005 acceleration card with a Stratix 10 SX FPGA, measuring aspects such as PE utilization, performance, energy efficiency, and hardware resource consumption. The implementation is done in Verilog HDL, and the results demonstrate the architecture's capacity to optimize workload distribution and improve system utilization, especially during the initial computation rounds.

Overall, the paper highlights the effectiveness of the proposed evil row remapping technique in enhancing the operational efficiency of SpMM engines within AWB-GCNs, ultimately contributing to better performance in graph convolutional networks.
