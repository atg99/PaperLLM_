The paper discusses the execution order and architecture design for sparse matrix-matrix multiplication (SpMM) in various datasets, including Cora, Citeseer, Pubmed, Nell, and Reddit. It highlights significant differences in operations required for different execution orders, demonstrating that performing X*W before multiplying with A is more efficient.

The SpMM execution order is based on a column-wise product approach, allowing for efficient computation by reformulating matrix operations to minimize memory access. The design incorporates inter-layer data forwarding and matrix blocking techniques to enhance performance, particularly when matrices are stored in Compressed Sparse Column (CSC) format. This method enables pipelining of multiple SpMM operations and optimizes workload distribution.

The baseline architecture for SpMM includes several components: sparse-matrix-memory (SpMMeM), dense-column-memory (DCM), task distributor and queue (TDQ), processing element array (PE-array), and accumulation buffers. The SpMMeM buffers the input sparse matrix, while the DCM handles the dense matrix. The TDQ distributes tasks to the PEs for concurrent multiplication of non-zero pairs and accumulation of results.

Depending on the sparsity of the matrix, two designs for TDQ are proposed: TDQ-1 for generally sparse matrices and TDQ-2 for denser formats. The architecture ensures that non-zero elements are processed efficiently, even under imbalanced distributions among PEs, by employing multiple task queues for concurrency.

Overall, the design aims to achieve high performance in SpMM calculations by optimizing data handling and parallel processing while addressing challenges related to sparsity and distribution of non-zero elements.
