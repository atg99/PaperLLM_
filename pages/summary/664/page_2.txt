The paper proposes AWB-GCN, a hardware accelerator designed to enhance the efficiency of Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) and Sparse Matrix Multiplication (SpMM) kernels, particularly for matrices characterized by a power-law distribution. The motivation stems from the challenges posed by real-world graphs, which are typically large and exhibit significant workload imbalances, making traditional processing methods inadequate.

Key contributions of the paper include the development of a novel architecture for GCN acceleration, an online workload profiling system, and three specific workload rebalancing techniques: distribution smoothing, remote switching, and evil row remapping. These techniques aim to balance the workload across processing elements (PEs) by addressing the clustering of non-zero elements in the adjacency matrices of graphs.

The paper illustrates the disparity in matrix density between structured graphs like NELL, which has a low density of 0.0073% and is heavily clustered, and unstructured matrices like those used in AlexNet, which have a much higher density of 27%. AWB-GCN effectively mitigates the issues arising from this clustering by redistributing workloads, leading to improved utilization of hardware resources.

Results from evaluations using an Intel D5005 FPGA Acceleration Card demonstrate that AWB-GCN achieves a significant improvement in PE utilization, averaging a 7.7 times increase compared to setups without workload rebalancing. In performance comparisons with CPUs and GPUs, AWB-GCN shows speedups of 3255 times and 80.3 times, respectively, highlighting its effectiveness in accelerating GCN workloads. 

The paper also briefly outlines the GCN algorithm's layer-wise forward propagation process, emphasizing the need for efficient hardware solutions to handle the unique characteristics of power-law graphs.
