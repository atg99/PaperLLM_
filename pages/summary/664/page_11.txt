The paper discusses the performance evaluation of the Adaptive Workload Balancing Graph Convolutional Networks (AWB-GCN) architecture, particularly focusing on the utilization of processing elements (PEs) during workload autotuning. Key findings indicate that AWB-GCN achieves high PE utilization rates across various datasets, with significant performance improvements noted when enabling remote switching. The architecture allocates hardware resources based on the operational demands of different layers, leading to consistent execution delays when optimal utilization is reached.

Figures presented illustrate the comparative performance of AWB-GCN against baseline designs, highlighting the reduction in synchronization overheads across various sparse matrix multiplication (SpMM) kernels. The results show that Design(D) effectively minimizes these overheads, particularly benefiting from workload balancing techniques such as distribution smoothing and remote switching.

Additionally, the paper provides a breakdown of hardware resource usage, comparing AWB-GCN's designs against traditional implementations. The findings reveal that the overheads associated with distribution smoothing and remote switching are minimal, suggesting that the benefits of improved performance and efficiency outweigh the costs. Overall, AWB-GCN demonstrates robust scalability and efficiency, making it a promising solution for graph-based computations.
