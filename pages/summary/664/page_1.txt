The paper presents AWB-GCN, a hardware accelerator designed to improve the performance of Graph Convolutional Network (GCN) inference, particularly in handling the challenges posed by large and unbalanced real-world graphs. GCNs are increasingly relevant for processing non-Euclidean data represented as graphs, which traditional neural networks struggle to analyze due to their irregular structure.

The authors introduce three hardware-based autotuning techniques to address workload imbalance: dynamic distribution smoothing, remote switching, and row remapping. AWB-GCN continuously monitors the sparse graph pattern and adjusts workload distribution among up to 4,000 processing elements (PEs) to optimize performance. Upon convergence, the ideal configuration is reused for efficiency.

Evaluation on an Intel D5005 FPGA using five common datasets shows that AWB-GCN significantly enhances PE utilization by an average of 7.7 times and achieves remarkable performance improvements compared to CPUs (3255 times), GPUs (80.3 times), and a previous GCN accelerator (5.1 times). The paper emphasizes the need for dedicated GCN hardware accelerators due to specific challenges such as unbalanced non-zero data distributions and extreme sparsity in graph adjacency matrices, which differ from the characteristics of accelerators designed for other deep learning domains like CNNs.
