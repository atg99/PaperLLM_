The paper presents AWB-GCN, a hardware-based framework designed to accelerate Graph Convolutional Network (GCN) inference by addressing workload imbalance issues. Unlike the global element-wise controller employed in SIGMA, AWB-GCN utilizes a lightweight, auto-tuning-based rebalancing mechanism to dynamically allocate tasks among processing elements (PEs). This mechanism includes three key techniques: distribution smoothing, remote switching, and row remapping, which allow for effective workload rebalancing while minimizing area and delay overhead.

AWB-GCN is particularly optimized for sparse matrix multiplications (SpMMs) with power-law matrices, and while it is tailored for GCNs, it is also applicable to other Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) that primarily rely on SpMMs. The evaluation of AWB-GCN on an Intel FPGA D5005 Accelerator Card using five widely used GCN datasets demonstrates significant performance improvements, achieving average speedups of 3255 times compared to high-end CPUs, 80.3 times against GPUs, and 5.1 times over previous works.

The research highlights the limitations of existing graph processing frameworks, which generally do not support complex floating-point operations or require substantial modifications to accommodate GCNs. Additionally, software optimizations for SpMM on GPUs and CPUs are inadequate due to their overhead in pre-scanning, which AWB-GCN effectively avoids. The findings underscore the potential for hardware autotuning to enhance the efficiency of GCN inference and similar applications.
