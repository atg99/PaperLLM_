The paper discusses optimizations for sparse matrix-matrix multiplication (SpMM) to enhance the performance of the AWB-GCN accelerator. Key strategies include matching data production and consumption rates across consecutive SpMMs by allocating processing elements (PEs) according to workload sizes. Matrix blocking is introduced to improve data locality and reduce off-chip bandwidth requirements, allowing for efficient usage of burst mode access in memory.

The optimization strategies employed include reusing matrix A across layers, utilizing matrix blocking to enhance data reuse, and implementing scratchpad memory to cache parts of matrix A on-chip. Experimental results indicate that these optimizations allow the AWB-GCN accelerator to operate with a maximum off-chip bandwidth demand of 459 Gbps, which is manageable by current hardware platforms.

The paper also addresses the workload balance problem, noting that performance degrades when non-zero elements are not evenly distributed among matrix rows. This imbalance leads to inefficient utilization of PEs, as demonstrated through utilization waves for various datasets. Instead of relying solely on time-consuming preprocessing methods, the AWB-GCN architecture dynamically adjusts hardware configurations for workload rebalancing at three levels: distribution smoothing, remote switching, and row remapping.

The autotuning process aims to equalize execution times across PEs by redistributing workloads, ultimately achieving balanced utilization and improved performance. The paper emphasizes the importance of these optimizations and dynamic adjustments in enhancing the efficiency of processing sparse data structures in GCNs.
