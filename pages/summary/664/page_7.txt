The paper discusses a method for optimizing the execution time and workload balance among parallel processing elements (PEs) in matrix computations. The approach consists of several phases, including distribution smoothing, remote switching, and row remapping, aimed at enhancing PE utilization and reducing execution time.

In the first round, distribution smoothing is implemented to average workloads among neighboring PEs. The architecture monitors PE utilization by tracking pending tasks and redistributes workloads from busier PEs to their less active neighbors. This process involves returning the results to the original PE for aggregation.

The second phase involves remapping "evil rows"—rows that contribute to workload imbalances—by switching their assignments from PEs experiencing high loads (troughs) to those with lighter loads (minor crests). This remapping is refined in subsequent rounds of autotuning, leading to a more balanced workload distribution.

The design details illustrate how task forwarding occurs between PEs to achieve distribution smoothing. The architecture allows for adjustments among direct neighbors, with a potential for extending to more distant neighbors, albeit with increased hardware complexity.

The overall goal is to converge to an optimal balance in PE workloads, allowing for efficient computation with minimized execution time. The autotuning process dynamically adapts to workload variations in real-time, ensuring sustained high utilization of PEs throughout the computation phases.
