The paper discusses the framework of an actor-critic method within the context of reinforcement learning, focusing on optimizing a Markov policy π. The return of a sequence is defined, and the goal is to identify the optimal policy π* that maximizes the expected return. The states considered are time-aware, ensuring the policies remain stationary over time.

The actor-critic approach involves two loss functions: Lh for the policy and Lg for the critic. Two classes of parameters, ω and θ, are introduced, alongside an additional controlled Markov process z, which can influence the policy's greediness and facilitate trust region methods.

The first loss function Lh(θ,ω,z) aims to optimize the policy by minimizing it with respect to θ. This function can be defined based on either the expectation of a loss evaluated over trajectories generated by the policy or a behavioral policy. The gradient of this loss is denoted as h(θ,ω,z).

The second loss function Lg(θ,ω,z) is minimized concerning ω to find the optimal critic function g(τ;ω,z). The functions g and Φ are required to be smooth to satisfy certain conditions. The gradient for this loss is denoted by f.

As exact gradients cannot be computed analytically, the paper proposes using stochastic approximations derived from sampled trajectories to estimate these gradients. The losses are minimized through online stochastic gradient descent (SGD), with specific learning rates defined for each parameter.

The paper provides formal expressions for the losses and gradients for both the policy and the critic, detailing the mathematical foundations of the proposed method. Overall, the framework aims to enhance the performance of reinforcement learning algorithms through structured optimization of policies and critics.
