The paper outlines several key assumptions regarding transition kernels and associated ordinary differential equations (ODEs) in the context of controlled Markov processes. 

Assumption A5 describes the continuity of the state-action map, which relates the state-action pairs to the transition probabilities. This continuity is essential for establishing the behavior of the system under study. 

Assumption A6 delves into the properties of occupation measures, which represent the probability of observing specific state-action pairs. It asserts the existence of a unique ergodic occupation measure denoted as Γθ,ω, which simplifies the analysis by allowing the use of ODEs instead of differential inclusions. The paper further outlines the existence of a unique asymptotically stable equilibrium for the ODE governing ω(t), characterized by a Lipschitz map. It also discusses the continuous differentiability of the associated Lyapunov function and the definition of a global attractor set for the ODE governing θ(t). An important probability assumption is that, eventually, the process ωn enters a compact subset of the attractor set for almost all initial conditions.

Assumption A7 ensures that the iterates of the system are bounded, which is crucial for the convergence and stability of the processes under consideration.

The section A.5 elaborates on the sampling process, introducing the function Sπ, which allows for sampling from a multinomial distribution based on the probabilities derived from the controlled process. The relationship to inverse transform sampling is highlighted, providing a foundational understanding of how samples are drawn from the defined distributions.

In A.6, further technical details are provided to support the assumptions necessary for proving the main result, Theorem 1. The analysis begins with a discussion on controlled Markov processes, particularly focusing on a sequence defined recursively, which converges to a specific limit, illustrating the underlying Markovian structure.

Overall, the paper presents a rigorous framework for understanding the dynamics of controlled Markov processes through well-defined assumptions, focusing on stability and sampling mechanisms.
