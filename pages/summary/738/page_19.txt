The paper presents a lemma regarding the relationship between Q-values and the optimal policy in reinforcement learning. It defines the optimal action as the one that maximizes the Q-value, denoted as Forimax, and establishes that the value function vπ∗(s) is also maximized at this action. The lemma introduces parameters epsilon and delta, which bound the differences between the Q-values and the function ψ(s, ai) that defines the policy.

The policy π(ai|s; β) is expressed in terms of ψ(s, ai) and a temperature parameter β, which influences the degree of exploration versus exploitation in the policy. As β approaches infinity, the policy converges to the optimal deterministic policy π∗(ai|s). The paper asserts that for a sufficiently large β, the Q-value-based methods and policy gradients will converge to the optimal policy, provided that the optimal policy represents a local minimum of the loss functions.

The proof begins by assuming the optimal policy is unique and deterministic. It derives inequalities that show the probability of selecting the optimal action relative to non-optimal actions, demonstrating that the probability of choosing the optimal action approaches 1 as β increases. This leads to the conclusion that, over time, the cumulative probability of selecting the optimal action remains high, reinforcing the convergence of the learning process towards the optimal policy.
