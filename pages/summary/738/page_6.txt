This section discusses the stochastic approximation theory, referencing works by Borkar and Karmakar, as well as Bhatnagar. Stochastic approximation algorithms are designed to find stationary points of functions under noisy observations, utilizing a two time-scale approach with coupled iterations that progress at different speeds. The convergence of these interwoven iterates relies on a slower iterate that allows the faster iterate to converge, despite being perturbed by it.

Several assumptions are outlined to ensure convergence:
- A1 specifies the controlled Markov processes and their dynamics.
- A2 addresses the continuity and Lipschitz conditions of the update functions.
- A3 ensures the martingale sequences have bounded second moments.
- A4 discusses the behavior of learning rates.
- A5 requires continuity of transition kernels.
- A6 involves occupation measures and their properties related to ODEs, emphasizing the uniqueness of ergodic measures and the stability of associated equilibria.
- A7 states that the iterates are uniformly bounded almost surely.
