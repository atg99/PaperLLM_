The paper discusses the derivation of the expected return under a given policy in a reinforcement learning context. Specifically, it presents the mathematical formulation for the expected return Eτ∼π[Rt+1], where τ denotes a trajectory of states and actions. The expressions involve summations over states and actions, as well as the transition probabilities and policy distributions.

The Policy Gradient Theorem is introduced, which assumes that the policy π is parameterized by θ. The gradient of the expected return with respect to the policy parameters is derived, leading to the conclusion that the gradient can be expressed in terms of the log probability of the actions taken under the policy and the corresponding returns. The key equation shows that the gradient of the expected return can be computed as the expectation of the product of the gradient of the log policy and the return.

The paper emphasizes that when considering the reward-to-go, the gradient of the expected reward at the initial step does not depend on the policy, allowing simplifications in the overall expression for the gradient. The final result highlights the importance of these gradients in optimizing the policy based on the received rewards.
