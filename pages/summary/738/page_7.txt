The paper discusses the convergence of iterates defined by equations (13) and (14) as stated in Theorem 2 from Karmakar and Bhatnagar. Under specific assumptions (A1) to (A7), it is established that the iterates converge almost surely to a set of optimal solutions. The authors further explain how Theorem 2 supports Theorem 1 by validating the assumptions in the context of Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) and RUDDER.

The assumptions include the existence of a controlled Markov process, which is simplified in the applications to PPO and RUDDER. The process is characterized by sequences that adhere to the Markov property, with specific definitions for components (z1)n and (z2)n, where (z1)n is used as a slope parameter for the softmax policy and (z2)n serves to weight an additional term in the objective function, ultimately converging to zero.

The paper also outlines the sampling process using a defined probability space and introduces a set of trajectories based on the policy π. It details the construction of a probability measure for these trajectories and describes how to sample from a multinomial distribution. The authors introduce functions that approximate certain expectations using sample trajectories, leading to the definition of martingale errors that quantify deviations from expected values.

In conclusion, the paper emphasizes the importance of these components and processes in ensuring the convergence of the policies over time, allowing them to become increasingly greedy. Further details and clarifications are provided in the appendices.
