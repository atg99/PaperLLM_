The paper presents an iterative algorithm optimizing losses via online stochastic gradient descent (SGD), represented by the update equations for parameters θ and ω. The main theorem establishes local convergence of this algorithm, provided specific starting points and neighborhood conditions are met, alongside certain loss assumptions. 

The theorem asserts that, under these conditions, the iterative process will converge almost surely to a local minimum of the associated loss functions as the number of iterations approaches infinity. The convergence proof is specifically applied to the Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) algorithm, which incorporates deep neural networks and various regularization techniques, ensuring smoothness and boundedness of the loss functions.

The PPO algorithm aims to minimize specific loss functions, such as Lh, LTDg, and LMCg, which relate to policy evaluation and improvement. Key components include the approximation of Q-values, temporal difference errors, and the use of controlled Markov processes to define trust region terms. The paper concludes with a corollary confirming the convergence of both Temporal Difference and Monte Carlo versions of the PPO algorithm.
