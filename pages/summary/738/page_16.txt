The paper discusses the relationship between policy gradients and Q-values in the context of reinforcement learning. It begins by expressing the expected return under a policy using Q-values, leading to a formulation that connects the gradient of the expected return with the Q-values of state-action pairs. Specifically, the paper derives the relationship:

∇θEπ[T∑t=0Rt+1] = Eτ∼π[T∑k=1∇θlogπ(ak|sk;θ)qπ(sk,ak)],

indicating how the policy gradient can be computed through the expected Q-values.

The paper then outlines specific assumptions necessary for the theoretical framework, drawing from previous work by Karmakar and Bhatnagar. These assumptions include:

1. Assumptions on the controlled Markov processes, which stipulate conditions for the state space and transition dynamics.
2. Assumptions on the update functions, ensuring continuity and Lipschitz conditions for the functions involved in the learning process.
3. Assumptions on the additive noise, which require that the noise terms are martingale differences with bounded moments.
4. Assumptions on the learning rates, which dictate the behavior of the learning sequences and their convergence properties.

These assumptions provide a foundation for the theoretical results presented in the paper, ensuring the robustness of the derived relationships and the applicability of the proposed methods in reinforcement learning scenarios.
