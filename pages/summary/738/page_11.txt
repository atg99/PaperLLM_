The references provided encompass significant contributions to the fields of reinforcement learning and stochastic optimization. 

Metrikopoulos et al. (2020) explore the almost sure convergence of stochastic gradient descent in non-convex problems, highlighting the theoretical framework necessary for understanding convergence in complex landscapes. 

Mnih et al. (2013) and (2015) present foundational work in deep reinforcement learning, demonstrating the application of deep learning techniques to play Atari games and achieve human-level control, respectively. These studies illustrate the potential of combining deep learning with reinforcement learning to tackle complex tasks.

Munro (1987) introduces a dual back-propagation scheme for reinforcement learning, while Robinson (1989) discusses dynamic error propagation networks, both contributing to the development of learning algorithms that adapt based on feedback.

The work by Schulman et al. (2015, 2018) on trust region policy optimization and proximal policy optimization algorithms presents advancements in policy gradient methods, focusing on improving stability and performance in reinforcement learning.

Sutton and Barto (2018) provide a comprehensive introduction to reinforcement learning, covering fundamental concepts and methodologies. Their earlier work with McAllester et al. (2000) on policy gradient methods further emphasizes the importance of function approximation in reinforcement learning strategies.

Tsitsiklis (1994) examines asynchronous stochastic approximation and Q-learning, contributing to the understanding of how learning can be performed in parallel across multiple agents or environments.

Vinyals et al. (2019) highlight the application of multi-agent reinforcement learning to achieve grandmaster-level performance in StarCraft II, showcasing the capabilities of reinforcement learning in competitive environments.

Overall, these references collectively underscore the evolution and sophistication of reinforcement learning techniques, emphasizing both theoretical and practical advancements in the domain.
