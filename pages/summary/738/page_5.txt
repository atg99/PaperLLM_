The paper presents two corollaries regarding the convergence of the Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) algorithm and the RUDDER algorithm under specific assumptions related to their respective loss functions and learning rates.

**Corollary 1 (Convergence PPO)** establishes that, given a starting point and the appropriate neighborhood, the PPO algorithm converges to a local minimum of its associated losses with probability one as the iteration number approaches infinity. This result relies on the fulfillment of the assumptions outlined in Theorem 1.

**Corollary 2 (Convergence RUDDER)** similarly asserts that the RUDDER algorithm, which is effective for tasks characterized by sparse and delayed rewards, also converges to a local minimum of its associated losses. The proof follows the same rationale as that of the PPO algorithm, confirming that all necessary assumptions are satisfied.

The paper emphasizes the requirements for smoothness in functions and boundedness of losses, as well as the specific forms of the losses involved in both algorithms. The results are supported by a theoretical framework drawn from previous works in the field, which underpins the convergence proofs for both algorithms. The authors detail the components of the algorithms, including the roles of approximated Q-values and regularization terms, while ensuring that the formulation remains consistent with the assumptions necessary for convergence.

Overall, the findings reinforce the reliability of both the PPO and RUDDER algorithms in achieving convergence under the specified conditions, thereby contributing to the understanding of optimization processes in reinforcement learning contexts.
