This paper discusses the concept of gradient estimation with reward-to-go in the context of policy-based reinforcement learning. It introduces the notation for the probability of state-action sequences under a given policy, denoted as p(τ). The state-action sequence is defined as τ = (s0, a0, s1, a1, ..., sT, aT), where s represents states and a represents actions.

The paper breaks down the probability of a state-action sequence into components based on the initial state, subsequent state transitions, and action probabilities given the states. Specifically, it presents formulas for p(τ), p(τ0,t), and p(τt+1,T|st,at), highlighting the dependence of each on the preceding states and actions.

The expected reward, denoted Eπ[Rt+1], is analyzed, emphasizing that it can be expressed as the sum of expected rewards over time, with each reward dependent on the current state-action pair (st, at). The expected reward function r(st, at) is defined as the expectation of the reward given the state-action pair, relying on the distribution p(rt+1|st, at).

The paper asserts a causal relationship where the reward Rt+1 is influenced solely by past state-action pairs and not by future actions, aligning with the Markov property of Markov Decision Processes (MDPs). This leads to the conclusion that the expected reward given the policy can be derived from the state-action pairs observed up to that point in time.
