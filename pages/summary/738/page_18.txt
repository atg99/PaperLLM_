This paper discusses the behavior of discrete time Markov processes, focusing on their invariant measures and the implications for integration with respect to the Dirac measure. The authors establish that the unique invariant measure is the Dirac measure at β, leading to the conclusion that integration corresponds to evaluating limits as sequences approach β.

The paper outlines several assumptions, including Lipschitz continuity for functions f and h, which ensures that their derivatives with respect to parameters θ and ω are uniformly bounded. The martingale difference property is established for sequences (m1)n+1 and (m2)n+1, demonstrating their expected values conditioned on the filtration are zero, with bounded second moments as derived from earlier assumptions.

The continuity of transition kernels is confirmed through weak topology considerations, and stability properties of the associated ordinary differential equations (ODEs) are analyzed. The authors argue that the ODE governing ω(t) behaves as a gradient system, leading to unique asymptotically stable equilibria under the given assumptions. They also note that the associated Lyapunov function is differentiable, further supporting the stability of the system.

Finally, the paper addresses the convergence of the optimal policy using a finite parameter β greater than one. It demonstrates that the Q-values for policy π can be expressed in terms of expected rewards, establishing the uniqueness and deterministic nature of the optimal policy π*. The findings suggest that finite greediness is sufficient for convergence to the optimal policy, thus providing a pathway for achieving optimality in the analyzed Markov decision processes.
