This paper presents a convergence proof for actor-critic reinforcement learning algorithms, specifically for Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) and RUDDER. The authors demonstrate that both the actor (policy function) and the critic (value function) can be represented as deep neural networks of arbitrary complexity while ensuring convergence under certain commonly accepted assumptions. 

The convergence proof utilizes techniques from two time-scale stochastic approximation theory, addressing limitations of previous proofs which either relied on linear function approximation, could not handle episodic samples, or did not account for policies that become greedy during learning—a critical aspect since optimal policies are typically deterministic. 

The introduction highlights the absence of convergence proofs for popular methods like PPO, noting that challenges arise due to the complexity of deep neural networks, the nature of episodic samples, and the use of trust region methods with previous policies. The paper contrasts the convergence of actor-critic methods with established results for Q-learning and temporal difference methods, emphasizing the advancements and successes of actor-critic algorithms in complex environments like Dota 2 and Starcraft II.

Overall, this work fills a significant gap in the understanding of convergence in actor-critic methods, providing a solid theoretical foundation for their application in reinforcement learning.
