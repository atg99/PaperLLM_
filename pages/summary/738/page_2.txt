This paper presents a convergence proof for general actor-critic reinforcement learning algorithms, expanding on previous work that primarily focused on simpler settings and linear neural networks. The authors specifically apply this proof to two established actor-critic methods: Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) and RUDDER. PPO is an on-policy gradient method that minimizes the policy gap through trust region penalties, while RUDDER addresses sparse and delayed rewards by redistributing rewards to relevant state-action pairs, significantly enhancing learning efficiency.

Key contributions of the paper include the introduction of a two time-scale stochastic approximation framework enriched by controlled Markov processes, allowing for the treatment of policies that become more greedy and the use of previous policies. This framework supports convergence proofs for deep neural networks, which were previously challenging due to the complexity of non-linear approximations.

The authors outline their main contributions: utilizing Markov control within the two time-scale framework, employing episodes as samples, permitting greedy policies, and incorporating trust region methods. They provide a main theorem demonstrating local convergence of actor-critic methods, followed by specific results for PPO and RUDDER as corollaries. The paper concludes with a roadmap for the proofs and discussions on the technical assumptions necessary for their findings.
