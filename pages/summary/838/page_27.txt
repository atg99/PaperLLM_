The paper discusses strategies for optimizing deep neural networks, particularly in the context of reducing power consumption and memory footprint during classification tasks. It highlights a method where a larger neural network is employed only when the confidence of the initial classification is low, leading to faster inference times for the majority of inputs. Although this approach improves response times, it does not address memory usage.

To further reduce memory requirements while maintaining accuracy, techniques like structured pruning are suggested, which involves completely removing entire filters from convolutional neural networks. The paper also mentions other optimization methods, including the MCUNet framework and its TinyNAS tool, aimed at identifying efficient neural network models.

Future work will focus on deploying these architectures on FPGA using High-Level Synthesis tools, with preliminary studies indicating feasibility. Additionally, there is ongoing development to support automatic deployment with PyTorch, utilizing features from the torch.fx module.

The authors are applying their framework to real-world scenarios, specifically in smart glasses for human activity recognition in elder care. The research is funded by various institutions, with no conflicts of interest declared.

An appendix provides a comparison of inference times and power consumption across different platforms: a microcontroller (STM32L452RE), a CPU (Intel Core i7-8850H), and a GPU (Nvidia Quadro P2000M), illustrating significant differences in power consumption between these devices.
