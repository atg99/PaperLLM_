The paper discusses the performance of fixed-point quantization in machine learning models, particularly focusing on the implications of using 8-bit and 16-bit integers for inference in embedded systems. It highlights the high variance in accuracy results across three datasets, making it challenging to draw definitive conclusions. However, a consistent trend emerges indicating that both 8-bit and 16-bit quantization significantly reduce inference time and power consumption, which is crucial in embedded applications.

The study finds that 16-bit quantization maintains accuracy without requiring quantization-aware training, making it preferable to 32-bit floating-point inference. In contrast, 8-bit quantization does not offer substantial benefits over 16-bit and necessitates quantization-aware training, which can introduce variability in results. The paper suggests using conservative optimization methods to stabilize training outcomes for 8-bit quantization.

The authors observe that TensorFlow Lite's 8-bit Post-Training Quantization outperforms their 8-bit Quantization-Aware Training, likely due to TensorFlow Lite's advanced quantization techniques. Additionally, implementing a 9-bit quantization scheme can yield improved results, emphasizing the importance of precision in quantization methods.

Finally, the paper notes the potential for using SIMD instructions with 8-bit quantization to enhance inference speed and reduce power consumption, although these are not yet integrated into their framework. The findings advocate for the adoption of 16-bit quantization due to its balance of performance and resource efficiency.
