The paper discusses the performance and energy efficiency of various inference frameworks on different hardware boards, specifically focusing on the SparkFun Edge and the Nucleo-L452RE-P. The results indicate that 32-bit floating-point inference is consistently slower than 16-bit and 8-bit quantization across all tools and targets. The proposed framework demonstrates inference times of 1561 ms and 1512 ms on the SparkFun Edge and Nucleo-L452RE-P boards, respectively, which are comparable to the STM32Cube.AI's 1387 ms on the Nucleo-L452RE-P. 

In terms of energy consumption, the SparkFun Edge board shows superior power efficiency, consuming about six times less power than the Nucleo-L452RE-P. Specifically, it requires 0.45 µWh for 8-bit integer inference using TensorFlow Lite for Microcontrollers, while the proposed framework consumes 0.75 µWh for 8-bit and 0.78 µWh for 16-bit integer inference. For 32-bit floating-point inference, the proposed framework is more energy efficient than TensorFlow Lite, needing 1.17 µWh compared to 1.57 µWh. 

On the Nucleo-L452RE-P, the proposed framework consumes 4.58 µWh for 8-bit, 5.42 µWh for 16-bit, and 6.70 µWh for 32-bit floating-point inference. In contrast, STM32Cube.AI requires 6.15 µWh for 32-bit floating-point and 1.56 µWh for 8-bit integer inference. Overall, the findings highlight the trade-offs between inference speed and energy efficiency across different quantization levels and frameworks.
