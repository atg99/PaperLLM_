The paper discusses the trade-offs involved in using fixed-point and floating-point representations in the context of deep neural networks. It highlights that while increasing the integer part of a fixed-point representation can enhance dynamic range, it simultaneously diminishes the precision of the fractional part. Specifically, a Q16.16 signed number stored in a 32-bit register offers a dynamic range of approximately [-32,768, 32,767.9999847] and a constant resolution of 1.5259 x 10^-5, which is less precise than floating-point representations near zero.

The training of deep neural networks is conducted using single precision floating-point computation, and the conversion from floating-point to fixed-point representation occurs before deployment on the target system. This conversion necessitates determining a scale factor to represent floating-point numbers as integers multiplied by this factor, ideally avoiding divisions due to their higher computational cost.

The paper notes that convolutional layer weights typically exhibit a Gaussian distribution centered around zero, suggesting that non-uniform quantization could enhance representation. However, for the purpose of efficient computation on microcontrollers, uniform quantization is favored despite the potential loss in precision. This choice is due to the simplicity and speed of uniform quantization methods, which involve constant quantization steps and leverage efficient hardware implementations for multiplication and shifts.
