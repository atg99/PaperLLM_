The German Traffic Sign Recognition Benchmark (GTSRB) is a dataset consisting of color images of various road signs, with image sizes ranging from 15x15 to 250x250 pixels. For this experiment, images are resized to 32x32 pixels while maintaining three color channels. The dataset is split into a training set of 39,209 images and a testing set of 12,630 images, covering 43 different classes of road signs.

Training is conducted in two phases: the initial training without quantization uses a batch size of 128 for 120 epochs, with an initial learning rate of 0.01, momentum of 0.9, and a weight decay of 5x10^-4. The learning rate is reduced at specified epochs. The quantization-aware training employs 8-bit integers with a larger batch size of 512, maintaining the same parameters as the initial training.

Results show that 16-bit quantization achieves accuracy comparable to the 32-bit floating-point baseline, while 8-bit quantization experiences a slight accuracy drop of up to 1.1%. However, this drop decreases to 0.33% when using more filters (64 filters). Despite not surpassing 16-bit quantization, 8-bit quantization presents a viable option for two-dimensional networks applied to image datasets.
