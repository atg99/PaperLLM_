In this study, the authors investigate the performance of a residual neural network trained on the UCI Human Activity Recognition (UCI-HAR) dataset, focusing on the effects of quantization on accuracy and inference time. The initial training is conducted using 32-bit floating-point numbers without quantization, referred to as float32. The network is then quantized for fixed-point inference using 16-bit integers (int16) and evaluated without further training. Additionally, quantization-aware training is applied for 8-bit integers (int8), allowing layer-specific fixed-point precision.

The experiments utilize the Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD) optimizer due to its stability, particularly important for quantization-aware training. The dataset comprises 7352 training vectors and 2947 testing vectors, with each vector representing a 2.56-second one-dimensional time series captured at 50 Hz. The training process employs a batch size of 64 over 300 epochs, with specific learning rate adjustments.

Results indicate that the accuracy of the network remains comparable between the float32 and int16 quantization methods, regardless of the number of filters used in convolutional layers. However, the int8 quantization results in a slight accuracy decline, increasing up to 0.81% as the number of filters rises. Notably, the accuracy for int8 and int16 quantizations is similar only in networks with fewer parameters, highlighting a trade-off between memory footprint and accuracy. For instance, with 16 filters, the int8 quantization achieves 92.41% accuracy using 3958 bytes, while int16 yields 92.46% accuracy at the expense of increased memory usage (7916 bytes). Overall, the study emphasizes the implications of quantization on neural network performance in real-time applications.
