The paper presents a comparative analysis of energy consumption across different frameworks for deploying neural networks on microcontrollers, specifically examining TFLite Micro, STM32Cube.AI, and MicroAI. Energy consumption is measured in micro-watt hours for varying numbers of filters and data types, including float32, int16, and int8. The results indicate that MicroAI, particularly with int8 data type, demonstrates lower energy usage across multiple configurations compared to TFLite Micro and STM32Cube.AI.

In addition to energy consumption, the paper discusses the number of arithmetic and logic operations involved in inference for a fixed-point residual neural network. The operations are categorized into MACC, Add, Shift, and Max/Saturate, with formulas provided for calculating these based on parameters such as the number of filters, input samples, input channels, kernel size, and neurons.

The references cited in the paper include benchmarks and developments in deep learning on IoT devices, highlighting advancements and methodologies in reducing energy consumption and improving computational efficiency in edge computing scenarios.
