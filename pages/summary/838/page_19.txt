The paper discusses the effects of quantization-aware training on the performance of neural networks using fixed-point representation, specifically focusing on 8-bit and 16-bit quantization. It employs a training method with a batch size of 1024 over 140 epochs, maintaining the same initial learning rate, momentum, and weight decay as the initial training. The learning rate is adjusted at specific epochs.

Results indicate that 16-bit quantization retains accuracy comparable to the floating-point baseline, while 8-bit quantization leads to a maximum accuracy drop of 1.07%. This drop is less pronounced when using 48 filters per convolution, stabilizing around 0.5% to 0.6% with a higher number of filters. 

In terms of memory efficiency, 16-bit quantization remains superior. Although 8-bit quantization performs closer to 16-bit quantization on the SMNIST dataset compared to UCI-HAR, it does not offer advantages in accuracy versus memory ratio, even for smaller neural networks. Figures illustrate the accuracy relative to the number of filters and the parameters' memory usage, reinforcing the conclusion that 16-bit quantization is the more effective choice.
