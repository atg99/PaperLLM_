The paper discusses the quantization of deep neural networks, focusing on the representation of weights and activations using fixed-point arithmetic. It introduces the calculation of remaining bits for the fractional part of fixed-point representation, emphasizing that the width of the data type affects the precision of representation. A formula is presented to compute the number of bits available for the fractional part, which is crucial for maintaining precision in quantized formats.

Two main methods of quantization are explored: Post-Training Quantization and Quantization-Aware Training (QAT). In Post-Training Quantization, the neural network is trained using floating-point computations, and after training, the parameters are quantized. This approach can introduce quantization errors that may affect the accuracy of predictions, especially as the bit width decreases. Interestingly, in some cases, a slight increase in quantization error may enhance the model's generalization capabilities.

QAT aims to mitigate quantization errors by using the quantized version of the model during the forward pass of training while still relying on non-quantized values for backpropagation. The paper outlines a method where the inputs, weights, and biases are quantized prior to layer computations, and the output is quantized afterward. The scale factor used for quantization is adjusted dynamically during training but remains fixed during inference. This approach seeks to stabilize the learning process and improve overall model performance.
