This paper discusses the challenges of deploying deep neural networks (DNNs) on microcontrollers, especially in the context of Internet of Things (IoT) devices and wearables. Microcontrollers typically have limited memory (often less than 1 MiB) and operate at lower frequencies compared to GPUs and CPUs, making real-time inference difficult due to constraints on power consumption, memory footprint, and response time. The authors emphasize the trade-off between model complexity (width and depth) and performance metrics such as accuracy, memory usage, and power efficiency.

To address these challenges, the paper introduces a framework called MicroAI, which facilitates end-to-end training, quantization, and deployment of DNNs on microcontrollers. MicroAI leverages existing deep learning frameworks like TensorFlow and PyTorch for the training phase. A key technique discussed is network quantization, which reduces the number of bits used to represent model weights, allowing for fixed-point arithmetic that is more efficient on microcontroller architectures.

The paper presents comparative results using two microcontrollers (STM32L452RE and Ambiq Apollo3) and three inference engines (TensorFlow Lite for Microcontrollers, STM32Cube.AI, and MicroAI). These comparisons focus on memory footprint, inference time, and power efficiency. The authors apply 8-bit and 16-bit quantization methods across three datasets: UCI-HAR for body-worn sensors, Spoken MNIST for speech, and GTSRB for images, demonstrating the feasibility of running DNNs on microcontrollers.

The structure of the paper includes an overview of the challenges in deploying DNNs on microcontrollers, a discussion on floating-point versus fixed-point representation, the quantization methodology of MicroAI, and a comparison of its performance against existing solutions. Overall, the paper highlights the potential of MicroAI to improve the deployment of deep learning models in resource-constrained embedded systems.
