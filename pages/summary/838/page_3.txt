The paper discusses the optimization of embedded deep neural networks through quantization methods, focusing on two microcontroller platforms: Nucleo-L452RE-P and SparkFun Edge. It evaluates the inference time and power efficiency of these platforms while applying 8- and 16-bit quantization techniques across three datasets: UCI-HAR, Spoken MNIST, and GTSRB. 

The literature review highlights various optimization families for quantized neural networks. The first family involves reducing precision by employing low-bit quantization, such as PACT, SAWB, LSQ, Bit-Pruning, and DQDNN. Binarization of network parameters is also mentioned as an extreme case, with the goal being to find a balance between precision and performance.

The second family targets low-cost architectures, exemplified by MobileNet and SqueezeNet. These architectures are often designed for specific tasks like image classification, which can restrict their applicability to broader problems. In contrast, this work opts for a generic network like ResNet to allow versatility across different data types including time series, audio, and images.

The third family addresses parameter reduction through pruning techniques, which can significantly decrease the number of parameters—up to 90% in some cases—by removing non-essential parts of the network. Recent advancements have focused on structured pruning to enhance operational feasibility.

Lastly, the paper discusses factorization methods that reduce parameters by replacing them with memory pointers, alongside techniques to improve accuracy through additional learning steps. While these compression techniques effectively reduce architecture size, they often sacrifice performance. The study emphasizes quantization-based compression, which involves mapping values from a larger set to a smaller one, potentially utilizing non-uniform quantization schemes to minimize quantization error, particularly in convolutional neural networks where weight distributions can be approximated by Gaussian models.
