This paper discusses TFLite Micro and STM32Cube.AI as frameworks for deploying deep neural networks on microcontrollers. TFLite Micro is designed for a wide range of 32-bit microcontrollers, supporting various operations and allowing for the deployment of neural networks like multi-layer perceptrons and convolutional neural networks. However, it lacks integration with specific microcontroller tools and requires manual coding for application testing. TFLite Micro supports both floating-point and 8-bit fixed-point computations, employing quantization techniques for efficient processing. Despite being open-source, its complex architecture may hinder manipulation and optimization.

Conversely, STM32Cube.AI, developed by STMicroelectronics, offers a more integrated solution for their STM32 microcontrollers. It supports trained models from frameworks like Keras and TensorFlow Lite and provides an easy deployment process through integration with other STMicroelectronics tools. STM32Cube.AI includes a test application for evaluating models without coding, offering metrics on inference time and memory usage. Like TFLite Micro, it supports floating-point and 8-bit fixed-point computations but does not support 16-bit integers. However, STM32Cube.AI's inference engine is proprietary, limiting customization and extension, which poses challenges for research environments.
