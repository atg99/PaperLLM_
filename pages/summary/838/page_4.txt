The paper discusses a non-uniform quantization scheme implemented on an FPGA, which codes values as the nearest power of two rather than using a fixed-point format. This method enhances resolution for smaller values while accommodating larger values, albeit with reduced resolution. The quantization step is optimized to minimize output error, balancing precision and dynamic range. The reliance on bit shifts instead of integer multiplications offers advantages in resource usage and latency for FPGA implementations. However, the proposed non-uniform quantization results in slight accuracy degradation compared to uniform quantization.

The paper also reviews prior work on lower precision computation for deep neural networks, highlighting comparisons of test error rates across various precision formats. Notably, training with fixed-point arithmetic has shown some accuracy loss in diverse applications. It points out that while using 8-bit integer instructions can significantly speed up execution without accuracy loss, most existing research has not focused on embedded computing on microcontrollers.

Microcontrollers face limitations due to their Instruction Set Architecture (ISA), particularly the absence of SIMD instructions and reliance on 32-bit registers, which complicates the processing of lower precision data. Although some advanced microcontrollers can handle packed data, they still lack support for intermediate precision. The authors propose extending the open RISC-V ISA to support sub-byte quantization, but the scarcity of RISC-V microcontrollers hinders practical deployment in IoT applications.

Consequently, the paper focuses on off-the-shelf microcontrollers and restricts its study to 8-bit, 16-bit, and 32-bit precision. Previous attempts to deploy deep neural networks on 8-bit microcontrollers necessitate complex implementations, including pseudo-floating-point coding and custom algorithms for multiplication and activation functions.
