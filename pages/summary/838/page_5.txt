Fixed-point arithmetic is typically faster and uses less power compared to floating-point computations, making it suitable for low-power microcontrollers. However, it lacks the dynamic range and flexibility of floating-point representations, particularly for applications requiring a wide range of values.

In summary, the paper discusses advancements in neural network implementations on low-power microcontrollers, emphasizing the transition to 32-bit architectures and the adoption of ReLU as an efficient activation function. It highlights the significance of floating-point representation, particularly the IEEE754 standard for single precision, which is sufficient for most deep learning tasks. The paper also covers fixed-point representation as an alternative for low-power applications, noting its advantages in speed and power efficiency despite its limitations in dynamic range and precision.
