The study examines the impact of filter quantity and quantization levels on accuracy and memory efficiency in neural networks, particularly using the UCI Human Activity Recognition dataset. Results indicate that with over 24 filters per convolution, 16-bit quantization yields the best accuracy to memory ratio. However, when using more than 48 filters, 8-bit quantization performs worse than the baseline. 

Additionally, the research introduces the Spoken MNIST dataset, which consists of spoken digit audio signals. The dataset contains 60,000 training vectors and 10,000 testing vectors, categorized into 10 classes for digits 0 to 9. The training process employs a batch size of 256 over 120 epochs, with specific adjustments to the learning rate at designated epochs to optimize performance.
