The paper discusses the evolution of object detection algorithms, specifically focusing on the YOLO (You Only Look Once) family of models. Prior to YOLO, two-stage detectors such as R-CNN, Fast R-CNN, and Faster R-CNN dominated the field with state-of-the-art (SOTA) accuracy but had limitations in speed. This trade-off between speed and accuracy is a recurring theme in object detection.

YOLO emerged as a solution that balanced both speed and accuracy, with YOLOv2 achieving a mean average precision (MAP) of 78.6% at 40 frames per second on the VOC 2007+2012 dataset. The paper notes that subsequent YOLO versions continued to navigate the speed-accuracy trade-off.

The discussion progresses to YOLOv5, YOLO-R, and YOLOv7, with an emphasis on outlining the limitations of the first two models before delving into the improvements and structure of YOLOv7. The YOLO algorithm employs convolutional neural networks (CNN) for rapid object identification, requiring just one forward pass through the network.

Specifically, YOLOv5 utilizes CSPDarknet for feature extraction and incorporates a feature pyramids network (PANet) for feature aggregation, ultimately predicting detection outputs such as class, location, confidence score, and size. The paper sets the stage for a performance comparison among the three models to determine which one offers superior accuracy.
