The paper discusses advancements in the YOLOv7 architecture, particularly its modular approach to model training, which enhances parameterization. The architecture comprises three main components: the neck, head, and backbone, with outputs generated in the head. YOLOv7 distinguishes itself by employing multiple heads rather than a single one, a technique previously seen in Deep Supervision methods used in deep learning models.

In YOLOv7, two heads are identified: the Lead Head, which serves as the final output head, and the Auxiliary Head, which facilitates middle-layer training. The Auxiliary Head benefits from an assistant loss that updates its weights, thereby improving the model's learning capacity through Deep Supervision. This process is linked to the Label Assigner, which generates soft labels by considering both the ground truth and network predictions, emphasizing the importance of creating coarse and soft labels instead of hard ones.

The Lead Head in the YOLOv7 network predicts outcomes that are then used to produce soft labels. These soft labels are crucial as they are employed to compute loss for both the Lead and Auxiliary Heads. The robust learning capability of the Lead Head allows it to generate more accurate soft labels, which aids in capturing the distribution and correlation between source and target data. Consequently, the Lead Head focuses on learning residual information, while the Auxiliary Head benefits from the foundational knowledge established by the Lead Head, leading to improved overall model performance.
