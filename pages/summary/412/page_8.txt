The paper discusses a novel technique for generating soft labels in object detection models, specifically focusing on the YOLOv7 architecture. Two distinct sets of soft labels are produced: a fine label set for the lead head and a coarse label set for the auxiliary head. The fine labels are created directly, while the coarse labels are constructed by relaxing the limitations of the positive sample assignment procedure, allowing more grids to be treated as positive targets.

The performance of YOLOv7 is highlighted, showcasing its ability to achieve a 56.8% average precision (AP) with an inference time of 28 ms. This performance slightly surpasses YOLO-R, which achieves 56.4% AP at the same inference time. Other models, including YOLOv5, Scaled YOLOv4, PPYOLOE, and YOLOX, demonstrate lower AP scores compared to YOLOv7.

The results section compares the performance of YOLOv7 with other models such as YOLOR, YOLOv5, and YOLOX, focusing on metrics like frames per second (FPS) and accuracy. The findings indicate that YOLOv7 outperforms the other models in both accuracy and processing speed, reinforcing its superiority within the YOLO family of models.
