The paper discusses semantic segmentation, a crucial aspect of computer vision that involves assigning class labels to each pixel in an image. This task is vital for applications like autonomous driving. The authors highlight the dominance of deep convolutional networks since the introduction of Fully Convolutional Networks (FCN) and emphasize recent advancements focusing on contextual aggregation, which is the core interest of this study.

Contextual representation refers to the relationship between a pixel and its surrounding pixels, which can vary in spatial scale. Previous works have explored multi-scale contexts, while more recent studies have focused on the relational dynamics between a pixel and its context, enhancing the representation of pixels with similar contextual features.

The authors introduce their Object-Contextual Representation (OCR) scheme, which aims to enrich the representation of a pixel by incorporating information from the entire object region associated with its class label. They provide empirical evidence demonstrating that when the ground-truth object region is utilized, this representation augmentation significantly enhances segmentation performance, as illustrated in their experimental results across various datasets.
