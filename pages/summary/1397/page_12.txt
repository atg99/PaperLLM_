In this paper, the authors detail the training setups for various segmentation approaches, including PPM and ASPP, ensuring fairness by adhering to prior works. For the Cityscapes dataset, they establish specific parameters such as an initial learning rate of 0.01, weight decay of 0.0005, a crop size of 769x769, and a batch size of 8. The training iterations are set to 40K for the train set and 100K for the combined train and validation set. Additional data is incorporated in different ways: for coarse data, the model is first trained on the train and validation set for 100K iterations, then fine-tuned on the coarse set for 50K iterations, followed by another fine-tuning on the train and validation set for 20K iterations. When using both coarse and Mapillary datasets, the model undergoes extensive pre-training and fine-tuning with adjusted learning rates and batch sizes.

For other datasets like ADE20K, LIP, PASCAL-Context, and COCO-Stuff, the authors specify different initial learning rates, weight decay, crop sizes, batch sizes, and training iterations.

The paper also compares the proposed OCR method with existing multi-scale context schemes (PPM and ASPP) and various relational context schemes (Self-Attention, Criss-Cross Attention, DANet, and Double Attention) across three benchmarks: Cityscapes, ADE20K, and LIP. The results indicate that OCR significantly outperforms both multi-scale context schemes, achieving notable improvements over PPM and ASPP, despite the complexity of OCR being lower. These findings demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed method in enhancing segmentation performance.
