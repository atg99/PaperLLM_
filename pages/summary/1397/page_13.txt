The paper presents the Segmentation Transformer, focusing on the Object Contextual Representation (OCR) method for Optical Character Recognition (OCR) in semantic segmentation tasks. It compares OCR with existing multi-scale context schemes such as Pyramid Pooling Module (PPM) and Atrous Spatial Pyramid Pooling (ASPP), demonstrating that OCR consistently outperforms these methods across various benchmarks including Cityscapes and ADE20K.

In the comparison of performance metrics, OCR achieves the highest accuracy across all datasets, with notable improvements over PPM and ASPP. For instance, in Cityscapes without coarse annotations, OCR achieves 81.8% accuracy compared to 80.3% for PPM and 81.0% for ASPP. This trend continues in other datasets, highlighting OCR's effectiveness in capturing contextual information.

Furthermore, the complexity analysis reveals that OCR is more efficient, requiring fewer parameters, GPU memory, and computation time compared to its counterparts. For example, OCR uses only 10.5M parameters, which is significantly lower than the 23.1M and 15.5M required by PPM and ASPP respectively. Additionally, OCR demonstrates the least runtime of 45ms during inference, showcasing its suitability for real-time applications.

Overall, the findings indicate that OCR not only enhances segmentation performance but also does so with greater computational efficiency, making it a valuable contribution to the field of semantic segmentation.
