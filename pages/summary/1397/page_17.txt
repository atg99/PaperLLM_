The paper introduces an object-contextual representation approach for semantic segmentation, utilizing a segmentation transformer combined with an OCR (Object Contextual Representation) head to enhance segmentation quality. The authors implement this by using predictions from the Panoptic-FPN's semantic segmentation head to compute soft object regions, followed by refinement through the OCR head. Both heads are assigned a loss weight of 0.25, maintaining other training settings for consistency.

Results demonstrate a performance improvement in PQ (Panoptic Quality) from 43.0% to 44.2% with the addition of OCR, primarily attributed to enhanced segmentation quality in the "stuff" category, reflected by a 1.0% increase in mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) and a 2.3% increase in PQSt (Panoptic Quality for "stuff"). The performance of the combined model is competitive with recent state-of-the-art methods.

In conclusion, the proposed method effectively strengthens pixel representation by associating each pixel with its corresponding object region, leading to consistent improvements across various benchmarks.
