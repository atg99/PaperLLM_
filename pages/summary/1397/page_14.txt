The paper presents a novel Optical Character Recognition (OCR) method that significantly reduces GPU memory consumption, FLOPs, and runtime compared to existing techniques such as PPM, DANet, CC-Attention, and Self-Attention. Specifically, the OCR method consumes only a fraction of the memory and computational resources of these alternatives, making it a more efficient choice for balancing performance and resource requirements.

The authors categorize existing methods into two groups based on the baselines used: simple baselines (e.g., dilated ResNet-101) and advanced baselines (e.g., PSPNet, DeepLabv3). The performance of the proposed OCR is evaluated against both baseline types. When applied to a simple baseline, the OCR achieves an impressive performance of 81.8% on the Cityscapes test, surpassing several advanced baseline methods. When using an advanced baseline (HRNet-W48), the OCR achieves 84.2% after pre-training on the Mapillary dataset, with an additional 0.3% improvement from a novel post-processing scheme, SegFix, culminating in a final score of 84.5%. This score ranks first on the Cityscapes leaderboard at the time of submission.

Furthermore, the paper highlights that while other methods like PPM and ASPP do not enhance performance when applied to HRNet-W48, the OCR consistently improves results. A recent work has set a new state-of-the-art performance of 85.4% on the Cityscapes leaderboard by integrating the proposed OCR with a new hierarchical multi-scale attention mechanism.
