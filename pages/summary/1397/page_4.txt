ASPP utilizes a set of sparsely sampled pixels with varying dilation rates to gather context from both object and background regions. In contrast, OCR focuses on pixels strictly within the object, aiming to create a more relevant context.

The authors highlight the connection of their method to existing works like Double Attention and ACFNet, emphasizing their unique approach in forming regions with supervision from ground-truth segmentation, unlike previous unsupervised methods. Additionally, their methodology computes the relationship between pixels and regions using both pixel and region representations, enhancing contextual understanding.

The paper also addresses coarse-to-fine segmentation schemes, noting that their approach uses coarse segmentation maps to generate contextual representations rather than simply combining them with other representations. Finally, while acknowledging the existence of region-wise segmentation methods that classify regions, the authors assert that their approach diverges by not classifying regions but instead utilizing relational context for segmentation.
