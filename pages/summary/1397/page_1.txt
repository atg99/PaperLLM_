In this paper, the authors address the problem of context aggregation in semantic segmentation by introducing a novel approach known as object-contextual representations. The core idea is based on the observation that a pixel's label corresponds to the category of the object it belongs to. The proposed method involves three main steps: first, the authors learn object regions using ground-truth segmentation; second, they aggregate the representations of pixels within these object regions to compute an object region representation; and third, they establish relationships between each pixel and the object regions, enhancing the pixel representation with a weighted aggregation of the object region representations.

The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of their method through empirical results, achieving competitive performance across various benchmarks, including Cityscapes and COCO-Stuff. Their model, termed "HRNet + OCR + SegFix," secured the top position on the Cityscapes leaderboard by the ECCV 2020 submission deadline. 

Additionally, the authors reformulate the object-contextual representation approach using a Transformer encoder-decoder framework. Specifically, they integrate the first two steps—object region learning and representation computation—into a cross-attention module within the decoder, while the final step is implemented as a cross-attention module in the encoder. This paper presents a significant advancement in semantic segmentation by effectively leveraging contextual information through a Transformer-based approach.
