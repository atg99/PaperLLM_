The paper presents a comprehensive analysis of fraud detection in the context of power systems, specifically focusing on False Data Injection Attacks (FDIA). It evaluates various detection models including CNN, LSTM, and a proposed method using SecFed, within the IEEE 14-bus and IEEE 118-bus systems.

Key findings include:

1. **Model Performance**: The proposed SecFed model demonstrates superior detection accuracy compared to CNN and LSTM models, particularly in the presence of weak and strong attacks. It effectively addresses issues such as communication delays and privacy concerns that are prevalent in ideal models.

2. **Impact of Communication Rounds**: The accuracy of the detection models varies with the number of communication rounds. The paper includes visual comparisons (Figures 4, 5, 6, and 7) to illustrate how each model performs under different conditions and attack strengths.

3. **Measurement Noise Effects**: The research also investigates the influence of measurement noise on model accuracy. Gaussian noise levels ranging from 1% to 4% were applied to client data. Results indicate that all models experience a decrease in accuracy as noise levels increase, making it harder to distinguish between normal and compromised data.

Overall, the study concludes that the proposed model not only matches but often exceeds the performance of traditional methods, making it a promising solution for enhancing FDIA detection in power systems.
