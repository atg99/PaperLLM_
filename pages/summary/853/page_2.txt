The paper discusses the challenges of detecting False Data Injection Attacks (FDIA) in smart grids, particularly focusing on the limitations of traditional centralized detection methods. These methods require all data from grid nodes to be sent to a central workstation, presenting issues such as reliance on powerful central resources, limited real-time processing capabilities, vulnerability to data breaches, and privacy concerns regarding sensitive attack data. 

To address these challenges, the authors propose a novel federated learning-based FDIA detection method. This approach involves establishing edge node detectors at each power system node, enabling local data collection and detection while preserving data privacy. By utilizing a Transformer model, the method effectively extracts features from the data through a self-attention mechanism, enhancing the detection of various intensities of FDIA.

Key contributions of the paper include:
1. A federated learning approach that keeps data local during training, thus protecting privacy and reducing communication delays during online detection.
2. Development of a Transformer-based FDIA detection model that leverages multi-head self-attention for deep analysis of electrical quantities.
3. Introduction of a secure federated learning scheme (SecFed) that employs the Paillier cryptosystem to encrypt data exchanged during training, enhancing security against potential breaches.
4. Extensive experimental validation demonstrating the effectiveness and robustness of the proposed detection method, even under noisy measurement conditions.

The paper also highlights the necessity of effective bad data detection mechanisms in power systems, noting existing vulnerabilities that may allow stealthy attacks to bypass traditional detection methods.
