The paper presents a novel method for detecting false data injection attacks (FDIA) in smart grids by combining secure federated learning with a Transformer-based model. This approach is the first of its kind to leverage secure federated learning specifically for FDIA detection. The proposed model enables local training at each node while ensuring the privacy of local data through a federated learning framework. Additionally, it employs the Paillier cryptosystem to enhance data security during the training process.

Extensive experiments demonstrate that the Transformer-based detection model significantly outperforms traditional deep learning algorithms, highlighting its robustness against noise interference. The secure federated learning approach also shows advantages in protecting data privacy and reducing communication overhead compared to centralized detection methods.

Future work will aim to extend this method to address multiple types of cyber attacks, including denial-of-service and replay attacks. It will also explore more realistic scenarios where attackers have incomplete system information and investigate hyper-parameter optimization using automated machine learning techniques. 

The appendix provides a detailed explanation of the Paillier cryptosystem, outlining its key mechanisms for securing federated learning data, including key generation, encryption, aggregation, and decryption processes. This technical foundation supports the overall effectiveness of the proposed detection framework.
