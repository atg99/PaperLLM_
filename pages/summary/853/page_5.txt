The paper presents a Secure Federated Learning (SecFed) algorithm aimed at enhancing data privacy in federated learning scenarios. The algorithm involves multiple clients that collaboratively train a model while keeping their data decentralized. Key steps include the initialization of cryptographic keys by a trustee, iterative local training for each client, and secure aggregation of model updates.

The algorithm operates in R communication rounds, where each client performs E local training epochs using a mini-batch of data. The model updates are calculated using gradient descent, employing techniques such as bias-correction for moment estimation to improve convergence. The updated model weights are encrypted and sent to a cloud server, which aggregates them and redistributes the aggregated ciphertext back to the clients for decryption.

Additionally, the paper introduces a Transformer-based bad data detector for power systems, utilizing a multi-head self-attention mechanism to discern genuine data from anomalies. The model incorporates positional encoding to maintain the order of features and employs an Add&Norm layer to enhance training stability through residual connections.

Overall, the proposed methods aim to bolster the security and reliability of federated learning applications, particularly in sensitive domains like power system monitoring. The combination of federated learning and advanced deep learning techniques like Transformers provides a robust framework for handling data privacy and integrity challenges.
