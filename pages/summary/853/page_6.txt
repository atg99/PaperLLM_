The paper discusses a normalization technique applied to the activation values in a neural network, specifically using LayerNorm in conjunction with multi-head attention and feedforward neural network modules. This approach addresses issues related to gradient dispersion and explosion, enhancing training stability and speeding up convergence through normalization of layer inputs.

The feedforward neural network module incorporates a fully connected layer followed by a dropout layer to mitigate overfitting. The output is processed through a sigmoid layer that produces classification results based on the transformer's output.

In the context of federated learning, the paper introduces a secure framework that allows multiple clients to collaboratively train a model without sharing raw data. Each client retains its data locally while uploading model weights to a central server for aggregation. To safeguard against potential data leakage from model weights, the authors propose the integration of the Paillier cryptosystem, a homomorphic encryption method, ensuring that the original data remains confidential.

The architecture includes a cloud server, clients, and a trustee responsible for managing encryption and decryption of model weights. The cloud server initializes the global model, facilitates communication with clients, and aggregates weights until convergence. Clients develop local models based on their data, while the trustee handles cryptographic operations.

To validate the proposed SecFed-Transformer method, the paper outlines a series of experiments aimed at assessing its performance, security, and efficiency, detailing data generation, implementation specifics, and hyperparameter settings.
