The paper presents a novel approach for detecting false data injection attacks (FDIA) in smart grids using a secure federated deep learning framework. The authors highlight the vulnerability of smart grids, a critical cyber-physical system, to various cyber threats, with FDIA being a significant concern due to its potential to disrupt operations. The proposed method integrates Transformer architecture, federated learning, and the Paillier cryptosystem to enhance detection capabilities while ensuring data privacy.

The Transformer model is utilized at edge nodes to analyze the relationships between electrical quantities through its multi-head self-attention mechanism. By employing federated learning, the system trains detection models collaboratively across nodes without sharing sensitive data, thus preserving privacy. Additionally, the integration of the Paillier cryptosystem enhances the security of the federated learning process.

The effectiveness of the proposed detection method is validated through extensive experiments conducted on the IEEE 14-bus and 118-bus test systems, demonstrating its superiority over existing techniques. The paper underscores the importance of developing secure and efficient FDIA detection mechanisms to safeguard the operation of smart grids against cyber threats.
