The paper presents a comparative analysis of different detection methods applied to two nodes of the IEEE 14-B and 118-B test systems under varying communication rounds and attack conditions. It highlights the effectiveness of the proposed method over traditional models, such as RCNN and LSTM, in terms of precision, recall, and F1-score, particularly under strong attack scenarios.

In the IEEE 14-B system, the proposed method consistently achieved perfect scores across all metrics for most communication rounds, outperforming both RCNN and LSTM. Similarly, in the IEEE 118-B system, the proposed approach demonstrated superior performance, especially under strong attacks, where it reached a precision and recall of 1.0000 in numerous cases.

The analysis also shows that the proposed method's accuracy exceeds 90% for both strong and weak attacks, indicating its robustness. The results suggest that strong attacks reveal more distinct differences between normal and compromised data, thereby enhancing detection performance.

Furthermore, the paper explores the effectiveness of federated learning by comparing it to local and ideal models. The proposed federated model significantly outperformed the local model, which relied solely on local data, indicating that communication among nodes enhances overall model effectiveness. The proposed model achieved near-perfect performance metrics, emphasizing its potential in real-world applications for detecting cyber threats.
