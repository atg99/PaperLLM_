This paper presents a comparative study of the SecFed-Transformer model against traditional deep learning detection models, specifically CNN and LSTM, within a federated learning framework. The experiments focus on evaluating the performance of the proposed model using various metrics, and the robustness of the model is assessed by analyzing accuracy under different noise levels.

Data generation involves creating normal data based on the IEEE 14-bus and 118-bus systems, with specified mean and variance. Compromised data is generated to simulate stealthy False Data Injection Attacks (FDIAs), categorized into weak, strong, and medium attacks based on the deviations in power injections and voltage magnitudes.

For the experiments, a training set of 10,000 normal and 10,000 compromised samples is created, alongside a test set of 1,000 samples each. The federated learning setup requires consistent data features from clients, focusing on bus and branch data.

The experiments are conducted on a high-performance machine, with hyperparameters for the Transformer model including a learning rate of 0.0001, three encoder blocks, and varying local epochs based on attack strength. Performance is evaluated through metrics such as accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score.

Results indicate that the SecFed-Transformer outperforms both CNN and LSTM, particularly under weak attack conditions. For instance, at communication round six, the proposed method achieves an accuracy of 0.9965 for bus 2 and 0.9230 for bus 3, showcasing its effectiveness in detecting compromised data. The findings underscore the advantages of the proposed approach in enhancing detection capabilities in the context of federated learning.
