The paper discusses the estimation of state vectors in power systems, focusing on the Weighted Least Squares (WLS) method, which minimizes the difference between actual measurements and estimated values. The measurement function and associated noise are outlined, emphasizing the challenges posed by bad data, which can arise from various sources such as sensor errors and human mistakes. These inaccuracies can significantly impact the control center's assessment of the system's state.

To identify bad data, the residual is defined, and its Euclidean norm is compared against a threshold to determine the presence of erroneous measurements. The paper also addresses the issue of False Data Injection Attacks (FDIA), where attackers manipulate measurement vectors to alter state vectors without detection. The fundamental mechanics of these attacks involve adhering to power flow equations while stealthily modifying state values.

The authors refer to a method for constructing stealthy attack vectors that leave residual values unchanged, thus evading conventional bad data detection mechanisms. This highlights the vulnerabilities of existing systems to such attacks.

The paper proposes a new method, the SecFed scheme, which includes a transformer-based FDIA detection model and a federated learning framework to enhance security against these vulnerabilities. The workflow of this proposed scheme is detailed, aiming to improve the robustness of state estimation in power systems against potential attacks.
