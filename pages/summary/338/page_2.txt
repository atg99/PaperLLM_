The paper discusses the advancements and applications of pre-trained language models, particularly in the field of biomedicine. It highlights the success of models like BERT, which utilizes self-supervised tasks such as masked language modeling and next sentence prediction for pre-training on large-scale textual data. BERT's contextualized representations have proven effective for various natural language understanding tasks, although its architecture limits its generative capabilities.

In contrast, the Generative Pre-trained Transformer (GPT) models, including GPT-2 and GPT-3, focus on language generation tasks. They are trained on large corpora using causal language modeling, enabling them to excel in tasks like translation and summarization, often without the need for fine-tuning.

The paper also examines the adaptation of these models within the biomedical domain. It notes that while general-domain BERT models can be improved by further pre-training on biomedical texts, there are limitations due to vocabulary differences. Some researchers have proposed creating domain-specific models, such as PubMedBERT, which is pre-trained from scratch on biomedical literature, showing improved performance on related tasks.

However, GPT models have demonstrated poor performance on biomedical tasks, despite their generative strengths. Previous attempts to pre-train GPT on biomedical texts were limited in scope, and more recent work has explored innovative applications of GPT-3 for clinical tasks.

The paper outlines downstream tasks relevant to the biomedical domain, including relation extraction and question answering. Relation extraction is crucial for biomedicine, with various approaches ranging from classical methods to joint extraction and text generation methods. The authors emphasize their focus on end-to-end relation extraction, which generates relational triplets directly from text without requiring additional annotations. In question answering, generative models are highlighted for their ability to directly produce answers based on context.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the need for further exploration of generative models in the biomedical domain and presents a framework for addressing key tasks in biomedical text processing.
