The training and test sets were carefully constructed, yielding a total of 14971 abnormal and 15169 normal samples for training, and 127 abnormal and 150 normal samples for testing. The data preparation involved converting EEG recordings into montages and focusing on the transverse central parietal montage to highlight spike activity. We specifically utilized only the first minute of EEG data for training and testing, as previous studies indicated that this segment is sufficient for neurologists to classify EEG sessions accurately.

However, recognizing the limitations of training with just one minute of data, we expanded our approach by including additional time segments from the training sessions. This substantially increased the training data size, allowing us to utilize up to 11 minutes without compromising performance. Our methodology resulted in a significant increase in training data and addressed the overfitting concerns typically associated with small datasets.

The networks were trained using a robust optimization algorithm over 500 epochs with a batch size of 64. The results demonstrated competitive mean accuracies when compared to existing methods, underscoring the effectiveness of our deep recurrent neural network architecture in classifying EEG signals based on minimal input data. The findings contribute to the ongoing research in automated EEG classification, highlighting the advantages of leveraging deep learning techniques in the absence of extensive feature extraction.
