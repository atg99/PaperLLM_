The paper discusses the challenges associated with training recurrent neural networks (RNNs), particularly the issues of gradient growth or decay over long sequences, which complicate the learning of long-term dependencies. To address this, the authors highlight the development of more advanced recurrent units, specifically Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) and Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) architectures. While both have been shown to outperform classical RNNs, the authors choose to utilize GRUs due to their fewer parameters, leading to faster training times and better generalization with data.

The GRU architecture includes mechanisms for managing the hidden state through linear interpolation between an intermediate candidate hidden state and the previous hidden state. This is governed by two gates: the update gate, which controls how much of the previous state is retained, and the reset gate, which determines how much of the previous state is forgotten. The mathematical formulation of the GRU is presented, highlighting the use of nonlinear activation functions.

Additionally, the paper introduces the inception module, a key component of the GoogLeNet architecture, which employs filters of various sizes to capture features at different levels of abstraction. This approach enhances the network's ability to extract relevant features while addressing the increase in computational demands due to additional filters by incorporating 1x1 filters for dimensionality reduction.

The DenseNet architecture is also discussed, which connects each layer to every other layer in a feed-forward manner. This design helps mitigate the vanishing or exploding gradient problem commonly found in deep networks by providing shortcut paths for gradients during back-propagation, enabling more effective training through better management of model complexity.
