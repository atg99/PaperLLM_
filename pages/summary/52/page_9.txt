This paper presents ChronoNet, a novel recurrent neural network architecture designed for the analysis of EEG time-series data. The primary aim is to automate the classification of EEG recordings as normal or abnormal, which is crucial for diagnosing neurological conditions. Manual interpretation of EEGs is noted to be labor-intensive and costly, highlighting the importance of automated solutions to expedite treatment and alleviate the burden on clinical staff.

ChronoNet achieves an impressive average accuracy of 92.84% over five runs on a 30-class problem, surpassing the previous best accuracy by 1.17%, thus establishing a new benchmark in the field. The architecture's flexibility and adaptability enable it to effectively handle various types of time-series data, as demonstrated by its successful application in classifying speech.

The paper concludes with a commitment to expanding the research to include different time-domain datasets in future studies, indicating the potential for broader applications of ChronoNet beyond EEG analysis.
