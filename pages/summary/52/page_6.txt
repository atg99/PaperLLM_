The paper presents an advanced architecture called ChronoNet for abnormal EEG classification, integrating a Convolutional Densely Connected Gated Recurrent Neural Network (C-DRNN) framework. The architecture features densely connected GRU layers, where the output of each GRU layer serves as input to all other GRU layers, enhancing feature propagation and reducing issues related to vanishing or exploding gradients. This design allows for deeper networks capable of handling complex tasks.

ChronoNet also employs multiple filter lengths in the 1D convolutional layers, which aids in capturing diverse temporal features from the EEG data. The dataset used for experiments is the TUH Abnormal EEG Corpus, which consists of annotated EEG records classified as abnormal or normal. This corpus is part of the larger TUH EEG Corpus, known for being the largest publicly available clinical EEG database, with a significant portion of its data representing abnormal EEG sessions.

The study highlights the effectiveness of the proposed architecture and discusses the data augmentation techniques employed to enhance model performance. Overall, the architecture aims to improve the accuracy and reliability of abnormal EEG classification.
