The paper explores various design choices and training strategies for the MaskIoU head within the Mask R-CNN framework, focusing on improving mask scoring performance. It presents different configurations for input representations and evaluates their impact on Average Precision (AP) metrics. The study compares a baseline Mask R-CNN model with several settings, including using target masks with Regions of Interest (RoIs) and different combinations of masks.

Key findings include that learning the MaskIoU for the target category yields the best performance, while attempting to learn MaskIoU across all categories significantly degrades results. This suggests that training classification and regression together in a single objective function is challenging. Additionally, the paper discusses sample selection for training, indicating that using all samples yields superior performance compared to using a threshold for MaskIoU.

The results underscore the importance of careful design choices in the training process and highlight potential areas for further exploration, including the quality of predicted MaskIoU and its implications for computational complexity. Overall, the study provides valuable insights into optimizing mask scoring in object detection tasks.
