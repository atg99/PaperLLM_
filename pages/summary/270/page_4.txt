This paper reviews various methods for predicting traffic flow parameters, focusing on both traditional statistical models like ARIMA and machine learning techniques. It highlights the advantages of non-parametric machine learning methods over parametric models, emphasizing their higher accuracy and flexibility regarding data distribution assumptions. The paper discusses the application of techniques such as k-nearest neighbors and support vector machines in traffic speed and travel time prediction.

The authors note the challenges machine learning faces with complex high-dimensional data and describe early neural network (NN) approaches, including artificial NNs and fuzzy NNs. More recently, advanced deep learning models like deep belief networks (DBNs), stacked autoencoders, convolutional neural networks (CNNs), and recurrent neural networks (RNNs) have been employed in traffic forecasting. Specifically, long short-term memory (LSTM) networks have shown promising results in traffic prediction tasks.

The paper also critiques the limitations of conventional CNNs in modeling spatial-temporal relationships in traffic data, particularly their inability to address issues like overlapping roadways and the physical characteristics of traffic networks. To overcome these limitations, the authors propose a new capsule network (CapsNet) combined with a nested LSTM structure, creating a novel deep learning architecture aimed at improving the accuracy of traffic predictions.

In the methodology section, the authors define the traffic state of a roadway link based on the average speed of vehicles, presenting a formula to calculate the average speed over a specified period. This foundational metric serves as a basis for further analysis and prediction within their proposed framework.
