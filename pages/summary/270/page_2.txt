The paper by X. Ma et al. discusses the evolution of traffic prediction methodologies, highlighting a shift from traditional statistical models to advanced machine learning techniques, particularly deep learning models. Initially, traffic prediction studies were limited by the availability of transportation data and relied on statistical methods. However, the advent of improved traffic sensing technology and computational power has enabled the application of machine learning, which excels in managing complex spatiotemporal data.

Deep learning models, such as deep belief networks (DBNs), convolutional neural networks (CNNs), and recurrent neural networks (RNNs), have demonstrated superior performance in traffic forecasting compared to traditional machine learning approaches. These models effectively extract high-dimensional features from traffic data but still face challenges in accurately capturing spatial and temporal dependencies.

The paper categorizes existing CNN-based approaches into two strategies for handling spatiotemporal traffic data. The first treats traffic data as a 2D matrix, separating spatial and temporal dimensions, which fails to represent the complexities of roadway networks adequately. The second strategy uses CNNs to project traffic states onto physical roadway links, treating network maps as images. Despite these advancements, CNNs encounter limitations, such as loss of critical traffic correlations due to pooling operations and an inability to represent various properties of traffic states adequately. The authors argue that the current CNN approach is insufficient for capturing the intricate spatial dependencies in traffic networks, especially in complex structures with features like viaducts and intersections.
