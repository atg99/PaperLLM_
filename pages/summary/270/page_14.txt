In the study by X. Ma et al., a CapsNet combined with an NLSTM approach is proposed for traffic state prediction, addressing limitations of traditional statistical models and machine learning methods in managing complex spatial relationships within roadway networks. The research emphasizes the importance of traffic prediction for effective traffic management systems.

The comparison of NLSTM and traditional LSTMs reveals that NLSTM demonstrates lower mean squared error (MSE) values and improved efficiency in predicting multistep traffic speeds, particularly in short-term predictions. The NLSTM's performance is stable, especially when utilizing long-term historical data. Both models have a similar parameter scale of 8.8 million, but NLSTM requires significantly less time for predictions, confirming its efficiency.

The CapsNet component excels in capturing spatial features of traffic states, outperforming conventional CNNs. The integrated CapsNet+NLSTM model shows superior performance against other deep learning algorithms, making it a promising tool for forecasting traffic conditions in extensive urban road networks.

The study's key contributions include the development of a novel CapsNet for spatial feature extraction, the incorporation of NLSTM for temporal dependency modeling, and the overall enhancement in prediction accuracy compared to baseline models. The results highlight the model's effectiveness in navigating intricate road networks with complex link configurations.

Future research directions may explore dynamic routing and other enhancements to further improve traffic prediction capabilities.
