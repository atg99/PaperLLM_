The paper discusses advancements in traffic state modeling using a capsule network (CapsNet) and a novel nested long short-term memory (NLSTM) architecture. The CapsNet model is utilized to extract spatial features from input traffic images, optimizing routing weights through a dynamic routing algorithm. This process involves multiple iterations to enhance performance, particularly demonstrated on the MNIST dataset.

In analyzing traffic states, which exhibit strong temporal patterns and long-term dependencies, the study introduces NLSTMs. Unlike traditional stacked LSTMs, NLSTMs utilize a nested architecture that allows for improved learning of long-term time series data. This design enables the internal LSTM units to manage their own memory cells, facilitating selective access to long-term information. Consequently, NLSTMs demonstrate enhanced capability in capturing the long-term dependencies of traffic states, particularly when events are not immediately relevant. The mathematical framework governing the internal LSTM units aligns closely with that of standard LSTMs, ensuring consistency in performance.
