In the study by X. Ma et al., a deep learning architecture for traffic forecasting is presented, comprising multiple convolutional and LSTM layers. The input layer processes data with dimensions of 164 × 148 × 1, leading to a series of convolutions and pooling operations that progressively reduce the spatial dimensions while increasing the depth of feature maps. The architecture includes four convolutional layers followed by LSTM layers, resulting in a total of 53,062,230 parameters.

The evaluation of the model's performance is conducted using mean squared error (MSE) and mean absolute percentage error (MAPE) as metrics. The mathematical formulations for these metrics are provided, where 𝑦̂𝑖 represents the predicted values and 𝑦𝑖 denotes the actual traffic speeds.

Experimental results highlight the performance of the CapsNet+NLSTM model in comparison to five baseline models across different prediction time steps (2 min, 10 min, and 20 min). The results indicate that CapsNet+NLSTM consistently outperforms other models, achieving the lowest MSE and MAPE values across all time steps, demonstrating its effectiveness in traffic forecasting.
