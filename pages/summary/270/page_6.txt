In this study, Ma et al. address the limitations of traditional Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) in analyzing traffic conditions on complex road segments. The authors highlight three main drawbacks of CNNs: the loss of valuable information due to the pooling layers, the inability to distinguish between non-spatially connected links, and challenges in handling overlapping areas due to low-resolution grid-based images. To overcome these issues, the authors propose using a Capsule Network (CapsNet), which utilizes capsules in vector form instead of scalar neurons.

CapsNet effectively encapsulates all relevant information about detected features in a vector, with the length representing the detection probability and the direction encoding various states of the features, such as rotation and size. This structure enables CapsNet to distinguish overlapping regions and interlaced links in complicated road structures, addressing the low-resolution challenges present in traffic images. Furthermore, CapsNet retains all extracted local features by replacing pooling operations with dynamic routing, thus preserving spatial relationships among links.

The CapsNet model described consists of two convolutional layers and a fully connected layer, referred to as TrafficCaps. The first layer extracts spatial relations between adjacent links, while the second converts scalar outputs into primary capsules in vector form. The TrafficCaps layer captures the spatial relationships among local features derived from the primary capsules. Unlike CNNs, which use scalar inputs and standard backpropagation for learning, CapsNet employs unique operations tailored to its vector-based capsule structure. Overall, the study demonstrates the potential of CapsNet to enhance the analysis of network-level traffic states by effectively addressing the limitations of CNNs.
