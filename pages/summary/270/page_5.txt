In the study by X. Ma et al., the authors explore the representation of traffic states as images through the integration of average vehicle speeds with GIS mapping. The average speed for each link in the road network is visualized using color coding, which is then converted into grayscale images for analysis.

To characterize the traffic states, the road network is divided into grids measuring 0.0001° by 0.0001° in latitude and longitude. Each grid's value is determined based on the traffic speed of the links it contains. If no link passes through a grid, its value is zero; if one link is present, its speed is assigned; and if multiple links occupy a grid, their average speed is used.

This process results in a series of images, where each grid acts as a pixel representing the projected velocity. The images generated reflect not only the traffic states but also the spatial structure and topology of the road network over time intervals of 2 minutes.

Furthermore, the paper discusses the application of CNN methods, specifically CapsNet, which have shown effective results in capturing spatial relationships among links within urban road networks, particularly in identifying congestion patterns.
