The paper presents a framework for traffic state prediction using a combination of Capsule Networks (CapsNet) and a modified Long Short-Term Memory (NLSTM) model. The NLSTM model processes temporal features of traffic states through a series of equations that define the internal state updates, including input, forget, cell, and output gates, represented by equations (7) to (11). The internal LSTM unit uses inputs derived from the external LSTM unit, which simplifies the cell state update to the output of the internal LSTM (equation 14).

The CapsNet is utilized to learn the spatiotemporal features of traffic states, with its outputs transformed into a vector format to serve as input for the NLSTM (equation 15). The final predictions of traffic states are generated through a fully connected layer following the NLSTM, where the predicted speed is computed as shown in equation (16). The model is trained end-to-end and is capable of making multi-step predictions based on historical traffic data. This integrated approach aims to enhance the accuracy of traffic state forecasting by leveraging both spatial and temporal learning mechanisms.
