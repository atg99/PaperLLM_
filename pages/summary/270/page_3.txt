However, these methods often struggle with non-linear patterns and complex interactions in traffic data. On the other hand, artificial intelligence techniques, particularly machine learning and deep learning approaches, have gained popularity due to their ability to model complex relationships and capture temporal dependencies.

Recent studies have highlighted the effectiveness of recurrent neural networks (RNNs) and their variants like long short-term memory (LSTM) networks in capturing the temporal features of traffic states. Despite their success, existing stacked LSTM models have limitations in characterizing the temporal hierarchy, especially under conditions like severe weather or disasters, where long-term dependencies may not provide useful short-term information.

To address these challenges, this paper introduces a novel approach combining capsule networks (CapsNet) and a newly proposed nested LSTM (NLSTM) structure. CapsNet offers a unique way to extract spatial features from traffic networks using capsule vectors that encode high-level features and their detection probabilities. This method improves upon conventional CNNs by effectively considering slightly active features and enhancing spatial dependency extraction. The NLSTM enhances the model's ability to dynamically capture hierarchical temporal dependencies in traffic data.

The main contributions of this study include the introduction of CapsNet for spatial feature extraction, the utilization of NLSTM for dynamic temporal dependency capture, and the development of a new framework for network-level traffic prediction that outperforms existing models. The efficacy of this combined approach is demonstrated through comprehensive visual and quantitative evaluations.

The paper is structured into sections that review relevant literature, outline the methodology, present experimental data and results, and conclude with implications and future research directions.
