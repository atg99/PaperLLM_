The paper discusses the effectiveness of a DenseNet-121 architecture-based convolutional neural network (CNN) for detecting COVID-19 using a relatively large dataset of 2482 images, comprising 1252 COVID-19 and 1230 non-COVID-19 images. The study highlights that many existing approaches utilize smaller datasets, making fair performance comparisons challenging. Despite this, the DenseNet-121 model achieved an overall accuracy of 92%, with 95% sensitivity, 84% precision, 89% F1-score, and 89% G-Mean. 

The paper references various studies that employed different methodologies for COVID-19 detection, with some achieving over 95% accuracy using smaller datasets, such as the techniques by Ouchicha, Ammor and Narayan Das, Kumar, which reached 97.2% and 97.4% accuracy, respectively. In contrast, the DenseNet-121 model stands out for its ability to maintain high accuracy while utilizing a larger dataset, thus demonstrating its robustness and effectiveness in COVID-19 detection. The study also notes the importance of data augmentation in achieving better results compared to prior studies using similar datasets. Overall, the DenseNet-121 model is positioned as a superior alternative for COVID-19 image classification, showcasing its capabilities in both accuracy and computational efficiency.
