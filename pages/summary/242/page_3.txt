This paper discusses the application of DenseNet-based Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN) for the classification of medical images, particularly for COVID-19 detection. It highlights the advantages of DenseNet over other models, such as its ability to achieve accurate predictions with fewer parameters and its effectiveness in handling complex medical image data. The paper critiques existing studies, noting limitations such as the inability to perform binary classification and the reliance on small datasets for training.

Several recent studies utilizing deep learning techniques for COVID-19 detection through radiology images are reviewed. These include hierarchical image classification and fully automated models, which, while innovative, suffer from limitations in data quantity. The paper emphasizes the need for larger datasets to improve model performance and accuracy. Strategies such as data augmentation are suggested to enhance training processes.

The DenseNet architecture is specifically highlighted for its efficient parameter optimization and superior performance in image classification tasks. The paper details the structure of DenseNet-121, noting its layers and how it differs from other architectures like ResNet. It concludes that DenseNet-based CNNs represent a promising diagnostic tool for identifying COVID-19 cases using CT images, advocating for further exploration of new architectures to enhance outcomes. The primary motivation for this study lies in leveraging a larger training dataset to improve the classification of COVID-19 using advanced CNN models.
