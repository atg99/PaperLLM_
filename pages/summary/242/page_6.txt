The paper details a model for identifying COVID-19 patients using a DenseNet-121 architecture. The study utilized a computer with 16 GB of primary memory and 4 GB NVIDIA GeForce 940MX graphics, running a 64-bit Windows operating system. The dataset was divided into training (70%), validation (10%), and testing (20%) sets, with the model trained over 50 epochs. Performance was evaluated using a confusion matrix, which enabled the calculation of accuracy and other metrics such as precision, recall, F1-measure, and G-Mean. 

While accuracy is defined as the ratio of correctly predicted instances (TP + TN) to total instances (TP + TN + FP + FN), the paper highlights its limitations, particularly in distinguishing performance across different classes, especially for the positive class. The model summary indicates a total of 7,305,622 parameters, with 7,219,414 being trainable. The layers include convolutional, batch normalization, dropout, and dense layers, culminating in a final output for classification into two categories. The DenseNet-121 code for COVID-19 detection is publicly available, providing transparency and reproducibility for further research.
