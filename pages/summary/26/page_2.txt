The study aimed to assess the effectiveness of deep convolutional neural networks (CNNs) in identifying and categorizing the severity of alveolar bone loss in periodontally compromised incisor teeth. The research highlighted the limitations of traditional diagnostic methods, such as radiographs and clinical assessments, in accurately detecting bone loss severity. It emphasized the potential of AI, particularly CNNs, to improve diagnostic accuracy by analyzing complex patterns in dental images.

A dataset comprising 1724 periapical images from adult patients with periodontitis was utilized. Rigorous criteria were applied to exclude low-quality images and ensure the reliability of the data. The images underwent annotation by trained examiners to classify the severity of bone loss according to established periodontal classification standards.

The methodology involved using a combination of transfer learning and CNN architecture, specifically the VGG-16 model, to classify the images into healthy or diseased categories, as well as into multiple severity levels. The dataset was appropriately divided for training, validation, and testing purposes, ensuring robust model evaluation.

In conclusion, this study presents a promising approach to enhancing the diagnosis of periodontal disease through advanced AI techniques, potentially leading to improved patient outcomes in dental health management.
