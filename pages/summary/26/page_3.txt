The paper by Alotaibi et al. discusses the use of transfer learning to classify dental radiographic images for periodontal treatment. The proposed model features 13 convolutional layers and 2 dense layers, with the dense layer containing 256 neurons for multi-class classification and 2 neurons for binary classification. To mitigate overfitting, a dropout layer was implemented, randomly dropping 50% of the neurons. The model was trained using the RMSprop optimizer and categorical cross-entropy loss over 100 epochs with a batch size of 16.

The dataset comprised 1724 periapical radiographic images, categorized into binary (healthy vs. disease) and multi-class classifications (normal bone and varying levels of bone loss). The training, validation, and test datasets were divided accordingly, with 70% of images used for training. Performance metrics, including accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, Matthews Correlation Coefficient (MCC), and Cohen Kappa, were employed to evaluate the CNN model. The results indicated that 47.21% of the images were classified as normal, while 52.78% were classified as non-normal, with varying degrees of bone loss.

The model achieved an accuracy of 73.04% for binary classification and 59.42% for multi-class classification, highlighting the effectiveness of the CNN in detecting periodontal conditions while suggesting the need for further improvements in accuracy and speed for radiology AI applications.
