The study by Alotaibi et al. evaluates the efficacy of machine learning (ML) models in classifying alveolar bone levels using binary and multi-classification approaches. A significant difference in accuracy was found between the two methods (p = 0.037). The binary classification achieved a total diagnostic accuracy of 73.04%, with the highest accuracy for detecting alveolar bone loss (72%) and the lowest for its absence (59%). In contrast, the multi-classification method had a lower overall accuracy of 59.42%, with the highest accuracy for normal alveolar bone and decreasing accuracy for varying degrees of bone loss.

The performance of the ML models was assessed through precision, recall, and F1-scores. The binary classifier demonstrated strong performance, with scores above 70%, indicating effective classification of alveolar bone presence and absence. The multi-classification showed more variability, with precision ranging from 45% to 83%, and the lowest sensitivity and F1 score observed for mild bone loss (0.45). Normal alveolar bone levels recorded the highest F1 score of 0.70.

The results are summarized in tables detailing the accuracy of validation and test for each class in both classification models, alongside statistical evaluations highlighting the precision, recall, and F1-scores for each classification category. Overall, the binary classification model outperformed the multi-classification model, suggesting its superior ability to classify the presence or absence of alveolar bone loss effectively.
