In this study by Alotaibi et al., the diagnostic performance of a convolutional neural network (CNN) model, specifically VGG-16, was evaluated for detecting periodontal bone loss and classifying alveolar bone levels in teeth affected by periodontal disease. The Matthews correlation coefficient (MCC) indicated moderate correlation between predicted and true classes, with values of 0.51 for binary and 0.65 for multi-classification. Cohen's Kappa showed moderate agreement (k = 0.512) for presence/absence of bone loss and fair agreement (k = 0.41) for multi-classification.

The sensitivity and specificity of the machine learning model were reported as 73% and 79.1%, respectively, using periodontists' assessments as the gold standard. The model achieved a diagnostic accuracy of 73.04% in detecting alveolar bone loss in anterior teeth, with a multi-classification accuracy of 59.42%. The study highlighted the model's ability to categorize bone loss severity based on established classifications.

Compared to previous studies, the findings align with other deep learning models that demonstrated high diagnostic accuracy for periodontal conditions. The study emphasized the advantages of using periapical radiographs, avoiding exclusions of caries or restorations to reflect real-life scenarios. The results suggested that the CNN model could streamline the detection and categorization of bone loss, potentially reducing the time clinicians spend on manual assessments.

While the deep CNN showed promising results in differentiating between normal and lost bone, it struggled with accurately categorizing severe bone loss. Future research is recommended to enhance the model's performance through advanced techniques and larger datasets. Overall, the study concludes that the CNN model has good potential in assisting dental professionals with diagnosing periodontal bone loss efficiently.
