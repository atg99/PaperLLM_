The study by Alotaibi et al. focuses on developing a computer-assisted detection system using a deep convolutional neural network (CNN) to assess periodontal bone loss through periapical radiographs. The research involved a dataset of 1724 images from 1610 adult patients, sourced from the ROMEXIS software. The CNN algorithm was optimized using a combination of pre-trained models and self-training methods.

The results indicated that the diagnostic accuracy for distinguishing between normal and diseased conditions was 73.0%, while the accuracy for classifying the severity of bone loss was 59%. The study employed various metrics, including precision, recall, and the Matthews Correlation Coefficient, to evaluate the model's performance, revealing significant differences in outcomes between binary and multi-classification approaches.

In conclusion, the VGG-16 deep CNN algorithm demonstrated effectiveness in detecting alveolar bone loss and categorizing its severity in periapical radiographs. The findings suggest that further optimization of the dataset could enhance the potential of computer-aided detection systems in diagnosing and staging periodontal disease, thereby improving clinical outcomes in dental health.
