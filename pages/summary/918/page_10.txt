In this paper, the authors evaluate their algorithm using three publicly available datasets: CUB-200-2011, Cars 196, and Stanford Online Products. They follow a conventional splitting procedure for training and testing, utilizing a fixed number of classes and samples for each chosen class. 

The CUB-200-2011 dataset comprises 200 bird species with a total of 11,788 images, where the first 100 species are designated for training, and the remaining for testing. The Cars 196 dataset includes 16,185 images across 196 classes, similarly split into 98 classes for training and testing. The Stanford Online Products dataset features 22,634 classes with 120,053 images, again split evenly for training and testing.

For performance evaluation, the authors focus on retrieval performance and clustering quality, particularly examining unseen classes. They employ Recall@K as the primary metric for retrieval, measuring the percentage of test examples whose K-nearest neighbors include at least one example from the same class. Clustering is assessed using the K-means algorithm and Normalized Mutual Information (NMI) to ensure label permutation invariance.

The results demonstrate that their model performs competitively compared to state-of-the-art methods, including different loss functions like triplet loss. They introduce a simple ensemble version of their model, combining multiple independent neural networks to enhance performance. This ensemble approach yields improved clustering and retrieval results compared to existing literature, particularly when optimized for performance. The paper primarily presents results using the Inception network.
