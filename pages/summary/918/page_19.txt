To address this, we propose an efficient way to compute and update the similarity matrix, ensuring that the computational burden remains manageable even with a large number of classes. 

Our comparative analysis between shifting and clamping methods for handling negative outputs reveals that clamping significantly outperforms shifting, particularly in datasets with a higher number of negative similarity values. The CUBS-200-2011 dataset demonstrated a notable improvement in the Recall@1 metric when using clamping, validating our hypothesis that this approach is more effective in mitigating the influence of negative similarities.

Furthermore, while our method primarily assumes the presence of absolute labels, it can adapt to scenarios with relative labels. This flexibility allows for the initialization of the similarity matrix using relative labels, which could enhance the model's performance. Our findings indicate that the proposed Group Loss method is robust in various contexts, including those with a large number of classes and when utilizing relative labels, thereby advancing deep metric learning methodologies.
