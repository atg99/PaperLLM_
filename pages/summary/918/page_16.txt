The paper discusses the implicit regularization of a proposed method that does not necessitate strong regularization. It introduces temperature scaling as a technique to calibrate the softmax layer of neural networks, enhancing the prior information for a dynamical system. This calibration, achieved by adjusting the entropy of the softmax distribution, facilitates more effective prediction adjustments. The implementation involves a straightforward equation that divides prediction logits by a constant temperature. The authors note that recent studies indicate temperature calibration improves accuracy in various learning tasks, and they report similar enhancements in metric learning, achieving significant improvements in Recall@1 scores on CUB-200-2011 and Cars 196 datasets.

Additionally, the authors extend their experiments beyond the GoogleNet backbone, exploring Densenet architectures to demonstrate the versatility of their loss formulation. They aim to assess the performance across different network capacities and the transferability of hyperparameters. The results indicate that using Densenet backbones, while maintaining the same hyperparameters from the GoogleNet experiments, yields state-of-the-art results on CARS 196 and Stanford Online Products datasets. This showcases the efficacy of their method across various architectures.
