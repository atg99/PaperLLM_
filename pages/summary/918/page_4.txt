This paper discusses advancements in metric learning, particularly focusing on loss functions and sampling strategies for improving clustering and ranking tasks. Initial work on triplet loss has evolved, with various modifications proposed to enhance performance. Notable contributions include a structured prediction metric aimed at optimizing clustering quality and the introduction of an angular loss that considers angular relationships.

The authors highlight the challenge posed by the vast number of possible triplets in datasets, leading to the exploration of sampling methods. Semi-hard negative mining was identified as a way to train networks effectively, though it is computationally intensive. The paper emphasizes the significance of selecting informative training examples, with distance-weighted sampling and hierarchical triplet loss being notable approaches that enhance performance.

The discussion also acknowledges related research areas, such as person re-identification and landmark recognition, where global structure in mini-batches is increasingly being considered. The paper references innovative methods in these domains, including new hashing methods and ranking loss functions tailored for specific tasks.

Lastly, the authors explore classification-based losses, noting the work that optimizes triplet loss in a proxy space, which closely resembles softmax cross-entropy loss. This challenges the notion that classification losses are unsuitable for metric learning, suggesting that a well-tuned normalized softmax loss combined with balanced sampling can yield significant improvements. Overall, the paper presents a comprehensive overview of ongoing developments in metric learning, particularly regarding triplet loss and its various adaptations.
