The paper presents a robustness analysis of the Cars 196 dataset, paralleling findings from the CUB-200-2011 dataset. A grid search was conducted to evaluate the effect of varying the number of elements per class (from 5 to 10) and the number of anchors (from 0 to 4). Results indicated a decline in performance, particularly when no labeled samples (zero anchors) were used. The performance of the method showed similar robustness across both datasets, with a marginal improvement of 2 percentage points at the Recall@1 metric.

Additionally, the implementation details reveal that all networks were pre-trained for 10 epochs on a classification task, followed by 60 epochs of training across multiple datasets. A learning rate schedule was employed, reducing the learning rate after the first 30 epochs. Hyperparameters were determined via random search, with specific values for weight decay and learning rate established for optimal performance on each dataset. Notably, the best regularization parameters were found to be 10^(-6) for CUB-200-2011, 10^(-7) for Cars 196, and 10^(-12) for Stanford Online Products. The features were normalized using the L2-norm during inference.
