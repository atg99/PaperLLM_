The paper discusses the computational challenges associated with calculating softmax probabilities in scenarios with a growing number of classes, as the complexity increases linearly with the number of classes. To address this, the authors propose a matrix sparsification technique, where only a small number of classes are considered in each mini-batch. This allows for setting non-relevant entries to zero, facilitating both storage efficiency and effective tensor-tensor multiplication. The authors note that while retrieval tasks generally involve fewer classes, fields like face recognition may have millions of classes, making this approach particularly useful.

Additionally, the paper presents a visualization of embedding vectors using t-distributed stochastic neighbor embedding (t-SNE) on the CUB-200-2011 dataset. The resulting plot illustrates how the proposed method successfully creates a compact feature mapping that maintains semantic similarity, despite variations in pose and appearance. The visualization is best appreciated on high-resolution displays when zoomed in, highlighting the effectiveness of the method in clustering similar features.
