The paper presents the Group Loss algorithm for deep metric learning, aimed at enhancing image retrieval and clustering performance. The algorithm processes a mini-batch of images alongside their corresponding labels using a neural network with learnable parameters. It calculates feature embeddings through a forward pass and constructs a similarity matrix based on a defined similarity function. An iterative procedure is employed to update a matrix of priors derived from the softmax layer, combining sample similarity and local information to optimize the embeddings of samples within the same class.

During inference, the method efficiently retrieves images by computing embeddings through a forward pass without the iterative procedure, ensuring speed comparable to other methods. The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of Group Loss through experiments on three public benchmark datasets, achieving state-of-the-art results in both image retrieval and clustering tasks.

For implementation, the authors utilize PyTorch and GoogleNet as the backbone network, pre-trained on the ILSVRC 2012-CLS dataset. They detail their preprocessing steps, network training procedures, and hyperparameter optimization strategies. The training includes a classification task followed by the Group Loss task, with specific adjustments made to the learning rate and mini-batch sizes to enhance performance.
