The paper discusses a refinement procedure for deep metric learning, particularly focusing on the classification of an unlabeled sample based on its similarity to other samples. The example illustrates how the affinity matrix is utilized to update soft assignments for classifying sample C in relation to samples A and B. Initially, the affinity matrix reflects the probability of class membership for each sample.

The algorithm begins with an initial assignment matrix, denoted as X(0), and iteratively refines it using an update rule. This rule adjusts the soft assignment of the unlabeled sample by weighing the contributions from similar images, ensuring that the updated matrix remains normalized. The update process follows the principles of multi-population replicator dynamics from evolutionary game theory and is conceptually similar to non-linear relaxation labeling processes.

The update rule is mathematically expressed, indicating how the soft assignments evolve over iterations. The normalization factor ensures that the rows of the updated matrix sum to one, maintaining the integrity of the classification probabilities. Overall, the method aims to enhance the accuracy of deep metric learning by leveraging the relationships between samples in a structured manner.
