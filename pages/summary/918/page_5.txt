The paper introduces a novel approach to deep metric learning through the proposed Group Loss, which aims to improve classification accuracy without increasing model parameters. Traditional methods often rely on embedding distances rather than classification loss functions, which can lead to scenarios where samples of the same class are not closely embedded, even if they are correctly classified. The authors argue that classification loss can be effectively integrated into deep metric learning by considering the relationships among samples in a mini-batch collectively rather than in isolation.

Group Loss operates by utilizing global information from the entire mini-batch to refine the local outputs of the softmax layer in a neural network. This method categorizes samples into groups and ensures consistent labeling within those groups, thus promoting similar embeddings for images belonging to the same class. Unlike the softmax cross-entropy loss, which evaluates each sample independently, Group Loss assesses the overall class separation for all samples in the mini-batch.

The proposed method begins with an initialization phase, where an image-label assignment matrix is created based on the softmax outputs of the neural network. A pairwise similarity matrix is then computed using the embeddings generated by the network. The subsequent sections of the paper will detail the mathematical properties of Group Loss and compare its effectiveness to traditional loss functions.
