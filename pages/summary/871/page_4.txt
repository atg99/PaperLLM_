The paper presents a novel deep learning framework known as the Dual Self-Attention Network (DSANet) for multivariate time series forecasting. The evaluation of various forecasting methods is summarized in Table 1, where results for different window-horizon pairs are reported using two metrics: Relative Root Squared Error (RRSE) and Mean Absolute Error (MAE). The findings indicate a consistent trend where performance metrics decline as the window-horizon increases, highlighting the increased difficulty of forecasting over longer horizons.

Among the methods evaluated, deep learning approaches such as GRU, LSTNet-S, LSTNet-A, TPA, and DSANet generally outperform traditional methods. Notably, DSANet consistently showcases superior performance across all cases, suggesting its robustness in handling complex and dynamic patterns in multivariate time series data.

An ablation study is conducted to assess the effectiveness of each component within the DSANet architecture. Results demonstrate that the complete DSANet outperforms its variants (DSAwoGlobal, DSAwoLocal, and DSAwoAR), indicating that all components contribute significantly to the model's overall performance. The removal of the autoregressive (AR) component leads to the most substantial drop in performance, underscoring its importance in managing data scale variations, while the other branches also show diminished performance but to a lesser extent.

In conclusion, the study emphasizes the potential of DSANet for multivariate time series forecasting, particularly for datasets exhibiting dynamic or nonperiodic patterns, with experiments on a large real-world dataset yielding promising results.
