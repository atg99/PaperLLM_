The paper presents a comparative analysis of different rendering models, specifically focusing on SoftRas and PyTorch3D, in the context of 3D shape reconstruction. The experiments utilize a Sphere FC model to deform a sphere with 642 vertices and 1280 faces, rendering at a resolution of 64 by 64. While PyTorch3D exhibits superior performance in this setting, both models yield unsatisfactory absolute performance, indicated by their respective Chamfer distances of 1.475 for SoftRas and 0.989 for PyTorch3D. Increasing the rendering resolution to 128 by 128 significantly enhances performance, with SoftRas achieving a Chamfer distance of 0.346 and PyTorch3D 0.313.

The Sphere GCN, a more geometry-aware architecture, consistently outperforms both SoftRas and PyTorch3D across all resolutions, achieving a Chamfer distance of 0.301 with SoftRas and 0.293 with PyTorch3D. This model effectively captures instance-specific details, such as chair legs and tables, which Sphere FC struggles with. The authors leverage PyTorch3D’s scale efficiency by employing a larger sphere, yielding an improved reconstruction quality (Chamfer 0.281).

However, both Sphere FC and Sphere GCN are limited to predictions homeomorphic to spheres, resulting in face intersections and irregular shapes to accommodate complex topologies. In contrast, the Voxel GCN variant demonstrates improved reconstruction quality by refining shape topologies and effectively capturing holes and complex shapes while maintaining regular face shapes. Voxel GCN outperforms the Voxel Only baseline, achieving a Chamfer distance of 0.267 compared to 0.916.

The paper also discusses additional mesh predictions through silhouette and textured rendering, noting that their simple texture model performs well despite its basic nature. Textures are more accurate for Voxel GCN due to regular-shaped faces, while Sphere GCN suffers from texture inaccuracies due to face intersections. The authors explore various shading techniques, finding that sophisticated methods like Phong and Gouraud yield better texture reconstructions.

In the section on unsupervised point cloud prediction, the authors elaborate on their Point Align model, which utilizes a random initial point cloud and incorporates features from a backbone network. The model is trained using silhouette rendering and L1 loss for textured rendering, without shape regularizers. The evaluation shows that the unsupervised point cloud model achieves slightly better reconstruction quality than mesh models, with a Chamfer distance of 0.272 for Point Align versus 0.281 for High Res Sphere GCN, indicating the effectiveness of their point cloud renderer. Additional visualizations of shape and texture predictions are provided to support their findings.
