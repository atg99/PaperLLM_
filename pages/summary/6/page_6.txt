The paper discusses the development of a differentiable point cloud renderer within the PyTorch3D framework, designed to efficiently handle both homogeneous and heterogeneous batches of point clouds. This renderer is modular, consisting of a rasterizer that identifies the K-nearest points to each pixel and shaders implemented in PyTorch for pixel color computation. The renderer supports various blending methods, specifically Alpha-compositing and Normalized weighted sums, which are differentiable and allow for gradient propagation back to point features and opacities.

The blending methods utilize the depth ordering of points, with Alpha-compositing giving more weight to nearer points, while Norm treats all points equally regardless of depth. The paper presents benchmarks demonstrating the renderer's efficiency, noting that rendering a batch of point clouds with 200,000 points each takes approximately 75 milliseconds and uses just over 1GB of GPU memory. The results indicate that rendering performance is comparable for homogeneous and heterogeneous batches, with the Normalized approach being faster for larger K values due to reduced computational complexity. Overall, the point cloud renderer is positioned as a viable differentiable layer for training neural networks, offering advantages over traditional mesh rendering in terms of computational efficiency.
