The paper presents a system for unsupervised shape prediction, detailing both the training and inference processes. During training, a two-view setup is utilized where an additional view with known rotation and translation is assumed. The predicted shape is transformed accordingly, and its rendered silhouette is compared to the ground truth silhouette. For inference, the system operates on a single RGB image to output the predicted shape in camera coordinates.

The authors explore various 3D mesh network architectures, including Sphere FC, Sphere GCN, and Voxel GCN, in their experiments. The optimization objective is defined as L = Ls + λlLl + λeLe, where Ls represents the negative intersection over union between the rendered and ground truth silhouettes. The silhouette loss is computed using a formula that incorporates predicted and actual silhouettes. Additionally, shape regularizers are applied to ensure smoothness in the predicted shapes, including an edge length regularizer and a Laplacian regularizer.

The paper compares the performance of different shape networks using PyTorch3D and SoftRas across varying rendering resolutions, highlighting the efficiency of their approach. The loss calculation method is noted to differ from that of SoftRas, as the authors average the loss across vertices and batch elements rather than summing them.
