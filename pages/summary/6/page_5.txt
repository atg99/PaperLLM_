The paper discusses advancements in rendering techniques using PyTorch3D and SoftRas, focusing on silhouette and textured rendering benchmarks. It highlights the modular design of shaders, which are implemented separately from the rasterizer, enhancing efficiency and enabling the reuse of cached Fragment data. This modularity allows for the easy implementation of new shaders while leveraging PyTorch's tensor operations for gradient computation through autograd.

Two algorithms are presented: one for silhouette blending and another for softmax blending. These algorithms utilize signed distance tensors and other Fragment data to compute pixel values, showcasing a simplified approach compared to previous methods that relied on monolithic CUDA kernels.

The performance evaluation demonstrates that the proposed renderer significantly outperforms SoftRas, particularly for large, heterogeneous mesh batches and higher resolutions. While the new renderer requires more GPU memory due to the explicit storage of Fragment data, its memory usage remains manageable relative to modern GPU capacities. The authors argue that the benefits of improved modularity and speed justify the increased memory demand.
