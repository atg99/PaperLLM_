In this paper, the authors introduce PyTorch3D, a differentiable library designed for 3D research that integrates seamlessly into deep learning frameworks. It is modular, efficient, and capable of handling large datasets with varying shapes, thereby facilitating rapid experimentation and extension. PyTorch3D aims to serve as a foundational tool for 3D deep learning, similar to existing frameworks like PyTorch and TensorFlow, and is committed to supporting the research community while addressing potential impacts of data-driven models in real-world applications.

The appendix details the methodology for sampling batches of 3D data from ShapeNetCoreV1 for benchmarking purposes. It describes the process for creating both homogeneous and heterogeneous batches for meshes and point clouds, ensuring a uniform sampling strategy.

In the experiments section, the authors explore unsupervised 3D shape prediction from a single image using PyTorch3D renderers. They acknowledge the inherent ambiguity in predicting 3D shapes from 2D images and propose a solution involving two views of the object during training to resolve this ambiguity. Various architectures for the 3D shape network are tested, including Sphere FC, Sphere GCN, and Voxel GCN, with a focus on differentiable rendering and the ability to predict diverse shapes without direct supervision. The paper emphasizes the versatility of PyTorch3D in advancing research at the intersection of 3D modeling and deep learning.
