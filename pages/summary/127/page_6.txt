The paper presents ControlVAE, a controllable variational autoencoder designed to address the issue of KL vanishing in text generation. It compares ControlVAE with existing methods such as cost annealing and cyclical annealing on the Penn Treebank (PTB) and Switchboard (SW) datasets. 

Figures in the paper demonstrate that ControlVAE effectively stabilizes KL divergence during training while maintaining lower reconstruction loss compared to other methods. Specifically, as shown in Figure 3(a), ControlVAE successfully avoids KL vanishing, in contrast to cost annealing, which suffers from this issue even after extensive training. The ability of ControlVAE to maintain a controlled KL divergence allows it to produce more diverse outputs, as indicated by the results in Table 1.

The evaluation metrics used include Dis-n, self-BLEU, and perplexity (PPL), where ControlVAE consistently outperforms the baselines in generating distinct and high-quality dialog responses. The results reveal that with specific KL-divergence values set to 35 and 25, ControlVAE yields higher diversity and lower perplexity, indicating better performance in text generation tasks.

Overall, the findings suggest that ControlVAE is a robust approach for enhancing the diversity and effectiveness of generated text, making it a valuable contribution to the field of language modeling. The paper includes detailed model configurations and hyperparameter settings in the appendix for further reference.
