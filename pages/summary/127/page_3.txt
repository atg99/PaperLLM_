The paper discusses the ControlV AE, a controllable Variational Autoencoder designed to manage the balance between reconstruction accuracy and KL-divergence during training. It begins with an overview of Variational Autoencoders (V AEs), which are generative models that encode input data into a latent representation and then reconstruct the original data from this representation. The objective of V AEs is to maximize the marginal likelihood of the reconstructed data, but they face challenges related to intractable posterior inference and KL-divergence control.

The paper introduces the concept of the β-VAE, an extension of the basic V AE that enhances disentangled representation learning by introducing a hyperparameter β to weight the KL-divergence term in the loss function. While β-VAEs improve disentangling, they can compromise reconstruction quality if β is set too high.

To address the challenge of balancing reconstruction accuracy and KL-divergence, the authors propose ControlV AE, which employs a nonlinear PI controller to dynamically adjust the hyperparameter β during training. This controller monitors the sampled KL-divergence and compares it to a predefined set point, adjusting β based on the difference. The intention is to maintain KL-divergence around the set point while optimizing the model's performance.

The ControlV AE framework is formalized through a variational lower bound that incorporates the adjusted hyperparameter, enabling better control over the trade-off between reconstruction quality and KL-divergence. The paper highlights the algorithm's potential to improve the performance of V AEs in applications requiring controlled latent representations.
