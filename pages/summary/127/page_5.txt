The paper introduces ControlV AE, a Controllable Variational Autoencoder that employs a Proportional-Integral (PI) algorithm to manage the KL-divergence during training. The algorithm is structured to adjust the hyperparameter at each training step based on the difference between the desired and sampled KL-divergence. The PI controller includes mechanisms to prevent integral windup, ensuring that the output hyperparameter remains within specified minimum and maximum bounds.

The authors demonstrate the versatility of ControlV AE across three applications: language modeling, disentangling, and image generation. In language modeling, ControlV AE addresses the KL vanishing problem by maintaining the KL-divergence at a specified level, enhancing data diversity. For disentangling, the model dynamically adjusts the hyperparameter to balance reconstruction quality with the control of KL-divergence, thus improving performance over static approaches. In image generation, the model slightly increases the KL-divergence to enhance reconstruction quality, showcasing its adaptability.

The experiments leverage benchmark datasets for each application, including the Penn Tree Bank and Switchboard for language modeling, 2D Shapes for disentangling, and CelebA for image generation, demonstrating the efficacy of ControlV AE in diverse scenarios.
