This paper explores the training of Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) with a focus on improving decoder calibration through the choice of decoding distributions. The authors emphasize the importance of accurately representing the probability distribution p(x|z) and propose that a calibrated decoder leads to better performance.

The analysis begins with the commonly used Gaussian decoders, highlighting the limitations of mean squared error (MSE) reconstruction loss, which assumes a constant variance in the Gaussian distribution. This assumption can lead to suboptimal performance and poor calibration. Instead, the authors advocate for learning the variance as a dynamic parameter, suggesting a diagonal covariance matrix for pixel-wise variance or a simpler approach using a single shared variance parameter across the entire image.

The proposed model is referred to as the Gaussian ϵ-VAE, where the decoding likelihood incorporates this learned variance, allowing for a more nuanced interpretation of uncertainty. The full objective function includes terms for both the MSE loss and Kullback-Leibler divergence, with the variance parameter acting as a weighting factor. This approach enhances the calibration of the decoder, aligning predicted probabilities with observed frequencies.

The paper also draws connections to previous work on the α-VAE, showing how it can be viewed as a special case of the proposed method where the variance is constant. The authors argue that by incorporating the learned variance, they can achieve a more effective and calibrated decoder, ultimately improving the quality of VAE-generated outputs.

In conclusion, the paper presents a framework for training VAEs with calibrated decoders, demonstrating the significance of learning variance for better uncertainty estimation and enhanced model performance.
