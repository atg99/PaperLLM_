The paper discusses the performance of a novel Variational Autoencoder (VAE) architecture, referred to as the calibrated VAE, in generative modeling across various datasets. The results presented in Table 2 illustrate that uncalibrated decoders, such as mean-only Gaussian, yield poor performance compared to calibrated options like categorical or the proposed VAE, which demonstrate superior results in terms of log probability and Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) metrics.

The authors highlight the significance of hyperparameter tuning in traditional VAEs, particularly in controlling mutual information and the match between variational and prior distributions. The calibrated VAE automatically adjusts the necessary amount of information, leading to informative latent representations without sacrificing sample quality. This capability allows it to maintain a close match between the marginal and prior distributions, enhancing the visual quality of generated samples.

A critical issue identified is the common challenges faced by practitioners in learning decoder variances. The paper outlines that naive approaches to learning variances often result in numerical instability, causing rapid divergence and suboptimal performance. Instead, employing shared variances proves to be more stable and effective. The authors argue that per-pixel variances, although they can improve ELBO values, lead to poor sample quality due to an overemphasis on specific pixels rather than a balanced focus across the image. This observation aligns with findings from prior research indicating that likelihood-based models may concentrate excessively on minor details, resulting in degraded outcomes.

The paper concludes that the choice of variance learning strategy significantly impacts VAE performance, with shared and per-image variances generally providing better results compared to per-pixel variances, which are prone to instability and lower visual quality in generated samples.
