The paper presents a novel approach to training Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) using calibrated decoders, which automatically control mutual information between input and latent variables. The proposed method, termed ϵ-VAE, optimizes the decoder variance analytically without requiring it to be an additional output of the decoder network. This approach simplifies implementation and extends the capabilities of VAEs, outperforming traditional gradient descent methods for variance learning in various experiments across VAE and sequence VAE models using diverse image and video datasets.

The paper also reviews related work on VAE decoder parameterizations, highlighting challenges with continuous distributions such as instability in variance estimates. Techniques like dequantization and optimizing variance through two-stage procedures have been explored, but the authors find that learning a single shared variance parameter yields stable training and effective performance without necessitating these complex methods. 

Additionally, the authors discuss the prevalence of uncalibrated decoders in various applications outside the core generative modeling community and critique the reliance on heuristic hyperparameters that complicate tuning and diminish likelihood bounds. They emphasize the importance of calibrated decoders and introduce an analytic method for learning calibrated distributions, providing a comprehensive evaluation of different decoding distributions.

The analysis focuses on Gaussian decoders with constant variance, illustrating how the hyperparameter controlling information in the latent variable can be effectively integrated into the decoding likelihood for improved VAE performance.
