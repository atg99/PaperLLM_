They present a counterexample demonstrating that a conditional shift—where class-conditional distributions of input features differ between the source and target domains—can lead to poor performance despite having aligned representations.

To provide a theoretical foundation, the authors propose a generalization upper bound that accounts for this conditional shift, offering a sufficient condition for effective domain adaptation. They also introduce an information-theoretic lower bound on the joint error of any domain adaptation method that seeks to learn invariant representations, illustrating a fundamental trade-off between the two objectives when the marginal label distributions differ.

The paper concludes with experiments on real-world datasets that support their theoretical findings, suggesting that their insights can inform future developments in domain adaptation and representation learning algorithms. Overall, the authors challenge existing beliefs about the sufficiency of invariant representations and source error minimization for ensuring target domain performance.
