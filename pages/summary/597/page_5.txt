The paper discusses the challenges of domain adaptation in machine learning, particularly focusing on learning invariant representations. It highlights an example where an error is made in one domain, leading to a relationship between source and target errors under a transformation. Specifically, when a transformation is applied, it is shown that every hypothesis incurs a joint error of 1, which contrasts with the optimal error of 0 when no transformation is applied. This situation raises questions about the generalization upper bound established in previous theorems.

The authors propose that the discrepancy between the labeling functions of the source and target domains is a crucial factor in understanding this apparent contradiction. They introduce a new generalization upper bound that aims to be more interpretable and less conservative than previous bounds by eliminating the pessimistic terms. This new bound incorporates three main components: the source error, the discrepancy between marginal distributions, and the distance between labeling functions of the two domains.

Key lemmas supporting this framework include the H-divergence measure, which quantifies the difference between distributions, and the triangular inequality, which facilitates the analysis of errors. The paper concludes by comparing their new bound to an earlier theorem, emphasizing that their approach reflects the underlying structure of the problem better by focusing on the conditional shift rather than relying on the choice of hypothesis class. Overall, the findings aim to provide a clearer understanding of the necessary conditions for effective domain adaptation.
