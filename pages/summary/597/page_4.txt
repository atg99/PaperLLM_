The paper discusses the challenges of achieving domain adaptation through invariant representation learning. It highlights the use of neural networks to approximate the H-divergence between source and target domains, framing the process as a two-player game where one network learns to generate features that deceive another network tasked with distinguishing between the two domains. The authors reference concurrent works that emphasize the limitations of merely learning invariant representations, noting that such representations do not guarantee effective generalization, particularly when there are significant differences in label distributions across domains.

The theoretical analysis reveals that finding invariant representations alone is insufficient for successful domain adaptation. A counterexample illustrates this point: a constant transformation can yield invariant representations while failing to retain necessary discriminative information, leading to poor performance on the target domain. The authors further explore the relationship between small source error and target error, demonstrating through another counterexample that both small source risk and invariant representations do not ensure small target error.

To address these findings, the paper proposes a generalization upper bound that provides a sufficient condition for successful domain adaptation. This is complemented by an information-theoretic lower bound on the joint error of any approach relying on invariant representation learning. The authors conclude that while invariant representations are valuable, they must be accompanied by additional considerations to ensure effective domain adaptation.
