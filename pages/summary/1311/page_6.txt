The paper discusses enhancements in object detection performance through modifications to a single-stage detection system, resulting in a state-of-the-art mean Average Precision (mAP) of 50.7 on the COCO dataset. Key improvements include increasing the number of detection anchors and combining the top four learned sub-policies, leading to a total of 20 sub-policies. Unlike prior models that necessitated multiple evaluations of the same image, this approach only requires a single pass, thus simplifying the process.

The study also explores the transferability of learned augmentation policies to different datasets, specifically applying them to a Faster R-CNN model with a ResNet-101 backbone on the PASCAL VOC dataset. The implementation of the augmentation policy resulted in a 2.7% increase in mAP50 without altering the training setup.

Moreover, the paper examines the effect of dataset size on the efficacy of learned augmentation policies. Experiments with varying amounts of training data (5,000 to 23,000 images) show that the augmentation policy significantly boosts performance, especially with smaller datasets. For instance, models trained on 5,000 samples exhibited over a 70% relative improvement in mAP. The augmentation policy proved particularly effective for detecting smaller objects, outperforming the benefits of simply increasing the dataset size. Additionally, models utilizing the learned augmentation demonstrated superior performance on the more challenging AP75 metric, indicating its effectiveness in enhancing bounding box alignment.
