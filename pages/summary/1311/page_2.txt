This paper presents a novel approach to data augmentation specifically designed for object detection tasks. It introduces a set of simple transformations applied during training to enhance the performance of detection models across various datasets and architectures. The transformations are categorized into three types: those that modify the entire image without altering bounding box locations, those that affect the whole image and change the bounding box locations, and those applied solely to the objects within the bounding boxes.

To effectively combine the numerous transformations, the authors implement a search method tailored for object detection datasets, optimizing the data augmentation policies. The results demonstrate significant improvements in accuracy across different detection architectures and datasets, surpassing state-of-the-art performance on the COCO dataset for single models and achieving competitive results on the PASCAL VOC dataset. The study emphasizes the advantages of learned augmentation strategies, particularly for smaller datasets, by providing regularization that helps prevent overfitting on small objects.

The paper also discusses related work in the field of data augmentation, noting the traditional methods used for classification tasks and highlighting the unique challenges posed by object detection due to the complexity of bounding box annotations. The authors aim to automate the search for optimal augmentation policies, considering the intricacies of maintaining consistency between bounding box locations and distorted images.

In terms of methodology, the authors frame the search for data augmentation as a discrete optimization problem, focusing on generalization performance. They define an augmentation policy as a set of sub-policies, with one being randomly selected and applied during training, incorporating various transformations tailored to the needs of object detection.
