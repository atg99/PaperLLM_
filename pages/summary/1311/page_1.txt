The paper "Learning Data Augmentation Strategies for Object Detection" by Barret Zoph and colleagues from Google Research explores the significance of data augmentation in enhancing the performance of object detection models. While data augmentation has been proven beneficial for image classification, its application to object detection is less understood, especially given the higher costs associated with annotating data for detection tasks.

The authors begin by assessing traditional data augmentation techniques from image classification, demonstrating that while these methods can be useful for training detection models, their impact is limited. Consequently, the research shifts focus to developing learned, specialized data augmentation policies that specifically target improvements in object detection generalization performance. Crucially, these policies only influence the training phase and do not alter the model during evaluation.

Experiments conducted on the COCO dataset reveal that an optimized data augmentation policy can enhance mean Average Precision (mAP) by over 2.3 points, achieving a state-of-the-art accuracy of 50.7 mAP with a single inference model. The study also illustrates that the best augmentation policy identified on the COCO dataset can be effectively transferred to other datasets, such as PASCAL-VOC, resulting in a 2.7 mAP improvement.

The findings emphasize the superiority of learned augmentation strategies over traditional architecture regularization methods for object detection, even when applied to strong baseline models. The authors provide access to the code for implementing the learned augmentation policy, highlighting its practical utility for future research and applications in object detection.
