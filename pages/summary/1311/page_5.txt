The most effective operation identified is Rotate, which benefits from enlarging bounding boxes to encompass all rotated objects. Other notable operations include Equalize, which adjusts pixel value distributions without altering bounding box dimensions, and BBox Only TranslateY, which shifts bounding boxes vertically.

In rigorous testing with the RetinaNet detector, the learned augmentation policy consistently yielded improvements in mean Average Precision (mAP) across various ResNet backbone architectures, achieving gains of 1.6 to 2.3 mAP compared to baseline methods. The study further breaks down the contributions of color, geometric, and bounding box-specific operations, highlighting that the combination of all three yields the most substantial performance enhancement.

Additionally, the policy proved adaptable, transferring effectively to different model architectures and detection algorithms, such as replacing ResNet-50 with AmoebaNet-D and transitioning from RetinaNet to NAS-FPN. This adaptability is evidenced by a 1.5% mAP improvement when employing the learned policy under varied training conditions and image sizes. The potential for further enhancements is noted with increased image resolution and batch size, indicating a promising area for future exploration in object detection methodologies.
