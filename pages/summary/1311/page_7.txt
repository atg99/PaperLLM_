This paper investigates the effectiveness of a learned data augmentation policy on object detection performance. It presents results demonstrating that this approach significantly enhances detection accuracy, particularly in scenarios with small datasets and small object detection tasks. The study reports improvements in mean average precision (mAP) across various configurations, notably achieving state-of-the-art results with a single inference computation at a single spatial scale, as opposed to previous methods requiring multiple scales.

Key findings include:

1. **Performance Metrics**: The incorporation of learned augmentation, along with adjustments to image size and anchor configurations, led to a peak mAP of 50.7 on the COCO validation set, surpassing previous benchmarks.

2. **Transferability**: The learned augmentation policy not only improved performance on the COCO dataset but also transferred effectively to other object detection tasks, as evidenced by enhanced mAP scores on the PASCAL VOC 2007 dataset using a Faster R-CNN detector.

3. **Dataset Size Impact**: The augmentation policy showed pronounced benefits, particularly for smaller training datasets, improving detection precision for small objects and contributing to a lower training loss.

4. **Regularization Effect**: The application of learned data augmentation resulted in a lower L2 norm of model weights, indicating effective regularization. This effect was more pronounced when larger datasets were used, suggesting that learned augmentation can serve as a powerful regularization technique.

5. **Comparison with Other Techniques**: The paper notes that traditional regularization methods like Input Mixup and Dropblock did not provide additional benefits when used alongside learned data augmentation, suggesting that this policy may independently enhance model performance without the need for supplementary regularization.

The authors conclude that learned data augmentation is a promising direction for improving object detection performance and outline plans for future research to expand on these findings.
