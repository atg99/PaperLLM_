The paper discusses the effectiveness of learned augmentation policies in improving model performance on tasks with limited training data, as evidenced by the percentage improvements in mean Average Precision (mAP), AP50, and AP75 compared to baseline augmentation methods. Figures presented illustrate the training loss trends, showing that models utilizing learned augmentation policies experience lower loss relative to those using baseline methods. Additionally, the study highlights the potential for transferring learned augmentation policies across different perceptual domains, such as semantic and instance segmentation, and point cloud data. The findings suggest that learned augmentation can serve as a cost-effective alternative to collecting additional human-annotated training data, as it leads to a reduction in the L2 norm of model weights with increasing training set sizes. The authors acknowledge contributions from team members and reference various foundational works in machine learning and data augmentation.
