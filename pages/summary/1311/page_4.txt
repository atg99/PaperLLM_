This paper presents a method for discovering a learned augmentation policy for object detection through a discrete optimization approach. The authors construct a search space consisting of five sub-policies, each containing two sequential operations applied to images. Each operation is defined by two hyperparameters: the probability of application and the magnitude of the operation, introducing stochasticity into the augmentation process.

Preliminary experiments identified 22 beneficial operations for object detection, categorized into three types: color operations that distort color channels without affecting bounding boxes, geometric operations that modify the image's geometry and consequently the bounding box annotations, and bounding box operations that only alter the contents within the bounding boxes. The operations are parameterized and discretized into uniformly spaced values for effective optimization.

The optimization problem is addressed using reinforcement learning, specifically employing a recurrent neural network (RNN) structured to predict augmentation policies. The RNN is trained using proximal policy optimization over a subset of 5,000 images from the COCO dataset, leading to computational efficiency while ensuring generalization to the full dataset.

The results demonstrate the effectiveness of the learned augmentation policies when applied to various datasets and configurations, highlighting the method's ability to adapt and perform well in limited data scenarios. Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of automating the search for augmentation strategies to enhance object detection performance.
