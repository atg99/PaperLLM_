The paper discusses a study on crop yield prediction using machine learning (ML) approaches, particularly focusing on the XGBoost method. The authors note that while XGBoost has delivered superior results in previous studies, it is essential to compare its performance against other methods such as Random Forest (RF) and K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN). The study evaluates the models using regression metrics including R-squared (R2), Mean Absolute Error (MAE), and Mean Squared Error (MSE), aiming for a comprehensive analysis that compares the results with prior research.

The research methodology consists of four main stages: dataset collection, preprocessing, XGBoost prediction, and evaluation. The datasets used are sourced from the Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO) and the World Bank, providing crucial information on rice harvest yields, pesticide usage, rainfall, and temperature. The FAO dataset includes features like production yield and pesticide usage, while the World Bank dataset includes average rainfall and temperature. The integration of these datasets is intended to enhance the predictive accuracy of the ML models.

In summary, the study aims to refine crop yield predictions by leveraging a combination of robust datasets and advanced ML techniques, thereby contributing valuable insights to agricultural forecasting.
