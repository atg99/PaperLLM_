The paper evaluates the performance of various regression models using 5-fold cross-validation on FAO and World Bank datasets, as well as on subsets of these datasets. The results are presented in three tables, highlighting metrics such as R2, Mean Absolute Error (MAE), and Mean Squared Error (MSE). 

Table 4 shows that the XGBoost model, particularly the tuned version, yields the best performance when using a combination of the FAO and World Bank datasets, demonstrating optimal values for R2, MAE, and MSE. This indicates that selecting appropriate features enhances model accuracy. However, Table 5 reveals a decline in performance across all models when only the FAO dataset is utilized, suggesting that this dataset lacks sufficient features for robust predictions. 

In Table 6, the removal of the pesticide feature from the combined datasets led to a decrease in prediction accuracy, despite its low correlation with other features. The paper argues that while pesticides may not show significant statistical correlation, they are crucial for maintaining crop quality, thus their absence negatively impacts predictions. 

Overall, the tuned XGBoost model consistently performs best across experiments, although it doesn't outperform the default XGBoost when only the FAO dataset is considered. The findings underscore the importance of feature selection and dataset combination in enhancing regression model performance.
