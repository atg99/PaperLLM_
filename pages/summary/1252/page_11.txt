The paper presents a study on rice yield prediction using the XGBoost model, which demonstrates superior performance compared to other methods evaluated, including Random Forest and K-Nearest Neighbors, as shown in Table 7. The XGBoost model achieved an R² value of 0.99, indicating excellent data variation explanation; however, it also recorded high values for Mean Absolute Error (MAE) and Mean Squared Error (MSE), suggesting significant individual prediction errors. This discrepancy indicates that while the model effectively captures general trends, it may struggle with precision in specific cases, potentially due to the dataset's scale and characteristics.

The research emphasizes the importance of combining FAO and World Bank datasets to enhance prediction accuracy and highlights the role of feature correlation analysis. Notably, the pesticide feature, despite showing low correlation, significantly impacts predictions due to its relevance in plant protection. The paper concludes that while the XGBoost model proves reliable through K-Fold cross-validation, further development is necessary to improve its prediction performance.
