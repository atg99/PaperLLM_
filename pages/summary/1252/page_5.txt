The paper discusses the use of XGBoost for predicting rice yields, highlighting key parameters such as n_estimators, learning_rate, and random_state. A higher n_estimators increases model complexity, while a lower learning_rate can slow down learning and affect generalization. The random_state parameter ensures repeatability in experiments. The study presents tuned parameter values, optimizing model performance for better predictions.

For evaluation, the research employs metrics like R2, MAE, and MSE to assess model accuracy. R2 measures the fit of the regression model, MAE provides an average of absolute prediction errors, and MSE emphasizes larger errors by squaring them. These metrics are essential for understanding model performance in regression tasks.

The results section includes feature analysis using data visualization, particularly a correlation heatmap. Notably, a strong negative correlation (-0.68) is found between rice yield and average temperature, indicating that rising temperatures may lead to decreased yields, which is significant in the context of climate change. Additionally, a moderate negative correlation between area size and pesticide use suggests efficiency in pesticide application, while the weak correlation between pesticide use and crop yield highlights a complex relationship. The study further explores the impact of excluding pesticide features from the analysis.
