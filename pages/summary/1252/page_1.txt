The research article presents a study on predicting rice production using the eXtreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost) method, which is essential for global food security as rice is a primary food source for nearly half of the world's population. The authors, Ella Budi Wijayanti, De Rosal Ignatius Moses Setiadi, and Bimo Haryo Setyoko, focus on utilizing datasets from the Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO) and the World Bank to enhance prediction accuracy.

The study emphasizes the importance of feature analysis, data cleaning, and parameter tuning to optimize the XGBoost model's performance. Results indicate an impressive R-squared value of 0.99, demonstrating the model's high predictive capability when compared to traditional regression methods such as Random Forest, Gradient Boosting, Bagging Regressor, and K-Nearest Neighbor.

Additionally, an ablation study was conducted to assess the impact of different features on model performance, confirming the superiority of the XGBoost method. The findings suggest that this model can significantly support agricultural sustainability and effective rice production forecasting. Overall, the research underscores the critical role of accurate production predictions in informing agricultural policies and enhancing food security globally.
