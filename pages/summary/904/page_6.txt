The paper discusses two geometric image data augmentation techniques: cropping and translation. 

Cropping, also referred to as zooming or scaling, involves magnifying an original image through two main operations. The first operation entails cutting the image from a specified start location (X, Y) to another (X, Y), with examples given of different cut sizes from a 200 by 200 pixel image. The second operation involves rescaling the cropped image back to its original dimensions. The scaling process is mathematically represented, emphasizing the new pixel coordinates after the operation.

Translation, as described in the paper, involves moving an object within the image to a new position. This technique may leave a portion of the image blank or filled with noise to preserve data integrity. Translation can occur in either the X or Y direction, or both simultaneously, which helps mitigate positional bias in the data. 

Overall, these techniques contribute to enhancing the diversity of image datasets, which is crucial for training robust machine learning models.
