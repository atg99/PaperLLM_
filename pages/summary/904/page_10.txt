The paper discusses various types of noise encountered in images, specifically Gaussian, Poisson, salt and pepper, and speckle noise. Gaussian noise is characterized by its probability density function (PDF), while Poisson noise relates to the mean number of events per interval. Salt and pepper noise alters certain pixel values, and its effects are illustrated through specific equations. Speckle noise, which can be both multiplicative and additive, is linked to optical devices and also described using a gamma distribution PDF.

The authors highlight the challenges associated with classical photometric image data augmentation, including increased memory and processing demands, as well as potential loss of important image features, particularly color features critical for distinguishing between different classes. They recommend careful application of photometric image data augmentation, suggesting that researchers should first analyze the features of the original dataset.

Additionally, the paper references previous research by Khalifa et al. that combines various classical image data augmentation techniques, indicating a trend towards a mixed approach in enhancing image datasets. The equations presented illustrate the mathematical foundation behind the noise models discussed.
