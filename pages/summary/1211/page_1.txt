The paper discusses the challenges of explaining predictions made by unsupervised learning models, which operate without labeled data. It introduces the 'neuralization-propagation' (NEON) approach that enables the application of explainable AI (XAI) methods to unsupervised learning techniques, specifically kernel density estimation and k-means clustering. NEON works by transforming an unsupervised model into a functionally equivalent neural network without the need for retraining, allowing the use of established XAI techniques such as layer-wise relevance propagation (LRP) in the second stage.

The authors provide two application examples to illustrate the effectiveness of NEON: analyzing spending behavior in wholesale customer data and examining visual features in industrial and scene images. Through these examples, the paper highlights the potential of NEON to enhance the interpretability of unsupervised learning models, addressing the gap in XAI methods for this area of machine learning. The work emphasizes the need for transparency in machine learning models, particularly as deep neural networks continue to evolve and be applied in various domains.
