The paper discusses an improved measure of outlierness, denoted as o(x), which is defined in relation to the logarithm of the probability density function ˜p(x). Unlike ˜p(x), o(x) increases as the probability decreases and does not saturate for distant points from the dataset. The authors propose a neural network architecture to compute o(x) using a two-layer structure. The first layer computes the squared distance from the new example to each point in the dataset, while the second layer employs a generalized log-mean-exp pooling method to derive the outlier score.

This neuralized approach allows for better interpretation and explanation of the outlier computation. The paper introduces a soft argmin operation to redistribute o(x) through an intermediate layer, utilizing a hyperparameter β for adjustment. Furthermore, it discusses how the propagation of input features can incorporate the geometry of the distance function, aided by a stabilization term in the denominator that reduces the relevance when the new example is close to any dataset point. The findings are illustrated with a one-dimensional toy example, emphasizing the structure's utility in outlier prediction.
