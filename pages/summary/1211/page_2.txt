This paper discusses the challenges and advancements in explainable artificial intelligence (XAI), particularly focusing on unsupervised learning models. It highlights the gap in research on explaining unsupervised models compared to supervised ones, despite the increasing need for interpretability in the former. The authors introduce the 'neuralization-propagation' (NEON) approach, which aims to make unsupervised model predictions explainable. NEON consists of two steps: first, it reformulates the decision function of the unsupervised model as a functionally equivalent neural network (neuralization), and second, it utilizes layer-wise relevance propagation (LRP) to generate explanations for model predictions. 

The paper reviews the application of NEON to kernel density estimation for outlier detection and k-means clustering, extending the work to include explanations for inlier detection using random features. Practical examples, including wholesale customer data analysis, image-based industrial inspection, and scene image analysis, illustrate the effectiveness of the NEON approach. 

The authors also provide a brief overview of XAI, noting the variety of explanation techniques developed to cater to different machine learning models and user inquiries. Explanations can take multiple forms, such as decision trees, prototypes, or highlighting important input features for model predictions. Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of interpretability in unsupervised learning and presents NEON as a promising solution to this challenge.
