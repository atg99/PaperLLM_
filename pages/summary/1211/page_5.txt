The paper discusses the neuralization-propagation (NEON) approach for explaining predictions made by unsupervised learning models, particularly through the transformation of these models into functionally equivalent neural networks without retraining. This process involves two key requirements: replicating the decision function of the original unsupervised model and ensuring the neural network structure allows for meaningful redistribution of contributions from output to input features.

Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) is highlighted as a common unsupervised learning method, especially for anomaly detection. KDE operates on an unlabeled dataset and utilizes a kernel, often Gaussian, to estimate the probability density function for new data points. The model predicts whether a data point is an inlier or outlier based on this density score.

To explain why a specific example is classified as an outlier, the paper emphasizes the need to identify the contributing features. The outlierness is quantitatively assessed using the probability density function produced by KDE, where lower values indicate higher outlierness. The paper sets the groundwork for further exploration of how to neuralize the KDE model to elucidate its predictions regarding inlier and outlier classifications.
