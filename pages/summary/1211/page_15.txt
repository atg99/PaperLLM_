The paper discusses the application of unsupervised learning models, specifically focusing on the analysis of predicted inlierness and outlierness in images using the VGG-16 network. The authors detail a method for attributing relevance scores derived from Block 5 of the network to the input pixels, emphasizing the need for careful implementation due to the constraints posed by deactivated neurons. The relevance scores, computed for inlier and outlier predictions, are propagated back to the pixel level using Layer-wise Relevance Propagation (LRP) rules.

The results demonstrate that inlierness is primarily associated with regular wood patterns, while outlierness is linked to irregular features, such as scratches on wood panels. This pixel-wise analysis allows for a clear identification of factors contributing to the model's predictions.

Additionally, the authors explore the application of k-means clustering on activations from Block 5 in the SUN 2010 dataset, which contains various indoor scenes. Similar adjustments are made to the LRP rules to ensure accurate relevance attribution, leading to further explanations of cluster memberships at the pixel level. The findings are visually represented in figures, showcasing the effectiveness of the proposed explanation methods for both inlierness/outlierness and clustering scenarios.
