The paper discusses the mechanisms of relevance attribution in unsupervised learning models, specifically focusing on two models: the inlierness model based on random features and the neuralized cluster membership model. 

In the inlierness model, neuron activations are expressed through a trigonometric representation, allowing the relevance scores to be reformulated using sine and cosine functions. The proposed redistribution rule for relevance incorporates terms that adjust for near-zero responses, ensuring that relevance is appropriately allocated based on the activations of the neurons.

For the cluster membership model, neuron activations are represented linearly with relevance scores derived from a softmax function. The relevance is decomposed into two components, but only the primary term related to the activations is propagated for relevance attribution. This model similarly emphasizes that only activated neurons receive relevance, ensuring that inactive neurons do not contribute to the model's decisions.

Overall, the paper highlights the importance of neuron activation in determining relevance and presents mathematical frameworks for redistributing relevance scores effectively within unsupervised learning contexts.
