In this paper, we explored the explanation of predictions made by unsupervised learning models, specifically focusing on the neuralization/propagation method. This approach involves transforming unsupervised models into functionally equivalent neural networks without retraining, followed by the application of Layer-wise Relevance Propagation (LRP). We examined techniques for neuralization in kernel density estimation and k-means clustering, including log-mean-exp pooling, random features, and converting distance metrics into linear layers. Our findings demonstrated that this method can provide meaningful explanations for both tabular data and images, effectively mapping these explanations to layers in convolutional networks.

Despite the effectiveness of our approach at a low computational cost, several challenges remain. One significant question is the method's applicability to a wider range of unsupervised learning scenarios, as various algorithms such as energy-based models and spectral methods exist. Future work should seek to adapt the neuralization framework to accommodate this diversity.

Another area of concern is the validation of explanation techniques. With multiple potential LRP rules and neural network formulations for the same unsupervised model, establishing reliable evaluation methods is crucial. Existing techniques from supervised learning may not directly translate to unsupervised contexts, as alterations to features could inadvertently generate new artifacts, skewing evaluation outcomes.

Lastly, we must consider how to evolve these explanation techniques beyond mere visualization or exploratory tools. Investigating the potential to combine these methods with user feedback for systematic verification and improvement of unsupervised models represents a promising direction for future research.
