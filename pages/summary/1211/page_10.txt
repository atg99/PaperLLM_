The two-layer neural network derived from the contrastive strategy effectively captures the relationships between different customers by utilizing the centroid locations of clusters in the first layer and applying a hard min-pooling operation in the second layer. This structure allows for clear differentiation between clusters, facilitating the application of explanation techniques like Layer-wise Relevance Propagation (LRP) to understand cluster memberships.

In the experiments, the authors apply the proposed neuralization approach to both vector data from wholesale customer spending and image data from industrial inspections and scene images. The first case focuses on customer data, where the method aims to uncover spending patterns among 440 instances. The results demonstrate the effectiveness of the neuralization approach in providing meaningful cluster evidence and enabling insightful attribution of this evidence to input features. The soft argmin redistribution method is particularly highlighted for its ability to avoid abrupt transitions in cluster decisions, ensuring that similar data points yield consistent explanations.

Overall, the paper illustrates how the proposed approach leverages neural network structures to enhance clustering and explanation techniques, thus providing a robust framework for analyzing diverse datasets.
