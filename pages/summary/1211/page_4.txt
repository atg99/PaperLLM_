In this paper, G. Montavon et al. discuss a propagation-based approach for explaining neural network predictions, specifically through Layer-wise Relevance Propagation (LRP). The LRP method operates by performing a backward pass through the neural network, redistributing relevance scores from the output layer back to the input layer. This process involves conservative propagation rules that ensure the total relevance is conserved throughout the layers, meaning the sum of relevance scores at each layer equals the model's output. This characteristic allows LRP to efficiently provide explanations for large models and datasets, achieving rapid computation of explanations, particularly with GPU implementations.

The authors also introduce the concept of "neuralization-propagation," which addresses the limitation of LRP being applicable only to preexisting neural networks. They propose a method to convert non-neural unsupervised learning models, like kernel density estimation or k-means, into functionally equivalent neural networks without retraining. This involves two steps: first, neuralizing the unsupervised model and second, applying the LRP method to generate explanations for the original model’s predictions. This approach expands the applicability of LRP to a broader range of models, provided that the neural network constructed has neurons with clear directionality to facilitate effective relevance propagation.
