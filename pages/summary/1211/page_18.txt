The paper discusses advancements in understanding and interpreting neural network activations, particularly in the context of supervised models. It highlights the need for methods to propagate relevance through network layers to achieve pixel-wise explanations of model decisions. The authors propose a decomposition of relevance propagation into two components: a propagating term and a non-propagating or dissipating term, which can provide partial yet meaningful explanations.

The research acknowledges the support from various funding bodies, including the German Ministry for Education and Research, the European Union’s Horizon 2020 programme, and the Korean Government's Institute of Information & Communications Technology Planning & Evaluation. 

In the section on attributing outlierness, the paper details the mathematical formulation for relevance associated with neuron activations in a neuralized outlier model. It presents equations that describe how relevance can be calculated based on the activations and the relationships between different neurons. The work aims to enhance the interpretability of deep learning models by grounding relevance to specific patterns in pixel space, thereby improving the understanding of model behavior and decision-making processes.
