This collection of references encompasses a variety of studies and papers related to crisis management, social media analysis, and machine learning techniques, particularly in the context of data augmentation and neural networks. 

Bayer et al. (2021) explore the challenges of information overload during crises and evaluate bilingual embedding models for clustering relevant social media posts. Kaufhold et al. (2020) focus on rapidly classifying social media content during emergencies, emphasizing the utility of active and online learning methods. Further, Kaufhold et al. (2022) present a dashboard designed for computer emergency response teams, enhancing situational awareness in cybersecurity contexts. 

Several papers delve into machine learning methodologies, including Minaee et al. (2021) who provide a comprehensive overview of deep learning for text classification. Srivastava et al. (2014) introduce dropout as a technique to mitigate overfitting in neural networks, while Geman et al. (1992) discuss the bias-variance dilemma in neural computation.

Data augmentation techniques are extensively covered, with works by Hernández-García and König (2018) suggesting alternatives to explicit regularization, and Zhong et al. (2020) introducing random erasing as a novel augmentation strategy. The use of generative adversarial networks (GANs) for synthetic data augmentation is highlighted in studies by Frid-Adar et al. (2018) and Zhu et al. (2018), demonstrating the efficacy of these approaches in improving classification tasks.

Overall, these references collectively underscore the importance of advanced machine learning techniques and effective crisis management strategies in processing and analyzing vast amounts of data during emergencies.
