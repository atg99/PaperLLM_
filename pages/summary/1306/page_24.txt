The paper discusses various methods for embedding modifications in natural language processing, specifically focusing on cutoff strategies to enhance model performance. The authors introduce three primary methods: token cutoff, feature cutoff, and span cutoff. Token cutoff eliminates the embedding of a specific word, while feature cutoff sets one dimension of each word's embedding to zero. Span cutoff applies token cutoff across a coherent series of words. These methods can be slightly modified to generate different perspectives, which are integrated through a multi-view learning approach utilizing consistency training, where the model aims to produce similar outputs across various views.

The authors evaluate their model against the GLUE benchmark, comparing it to adversarial training methods and round-trip translation. They report improvements in three out of eight tasks when using their methods. Furthermore, they extend these cutoff strategies to language generation tasks, significantly outperforming baseline models and existing adversarial training approaches.

The paper presents comparative results in a table, illustrating performance metrics across different models on various tasks. The cutoff methods demonstrate competitive results, particularly in accuracy and correlation metrics, indicating their effectiveness in enhancing model performance. Additionally, the authors touch on interpolation methods, which, while primarily applied in feature space due to the challenges of combining text instances, show promise in creating new meanings from the hidden states of two sentences. Overall, the study highlights the potential of these innovative methods in advancing natural language processing capabilities.
