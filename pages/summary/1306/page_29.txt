The section discusses various training strategies that combine semi-supervised learning with consistency training, which aims to enhance model predictions by minimizing discrepancies between output distributions of original and augmented instances. Consistency training can be effectively used with unlabeled data, allowing models to learn invariances that improve generalization.

Xie et al. demonstrate significant accuracy improvements using consistency training with round-trip translation and a TF-IDF-based replacement method, achieving a 22.79% increase on the Amazon-2 dataset. Chen et al. extend this with their MixText system, generating new instances and applying a weighted average of predictions to enhance training. Shen et al. integrate consistency training into a supervised framework, showing a modest accuracy gain of 0.15%.

Qu et al. combine supervised consistency training with contrastive learning, where original and augmented instances are brought closer in representation space. This approach has shown to enhance performance on downstream tasks. Various augmentation techniques, including word deletion and synonym substitution, are explored, but no single method proves superior. Other studies by Fang et al. and Yan et al. highlight the benefits of contrastive learning with pretrained models and various augmentation strategies.

Additionally, different training sequences are analyzed, such as Liu et al.'s curriculum learning, which starts with simpler instances, and Yang et al.'s reverse approach, which begins with augmented data to correct model biases. Overall, these strategies illustrate the importance of combining various techniques to improve model performance in semi-supervised settings.
