The references provided focus on various methods and advancements in data augmentation techniques for text classification and natural language processing tasks. Notable contributions include the work by Rizos et al. on short-text data augmentation for hate speech classification, highlighting the importance of augmenting limited datasets to improve model performance. Sun and He propose a novel approach for generating large volumes of supervised data specifically for short text sentiment analysis. 

Xie et al. introduce unsupervised data augmentation strategies aimed at enhancing model robustness through consistency training. Cheng et al. explore adversarial inputs in neural machine translation, demonstrating how such techniques can improve translation accuracy. Wang et al. present Switchout, an efficient data augmentation method for neural machine translation, while Andreas discusses compositional data augmentation as a means to enhance model training.

Guo et al. and Kurata et al. focus on sequence-level data augmentation and labeled data generation for semantic tasks, respectively. The significance of model portability and temporal analysis in text processing is addressed by Kolomiyets et al., and Miller et al. provide foundational insights into lexical databases through WordNet.

Other studies, such as those by Marivate and Sefara, propose global augmentation methods to improve short text classification, while Zhang and Lecun's work emphasizes text understanding from a foundational perspective. Jungiewicz and Smywiński-Pohl investigate the use of synonyms in augmentation, and Hu et al. delve into learning data manipulation techniques for effective augmentation and weighting strategies.

Overall, these references collectively underscore the critical role of data augmentation in enhancing the robustness and accuracy of text classification models across various applications.
