Rizos et al. develop a class-specific recurrent neural network (RNN) for language modeling aimed at generating training data. However, they find that this approach yields poorer results compared to embedding substitution and noise generation techniques. Ollagnier and Williams employ a language model (LSTM-CNN) to generate new sentences for a portion of a minibatch, using part of existing sentences as prompts.

Sun and He introduce seqGAN, a generative adversarial network (GAN) architecture that generates text and assesses its authenticity through a discriminator. Despite its innovative approach, the application of seqGAN as a data augmentation technique yields only minor classification improvements. Inspired by seqGAN, Li et al. propose CS-GAN, which integrates a GAN, RNN, and reinforcement learning to generate sentences guided by label information, resulting in meaningful outputs.

Several studies utilize the GPT-2 model for text generation. Wang and Lillis mention using rare instances for generation, while Anaby-Tavor et al. enhance label preservation by fine-tuning GPT-2 with task-specific data, achieving significant classification improvements. Their method outperforms conditional VAEs and other techniques in low-data scenarios. Abonizio and Junior further refine this by concatenating multiple samples as prompts and using DistilGPT-2, achieving consistent performance improvements.

Bayer et al. extend GPT-2's application to both short and long text tasks, ensuring high label preservation through fine-tuning, specialized prompts, and document embedding-based filtering. They note limitations based on specific datasets. Claveau et al. also fine-tune GPT-2 with class-specific data and apply a classifier for filtering, testing their approach across English and French datasets. Liu et al. enhance GPT-2's predictions through reinforcement learning, demonstrating improved performance across various model architectures, especially with larger pre-trained models.
