The paper discusses various advancements in adversarial training techniques aimed at enhancing model performance in supervised and semi-supervised learning settings. It highlights the use of 23 perturbations to maximize KL divergence and the effectiveness of virtual adversarial training in improving the DBpedia topic classification task, yielding a 0.11 accuracy increase over the baseline and outperforming conventional adversarial methods by 0.03 points.

Jiang et al. introduce the SMART method, which incorporates Bregman proximal point optimization with momentum to mitigate aggressive model updates, leading to significant baseline improvements and enhanced robustness across various applications. Wang et al. and Liu et al. explore adversarial training strategies for pre-training language models. Wang et al. generate adversarial examples for output embeddings, achieving a notable reduction in perplexity for AWD-LSTM and QRNN models. Liu et al. propose the ALUM method, which applies noise to input embeddings and builds on virtual adversarial training, demonstrating promising generalization and robustness improvements, particularly with large transformer models like RoBERTa.

The paper also examines the transformation of adversarial examples into the data space for better inspection. Wan et al. focus on improving grammatical error correction systems using adversarial examples generated through loss-increasing noise, while Liu et al. utilize a transformer autoencoder for machine question generation tasks. Both approaches significantly enhance baseline performance.

Finally, acknowledging the computational expense of adversarial training, Shen et al. propose efficient data augmentation methods in the feature space to streamline the process.
