The paper discusses the effectiveness of existing data augmentation techniques in enhancing performance, particularly in text-based applications. It highlights the importance of usability in data augmentation tools, suggesting the publication of code and the development of comprehensive libraries to facilitate their application. The paper references frameworks by Dhole et al. and Papakipos et al. that aim to consolidate various augmentation methods, though they also note limitations regarding the abstraction of these processes.

Moreover, the paper emphasizes the significance of integrating data augmentation within the learning process, distinguishing between offline and online methods. Offline augmentation operates independently of the training model, while online methods incorporate augmented instances directly into the training workflow. The authors argue that maintaining continuity in the learning process is crucial to avoid splitting model training, which can hinder performance.

In conclusion, the survey outlines the utility of data augmentation in various contexts, such as regularization, reducing label effort, and improving robustness against adversarial attacks. It categorizes augmentation methods into feature and data space approaches, further refining them into specific groups. The paper calls for future research to explore more advanced techniques that can introduce novel linguistic patterns, particularly in light of the growing use of transfer learning. While acknowledging the promise of data augmentation, it also points out its limitations, particularly concerning the need for substantial original datasets to generate high-quality augmented data.
