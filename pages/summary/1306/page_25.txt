The paper discusses the effectiveness of interpolation methods, particularly focusing on the Synthetic Minority Over-sampling Technique (SMOTE), in improving classification performance by addressing class imbalance. Key factors contributing to the success of these methods include class balancing, regularization that smooths decision boundaries, and enhanced data representations.

SMOTE, designed to oversample minority classes, is shown to outperform simple oversampling techniques by creating less specific decision boundaries, which helps reduce overfitting and improves generalization. The method works by interpolating between a source instance and its nearest neighbors of the same class, using a defined formula that incorporates a distance measure. This class-preserving approach enhances the safety of the technique, although it may limit the novelty of the generated instances.

The paper emphasizes that the application of SMOTE can lead to a more balanced dataset and a less specific decision boundary, ultimately facilitating better generalization in classification tasks. The authors also note the integration of SMOTE with textual data augmentation in other studies, highlighting its versatility, although specific implementation details were not provided in those cases.
