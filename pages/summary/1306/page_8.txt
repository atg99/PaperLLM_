The paper discusses various methods of data augmentation through noise induction to enhance the performance of machine learning models. One method highlighted is "blank noising," where words are replaced with underscores, contributing to improved experimental results. Li et al. explore syntactic and semantic noise generation, utilizing techniques like sentence shortening, adjective alteration, and word dropout, achieving accuracy improvements of up to 1.7 points when combining these methods.

The Easy Data Augmentation (EDA) method by Wei and Zou, specifically random swap and deletion, is also noted for its effectiveness in noise induction, although some studies report that EDA can sometimes decrease classification scores due to its non-label-preserving nature. Wu et al. expand on this by introducing random span deletion, which deletes consecutive words but is limited to language modeling tasks.

Rizos et al. propose a unique noise induction method that shifts training instances within their padding, resulting in a significant performance gain of over 5.8% in hate speech detection. Sun and He's method of adding meaningless words is mentioned, though its isolated impact remains unassessed.

Xie et al. introduce a TF-IDF based replacement method for uninformative words, while Choi et al. mask casual features to create counterfactual examples. The paper also references additional noise data augmentation methods from various authors, indicating a rich exploration of techniques to enhance model training through noise induction.

In the section on synonym replacement, the paper describes this common data augmentation technique, where words are substituted with synonyms to maintain semantic integrity, as initially demonstrated by Kolomiyets et al. through the use of WordNet for temporal expressions.
