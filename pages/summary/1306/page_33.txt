The paper discusses the performance of various interpolation techniques applied to transformer models, particularly BERT, noting improvements in accuracy and F1 scores under different scenarios. A significant point raised is the need for comprehensive evaluation criteria in data augmentation research, emphasizing that improvements are often reported solely based on prediction performance, while other important factors such as time, resource usage, language variety, and configurability are frequently overlooked. For instance, the GPT-2 based data augmentation method can be time-consuming, which may not be suitable for urgent applications like crisis management.

The authors advocate for a standardized framework to facilitate reliable comparisons of data augmentation methods, similar to existing benchmarks in fields like few-shot learning and natural language generation. They suggest establishing a small benchmark that includes diverse datasets to evaluate different data augmentation goals while ensuring it is manageable for specific evaluations.

Additionally, the paper highlights the complexity of understanding the effectiveness of text data augmentation techniques compared to image techniques. While some text methods, such as paraphrasing, are intuitively beneficial, others, particularly those working in feature space, require deeper investigation. The authors call for enhanced methods of visualizing these augmentations and understanding their underlying mechanisms, especially in light of the evolving landscape of large language models, which may render some traditional augmentation methods less relevant.
