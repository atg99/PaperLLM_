The paper discusses advancements in data augmentation techniques, particularly focusing on generative methods. It highlights the increasing interest in generative models due to their enhanced capabilities in producing diverse text. Qiu et al. introduce a variational autoencoder (VAE) approach for text generation, distinguishing between unconditional and conditional VAEs. Unconditional VAEs train separate models for each class, while conditional VAEs use label information as additional input. The study finds that sampling from the prior distribution with unconditional VAEs yields the highest F1-score improvements.

Malandrakis et al. also explore VAEs for augmentation, focusing on natural language understanding with limited resources. They evaluate different VAE variants and propose both conditional and unconditional approaches, testing reconstruction tasks and the inclusion of a discriminator network. Their findings reveal that conditional VAEs perform best in evaluations, although the discriminator variant struggles due to insufficient training data.

The paper further mentions the NeuralEditor by Guu et al., which generates new texts based on edited vectors derived from lexically similar instances. While this method may introduce errors due to its reliance on lexical rather than semantic similarity, it effectively serves its purpose in language modeling, showing improvements in generation quality and perplexity. Raille et al. adapt this concept with the Edit-transformer, enabling cross-domain functionality and demonstrating enhanced speed and performance across various classification tasks. Overall, the paper emphasizes the potential of generative methods in the realm of data augmentation.
