The paper discusses four application scenarios where insufficient data hampers the development of high-quality classifiers, particularly in the context of deep learning. It highlights the tendency of deep neural networks to overfit, as noted by Srivastava et al., leading to poor generalization on unseen data. This challenge is framed within the bias-variance dilemma, where deep models exhibit low bias but high variance across different training subsets. Solutions include regularization techniques like dropout and L2 norms, or increasing training data through data augmentation, which is emphasized as a preferred method for enhancing model generalization without degrading representational capacity.

However, the effectiveness of data augmentation is context-dependent, and it may not be applicable to all classification problems. The paper also addresses the emergence of adversarial examples, which are slight modifications to input data that can mislead algorithms, as illustrated by Alzantot et al. These adversarial examples can be utilized in data augmentation to bolster model robustness against such attacks.

Additionally, the paper points out the challenge of unbalanced datasets, where a small relevant class is overshadowed by a larger irrelevant class. Data augmentation can help mitigate this issue by increasing the representation of the minority class, leading to a more robust classifier. In sensitive domains, data augmentation can also be employed to generate artificial data, thereby minimizing the use of real-world data and protecting privacy. The paper cites Carlini et al. on the importance of employing anonymizing data augmentation techniques to ensure privacy in training data. 

Overall, the scenarios outlined emphasize the need for innovative strategies to enhance classifier performance in data-scarce environments while addressing concerns related to data imbalance and privacy.
