In summary, the section discusses various structure-based transformations and interpolation techniques for data augmentation in natural language processing. The structure-based methods, such as those proposed by Şahin and Steedman, focus on manipulating grammatical components, which are particularly beneficial for low-resource languages. The Semantic Text Exchange method by Feng et al. aims to alter semantics while maintaining fluency but has shown mixed results in preserving content. Min et al. introduce inversion and passivation techniques that improve generalization in natural language inference tasks, suggesting that syntactic information is crucial yet underutilized in existing datasets. The SUB² method by Shi et al. substitutes substructures in training examples, demonstrating significant improvements in classification accuracy for low-resource tasks. Overall, these approaches highlight the importance of leveraging structural and syntactic features to enhance the effectiveness of data augmentation in NLP.
