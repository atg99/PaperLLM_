Mixup interpolation, introduced by Zhang et al., is a method that generates new training examples by linearly combining input vectors and their corresponding labels. The technique is defined by the formulas x̃ = λxi + (1−λ)xj and ỹ = λyi + (1−λ)yj, where xi and xj are input vectors, and yi and yj are one-hot-coded labels. The parameter λ can be fixed or drawn from a Beta distribution.

While Mixup is broadly applicable to various types of data, its application to text is more complex due to dimensionality challenges. Verma et al. suggest applying Mixup in hidden layers of neural networks, providing a foundation for its use in text-related tasks, although their tests were limited to image datasets. Marivate and Sefara have utilized Mixup with bag of words, TF.IDF, word embeddings, and language models, though they did not detail the interpolation process, which raises concerns about handling varying instance sizes.

Qu et al. detail their approach, which involves linear combination of embedding matrices from instances in a mini-batch, resulting in a 0.6% accuracy improvement on the MNLI-m task. Guo et al. introduce wordMixup and senMixup, applying interpolation in the word embedding space and on hidden layers, respectively, with the former requiring zero padding. Guo also enhances wordMixup with a nonlinear interpolation policy that adaptively learns labels based on mixed embeddings, yielding diverse examples but potentially harming classification quality.

Sun et al. and Chen et al. propose methods that apply Mixup to transformer model outputs and encoder hidden representations, respectively, with TMix interpolating across various encoder layers identified for their semantic and syntactic information. Both approaches demonstrate improvements on the GLUE benchmark.

Overall, these studies highlight the potential and challenges of applying Mixup interpolation to text data, with varying degrees of success across different methodologies and datasets.
