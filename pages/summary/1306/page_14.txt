The paper discusses various approaches to augmenting language models for word substitution, focusing on the use of GloVe embeddings and language models like LSTM and BERT. Alzantot et al. proposed a method that generates similar words using GloVe embeddings combined with a counter-fitting approach, which allows for localized replacements. Kobayashi's work utilizes an LSTM language model to identify contextually fitting substitution words but faces the risk of label distortion. To address this, he introduces a label-conditional language model that integrates labels into the prediction process.

Wu et al. build on Kobayashi's method by modifying BERT into a label-conditional version (c-BERT), demonstrating improved classifier performance across various tasks. However, c-BERT is limited in low-data scenarios where augmentation may not preserve labels. Hu et al. enhance c-BERT by incorporating it into a reinforcement learning framework, leading to significant performance improvements in low-data settings.

Additionally, Jiao et al. further refine the c-BERT method for TinyBERT, addressing the challenge of poor quality data when handling multiple-piece words by employing GloVe embeddings for substitution. The paper concludes by acknowledging other proposed language model augmentations by various researchers, highlighting the ongoing advancements in this field. Evaluation results showcase the effectiveness of these methods, with improvements in accuracy across different datasets.
