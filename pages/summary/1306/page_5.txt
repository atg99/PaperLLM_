This paper discusses various types of data augmentation, particularly in the textual domain, and categorizes them into two main types: transformations of raw data and processed representations of data. While data augmentation techniques are well-established in computer vision, where simple label-preserving transformations are intuitive, their application varies across different fields, including speech processing and text classification.

In computer vision, methods such as geometric transformations, neural style transfers, and generative adversarial networks significantly enhance accuracy. In speech processing, acoustic transformations, such as modifying speed or adding noise, have shown to improve classifier performance. However, textual data augmentation presents challenges due to the complexity of preserving labels during transformations.

The paper provides a detailed overview of textual data augmentation methods, focusing primarily on text classification, which involves categorizing units like sentences and paragraphs into class labels. It emphasizes methods applicable to tasks such as topic classification and sentiment analysis, while mentioning other tasks like machine translation in a less comprehensive manner.

The authors adopt a method-oriented perspective to categorize augmentation techniques based on data structure, contrasting their approach with a broader task-driven view presented by other researchers. They aim to provide insights into the effectiveness and contextual application of different augmentation methods, helping researchers and practitioners identify suitable techniques for their specific use cases. The paper concludes with a discussion of future research directions to advance the field of data augmentation.
