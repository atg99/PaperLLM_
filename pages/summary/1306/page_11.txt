The paper discusses advanced techniques for integrating data augmentation into learning processes, particularly focusing on synonym and embedding replacement methods. Jungiewicz and Pohl emphasize a sophisticated approach where synonyms are only replaced if they maximize the loss of the classifier model. Other studies, such as those by Kashefi and Hwa, and Feng et al., adapt thesaurus-based replacements for specific tasks.

Embedding replacement methods, similar to synonym substitution, aim to find contextually appropriate words that do not alter the text's core meaning. This is achieved by translating words into a latent representation space, where similar context words are clustered, based on the distributional hypothesis of semantics. Rizos et al. highlight that this method encourages models to focus on capturing similar sequential patterns rather than associating individual words with labels, particularly in tasks related to hate speech. Compared to synonym substitution, embedding replacement is more comprehensive and considers the context of texts, leading to grammatically coherent sentences. This approach is especially beneficial for languages lacking extensive thesauri but rich in general text resources, facilitating the training of self-supervised embedding models.
