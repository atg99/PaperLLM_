The paper discusses various loss functions used in deep metric learning, emphasizing the importance of pair weighting in improving model performance. It begins by defining binary indicators for positive and negative pairs, followed by an analysis of triplet loss, which aims to ensure that the similarity of negative pairs is less than that of positive pairs by a margin. Both triplet and contrastive losses treat all selected pairs equally, limiting their effectiveness in capturing more informative pairs.

The lifted structure loss is introduced as a more advanced approach that utilizes all positive and negative pairs in a mini-batch, allowing for differentiated weighting based on relative similarities. This is further refined with weights for positive and negative pairs derived from their similarities.

Binomial deviance loss is presented as a soft alternative to contrastive loss, employing a softplus function to create a more nuanced weighting system. This method emphasizes the importance of distinguishing between negative pairs with varying similarities.

The paper culminates in the introduction of multi-similarity loss, which integrates three types of similarities—self-similarity, relative similarity, and others—into a unified framework for sample selection and weighting. The authors argue that utilizing multiple similarities enhances the model's ability to learn from more informative pairs, particularly in the context of hard negative samples, which are crucial for developing robust discriminative features.
