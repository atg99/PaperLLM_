The paper presents a novel approach for pair mining and weighting in similarity learning, structured around two main steps: pair mining using Similarity-P and pair weighting using Similarity-S and Similarity-N. 

In the pair mining phase, informative pairs are selected based on the relative similarities of negative and positive pairs with a common anchor. The negative pair is chosen based on its comparison to the hardest positive pair, while the positive pair is sampled from negative pairs that exhibit high similarity. The conditions for selecting negative and positive pairs are provided mathematically, ensuring that the selected pairs are challenging and informative. This strategy draws inspiration from the large margin nearest neighbor (LMNN) approach, focusing on creating an embedding space where positive neighbors share the same class label as the anchor.

The second phase involves a soft weighting scheme that enhances the accuracy of the selected pairs by integrating both self-similarity (Similarity-S) and relative similarity (Similarity-N). The weights for negative and positive pairs are derived from equations that incorporate hyper-parameters, allowing for a nuanced adjustment of pair importance based on their similarities. The proposed weighting mechanism is compared against a simpler average weighting scheme, demonstrating that a direct combination of loss functions does not necessarily yield improved performance.

The paper culminates in the formulation of a multi-similarity (MS) loss function, integrating the pair mining and weighting processes. This loss function can be optimized through gradient descent, facilitating the iterative improvement of pair selection and weighting.

Empirical validation is conducted using various datasets, including CUB200, Cars-196, Stanford Online Products, and In-Shop Clothes Retrieval. The experiments utilize a pre-trained Inception network, fine-tuned for the specific tasks, and employ data augmentation techniques to enhance model robustness. The results indicate the effectiveness of the proposed MS loss in improving similarity learning outcomes.
