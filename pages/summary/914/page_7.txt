This paper evaluates various methods for image retrieval, specifically focusing on the Recall@K performance metric across different techniques on the Cars-196 dataset. The methods compared include Binomial S, LiftedStruct∗N, MS mining P, and others, with results indicating that MS SNP achieved the highest Recall@1 at 77.3%. The study utilizes a dataset comprising 3,997 classes and 25,882 images for training, with separate query and gallery sets.

An ablation study was conducted to assess the effectiveness of multi-similarity (MS) strategies in the context of pair weighting. Key findings highlight the significance of Similarity-S, where the Binomial deviance loss demonstrated superior performance. The MS weighting approach outperformed LiftedStruct∗m, indicating that incorporating Similarity-S is beneficial. Similarity-N also contributed to improved performance, enhancing Recall@1 for various methods. Additionally, the integration of Similarity-P through a mining step led to notable gains in performance.

The proposed MS loss method outperformed all others by effectively leveraging multiple similarities for pair mining and weighting, although it emphasizes the necessity of integrating these similarities within a unified framework. The study also examined the impact of embedding size, showing that performance generally increased with larger dimensions, peaking at 512.

In comparison with state-of-the-art techniques, the MS loss method showed improvements of 7% on the CUB200 dataset and 4% on Cars-196 over Proxy-NCA, demonstrating its effectiveness in the image retrieval task.
