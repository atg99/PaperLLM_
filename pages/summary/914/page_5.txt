The paper discusses the limitations of existing pair-based methods in measuring similarities for negative pairs in embedding spaces. It identifies three types of similarities: self-similarity (S), negative relative similarity (N), and positive relative similarity (P). The authors illustrate through three cases how these similarities impact the assessment of pair weights when negative or positive pairs move closer to an anchor point.

Table 1 summarizes various pair-based loss functions and their consideration of these similarities. It highlights that while some methods focus on either self-similarity or relative similarities, none effectively utilize all three simultaneously. For instance, the lifted structure loss primarily accounts for negative relative similarity, neglecting the influence of relevant positive or negative samples.

To address this gap, the authors introduce a Multi-Similarity (MS) loss framework, which employs a new pair weighting scheme that incorporates all three types of similarities through a two-step process: mining informative pairs and weighting them accordingly. This approach aims to enhance the model's capability to learn discriminative features by fully leveraging the relationships among pairs in the embedding space.
