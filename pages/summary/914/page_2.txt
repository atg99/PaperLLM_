This paper addresses the challenges in deep metric learning, particularly in the sampling of informative pairs for training. It reviews existing pair-based loss functions such as contrastive loss, triplet loss, lifted structure loss, and N-pairs loss, noting that while they aim to learn from informative pairs, they often fail to fully leverage the potential of negative pairs due to their large quantity.

The authors introduce a General Pair Weighting (GPW) framework, which provides a unified formulation for pair-based loss functions through gradient analysis. They identify the importance of computing different types of similarities for pairs, specifically self-similarity and relative similarities, the latter being heavily influenced by other pairs. Most existing methods only partially consider these similarities, limiting their effectiveness.

To overcome these limitations, the authors propose a multi-similarity (MS) loss that incorporates both self-similarity and relative similarities in a two-step iterative process of sampling and weighting. This approach improves the efficiency and accuracy of identifying informative pairs, resulting in significant performance enhancements on image retrieval benchmarks. The MS loss outperforms state-of-the-art methods, achieving notable improvements in recall metrics on datasets such as CUB200 and the In-Shop Clothes Retrieval dataset.

The paper emphasizes the critical role of sample weighting in pair-based metric learning and provides insights into how current methods can be enhanced by fully exploring the relationships between pairs.
