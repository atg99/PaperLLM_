This paper discusses advancements in the field of deep metric learning, focusing on the general pair weighting (GPW) framework as a more comprehensive approach to sampling and loss functions in this area. The authors critique existing methods that prioritize sampling or hard sample mining, such as the smart mining framework by Harwood et al. and the hierarchical triplet loss proposed by Ge et al. Instead, they introduce GPW, which redefines the sampling problem through a unified perspective of pair weighting.

The authors explain that instance weighting techniques have been previously employed in various tasks, such as focal loss for object detection and active bias learning for neural networks. However, these methods primarily rely on self-similarity, whereas the GPW framework incorporates both self-similarity and relative similarities, allowing for a more nuanced understanding of sample correlations.

The GPW framework is mathematically formulated, beginning with a similarity matrix derived from instance vectors processed through a neural network. The framework analyzes pair-based loss functions, deriving a new function that emphasizes pair weighting. The transformation of the loss function into a weighting context enables the GPW to generalize existing pair-based methods, illustrating its applicability across various loss functions, including contrastive loss and triplet loss.

Overall, the GPW framework offers a robust alternative to traditional sampling approaches by integrating multiple sample correlations, thereby enhancing the performance of deep metric learning models.
