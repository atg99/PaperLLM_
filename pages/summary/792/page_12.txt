The paper discusses various techniques for pruning layers and structures in neural networks, particularly focusing on methods to enhance model efficiency without significantly sacrificing performance. 

In the section on pruning layers, the authors note that simply dropping attention heads does not reduce runtime due to their parallel computation. They introduce the LayerDrop approach, which is inspired by DropConnect and involves structured dropout of weights, heads, feed-forward network matrices, or entire layers. Three strategies for layer pruning are outlined: 

1. **Every Other**: This method involves pruning every other layer at a specified rate, which has shown promising results across several tasks.
2. **Search on Validation**: This method entails evaluating various combinations of layers to identify those that can be pruned with minimal impact on performance.
3. **Data Driven Pruning**: This approach learns individual drop rates for each layer based on data, ensuring the average drop rate aligns with a target.

The paper also explores pruning general structures, where Prasanna et al. investigate the pruning of feed-forward layers and attention heads in BERT networks. They utilize a sensitivity analysis to determine which structures to retain, finding that it is feasible to create a subnetwork that matches the performance of the full model.

In the summary, the authors compare multiple pruning methods across different tasks and datasets, noting that pruned models can sometimes outperform their unpruned counterparts due to regularization effects. For language modeling, the Bank-Balanced Sparsity method achieved the lowest perplexity on the PTB dataset. The RPP method was highlighted for its efficiency in various tasks, outperforming iterative magnitude pruning in several cases. Notably, LayerDrop showed significant improvements in sentiment analysis tasks, and Block Sparsity methods were effective in speech recognition tasks. Overall, the findings suggest that strategic pruning can lead to substantial model size reduction while maintaining or even enhancing accuracy.
