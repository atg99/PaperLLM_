The paper "Compression of Deep Learning Models for Text: A Survey" published in TKDD in August 2020 discusses the importance of compressing deep learning models, particularly for text applications. The authors emphasize the need for smaller models that can be run on local devices to mitigate issues related to network delays, user privacy, and power consumption. Smaller models not only improve prediction latencies but also facilitate easier software development, deployment, and updates, as larger models often require extensive computational resources that may not be accessible to all users.

The survey outlines various model compression techniques, including pruning, quantization, knowledge distillation, parameter sharing, tensor decomposition, and sub-quadratic Transformers. Pruning is highlighted as a key method to reduce model size by removing certain weight entries, neurons, weight blocks, attention heads, or entire layers. The authors also address the challenges of deciding which components to prune and whether to prune existing large networks or create smaller ones from the outset.

Additionally, the paper notes the redundancy present in large neural networks, where a small subset of weights can often suffice to maintain accuracy. This observation has spurred significant research into compression strategies across the natural language processing (NLP) community. The authors aim to systematically categorize and explore this body of literature, focusing specifically on methods applicable to text rather than those from other domains such as vision or speech.

In summary, the survey provides a comprehensive overview of model compression methods relevant to text-based applications, emphasizing their practicality and necessity in real-world deployment scenarios.
