The paper discusses an optimization problem aimed at minimizing the Euclidean distance between full precision weights, denoted as W, and ternary weights, T, with a scaling factor α. The goal is to find the optimal parameters α* and T* that satisfy specific constraints. The authors note that this problem does not yield a closed-form solution, leading to the use of a threshold-based ternary function for approximation.

The ternary function assigns values of +1, 0, or -1 to weights based on a threshold Δ, which is determined to maximize the sum of the magnitudes of weights above Δ. The paper provides approximations for Δ based on the distribution of the original weights, suggesting practical values derived from uniform and normal distributions.

Additionally, the authors propose a loss-aware method for determining the quantization step size Δ, which involves training the network with full precision weights and quantizing layer by layer while measuring output errors. They also introduce an alternative training method that allows for the adjustment of weights to specific ternary values, incorporating back-propagation for optimizing trainable parameters.

Two heuristics are presented for setting the quantization step size for each layer: one based on the maximum weight of the layer and another that maintains a constant sparsity across layers. This approach facilitates the creation of ternary weight networks with varying levels of sparsity throughout the training process.
