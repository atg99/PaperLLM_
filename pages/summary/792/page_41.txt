The paper surveys various methods for compressing deep learning models applied to text tasks, highlighting their effectiveness in reducing model size while maintaining performance. It presents a comparative analysis of different datasets and tasks, such as CTR prediction, language modeling, neural machine translation (NMT), and sentiment analysis. Key methods discussed include tensor decomposition techniques such as BTD, FLOP, and TT-embedding.

For instance, BTD shows significant compression, achieving up to 2011 times reduction with slight improvements in test loss, and effective performance on language modeling tasks compared to traditional methods. FLOP-based pruning outperforms other pruning strategies on datasets like PTB and WikiText-103, yielding lower perplexity scores.

In NMT, methods like compositional coding demonstrate high compression rates (up to 94% on ASPEC) with minimal performance loss, indicating their superiority over pruning techniques. The paper also notes that sparse coding enhances accuracy across various text classification tasks while keeping the model size unchanged.

Overall, the findings suggest that advanced compression techniques can lead to substantial reductions in model size without compromising performance, making them valuable for deploying deep learning models in resource-constrained environments.
