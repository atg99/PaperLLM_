The paper discusses various methods for compressing deep learning models focused on text processing, particularly through knowledge distillation (KD) and parameter sharing. It highlights the performance of SqueezeBERT and MobileBERT, noting that SqueezeBERT is 4.3 times faster than BERT-base, while MobileBERT is 3.0 times faster. Both models significantly outperform BERT-base across multiple GLUE tasks, with each model excelling in different tasks.

KD is identified as a prominent model compression technique, utilizing various strategies to transfer knowledge from a larger teacher model to a smaller student model. This includes leveraging logits, attention outputs, and relative dissimilarity information. The effectiveness of KD extends to complex scenarios, such as multilingual tasks and those with numerous classes. The approach is particularly beneficial when a large amount of unlabeled data is available, allowing for effective student model training through pseudo-labeling.

In addition to KD, parameter sharing methods are explored as a means to reduce model size without removing weights. These methods involve sharing weights among blocks, such as in character-based language models which learn character embeddings to compose word embeddings. The paper presents the character to word (C2W) model, which constructs word representations using BiLSTMs while requiring only a single vector per character type, thus reducing the size of the word embedding matrix.

Various architectures for character-aware language models are discussed, including two proposed by Jozefowicz et al. The first, the CNN-Softmax variant, uses character CNNs to compose word embeddings, while the second, Char-LSTM variant, integrates character-level and word-level models for predictions. Additionally, Kim et al. introduce a model using a highway network in conjunction with character CNNs to optimize word embedding composition.

Overall, the survey presents a comprehensive overview of current techniques for compressing deep learning models in text applications, highlighting the balance between model efficiency and performance.
