The section on Distilling Transformers discusses various methods for compressing large Transformer models into smaller, more efficient architectures. These methods focus on maintaining or improving the accuracy while reducing model size, often involving the use of Bidirectional LSTMs. One notable approach by Zhao et al. introduces dual training, where a student model with a smaller vocabulary learns from a teacher model with a larger vocabulary. This is achieved by mixing vocabularies during training, allowing the student to learn embeddings from the teacher's context words.

Patient Knowledge Distillation (PKD) allows the student to learn from the teacher's outputs at intervals or from the last few layers. TinyBERT builds on this concept by utilizing knowledge from multiple layers of the teacher model. Liu et al. explore multi-task distillation, where a student learns from a multi-task teacher by averaging losses from correct and soft targets. MiniLM emphasizes mimicking the self-attention behavior of the teacher's last layer, introducing value-relation transfer to enhance mimicry.

Pretrained Distillation first pretrains the student with a self-supervised objective before applying knowledge distillation on supervised tasks. While many methods utilize a one-to-one layer mapping, Li et al. propose a more flexible approach where each student layer learns from all teacher layers using learnable attention weights.

Additionally, Tang et al. and Mukherjee et al. focus on distilling BERT models to BiLSTM architectures, employing KL divergence to align logits and internal representations between the teacher and student models. These methods involve non-linear projections to ensure compatibility in output spaces, effectively enabling the transfer of knowledge across different model architectures.
