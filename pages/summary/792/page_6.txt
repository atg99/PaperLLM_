In the paper by Gupta and Agrawal, published in TKDD in August 2020, the authors explore the effectiveness of large-sparse models in Neural Machine Translation (NMT), demonstrating that these models can outperform small-dense counterparts while achieving a significant reduction in non-zero parameters without greatly sacrificing accuracy.

The paper discusses weight pruning as a method for model compression, detailing two primary heuristics for selecting weights to prune: Hessian matrix computation and weight magnitude. It categorizes four main approaches to unstructured weight pruning: Hessian-based methods, magnitude pruning, iterative magnitude pruning, and a combination of iterative pruning and densification.

In the Hessian-based methods, the authors reference the Optimal Brain Damage (OBD) approach, which defines the saliency of a weight based on its impact on the objective function. This method uses Taylor series approximations to quantify saliency, allowing for the pruning of weights with low saliency followed by retraining of the remaining weights. However, OBD does not consider cross-terms in the Hessian matrix, which can lead to the removal of important weights.

To address this limitation, the Optimal Brain Surgeon (OBS) method is introduced, which incorporates cross-terms into the saliency calculation and provides a more efficient recursion for computing the inverse Hessian. Unlike OBD, OBS does not typically require retraining after pruning, making it more practical despite the computational complexity involved in second derivative calculations.

Magnitude pruning methods, introduced by Han et al., offer a simpler alternative by removing weights based on their magnitudes. This method creates sparse matrices that can be efficiently stored and compressed, enhancing the model's performance while reducing memory requirements.

Overall, the paper highlights the advancements in weight pruning techniques, emphasizing the trade-offs between computational feasibility and model performance in the context of NMT.
