The paper titled "Compression of Deep Learning Models for Text: A Survey" provides a comprehensive overview of various model compression techniques, with a focus on pruning methods. It categorizes pruning into two main types: unstructured and structured. Unstructured pruning removes individual weight connections, resulting in sparse matrices, while structured pruning targets entire structures such as neurons, blocks, attention heads, or layers. 

The survey highlights the evolution of pruning methods, illustrating the process with a figure that delineates different pruning strategies, including no pruning, unstructured pruning, and structured pruning. The motivation for pruning is drawn from biological processes in the human brain, where redundant synapses are naturally pruned over time.

The paper raises an important question regarding the comparison between pruning large networks versus constructing smaller, dense networks. It points out the additional processing required for pruning and the complexities involved with handling sparse matrices. The authors reference experiments by Zhu et al. that examined various model sizes and pruning strategies in the context of language modeling, contributing to the ongoing debate over the efficiency of pruning versus training smaller models. 

Overall, the paper serves as a foundational resource for understanding the methodologies and implications of pruning in deep learning model compression for text processing.
