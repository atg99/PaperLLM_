The paper by Gupta and Agrawal, published in TKDD in August 2020, investigates various quantization methods for neural networks, particularly focusing on weight and activation binarization techniques. The authors highlight that binarization can lead to significant accuracy degradation, as noted by Hubara et al. They reference Xu et al.'s approach, which employs 2 bits for weights and 3 bits for activations, resulting in improved performance.

Table 2 in the paper presents a comparison of quantization methods across multiple tasks and datasets, including accuracy metrics for both original and quantized models, as well as the number of bits used for weights and activations. The authors emphasize that variations in training hyper-parameters can lead to discrepancies in full precision model accuracy across studies.

For language modeling, popular datasets such as PTB, Text-8, WikiText-2, and IMDB are analyzed. Loss-aware binarization is shown to outperform other schemes, with notable performance on the Linux Kernel dataset, where it surpasses the full-precision model. Xu et al.'s Alternating LAQ method achieves competitive results with significant memory savings and acceleration.

The paper also discusses balanced versus uniform quantization techniques, highlighting that balanced quantization yields better results, particularly with 2-bit weights. Mixed precision quantization of BERT is demonstrated to be superior to QBERT, as it accounts for varying layer sensitivities to quantization. The findings suggest that mixed precision can retain model performance with a relatively small increase in memory requirements.

In machine translation, the authors note that the Transformer architecture remains robust to quantization, achieving performance close to the original model with 4-bit representations. Several studies are cited, indicating that quantized word embeddings can outperform full precision vectors in certain tasks. The paper concludes with observations on the effectiveness of quantization across various applications, including sentiment analysis and speech recognition, revealing a trend towards improved performance with specific quantization strategies.
