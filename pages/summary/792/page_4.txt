Gupta and Agrawal's paper in TKDD discusses various methods for model compression, focusing on pruning, quantization, knowledge distillation, parameter sharing, and tensor decomposition. 

In Section 2, the authors examine pruning strategies, debating the merits of one-shot versus iterative pruning and the role of regularization in enhancing pruning effectiveness. They highlight that pruning can reduce the size of weight matrices, which can be further optimized through quantization.

Section 3 delves into quantization techniques, explaining how weights, typically stored as 32-bit values, can be reduced to fewer bits, such as binary or ternary representations. The authors note that quantization can vary in approach, being either uniform or non-uniform, deterministic or stochastic, and can be loss-aware or unaware. They emphasize the importance of tailoring quantization levels across different layers of neural architectures like RNNs, LSTMs, or Transformers to balance model size and accuracy.

Knowledge distillation, discussed in Section 4, involves transferring knowledge from a larger teacher model to a smaller student model. The authors outline various design choices in this process, such as the type of teacher model used, the loss functions for training, the datasets for student training, and the specific aspects of the teacher's knowledge that the student aims to replicate.

Section 5 addresses parameter sharing, where the authors describe how sharing parameters across multiple weight structures can lead to reduced model sizes. They discuss the criteria for which parameters are shared and the techniques for sharing them.

Finally, Section 6 introduces tensor decomposition methods, which approximate large matrices by factorizing them into smaller components. The authors outline different factorization techniques and the properties of weight matrices that can be exploited for effective compression.

The paper concludes by noting that in Transformer models, both model size and latency are critical factors, especially due to the quadratic complexity associated with input sequence sizes.
