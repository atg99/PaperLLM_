In their paper, Gupta and Agrawal discuss various techniques for model compression, focusing on pruning and quantization. The authors highlight that to achieve optimal performance in sparse models, training a larger dense model (5x-10x) before pruning is more effective than pruning a larger model by significant factors (20x or more). They reference research by Guo et al., which indicates that Random Pruning Policy (RPP) preserves the language representation of models like BERT better than traditional iterative pruning.

The paper also examines block pruning as described by Narang et al., noting that block-sparse RNNs can achieve 80%-90% sparsity with minimal accuracy loss, but there is a trade-off between sparsity, block size, and accuracy. More aggressive pruning occurs in initial layers, while increased sparsity in final layers negatively impacts accuracy. Additionally, Cao et al. compare block sparsity with Bank-Based Sparsity (BBS), finding that BBS maintains accuracy across varying bank sizes, unlike block sparsity.

Attention head pruning is discussed, with findings from Michel et al. indicating that 20%-40% of heads can be pruned from models like NMT Transformer and BERT without significant performance loss. Voita et al. further show that pruning 38 out of 48 encoder heads results in only a minor drop in performance.

The authors conclude that pruning remains a prevalent method for model compression, with unstructured and structured approaches available. They emphasize the importance of careful implementation of pruning methods alongside regularization, noting that RPP is particularly effective for Transformer models. They also argue that while pruning heads alone may not yield substantial compression, a combination of head and layer pruning is more effective.

In addition, the paper addresses quantization, which reduces the bit representation of weights. Traditional architectures use 32 bits, but the authors argue that neural networks could potentially operate with weights stored in 4-7 bits, based on empirical evidence from biological systems. They explore various quantization methods, including binary and ternary quantization, as potential solutions for efficient weight storage.
