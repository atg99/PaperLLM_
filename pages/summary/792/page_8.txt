The paper by Gupta and Agrawal discusses techniques for pruning Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) models to enhance efficiency without sacrificing accuracy. It presents a method for setting pruning thresholds based on iterations, with specific formulas for different phases of pruning. The authors highlight that biases are typically retained during pruning due to their minimal quantity, and assert that applying gradual pruning can reduce model size by up to 90% while achieving speed improvements of 2x to 7x.

The paper also critiques the iterative pruning approach, particularly its application to Transformer models like BERT, as noted by Cheong et al. They identify issues with traditional L1 regularization, which inadvertently penalizes larger weights disproportionately and diverges from the original loss function. To mitigate these problems, Guo et al. propose a reweighted L1 minimization strategy that adjusts penalties based on the magnitude of weights, thus optimizing performance during pruning.

Additionally, the authors discuss the concept of alternating between pruning and densification across multiple iterations to enhance the effectiveness of model training. This dual approach allows the model to maintain capacity and avoid overfitting while promoting regularized training that aids convergence. Overall, the paper presents a comprehensive analysis of pruning strategies, emphasizing their importance in optimizing neural network models.
