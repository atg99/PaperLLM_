This paper provides a comprehensive survey on the compression of deep learning models specifically for text applications. It highlights several key points regarding model compression techniques and their implications for performance and efficiency.

The authors note that while model compression can lead to significant improvements in model efficiency, more experimentation is necessary to understand how various compression methods interact and their effects on different models. They emphasize that latency outcomes can differ based on GPU architectures, suggesting that advancements in hardware may enhance the effectiveness of methods like quantization.

The complexity of real-world applications is acknowledged, including multi-modal, multi-task, and small-data scenarios. The paper calls for efficient recommendations for the most promising compression methods tailored to such complexities. It also points out that different model components may respond variably to compression techniques, indicating a need for a more systematic approach to select appropriate methods based on model structure.

Moreover, the authors raise questions about the trade-offs between model accuracy, size, latency, and interpretability in the context of compression. They suggest exploring task-specific patterns that could lead to further compression and reference recent developments in deep reinforcement learning for potential applications in natural language processing tasks.

Overall, the survey aims to guide researchers and practitioners in academia and industry, highlighting the need for continued research in model compression to achieve optimal performance in NLP tasks while managing model sizes and latencies effectively.
