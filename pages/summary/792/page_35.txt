The paper presents a comprehensive survey on the compression of deep learning models for text processing, highlighting various techniques aimed at reducing the size of word embeddings in neural networks, particularly in recurrent neural networks (RNNs) and long short-term memory networks (LSTMs).

A key method discussed is the use of sparse coding for word embeddings, where a learned embedding matrix is transformed into a sparse representation. The optimization problem involves minimizing the difference between the learned embeddings and those for rare words, with regularization terms ensuring non-negativity and a sum constraint.

LightRNN is introduced as a method that compresses the word embedding matrix from O(|V|) to O(√|V|) by utilizing a 2-component shared embedding approach. This method organizes words into a word-allocation table, allowing each word to be represented by a combination of row and column vectors, significantly reducing the number of vectors needed.

The paper also details the Skipgram training method with parameter sharing, which divides embedding vectors into sub-vectors and assigns a limited number of reference vectors to each sub-vector. This method retains the optimization characteristics of traditional Skipgram training while introducing constraints for parameter sharing.

Additionally, Slim Embeddings are proposed, which allow for arbitrary compression ratios and utilize RNNs for embedding learning. This method contrasts with LightRNN by offering more flexibility in compression. The paper references other related approaches, such as HashedNets and character-aware language models, noting their complexities and implications for inference speed.

Overall, the survey synthesizes a variety of techniques aimed at effectively compressing deep learning models for text, emphasizing the balance between model performance and computational efficiency.
