The paper presents a comprehensive survey on the compression of deep learning models for text processing, focusing on various methodologies and their performance across multiple tasks and datasets. It categorizes models based on their architecture and evaluates their effectiveness using different metrics where high values are better and low values are preferred.

Key findings include:

1. **Model Performance**: Longformer consistently surpasses the RoBERTa baseline in tasks like coreference resolution, question answering, sentiment analysis, and text classification. Its strength is particularly evident in tasks requiring long context, such as WikiHop and Hyperpartisan, while its improvements are less pronounced in shorter context tasks like IMDB.

2. **Task-Specific Insights**: The performance gains for Longformer vary; for instance, in TriviaQA, the improvements are modest due to the sufficiency of local context. In HotpotQA, the use of auxiliary supervision enhances the model's ability to utilize local context effectively.

3. **Efficiency in Speech Recognition**: Linear Transformers demonstrate comparable performance to traditional transformers while achieving significant speed improvements in autoregressive predictions of lengthy sequences. They outperform both LSTM and Reformer models, with training times substantially reduced (824 seconds per epoch for Linear Transformers compared to 2250 seconds for Reformer).

Overall, the survey highlights the advancements in model architectures designed to manage long contexts and improve efficiency, emphasizing the balance between accuracy and computational resources in deep learning for text.
