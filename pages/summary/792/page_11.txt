The paper provides a comprehensive survey on the compression of deep learning models specifically for text processing. It discusses various techniques for inducing sparsity in models like RNNs and GRUs, emphasizing that smaller block sizes, such as 4x4 blocks, generally yield better results compared to larger sizes, which require lower sparsity to maintain accuracy. A significant challenge highlighted is the difficulty in preserving model accuracy when applying block sparsity, with block sizes being sensitive to specific applications.

To address these issues, the authors introduce Bank-Balanced Sparsity (BBS), a method that divides weight matrix rows into equal-sized banks and applies fine-grained pruning independently to each bank, ensuring balanced sparsity across them. BBS allows iterative pruning followed by fine-tuning, achieving model accuracy comparable to unstructured sparsity while outperforming block sparsity at equivalent sparsity levels. Additionally, BBS is favorable for FPGA acceleration due to its balanced matrix partitioning.

The paper also explores structured pruning methods for Transformer models, particularly focusing on pruning attention heads and layers. It notes that a significant number of attention heads can be removed with minimal impact on model performance, with some cases even resulting in improved accuracy. The authors detail an iterative approach to pruning based on head importance scores, which measure the sensitivity of the model to changes in attention heads. They report that pruning up to 40% of heads from models like BERT does not significantly harm accuracy. Other methods for head importance scoring are also discussed, including Layer-wise Relevance Propagation and confidence-based scoring, which help identify heads that can be pruned without affecting translation quality.
