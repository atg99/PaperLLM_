The paper discusses advancements in parameter sharing techniques within Transformer models, specifically focusing on the ALBERT model and Universal Transformers. ALBERT employs two main parameter reduction strategies: factorized embedding parameterization, which reduces the size of the vocabulary embedding matrix, and cross-layer parameter sharing, allowing all layers to share parameters, resulting in a model with significantly fewer parameters and faster training times compared to BERT-large. Universal Transformers further innovate by not fixing the number of encoder layers and dynamically allocating processing resources to ambiguous symbols, enhancing the efficiency of self-attention transformations.

A summary table in the paper compares various parameter sharing methods across different tasks and datasets, highlighting differences in model accuracy and size. It notes that despite variations in reported accuracies due to hyper-parameter changes, many parameter sharing techniques have been successfully applied in language modeling tasks. The paper details experiments conducted on datasets like the One Billion Word Benchmark and PTB, showcasing how methods like Char-CNN and LightRNN achieve substantial model size reductions while improving or maintaining performance metrics. Notably, C2W outperforms traditional word lookup tables, especially in morphologically rich languages, and Slim embeddings demonstrate superior performance compared to LightRNN with lower perplexity and smaller model sizes.
