This paper presents a survey on the compression of deep learning models for text, focusing on various quantization methods applied to different tasks and datasets. It summarizes the performance of these models based on metrics such as accuracy, perplexity per word (PPW), bits per character (BPC), and cross-entropy (CE).

The survey categorizes results by task and dataset, detailing the model type, quantization method, and evaluation metrics. Key findings include the effectiveness of quantization techniques like uniform quantization, mixed-precision quantization, and binarization across tasks such as language modeling, named entity recognition, neural machine translation (NMT), and sentiment analysis.

For example, in language modeling, various GRU and LSTM models were evaluated on datasets like IMDB and PTB, with methods like uniform quantization and loss-aware binarization showing promising results. In sentiment analysis, BERT-based models demonstrated improved accuracy with mixed-precision quantization.

The authors note that while weight binarization has limitations with recurrent neural networks (RNNs), newer approaches that quantize both weights and activations yield competitive results compared to full-precision models. The paper highlights the ongoing advancements in model compression techniques, emphasizing their importance for efficient deployment in real-world applications.
