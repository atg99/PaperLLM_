The section discusses Loss Aware Quantization (LAQ) and its application in quantizing neural network weights and word embeddings. The authors generalize a previous binarization method to formulate a k-bit quantization problem that minimizes the difference between the original weight vector and its quantized representation. This NP-hard problem is addressed using an iterative greedy approximation that reduces quantization error through residue computation and updates to quantization variables.

Refined greedy approximation further enhances this method by applying a least squares solution to refine the quantization variables after each update, leading to improved accuracy. The authors suggest that alternating minimization of the quantization variables offers a practical approach, with findings indicating that two alternating cycles are sufficient for achieving high precision.

Additionally, the text explores quantization techniques for word embeddings and transformer models, highlighting the significant memory requirements of typical word vectors. The authors emphasize that quantization can be beneficial both post-training and during training, with studies showing that training with quantization yields better outcomes. They note that transformer models can effectively maintain performance with quantization down to 4 bits, outperforming simple iterative pruning methods. Finally, they introduce mixed-precision quantization for models like BERT, advocating for varying bit precision across different layers based on the curvature of the loss gradient surface.
