The paper "Compression of Deep Learning Models for Text: A Survey" published in TKDD in August 2020 provides a comprehensive overview of various methods for compressing deep learning models specifically applied to text-based tasks. It presents a table summarizing the performance of different models, datasets, and pruning methods, highlighting key metrics such as model size, evaluation scores, and the effectiveness of various pruning techniques.

The survey categorizes the tasks into language modeling, linguistic acceptability, machine reading comprehension, neural machine translation (NMT), paraphrasing, question answering, sentiment analysis, speech recognition, summarization, and textual entailment. Each task is associated with specific datasets and models, detailing the original and pruned model sizes as well as their respective evaluation metrics, indicating whether higher or lower scores are preferred.

Key findings include the observation that magnitude-based pruning methods dominate recent approaches, with class-blind pruning outperforming other schemes. The study also notes that higher layers in models tend to be more critical for performance, and that pruning can lead to significant reductions in model size with minimal performance loss, particularly in NMT tasks where retraining can recover and even enhance baseline performance.

The authors conclude that while traditional methods like Hessian-based pruning were once considered superior, the trend has shifted towards more effective magnitude-based techniques, which allow for substantial pruning without significantly degrading model accuracy. Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of understanding the structure and sensitivity of models to guide effective pruning strategies.
