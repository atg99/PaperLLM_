The paper discusses hybrid ternary quantization methods for deep learning models, specifically focusing on their application in recurrent neural networks (RNNs). Wang et al. found that threshold ternary quantization (TTQ) is more effective for weights, while Bernoulli Ternary Quantization (BTQ) suits activations better. This distinction arises from the differing distributions of weights and activations in RNNs, leading to significant memory savings—up to 16 times when both weights and activations are quantized, compared to a 1.4 times saving for weights alone.

The paper also introduces the HitNet architecture, which utilizes hybrid ternary quantization through specific equations governing the input, forget, and output gates, as well as cell and hidden layer outputs. 

Furthermore, the document shifts focus to general k-bit quantization methods, categorizing them into uniform quantization, non-uniform quantization, loss-aware quantization, and methods tailored for Transformer models. Uniform k-bit quantization involves splitting the range of original weights into equal intervals, with specific formulas provided for quantizing weights within predefined ranges. The overall quantization result can be approximated by applying a reverse transform to the quantized values, thus maintaining the integrity of the original data.
