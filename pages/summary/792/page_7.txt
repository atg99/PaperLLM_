The paper discusses various compression techniques for deep learning models, specifically focusing on pruning methods applied to encoder-decoder LSTM neural machine translation (NMT) models. It highlights that magnitude pruning can be effectively utilized to reduce model size by identifying and removing weights based on their magnitudes. The study reveals that higher layers, attention, and softmax weights are critical, while lower layers and embedding weights often contain redundancy.

The authors categorize pruning into three strategies: class-blind, class-uniform, and class-distribution. Class-blind pruning indiscriminately removes the smallest weights across all classes, while class-uniform pruning maintains a uniform percentage of pruning within each weight class. Class-distribution pruning adjusts the pruning based on the standard deviation of each class to ensure a specific total pruning percentage. The findings indicate that class-blind pruning generally outperforms the other methods.

Additionally, the paper introduces iterative magnitude pruning, which suggests a gradual pruning process over epochs rather than a one-time reduction. This method involves setting initial and final pruning percentages and redistributing pruning across iterations. The effectiveness of pruning is enhanced with regularization techniques and dropout methods. The paper also discusses the mechanics of pruning thresholds and their adjustment throughout the training process, emphasizing the importance of a structured approach to pruning to maintain model accuracy while reducing size.
