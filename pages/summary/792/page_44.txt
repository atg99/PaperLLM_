The paper discusses advancements in reducing the computational complexity of Transformer models, specifically focusing on self-attention mechanisms. It highlights the work of Katharopoulos et al., who reformulate self-attention as a linear dot-product of kernel feature maps, decreasing complexity from O(n²) to O(n). Longformer is introduced as a method to further reduce this complexity to linear by employing a sparsified self-attention matrix with various attention patterns, such as the sliding window pattern, which allows each token to attend to a fixed number of neighboring tokens, resulting in O(nw) complexity. 

To enhance the model's flexibility in learning task-specific representations, Longformer incorporates "global attention" on select input locations, enabling a token with global attention to interact with all other tokens. The paper highlights that Longformer consistently outperforms RoBERTa on multiple long document tasks.

The summary section compares various sub-quadratic Transformer methods across different applications, including language modeling, coreference resolution, and sentiment analysis. It notes that Star Transformers outperform standard Transformers in several tasks, achieving significant accuracy improvements and faster average test times. Longformer is noted for setting new state-of-the-art results in specific benchmarks, such as text8 and enwik8, and for demonstrating superior performance in question answering tasks compared to other models like Sparse-BERT. The findings underscore the effectiveness of these optimized models in various NLP tasks.
