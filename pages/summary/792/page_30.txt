The paper discusses various methods of knowledge distillation applied to Transformer models, focusing on the optimization of student models through a multi-faceted approach. It highlights the use of different loss functions—supervised hard loss, soft loss with respect to output logits, and soft loss concerning internal teacher layers—employing stage-wise training to optimize these losses sequentially rather than jointly.

MobileBERT, a compact version of BERT-large, is introduced as an example of a distilled Transformer architecture. It utilizes inverted-bottleneck structures to manage feature map sizes and incorporates a rebalanced architecture with stacked feed-forward networks to optimize the relationship between multi-head attention and feed-forward networks. Knowledge transfer from the teacher model, IB-BERT, to MobileBERT is executed through feature map and attention transfer, with progressive training of each layer while freezing parameters of lower layers.

SqueezeBERT is another model discussed, which implements convolutions in place of fully connected layers. Its distillation process is simpler, focusing on the final layer during fine-tuning with a soft cross-entropy loss based on the teacher's logits and ground-truth encoding. Xu et al.’s method for compressing BERT through progressive module replacement is also presented, which allows for the gradual integration of compact substitutes for original modules during training.

The paper concludes with a summary table comparing various distillation methods across different tasks and datasets, noting that smaller student models can outperform their larger teacher counterparts due to better regularization or enhanced training processes. Knowledge distillation has been notably effective in natural language processing tasks, particularly in neural machine translation and summarization applications, where models like MiniLM demonstrate significant reductions in size with minimal loss in performance metrics.
