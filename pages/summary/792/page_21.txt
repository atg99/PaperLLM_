The paper discusses various methods for compressing deep learning models, particularly focusing on quantization techniques applicable to text models. It highlights the importance of efficiently mapping weight values to discrete levels to reduce model size without significant loss in accuracy. 

One method described is a histogram equalization technique that avoids sorting weight values during back-propagation by using approximate median calculations, which helps maintain training speed. Another approach is the static-KMeans quantization, where KMeans clustering is applied to weights after training a model with full-precision parameters. This requires storing a mapping from integers to cluster centers, allowing for significant compression with minimal bit usage.

A more advanced method involves performing KMeans clustering during training, where multiple connections share weights based on cluster indices. This enables efficient weight updates during back-propagation by aggregating gradients from shared weights, leading to a defined compression rate based on the number of clusters.

The paper also introduces Product Quantization (PQ) and Residual Quantization (RQ) as additional KMeans-based methods. PQ involves partitioning the weight matrix into submatrices and performing KMeans on each, while RQ quantizes vectors into centers and recursively quantizes the residuals. Both methods require careful storage of cluster indexes and codebooks but can achieve substantial compression rates.

Overall, the survey provides a comprehensive overview of quantization techniques, emphasizing their potential to significantly reduce the size of deep learning models while maintaining performance.
