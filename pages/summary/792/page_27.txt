The paper "Compression of Deep Learning Models for Text: A Survey" discusses various methods of knowledge distillation (KD) and collaborative learning to enhance the efficiency of deep learning models, particularly for text-related tasks. It highlights how students can learn effectively from teachers by minimizing the L2 difference between their feature similarity preservation (FSP) matrices across multiple training instances. Further, it introduces Representational Distance Learning (RDL) as a related approach.

The paper differentiates between word-level and sequence-level KD, detailing how sequence-level approaches, such as Seq-KD and Seq-Inter, utilize the teacher's output during training to improve student performance in tasks like neural machine translation.

Additionally, the concept of collaborative learning is explored through the Deep Mutual Learning (DML) method, where multiple student networks learn from each other without the need for a strong static teacher. This approach involves using both supervised learning losses and mimicry losses to align the outputs of the students.

The discussion also covers scenarios involving multiple teachers, noting that student performance may decline when the disparity between the student and teacher is significant. To address this, multi-step KD is proposed, which involves using a teacher assistant to facilitate knowledge transfer. The paper suggests methods such as averaging predictions from multiple teachers and incorporating a relative dissimilarity loss to enhance the effectiveness of this multi-teacher approach. 

Overall, the survey emphasizes the importance of innovative learning strategies in compressing deep learning models while maintaining performance in text-related tasks.
