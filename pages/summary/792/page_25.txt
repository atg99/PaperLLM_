This paper provides a comprehensive survey on the compression of deep learning models for text, with a focus on methods such as quantization and knowledge distillation (KD). 

In quantization, the authors discuss how reducing the number of bits per weight value can significantly compress models while maintaining performance. They note that while binary quantization is ineffective for text-based models, ternary and higher-bit quantization can achieve substantial size reductions with minimal accuracy loss. Notably, Shen et al. demonstrated that BERT can be quantized to as low as 2 bits with only a 2.3% performance decline across various tasks. The paper highlights the advantages of non-uniform and mixed-precision quantization combined with pruning, particularly for Transformer models.

The section on knowledge distillation outlines the process of training a shallow student model to mimic a deep teacher model. The authors explore various architectures, including the use of logits from the teacher for training the student. This approach allows the student to learn from the teacher's "dark knowledge," which provides more informative targets than original labels. The paper also discusses the addition of Gaussian noise to teacher logits as a regularization technique and the dual loss functions proposed by Hinton et al., which combine soft and hard losses to enhance the training process.

Overall, the survey emphasizes the effectiveness of these compression techniques in reducing model size while preserving accuracy, particularly for modern Transformer-based architectures.
