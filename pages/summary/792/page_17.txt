The paper discusses techniques for compressing deep learning models, specifically focusing on weight quantization methods such as binary and ternary quantization. It begins by formulating weight binarization as an optimization problem that minimizes the network's overall loss function. The binary weights are represented as a product of a scaling factor and a binary value, allowing for loss-aware binarization that can be efficiently solved using proximal Newton algorithms.

The paper highlights challenges with binary quantization in recurrent neural networks (RNNs) and long short-term memory networks (LSTMs), particularly when weights are close to zero, leading to issues like vanishing and exploding gradients. To address this, ternary quantization is introduced as a viable alternative, achieving significant compression ratios.

The section on ternary weight networks describes the deterministic approach of ternary connect, where weights are quantized into three values: +1, 0, or -1, based on predefined thresholds. This method eliminates multiplications during the forward pass, enhancing computational efficiency. Additionally, stochastic forms of ternary quantization are explored, incorporating probabilistic approaches to assign ternary values based on the original weight distribution.

The paper also discusses trained ternary quantization, where the boundaries for quantization are learned rather than fixed, allowing for adaptability to individual weights. Overall, the survey provides insights into various quantization strategies aimed at optimizing deep learning models for improved performance and efficiency.
