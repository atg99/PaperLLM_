The paper presents a comprehensive survey on the compression of deep learning models for text, highlighting various techniques such as pruning, quantization, knowledge distillation, parameter sharing, tensor decomposition, and sub-quadratic Transformer methods. It emphasizes the effectiveness of distillation combined with quantization, particularly in achieving superior performance on GLUE tasks compared to other methods.

The survey includes an analysis of 123 (task, dataset) combinations across multiple tables, indicating that while certain methods are popular, not all have been extensively tested across the same datasets. Key tasks identified include language modeling, sentiment analysis, and neural machine translation (NMT), with specific datasets like PTB, SST, and WMT14 being frequently used.

For language modeling, the bank balanced sparsity pruning method performed best with LSTM models, while Block Term Decomposition (BTD) excelled with Transformer models, achieving a low perplexity of 19.5 with 0.16B parameters. In NMT, the Pruned Seq-KD + Seq-Inter method yielded an 8M model with 18.5 BLEU on WMT14, while BTD led to a Transformer model with 34.91 BLEU at a 21.2M size.

The paper also discusses the effectiveness of combining various compression methods, such as pruning with tensor decomposition or quantization, and highlights a recent approach by Kim et al. that integrates knowledge distillation, structured pruning, and quantization. This combination resulted in significant reductions in model size and improved inference efficiency, achieving up to 12.4x GPU speed-up and 6.9x-125.8x reductions in energy consumption.

Additionally, the paper provides a summary of applications, listing tasks, popular datasets, and references for further exploration of the model size versus accuracy tradeoff. Overall, the survey underscores the advancements in model compression techniques and their applicability across different text processing tasks.
