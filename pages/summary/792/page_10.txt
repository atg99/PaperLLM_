The paper discusses methods for optimizing neural networks by removing redundant neurons and employing block-based pruning techniques. 

In the section on removing redundant neurons, the authors explain that if two neurons have identical weight sets, one can be eliminated by merging their coefficients. This process involves calculating the saliency of neuron pairs to identify and remove the least significant neurons. The method proposed by Srinivas et al. involves three steps: computing saliency values, deleting the neuron with the minimum saliency, and updating the saliency matrix accordingly.

The paper then transitions to block pruning, which addresses the limitations of weight pruning related to the irregularity of sparse matrices. Block pruning allows for more efficient storage and access by zeroing out entire blocks of weights when their maximum magnitude falls below a predetermined threshold. The pruning process is iterative and guided by a threshold that increases over time, with a modified start slope that accounts for the number of elements in each block. 

Furthermore, the authors introduce the group Lasso regularization method, which facilitates the effective removal of entire blocks by adding a loss term proportional to the L2 norm of the weights in each block. This approach allows for a coordinated pruning and regularization schedule, enhancing the overall efficiency and performance of the neural network.

Overall, the paper highlights innovative strategies for neuron removal and block pruning, aiming to optimize neural network architectures for better performance and resource efficiency.
