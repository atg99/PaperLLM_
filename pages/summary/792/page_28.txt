The paper by Gupta and Agrawal, published in TKDD in August 2020, discusses a novel approach to knowledge distillation (KD) using an ensemble of teacher models to train a student model. The authors outline a method where the student model focuses on the intermediate layer, while the teacher models contribute to establishing order relationships among instances through a voting strategy.

The proposed Relative Distance (RD) loss function is defined for triplets of instances, ensuring that the distance between the student’s activations respects the relationships indicated by the teacher models. Specifically, the RD loss is calculated as the maximum of zero and the difference in distances, augmented by a small constant to avoid trivial solutions. For multiple teachers, the order of instances is determined by majority voting.

The paper highlights scenarios where distilling knowledge from multiple teachers is advantageous, such as in cases with a large number of classes or in multi-lingual settings. In the context of a large class number, the authors propose employing a generalist model alongside several specialist models focused on confusable subsets of classes. The training involves minimizing a combination of Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence losses from both the generalist and relevant specialists.

In multi-lingual neural machine translation (NMT), individual language pair models serve as teachers, and a multilingual model is trained to align with these outputs. Once the multilingual model reaches a certain accuracy threshold, the distillation loss is replaced with the original negative log-likelihood loss for that language pair.

To address memory constraints during the distillation process, the authors suggest pre-computing the output probability distributions of the teacher models offline and only loading the top probabilities into GPU memory during training. This method significantly reduces memory usage while maintaining the effectiveness of knowledge distillation.
