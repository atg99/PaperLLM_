In the paper by Gupta and Agrawal, various techniques for parameter sharing in embedding matrices and language models are discussed. The authors highlight the importance of efficiently managing weights within a weight matrix, denoted as W, with a specified budget K for unique values. A traditional approach to weight sharing involves using a secondary matrix for group assignments, which can be memory-intensive. To address this, the concept of HashedNets is introduced, utilizing a low-cost hash function to allocate weights into hash buckets, thereby enabling shared parameters within the same bucket.

The paper contrasts this random grouping with the deterministic nature of parameter sharing in Toeplitz-like structured matrices. These matrices tie parameters along diagonals and allow for a higher displacement rank, which increases modeling capacity while maintaining structured organization. The authors reference the work of Tay et al., which applies similar concepts in Quaternion Algebra to achieve significant parameter reduction in Transformer architectures.

Another proposed method involves sharing low-rank factors across layers in recurrent models by representing a weight matrix W as a product of two matrices, Wa and Wb. This approach facilitates model compression by allowing some low-rank factors to be shared between layers.

Additionally, the authors discuss a vocabulary compression strategy that categorizes words into frequent and infrequent sets. Infrequent words are represented using sparse linear combinations of frequent words, leveraging the idea that unfamiliar terms are often defined through more common vocabulary. This method enhances the efficiency of embedding representation, contributing to overall model compression.
