The paper by Gupta and Agrawal discusses various methods aimed at reducing the computational complexity of the standard Transformer model, which typically operates with quadratic complexity. Sparse Transformers and Reformers achieve reductions to O(n√n) and O(n log n), respectively. Other approaches, including Star Transformer, Linformer, Sparse Sinkhorn Transformer, Efficient Attention, Linear Transformers, and Longformer, promise even more efficient linear complexity. Notably, Sparse Sinkhorn Transformer and Longformer demonstrate strong performance in terms of accuracy, latency, and RAM usage across numerous tasks.

In the section on model compression, the authors explore techniques such as pruning, quantization, knowledge distillation, parameter sharing, tensor decomposition, and sub-quadratic Transformer methods. These techniques not only decrease model size but also enhance prediction speed and reduce power consumption. Pruning, while the oldest method, is less frequently applied to Transformers. Quantization is beneficial, especially when employing mixed precision for GPU efficiency. Knowledge distillation stands out as the most popular compression method for Transformer models, while parameter sharing is useful but often requires combination with other approaches. Matrix decomposition, particularly the BTD method, holds significant potential despite its limited use. Sub-quadratic Transformers are crucial for processing lengthy documents in applications such as query-document similarity and summarization.

The paper provides a comparative analysis of model compression methods across various types, highlighting performance metrics from the GLUE and SQuAD benchmarks. Knowledge distillation is the predominant approach for GLUE datasets, although methods like iterative magnitude pruning, mixed precision quantization, and parameter sharing are also noted. The findings reveal that Distilled-BiLSTM offers the best accuracy-to-size ratio on GLUE tasks, followed by mixed-vocabulary training and ALBERT-B. However, the authors emphasize the importance of considering models that report results on multiple tasks, leading to TinyBERT and BERT-EMD emerging as top contenders based on their ratios of GLUE score to model size.
