The paper by Gupta and Agrawal discusses various methods of Knowledge Distillation (KD), a technique used to transfer knowledge from a larger teacher model to a smaller student model. The authors categorize different KD methods, including Standard KD, FitNets, Sobolev Training, FSP Method, Co-distillation, Teacher Assistant, Teacher Ensemble, and Patient KD, among others. 

Key points include:

1. **Temperature Scaling**: A temperature greater than one is recommended for training both teacher and student networks, while the temperature can be set to one during testing. This approach helps in softening the output distributions.

2. **Sobolev Training**: This method incorporates target derivatives alongside target values in the training of the student network, enhancing the learning process.

3. **Hint-based Training in FitNets**: This method allows the student to learn not only from the teacher's final outputs but also from intermediate representations, termed as hints. Additional parameters are introduced to align the student’s hidden layers with the teacher’s.

4. **Flow of Solution Procedure (FSP)**: Proposed by Yim et al., this method transfers knowledge by capturing the flow between layers through inner products of features from two layers, representing intermediate results in the problem-solving process.

5. **Quantization**: The paper also mentions the integration of KD with model quantization to achieve better compression of the student model.

Overall, the authors provide a comprehensive overview of KD techniques, highlighting their mechanisms and the rationale behind their effectiveness in training smaller models while retaining performance similar to larger teacher models.
