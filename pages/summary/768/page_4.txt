The paper discusses an architecture designed for dynamic tiling in the DepthConv module, specifically focusing on optimizing performance for a 3x3 kernel in the OpenPose network. It outlines a method of dynamic tiling that connects rows of line buffers through a multiplexer, facilitating data feeding from various locations. 

In the design space exploration section, the authors introduce two analytical models to estimate resource usage and latency, focusing on block RAM and DSP usage, which are critical bottlenecks. The design space is systematically explored to prune suboptimal configurations and optimize tiling factors, achieving results rapidly on standard workstations.

Data layout optimization is emphasized to minimize DRAM access and enhance bandwidth. The optimized organization involves strategically placing outputs from various network stages to reduce unnecessary DRAM movements. The authors also discuss modifying feature map layouts to improve burst lengths and address communication bottlenecks, particularly in layers with low computation-to-communication ratios.

Furthermore, the integration of the FPGA with TensorFlow is outlined, detailing the steps necessary for invoking FPGA computations within the TensorFlow framework. This integration includes preprocessing, data transfer, and the execution of CNN computations on the FPGA, followed by result retrieval and additional CPU processing.

The paper highlights the efficiency of the proposed architecture, with FPGA computations constituting only a small fraction of the total pipeline time, despite integration overheads that slow performance. To mitigate these overheads, an optimized two-level pipelining strategy is implemented, allowing for concurrent processing of various steps, thus enhancing overall system performance.
