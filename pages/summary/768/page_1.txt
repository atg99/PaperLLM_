The paper "End-to-End Optimization of Deep Learning Applications" by Atefeh Sohrabizadeh, Jie Wang, and Jason Cong addresses the challenges of integrating FPGAs with deep learning frameworks like TensorFlow, particularly in the context of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs). The authors highlight that the irregularity in modern CNNs, resulting from network pruning and simplification, leads to reduced data reuse and parallelism, which complicates FPGA acceleration. They reveal that naive FPGA integration can result in performance degradation of up to 8.45 times.

To tackle these issues, the paper proposes a software/hardware co-design approach, introducing a flexible architecture called FlexCNN. This architecture enhances computation efficiency for various convolution layers through techniques such as dynamic tiling and data layout optimization. Additionally, FlexCNN is integrated into TensorFlow with a fully-pipelined software-hardware integration flow, reducing the overhead associated with the FPGA handshake and non-CNN processing stages.

The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of their approach using OpenPose, a CNN-based application for human pose recognition, as a case study. Experimental results show that the FlexCNN optimizations yield a performance improvement of 2.3 times, and the integrated pipeline results in an additional 5 times speedup. Overall, the co-optimization leads to an impressive 11.5 times speedup, achieving an end-to-end performance of 23.8 frames per second with floating-point precision, marking the highest performance reported for this application on FPGA to date.
