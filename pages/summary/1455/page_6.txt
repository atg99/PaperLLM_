The paper presents experimental results on various natural language understanding tasks using different models, particularly focusing on the RoBERTa-large model and its performance on the GLUE benchmark. Key findings from the results are summarized in two tables. 

Table 1 shows the performance of the RoBERTa-large model across multiple tasks such as MNLI, QNLI, QQP, RTE, SST-2, MRPC, CoLA, and STS-B. The reported accuracy values are compared with results from a reimplementation of RoBERTa-large and several adversarial training methods including PGD, FreeAT, and FreeLB. FreeLB consistently outperforms the other methods across most tasks, indicating its effectiveness in enhancing model robustness.

Table 2 provides results from the evaluation server as of September 2019, comparing single-model and ensemble results for various models on the GLUE tasks. The FreeLB-RoB model shows competitive performance, particularly on tasks with larger training sets.

The paper also discusses the ARC and CommonsenseQA benchmarks, which assess the ability of models to answer science questions and commonsense reasoning questions, respectively. These datasets are designed to challenge models beyond simple retrieval techniques.

Overall, the experiments demonstrate the advantages of the FreeLB method in improving performance on GLUE tasks and its potential applicability to other benchmarks requiring reasoning capabilities. The results suggest that adversarial training can significantly enhance model robustness in natural language understanding.
