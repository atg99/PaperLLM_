The paper presents advancements in the performance of various models on the ARC (AI2 Reasoning Challenge) and CommonsenseQA (CQA) benchmarks through the application of fine-tuning and multi-task learning strategies. Key results are summarized in Table 3, highlighting the performance of models such as RoBERTa and FreeLB-RoBERTa across various datasets.

The authors employed a retrieval model to select relevant documents for each question-answer pair in ARC, utilizing the RoBERTa-large model to encode the inputs and compute final logits using a fully connected layer. Initial fine-tuning on the RACE dataset resulted in notable accuracy improvements, which were further enhanced by integrating FreeLB, achieving 84.81% accuracy on ARC-Easy and 65.36% on ARC-Challenge.

Additionally, a multi-task learning approach was implemented by fine-tuning on a combined dataset of multiple QA tasks, which led to improved results, surpassing previous submissions on official leaderboards. For CommonsenseQA, the methodology involved generating multiple inputs for each question and using the [CLS] token for scoring. The implementation of FreeLB fine-tuning yielded a dev-set accuracy of 78.81%, outperforming prior results.

Overall, the findings demonstrate the effectiveness of FreeLB and multi-task learning strategies in enhancing model performance on complex question-answering tasks without reliance on external knowledge bases like ConceptNet.
