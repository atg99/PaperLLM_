The paper introduces FreeLB, an enhanced adversarial training algorithm aimed at improving the generalization of natural language understanding models. While traditional adversarial training focuses on robustness, often at the expense of generalization in computer vision, this study highlights that adversarial training can enhance both robustness and generalization in language models.

FreeLB operates by introducing adversarial perturbations directly to word embeddings and minimizing the adversarial risk within various regions around input samples. This approach was validated using Transformer-based models on tasks like commonsense reasoning and evaluated against the GLUE benchmark. The results showed significant improvements: the BERT-base model's test score increased from 78.3 to 79.4, while RoBERTa-large improved from 88.5 to 88.8. Additionally, FreeLB achieved state-of-the-art accuracies of 85.44% and 67.75% on ARC-Easy and ARC-Challenge, respectively.

The paper emphasizes that FreeLB enhances the invariance in the embedding space, which correlates positively with generalization performance. It also demonstrates that the FreeLB method can be generalized to improve performance across various tasks when applied to other models like RoBERTa-large and ALBERT. Overall, the study presents a promising direction for leveraging adversarial training to enhance natural language understanding capabilities.
