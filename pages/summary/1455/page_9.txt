The paper presents FreeLB, an adversarial training approach aimed at enhancing natural language understanding through improved robustness and generalization of Transformer-based models such as BERT, RoBERTa, and ALBERT. FreeLB introduces perturbations to continuous word embeddings using a gradient method to minimize adversarial risk efficiently.

The empirical results on the GLUE benchmark demonstrate that FreeLB outperforms various models, including BERT-large, XLNet-large, RoBERTa-large, and ALBERT-xxlarge-v2, achieving new state-of-the-art performance across multiple datasets. Specifically, FreeLB models consistently show improved accuracy and metrics compared to their non-adversarial counterparts, highlighting its effectiveness in boosting model performance.

The paper also contrasts FreeLB with YOPO, indicating that FreeLB consistently delivers superior results across datasets by implementing a modified approach to adversarial updates, thereby addressing limitations found in YOPO's original design.

In conclusion, while FreeLB shows significant advancements in adversarial training, the authors note that it incurs higher computational overhead compared to standard SGD, suggesting future work could focus on optimizing this process to balance efficiency and performance.
