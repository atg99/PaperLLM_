The paper discusses an approach to enhance the robustness of pre-trained large-scale language models, such as BERT and RoBERTa, through adversarial training focused on the embedding space. The authors propose creating virtual adversarial examples by applying norm-bounded perturbations to the input embeddings during fine-tuning, rather than generating actual adversarial examples, which is challenging due to the contextual nature of language.

The method involves modifying only the concatenated word or sub-word embeddings while keeping other components of the sentence representation intact. The authors utilize a gradient-based approach to add adversarial perturbations, ensuring that the model's predictions remain stable despite these modifications.

The paper presents a formulation for standard adversarial training, aiming to minimize the maximum risk over perturbations, and employs Projected Gradient Descent (PGD) for the optimization process. However, generating multi-step adversarial examples, which are crucial for robustness, significantly increases computational costs.

To address this issue, the authors introduce a technique called Free Adversarial Training (FreeAT), which allows for efficient gradient computation during PGD steps, thereby reducing the computational burden associated with adversarial training while maintaining model performance. This approach aims to achieve robust language understanding in a more resource-efficient manner.
