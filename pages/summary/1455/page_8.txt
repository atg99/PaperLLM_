This paper presents a comparison of various training methods for the RoBERTa-Large model on the GLUE benchmark, focusing on the effectiveness of FreeLB, a method designed to enhance robustness and generalization. The results are summarized in two tables: the first details the median scores and standard deviations for tasks RTE, CoLA, and MRPC, showing that FreeLB outperforms vanilla training and PGD in terms of accuracy. The second table analyzes the maximum increase in loss in the vicinity of clean samples, revealing that FreeLB maintains lower loss increments compared to other methods, indicating better robustness.

Key findings include the importance of reusing dropout masks in FreeLB, which leads to significant improvements in model performance. An ablation study demonstrates that FreeLB yields better robustness and generalization than PGD, particularly in the context of shallow-layer updates. The analysis shows that even models with high accuracy can exhibit vulnerabilities, but FreeLB consistently results in the smallest loss increments across different datasets, affirming its effectiveness in enhancing model robustness.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the advantages of FreeLB in training robust models and provides a comprehensive evaluation of its performance relative to other methods, contributing valuable insights to the field of natural language processing.
