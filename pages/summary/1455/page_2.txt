This paper presents a thorough evaluation of various adversarial training algorithms applied to advanced language understanding models. The study compares algorithms such as standard PGD, FreeAT, and YOPO, highlighting FreeLB as the most effective method. FreeLB significantly enhances the performance of models on key language benchmarks, notably improving the BERT-base model's score on the GLUE benchmark from 78.3 to 79.4 and elevating the RoBERTa-large model's score from 88.5 to 88.8, achieving top results on most sub-tasks. The paper also demonstrates FreeLB's effectiveness in enhancing RoBERTa-large's performance on question answering tasks like ARC and CommonsenseQA.

In the context of adversarial training, the paper discusses previous work, particularly the PGD-based approach, which effectively addresses the obfuscated gradient issue but incurs high costs and can produce complex loss surfaces. Alternative methods like FreeAT and YOPO aim to reduce the computational burden by optimizing model parameters and adversarial perturbations more efficiently.

The paper highlights the growing interest in adversarial examples within the text domain, contrasting it with the primarily image-focused research. Previous studies have explored various strategies for generating text adversaries, but this work focuses on leveraging adversarial training to enhance the performance of state-of-the-art transformer models without generating adversarial examples. The findings suggest that robust language models can achieve better results on clean data, reinforcing the potential of adversarial training in improving language understanding tasks.
