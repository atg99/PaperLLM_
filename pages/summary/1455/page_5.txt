The paper presents FreeLB, a novel approach to adversarial training that enhances the robustness of models compared to traditional methods like PGD. FreeLB operates under multiple norm constraints, promoting greater invariance to adversarial perturbations and leading to improved performance in embedding spaces. The authors argue that this enhanced robustness can facilitate better generalization, supported by empirical results and previous research.

The paper also explores the interaction between adversarial training and dropout, noting that dropout is typically not used with adversarial training. However, when applied to certain language models, it results in optimizing different network configurations at each ascent step. The authors propose a more effective strategy that maintains a consistent dropout mask during optimization, aligning with the goal of minimizing the expected loss across varying network states.

To validate FreeLB, the authors conducted extensive experiments across three Natural Language Understanding benchmarks: GLUE, ARC, and CommonsenseQA. They compared the robustness and generalization capabilities of FreeLB against other adversarial training algorithms, providing detailed experimental setups and results in the appendix. The GLUE benchmark, comprising nine distinct tasks, was employed to assess the effectiveness of FreeLB in various classification and regression contexts, utilizing implementations of BERT and RoBERTa for consistency with established methodologies.
