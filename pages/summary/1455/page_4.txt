The paper presents FreeLB, a novel approach for large-batch adversarial training that aims to enhance robustness and generalization in neural networks. Unlike previous methods such as FreeAT and YOPO, which face challenges like stale gradients and assumptions about differentiable losses, FreeLB performs multiple iterations of Projected Gradient Descent (PGD) to generate adversarial examples while simultaneously accumulating parameter gradients. This process allows for a more effective update of model parameters by leveraging the gradients from each ascent step, thus approximating a local maximum within defined bounds.

The algorithm operates by initializing model parameters and iteratively refining adversarial perturbations and gradients through a series of ascent steps. FreeLB effectively expands the training batch size by incorporating adversarial examples generated at different steps, which leads to a minimized maximum risk across these perturbations. The authors argue that this method can achieve lower generalization error compared to traditional PGD-based training, supported by theoretical insights regarding the benefits of invariance to transformations.

The paper includes empirical comparisons with YOPO to demonstrate the effectiveness of FreeLB. Overall, FreeLB represents a significant advancement in adversarial training methodologies by addressing limitations of prior approaches and providing a more robust framework for training models against adversarial attacks.
