The paper discusses ethical and environmental considerations related to the use of language models, focusing on the empirical study of instruction tuning's impact on zero-shot performance. It highlights two main ethical concerns: the potential propagation of biases from labeled datasets into downstream tasks and the risk associated with easier access to instruction-tuned models, which may enhance both benefits and risks. 

In terms of environmental impact, the authors report the energy cost and carbon footprint of pretrained models, noting that the additional energy required for instruction tuning is minimal compared to pretraining. 

The contributions of various authors are detailed, with specific roles in idea conception, implementation, experimentation, and manuscript writing outlined. Acknowledgments are given to individuals who provided feedback and support throughout the research process.
