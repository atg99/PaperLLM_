The paper examines the performance of the FLAN model on various datasets, specifically focusing on the impact of data cleanliness on evaluation outcomes. The authors analyzed the correlation between the percentage of clean data in datasets and the performance metrics (accuracy, F1, BLEU) of FLAN. They found no significant correlation suggesting that evaluation on clean data did not consistently outperform evaluations on all data, including contaminated examples.

The study highlights that datasets ANLI R1 and R2 exhibited high levels of data contamination, primarily due to the overlap of example contexts from Wikipedia, which was more pronounced than in GPT-3. The authors argue that this contamination is unlikely to negatively influence performance since the model's ability to generalize to new sentences is not hindered by prior exposure to specific contexts.

Notably, only the ReCoRD and PIQA datasets showed a clean subset performance that was significantly lower than overall performance, indicating that prior exposure to complete sentences in these language modeling tasks could aid in downstream evaluations. The authors reference previous findings by Brown et al., noting substantial contamination rates in these datasets.

The analysis is cautious, acknowledging the limitations of their n-gram matching procedure and the challenges in accurately detecting test contamination. They also point out that the larger pretraining corpus used for FLAN compared to GPT-3 may result in a higher incidence of false positives in identifying dirty data.
