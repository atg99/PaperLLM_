The paper presents FLAN, a large language model (LM) that improves zero-shot performance through instruction tuning. While existing models like GPT-3 excel in few-shot learning, they struggle with zero-shot tasks. The authors hypothesize that the lack of exemplars in zero-shot scenarios hinders performance, particularly when task prompts differ from pretraining data formats.

FLAN, which is based on a 137 billion parameter model, is fine-tuned on over 60 NLP datasets represented as natural language instructions. This approach enables the model to generalize better to unseen tasks. The authors evaluate FLAN's zero-shot capabilities by clustering NLP datasets and holding out specific clusters during instruction tuning. Results indicate that FLAN significantly surpasses the zero-shot performance of GPT-3, even outperforming its few-shot performance on several datasets.

The study highlights that increasing the number of task clusters during instruction tuning enhances zero-shot performance and that the effectiveness of instruction tuning is contingent upon model scale. Instruction tuning effectively combines elements of pretraining and prompting, allowing the model to learn from diverse tasks and improve its responsiveness to natural language instructions. The source code for the instruction tuning dataset is publicly available.
