The paper discusses the differences between FLAN instructions and prompts used in GPT-3 and T5. Unlike GPT-3, which uses prompts resembling pretraining data, and T5, which uses simple tags, FLAN prompts are designed to mimic human task requests. This design leads to poor performance in pretrained language models without fine-tuning, as illustrated by FLAN's failure to translate sentences correctly.

The paper identifies several limitations of FLAN, including difficulties with simple tasks, a context length limitation of 1024 tokens, and a predominance of English training data. Despite these challenges, the authors suggest that instruction tuning could enhance task transfer and performance in future work.

Additionally, the paper clarifies that the ten unique templates used are specific to each dataset rather than task clusters, due to slight variations in datasets within the same cluster. Finally, it addresses performance concerns related to the untuned LaMDA-PT model, noting that while scaling improves performance for tasks seen during instruction tuning, untuned models may not benefit similarly.
