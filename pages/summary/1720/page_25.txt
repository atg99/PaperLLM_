This paper presents an analysis of zero-shot performance on unseen task clusters using various prompt tuning techniques and investigates the impact of data contamination on model evaluation. The study compares the effectiveness of different input formats during multi-task fine-tuning, focusing on the Natural Instructions framework (FLAN) and its performance across various datasets, including NLI, reading comprehension, closed-book QA, and translation tasks.

Key findings include:

1. **Ablation Study Results**: The inclusion of task/dataset names during multi-task fine-tuning significantly improved performance, particularly in NLI tasks, suggesting that structured inputs enhance model understanding. The highest NLI score was achieved with the task/dataset name format.

2. **Prompt Tuning Performance**: FLAN outperformed LaMDA-PT in several tasks, indicating that instruction tuning leads to better performance when combined with prompt tuning. The performance metrics showed that FLAN consistently yielded higher accuracy across various datasets.

3. **Data Contamination Analysis**: The study employed a data contamination procedure to assess the influence of pretraining data overlap on model performance. Results indicated that many datasets contained significant overlaps with the pretraining corpus, but evaluating on clean data did not consistently yield worse results than evaluating on the full dataset. Moreover, datasets with fewer clean examples exhibited higher performance variance.

4. **Overlap Findings**: The analysis revealed that datasets like DROP and SQuADv2 had substantial overlap with pretraining data, mostly in the context of examples rather than in both questions and answers, suggesting a limited impact on overall evaluation integrity.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of instruction tuning and careful evaluation of data contamination in understanding model performance on various NLP tasks.
