This paper discusses the advancements in language models, particularly focusing on instruction tuning as a form of multi-task learning (MTL) aimed at enhancing zero-shot generalization to unseen tasks. The authors reference a broad spectrum of prior research in NLP, highlighting that while traditional MTL emphasizes performance improvements across known tasks, their approach seeks to improve adaptability to new tasks without prior exposure.

The paper also situates its work within the context of zero-shot learning and meta-learning, noting the historical use of these concepts in classifying unseen categories and adapting to new tasks based on limited examples. The emergent capabilities of large language models, such as those demonstrated by GPT-3, have sparked interest in how these models generalize to novel tasks.

Additionally, the authors discuss prompting techniques that leverage the extensive knowledge embedded in large language models to perform various NLP tasks. They compare their approach to existing methods that use continuous inputs for optimization and highlight the potential of instruction tuning to enhance performance further. The paper emphasizes the goal of making large models more accessible to non-technical users by finetuning them to respond to natural language instructions rather than relying solely on prompt engineering.

Lastly, the paper reviews the established practice of finetuning pretrained language models, particularly those with hundreds of billions of parameters, and notes recent efforts in applying these models to specific tasks like program synthesis and summarization. The authors also mention the development of sparse mixture of experts models, which significantly increase parameter counts, reflecting ongoing innovations in the field.
