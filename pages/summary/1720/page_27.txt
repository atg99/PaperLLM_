The paper presents an analysis of various datasets used in natural language processing, focusing on evaluation metrics such as accuracy, F1 score, and BLEU score. It categorizes datasets based on their cleanliness, defined by the presence of n-gram collisions with the pretraining corpus. The results indicate a range of performance across datasets, with total counts and metrics reported for each. For instance, the DROP dataset has an F1 score of 22.4, while SQuADv2 achieves 41.3. The ANLI datasets show varying accuracy, with R1 at 48.1 and R2 at 42.9. The paper highlights the differences in performance metrics between clean and dirty examples, providing insights into the impact of dataset quality on model evaluation. Overall, the findings underscore the importance of dataset cleanliness in training and evaluating language models.
