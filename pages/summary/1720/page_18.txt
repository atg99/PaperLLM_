The paper presents DART, a method for generating text from open-domain structured data records. It builds on previous work in natural language processing, including techniques for data summarization, zero-shot translation, and contextual word representations. The authors discuss the importance of structured data in generating coherent and contextually relevant text. They emphasize the role of human feedback in improving language models, as highlighted in recent research. DART aims to bridge the gap between structured data and natural language generation, addressing challenges in extreme summarization and multilingual applications. The paper references various foundational studies and datasets that inform its approach, underscoring the collaborative nature of advancements in natural language understanding.
