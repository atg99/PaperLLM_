This document references a collection of significant research papers in the field of natural language processing and machine learning. Key contributions include the development of BERT for deep bidirectional language understanding, the introduction of semi-supervised sequence learning, and advancements in zero-shot abusive language detection using hybrid emoji-based masked language models. The PASCAL Recognising Textual Entailment challenge is highlighted for its role in evaluating textual entailment, while GLaM presents efficient scaling of language models with a mixture-of-experts approach. The CommitmentBank investigates projection in discourse, and DROP establishes a benchmark for reading comprehension requiring discrete reasoning. Other notable works address paraphrase generation, the impact of semantic noise on language generation, and the understanding of back-translation in machine translation. Additionally, the Turking Test explores the comprehension of instructions by language models, and Multi-news provides a dataset for multi-document summarization. Each cited work contributes to advancing the understanding and capabilities of language models and their applications.
