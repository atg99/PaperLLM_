The paper discusses the FLAN model, an instruction-tuned version of the LaMDA-PT language model, designed for both classification and generation tasks. For classification, the authors address limitations in prior rank classification approaches by introducing an options suffix, which appends the token OPTIONS and a list of output classes to improve the model's awareness of desired responses. 

The model architecture employs a dense left-to-right transformer with 137 billion parameters, pretrained on diverse datasets including web documents and dialog data. The instruction tuning process involves mixing datasets and limiting training examples to balance sizes, followed by 30,000 gradient steps using the Adafactor optimizer. 

Evaluation results show that FLAN outperforms LaMDA-PT, GPT-3, and GLaM across multiple natural language tasks, demonstrating significant improvements due to instruction tuning. FLAN achieves better zero-shot performance than GPT-3 on 20 out of 25 datasets and surpasses GPT-3's few-shot performance on 10 datasets. It also outperforms GLaM in several comparisons. Overall, the findings highlight the effectiveness of instruction tuning in enhancing model performance across various natural language processing tasks.
