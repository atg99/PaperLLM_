The paper investigates the impact of instruction tuning on the performance of language models in zero-shot settings across various unseen tasks. It contrasts its approach with previous studies that focused on fine-tuning models on specific downstream tasks. The authors highlight the effectiveness of framing NLP tasks as question-answering, referencing works by McCann et al. and UniﬁedQA, which demonstrated transfer learning benefits.

The study emphasizes the growing interest in instruction-based NLP, noting that while some prior research involved fine-tuning for few-shot learning, the authors specifically address zero-shot learning with a significantly larger model. They compare their findings with recent works by Mishra et al. and Ye et al., both of which explored few-shot learning improvements, but the current study distinguishes itself by focusing on zero-shot performance and the significance of model scale.

The paper also references related works, including those by Min et al. and Sanh et al., which examined instruction tuning and its effects on model performance. Lastly, it mentions OpenAI's InstructGPT, which utilizes human feedback for model refinement. The authors conclude that instruction tuning and model scale are crucial for enhancing performance on unseen tasks.
