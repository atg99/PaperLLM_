The paper presents findings on the effectiveness of instruction tuning across various natural language processing tasks, particularly highlighting its strengths and weaknesses. Instruction tuning significantly enhances performance in tasks that can be framed as instructions, such as natural language inference (NLI), reading comprehension, closed-book question answering (QA), and translation. However, it is less effective for tasks that are inherently structured as language modeling, where instructions may be redundant.

In NLI, FLAN, the model evaluated, outperforms all baseline models by a substantial margin. This success is attributed to a more natural phrasing of NLI tasks compared to traditional sentence continuation methods. In reading comprehension, FLAN also shows superior performance across multiple datasets, significantly outperforming GPT-3 on BoolQ, although LaMDA-PT performs well on this task too.

For closed-book QA, FLAN surpasses GPT-3 across all datasets tested, demonstrating better performance on certain datasets compared to GLaM. In translation tasks, FLAN's performance is comparable to that of GPT-3, despite the training data being predominantly in English.

The results indicate that instruction tuning is particularly beneficial when tasks can be explicitly framed as instructions, leading to improved model performance across various benchmarks.
