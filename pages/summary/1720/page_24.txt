The paper investigates the impact of instruction tuning on model performance across various task clusters, emphasizing the importance of dataset diversity. It builds on earlier findings that increasing task clusters enhances performance. The authors conduct experiments by varying the number of datasets and instruction templates used for tuning. Results indicate that using more datasets per task cluster boosts performance by nearly 10% on average for held-out clusters, while the effect of increasing instruction templates is minimal, particularly when only one task is present per cluster.

The analysis also highlights that the unpredictability of fine-tuning large language models suggests they do not easily overfit to single tasks. Furthermore, the study explores how instruction tuning aids in prompt tuning, with suggestions for future research extending beyond classification tasks.

Lastly, the authors address potential data contamination concerns, noting that the extensive pretraining corpus may include examples from evaluation datasets, potentially skewing performance results. They conduct a post-hoc analysis similar to that of GPT-3 to assess this issue.
