The paper evaluates the current state of AI ethics, highlighting the importance of virtue ethics in fostering moral character among individuals involved in AI development. It argues that cultivating technomoral virtues such as honesty, justice, and empathy is essential for ethical decision-making within organizations. Responsibility for the implications of AI applications should be collectively embraced by all professionals in the field, as suggested by Floridi's concept of distributed responsibility. The paper emphasizes the need for practitioners to consider the potential consequences of their actions and to explore ethical alternatives in their work.

Additionally, the paper calls for institutional reforms, including legal frameworks, independent auditing mechanisms, and the incorporation of ethics into university curricula. However, it notes that many of these recommendations remain unaddressed. The conclusion indicates that AI ethics is often ineffective, lacking enforcement mechanisms and frequently used as a marketing tool rather than a genuine ethical guide. Empirical evidence suggests that ethics guidelines do not significantly impact the decision-making of software developers, leading to a perception of ethics as extraneous in practice.
