The paper discusses the ethical challenges surrounding artificial intelligence (AI) development, emphasizing the disconnect between technical frameworks and the moral responsibilities of software developers. It argues that distributed responsibility and a lack of awareness regarding the societal impacts of AI lead to diminished accountability among developers, often overshadowed by economic incentives that conflict with ethical principles such as beneficence and justice.

Despite some ethical initiatives aimed at addressing specific issues like accountability and privacy, many critical ethical dimensions are overlooked in existing guidelines. These include concerns about the potential dangers of advanced AI, social impacts of algorithmic filtering, and the influence of funding sources on research. The paper suggests that while some omissions can be justified, others necessitate updates to ethical guidelines.

The author advocates for a shift from a rigid, rule-based ethical framework to a more flexible, virtue-oriented approach that considers the nuances of individual situations. This new AI ethics should bridge the gap between technical details and ethical considerations, fostering a deeper understanding of the social implications of AI while encouraging responsible, empathetic decision-making among developers. Ultimately, the paper calls for a more integrated approach to AI ethics that balances technical knowledge with an understanding of social and personal dynamics.
