The paper evaluates the recurring ethical issues in AI guidelines, highlighting that accountability, privacy, and fairness appear in around 80% of them. These aspects are often associated with technical solutions, as substantial efforts are made to achieve ethical targets through advancements in areas like explainable AI and bias mitigation. Major tech companies have developed tools aimed at ensuring fairness in machine learning.

However, the paper critiques the predominant male-oriented perspective in AI ethics, referencing psychologist Carol Gilligan's work on moral reasoning. It points out that the ethical discourse in AI is largely shaped by a male viewpoint that emphasizes rationality and technical solutions, neglecting a more empathic approach that considers care, welfare, and social responsibility. The analysis reveals an imbalance in gender representation among authors of guidelines, particularly in technical communities like FAT ML, where the proportion of female authors is significantly lower.

The paper concludes that many AI ethics guidelines focus on technical fixes, often overlooking the broader social and ecological contexts in which AI operates. In contrast, reports from AI Now, a women-led organization, emphasize these relational networks, advocating for a more holistic approach to AI ethics that transcends mere technical optimization.
