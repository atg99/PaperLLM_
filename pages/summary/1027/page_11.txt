the AI Now Report 2018 emphasizes the urgent need for improved governance in the AI industry, highlighting the inadequacy of current internal governance structures in ensuring accountability for AI systems. Ethical guidelines are often viewed as non-binding corporate self-governance measures, leading to skepticism about companies' voluntary adherence to ethical practices.

The paper discusses the tension between ethical principles and the objectives of research, industry, and business, using sociological theories, particularly system theory, to explain this dynamic. Different social systems operate with distinct codes and communication media, and while structural couplings can influence decisions across systems, they do not alter their overall autonomy. This autonomy results in a limited capacity for ethical interventions within the AI industry, as decisions are primarily driven by each system's own codes and values.

Moreover, the paper notes that unethical behavior is influenced not only by economic incentives but also by individual characteristics and organizational environments. However, these factors are often shaped by the overarching economic system, rendering ethical considerations "operationally effectless."

Despite these challenges, the paper recognizes instances of deviation from purely economic motivations in the tech industry, citing protests against unethical AI projects by employees at companies like Google and Microsoft. These actions may reflect genuine ethical concerns but can also be interpreted through an economic lens, as reputational risks play a significant role.

Finally, the paper points out that compliance with ethical guidelines in AI practices is lacking, although advancements have been made in areas such as privacy, fairness, and explainability.
