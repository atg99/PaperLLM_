The paper evaluates various ethical guidelines related to artificial intelligence (AI) and identifies significant gaps in their coverage. It highlights that only the Montréal Declaration for Responsible Development of AI and the AI Now 2019 Report address democratic control and governance of AI systems, explicitly prohibiting the imposition of lifestyles by AI, as seen in the Chinese scoring system. The paper criticizes the lack of discussion regarding the potential political abuse of AI, such as automated propaganda and election fraud, and emphasizes the absence of diversity within the AI community, where predominantly white men influence AI design and objectives.

Furthermore, it notes that robot ethics, while existing, are rarely mentioned in the guidelines analyzed. The ethical implications of anthropomorphized AI devices and their potential to desensitize users to violence are discussed, alongside the neglect of ethical dilemmas like the trolley problem in relation to autonomous vehicles. The paper also points out that most guidelines fail to address the hidden social and ecological costs of AI, including issues related to sustainability, such as lithium mining and the exploitation of low-wage workers in data labeling. Overall, the analysis reveals a need for more comprehensive ethical frameworks that address these critical concerns in AI development and deployment.
