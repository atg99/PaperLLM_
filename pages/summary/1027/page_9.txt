The paper discusses the ethical implications of artificial intelligence (AI) and highlights the challenges posed by the competitive landscape of AI development, particularly between the United States and China. It notes the limited literature addressing the misuse of AI technologies, despite evidence of their potential for significant harm. The discussion includes China's ambition to lead in AI by 2030, leveraging vast data resources and specialized companies for data preparation. However, the paper identifies a gap in foundational research compared to the US, where investments are more diversified.

The narrative of an AI race fosters a nationalistic mindset, where failure to keep pace with perceived competitors could lead to technological inferiority, particularly in military contexts. This competitive atmosphere may discourage ethical considerations in AI research and development, promoting a reckless focus on technological superiority instead of collaboration for global benefit. The paper contrasts this competitive approach with the ideals of developing AI for people and for global good, as advocated by various researchers and initiatives.

Ultimately, the paper argues that the established AI race promotes an adversarial mentality, framing competitors as threats, which contradicts ethical principles that caution against such divisions. This mindset undermines the potential for cooperative efforts to create benevolent AI systems that prioritize societal welfare.
