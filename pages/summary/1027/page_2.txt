The paper by T. Hagendorff discusses the limitations of ethical guidelines in the context of artificial intelligence (AI) and their implications for accountability. It argues that while reputational losses and restrictions on professional memberships exist as mechanisms for addressing misconduct, they are insufficient to create a significant deterrent against unethical practices. The author highlights that the appeal of ethics to AI companies stems from the ability to self-govern, thereby undermining the need for robust legal frameworks that could mitigate technological risks and prevent abuse.

Hagendorff critiques the tendency of AI companies to adopt ethical guidelines, suggesting that such measures often serve to placate public concerns rather than enact meaningful change. The paper examines the role of industry-led ethics, noting that these guidelines may create an illusion of accountability while allowing organizations to maintain criticized practices. The author uses the example of the "Partnership on AI," a coalition of major tech companies, to illustrate how membership can be leveraged to deflect calls for stricter regulations.

The paper proposes a theoretical analysis of 22 major AI ethics guidelines to identify gaps and omissions within them. It further aims to compare these principles with actual practices in AI research and development, assessing their real-world impact. Finally, the author seeks to explore ways to transform AI ethics from a theoretical discourse into actionable frameworks that can guide ethical practices in the industry.
