The paper discusses the perception of individuals as "others" in the context of AI development, suggesting that reframing this perspective towards global cooperation is essential for creating beneficial and safe AI. It examines the effectiveness of ethical guidelines in influencing decision-making among software engineers. A controlled study highlighted that these guidelines, such as the ACM code of ethics, have minimal impact on ethical decision-making, with no significant differences observed in responses from those aware of the guidelines versus those who were not.

The paper argues that the ineffectiveness of ethics can be attributed to broader socio-economic factors. Companies prioritize profit from AI applications over ethical considerations, leading to a lack of systematic education on ethical issues for engineers. The urgency to deliver results often results in the neglect of ethical principles, with ethics serving merely as a superficial public relations tool rather than a genuine guiding framework. This situation is exacerbated by the absence of enforcement mechanisms for ethical practices in AI, resulting in closed industry settings where user consent and privacy are frequently compromised for the sake of functionality and profit.
