The paper argues that to bridge the gap between ethical principles and technical implementation, guidelines must evolve from broad normative statements to more concrete, actionable standards that can be directly applied in AI development. 

The authors highlight the importance of developing a "microethics" approach that involves a detailed examination of the technical aspects of AI systems. This includes understanding data handling, algorithm design, and the selection of training datasets. By focusing on these specifics, ethicists can provide more relevant guidance that aligns with the realities of AI technology.

A notable example cited is the work by Gebru et al., which advocates for standardized datasheets for training datasets. This initiative aims to enhance transparency and fairness in machine learning by providing practitioners with essential information about the datasets they use. However, such concrete contributions are rare, and most existing ethical guidelines remain too abstract to be effectively operationalized in practice.

Ultimately, the paper calls for a transformation in ethical discourse, urging ethicists to engage more deeply with the technical dimensions of AI to ensure their principles can be truly impactful in shaping responsible AI development.
