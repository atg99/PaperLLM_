The paper discusses the challenges of applying AI ethics within geographically and conceptually diverse groups of researchers, where ethical guidelines often lack specificity and depth. This superficiality can lead to a diffusion of ethical responsibility, with developers feeling disconnected from the guidelines.

The author contrasts two ethical frameworks: deontology and virtue ethics. Deontological ethics relies on strict principles and duties, while virtue ethics emphasizes character traits and situational judgment. Current AI ethics predominantly follows a deontological model, which can reduce ethics to a checklist approach. In contrast, virtue ethics encourages a deeper understanding of the moral character of technology developers, focusing on their personal development and social context.

The paper argues for integrating virtue ethics into AI ethics, suggesting that ethics should not merely restrict actions but should foster personal growth, responsibility, and the courage to act ethically. By doing so, ethics can enhance rather than inhibit technological innovation, promoting a more holistic approach to ethical considerations in technology development.
