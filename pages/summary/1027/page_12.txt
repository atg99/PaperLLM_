The paper discusses the paradox of advancements in artificial intelligence (AI) occurring alongside a decline in privacy. While various privacy-preserving techniques, such as cryptography and differential privacy, have been developed, the AI boom has largely relied on vast amounts of personal data obtained from privacy-invasive platforms and devices. This situation has led to what the author describes as a post-privacy and black box society, where despite efforts to enhance transparency and accountability, significant non-transparency persists due to the complexity of technological systems and strategic organizational choices.

The author highlights several areas where ethical goals related to AI are being underachieved, such as safety, cybersecurity, and human oversight. Notably, issues of gender diversity in AI are emphasized, with statistics showing that a majority of faculty in top universities and job applicants in the U.S. AI field are male. Furthermore, the paper critiques the treatment of individuals as mere data subjects, pointing out that AI-driven decisions often overshadow individual circumstances, undermining human autonomy. Lastly, it notes that AI has contributed to social discord through phenomena like echo chambers and the spread of misinformation, which have detrimental effects on social cohesion and public discourse.
