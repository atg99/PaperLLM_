The paper by T. Hagendorff discusses the intersection of artificial intelligence (AI), ethics, and the influence of corporate funding on research. It highlights several critical issues, including the neglect of important factors such as low wages, working conditions, and psychological impacts on workers within the AI community. The author points out that existing guidelines fail to address the implications of public-private partnerships and the role of industry funding in AI research, raising concerns about the potential erosion of research independence.

In the section on AI in practice, the close relationship between business and science is emphasized, illustrated by the sponsorship of major AI conferences by corporate entities and the significant increase in corporate-affiliated AI publications. The burgeoning AI market, valued at over 7 billion dollars, is characterized by a rapid rise in AI startups and patent registrations across various industries.

However, the paper also critically examines the adverse effects of AI implementation. It details potential malevolent uses of AI, particularly in military applications, automated propaganda, surveillance, and social control by governments. The author raises alarms over job displacement caused by AI, unethical experimentation on society, data breaches, biased algorithms, and the deployment of unsafe AI products. Additionally, the risks posed by malicious actors leveraging AI for cyberattacks and misinformation campaigns are discussed, underscoring the urgent need for ethical considerations and regulatory frameworks in the AI landscape.
