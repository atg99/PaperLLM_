This paper analyzes AI ethics guidelines with a focus on their content and omissions. It highlights that industrial guidelines typically mention an average of 9.1 ethical aspects, while scientific guidelines average 10.8. Microsoft’s AI ethics principles are noted for their brevity, while the IEEE guidelines are extensive, exceeding 100,000 words. Despite the guidelines addressing various ethical concerns, only two provide substantial technical explanations, with the “Malicious Use of AI” guidelines offering the most detail.

The paper also discusses significant omissions in the guidelines, emphasizing that while some omissions, like those regarding political abuse and hidden social costs of AI, lack justification, others, such as discussions on artificial general intelligence and machine consciousness, may be considered speculative. However, given the concerns about potential existential threats posed by artificial general intelligence, the author argues that these topics warrant inclusion in ethics discussions. The lack of focus on such issues may stem from the backgrounds of those drafting the guidelines, primarily in computer science rather than philosophy, leading to a disconnect between technical expertise and ethical considerations.
