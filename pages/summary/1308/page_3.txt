The paper introduces a Markov Chain Augmentation Process for data augmentation aimed at enhancing model generalization through class-preserving transformations. The authors propose a method where augmentations are applied sequentially to original data points, with occasional retraction to original examples to maintain proximity to the training set. 

Formally, the augmentation process is modeled as a discrete time Markov chain, where transitions between states (augmented examples) are governed by stochastic transition matrices. The paper defines a transition matrix and stationary distribution, demonstrating that as the rate of augmentation increases, the distribution of augmented data becomes more distorted.

The authors further show that classifying unseen examples using this augmentation model results in a kernel classifier. Specifically, they prove that under certain conditions, the classification can be expressed in terms of a kernel matrix and a weighted sum of the original labels. This establishes a connection between data augmentation and kernel methods, highlighting that kernels naturally emerge from the augmentation process.

The findings suggest that augmentations not only improve data diversity but also influence the feature representation learned by classifiers. The paper concludes with implications for the understanding of augmentation in machine learning, emphasizing the relationship between augmentation strategies and kernel-based classification methods.
