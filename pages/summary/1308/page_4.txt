The paper presents a kernel theory of modern data augmentation, emphasizing its relevance even when starting with non-kernel classifiers. It establishes a connection between data augmentation and kernel methods, particularly in how augmentation impacts features and learned models. The authors argue that data augmentation acts as a regularizer, reducing generalization error without affecting training error, and identify two key effects: increasing invariance through feature averaging and penalizing model complexity via variance regularization.

In Section 4, the authors explore these effects in detail, particularly focusing on kernel classifiers. They introduce a general augmentation process that modifies the objective function used in training by incorporating an augmentation kernel. This process allows for the computation of an average augmented feature for each data point, leading to a new kernel that reflects the distribution of transformed points.

The analysis shows that training with augmented data is akin to training with an augmented kernel, which enhances model invariance by averaging features from transformed inputs. This theoretical framework is supported by empirical validation and is demonstrated to have practical utility in both kernel and deep learning contexts. Overall, the paper highlights the critical role of data augmentation in improving model robustness and performance.
