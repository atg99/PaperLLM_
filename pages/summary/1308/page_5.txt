The paper presents a kernel theory of modern data augmentation, focusing on the effectiveness of approximate objectives in enhancing model performance. The authors validate two proposed approximations, denoted as ^g(w) and ~g(w), against the true augmented objective g(w) using the MNIST dataset. They demonstrate that the approximations closely align with the true objective in both objective value and prediction accuracy, as evidenced by the plots in Figure 1.

The authors leverage Jensen’s inequality to assert that the approximated objective ^g(w) is less than or equal to the true objective g(w), particularly when the loss function is strongly convex and smooth. They provide a mathematical framework to quantify the differences between the solutions of the original and approximated problems.

Furthermore, the paper establishes that the second-order approximation ~g(w) serves as variance regularization, which enhances the robustness of classifiers. By analyzing the Taylor expansion of the objective, they reveal that data augmentation effectively introduces a regularization term that is independent of labels, thus favoring weight vectors that yield similar outputs for augmented data.

Empirical validation on MNIST and CIFAR-10 datasets confirms that the second-order approximation outperforms the first-order approximation in approximating the actual objective, underscoring the importance of the variance regularization aspect of data augmentation. The findings support the argument that data augmentation acts as a form of data-dependent regularization, contributing to improved model accuracy.
