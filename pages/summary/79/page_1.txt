The paper discusses the development of an intrusion detection system (IDS) that utilizes a Long Short-Term Memory Recurrent Neural Network (LSTM-RNN) combined with a genetic algorithm (GA) for optimal feature selection to classify network attacks. The study focuses on the NSL-KDD dataset to assess the effectiveness of this method in distinguishing between normal and abnormal network traffic, as well as identifying specific attack types, including Denial of Service (DoS), Probing, User to Root (U2R), and Remote to Local (R2L).

The research reveals that integrating the GA significantly enhances the classification accuracy of the LSTM-RNN in both binary (normal vs. abnormal) and multi-class classifications. Performance metrics such as accuracy, recall, precision, F-score, and confusion matrix were used to evaluate the IDS's effectiveness. The findings indicate that the LSTM-RNN with GA outperforms traditional methods like Support Vector Machine (SVM) and Random Forest (RF) in multi-class classification tasks, while achieving comparable accuracy in binary classification as RF and better than SVM.

The paper highlights the importance of evolving IDS technologies to counteract the increasing frequency of cyberattacks and emphasizes the potential of deep learning techniques, particularly LSTM-RNN, in enhancing network security. Overall, the study provides valuable insights into improving IDS performance through advanced machine learning methodologies.
