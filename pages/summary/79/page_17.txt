The study presents findings on the effectiveness of a genetic algorithm (GA) in improving multi-class classification accuracy within network intrusion detection systems. The results, shown in confusion matrices, indicate that using 122 features leads to some misclassifications, particularly in the R2L and U2R classes due to limited training data. In contrast, utilizing 99 features with the GA significantly enhanced classification accuracy for R2L and U2R, as reflected in evaluation metrics such as precision, recall, F1-score, and overall accuracy.

Specifically, the classification metrics demonstrated that the model achieved an accuracy of 98.47% for Normal traffic and a notable improvement in R2L and U2R classes when using 99 features, with accuracies of 61.35% and 68.66%, respectively. The study highlights the potential of self-taught learning approaches, such as LSTM-RNN, which achieved high accuracy rates in classification tasks, thereby affirming the effectiveness of combining GA with deep learning techniques for enhanced performance in network security applications. The findings suggest that the choice of features and algorithm significantly influences classification outcomes in multi-class scenarios.
