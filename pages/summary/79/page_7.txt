The paper discusses the distribution of normal and attack records in the NSL-KDD dataset, detailing a total of 125,973 instances in the training set and 22,544 instances in the testing set, categorized into normal and various attack types.

In the related work section, the authors highlight the effectiveness of Recurrent Neural Network (RNN)-based intrusion detection systems (RNN-IDS) in achieving high classification accuracy, surpassing traditional machine learning methods such as J48, Support Vector Machines (SVM), Random Forest (RF), and Artificial Neural Networks (ANN). Various models, including CNN-LSTM and LSTM-RNN, have been tested on the KDD Cup'99 datasets, with LSTM showing excellent performance across multiple studies. The authors draw on feature selection methods from previous research, specifically utilizing Genetic Algorithms (GA) on the NSL-KDD datasets to optimize feature sets.

The paper introduces an LSTM-RNN-based Network Intrusion Detection System (NIDS) enhanced with GA for feature selection. The classification process involves both binary and multi-class classifications, focusing on metrics like accuracy, precision, recall, and F-score, supported by confusion matrix analysis. The LSTM-RNN models were configured with varying neuron counts in hidden layers to evaluate performance, and the implementation was carried out using Python 3.7 with relevant libraries like scikit-learn, TensorFlow, and Keras.
