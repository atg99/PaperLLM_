In this paper, the authors describe the development of an LSTM-RNN model for binary and multi-class classification tasks. They detail the selection of hyper-parameters, including batch size, epochs, learning rate, dropout, and activation function. Batch size refers to the number of training records processed in one iteration, while an epoch encompasses one full pass of the training dataset. The learning rate determines the extent of weight updates during training, and dropout serves as a regularization technique to prevent overfitting by randomly ignoring certain neurons during training. The activation function is utilized to convert input signals into output signals across the network layers.

The authors chose stochastic gradient descent (SGD) for multi-class classification due to its lower computational cost and efficiency compared to other optimizers that require all training samples for each iteration. For binary classification, the Adam optimizer was selected for its ease of implementation and lower memory requirements.

Following model training, the authors evaluated performance metrics such as accuracy, precision, recall, F-score, true positive rate, false positive rate, and confusion matrix using a testing dataset. They compared the performance of the LSTM-RNN model with SVM and RF classifiers based on these metrics.

The experimentation was conducted using TensorFlow and Keras for the LSTM-RNN model and scikit-learn for SVM and RF. The study involved binary classification for normal versus anomaly detection and multi-class classification for detecting various types of attacks. The feature set consisted of 122 features, with an optimal subset of 99 features identified through a genetic algorithm.

To mitigate overfitting, the authors adjusted dropout rates during training, varying from 1% to 30% depending on the number of hidden layers. Each experiment was repeated multiple times to ensure reliability in results.
