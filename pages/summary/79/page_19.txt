The paper discusses key performance metrics used in classification tasks, particularly in the context of anomaly detection. It defines several important metrics:

1. **False Positive Rate (FPR)**: This measures the percentage of normal instances incorrectly predicted as positive, calculated as FPR = FP / (FP + TN).

2. **Accuracy (AC)**: This reflects the percentage of correctly classified records over the total records, where accuracy is best when false positives and negatives have similar costs, expressed as AC = (TP + TN) / (TP + TN + FP + FN).

3. **Precision (P)**: This indicates the percentage of predicted anomalies that are actual anomalies, calculated by P = TP / (TP + FP) × 100%.

4. **Recall (R)**: This measures the ratio of correctly classified anomalous records to the total number of positive records, with high recall indicating effective recognition of the class, defined as R = TP / (TP + FN) × 100%.

5. **F-Score**: This is the harmonic mean of precision and recall, providing a balanced measure of a test's accuracy when the costs of false positives and negatives differ significantly, given by F = 2 × P × R / (P + R) × 100%.

6. **Confusion Matrix**: A table that outlines the performance of classification on a test dataset with known true values, detailing true positives (TP), false negatives (FN), false positives (FP), and true negatives (TN).

The paper also references various studies related to intrusion detection systems, emphasizing the importance of these metrics in evaluating the effectiveness of such systems.
