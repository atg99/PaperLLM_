Anomaly-based NIDSs utilize heuristic methods to identify unknown attacks, but can suffer from high false-positive rates. Despite this limitation, they are valued for their potential to recognize novel threats.

Various machine learning techniques have been applied to intrusion detection, including support vector machines, decision trees, random forests, k-means, naive Bayes, multilayer perceptrons, and artificial neural networks. Deep learning methods such as deep neural networks, recurrent neural networks, convolutional neural networks, deep belief networks, and long short-term memory networks have also been leveraged, demonstrating success in various domains, including voice and image detection.

In this research, a new NIDS was developed by integrating genetic algorithms (GA) for optimal feature selection with LSTM-RNN for classification, using the NSL-KDD dataset. The study emphasizes the importance of feature selection in improving classifier accuracy and discusses three approaches: filter, wrapper, and embedded methods. The implemented LSTM-RNN model underwent several experiments to determine an optimal hyper-parameter set, impacting the classifier's performance.

Results indicated that the combination of GA and LSTM-RNN significantly enhanced accuracy compared to using the original feature set. The classifier effectively learned to detect multiple attack classes, leading to a reduction in misclassification rates. Performance metrics, including training and testing accuracy, precision, recall, F-score, true positive rate, false-positive rate, and confusion matrix, were calculated. A comparative analysis showed that LSTM-RNN outperformed both SVM and random forest in the context of NIDS, confirming its effectiveness in accurately detecting a wide range of attacks.
