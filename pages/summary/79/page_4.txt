An RNN, or Recurrent Neural Network, extends traditional feed-forward neural networks (FFNNs) by allowing information to cycle within the network, enabling it to process sequences of data. In FFNNs, information flows only in one direction, while RNNs utilize hidden layers to maintain a memory of previous inputs. The hidden vector sequence (H) and output vector sequence (Y) are computed from the input vector sequence (X) using equations that involve weight matrices and bias terms.

RNNs learn time sequences through gradient-based methods, primarily back-propagation through time (BPTT) and real-time recurrent learning (RTRL). BPTT unfolds the RNN into a multilayer FFNN for training, while RTRL computes gradients and updates weights in real-time. However, standard RNNs face challenges with long sequences due to the vanishing gradient problem, where gradients become too small to effectuate meaningful weight updates, hindering learning.

To address these challenges, Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks are often integrated with RNNs. LSTMs are designed to effectively manage long-term dependencies and can learn from over 1000 time steps, making them suitable for tasks such as time series prediction and classification.

Research has demonstrated the effectiveness of LSTM-RNNs in various applications, including intrusion detection, where they successfully learned to identify different attack classes. Performance evaluation metrics like the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve and the area under the ROC curve (AUC) are used to assess model accuracy, with AUC values indicating the correctness of predictions.

Overall, while RNNs are powerful for sequence prediction, their training difficulties necessitate the use of LSTMs to enhance performance and learning capabilities.
