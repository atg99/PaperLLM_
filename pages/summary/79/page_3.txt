The paper discusses the application of Long Short-Term Memory Recurrent Neural Networks (LSTM-RNN) in binary and multiclass classification tasks, achieving an accuracy of approximately 99.91% for binary classification and 93.88% for multiclass classification. The LSTM-RNN model demonstrated a high True Positive Rate (TPR) and a low False Positive Rate (FPR).

The structure of the paper is organized into several sections. Section 2 provides background information on deep learning, focusing on LSTM networks, RNNs, and genetic algorithms (GAs), as well as detailing the NSL-KDD dataset utilized in the research. Section 3 reviews existing studies related to LSTM and RNN applications in Network Intrusion Detection Systems (NIDS). Section 4 outlines the methodology employed in the study, including the integration of the LSTM-RNN with the GA model. Section 5 presents experimental results and compares the performance of various classifiers. Section 6 discusses the implications of the experimental findings, and Section 7 concludes the paper while outlining future research directions.

In the background section, deep learning is defined as a machine learning method that employs artificial neural networks, including various architectures such as deep neural networks (DNN), convolutional neural networks (CNN), and recurrent neural networks (RNN). The paper describes RNNs in detail, highlighting their structure, which consists of input, output, and multiple hidden layers. RNNs process signals similarly to biological neurons, with weights adjusting during learning to optimize output. The architecture features a one-way flow of information and a feedback loop that refines weights based on error comparison across hidden layers.
