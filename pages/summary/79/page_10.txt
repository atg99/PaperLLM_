The paper discusses the application of a Genetic Algorithm (GA) for feature selection in a dataset, with a focus on enhancing the predictive capability of models. The GA employs a uniform crossover method to select the best feature combinations from a population of individuals. Mutation is also applied with a low probability of 0.05 to maintain diversity and avoid premature convergence, thereby improving the search ability for diverse feature sets.

The GA's process concludes after 10 generations, selecting the individual with the highest predictive feature set. In the conducted experiment, 70 unique features were identified from the training dataset, while 66 unique features were derived from the testing dataset. The combined feature set resulted in 99 unique features, predominantly from service, protocol type, and flag categories, which are crucial for predicting attack types.

Following feature selection, the paper outlines the construction of an LSTM-RNN model, detailing the selection of hyper-parameters such as batch size, number of epochs, learning rate, dropout, and activation function. These parameters are critical for optimizing the model's performance in both binary and multi-class classification tasks. The findings indicate that the GA effectively identifies features with significant predictive value, particularly in the context of cybersecurity.
