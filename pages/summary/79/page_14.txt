The paper presents a detailed analysis of classification performance using both binary and multi-class models, specifically focusing on the results obtained from utilizing a set of 99 features. 

In binary classification, various configurations of neurons in hidden layers were tested, achieving near-perfect accuracy of 99.99% across all setups. Key performance metrics such as precision, recall, and f1-score were consistently high, with values reaching 1.00 for recall and f1-score, indicating optimal model performance.

For multi-class classification, the results varied with the number of neurons. The highest testing accuracy was recorded at 99.0% with 60 and 80 neurons, while the highest precision was 93.88% with 80 neurons. The analysis indicated that the models generally performed well, albeit with a decrease in precision and recall as the complexity of the multi-class classification increased.

Furthermore, the paper compares the performance of the LSTM-RNN classifier with SVM and Random Forest (RF) classifiers using a larger feature set of 122. In binary classification, the Random Forest outperformed both SVM and LSTM-RNN, achieving an accuracy of 99.99%, while SVM and LSTM-RNN recorded 92.90% and 96.51%, respectively.

The findings emphasize the effectiveness of the Random Forest classifier in both binary and multi-class scenarios, while also highlighting the strong capabilities of the LSTM-RNN model, particularly in binary classification contexts.
