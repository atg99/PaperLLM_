Technological change, particularly through advancements in artificial intelligence, robotics, and automation, has the potential to drive significant economic progress while also causing disruption. Historical examples, such as the steam engine, illustrate this duality, resulting in both economic growth and job displacement. Organizations must proactively understand and anticipate the impacts of these technologies, adjusting their structures accordingly.

The paper identifies key differences in the current wave of automation compared to previous ones, including the rise of platform business models, the disproportionate impact of AI on white-collar jobs versus blue-collar jobs, and the potential changes in relationships between establishments and firms due to AI's capabilities.

The article serves as a primer on the definitions and implications of artificial intelligence, robotics, and automation. It emphasizes the importance of clear definitions within the scholarly discourse and discusses how these technologies can serve as both independent and dependent variables in research. The International Federation of Robots defines an industrial robot as a reprogrammable manipulator used in industrial automation, although definitions may vary among experts.

The authors call for further research to deepen the understanding of how these technologies affect work, labor, and organizational structures, as well as to explore the use of AI tools in organizational research methodologies.
