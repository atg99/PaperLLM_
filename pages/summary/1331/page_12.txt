The paper discusses the implications of artificial intelligence (AI) and robotics on various aspects of work, inequality, and competition within industries. Key questions raised include the speed of adoption of these technologies, market forces influencing adoption decisions, and their effects on inequality across different occupations, firms, and regions. The authors explore how management and regulatory policies can either mitigate or worsen the negative impacts of AI and robotics.

Additionally, the paper examines the competitive landscape, detailing how one firm's adoption of these technologies can influence its competitors, suppliers, and customers. It also considers the conditions under which AI and robotics enable new entrants to compete with established firms. The relationship between AI, robotics, and the nature of work is analyzed, focusing on how these technologies alter the skills and tasks relevant to various occupations, as well as the dynamics of worker interactions.

The paper emphasizes the need for further research in this field, highlighting its significance for organizational scholars and the broader implications for management practices and policy-making. The authors express hope that this Primer will serve as a valuable resource for future studies in organization and management related to AI and robotics.
