Brynjolfsson and McAfee (2017) assert that artificial intelligence is poised to become a critical general-purpose technology of our time. Graetz and Michaels (2018) further illustrate the economic impact of robotics, noting an approximate 0.37 percentage point increase in annual GDP growth across 17 countries between 1993 and 2007, comparable to the effects of steam engine adoption during the industrial revolution.

Despite the promise of these technologies, historical trends indicate a duality in their impact on labor, where automation can both complement and substitute for human workers (Mokyr et al. 2015). Acemoglu and Restrepo (2018) analyze the influence of industrial robotics on U.S. labor markets from 1990 to 2007, revealing a negative correlation with employment and wages, with each robot reducing employment by six workers and wages by 0.5%. Similarly, while Graetz and Michaels find that robot usage generally increases wages, it concurrently reduces hours worked for low- and middle-skilled workers.

Research extending to artificial intelligence highlights its potential risks to employment. Frey and Osborne (2017) estimate that 47% of U.S. jobs are at high risk of automation, a trend echoed in studies of other countries, such as Germany and Finland, where 59% and 35% of jobs, respectively, face similar threats. Brynjolfsson et al. (2018b) adopt a task-based approach to examine the susceptibility of various occupations to machine learning, indicating that the potential impacts of automation will span across different workforce segments.

The discourse on automation and labor often varies in scope, with much of the research focusing on the economy as a whole, including global and country-level analyses (Frey and Osborne 2017; MGI 2017; Accenture 2018). Some studies take a more sector-specific focus, highlighting that the most significant effects of technology adoption are likely to manifest in manufacturing, particularly affecting manual and blue-collar jobs, as well as workers without college degrees (Acemoglu and Restrepo 2018).
