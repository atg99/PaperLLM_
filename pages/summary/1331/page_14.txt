The collection of works summarized here addresses various aspects of technological change, its impact on employment, productivity, and economic growth, as well as the strategic implications for firms. 

Menon et al. (2018) explore the significance of articulating strategies through natural language processing of unstructured text, highlighting the relationship between expressed strategies and organizational effectiveness. Mokyr et al. (2015) discuss historical perspectives on technological anxiety, questioning whether current anxieties about technology differ from past experiences and their implications for future economic growth. 

Mullainathan and Spiess (2017) advocate for the integration of machine learning into econometric approaches, emphasizing its potential to enhance data analysis in economics. Pajarinen and Rouvinen (2014) warn that automation threatens a significant portion of Finnish jobs, reflecting broader concerns about employment in the face of advancing technology.

Powell and Dent-Micallef (1999) analyze how information technology can serve as a competitive advantage, stressing the importance of aligning human, business, and technology resources. Raj and Seamans (2018) call for firm-level data to better understand the intersection of artificial intelligence, labor, and productivity.

Romer (1990) introduces the concept of endogenous technological change, suggesting that technology is a product of economic activities rather than an external factor. Roy and Islam (2017) examine the influence of prior experience on the market success of disruptive products in industrial robotics, while Roy and Sarkar (2016) discuss how knowledge and firm boundaries impact innovation during periods of radical change.

Solow (1957) presents foundational ideas on the relationship between technical change and overall production capacity, while Tushman and Anderson (1986) focus on the effects of technological discontinuities on organizational environments. Finally, Williamson (1985) explores the economic institutions that shape capitalism, providing a framework for understanding the interactions between technology and economic structures. 

Together, these papers contribute to a nuanced understanding of the interplay between technology, economic dynamics, and strategic management in today's rapidly evolving landscape.
