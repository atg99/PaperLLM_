The paper discusses the implications of artificial intelligence and robotics on organizational design, highlighting how technological advancements historically reshape workforces and necessitate organizational adaptations. The rise of the Internet has already introduced challenges such as managing remote work and virtual teams, prompting firms to evaluate when reorganization is beneficial.

Artificial intelligence and robotics are poised to further transform organizations by altering the skills and tasks associated with various occupations. This transformation will likely lead to shifts in occupational boundaries as automation increases, requiring firms to restructure to optimize human capital. Additionally, the labor force composition may evolve to prioritize new skill sets that align with these technologies.

Interfirm boundaries are also expected to change with wider adoption of AI and robotics. The paper references Coase's theory that firms expand until the cost of in-house transactions equals market transactions. Enhanced efficiency through AI and robotics could lead to more tasks being managed internally, while some tasks previously handled in-house may be outsourced to firms better equipped with these technologies.

The literature indicates that incumbent firms often struggle during periods of technological discontinuity. However, those that are "pre-adapted" and can leverage existing capabilities may successfully navigate these changes. Evidence suggests that firms with in-house robotics users and access to scientific knowledge are better positioned to adapt to advanced robotics technologies. Therefore, organizations may benefit from hiring individuals experienced with these technologies and fostering scientific knowledge to maximize the advantages of adoption.
