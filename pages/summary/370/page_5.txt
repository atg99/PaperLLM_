The paper discusses the evaluation of RoBERTa models through experiments measuring their ability to generalize across different linguistic features and tasks. It emphasizes the importance of assessing models on out-of-domain data to ensure that high performance is due to genuine linguistic generalization rather than overfitting to specific templates or memorization of keywords. The study manipulates domains by generating distinct training and testing data, ensuring that models cannot simply rely on recognizing patterns from the training set.

The experiments measure Matthews correlation for both surface and linguistic control tasks, with a focus on how well models handle lexical variation while maintaining grammatical correctness. The authors highlight that while generated sentences may lack semantic plausibility, the features examined should still be effectively encoded by the models.

Pretraining for the RoBERTa models is conducted using a scaled-down version of the datasets originally used for BERT, including English Wikipedia and a recreation of BookCorpus. The authors detail their data collection methods and the importance of ensuring that all word stems are represented in the pretraining datasets to avoid out-of-vocabulary issues.

Overall, the paper aims to illustrate the capacity of RoBERTa to perform linguistic generalizations across varied syntactic and lexical contexts, contributing to a deeper understanding of the model's capabilities in natural language processing tasks.
