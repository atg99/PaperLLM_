This paper investigates the effects of pretraining data quantity on the linguistic biases of models, particularly focusing on RoBERTa and its variants. The findings indicate that models with more pretraining data exhibit stronger linguistic biases, while those with less data, such as the 1M model, tend to favor surface-level features. RoBERTa BASE, positioned in the middle, demonstrates a unique ability to generalize based on linguistic features, even in the absence of inoculating data, which distinguishes it from other models.

The results show that as pretraining data increases, models with the same inoculation data consistently achieve higher Linguistic Bias Scores (LBS). Notably, RoBERTa BASE's preference for deeper linguistic generalizations over superficial correlations, such as the presence of specific words, highlights its advanced capabilities. In tasks related to morphology and orthography, RoBERTa BASE requires significantly less inoculation data to adopt linguistic generalizations compared to other models.

The paper also underscores the importance of massive datasets for effective model pretraining, as they facilitate learning not only linguistic features but also the central aspects of language necessary for generalization. The data scale at which RoBERTa begins to exhibit a linguistic bias aligns with the scales used in successful models like ELMo and BERT, suggesting a critical threshold for effective pretraining.

Lastly, the research connects its findings to broader discussions in the field regarding the inductive biases of neural networks, noting that models often struggle to generalize as intended due to ambiguities in datasets. This highlights the necessity for extensive data to capture high-level linguistic features, which are essential for complex tasks involving syntax, semantics, and world knowledge.
