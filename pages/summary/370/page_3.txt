The paper discusses the exploration of inductive biases in neural architectures, particularly in the context of language models like BERT and RoBERTa. It highlights that while some models can learn linguistic features, it is crucial to determine if they will apply these features for generalization during task training or rely on surface-level features. 

To investigate this, the authors conducted three types of experiments: control experiments, ambiguous experiments, and inoculation experiments. Control experiments assess whether models can represent both linguistic and surface features clearly. Ambiguous experiments challenge models with data that supports both types of generalizations, allowing researchers to observe which bias the model adopts. In these experiments, they introduce a linguistic task where data is structured to encourage either a linguistic or surface bias, and then measure the model's performance on disambiguating data using a metric called the linguistic bias score (LBS). 

The final set of inoculation experiments introduces small amounts of disambiguating data into ambiguous training sets to observe how this affects models that initially show a surface bias. The paper also introduces the MSGS dataset, designed for experiments examining inductive biases, featuring tasks that test preferences for linguistic versus surface generalizations. The findings aim to clarify the conditions under which models can successfully generalize linguistic properties, contributing to the understanding of biases that facilitate or hinder performance in natural language processing tasks.
