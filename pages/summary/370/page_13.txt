The paper introduces the MSGS dataset, which comprises five surface features and four linguistic features used to analyze sentence structures. Each feature is defined with specific criteria and variations based on in-domain and out-of-domain contexts.

1. **Absolute Position**: Indicates if a sentence starts with "the," typically involving two clauses and four determiners, with variations in clause order between domains.
   
2. **Length**: Reflects sentence length, determined by a variable token threshold influenced by other linguistic features, often adjusted by adding subordinate clauses.
   
3. **Lexical Content**: Marks sentences that contain "the," generally involving at least two clauses and four determiners, with positional differences across domains.
   
4. **Relative Position**: Assesses the order of "the" and "a" in sentences, with specific configurations categorized into in-domain and out-of-domain templates.
   
5. **Orthography**: Identifies sentences in title case, typically comprising two clauses with positional variations depending on the domain.
   
6. **Lexical Semantics**: Indicates the presence of antonym pairs in a sentence. To avoid solvability through lexical content alone, these pairs are separated into in-domain and out-of-domain sets, with various templates based on the type of antonyms.
   
7. **Morphology**: Differentiates between irregular past tense and regular third-person present plural verbs in sentences, ensuring the task remains focused on morphological distinctions across domains.
   
8. **Syntactic Category**: Flags sentences containing adjectives, diversifying templates with various grammatical combinations, including single-word predicates in out-of-domain sentences.
   
9. **Syntactic Construction**: Identifies sentences using control constructions, where a semantic argument fills an argument slot of an embedded verb, contrasting with raising constructions. Different predicates are analyzed across in-domain and out-of-domain sets.

Overall, the dataset is designed to explore complex linguistic features while maintaining control over various syntactic and semantic elements.
