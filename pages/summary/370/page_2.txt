We conducted a series of experiments using RoBERTa pretrained on datasets ranging from 1 million to 1 billion words. The experiments were designed to probe the model's ability to learn and generalize linguistic features compared to surface features. The first set of experiments involved clear binary classification tasks to test the model's representation of linguistic and surface features. The second set tackled ambiguous tasks, where the training data supported both linguistic and surface generalizations, allowing us to discern which type of generalization the model favored. Finally, we conducted inoculation experiments to determine the difficulty of shifting the model from a surface bias to a linguistic generalization by introducing small amounts of disambiguating data.

Our findings indicate that RoBERTa develops a more pronounced linguistic bias as pretraining data increases. Notably, the RoBERTa BASE model exhibited a robust linguistic bias, requiring minimal inoculating data to make linguistic generalizations. Models with more pretraining data generally needed less inoculating data to adopt linguistic generalizations. While models with limited pretraining could represent linguistic features, they still displayed a strong inclination towards surface biases. This suggests that the primary benefit of pretraining lies not in feature extraction but in determining which features are significant for generalization.

We conclude that effective inductive biases can be cultivated through extensive pretraining, but current models necessitate substantial data to achieve this. This has two significant implications: ongoing pretraining on larger datasets could enhance the generalization capabilities of models like T5 and GPT-3, and advancements in reducing the data requirements for learning important features could further accelerate progress in the field. To facilitate further research, we have made the MSGS dataset, our pretrained RoBERTa models, and related code publicly available.
