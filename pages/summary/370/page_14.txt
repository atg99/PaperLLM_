The paper presents a structured framework for analyzing sentences based on various linguistic and surface features. It categorizes features into two main types: surface features and linguistic features. 

Surface features include aspects such as absolute position, length, lexical content, relative position, and orthography. For instance, a positive example for the absolute position feature is a sentence that begins with "the," while a negative example does not. Similarly, the length feature checks if a sentence exceeds a specific word count, with examples illustrating sentences that meet or fail this criterion.

Linguistic features encompass morphology, syntactic category, construction, and position. For example, the presence of an irregular verb serves as a morphological feature, while the existence of an adjective represents the syntactic category feature. The paper also discusses the syntactic position of sentences, noting that all examples contain one or two embedded clauses. 

The research distinguishes between in-domain and out-of-domain sets, indicating that while the predicates appear in both domains, the specific sentence types are generated from varying templates to maintain diversity in clause positioning. The inclusion of -ing verb forms remains consistent across domains, emphasizing the structured approach to sentence generation and analysis.
