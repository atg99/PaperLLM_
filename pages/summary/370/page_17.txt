The paper presents an analysis of various factors influencing linguistic features, including absolute position, morphology, lexical content, relative position, orthography, syntactic category, and syntactic position. It uses a quantitative approach, measuring these factors across different scales ranging from 1M to 1B base units. The results highlight the relationship between these linguistic variables and their impact on syntactic constructions. Additionally, the paper discusses the inoculation rate in a controlled setting, revealing that a 0% inoculation rate results in failed control outcomes, while varying inoculation rates (0.1%, 0.3%, and 1.0%) are tested to assess their effects on performance in held-out items. The findings contribute to understanding how linguistic variables interact and affect syntactic processing.
