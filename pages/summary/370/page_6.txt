The paper investigates the relationship between pretraining dataset size and the linguistic bias of RoBERTa models, specifically focusing on how inoculation rates affect this bias. Various sizes of pretraining datasets (1M, 10M, 100M, and 1B words) were used, with models evaluated based on their Linguistic Bias Score (LBS). The results indicate that models with higher linguistic bias achieve higher LBS with less inoculation data, as shown in Figure 3.

The study meticulously configures model sizes to mitigate overfitting, using RoBERTa BASE as the largest model. The authors detail their hyperparameter search strategies, which include dropout rates, learning rates, and batch sizes, ensuring systematic exploration of configurations while maintaining a focus on avoiding confounding variables.

In the fine-tuning phase, the authors followed previous methodologies but adapted certain parameters based on preliminary experiments. They executed a substantial number of fine-tuning runs across multiple models and tasks, leading to significant findings. Key results reveal that models can learn to represent both surface and linguistic features with minimal data, that a linguistic bias begins to emerge with over 1B words of pretraining, and that increased dataset size correlates with a stronger linguistic bias. Additionally, the study highlights the variability in models' preferences for specific linguistic and surface features.

Overall, the paper contributes valuable insights into how pretraining data size and inoculation rates influence linguistic bias in language models, reinforcing the importance of these factors in model training and evaluation.
