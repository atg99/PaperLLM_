The paper discusses the MSGS dataset, which consists of 20 ambiguous binary classification tasks combining linguistic and surface features, as well as 9 unambiguous control tasks. Each task involves generating sentences that vary in specific features, such as tense or the presence of certain articles. The dataset includes 50,000 sentences for ambiguous tasks and 30,000 for control tasks, divided into training, evaluation, and inoculation sets. The generation of data follows a structured paradigm to explore how well models like RoBERTa can learn and generalize linguistic knowledge from surface-level features.

Key components include:

1. **Feature Representation**: The dataset pairs linguistic features (like verb tense and parts of speech) with surface features (like the presence of specific articles). Each task is denoted as FEAT1-FEAT2.

2. **Data Structure**: Ambiguous datasets consist of 8 sentences in a 2x2x2 design, while control datasets have 4 sentences in a 2x2 design. The training set is primarily composed of in-domain data, with a small portion of disambiguating data used for inoculation.

3. **Training and Testing**: The training phase uses mostly ambiguous sentences, while the testing phase evaluates the model's ability to generalize to disambiguated sentences from out-of-domain contexts.

4. **Data Generation**: Sentences are generated using a toolkit that incorporates a comprehensive vocabulary, allowing for systematic exploration of grammatical features.

Overall, the study aims to understand how well language models can distinguish between linguistic and surface features and whether they can effectively generalize from training to testing scenarios.
