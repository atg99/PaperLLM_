The paper examines how the RoBERTa model learns to prioritize linguistic generalizations over surface-level features during fine-tuning. The authors introduce the Mixed Signals Generalization Set (MSGS), a diagnostic tool consisting of 20 ambiguous binary classification tasks, to evaluate this preference. They pretrain RoBERTa models on varying data sizes from 1 million to 1 billion words and compare their performance with the publicly available RoBERTa BASE model.

The findings indicate that while RoBERTa models can effectively learn to represent linguistic features with minimal pretraining data, they require significantly more data to develop a preference for these linguistic generalizations over surface ones. Notably, with around 30 billion words of pretraining data, RoBERTa BASE begins to show a consistent linguistic bias. The study concludes that self-supervised pretraining is valuable for acquiring inductive biases, but there is potential for enhancing the efficiency with which models learn to identify relevant features.
