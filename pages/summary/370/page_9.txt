The paper investigates the relationship between pretraining data and inductive biases in neural language models, particularly regarding their ability to generalize linguistically. It references several studies demonstrating that recurrent neural networks (RNNs) and sequence-to-sequence models exhibit biases in learning linguistic structures, such as agreement rules and question formation. The findings indicate that while some linguistic features can be learned from limited pretraining data, significant pretraining is required for models to develop a bias toward utilizing these features for generalization.

The authors introduce a new diagnostic dataset, MSGS, designed to probe the inductive biases of learning algorithms through a poverty of the stimulus design and inoculation. They also present 12 RoBERTa models pretrained on smaller data sets, which could serve as a resource for future research on learning curves related to pretraining data quantity.

The conclusion emphasizes the need for further exploration of models with extensive pretraining, like GPT-3, while also suggesting that there may be alternative approaches to enhancing inductive biases in neural networks.
