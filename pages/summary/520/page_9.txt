This paper investigates the effectiveness of the VICReg framework in enhancing representation learning through various ablation studies. The results demonstrate that the introduction of a local criterion in VICReg leads to significant improvements in both classification and segmentation tasks compared to contrastive methods like SimCLR. For instance, the classification accuracy with VICReg reaches 71.1% and mIoU for segmentation is 47.8%, while VICRegL, which incorporates the local criterion, achieves even higher segmentation accuracy at 55.9%.

Moreover, the impact of multi-crop techniques is explored, showing that employing these methods alongside the local criterion consistently boosts performance. The analysis reveals a trade-off between local and global feature learning, with the optimal balance found when the local criterion is weighted appropriately against the global criterion, maintaining high performance in both tasks.

The study also examines the importance of feature-based versus location-based local criteria, revealing that the location-based criterion yields the most substantial improvements in segmentation performance. Additionally, the number of selected matches for local losses is analyzed, finding that an intermediate number of matches optimizes segmentation accuracy without compromising classification performance.

Overall, the findings highlight the critical role of local feature regularization in enhancing the model's ability to learn meaningful representations while maintaining global interpretability, thus addressing the collapse problem inherent in representation learning.
