In this paper, we introduce VICRegL, a hierarchical reasoning system designed to learn features at both global and local scales. While traditional methods focus primarily on global feature learning using joint embedding architectures, our approach integrates spatial information to enhance feature representation. VICRegL employs the VICReg criterion, which allows for simultaneous learning of local and global features without the need for negative samples, thus avoiding the pitfalls associated with contrasting feature vectors from distant locations.

The method is particularly effective in situations where segmentation masks are not available, as it utilizes a self-supervised learning mechanism to match feature vectors based on a weighted l2 nearest-neighbor criterion. This approach mitigates the issue of negative contrasting effects that can arise from misaligned feature vectors. Our experiments demonstrate that VICRegL significantly improves segmentation accuracy while maintaining classification performance. In evaluations using frozen backbones, VICRegL achieves notable improvements in mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) scores on the Pascal VOC dataset, outperforming its predecessor VICReg.

The paper also contextualizes VICRegL within the broader landscape of global and local feature learning methods, highlighting the limitations of existing contrastive and clustering approaches. Our findings suggest that incorporating local feature learning is crucial for enhancing segmentation tasks, thus providing a valuable contribution to the field of computer vision.
