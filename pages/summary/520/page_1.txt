The paper introduces VICRegL, a self-supervised learning approach designed to simultaneously learn effective global and local visual features, addressing the trade-off inherent in current methodologies. While existing methods typically focus on either global features, which excel in classification tasks, or local features, which are better suited for detection and segmentation, VICRegL aims to balance both.

The method employs a dual-branch architecture of a convolutional neural network, where each branch processes different distorted versions of the same image. The VICReg criterion is applied to both global and local feature vectors, ensuring that local features maintain proximity to each other under certain conditions. Specifically, local feature vectors are encouraged to align if their l2-distance is below a set threshold or if their positions are consistent with known geometric transformations between the images.

The authors demonstrate that VICRegL achieves strong performance across linear classification, detection, and segmentation tasks, indicating its versatility. The paper highlights the limitations of current self-supervised methods that either focus on global characteristics or require complex local processing steps, positioning VICRegL as a more integrated solution. The code and pretrained models are made publicly accessible for further research and application.
