The paper introduces VICRegL, a method that enhances the learning of local and global visual features simultaneously by matching feature vectors based on their spatial and embedding distances. The study demonstrates that applying both the covariance and invariance criteria in local loss functions significantly improves segmentation performance, achieving a +0.8 mIoU gain from variance and an additional +1.3 mIoU from the covariance criterion over a baseline. The impact on classification accuracy remains minimal.

Additionally, the paper explores a collapse-prevention method by integrating local criteria with the contrastive criterion SimCLR, noting that while there is a performance gap between SimCLR and VICReg, the local criterion offers greater benefits when applied to VICReg. The effects of the multi-crop data augmentation strategy are also examined, revealing that the introduction of the local criterion consistently enhances segmentation results without compromising classification performance.

Visualizations included in the paper illustrate the coherence of feature-based and location-based matches, showing that the embedding space is structured to reflect semantic content. 

In conclusion, VICRegL is presented as a competitive approach for self-supervised learning, particularly with convolutional networks, which are shown to perform well against vision transformers. The paper acknowledges limitations regarding the understanding of the locality of feature vectors and suggests future work focused on hierarchical feature learning and deeper investigations into the local nature of features across different architectures.
