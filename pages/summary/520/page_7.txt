The paper presents a comparison of various global and local self-supervised learning methods using linear evaluation benchmarks. The evaluation focuses on the performance of features learned from ConvNeXt and ViT backbones across three tasks: linear classification accuracy on ImageNet, and linear segmentation on Pascal VOC and ADE20k. Key metrics include frozen and fine-tuned results for segmentation and classification accuracy, with the best results highlighted in bold. 

The authors emphasize the significance of using frozen weights for linear evaluation, arguing that it provides a clearer indication of learned representations without the influence of complex segmentation heads or ground truth masks. The methodology follows a protocol where learned feature maps are fed into a linear classifier, and results are averaged over multiple runs to ensure reliability.

The paper also compares their method, VICRegL, against prior work using a ResNet-50 backbone. Notably, VICRegL demonstrates substantial improvements in linear segmentation accuracy on Pascal VOC, achieving higher mIoU scores while maintaining competitive classification accuracy. The authors point out that while fine-tuning results appear promising for some methods, these may not reflect true performance due to reliance on labeled data. Overall, VICRegL shows robustness in both classification and segmentation tasks, outperforming other methods, especially in the frozen evaluation setting, indicating its effectiveness in learning meaningful representations.
