The paper discusses the development of a loss function for self-supervised learning that balances local and global features. It finds that a fixed number of matches for feature pairing is sufficient for maintaining performance, as good local features are consistently available due to low overlap probability between views. The proposed loss function combines location-based and feature-based components, along with a standard VICReg loss applied to pooled representations. This function is symmetrized since the matching process is not inherently symmetric.

The design of the feature matching procedure is compatible with various convolutional neural networks, with empirical results demonstrating that using a ConvNeXt backbone, which is an improved version of ResNet, can enhance performance in downstream tasks. The study reveals that while ResNet-50 struggles with multi-crop strategies and optimization, ConvNeXt effectively benefits from this approach. The optimal batch size is linked to the dimensionality of the representations, with ConvNeXt requiring a smaller batch size compared to ResNet-50.

The paper also details how the loss functions are modified for multi-crop scenarios, generating multiple views of images and adapting the matching loss calculations accordingly. This adaptation ensures that the model can effectively leverage the benefits of multi-crop sampling while maintaining the integrity of the loss function. Overall, the findings indicate that careful consideration of architecture and loss function design can lead to improved self-supervised learning outcomes.
