This paper presents a comparison of various global and local self-supervised learning methods, specifically focusing on their performance in linear evaluation benchmarks. The methods evaluated include MoCo v2, SimCLR, BYOL, VICReg, and several local feature methods such as PixPro, DenseCL, and DetCon. The results are measured in terms of linear classification accuracy on ImageNet and linear segmentation mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) on Pascal VOC and Cityscapes datasets.

The key finding is that VICRegL, particularly with different weighting parameters (α), consistently outperforms the original VICReg method in linear segmentation tasks, highlighting the advantages of incorporating a local criterion for better localized image understanding. The paper emphasizes that the best results for each benchmark are highlighted in bold.

Implementation details are thoroughly provided to ensure reproducibility of the results. The models were pretrained on the unlabelled ImageNet dataset, with hyperparameters largely unchanged from previous studies. The ResNet-50 backbone was trained on multiple GPUs using the LARS optimizer, while the ConvNeXt models utilized the AdamW optimizer with adjusted batch sizes.

The experimental results demonstrate that VICRegL significantly enhances segmentation performance compared to VICReg while maintaining competitive classification accuracy, showcasing its effectiveness in self-supervised learning applications.
