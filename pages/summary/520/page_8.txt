This paper investigates the trade-off between local and global criteria in self-supervised learning, specifically through the VICRegL framework. The study evaluates the performance of various alpha coefficients that control the balance between local and global features in linear classification on ImageNet and linear segmentation on Pascal VOC.

Key findings indicate that while local methods generally perform 10 to 20% lower in classification tasks compared to global methods, VICRegL effectively enhances segmentation performance without significantly sacrificing classification accuracy. The paper presents results showing that with a ConvNeXt-S backbone, employing a local criterion improved segmentation metrics, achieving notable gains in mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) on both Pascal VOC and ADE20K datasets. For instance, using an alpha value of 0.9 resulted in a 6.6 mIoU improvement on Pascal VOC, while an alpha of 0.75 yielded even higher gains.

Ablation studies further compare different matching criteria, revealing that using both feature-based and location-based matching simultaneously yields the best performance in segmentation tasks. The paper emphasizes the significance of balancing local and global features to optimize performance across different tasks, setting a new state-of-the-art in linear segmentation with a larger ConvNeXt-XL backbone pretrained on ImageNet-22k.

Overall, the results underscore the potential of VICRegL in achieving competitive performance in both classification and segmentation by strategically adjusting the importance of local versus global features.
