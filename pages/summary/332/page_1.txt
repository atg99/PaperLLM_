The paper titled "Generative Pre-Trained Transformer for Design Concept Generation: An Exploration" by Q. Zhu and J. Luo investigates the application of generative pre-trained transformers (GPT) for generating design concepts in the early stages of design. The authors emphasize the importance of novel concepts for design innovation and highlight existing limitations in generative design algorithms, which often produce either abstract or overly detailed outputs unsuitable for initial design exploration.

The study introduces GPT models, specifically GPT-2 and GPT-3, as tools for automated verbal design concept generation. These models, which are pre-trained on extensive textual data, demonstrate effective performance in generating design concepts through both problem-driven and analogy-driven reasoning. The authors aim to bridge the gap in "computer ideation," where computers autonomously generate ideas rather than merely assisting human designers.

The literature review categorizes existing research on design concept generation into three dimensions: the role of methods or tools, the form of concept representation, and the targeted design process stage. The paper discusses the roles of methods, which can serve as guides, stimulators, or generators in the design process.

Overall, the findings suggest that GPT models can significantly contribute to the early phases of design by providing a novel approach to concept generation, thus enhancing the creative capabilities of designers.
