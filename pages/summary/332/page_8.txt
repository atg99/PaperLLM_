The paper discusses the application of generative pre-trained transformers (GPT) in generating design concepts, demonstrating that customizing training data allows GPT to effectively perform conceptual design tasks. This research paves the way for natural language generation (NLG) to facilitate verbal concept generation, which can potentially be integrated with other methodologies for enhanced applications and automation. It emphasizes the importance of analogy-driven reasoning and the potential of knowledge graphs to support concept generation.

While the paper presents case studies focused on problem-driven and analogy-driven concept generation, it suggests further exploration into technology-driven concept generation and the application of various design heuristics like TRIZ. The authors highlight the challenges designers face in generating novel ideas due to limited knowledge and the complexities of design thinking. They propose that GPT can assist in this process by offering valuable insights and encouraging creative thinking beyond a designer's existing knowledge.

The paper also notes the necessity for an efficient concept evaluation algorithm to filter generated concepts, as the showcased results were drawn from a pool of low-quality outputs. Current NLG evaluation metrics, such as BLEU and WMD, are deemed unsuitable for assessing design concepts since they primarily focus on language quality rather than the usefulness and novelty of concepts. Therefore, new evaluation methods are recommended, alongside extensive experiments to compare different models and human assessments to validate both generation and evaluation performance.
