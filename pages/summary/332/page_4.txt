This paper discusses the application of Generative Pre-trained Transformers (GPT) in design concept generation tasks, highlighting the differences between GPT-2 and GPT-3. It explains that GPT-2 employs a two-step training strategy of pre-training on a vast text dataset followed by fine-tuning with task-specific data. In contrast, GPT-3, with its significantly larger dataset and parameters, utilizes few-shot learning, allowing it to learn from multiple examples without gradient updates.

The research method involves experimenting with different GPT models for design concept generation. The framework integrates knowledge from fine-tuning datasets or examples for few-shot learning, emphasizing the importance of tailored input for conditional learning based on specific reasoning types, such as analogy reasoning. The experiments are structured around problem-driven and analogy-driven reasoning, using data from RedDot award-winning designs.

The paper outlines the training processes for both GPT-2 and GPT-3 and presents an experimental framework that includes the preparation of data for natural language generation tasks. Overall, the study aims to demonstrate the capabilities of GPT models in generating innovative design concepts through advanced reasoning techniques.
