The paper discusses the role of Natural Language Generation (NLG) in the context of artificial intelligence and data-driven design. NLG is defined as a computer program that produces natural language output, with recent advancements including machine translation, text summarization, and paraphrasing. 

A key focus is the transformer model, introduced by Vaswani et al., which has emerged as the leading architecture for NLG. Unlike traditional recurrent neural networks (RNN) and long short-term memory (LSTM) models, transformers effectively address the vanishing gradient problem and allow for parallel training. This capability enables them to capture longer sequences, thereby enhancing language understanding and generation.

The paper highlights applications of transformers across various fields, such as generating structured medical information and assisting content creators. However, the authors note a gap in the use of transformers for engineering design tasks, which their work aims to address.

Additionally, the Generative Pre-trained Transformer (GPT) series by OpenAI is discussed, emphasizing its pre-training on extensive datasets for language-related tasks. While BERT is noted for its strength in natural language understanding, it is limited in language generation, making it less suitable for design concept generation. The paper underscores the potential for transformers and GPT models in enhancing design processes through improved language generation capabilities.
