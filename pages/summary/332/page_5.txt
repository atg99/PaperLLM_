The paper discusses the application of artificial intelligence, specifically GPT-2 and GPT-3, in data-driven design, focusing on problem-driven and analogy-driven reasoning in generating design solutions. The experiments utilize datasets from the RedDot award-winning designs, which include 14,502 product designs and 1,486 design concepts, to fine-tune the models.

In the problem-driven reasoning experiment, GPT-2 is fine-tuned to generate solution ideas based on provided problem statements. The training dataset is structured so that each design description begins with a problem followed by a solution, which aids the model in learning to generate appropriate responses. The authors hypothesize that the model can learn not only from problem-driven examples but also from other design descriptions present in the dataset. The fine-tuning process involves 14,000 steps with a batch size of 1, and the paper includes a figure illustrating the loss over these training steps.

For analogy-driven reasoning, GPT-3 employs few-shot learning using examples of design-by-analogy concepts. Here, the model is not fine-tuned but instead relies on its pre-trained capabilities to generate outputs based on a limited number of examples.

Overall, the study highlights the potential of leveraging AI in design processes by utilizing large datasets and advanced machine learning techniques to enhance problem-solving capabilities in design contexts.
