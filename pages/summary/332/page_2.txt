This paper discusses various methods and tools for concept generation in design, emphasizing the role of artificial intelligence and data-driven design. It highlights how stimuli can inspire designers, referencing studies that demonstrate the impact of visual stimuli on problem-solving and design performance. Notable methods include the use of design heuristics, word clouds, and the InnoGPS tool, which guides designers by linking stimuli to their design problems.

The paper also introduces the concept generator, an automated computational agent that creates new design concepts. Examples include function-based design synthesis and graph grammar for generating function structures. Additionally, it discusses topology optimization and generative adversarial networks (GANs) for producing aesthetically and functionally optimized designs.

Concepts can be represented in various forms such as diagrams, text, or visual models, and the choice of representation should align with different stages of the design process. The paper identifies four stages of design: planning, conceptual design, embodiment, and detail design, indicating that certain tools are more suited to specific stages.

While visual and diagrammatic stimuli are beneficial in later design stages, text-based concepts play a crucial role in the early brainstorming phase. The authors note a gap in automated tools for generating verbal concepts, which they aim to address by focusing on natural language generation (NLG) technology, specifically using generative pre-trained transformers to produce high-fidelity design descriptions.

In summary, the paper presents a taxonomy of concept generation methods, categorizing them by their roles, representation forms, and appropriate design stages while aiming to enhance verbal generation through advanced AI techniques.
