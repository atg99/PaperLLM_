The paper discusses the application of artificial intelligence, specifically GPT-2 and GPT-3, in generating design concepts through problem-driven and analogy-driven reasoning. It highlights two experiments that explore the model's capabilities to generate innovative solutions for both known and unknown design problems.

In the first experiment, the model addresses a known problem related to personal hygiene in public toilets, providing two design concepts. The first concept introduces a toilet design that incorporates a circular filter and an automated lid to prevent the spread of bacteria. The second concept, Clean Seat, features a toilet lid that opens and closes automatically, enhancing hygiene through anti-perspiratory properties and air-flow control.

The second experiment focuses on a life science problem involving cumbersome electrocardiograph testing methods. Two solutions are proposed: the Electric Micro-Inspection Tool Show'em!, a wearable device that simplifies heart function monitoring, and the Light Guide, a remote-controlled electrocardiograph that allows for stress-free testing at home.

The findings suggest that GPT-2 can generate diverse solution ideas for known problems based on its extensive training data. For unknown problems, while the model can comprehend and propose solutions, it may sometimes diverge into unrelated design features as the text length increases. 

Additionally, the paper explores analogy-driven reasoning as a method to generate design concepts by drawing parallels between different domains. The experiment utilizes prompts from a dataset to facilitate few-shot learning in GPT-3, aiming to generate concepts when explicit analogy mapping is not established.

Overall, the research demonstrates the potential of AI in enhancing design processes through innovative reasoning techniques.
