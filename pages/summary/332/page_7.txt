The paper discusses the application of GPT-3 in generating design concepts through analogy-driven reasoning. It highlights how the model can produce innovative ideas by updating the tokens that define source and target domains in input sentences. Various examples are provided, such as using an accordion to inspire computer mouse design, and the paper presents two specific drone concepts generated from the source domains of lantern and origami.

In the experiments, GPT-3 demonstrated its ability to understand and apply features from the source domains to create relevant drone designs, despite the cultural and technical distance between them. The model's pre-training on a vast dataset enables it to form analogical mappings with minimal prompts. Additionally, it was observed that adjusting frequency and presence penalties influences the novelty of generated ideas. Higher penalty values led to more original outputs, revealing differences between GPT-2 and GPT-3 in design concept generation tasks.

The drone concepts produced include "Skylight," a portable lantern that serves as a landing pad for drones, and "D-LIGHT," a decorative drone with a responsive lantern mechanism. Another concept, "Aerobowl," mimics origami folding, allowing the drone to compact for storage and deploy in rescue operations. "PaperDrone" focuses on a deformable chassis structure that maintains strength while being foldable. Overall, the paper emphasizes the potential of GPT-3 in creative design by leveraging analogy-based reasoning.
