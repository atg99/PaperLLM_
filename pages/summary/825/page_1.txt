The paper "Quantization Networks" addresses the challenges posed by high computational and memory costs of deep neural networks (DNNs), which limit their deployment on portable devices. To tackle this issue, the authors propose a novel approach to low-bit quantization that transforms a full-precision DNN into a low-bitwidth integer representation. 

Existing methods for quantization are categorized into approximation-based and optimization-based techniques. Approximation methods struggle with gradient mismatch, while optimization methods are typically limited to quantizing weights and can incur significant computational costs during training. The authors introduce a unified framework for quantizing both weights and activations by modeling the quantization process as a differentiable non-linear function. This function is expressed as a linear combination of Sigmoid functions, which possess learnable biases and scales, allowing for lossless and end-to-end training through continuous relaxation of the Sigmoid steepness.

Extensive experiments conducted on image classification and object detection demonstrate that the proposed quantization networks surpass existing state-of-the-art methods. The authors believe their approach offers new insights into the interpretation and implementation of neural network quantization, thereby contributing to the development of more efficient DNNs suitable for resource-constrained environments.
