The paper investigates low-bit quantization methods for deep neural networks (DNNs), specifically focusing on the implications of training from scratch versus using pre-trained models. Results indicate that utilizing pre-trained models significantly enhances performance, as evidenced by the metrics in Table 6. The study also details the time-space complexity of the quantized networks evaluated on the VU9P FPGA, highlighting substantial reductions in both time and space complexities when using low-bit quantization (Table 7).

A key aspect of the study is the convergence of the temperature parameter T during training, which influences the transition from a soft quantization function to a hard step function. The authors demonstrate that T can be adjusted based on validation performance, allowing for flexibility in training various backbone models.

The proposed soft quantization function, inspired by activation functions in DNNs, allows for end-to-end learning of quantization networks, making it adaptable for arbitrary-bit quantization of weights and activations. Extensive experiments across image classification and object detection tasks confirm the effectiveness of this method, suggesting its potential for broader applications in deep learning.
