This paper discusses the challenges and solutions in the quantization of neural networks, particularly focusing on low-bit quantization methods. It categorizes existing approaches into approximation-based and optimization-based methods. Approximation-based methods aim to convert full-precision values into discrete low-bit values during the forward pass, but they often lead to a gradient mismatch problem due to the saturation of gradients, necessitating additional approximations during the backward pass. This instability in optimization is a significant drawback.

On the other hand, optimization-based methods treat quantization as a constrained optimization problem, which is primarily suitable for weight quantization. However, these methods are computationally intensive and do not effectively address the quantization of activations.

To overcome these limitations, the authors propose a novel approach that formulates the quantization operation as a differentiable non-linear mapping function. This quantization function is constructed using a linear combination of Sigmoid functions with learnable parameters, enabling the quantization of both weights and activations without suffering from gradient mismatch. The proposed method allows for lossless and end-to-end learning, which is more efficient and straightforward compared to existing methods.

The contributions of the paper include the introduction of this new perspective on quantization and the demonstration of its effectiveness in outperforming state-of-the-art quantization methods in tasks such as image classification and object detection. The authors also provide a brief overview of related work, highlighting the limitations of current quantization strategies and positioning their approach as a significant advancement in the field.
