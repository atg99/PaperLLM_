This paper presents a novel quantization method for neural networks, focusing on the performance of ResNet architectures on the ImageNet classification task. The authors detail their approach, which involves a three-phase training process: first, training the weight quantization while disabling activation quantization; second, fixing weight quantization and training activation quantization; and finally, training both until convergence. The method demonstrates improved performance over existing quantization techniques, as evidenced by the results in Tables 1, 2, and 3, which show top-1 and top-5 accuracies for various configurations of ResNet-18 and ResNet-50.

The paper discusses the use of AlexNet as a benchmark for quantization and outlines the training parameters, including stochastic gradient descent, learning rate decay, weight decay, and temperature adjustments. The authors note that the quantization of more compact architectures like ResNet leads to greater performance degradation compared to larger models like AlexNet. Despite this, their method still outperforms state-of-the-art quantization techniques, indicating its effectiveness in maintaining accuracy while reducing model size. Overall, the findings highlight the potential for efficient quantization strategies in deep learning applications.
