This paper presents a novel quantization method for deep learning models, specifically focusing on improving performance in object detection tasks while using low-bit weight quantization. The authors demonstrate that their approach surpasses the full-precision model on ResNet-18 with only 3-bit weight quantization, achieving a mean Average Precision (mAP) of 77.7% on the SSD architecture tested on the Pascal VOC dataset. This performance is notable as it exceeds the results from existing methods like ADMM, while maintaining a simpler and more general framework.

The experimental setup involved training models on the Pascal VOC datasets, where the authors quantized parameters in the VGG16 backbone except for specific layers. They employed stochastic gradient descent (SGD) with momentum and implemented a unique temperature adjustment strategy during training, aiming to balance model learning and quantization accuracy.

Ablation studies revealed that non-uniform quantization, utilizing methods like K-means clustering, outperformed linear quantization, particularly due to the Gaussian distribution of full-precision parameters. The authors noted that fixing biases during training provided better performance than adapting them, and the gradual increase of temperature during training minimized the gap between soft and hard quantization functions, enhancing model performance.

Overall, the proposed method showcases effective strategies for achieving competitive results in object detection with low-bit quantization, contributing valuable insights to the field of model compression and optimization.
