The paper discusses advancements in optimizing neural network quantization, addressing both weight quantization and the design of non-linear activation functions. It highlights two methods: Leng et al.'s approach using convex linear constraints and ADMM, and Hou and Kwok's proximal Newton algorithm, both of which are limited to weight quantization and incur high computational costs. 

The paper elaborates on the evolution of activation functions in neural networks, starting with the perceptron and its binary output mechanism, moving to the sigmoid function for smoother learning through back-propagation, and finally introducing rectified linear units (ReLU) and Maxout functions. Maxout is particularly noted for its ability to approximate complex convex functions through linear combinations.

The core contribution of the work is the formulation of quantization as a differentiable non-linear function applicable to weights and activations. The authors propose a new quantization framework that represents the quantization operation as a combination of binary quantizations, akin to Maxout. This is mathematically expressed through a combination of unit step functions with learned parameters.

For training and inference, the paper introduces a continuous relaxation method that substitutes the ideal step function with a sigmoid function, enabling differentiable learning through back-propagation. The training process involves gradually reducing the gap between the ideal and soft quantization functions by adjusting a temperature factor in the sigmoid function, which allows for effective learning while aiming to converge to the ideal quantization during inference.

Overall, the paper presents a novel approach to quantization in neural networks that enhances learning efficiency while maintaining the integrity of the quantization process.
