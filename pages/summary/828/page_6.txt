The paper discusses various techniques for quantizing neural networks, focusing on the granularity of quantization, calibration methods, and the impact of symmetric versus asymmetric quantization.

Layerwise quantization applies a uniform clipping range across all filters in a layer, which can lead to poor resolution for channels with narrow distributions. In contrast, channelwise quantization assigns distinct clipping ranges to individual channels, enhancing quantization resolution.

The paper highlights the common practice of using the min and max values of signals for quantization, noting its susceptibility to outliers that can distort the range and reduce resolution. Alternative approaches include using percentiles for clipping ranges and minimizing Kullback-Leibler divergence to reduce information loss when quantizing.

The discussion also contrasts symmetric and asymmetric quantization. Symmetric quantization simplifies implementation but is less effective for skewed data distributions, making asymmetric quantization a better choice in such scenarios.

Furthermore, the paper differentiates between static and dynamic quantization methods. Static quantization uses a pre-calculated clipping range, incurring no computational overhead but often resulting in lower accuracy. Dynamic quantization, on the other hand, computes the clipping range in real-time for each activation map, which can lead to higher accuracy at the cost of increased computational overhead.
