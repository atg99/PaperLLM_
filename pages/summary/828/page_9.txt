The paper discusses the Quantization-Aware Training (QAT) procedure, emphasizing the role of the Straight Through Estimator (STE) in managing the challenges posed by non-differentiable quantization operators during backpropagation. The primary issue arises because the gradient of the quantization operation is generally zero, leading to potential zero-gradient scenarios or high error gradients in low-precision settings. The STE addresses this by approximating the quantization operation with an identity function, which, despite its coarse nature, has shown practical effectiveness, particularly in non-ultra low-precision cases.

The paper traces the origins of STE to earlier works that used an identity operator to approximate gradients in binary neurons. While STE remains the predominant method, various alternative approaches have been explored, including stochastic neuron methods, combinatorial optimization, target propagation, Gumbel-softmax, and Non-STE methods that eliminate the need for non-differentiable operators. Notable recent works such as ProxQuant, pulse training, and AdaRound have introduced innovative techniques to refine quantization without relying on traditional rounding methods.

Additionally, the paper highlights the importance of learning quantization parameters alongside model parameters during QAT. Techniques like PACT, QIT, and LSQ focus on dynamically learning clipping ranges, quantization steps, and scaling factors for various activation functions. This comprehensive approach to QAT aims to optimize both model performance and quantization accuracy.
