The paper discusses advancements in neural network quantization, highlighting how recent techniques differ from earlier approaches. While many contemporary algorithms echo past research, neural networks present unique challenges and opportunities due to their computational intensity and over-parameterization. This over-parameterization allows for reduced bit precision without significantly affecting accuracy, as neural networks can tolerate aggressive quantization and extreme discretization.

The paper argues that unlike classical approaches, which focused on minimizing signal distortion, neural networks can achieve good generalization performance even with notable discrepancies between quantized and non-quantized models. This difference is crucial for developing new quantization techniques. Additionally, the layered structure of neural networks introduces the potential for mixed-precision quantization, as different layers influence the loss function differently.

The basic concepts of quantization are introduced, beginning with the problem setup and notations for a neural network with multiple layers and learnable parameters. The goal is to reduce the precision of these parameters and the intermediate activation maps while maintaining generalization capability. The discussion includes uniform and non-uniform quantization methods, setting the stage for further exploration of fine-tuning and stochastic quantization techniques.
