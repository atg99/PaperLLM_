This paper discusses mixed-precision quantization techniques for neural networks (NNs), which involve varying bit precision across different layers to optimize performance while minimizing accuracy loss. The challenge lies in the exponential search space for selecting these bit settings, prompting various proposed solutions. Notably, reinforcement learning (RL) methods and differentiable neural architecture search (DNAS) have been explored, though they often demand significant computational resources and are sensitive to hyperparameters.

An alternative approach, HAWQ, leverages second-order sensitivity (Hessian) to determine optimal mixed-precision settings, demonstrating considerable speed advantages over RL methods. HAWQv3 further enhances this with an integer-only, hardware-aware quantization strategy using Integer Linear Programming to optimize bit precision for specific constraints, achieving notable speed improvements on hardware like T4 GPUs.

The paper emphasizes the importance of hardware-aware quantization, as the benefits of quantization vary across different hardware configurations, influenced by factors such as memory and bandwidth. Recent advancements have focused on directly measuring deployment latency for various quantization settings to enhance hardware efficiency.

Additionally, the paper examines distillation-assisted quantization, where a larger, more accurate model (teacher) helps train a smaller model (student). This technique incorporates soft probabilities from the teacher into the training process, potentially improving the quantization accuracy by blending the student loss with a distillation loss.

Overall, mixed-precision quantization, combined with hardware-aware strategies and model distillation, presents an effective framework for achieving improved efficiency and accuracy in low-precision neural network models.
