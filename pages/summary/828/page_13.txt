The paper discusses various quantization techniques in neural networks, emphasizing the importance of mixed-precision quantization for optimizing model performance while maintaining accuracy. It highlights the benefits of integer-only quantization, which utilizes lower precision arithmetic, making it more efficient in terms of energy and area compared to floating-point operations. The text references notable works in this area, including those that integrate Batch Normalization into convolution layers and extend integer-only methods to Transformer architectures.

Dyadic quantization is introduced as a method that simplifies computations by using rational numbers with integer numerators and powers of two in denominators, allowing for efficient integer arithmetic without division. The paper concludes that integer-only and dyadic quantization are generally preferred over simulated quantization, as they provide better hardware efficiency. However, it acknowledges that simulated quantization can still be beneficial in scenarios where bandwidth is a limiting factor, such as in recommendation systems.

Furthermore, mixed-precision quantization is proposed as a solution to the trade-off between model accuracy and computational efficiency, suggesting that careful selection of layer precision can mitigate the accuracy loss associated with ultra-low precision quantization.
