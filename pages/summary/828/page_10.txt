The paper discusses various quantization methods for neural networks, focusing on Quantization-Aware Training (QAT), Post-Training Quantization (PTQ), and Zero-Shot Quantization (ZSQ).

QAT is effective but computationally expensive, requiring extensive retraining to maintain accuracy, particularly with low-bit quantization. This investment is justified for long-term deployments where efficiency and accuracy are critical. 

PTQ offers a cost-effective alternative, allowing quantization without retraining. It is suitable for scenarios with limited or unlabeled data, though it often results in lower accuracy than QAT. Techniques have been developed to mitigate PTQ's accuracy loss, including bias correction, weight range equalization, and methods like ACIQ and AdaRound that optimize quantization strategies.

Zero-shot quantization (ZSQ) addresses situations where training data is unavailable, proposing two levels: Level 1 involves no data or fine-tuning, while Level 2 requires fine-tuning but no data, yielding higher accuracy. Techniques in Level 1, such as weight range equalization, can struggle with non-linear activation functions.

Overall, the paper highlights the trade-offs between computational cost and accuracy in quantization methods, emphasizing the need for tailored approaches depending on data availability and model requirements.
