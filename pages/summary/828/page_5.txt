The paper discusses the process of quantization, specifically focusing on uniform quantization techniques and the distinctions between symmetric and asymmetric quantization. Quantization is a method used to convert real-valued floating-point numbers into lower precision integer representations, which is essential for efficient computation in machine learning models.

The quantization function is defined as Q(r) = Int(r/S + Z), where r is the real-valued input, S is a scaling factor, Z is an integer zero point, and Int represents a rounding operation. The paper emphasizes that uniform quantization results in quantized values that are evenly spaced, while non-uniform quantization yields unevenly spaced values.

A crucial aspect of uniform quantization is the selection of the scaling factor S, which partitions the range of real values into distinct segments. The clipping range, denoted as [β, ϕ], is critical for determining S. Calibration is necessary to establish this range, often using the minimum and maximum values of the signal, leading to asymmetric quantization when the range is not centered around zero. In contrast, symmetric quantization uses a range that is equal on both sides of zero, simplifying the quantization function by setting Z to zero.

The paper outlines two approaches for the scaling factor in symmetric quantization: full range and restricted range. Full range uses the entire INT8 range of [-128, 127], while restricted range limits the values to [-127, 127]. The choice of symmetric quantization is often favored for weights due to its computational efficiency, whereas asymmetric quantization may provide a tighter clipping range for imbalanced data, such as activations post-ReLU.

Overall, the discussion highlights the significance of quantization methods in optimizing neural network performance while balancing accuracy and computational efficiency.
