The paper discusses the advancements in edge processors designed for neural network (NN) inference, emphasizing the importance of low-cost and power-efficient solutions. It highlights various processors, such as the Qualcomm Wear 4100+, GAP-8, and Google Edge TPU, which are tailored for edge computing. These processors often rely on quantization techniques to optimize performance, especially in systems like ARM Cortex-M microcontrollers that lack dedicated floating-point units. The CMSIS-NN library facilitates this process by enabling fixed-point quantization.

Figure 9 illustrates the throughput of various commercial edge processors, showcasing significant improvements in computing power that now allow for the deployment of complex NN models previously limited to server environments. The paper argues that quantization, alongside efficient low-precision logic and dedicated deep learning accelerators, has been pivotal in this evolution.

Moreover, quantization is not only beneficial for edge processors but also enhances performance in non-edge systems, such as NVIDIA's Turing GPUs, which leverage specialized Tensor Cores for efficient low-precision calculations.

The paper concludes by outlining future research directions in quantization, focusing on software advancements, hardware and NN architecture co-design, coupled compression methods, and quantized training. These areas present challenges and opportunities that could further enhance the capabilities of NN inference systems.
