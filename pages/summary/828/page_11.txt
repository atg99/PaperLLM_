The paper discusses advances in Zero Shot Quantization (ZSQ), highlighting methods for generating synthetic data to enhance model quantization without relying on original training or validation datasets. It describes how earlier approaches, such as those using Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), fall short in capturing the internal statistics of real data, which can lead to inadequate representation of data distributions. Subsequent methods improved upon this by utilizing Batch Normalization statistics to create more realistic synthetic data through techniques like minimizing KL divergence.

ZeroQ is introduced as a significant contribution, enabling sensitivity measurement and calibration for mixed-precision quantization without needing access to training data. Both ZeroQ and other recent studies utilize trainable input images and backpropagation to align synthetic data statistics with real data. ZSQ is particularly advantageous for Machine Learning as a Service (MLaaS) providers, allowing for rapid deployment without accessing sensitive datasets.

Additionally, the paper touches on stochastic quantization, which contrasts with the traditional deterministic approach. Stochastic quantization is posited to enable neural networks to explore weight updates more effectively, potentially leading to better training outcomes by providing a probabilistic framework for rounding operations. This method allows for more dynamic adjustments in weights, thereby enhancing model performance during training.
