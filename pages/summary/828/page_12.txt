This paper discusses the performance and efficiency of various data types used in neural network quantization, particularly focusing on lower precision formats such as FP32, FP16, INT8, and INT4. The analysis includes a comparison of peak throughput and energy costs associated with different bit-precision operations on Titan RTX and A100 GPUs. It highlights that lower precision operations yield significantly better energy efficiency and throughput.

The paper introduces stochastic quantization methods, such as the QuantNoise technique, which allows for effective lower-bit precision quantization with minimal accuracy loss in models for computer vision and natural language processing. However, it notes the challenge of computational overhead associated with generating random numbers for weight updates, which limits widespread adoption.

Advanced concepts in quantization are explored, including simulated quantization versus integer-only quantization. Simulated quantization uses low-precision parameter storage while performing operations in floating-point arithmetic, which does not fully leverage the benefits of low-precision logic. In contrast, integer-only quantization executes all operations in low-precision integer format, facilitating efficient inference without the need for dequantization.

The paper underscores the trade-offs between achieving high quantization accuracy using floating-point arithmetic and the advantages of low-precision logic in terms of latency, power consumption, and area efficiency. Overall, it emphasizes the importance of exploring various quantization techniques to enhance neural network performance while minimizing resource usage.
