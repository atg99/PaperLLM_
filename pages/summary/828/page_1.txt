This paper provides a comprehensive survey of quantization methods aimed at enhancing the efficiency of neural network inference. The authors, Amir Gholami, Sehoon Kim, Zhen Dong, Zhewei Yao, Michael W. Mahoney, and Kurt Keutzer from the University of California, Berkeley, highlight the growing importance of quantization in the context of neural networks, particularly as these models have become increasingly large and complex. The transition from floating-point to low-precision fixed integer representations can significantly reduce memory usage and latency, making it crucial for deployment in resource-constrained environments.

The paper outlines the challenges posed by the need for real-time inference with low energy consumption while maintaining high accuracy across various applications, including healthcare monitoring and autonomous driving. The authors categorize existing research efforts into two main approaches: optimizing neural network architectures and co-designing neural networks with hardware. The former includes both micro-architecture optimizations and automated methods like Neural Architecture Search, while the latter focuses on tailoring neural network designs to specific hardware constraints to enhance performance.

Overall, this survey aims to provide a structured overview of current quantization research, detailing the advantages and disadvantages of various methods, and serves as a foundation for evaluating future developments in the field.
