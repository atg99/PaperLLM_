The paper discusses the advancements and challenges in deploying low-precision quantized neural network (NN) models, particularly focusing on INT8 and lower bit-precision quantization. It highlights several software packages like Nvidia's TensorRT and TVM that facilitate the implementation of INT8 quantized models, noting that while these tools are effective and well-documented, support for lower bit-precision quantization is limited. For example, sub-INT8 quantization is not supported in TensorRT, and INT4 quantization was only recently introduced in TVM.

The paper emphasizes the importance of hardware and NN architecture co-design, noting that quantization-aware training can lead to different solutions with similar accuracy. This flexibility allows for adaptations in NN architecture during quantization, such as adjusting the width of the network to minimize generalization gaps.

Additionally, the paper discusses coupled compression methods, suggesting that combining quantization with other techniques like pruning and knowledge distillation could optimize model efficiency. However, there is a lack of research on the best combinations of these methods.

The paper also addresses the potential of quantized training to accelerate NN training processes using half-precision, although challenges remain in achieving stable training at INT8 precision. The authors argue that overcoming these challenges could significantly impact applications, particularly in edge computing.

In conclusion, the paper outlines the historical context of quantization in computer science and its relevance to modern NN design. It underscores the computational intensity and over-parameterization of NNs as critical factors that make efficient numerical representation essential. The transition from floating-point to low-precision representations can significantly reduce memory usage and latency, with empirical evidence showing notable speedups in popular computer vision models when using INT8 quantization.
