The paper discusses methods to recover accuracy degradation following the binarization and ternarization of models, highlighting improved training techniques for binary and ternary models. It identifies limitations in the Straight-Through Estimator (STE) used for backpropagation through the sign function, which only propagates gradients for weights and activations within a specified range. Solutions include the introduction of continuous approximations for the sign function and the use of identity shortcuts in Bi-Real Net to enhance gradient flow. The paper also emphasizes the importance of quantizing gradients alongside weights and activations to speed up training, as seen in DoReFa-Net.

Extreme quantization is noted for its effectiveness in reducing model size and inference latency in CNNs, with recent efforts extending this technique to Natural Language Processing (NLP) tasks. However, it is acknowledged that extreme low-bit precision quantization may lead to significant accuracy degradation unless extensive tuning is applied, although this may be acceptable in less critical applications.

Vector quantization methods, rooted in information theory and digital signal processing, are also explored. Techniques like k-means clustering for weight grouping and product quantization are shown to effectively reduce model size with minimal accuracy loss. Additionally, the paper discusses the advantages of quantization in enhancing performance on hardware platforms, particularly for edge devices with limited resources and no support for floating-point operations. Quantization is thus positioned as a crucial strategy for deploying deep neural networks in resource-constrained environments.
