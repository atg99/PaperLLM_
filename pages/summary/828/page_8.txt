This paper discusses two main methods for quantization in neural networks: Quantization-Aware Training (QAT) and Post-Training Quantization (PTQ). QAT involves fine-tuning a pre-trained model with quantized parameters using training data, allowing the model to recover from accuracy degradation caused by quantization. Conversely, PTQ calibrates a pre-trained model using a subset of training data to compute necessary scaling factors and clipping ranges before quantization, without additional fine-tuning.

The paper also highlights various quantization strategies, including rule-based non-uniform quantization, which employs logarithmic distributions, and binary-code-based quantization, which represents real-number vectors as binary vectors. Recent advancements have framed non-uniform quantization as an optimization problem, enhancing accuracy by minimizing the difference between the original and quantized tensors. Learnable quantizers are introduced, where quantization steps are optimized alongside model parameters.

While non-uniform quantization captures signal information more effectively, it poses challenges for deployment on standard hardware like GPUs and CPUs. Thus, uniform quantization remains the preferred method due to its simplicity and efficiency.

Fine-tuning after quantization is essential, and the paper elaborates on QAT's mechanism of re-training the model with quantized parameters to achieve a lower loss, contrasting it with PTQ, which does not involve retraining. The discussion emphasizes the importance of choosing the appropriate quantization method based on the specific needs and constraints of model deployment.
