The paper titled "DeepGCNs: Can GCNs Go as Deep as CNNs?" explores the potential of Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) to achieve the deep architectures that have benefitted Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs). While CNNs excel in handling Euclidean data, they struggle with non-Euclidean data, which GCNs are designed to address. However, traditional GCN models are typically limited to shallow architectures, often no deeper than four layers, due to the vanishing gradient problem.

To tackle this challenge, the authors propose adapting concepts from CNNs, such as residual and dense connections, as well as dilated convolutions, to enhance the training of deep GCNs. Their extensive experiments demonstrate that these adaptations lead to significant improvements in performance. Notably, they successfully construct a 56-layer GCN that outperforms existing models in point cloud semantic segmentation by 3.7% in mean Intersection over Union (mIoU).

The work emphasizes the growing relevance of GCNs in various applications, including social network analysis, drug discovery, and point cloud segmentation. By enabling deeper GCN architectures, the authors believe their research will open new avenues for advancements in GCN-based methodologies, similar to the impact that deep architectures have had on CNNs.
