This paper presents a comprehensive study on training very deep Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) by adapting successful methodologies from deep Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), such as residual connections, dense connections, and dilated convolutions. The authors demonstrate how these adaptations can be integrated into a graph framework, enhancing the accuracy and stability of deep GCNs. The research specifically applies these modified layers to point cloud semantic segmentation, achieving a significant milestone with a GCN architecture that is 56 layers deep, which surpasses previous models and improves state-of-the-art performance on the S3DIS dataset by 3.7%.

The contributions of the work are threefold: first, the adaptation of residual/dense connections and dilated convolutions for GCNs; second, extensive experimentation on point cloud data to analyze the impact of these layers on the stability and performance of deep GCNs; and third, the development of the deepest GCN architecture to date, which leads to notable performance improvements.

The paper also discusses the limitations of current GCN models, which often struggle with depth due to issues such as over-smoothing and vanishing gradients. By leveraging strategies from deep CNNs, such as those used in ResNet and DenseNet, the authors successfully address these challenges, demonstrating that these techniques can significantly enhance the training of deep GCNs for complex tasks, particularly in the context of non-Euclidean data applications found in various fields, including social networks, chemistry, and computer vision.
