The paper presents three models: PlainGCN, ResGCN, and DenseGCN, designed for graph convolutional networks (GCNs). 

PlainGCN serves as the baseline, comprising a backbone with 28 EdgeConv layers using dynamic k-NN, without skip connections. ResGCN enhances PlainGCN by integrating dynamic dilated k-NN and residual connections, maintaining parameter count. DenseGCN similarly adds dynamic dilated k-NN but utilizes dense connections that concatenate intermediate representations, following the same dilation schedule as ResGCN.

Implementation details include the use of TensorFlow, an Adam optimizer with a learning rate of 0.001, and batch normalization. The models are trained end-to-end without data augmentation. 

Results reveal that residual connections significantly stabilize training deeper networks, as evidenced by a 12% mIoU performance drop when omitted. Dilated convolutions improve mean IoU by 2.85%, especially when combined with residual connections, while dynamic k-NN enhances performance but incurs higher computational costs. Dense connections also yield performance gains, although they are more memory-intensive than residual connections. 

The analysis shows that increasing the number of neighbors generally improves performance, but only if network capacity is sufficiently large. Moreover, both increased depth and width of the network contribute positively to performance, underscoring the importance of residual connections and dilated convolutions in achieving optimal results. Overall, the study emphasizes the critical role of architectural choices in enhancing GCN performance.
