The paper presents an ablation study on the ResGCN-28 model, which employs residual graph connections and dilated graph convolutions for point cloud semantic segmentation. The study compares ResGCN-28 to various ablated variants, analyzing the impact of different configurations on mean Intersection over Union (mIoU). Results indicate that ResGCN-28 achieves an mIoU of 52.49, outperforming several other models, including PlainGCN-28 and Fixedk-NN, by substantial margins.

The qualitative analysis shows that ResGCN-28 and DenseGCN-28 excel in segmenting challenging classes such as board, beam, bookcase, and door, while PlainGCN-28 fails to effectively identify these objects. The paper further compares ResGCN-28 to state-of-the-art methods, demonstrating a notable 3.9% improvement over DGCNN in mean IoU, particularly excelling in difficult classes like board and sofa.

The authors argue that the enhanced performance stems from the architectural innovations of ResGCN, which include the introduction of skip connections and dilated convolutions. These modifications enable deeper GCNs to be trained effectively, overcoming the training difficulties typically associated with deeper architectures. The study concludes by emphasizing the potential for further improvement through hyper-parameter optimization and suggests that deep GCNs can achieve high performance even with a reduced number of nearest neighbors.
