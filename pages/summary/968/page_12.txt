This paper discusses advancements in deep reinforcement learning (DRL) and introduces various methodologies and findings from notable research in the area. The focus is on model-based approaches, which leverage learned dynamics to improve planning and decision-making in high-dimensional environments.

Key contributions include the development of recurrent world models that enhance policy evolution, the exploration of latent dynamics for planning from visual input, and the introduction of stochastic value gradients for continuous control policies. The authors also highlight the significance of combining improvements in DRL techniques, such as those presented in the Rainbow framework, which integrates several enhancements to boost performance.

The paper reviews foundational concepts like Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks, auto-encoding variational Bayes, and the role of imagination in reinforcement learning. Additionally, it addresses the evaluation protocols in environments like the Arcade Learning Environment, emphasizing the importance of data efficiency in recent advancements.

Overall, the paper underscores the evolving landscape of DRL, showcasing the interplay between model learning, planning, and the quest for human-level control in complex tasks.
