The paper discusses the SimPLe approach to model-based reinforcement learning, highlighting the significance of using random starts in rollouts to minimize compounding errors. Experiments demonstrate that omitting random starts leads to significantly worse outcomes, particularly in the game Seaquest.

The qualitative analysis section examines individual games without adjusting hyperparameters, revealing that SimPLe successfully achieved maximum scores in Pong and Freeway within a budget of 100k interactions. Freeway presents unique exploration challenges, as agents struggle to gain rewards due to the game's dynamics. SimPLe's ability to capture rare events in this context showcases its predictive capabilities, although some runs resulted in limited exploration due to rapid entropy decay in the policy.

In pixel-perfect games like Pong and Breakout, the models accurately predicted future states for short intervals, suggesting potential for future research in extending this accuracy over longer sequences. Despite some benign errors, such as non-physical outcomes in Bowling and Pong, the models still produced effective policies. However, the paper notes failures in more complex games where small, critical objects were missed, particularly in Atlantis and Battle Zone, indicating areas for improvement in training protocols and model development.
