The paper presents a detailed analysis of various configurations of the SimPLe framework for reinforcement learning, focusing on model choices, training parameters, and performance metrics. The results, summarized in Table 1, indicate that the stochastic discrete model outperforms other architectures, with the default configuration achieving the highest median scores across multiple games. 

Key findings include the impact of the number of steps in PPO rollouts, where configurations with 25 and 50 steps generally performed better than 100, likely due to reduced model error accumulation. The choice of discount factor gamma (γ) also influences performance, with γ=0.95 showing slight advantages, suggesting a better handling of model imperfections, although all tested values performed similarly overall.

The iterative training process is emphasized as critical for complex tasks, noting that some games yielded optimal policies early in training. This points to the potential for faster training through improved exploration strategies. Additionally, extending model training significantly enhanced performance, reinforcing the importance of resource allocation in training paradigms.

Overall, the findings support the efficacy of the stochastic discrete model and highlight significant factors influencing training success, such as model architecture, training duration, and the choice of hyperparameters like gamma. The authors provide their code in the Tensor2Tensor library for reproducibility and further exploration.
