The paper details the architecture of a world model that is pivotal to the proposed algorithm. It emphasizes that the model is versatile and not limited to Atari games, suggesting its applicability to various visual prediction tasks. The architecture comprises approximately 74 million parameters, with inference and backpropagation times of about 0.5 seconds and 0.7 seconds, respectively, when processed on an NVIDIA Tesla P100. In comparison, the ALE simulator operates at around 0.4 milliseconds per step.

The architecture includes a frame prediction network and a latent inference network utilized during training. The frame prediction network consists of multiple downscale and upscale convolution layers, action embedding, latent predictor components, and a reward predictor. Each layer's specifications, such as kernel sizes and strides, are meticulously outlined, with all activation functions being ReLU, except for output layers which use softmax.

Additionally, the downscale layers in the frame prediction network feature residual connections to their corresponding upscale layers, and dropout and layer normalization are applied to all convolutional layers. The latent predictor outputs sequentially across 128 bits in chunks of 8. Overall, the architecture is designed to enhance performance in visual prediction tasks while maintaining efficiency in processing.
