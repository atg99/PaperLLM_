The paper presents SimPLe, a novel approach for policy training in reinforcement learning, particularly in the context of Atari games. The authors detail the use of proximal policy optimization (PPO) for policy improvement, leveraging short rollouts from a simulated environment to address the challenges posed by model imperfections. SimPLe incorporates a strategy of restarting simulations from uniformly sampled states every 50 steps, enhancing the robustness of learning despite the limited rollout length. 

The training process involves iterating the main loop 15 times, with the world model trained initially for 45,000 steps and subsequently for 15,000 steps in later iterations. This incremental training allows the model to adapt to new game dynamics without significant performance loss. The agent interacts with the simulated environment extensively, performing 15.2 million interactions throughout the training.

The experiments evaluate SimPLe against established state-of-the-art methods, specifically Rainbow and PPO, across 26 Atari games. The results indicate that SimPLe is significantly more sample-efficient than Rainbow, requiring less than half the samples for over half of the games tested, and shows marked improvements over PPO as well. This efficiency is quantified with comparisons against fixed score baselines, demonstrating the method’s effectiveness in achieving high performance with fewer interactions. The findings highlight SimPLe's potential as a powerful tool in reinforcement learning, particularly in environments where sample efficiency is critical.
