The paper presents SimPLe, a model-based reinforcement learning method evaluated against state-of-the-art model-free algorithms, Rainbow and PPO, in terms of sample efficiency on Atari games. The main findings indicate that SimPLe significantly outperforms both Rainbow and PPO, often requiring fewer than 100,000 interaction steps to achieve comparable performance, as illustrated in Figure 3. The red line in the figure denotes this threshold, with bars exceeding it indicating greater step requirements for the model-free methods.

SimPLe's advantage is particularly pronounced in several games, where it reaches performance levels similar to PPO at 10 million steps, demonstrating its effectiveness in learning with reduced sample complexity. The evaluation involved averaging results from five runs per game, highlighting that SimPLe consistently outperforms a random policy across nearly all games, except for one instance. Notably, it surpassed the average human score in six games, suggesting potential for further performance improvements through stabilization of the method.

The paper acknowledges challenges such as high variance in training results, which may stem from interactions between policy and supervised training, as well as possible domain mismatches. Detailed numerical results are available in the appendix, providing insights into best scores and variances. The source code for SimPLe is accessible through the Tensor2Tensor library, facilitating replication of the experiments.
