This paper discusses advancements in model-based reinforcement learning (RL) applied to video games, specifically focusing on Atari 2600 environments. The authors optimized the Rainbow algorithm for enhanced performance over 1 million steps, highlighting the importance of learned simulators in training effective game-playing policies. They build on previous works, such as those by Oh et al. and Chiappa et al., which demonstrated the feasibility of predictive models in Atari games. 

While previous studies have tended to focus on implicit knowledge representation in game environments, this paper emphasizes the need for explicit modeling, referencing works that explore object interactions and behaviors in different gaming contexts. The authors note a scarcity of model-based RL approaches that utilize image data from games, with notable exceptions including contributions from Oh et al., Ha & Schmidhuber, and Holland et al., who have explored various ways to integrate model learning with policy training.

The paper presents a model-based RL algorithm that alternates between learning a model of the environment and optimizing a policy using model-free reinforcement learning techniques. This approach is grounded in earlier algorithms, such as Dyna-Q, and incorporates deep learning advancements. The authors report significant performance improvements of their method over existing algorithms like Dyna-DQN and GATS, achieving notably higher scores across multiple Atari games. 

Overall, the research highlights the potential of learned video prediction models to enhance policy training in reinforcement learning, bridging insights from both gaming and robotics domains.
