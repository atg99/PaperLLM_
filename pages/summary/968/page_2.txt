This paper presents Simulated Policy Learning (SimPLe), a novel approach to model-based reinforcement learning that enhances sample efficiency in playing Atari games using the Arcade Learning Environment (ALE) benchmark. The main loop of SimPLe involves the agent interacting with the real environment, collecting observations to train the world model, and updating the policy through simulations within this model. This method employs stochastic video prediction techniques, including a unique model utilizing discrete latent variables.

Empirical results demonstrate that SimPLe outperforms a highly optimized version of the Rainbow algorithm, achieving significantly better sample efficiency across nearly all tested games. In low data regimes, SimPLe requires fewer samples—sometimes over ten times less than Rainbow—to achieve similar or superior performance.

The paper situates its contributions within the broader context of reinforcement learning, highlighting the historical development of algorithms that learn directly from game images and the persistent challenge of sample complexity compared to human learning. The authors emphasize the importance of their work in advancing the state-of-the-art in model-based reinforcement learning, particularly in its ability to efficiently learn from limited data, which remains a critical hurdle in the field.
