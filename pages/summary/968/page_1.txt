The paper "Model Based Reinforcement Learning for Atari" presents a novel approach to reinforcement learning (RL) that leverages model-based strategies to enhance the efficiency of learning in complex environments such as Atari games. The authors, including researchers from Google Brain and various academic institutions, argue that while model-free RL methods require extensive interactions with the environment—often in the order of tens or hundreds of millions of time steps—humans can learn to play these games in a matter of minutes. This discrepancy suggests that humans utilize an intuitive understanding of the game's mechanics, allowing them to predict the outcomes of their actions.

The authors introduce Simulated Policy Learning (SimPLe), a comprehensive model-based deep RL algorithm that employs video prediction models to facilitate learning with significantly fewer interactions—targeting a budget of just 100,000 time steps, equivalent to approximately two hours of gameplay. Through their experiments, the authors compare various model architectures, including a novel architecture that demonstrates superior performance in the context of Atari games.

The results indicate that SimPLe outperforms leading model-free RL algorithms in most games, achieving improvements by over an order of magnitude in some instances. This work addresses a gap in existing literature by successfully demonstrating competitive model-based control in the Atari Learning Environment, a task previously identified as a significant challenge. Overall, the findings suggest that integrating predictive models can substantially reduce the amount of training data required for effective policy learning in RL.
