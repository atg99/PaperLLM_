In this section of the paper, the authors present their research on developing an effective world model for video prediction, specifically within the context of Atari games. They introduce a novel stochastic video prediction model that outperforms previous models. The architecture is based on a convolutional feedforward network and processes inputs of four consecutive game frames along with an action. The model employs a series of convolutional layers followed by dense layers, culminating in deconvolutional layers that output high-resolution images and reward predictions.

Key architectural features include the use of one-hot encoding for actions and the application of dropout and layer normalization to enhance performance. The authors emphasize the importance of loss functions, particularly the use of clipped loss to improve model training by focusing optimization on critical components of the game state.

To address issues of compounding errors from self-predictions, the authors implement scheduled sampling, which helps the model maintain accuracy during training. They also explore stochastic models to enhance prediction quality by incorporating variational autoencoders to model environmental stochasticity. However, they identify challenges related to the KL divergence loss term, which varies by game and can lead to poor predictions.

To mitigate these challenges, the authors propose a model with discrete latent variables, where latent values are discretized into binary bits. An auxiliary LSTM-based network is trained to predict these bits autoregressively. This approach improves robustness against unseen latent values during inference by incorporating noise into the approximated latent values. Overall, the proposed model demonstrates significant advancements in the realm of video prediction for game environments.
