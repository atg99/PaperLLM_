The paper discusses the importance of incorporating stochasticity into world models, particularly in the context of Atari games, which, despite being deterministic, exhibit stochastic behavior when viewed through a limited number of past frames. The authors highlight that the stochastic nature of games, such as Kung Fu Master, complicates predictions based solely on visual observations. They reference previous work that demonstrated the limitations of deterministic models in such environments, where outputs became blurred due to the inability to predict random movements.

Through experiments, the paper illustrates that the proposed stochastic discrete world model outperforms deterministic counterparts by effectively sampling potential opponents and rendering them clearly. The authors also address the impact of environment stochasticity on the performance of their method, SimPLe, showing that it can function effectively in truly stochastic environments, even when accounting for sticky actions.

The paper further evaluates various design decisions and their effects on model performance. It finds that the stochastic discrete model significantly outperforms other architectures, and that the length of training is a crucial factor, although constrained by training costs. The authors also analyze the effects of different rollout lengths and discount factors, finding that a discount factor of 0.95 yields slightly better results due to its tolerance to model imperfections.

Finally, the iterative nature of training the model and policy is emphasized as essential for achieving good results in complex tasks. The authors suggest that faster training could be possible with more directed exploration policies and highlight the importance of starting rollouts from randomly selected states to minimize compounding errors in the model.
