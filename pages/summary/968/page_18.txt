The paper presented at ICLR 2020 investigates the performance of reinforcement learning algorithms through various experiments, focusing on the effects of extended model training and adjustments to the discount factor γ in Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). Key findings show that extending model training leads to improved performance across multiple games, as illustrated in the graphs where the number of interactions required by the Rainbow algorithm to reach specific scores is compared to SimPLe's threshold of 100K interactions. Additionally, the results indicate that varying the discount factor γ influences the learning efficiency, with different values yielding distinct performance outcomes. The cumulative distribution function (CDF) analysis reveals the iterations needed to achieve maximum scores, highlighting the significant differences between employing random starts versus not using them in the Seaquest game, thus emphasizing the importance of initialization strategies in reinforcement learning. Overall, the paper underscores the critical impact of training duration and parameter adjustments on the effectiveness of reinforcement learning approaches.
