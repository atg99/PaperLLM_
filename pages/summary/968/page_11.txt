The referenced papers cover a range of topics within deep reinforcement learning (RL) and related fields, emphasizing advancements in sample efficiency, policy search, and model-based approaches. 

Azizzadenesheli et al. (2020) present a method for sample-efficient deep RL using generative adversarial tree search, aiming to enhance learning performance with fewer samples. Babaeizadeh et al. (2017a) explore stochastic variational video prediction, contributing to the understanding of dynamic environments in RL. They also investigate asynchronous advantage actor-critic methods for GPU use (2017b), which optimize computational efficiency in training.

Bellemare et al. (2015) introduce the Arcade Learning Environment, a benchmark for evaluating general agents, while Bengio et al. (2015) propose scheduled sampling for sequence prediction with recurrent neural networks, improving training stability. Buesing et al. (2019) focus on counterfactually-guided policy search, which enhances decision-making in uncertain environments.

Castro et al. (2018) discuss Dopamine, a research framework for deep RL, facilitating the implementation of various algorithms. Chiappa et al. (2017) present recurrent environment simulators, which aid in training RL agents in more realistic settings. Chua et al. (2018) contribute to model-based RL with probabilistic dynamics models, enabling efficient learning with fewer trials.

Deisenroth et al. (2013) provide a comprehensive survey on policy search methods for robotics, while Ebert et al. (2017) and Ebert et al. (2018) investigate visual foresight and self-supervised visual planning, respectively, both crucial for robotic control tasks. Ersen and Sariel (2014) focus on spatio-temporal reasoning for object interaction learning, enhancing the understanding of complex behaviors.

Espeholt et al. (2018) introduce IMPALA, a scalable architecture for distributed deep RL, improving training efficiency. Feinberg et al. (2018) propose a model-based value estimation technique to enhance model-free RL efficiency. Finally, Finn and Levine (2017) and Finn et al. (2016) explore deep visual foresight and spatial autoencoders, contributing to the planning and learning of robotic motions.

Overall, these works collectively advance the field of deep reinforcement learning, emphasizing efficiency, adaptability, and the integration of visual information for improved agent performance in complex environments.
