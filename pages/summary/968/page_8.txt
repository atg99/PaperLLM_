This paper presents findings on the performance of SimPLe, a model-based reinforcement learning algorithm, when compared to Rainbow and PPO, model-free algorithms, across varying numbers of interactions with the environment. The results indicate that SimPLe outperforms both Rainbow and PPO, even when the latter are provided with double the interaction time. Specifically, the study focuses on learning in environments with 100K interaction steps and extends to additional settings with 20K, 50K, 200K, 500K, and 1M interactions.

The analysis shows that SimPLe performs relatively poorly with 20K interactions, but its performance improves significantly with 50K interactions, reaching near optimal levels at 100K. Performance continues to improve up to 500K interactions, where it matches that of model-free PPO. This trend illustrates SimPLe's strength in low data regimes, although its advantages diminish as the amount of data increases. Such behavior aligns with previous observations regarding model-based versus model-free methods.

Additionally, the paper investigates whether a model trained with SimPLe at 100K interactions can effectively initialize further training with model-free PPO. The results confirm that this initialization is beneficial, although the lower performance ceiling may be attributed to poorer exploration during the PPO training phase, as the policy pre-trained with SimPLe exhibits low entropy at 100K interactions. Overall, the findings underscore the effectiveness of SimPLe in environments with limited data while highlighting the potential for enhancement through model-free training techniques.
