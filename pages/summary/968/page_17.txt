The paper presents a comparative analysis of the performance of the Rainbow reinforcement learning algorithm against various configurations of the SimPLe algorithm across multiple Atari games. The results are illustrated through a series of graphs showing the effect of different factors such as stochasticity, architecture type (non-recurrent vs recurrent), number of training epochs, and the number of steps taken during training.

1. **Effect of Stochasticity**: The analysis indicates how the introduction of stochastic elements in the environment influences the performance of the algorithms across various games.

2. **Effect of Recurrent Architecture**: The results demonstrate the impact of using recurrent architectures on the learning efficiency of the algorithms, suggesting that recurrent methods may enhance performance in certain scenarios.

3. **Effect of Adjusting Number of Epochs**: Different configurations of training iterations reveal how the number of epochs influences the interactions required to achieve scores comparable to SimPLe, with 15 iterations being the default.

4. **Effect of Adjusting Number of Steps**: The graphs further show how varying the number of steps during training (25, 50, and 100) affects the learning process, with the default set at 50 steps.

Overall, the findings highlight the importance of configuration choices in reinforcement learning and provide insights into optimizing algorithm performance across diverse environments. The red line in the graphs indicates a threshold of 100,000 interactions, serving as a benchmark for evaluating the algorithms' effectiveness.
