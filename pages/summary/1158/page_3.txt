This paper discusses the enhancement of decomposition methods through two primary techniques: shrinking and caching. Previous research provided a theoretical foundation for these techniques under certain assumptions; however, this paper offers a more generalized explanation without those assumptions. The authors establish the linear convergence of their proposed method, indicating how rapidly it approaches an optimal solution. This convergence analysis is relevant not only for support vector classification but also for regression and other variants of support vector machines (SVMs), with extensions detailed later in the paper.

In the second section, the paper explores existing methods for working set selection in Sequential Minimal Optimization (SMO) type methods, specifically introducing a new general scheme. One prevalent method discussed is the "maximal violating pair" selection technique, which identifies the most significant violations in the current model. This method, derived from the Karush-Kuhn-Tucker optimality conditions, selects pairs of indices based on their gradients and constraints, and has been widely implemented in software packages such as LIBSVM.

The paper thus contributes to the understanding and efficiency of SVM algorithms by refining existing techniques and providing a broader framework for their application.
