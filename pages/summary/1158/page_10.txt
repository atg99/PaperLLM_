The paper discusses the convergence properties of a specific algorithm, specifically Algorithm 2, which operates under the framework of Support Vector Machines (SVM). The analysis begins by establishing that a certain pair, identified as a violating pair, leads to a difference of 2 between two variables, thereby linking the optimal solutions of two equations. This relationship indicates that both equations share the same optimal point.

The paper then presents a lemma asserting that if a sub-sequence of the algorithm's output converges to a limit, then any shifted version of this sub-sequence also converges to the same limit. This is supported by the bounded nature of a related sequence and the properties of the algorithm, reinforcing the notion of convergence within the algorithm's iterations.

Following this, the paper introduces a theorem stating that any limit point of the sequence generated by Algorithm 2 is a stationary point of the optimization problem being solved. The proof involves demonstrating that if a limit point does not satisfy the optimality conditions, it leads to the identification of a violating pair, thus contradicting the assumption. 

Overall, the paper emphasizes the convergence and optimality properties of the algorithm, providing a rigorous foundation for its effectiveness in solving SVM-related optimization problems.
