The paper discusses the cyclic selection of pairs {i,j} in a specific format over a series of iterations, which aids in simplifying the convergence proof outlined in Theorem 3. The proof relies on the assumption that a limit point ¯α is not stationary and identifies its maximal violating pair {¯i,¯j}. This pair must be selected in subsequent iterations, leading to a contradiction based on the established conditions.

The conditions for working set selection, specifically (9) and (123), are noted to be distinct and neither is deemed necessary. A counter-example demonstrates that selected pairs can exhibit smaller violations than expected, prompting the introduction of additional working set selection strategies (WSS 2 and 3). The paper contrasts these strategies, explaining that while one emphasizes addressing maximal violating pairs, the other ensures that all current violating pairs are eventually selected. The authors argue that focusing on maximal violations may lead to faster convergence, albeit complicating the convergence proof due to the need for a counting procedure.
