The paper discusses the stopping condition and finite termination of the decomposition method used in optimization, particularly in the context of SMO-type methods. The method asymptotically approaches an optimum and typically terminates when a predefined stopping tolerance, denoted as ε, is satisfied by the inequality m(αk) - M(αk) ≤ ε. This condition is favored for its proximity to the optimality condition. 

To ensure the method terminates in a finite number of iterations, it is crucial to establish that the limit of m(αk) - M(αk) approaches zero as k approaches infinity. The paper identifies this as a key result that needs validation. Previous studies on stopping conditions have been referenced, noting that while some have considered stopping tolerances, the analysis is not directly applicable to the current method, which operates independently of ε.

The central theorem proposed asserts that under the assumption of a positive semi-definite matrix Q, the decomposition method will yield a sequence {αk} such that the limit of m(αk) - M(αk) equals zero. The proof approach is by contradiction, establishing that if the limit does not hold, it leads to an infinite set of indices with a constant positive difference between m(αk) and M(αk), which contradicts the convergence of the gradient of the function being optimized.
