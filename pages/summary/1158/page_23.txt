The paper discusses the iterative process of optimizing a function through the use of quadratic forms and specific variable transformations. It begins by calculating the difference between the (k+1)th and kth iterations, leading to a series of equations that express this difference in terms of the variables involved. The manipulation of these equations demonstrates the relationship between the current iteration and the adjustments made to reach the next one.

Key equations are derived, including the transformation of the difference into a quadratic form that involves the matrix Q and the variable changes represented by dk. The paper emphasizes the use of specific definitions for Q and v to simplify the expressions. Notably, it introduces the concept of eigenvalues to analyze the properties of the matrices involved, particularly in relation to the working set selection rule.

The selection rule is crucial in determining the distance between certain variables, establishing a criterion for the optimization process. This approach highlights the mathematical foundations necessary for understanding the convergence and stability of the iterative method being explored. The results are framed within the context of the optimization problem, providing a clear path from iteration to iteration while maintaining rigorous mathematical integrity.
