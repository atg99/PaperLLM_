The paper discusses a method for working set selection in optimization problems, particularly in the context of Support Vector Machines (SVM). It presents a procedure for selecting indices i and j from the upper and lower sets associated with a given αk, ensuring that a specific condition related to the gradients of the objective function is satisfied.

The function h(x) is introduced to guarantee the existence of at least one valid pair {i,j}. A simple form of this function is h(x) = σx, where σ is a constant between 0 and 1. More complex forms are also considered, such as a minimum function defined in relation to the difference between m(αk) and M(αk).

The paper then shifts focus to the asymptotic convergence of the method when using this working set selection strategy (WSS 3). It emphasizes the importance of the convergence analysis, particularly when the quadratic form Q is indefinite, which complicates proving sufficient decrease in function values.

A necessary condition for convergence involves ensuring that the function value at the new point is less than or equal to the function value at the current point minus a term proportional to the distance between the current and new points. The paper cites previous work that has established this condition for positive semi-definite Q but acknowledges the challenges posed by indefinite Q, particularly in the context of certain kernels like the sigmoid and edit-distance kernels.

The authors propose modifying the sub-problem to facilitate convergence, leading to a new optimization form that maintains the essential constraints while ensuring that the optimal solution at each iteration leads to a decrease in the objective function value. This adjustment is crucial for establishing strong convergence guarantees in the algorithm.
