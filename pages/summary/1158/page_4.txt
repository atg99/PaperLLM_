The paper discusses the conditions under which a feasible α is a stationary point of the optimization problem defined by (1). Specifically, it establishes that this is true if and only if the inequality m(α) ≤ M(α) holds, where m(α) and M(α) are defined based on the indices of the sets Iup(α) and Ilow(α). A "violating pair" is introduced as a crucial concept, defined as indices i and j that belong to Iup(α) and Ilow(α), respectively, where the condition −yi∇f(α)i > −yj∇f(α)j holds. The paper highlights that the indices that most violate condition (6) form a "maximal violating pair" and are essential for the working set in the decomposition methods.

Theorem 1 states that if Q is positive semi-definite, the decomposition method ensures a strict decrease in the objective function value at each iteration if the working set B includes at least one violating pair. However, the presence of a violating pair does not guarantee convergence to a stationary point, as illustrated by a specific example involving five data points and their corresponding labels. The example demonstrates how a sequence of working sets can lead to a series of α updates without necessarily converging to the optimal solution.
