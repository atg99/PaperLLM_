The paper discusses the convergence properties of an algorithm related to Support Vector Machines (SVM) and introduces extensions for Support Vector Regression and One-class SVM. It highlights that in the context of the algorithm, specifically condition (8) of Algorithm 2, the term h((v1−vl)/2)² impacts the convergence rate, with h(x) being bounded by x, leading to a linear convergence rate. 

Theorem 8 establishes that under the same assumptions as Theorem 7, there exists a constant c less than 1 and a threshold ¯k such that for all k greater than or equal to ¯k, the difference between the objective function values f(αk+1) and f(¯α) is bounded by c times the difference between f(αk) and f(¯α). The proof is derived from the quadratic form of the objective function, demonstrating that the convergence is maintained through the feasibility of αk.

The paper then extends the discussion to SVM variants, presenting a general optimization problem with constraints on the parameters α. It notes that the same SMO-type methods can be applied to this generalized form, affirming that the results established in previous sections remain valid for this more general case.
