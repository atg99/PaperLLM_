The paper discusses the convergence issues of an algorithm that selects working sets for optimization and highlights the importance of selecting maximal violating pairs. The current method results in an infinite number of iterations and converges to a non-optimal point with a specific objective value. An analysis of consecutive iterations reveals that the selected pairs do not effectively reduce the maximal violation, emphasizing the need for careful selection of working sets.

To address these issues, the authors propose a new approach for working set selection in SMO-type decomposition methods. They introduce the concept of a "constant-factor" violating pair, where the selected pair's violation is linked to the maximal violating pair through a constant factor. This new selection method is formalized in WSS 2, which ensures the quality of the chosen indices.

Additionally, the authors suggest a more generalized selection method in WSS 3, which involves a function that adheres to specific criteria, such as being strictly increasing and bounded. This framework aims to enhance the convergence properties of the algorithm while maintaining the integrity of the optimization process.
