The paper discusses the Sequential Minimal Optimization (SMO) method, a special decomposition technique for solving optimization problems by limiting the optimization to two variables at each iteration. The algorithm begins by selecting an initial feasible solution and continues until a stationary point is reached. At each iteration, a working set of two elements is chosen, and a sub-problem is formulated and solved under specific constraints. The algorithm ensures that the value of the optimization function decreases strictly with each iteration, although this does not guarantee that the sequence of solutions converges to a stationary point.

The paper emphasizes that proving the convergence of decomposition methods, particularly when using arbitrary working set sizes, is complex. However, it notes that asymptotic convergence can be established under certain selection rules for the working set. The authors provide a comprehensive study of SMO-type methods, detailing the selection rules for the two-element working set in a general context, which can be applied to various implementations of SMO. The paper includes discussions on existing selection strategies, proposes a generalized scheme, and presents proofs of asymptotic convergence, comparing these findings with previous studies on convergence in optimization methods.
