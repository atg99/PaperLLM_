The paper discusses the convergence of a sequence {αk} in the context of an optimization problem characterized by a quadratic function with a positive definite matrix Q. The analysis begins by establishing bounds on the sequences S1(k+1) and S2(k+1) based on the conditions of the working sets selected during iterations. The proof shows that if certain assumptions about the sequences are made, it leads to a contradiction, thus concluding that these assumptions must be incorrect.

It is highlighted that if the matrix Q is positive definite, the optimization problem has a unique optimal solution denoted as ¯α. The proof argues that if {αk} does not globally converge to ¯α, it would imply the existence of a subsequence that converges to a different point α*, contradicting the uniqueness of ¯α.

The paper also contrasts its approach with previous works that allowed for larger working sets. It emphasizes that when the working set is limited to two elements, specific criteria must be met for selection, which is not guaranteed in the current framework. The proof leverages the properties of the working sets to demonstrate that their sizes must shrink to zero, ultimately leading to a contradiction with the foundational assumptions.

In summary, the paper establishes conditions for global convergence of the sequence {αk} and discusses the implications of the positive definiteness of Q, while contrasting its methodology with earlier convergence analyses.
