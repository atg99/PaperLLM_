The paper discusses a modification to the Support Vector Machine (SVM) optimization process, particularly addressing challenges associated with non-convex scenarios in the sub-problem formulation. It highlights that many SVM implementations rely on a positive semi-definite matrix Q, which can lead to complications when Q is indefinite. To resolve these issues, the authors propose an alternative approach that ensures the sub-problem remains convex.

The proposed algorithm, referred to as Algorithm 2, retains steps from a previous algorithm but introduces a specific handling mechanism for indefinite Q. The key steps include:

1. Utilizing the original sub-problem when Q is positive definite.
2. Modifying the sub-problem to ensure convexity when Q is indefinite.

In the modified sub-problem, an additional term is introduced to the objective function, which depends on a constant τ and the characteristics of the matrix Q. This adjustment is crucial for maintaining convexity. The authors demonstrate that their approach leads to a solvable convex optimization problem, allowing for efficient resolution of both the original and modified sub-problems.

The paper emphasizes the significance of these modifications in advancing SVM optimization techniques, particularly in scenarios where traditional methods may falter due to non-convexity.
