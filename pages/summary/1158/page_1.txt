This paper discusses the Sequential Minimal Optimization (SMO)-type decomposition methods for training Support Vector Machines (SVMs), highlighting their flexibility in choosing two-element working sets. The authors present key findings, including a simple proof of asymptotic convergence, an explanation of shrinking and caching techniques, and the linear convergence of these methods. They also consider extensions to various SVM variants.

The introduction outlines the optimization problem that SVMs aim to solve, involving a symmetric matrix Q derived from the training data and kernel functions. Due to the density of matrix Q, traditional optimization methods face memory challenges, which the decomposition method addresses by iteratively focusing on a small subset of variables, termed the working set. This approach allows for efficient handling of the memory problem while training SVMs.
