The paper discusses the convergence rates of optimization methods used in support vector machines (SVMs), specifically focusing on the Sequential Minimal Optimization (SMO) algorithm. It contrasts two approaches for handling variables that remain at their bounds during iterations, as implemented in LIBSVM and SVMlight.

The authors establish that while asymptotic convergence has been proven, it is crucial to analyze the speed of convergence. They reference earlier work that demonstrated linear convergence in certain decomposition methods under specific conditions, extending this analysis to a new working set selection method (WSS 2) used in SMO. The results are contingent on certain assumptions, including the positive definiteness of the matrix Q and a nondegeneracy condition ensuring that optimal solutions yield unique conditions for bounded variables.

The convergence analysis shows that after sufficient iterations, all bounded variables stabilize and can be treated as constants, allowing for a simplified optimization problem to be formulated. The paper aims to provide a thorough proof of linear convergence for WSS 2 while excluding WSS 3, which introduces a quality control function that may influence convergence rates. Overall, the study contributes to understanding the efficiency of SVM training through enhanced convergence insights.
