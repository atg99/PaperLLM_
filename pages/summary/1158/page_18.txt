The paper discusses the conditions necessary for the effectiveness of decomposition methods in optimization, particularly focusing on the use of shrinking and caching techniques to enhance performance. The shrinking technique allows for the reduction of the optimization problem's size by ignoring certain bounded variables, which leads to faster computations and lower memory usage. Once the smaller problem is solved, the removed components are reintroduced to verify if an optimal solution has been attained.

Caching is another strategy employed to manage large matrices effectively. By storing recently used elements of the matrix Q in a designated cache, the method minimizes computational costs during final iterations, especially when only a few columns are required.

The paper presents Theorem 6, which establishes three key points under the assumption that Q is positive semi-definite and the SMO-type method uses WSS 3. First, it asserts that after a certain number of iterations, all relevant variables will converge to a unique and bounded optimal solution. Second, if an optimal solution exists, it is unique and can be reached in a finite number of iterations. Lastly, the limits of the sequences of m(αk) and M(αk) converge to the same value, which reflects the optimal solution and is independent of the specific solution chosen.

This theoretical foundation supports the implementation of shrinking and caching techniques without the need for assumptions about global convergence or specific subsequences, thereby providing a more general framework for these optimization strategies.
