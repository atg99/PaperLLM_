The paper presents a context-aware dynamics model (CaDM) aimed at improving generalization in model-based reinforcement learning (RL). The authors address the challenge of varying transition dynamics in different environments by introducing a framework that separates the task of learning dynamics into two main components: context encoding and transition inference.

In the proposed approach, a context encoder generates a latent vector that captures the dynamics-specific information from recent experiences. This latent vector is then used in both a forward dynamics model, which predicts the next state based on the current state, action, and context, and a backward dynamics model, which predicts the current state from the next state, action, and context. This dual modeling allows for improved predictions and generalization to unseen environments.

The authors emphasize that their method is designed to be robust against changes in dynamics, such as those caused by unexpected damages in robotic systems. By training on environments with varying contexts and leveraging a novel loss function, the context latent vector becomes effective for multiple auxiliary prediction tasks, enhancing the overall generalization capabilities of the model.

The framework is positioned within the standard RL paradigm as a Markov Decision Process (MDP), where the objective is to find a policy that maximizes expected returns. The paper argues that traditional methods may struggle with generalization, especially in scenarios with unseen contexts, and highlights the advantages of their context-aware approach in addressing these limitations.
