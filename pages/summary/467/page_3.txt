The paper presents qualitative and quantitative results of various segmentation models evaluated on the sitting people dataset, which focuses on semantic segmentation of human parts. The models assessed include VGG UNet, PSP Pretrained, ResNet50 UNet, ResNet50 PSPNet, SegNet, and MobileNet UNet. The performance metrics reported include mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) and frequency-weighted Intersection over Union (fIoU).

Key findings from the results indicate that the PSP Pretrained model achieved the highest mIoU of 62.19 and fIoU of 93.95, followed closely by ResNet50 UNet with mIoU of 62.16. In contrast, ResNet50 PSPNet showed the lowest performance with an mIoU of 44.83. The paper emphasizes the importance of skip connections in encoder-decoder architectures, which enhance boundary accuracy by providing essential information from earlier layers to the decoder.

The experiments were conducted using three distinct datasets: CamVid, which includes pixel-level semantic labels for driving scenarios; the sitting people dataset from the University of Freiburg, which focuses on various human parts; and the SUIM dataset, which contains underwater imagery with annotations for multiple categories.

The models benchmarked include SegNet, a standard encoder-decoder network without skip connections or pretraining, and MobileNet UNet, which combines lightweight feature extraction with precise pixel-wise predictions, making it suitable for real-time applications. The results highlight the effectiveness of different architectures in semantic segmentation tasks across diverse datasets.
