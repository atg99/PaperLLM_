The references listed encompass a range of significant works in the field of human-robot interaction (HRI) and robotic ethics. Notable contributions include foundational texts by Jason Borenstein and Ronald C. Arkin, which emphasize the importance of scientific research in understanding the ethical dimensions of robots and intimacy. Valentino Braitenberg's work on synthetic psychology through vehicles provides insights into the behavioral aspects of robots.

Research by Jurgen Brandstetter and colleagues explores social dynamics in HRI, including conformity experiments akin to Asch's study, and the phenomenon of lexical entrainment in human-robot communication. Cynthia Breazeal's pioneering work focuses on designing sociable robots and the application of emotion theory to enhance interactions, highlighting the significance of nonverbal cues and context in teamwork.

Further studies by Paul Bremner and others examine conversational gestures and their impact on HRI. The acceptance of healthcare robots among older populations is reviewed by Elizabeth Broadbent, indicating a growing interest in the integration of robots in elder care. Joost Broekens’ work provides a comprehensive overview of assistive social robots in elderly care, stressing their potential benefits.

Overall, these references illustrate the interdisciplinary nature of research in HRI, combining insights from psychology, robotics, and social sciences to advance the understanding of how robots can effectively interact with humans.
