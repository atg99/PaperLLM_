The references presented include a diverse array of studies focused on human-robot interaction, design practices, social conformity, and the psychological impacts of robots on individuals, particularly the elderly. Key works include Walter Vincenti's analysis of engineering knowledge, research by Vollmer et al. on peer pressure induced by robots, and a survey by Vredenburg et al. on user-centered design practices. Wada and Shibata explore the effects of seal robots on elderly care, while Walczyk et al. investigate cognitive mechanisms behind deception. Studies by Walden et al. and Walters et al. delve into the expectations of senior citizens regarding robot interactions and the influence of personality traits on personal space in human-robot exchanges. The role of culture in adhering to robot recommendations is examined by Wang et al., and Watanabe et al. question the feasibility of androids as salespeople. Overall, these references contribute to a deeper understanding of the complexities involved in designing and implementing robots in social contexts.
