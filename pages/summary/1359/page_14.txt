The references listed cover a range of topics related to emotion regulation, human-robot interaction, and communication in various contexts. Key contributions include:

1. Gunes et al. provide a survey on emotion representation and analysis in continuous space, highlighting advancements in automatic face and gesture recognition.
2. Hall et al. explore proxemics, the study of personal space in human interactions, offering foundational insights into social behavior.
3. Han et al. present a method for speech emotion recognition using deep learning techniques, emphasizing the role of technology in understanding emotions.
4. Hashimoto et al. investigate the role of robots as educational tools in a classroom setting, demonstrating their potential as teaching assistants.
5. Haslam reviews the concept of dehumanization, providing a psychological perspective on human interactions and perceptions.
6. Hayashi et al. discuss friendly patrolling models in robotics, focusing on natural social encounters between humans and robots.
7. Heider and Simmel's classic study examines human interpretation of animated shapes, shedding light on perception and interaction.
8. Heldner and Edlund analyze conversational dynamics, specifically pauses and overlaps, contributing to our understanding of verbal communication.
9. Ho and MacDorman revisit the Uncanny Valley theory, proposing a new framework for evaluating human-like robots.
10. Hoffman discusses the social implications of technology, particularly music companionship through robots.

These references collectively underscore the intersection of technology, psychology, and social interaction, advancing our understanding of human behavior in the context of robotics and emotion.
