The paper discusses the growing concerns regarding trust in autonomous systems, emphasizing the need for algorithmic transparency and continuous validation. It highlights several high-profile accidents involving automated vehicles, which have raised questions about system reliability, decision-making traceability, supervision, and liability in case of failures.

An overview of validation technologies for autonomous systems is presented, distinguishing between black box and white box approaches based on transparency and the degree of automation in validation techniques. Traditional testing methods are evaluated, focusing on static and dynamic validation technologies. Negative requirements, such as safety and cybersecurity, are often implied rather than explicitly stated.

The paper details specific validation methods, including fault injection, which introduces faults into a system to observe its behavior, and functionality-based testing, which categorizes a system's intelligence into sensing, decision, and action functionalities. The aim is to ensure autonomous vehicles can perform tasks similarly to humans. The paper also mentions various validation strategies, including simulation environments and intelligent validation techniques, to enhance the safety and reliability of autonomous systems.
