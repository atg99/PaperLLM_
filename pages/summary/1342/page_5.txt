The paper evaluates various validation technologies for autonomous systems, highlighting their characteristics, tool support, coverage, regression strategies, strengths, weaknesses, effectiveness, and efficiency. 

1. **FMEA and FTA**: These are static methods tailored for safety-critical systems. They utilize FMEA worksheets and component abstractions but require significant human knowledge and are labor-intensive. While well-established for safety and security, their effectiveness is dependent on the designer's foresight in anticipating system interface failures.

2. **Experiments and Empirical Test Strategies**: These methods facilitate empirical test generation for aspects like load and thermal performance, supported by tools such as Parasoft DTP. They cover a broad range of electrical systems but are also labor-intensive and rely heavily on human input, with minimal automation.

3. **Specific Quality Requirements Tests**: This dynamic approach includes penetration testing and fuzzing using dedicated tools like OWASP ZAP. While effective for ensuring known quality requirements, it often falls short in validating complete system security and safety.

4. **Brute-force Usage in Real Scenarios**: This method captures realistic scenarios through recording and replay, offering comprehensive coverage and validation. However, it suffers from high effort requirements, unclear coverage, and redundancy in test cases.

5. **Intelligent Validation**: Utilizing machine-learning frameworks, this dynamic approach automates test generation based on situational context. It enhances transparency and considers dependencies but demands significant setup effort and computational resources.

Overall, the evaluation indicates that while traditional methods are well-established, emerging technologies like intelligent validation present opportunities for improved efficiency and effectiveness in validating autonomous systems.
