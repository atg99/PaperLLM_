The paper discusses the evolution of automated systems toward fully autonomous systems, highlighting the transition from basic sensing and control to systems with high cognitive capabilities. It emphasizes that true autonomy, as exemplified by level 5 autonomous vehicles, requires intelligence comparable to or exceeding human capabilities to navigate both routine and unexpected traffic scenarios. Incidents involving self-driving cars have raised concerns about the adequacy of current validation measures, underscoring the need for new testing methods that can simulate unforeseen fatal traffic situations. 

The paper argues that testing should be integrated throughout the entire product lifecycle rather than being confined to final development stages. It contrasts automated systems, which follow predefined sequences without reflection, with autonomous systems, which can learn and adapt based on their experiences and goals. While acknowledging human limitations in processing and managing complexity, the paper points out that autonomous systems could significantly enhance safety and efficiency in various domains, particularly in automotive and aerospace applications. The potential for reducing accidents by 90% through automation is noted, positioning autonomous systems as valuable aids in future transportation and robotics.
