The introduction of transformers, particularly their multi-head attention mechanism, marks a significant advancement in the field. This method allows for the effective modeling of dependencies across time steps, which is crucial for recognizing signs that may vary in execution due to contextual factors.

In this study, we investigate the application of transformers to isolated sign language recognition, focusing on Flemish Sign Language. We utilize OpenPose for feature extraction, which provides keypoint estimation of human body movements, combined with deep learning techniques for end-to-end learning. Our results demonstrate that the transformer-based approach achieves an accuracy of 74.7% on a vocabulary of 100 signs, surpassing previous benchmarks in the field.

The implications of our findings extend beyond mere recognition; they offer a potential framework for developing suggestion systems that can assist annotators in labeling sign language corpora more efficiently. This could ultimately accelerate research and improve resources for sign language studies and applications. Our research emphasizes the importance of advancing machine learning techniques to better understand and facilitate the use of complex languages such as sign languages.
