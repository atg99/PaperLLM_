The paper discusses advancements in sign language recognition (SLR) using deep learning techniques, specifically focusing on the application of Long Short-Term Memory networks (LSTMs) and Hidden Markov Models (HMMs). Previous studies have shown that these models perform well in continuous SLR and sign language translation. However, challenges remain due to the limited size of sign language datasets, necessitating cross-domain learning for better accuracy. 

The introduction of OpenPose, an open-source human pose estimation system, has enhanced feature extraction for SLR by capturing critical body part positions. OpenPose stands out as the only full-body pose estimation technique, making it suitable for SLR, which considers both manual and non-manual components of sign language.

The paper also delves into the technical aspects of deep learning relevant to SLR. Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) are highlighted as effective tools for image and video recognition. Furthermore, the introduction of Transformer networks has revolutionized natural language processing, employing multi-head attention and feed-forward layers to process input features efficiently.

The combination of CNNs and Transformers has led to the development of Video Transformer Networks (VTNs) for action recognition in videos. Although VTNs do not currently outperform 3D CNNs in action recognition tasks, they offer advantages in computational efficiency and reduced overfitting risks, which is particularly beneficial for smaller sign language datasets.

The experimental section of the paper presents the use of the Flemish Sign Language (VGT) corpus, consisting of 140 hours of video data, to train and evaluate the proposed models. The dataset includes 100 classes corresponding to 104 glosses, with some classes representing visually similar signs that may be confused by neural networks. A total of 18,730 samples from 67 native signers are utilized for the study. The goal is to develop an annotation tool to assist in sign language corpus analysis.
