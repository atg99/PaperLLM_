This paper presents a comprehensive approach to utilizing keypoints extracted from OpenPose for sign language recognition through various neural network architectures. Each keypoint is represented as a triplet consisting of 2D coordinates and a confidence score. The authors focus on relevant keypoints for the upper body and face, discarding unnecessary lower body and facial keypoints to streamline the input data.

The preprocessing steps involve rotating poses for consistency and standardizing dimensions, alongside data augmentation techniques such as adding Gaussian noise and random rotations of hand keypoints. The models employed include PoseLSTM, Pose Transformer Network (PTN), Video Transformer Network (VTN), and a Multimodal Transformer Network (MTN).

The PoseLSTM uses LSTM architecture with specific hyperparameters and is trained for 100 epochs. PTN leverages multi-head attention with the same settings as PoseLSTM but demonstrates improved performance. The VTN utilizes a framewise CNN (ResNet-34) and applies standard preprocessing and data augmentation techniques for action recognition. The MTN combines OpenPose keypoints with learned features, utilizing a dimensionality reduction technique via spatial scaled dot-product attention to enhance feature relevance.

Evaluation metrics focus on top-n accuracy, indicating the frequency of ground truth labels appearing within the model predictions. Results show that the PTN and VTN outperform the PoseLSTM, suggesting the efficacy of transformer architectures over LSTMs for tasks beyond their initial design in machine translation. The MTN achieves the highest accuracy, indicating the benefit of multimodal approaches in sign language recognition.
