The paper presents a comprehensive study on sign language recognition (SLR) using a novel approach called the Multimodal Transformer Network (MTN). It highlights that networks trained with features from the OpenPose pose estimation technique significantly outperform previous methods. However, leveraging state-of-the-art computer vision techniques, particularly pretrained convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and transformers, allows for the extraction of more salient features than those provided by OpenPose. 

The authors emphasize the importance of combining both learned features and OpenPose keypoints through attention mechanisms to enhance network accuracy, achieving a notable 74.7% accuracy on an unseen test set with a vocabulary of 100 classes. The study also indicates that increasing the dataset size correlates with improved performance, suggesting the proposed methods could serve as tools for sign language corpus annotation, potentially accelerating the annotation process and enabling the development of larger datasets for SLR.

Future work will focus on continuous SLR and developing models capable of deeper understanding of sign language. The bibliography includes significant contributions to SLR and related fields, underscoring the interdisciplinary nature of the research.
