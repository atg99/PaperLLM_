The paper explores four methods for isolated sign language recognition (SLR), utilizing combinations of feature extractors and classifiers. The feature extractors include OpenPose and a pre-trained 2D CNN fine-tuned on sign language data. The classifiers consist of LSTM networks and deep multi-head attention networks. The methods are trained and evaluated on a consistent dataset, with hyperparameters tuned individually for optimal performance.

Data is divided into training (70%), validation (10%), and test (20%) sets, yielding 13,077 training samples, 3,743 test samples, and 1,910 validation samples. A random temporal cropping technique is applied for data augmentation, selecting 16 frames from longer samples or looping shorter ones. During inference, a sliding window approach is used to evaluate the entire clip, averaging accuracy across non-overlapping sequences.

The PoseLSTM method serves as a baseline, employing OpenPose to extract 137 keypoints per frame, which include body pose, facial keypoints, and hand pose. The use of OpenPose as a fixed feature extractor allows for a comparison of the impact of multi-head attention on the network's accuracy. All experiments are conducted using PyTorch on Nvidia GeForce GTX 1080 Ti GPUs.
