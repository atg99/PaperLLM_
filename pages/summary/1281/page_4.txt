In the paper by Hafemeister and Satija published in Genome Biology, the authors investigate the normalization of single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) data using statistical modeling techniques. They focus on the challenges posed by zero values common in droplet-based scRNA-seq and explore generalized linear models (GLMs) as a solution. The authors initially applied a negative binomial distribution for modeling the data, which is often used for overdispersed single-cell count data. 

However, they found significant heterogeneity in the parameter estimates across genes, despite the expectation that sequencing depth would have consistent effects on unique molecular identifier (UMI) counts. This variation raised concerns about potential overfitting in their regression models. To address this, the authors employed bootstrapping to assess the uncertainty in the parameter estimates. Their analysis revealed that the standard deviation of the parameter estimates was particularly high for low-abundance genes, indicating a risk of overfitting without proper regularization.

Overall, the study highlights the importance of choosing appropriate statistical frameworks for analyzing single-cell data and the necessity of incorporating regularization techniques to mitigate overfitting and enhance the reliability of the results.
