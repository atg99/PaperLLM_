The paper by Hafemeister and Satija discusses a regularized negative binomial (NB) model as a balanced approach for analyzing single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) data. The authors illustrate the model's effectiveness through a case study involving four genes. They highlight the inadequacy of the Poisson error model for the MALAT1 gene, which does not exhibit variation across cell types, as it fails to account for overdispersion. Conversely, the non-regularized NB model is shown to overfit data for genes like S100A9 and CD74, leading to a loss of biological heterogeneity. The authors also demonstrate that their normalization procedure effectively retains variation across different sequencing depths, confirming its applicability across multiple UMI datasets.

The study further emphasizes the importance of standardizing expression counts to mitigate the influence of cellular sequencing depth on downstream analytical tasks. The authors conducted dimensionality reduction and differential expression tests on Pearson residuals obtained from the regularized NB regression, comparing results with log-normalized data. They employed PCA followed by UMAP embedding to visualize the data, ensuring that normalization methods did not bias the analysis. Overall, the findings suggest that the regularized NB model can enhance the accuracy and reliability of scRNA-seq data analysis.
