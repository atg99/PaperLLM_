In the paper, Hafemeister and Satija present a modeling framework for the normalization and variance stabilization of single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) data, addressing the significant cell-to-cell variation caused by technical factors. The authors highlight that variability in molecular counts can confound biological heterogeneity, particularly due to differences in sequencing depth and other technical influences. 

To mitigate these effects, the authors propose using Pearson residuals from a regularized negative binomial regression model, where sequencing depth is treated as a covariate. This approach effectively removes technical variation while retaining biological differences. They argue that unconstrained negative binomial models may lead to overfitting, which can be avoided by pooling information across genes with similar abundances to achieve stable parameter estimates.

The proposed method eliminates the need for heuristic steps such as pseudocount addition or log-transformation and enhances common downstream analytical tasks, including variable gene selection, dimensionality reduction, and differential expression analysis. The methodology is applicable to any unique molecular identifier (UMI)-based scRNA-seq dataset and is made available through the R package sctransform, which integrates with the single-cell analysis toolkit Seurat.

The authors emphasize that effective normalization should ensure that normalized expression levels are uncorrelated with total sequencing depth and that the variance of normalized gene expression primarily reflects biological heterogeneity rather than technical artifacts.
