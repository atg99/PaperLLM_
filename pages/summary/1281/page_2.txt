In response to these challenges, the authors propose a novel statistical approach for normalizing UMI count data in single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq). They emphasize that different groups of genes cannot be normalized using the same constant factor, which is a fundamental issue for scaling-factor-based normalization schemes. The proposed method utilizes a generalized linear model (GLM) where UMI counts serve as the response variable and sequencing depth as the explanatory variable. 

The authors explore various error models for the GLM and identify that unconstrained negative binomial (NB) or zero-inflated negative binomial (ZINB) models can lead to overfitting and a reduction in biological variance. To mitigate this, they introduce a regularized negative binomial regression approach that pools information across genes with similar expression levels, yielding reproducible error models and effectively normalized data values.

The resultant normalized values retain biological variability and can be applied to downstream analyses, such as dimensionality reduction and differential expression testing, without the confounding effects of sequencing depth. The authors provide their methodology as an open-source R package, sctransform, with integration into the Seurat toolkit, making it accessible for broader use in UMI-based scRNA-seq datasets. 

Overall, the study underscores the importance of appropriate normalization techniques in scRNA-seq analysis and presents a compelling alternative to existing methods that can improve the accuracy of biological interpretations in single-cell datasets.
