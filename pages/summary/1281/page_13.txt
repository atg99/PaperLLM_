A regularized negative binomial model was developed for gene expression analysis using a subset of cells, allowing for adjustments in gene means to simulate different conditions.

The analysis involved generating count data through a systematic approach that included sampling total UMI counts and applying the model parameters to create a comprehensive count matrix. Variance contribution analysis was conducted to evaluate the role of different cell groups in overall gene variance, while density maps were created to visualize Pearson residuals for various modeling approaches.

Dimensionality reduction techniques, specifically truncated principal components analysis followed by UMAP, were applied to both log-normalized data and Pearson residuals for visualization purposes. Differential expression testing was performed using t-tests, with adjustments for multiple comparisons to identify significantly differentially expressed genes.

The paper also discussed model extensions to incorporate additional nuisance parameters, such as cell cycle state and experimental batch effects, highlighting the flexibility of the modeling approach. An example involving murine bipolar cells demonstrated the application of these model extensions in analyzing gene expression data across different experimental conditions. Overall, the study provides insights into the complexities of gene expression analysis and the importance of considering various factors influencing variance.
