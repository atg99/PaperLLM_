The paper by Hafemeister and Satija discusses the application of regularized negative binomial (NB) regression in the context of single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) data analysis. The authors illustrate how this method effectively removes variations attributed to sequencing depth while preserving biological heterogeneity among different cell types.

Key findings indicate that the distribution of residual means across genes is centered at zero, and the density of residual gene variance peaks at one, suggesting most genes do not vary significantly across cell types. The authors demonstrate that the variance of Pearson residuals is independent of gene abundance, confirming that the generalized linear model (GLM) has accurately captured the inherent mean-variance relationship in the data.

In contrast to the regularized NB model, the Poisson model fails to account for overdispersion, leading to misleading conclusions about biological variability. The unconstrained NB model overfits the data, attributing variations primarily to technical effects and resulting in low residual variance even for cell-type markers. The authors highlight specific genes, such as MALAT1 and S100A9, to illustrate these points, showing how the regularized model appropriately captures the biological variance while the unconstrained models do not.

Overall, the regularized negative binomial regression is presented as a balanced approach that accommodates overdispersion in scRNA-seq data, avoids overfitting, and maintains biological relevance in the analysis of gene expression variability across different cell types.
