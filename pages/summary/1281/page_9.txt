The study by Hafemeister and Satija examines the biological heterogeneity in peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMC) and highlights the impact of sequencing depth on data normalization techniques. The authors found that while both log-normalized data and Pearson residuals reveal major and minor immune cell subsets, the low-dimensional representation of log-normalized data was significantly influenced by sequencing depth. In contrast, Pearson residuals displayed reduced correlation with this technical metric, indicating a more reliable representation of biological variation.

The paper emphasizes the challenges posed by imperfect normalization in differential expression tests for single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq), as global normalization differences can lead to false positives for many genes. To illustrate this issue, the authors analyzed CD14+ monocytes and demonstrated that using Pearson residuals mitigated the variance explained by sequencing depth compared to log-normalization. Their findings suggest that Pearson residuals provide a robust alternative for downstream analyses, particularly in scenarios where sequencing depth varies among biologically identical cell groups. Overall, the study underscores the importance of appropriate normalization methods in accurately interpreting scRNA-seq data.
