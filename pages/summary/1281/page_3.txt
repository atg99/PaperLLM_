The study by Hafemeister and Satija examines the effects of sequencing depth on gene expression analysis using a PBMC dataset from 10X Genomics. The authors categorize genes into six groups based on their average expression and analyze the relationship between observed counts and cell sequencing depth. They find that gene variance is confounded with sequencing depth, particularly noting that cells with low total UMI counts exhibit higher variance for high-abundance genes. This imbalance dampens the variance contribution from other gene groups.

The authors also test an alternative normalization method, "relative counts" normalization, which mitigates some of the relationships between gene expression, variance, and sequencing depth, but residual effects persist. Their findings highlight the challenges associated with "size factor"-based normalization strategies, suggesting that a single normalization factor may not adequately account for the distinct effects on different gene sets based on their abundance. They propose that normalization strategies should be tailored to gene abundance levels, aligning with conclusions from previous studies on non-UMI-based single-cell RNA-seq data.
