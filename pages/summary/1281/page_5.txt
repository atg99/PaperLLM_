The study by Hafemeister and Satija presents an evaluation of the limitations of standard negative binomial (NB) regression models in analyzing single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) data, particularly highlighting the issue of overfitting. The authors found that parameter estimates were inconsistent across bootstrapped samples, especially for genes with low to moderate expression. They demonstrated that these discrepancies were exacerbated by overfitting when using a standard two-parameter NB distribution.

To address this, the authors proposed a regularization approach, which combines information from similar genes to enhance robustness and reduce sampling variation. This method contrasts with zero-inflated negative binomial models, which introduce additional parameters that can further complicate the fitting process. The study employed kernel regression to model the relationship between parameter values and average gene expression, resulting in regularized parameter estimates that maintained consistency across repeated subsamples.

The regularization procedure involves selecting a kernel bandwidth that controls the degree of smoothing. The authors showed that their method produced robust results across a range of bandwidth values while cautioning against extreme settings that could lead to over- or under-smoothing.

The proposed workflow includes fitting generalized linear models to estimate parameters based on sequencing depth, followed by kernel regression to derive regularized parameters. A second round of NB regression then constrains the model parameters to these estimates, allowing for normalization of expression levels. The resulting Pearson residuals serve as a variance-stabilizing transformation, addressing technical differences while preserving biological variation.

The authors highlighted several advantages of their approach, including the lack of assumptions regarding fixed total molecular counts, the explicit accounting for the mean-variance relationship in single-cell data, and the data-driven nature of the variance-stabilizing transformation. Their evaluation indicated minimal correlation between Pearson residuals and sequencing depth, affirming the method's effectiveness in normalizing scRNA-seq data while retaining essential biological information.
