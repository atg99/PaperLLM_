The paper introduces AutoGAN, a novel approach that integrates neural architecture search (NAS) into generative adversarial networks (GANs). This marks a pioneering effort in applying NAS techniques to the GAN domain, addressing unique challenges such as the instability of GAN training and the difficulty in selecting appropriate metrics for evaluation. The authors define a search space for generator architectures and employ an RNN controller to guide the search process, utilizing parameter sharing and a dynamic-resetting strategy to enhance training efficiency. The Inception score is used as a reward metric during the search.

Experiments demonstrate that AutoGAN can discover architectures that outperform existing state-of-the-art hand-crafted GANs, achieving notable FID scores of 12.42 on CIFAR-10 and 31.01 on STL-10. The authors highlight the importance of architecture design in improving GAN performance, arguing that more sophisticated network designs can lead to better image generation outcomes. The paper concludes with a discussion on the limitations of AutoGAN and its potential for future development. The code for AutoGAN is made publicly available for further research.
