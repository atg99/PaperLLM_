The paper presents AutoGAN, a novel approach that utilizes multi-level architecture search (MLAS) to optimize the design of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs). The motivation for this approach stems from the benefits seen in progressive GAN training. AutoGAN employs a bottom-up sequential search strategy and beam search to effectively identify architectures that outperform or match existing hand-crafted GANs.

Experimental results highlight the success of AutoGAN, achieving an Inception score of 8.55 and a Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) score of 12.42 on the CIFAR-10 dataset. Additionally, the architecture demonstrates strong transferability, performing competitively on the STL-10 dataset with an Inception score of 9.16 and an FID score of 31.01. These results establish new state-of-the-art FID scores for both datasets and provide insights into GAN generator design.

The paper also discusses the context of neural architecture search (NAS), which aims to find optimal neural network architectures. While NAS has been successfully applied to image classification and other tasks, this work is pioneering in applying NAS to generative models. Key components of NAS include the search space, optimization algorithms, and proxy tasks for efficiently evaluating architectures.

In terms of the technical approach, AutoGAN addresses the challenge of balancing the generator and discriminator networks. The authors propose a strategy where only the generator's architecture is searched using a recurrent neural network controller, while the discriminator is grown deeper following a predefined routine. This method aims to mitigate the instability commonly associated with GAN training.

Overall, AutoGAN represents a significant advancement in the field of GANs, combining architecture search techniques with a focus on generator design to achieve superior performance in image generation tasks.
