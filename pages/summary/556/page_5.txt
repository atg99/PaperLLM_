This paper presents AutoGAN, an automated approach to discovering architectures for Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) specifically for unconditional image generation on the CIFAR-10 dataset. The training setup includes a discriminator with a batch size of 64 and a generator with a batch size of 128, employing spectral normalization only on the discriminator. The controller is trained using the Adam optimizer with a learning rate of 3.5e−4, and the entropy of the controller’s output is included in the reward to promote exploration.

The architecture discovered by AutoGAN comprises three pre-activation convolution blocks and favors nearest neighbor and bilinear upsampling over deconvolution to avoid artifacts. Notably, it does not utilize any normalization and prefers denser skipping connections for effective multi-scale feature fusion.

When compared to hand-crafted GANs, AutoGAN demonstrates competitive performance, achieving results close to Progressive GAN in inception score and outperforming all state-of-the-art models in Fréchet Inception Distance (FID). The current search space of AutoGAN limits it to models like SN-GAN, yet it achieves superior results, highlighting the significance of generator structure and the efficacy of the search algorithm.

The discovered architecture is efficient, with 2.16G FLOPs, outperforming SN-GAN (1.69G FLOPs) and being comparable to Progressive GAN (6.39G FLOPs). The paper also provides performance metrics for the top three discovered architectures, all of which exhibit competitive results against existing state-of-the-art models.
