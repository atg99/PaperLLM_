The paper discusses the evaluation of AutoGAN's performance on the STL-10 dataset, emphasizing a designed proxy task that allows for the training of derived architectures from scratch over 30 epochs. The correlation between the evaluation metrics from the proxy task and the true evaluation using Inception Score (IS) is plotted, revealing a positive correlation (Spearman's rank correlation coefficient of 0.779), although the proxy task tends to underestimate the IS due to incomplete training.

Additionally, the paper compares the use of IS and the reciprocal of the Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) as rewards for AutoGAN. Both metrics exhibit comparable performance during training, but the computation of FID is more resource-intensive, leading to the conclusion that IS is the preferred reward metric.

To address the instability in GAN training and the potential for mode collapse, the authors introduce a parameter dynamic-resetting strategy. Experiments show that this method, alongside parameter sharing, leads to comparable Inception Scores while significantly reducing training time from 72 hours to 43 hours.

Finally, the paper highlights the effectiveness of a multi-level architecture search (MLAS) strategy in AutoGAN, contrasting it with a single-level architecture search (SLAS) to evaluate the improvement in performance.
