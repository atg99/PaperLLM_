The paper introduces the AutoGAN framework, which utilizes a recurrent neural network (RNN) controller to automate the architecture search for GANs. The architecture consists of multiple cells, each capable of employing various operations such as convolution blocks, normalization types, and upsampling methods. The RNN controller outputs a hidden vector at each time step, which is decoded into a specific operation. For each cell, the controller samples multiple candidate architectures and selects the top K, which are then used as inputs for the subsequent cell.

The search space for the generator cells includes operations like skip connections, convolution blocks with or without normalization, and different upsampling techniques. The evaluation metrics for the generated models are based on Inception Score (IS) and Fréchet Inception Distance (FID), with IS being favored due to its efficiency in calculation.

To enhance the efficiency of the neural architecture search (NAS), the paper incorporates a parameter-sharing strategy along with a dynamic-resetting mechanism. This approach addresses the instability often encountered in GAN training, particularly during mode collapse, by monitoring the variance of training loss. If the loss variance falls below a set threshold, the training of the current GAN is terminated, and the model parameters are re-initialized while retaining the RNN controller's parameters for continuity in the search process.

The optimization method involves alternating training phases for the RNN controller and the shared GAN parameters, ensuring that the architecture search is both efficient and effective. Overall, AutoGAN represents a significant advancement in automating the design of GAN architectures through reinforcement learning and dynamic parameter management.
