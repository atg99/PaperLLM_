The paper presents a shared GAN training process that utilizes a recurrent neural network (RNN) controller for architecture search. Initially, a candidate architecture is sampled by the RNN, and training is terminated when the standard deviation of the training loss falls below a specified threshold, activating a dynamic resetting flag (FDR). The training of the shared GAN continues without re-initialization until the current epoch's controller training is complete.

In the second phase, the controller samples child models of the shared generator and calculates their Inception scores (IS) as rewards. The RNN controller is updated using reinforcement learning with a moving average baseline, and after a set number of iterations, the top architectures are selected for further exploration. A new controller is initialized for the next stage of architecture searching.

The training of the shared GAN involves fixing the RNN controller's policy while updating shared parameters through standard GAN training with hinge adversarial loss. Multi-level architecture search (MLAS) is introduced, allowing for progressive growth of the generator and discriminator through a bottom-up approach using beam search.

During the controller training phase, the policy parameters are updated while the shared parameters remain fixed. The reward is defined as the IS of the sampled models, and the RNN controller is optimized using the Adam optimizer with an entropy term to promote exploration. An LSTM model is employed to generate operations for each GAN cell, with a new controller initialized as needed for adding cells.

Architecture derivation involves sampling multiple generator architectures from the learned policy, calculating their rewards, and selecting the top models for further training and evaluation based on their final Inception scores.

The experiments utilize the CIFAR-10 dataset for training, comprising 50,000 training images, and the STL-10 dataset to demonstrate the transferability of the discovered architectures. The training settings align with those of spectral normalization GANs, employing a learning rate of 2e-4 and hinge loss with the Adam optimizer.
