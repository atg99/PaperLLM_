The paper presents AdapterFusion, a novel two-stage learning algorithm designed to improve transfer learning by effectively incorporating knowledge from multiple tasks while avoiding common pitfalls such as catastrophic forgetting and dataset balancing issues. The first stage, knowledge extraction, involves learning task-specific parameters known as adapters, which encapsulate information pertinent to each task. The second stage, knowledge composition, combines these adapters, allowing the classifier to leverage the representations learned from various tasks in a non-destructive manner.

The authors empirically evaluate AdapterFusion across 16 diverse natural language understanding (NLU) tasks, demonstrating its superiority over traditional methods like full fine-tuning and multi-task learning. The architecture integrates into a transformer model, facilitating the mixing of encoded information from multiple adapters. Overall, AdapterFusion offers a more effective strategy for transferring knowledge across different tasks by separating the extraction and composition processes, thus enhancing the model's ability to maintain previously acquired knowledge while learning new tasks. The code and adapters used in the study are publicly available at AdapterHub.ml.
