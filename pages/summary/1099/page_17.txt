The paper presents results on the performance of various models initialized with BERT-base and RoBERTa-base weights across multiple datasets, focusing on different architectural setups and training methods. The results are summarized in tables that detail mean scores and standard deviations for each dataset and model configuration, with datasets categorized by training size.

For BERT-based models (Table 3), the performance varies across datasets, with the MultiNLI dataset achieving an accuracy of 84.60% using ST-A 2, while the lowest performance is noted in HellaSwag at 39.45%. The mean performance across all datasets indicates a slight decline from ST-A 2 to ST-A 64, suggesting diminishing returns with increased reduction factors.

In the RoBERTa-based models (Table 4), a similar trend is observed. The highest performance is noted in the IMDB dataset with an accuracy of 96.00% for the Full training setup, while HellaSwag again shows lower performance at 63.53%. The mean performance indicates that training only a classification head (Head) results in lower accuracy compared to full training or Single-Task adapters.

Overall, the results highlight the effectiveness of different training strategies and the impact of model architecture on performance across a variety of natural language processing tasks, with Single-Task adapters showing competitive results compared to full fine-tuning. The paper provides insights into the trade-offs between model complexity and performance, emphasizing the need for careful consideration of architecture in NLP tasks.
