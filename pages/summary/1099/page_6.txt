The paper presents a comprehensive analysis of various adapter training techniques for improving performance across multiple natural language processing tasks. The results are summarized in Table 1, which includes mean accuracy and standard deviation for 16 datasets under different architectural setups, such as training only a classification head, full training of BERT weights, and the use of Single-Task and Multi-Task Adapters.

Key findings include that training solely the classification head significantly underperforms compared to fine-tuning all weights, with an average accuracy drop of 10 points. The implementation of Single-Task Adapters (ST-A) shows a slight performance improvement of 0.66% over full model training, indicating their regularization effect on some datasets. However, Multi-Task Adapters (MT-A) demonstrate performance drops on specific datasets, suggesting that shared representations do not universally enhance task outcomes.

The paper also discusses AdapterFusion, which seeks to transfer task-specific knowledge from multiple adapters to improve performance on a target task. It is noted that while larger datasets perform adequately without fusion, smaller datasets benefit more significantly from this approach. Overall, the findings emphasize the importance of tailored adaptation strategies to enhance model performance across diverse tasks.
