This paper discusses the integration of adapters within the transformer architecture for natural language processing tasks. Adapters, introduced by Houlsby et al., serve as task-specific parameters that are added to each transformer layer to capture unique knowledge from training data. The paper highlights various adapter placements, with a preference for a two-layer feed-forward network positioned after both the multi-head attention and the feed-forward layers.

Adapters can be trained in single-task (ST-A) or multi-task (MT-A) settings, allowing for efficient storage of task-specific information. However, the distinct weights of these adapters hinder the ability to share information across tasks. To address this limitation, the authors propose a two-stage algorithm called AdapterFusion.

In the first stage, they train adapters for each task individually. The second stage involves combining these adapters using AdapterFusion, where new parameters are introduced to learn how to effectively integrate the knowledge from multiple adapters for a specific target task. This approach aims to prevent catastrophic forgetting and task interference, while stabilizing training.

The AdapterFusion framework employs Key, Value, and Query matrices at each transformer layer, drawing parallels to attention mechanisms. The proposed method allows for dynamic composition of adapter information, enhancing the model's ability to leverage multi-task knowledge without losing the benefits of task-specific training. Overall, AdapterFusion represents a significant advancement in efficiently utilizing adapters in multi-task learning scenarios.
