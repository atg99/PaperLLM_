This compilation of works primarily explores advancements in multi-task learning and transfer learning within deep neural networks, particularly in the context of natural language processing (NLP). 

Ruder's 2017 overview highlights the significance of multi-task learning in enhancing model performance by leveraging shared representations across related tasks. His 2019 Ph.D. thesis further elaborates on neural transfer learning, emphasizing its role in adapting pre-trained models to NLP tasks.

The 2019 paper by Ruder et al. introduces latent multi-task architecture learning, demonstrating how hierarchical structures can effectively manage multiple tasks. Similarly, Sanh, Wolf, and Ruder propose a hierarchical multi-task approach for embedding learning, showcasing the benefits of structured task management.

Sakaguchi et al. (2020) present Winogrande, an expansive adversarial challenge aimed at evaluating commonsense reasoning. The Social IQA by Sap et al. (2019) focuses on social interaction reasoning, while Talmor et al. (2019) introduce CommonsenseQA, a question-answering challenge centered on commonsense knowledge.

The foundational work by Socher et al. (2013) on recursive deep models highlights semantic compositionality, integral to understanding complex language constructs. Speer et al. (2017) contribute with ConceptNet 5.5, a resource for multilingual general knowledge.

Recent advancements include Stickland and Murray's (2019) exploration of projected attention layers for efficient adaptation in multi-task learning and the introduction of language adaptation techniques by Ustun et al. (2020) for Universal Dependency parsing.

The seminal paper by Vaswani et al. (2017) introduces the transformer architecture, revolutionizing NLP through the attention mechanism. Wang et al. (2018) present GLUE, a benchmark for evaluating multi-task performance, while Vidoni et al. (2020) and Wang et al. (2020) discuss zero-shot cross-lingual transfer and knowledge infusion into pre-trained models, respectively.

Overall, these works collectively advance the understanding and implementation of multi-task and transfer learning, laying a foundation for future research and applications in NLP.
