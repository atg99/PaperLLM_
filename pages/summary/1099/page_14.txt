The paper discusses the architecture and performance of adapters in the context of various NLP tasks, specifically SST-2, Argument, and CSQA datasets. An exhaustive search of adapter components reveals that a single adapter positioned after the feed-forward layer yields the best results across tasks, aligning with findings from Bapna and Firat (2019) while diverging from those of Houlsby et al. (2019). This optimal configuration requires fewer adapters, enhancing efficiency in operations.

Subsequent experiments utilize this best-performing architecture with a learning rate of 1e-4. The paper also explores AdapterFusion activations across layers for BERT-Base and ST-A, presenting various reduction factors that impact adapter dimensions. Furthermore, the findings are validated using RoBERTa-base weights. Results indicate that datasets with limited data benefit significantly from AdapterFusion, although RoBERTa-base does not perform well with high-capacity adapters when using a reduction factor of 2. Overall, the study emphasizes the importance of adapter placement and configuration in improving model efficiency and performance across NLP tasks.
