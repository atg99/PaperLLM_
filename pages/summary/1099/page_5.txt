The paper discusses AdapterFusion, a method designed to enhance the performance of transfer learning by combining various trained adapters. The process involves learning a parameterized mixer that identifies and activates the most relevant adapter based on the input data. The effectiveness of AdapterFusion is evaluated through experiments across 16 diverse datasets, focusing on accuracy as the primary metric.

In the experimental setup, comparisons are made between AdapterFusion using single-task adapters (ST-A) and multi-task adapters (MT-A) to assess the model's ability to mitigate issues like catastrophic forgetting and task interference. The BERT-base-uncased model is utilized for training, with specific configurations including reduction factors and learning rates outlined. The experiments also explore the versatility of AdapterFusion, demonstrating its benefits even when base adapters are trained jointly.

The tasks included in the evaluation cover commonsense reasoning, sentiment analysis, natural language inference, sentence relatedness, argument mining, and reading comprehension, with detailed descriptions provided in the appendix. Results indicate the effectiveness of AdapterFusion across all datasets, showcasing its potential to improve performance in transfer learning scenarios. Comparisons with existing architectures further validate the advantages of the proposed method.
