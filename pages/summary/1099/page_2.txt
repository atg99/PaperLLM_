The paper addresses the limitations of traditional multi-task learning methods, which require simultaneous access to all tasks during training and often lead to imbalances in task performance. Specifically, these methods can overfit low-resource tasks while underfitting high-resource ones, hindering effective knowledge transfer across tasks. 

To overcome these challenges, the authors introduce a novel approach called AdapterFusion. This method employs a two-stage learning algorithm that allows for the effective sharing of knowledge across multiple tasks while mitigating issues like catastrophic forgetting and task balancing. AdapterFusion consists of two components: an adapter for each task that does not alter the underlying pretrained language model, and a Fusion layer that integrates representations from various task adapters to enhance performance on a target task.

The key contributions of this work include the introduction of the AdapterFusion strategy, empirical evaluations across 16 diverse natural language understanding tasks, and comparisons with existing methods, demonstrating that AdapterFusion outperforms both multi-task approaches and fully fine-tuned models on single tasks. The findings indicate that the proposed method effectively leverages knowledge from multiple tasks, leading to improved performance on the target task.

Additionally, the authors provide a formalization of transfer learning, highlight its challenges, and outline the role of adapters in this context. The paper ultimately aims to enhance the applicability of multi-task learning in various scenarios by presenting a more efficient training strategy for models.
