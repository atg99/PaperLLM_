This paper discusses various image manipulation techniques and advanced data augmentation methods in deep learning. 

Table 1 outlines basic image manipulations, including flipping, rotation, scaling, noise injection, color space adjustment, contrast modification, sharpening, translation, and cropping, each with a concise description of its function.

The paper also reviews several data augmentation techniques. Mixup proposed by Yun et al. generates new training samples by mixing images, while CutMix enhances this by replacing removed regions with patches from other images, resulting in more natural outcomes. Fmix, introduced by Harris et al., employs random binary masks to improve performance over previous methods. AugMix, developed by Hendrycks et al., combines multiple augmentation operations into chains, mixing results from different pipelines. ManifoldMix by Verma et al. focuses on improving neural network representations by mixing hidden representations at various layers.

The section on advanced approaches highlights Auto Augment, a method that automates the search for optimal data augmentation policies, improving performance beyond manual designs. The AutoAugment framework consists of a search algorithm and a search space which outlines various augmentation operations. Challenges include the time cost associated with reinforcement learning for policy selection. Fast AutoAugment by Lim et al. addresses this by using a more efficient search strategy. Population Based Augmentation (PBA) by Ho et al. generates nonstationary schedules to reduce computational time while matching AutoAugment’s performance.

RandAugment, proposed by Cubuk et al., simplifies the search space and enhances the performance of previous techniques. However, it may introduce noisy examples, leading to negative effects on inference. To address this, Gong et al. introduced KeepAugment, which preserves important regions identified by saliency maps during augmentation. Tian et al. proposed an Augmentation-wise Weight Sharing strategy to improve efficiency in large-scale datasets. Lastly, Lin et al. formulated the augmentation policy as a parameterized probability distribution, optimizing it alongside network parameters in a method called OHL-Auto-Aug. 

Overall, the paper emphasizes the evolution and significance of data augmentation techniques in enhancing deep learning performance.
