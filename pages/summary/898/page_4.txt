The paper discusses two main techniques for data augmentation: feature augmentation and deep generative models. 

In feature augmentation, transformations are applied within a learned feature space rather than the input space. This approach is based on the premise that realistic samples are more likely to be found in feature space. Various methods such as noise addition, nearest neighbor interpolation, and extrapolation are explored. Notably, the FeatMatch method is highlighted for its ability to refine and augment data using both within-class and across-class information. Additionally, the Moment Exchange method encourages models to leverage moment information from latent features by exchanging them between training images.

Deep generative models aim to generate samples that reflect the original data distribution. Generative adversarial networks (GANs) are prominently featured for their effectiveness in data augmentation. The paper details several GAN-based techniques, including Pix2Pix, which requires paired data, and CycleGAN, which operates without it. StarGAN improves scalability by allowing image-to-image translation across multiple domains with a single model, while StarGAN v2 enhances diversity in generated images by distinguishing between domain and style.

The paper also evaluates the effectiveness of data augmentation across three computer vision tasks: semantic segmentation, image classification, and object detection, using well-established public datasets. In the semantic segmentation experiments conducted on the PASCAL VOC dataset, various image manipulation techniques were applied, such as flipping, scaling, and noise injection. The results indicate that data augmentation consistently improves performance, as reflected in the Intersection over Union (IoU) metric across different models, with observed improvements ranging from 0.53% to 2.61%. 

Overall, the findings underscore the significance of both feature augmentation and deep generative models in enhancing data representation and improving model performance in computer vision tasks.
