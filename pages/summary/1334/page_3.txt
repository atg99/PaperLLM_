This paper explores the evolving governance of robotics and artificial intelligence (AI) within the European Union (EU), highlighting the intersection of policy-making, ethical considerations, and economic impacts. It underscores the EU's strategic focus on robotics while recognizing the increasing use of the term "AI" in its policies.

The paper emphasizes that interest in robotics governance is longstanding, with regulatory frameworks established over decades. Notable initiatives include the founding of the European Robotics Research Network (EURON) in 2000 to promote research and technology transfer, and the European Robotics Platform (EUROP) in 2005 aimed at enhancing the EU's competitive edge in robotics. The paper discusses the economic implications of robotics, referencing foresight studies that assess the potential effects of automation on employment and economic growth, while also addressing concerns about the socioeconomic impact of industrial robots.

Furthermore, the paper highlights the recent rise in attention to ethical issues associated with robotics and AI in EU policy-making, particularly post-2015. Key reports from EU bodies, such as the European Parliament's Panel for the Future of Science and Technology, have identified significant ethical concerns including privacy, liability, and safety related to autonomous systems. The 2017 European Parliament resolution on civil law rules for robotics marks a pivotal moment in recognizing the complexities of artificial agency and its implications for governance.

Overall, the paper illustrates how the EU is navigating the challenges and opportunities presented by robotics and AI, shaping a narrative that balances the potential for economic growth with the need for ethical oversight and regulation.
