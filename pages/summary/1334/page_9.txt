The paper discusses the integration of ethical considerations in Human-Robot Interaction (HRI) research, emphasizing the importance of anticipating the consequences of robot development and implementation. It highlights ongoing discussions within the field regarding key ethical issues and the establishment of ethical codes and guidelines. Contrary to the perception that ethics may hinder innovation, the authors argue that a focus on ethics can foster socially sustainable innovations in the long run. This is particularly relevant in enhancing user acceptance of social robots through principles like non-maleficence and social justice.

The article further explores the role of ethical governance in interactive robots, suggesting that HRI should be viewed as a form of sociotechnical innovation. The authors advocate for translating high-level ethical principles into actionable rules and standards that can be tested and refined through robotics research. Additionally, the paper addresses the need for moral competence in artificial agents, suggesting that formalizing ethical reasoning in automated decision-making systems is essential for responsible AI development.

The paper also underscores the significance of ethical assessments in HRI, which align with EU governance plans aimed at promoting autonomous systems that meet societal needs while mitigating ethical risks. The institutionalization of ethical assessments, through expert committees, is presented as a vital mechanism for ensuring that ethical principles are effectively integrated into research and development processes. Overall, the authors call for further development of ethical narratives in HRI to enhance the field's ability to address emerging ethical challenges.
