The paper discusses the European Union's (EU) approach to the governance of robotics and artificial intelligence (AI), emphasizing the integration of ethical considerations into policy-making. For nearly a decade, the EU has prioritized anticipatory governance, focusing on building trust in autonomous systems and promoting a human-centered development trajectory. The paper critically examines how the ethics narrative has emerged within EU policy frameworks, highlighting its role in defining policy issues and ideas related to the societal impacts of autonomous systems.

As autonomous systems become more interactive, there are mixed expectations regarding their effects, leading to concerns about potential social disruptions. To address these issues, ethical approaches are increasingly recognized as essential in the development of policies aimed at mitigating societal risks. The paper outlines the ambiguities surrounding the future impact of these technologies and the resultant uncertainties in effective policy implementation.

To enhance the integration of ethical considerations in robotics and AI governance, the paper identifies four complementary pathways within human-robot interaction (HRI) research: improving ethical assessments, investigating the moral competence of artificial agents, engaging in value-based design and implementation, and participating in discussions about ethical sociotechnical systems. These pathways aim to provide clearer strategies for embedding ethics in the evolving landscape of robotics and AI governance.
