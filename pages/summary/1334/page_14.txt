The references listed encompass a broad spectrum of topics related to ethics, design, and the societal implications of robotics and artificial intelligence. Key themes include the moral considerations of human-robot interactions, responsible AI design, and the integration of ethical frameworks in robotic systems. Malle et al. (2015) explore differing moral norms applied to human versus robot agents, while Coeckelbergh (2013, 2015) discusses the vulnerabilities introduced by automation. Seibt et al. (2020) advocate for a value-driven, transdisciplinary approach in social robotics.

The importance of participatory design in robotics is highlighted by Darriba Frederiks et al. (2019) and Winkle et al. (2021), emphasizing user engagement in the design process. The concept of robot transparency and its impact on user trust is examined by Wortham et al. (2017). Ethical standards within robotics are further scrutinized by authors like Howard and Borenstein (2018), who address bias and social inequality in AI systems.

The references also underscore a shift towards a more comprehensive understanding of ethics in robotics, moving beyond traditional human-robot interaction to a broader human-robot-system interaction framework (van Wynsberghe & Li, 2019). This collection reflects ongoing discussions in the field about the moral responsibilities of robotic systems and the necessity of embedding ethical considerations in their development and deployment.
