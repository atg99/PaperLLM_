The paper discusses the evolving role of ethics in the governance of robotics and artificial intelligence (AI) within the European Union (EU). It highlights how ethical considerations have become integral to policy-making, reflecting a serious commitment from policymakers to address the potential impacts of autonomous systems. This shift towards ethical governance is seen as a promising development, potentially fostering a more inclusive and equitable approach to AI and robotics.

However, the paper also critiques the ethics narrative, emphasizing the need for a critical examination of its implications and the effectiveness of its implementation. While ethical perspectives are now a consensus in EU policy, there are concerns about their abstract nature and the risk of becoming mere platitudes if not rigorously debated. Critics argue that the focus on ethics may hinder broader public discourse on technological impacts by confining discussions to specialized expertise.

The paper points out that the establishment of ethical frameworks is crucial for regulating autonomous systems, linking EU values and rights to risk assessments. Legal experts and EU committees are currently evaluating these developments, which are significant in the global context of autonomous system regulation. The emphasis on ethics has also brought human rights considerations to the forefront in the design of interactive robots, especially following UNESCO's adoption of the 'Recommendation on the Ethics of Artificial Intelligence.'

In conclusion, the ethics narrative plays a central role in shaping EU policy on robotics and AI, framing autonomous systems as a key concern. The potential autonomy of these systems is increasingly recognized as a policy issue, with the AI Act incorporating ethical reasoning into regulatory frameworks, particularly through risk identification. Ethics is becoming a component of soft governance, influencing the development and implementation of autonomous technologies.
