The paper discusses the significant impact of artificial intelligence (AI) on society and emphasizes the necessity for European AI to be rooted in values such as human dignity and privacy protection. It references the EU Agency for Fundamental Rights report, which addresses the human rights implications of automated decision-making.

The paper outlines the coordinated plan communicated in April 2021, titled "Fostering a European approach to Artificial Intelligence," which highlights the importance of trustworthy technology in policy development. The ethical guidelines from the AI High-Level Expert Group (AI HLEG) serve as a cornerstone for this approach, aiming to position the EU as a global leader in ethical AI.

Additionally, the European Commission proposed the "AI Act," detailing specific regulations for the development and use of AI systems. This act aims for a harmonized approach across EU member states, emphasizing trust and ethical considerations. A risk-based framework is suggested, categorizing AI technologies into different risk levels, which aids in enforcing ethical reasoning.

The paper traces the evolution of the EU's strategy on AI and robotics, starting from the "Coordinated Plan on Artificial Intelligence" in 2018, which aimed for deeper collaboration among member states. It also highlights the importance of integrating ethics into the design and implementation of AI technologies, advocating for ethics to become a fundamental aspect of engineering processes.

Ursula von der Leyen's Commission continues the work of previous administrations, focusing on establishing legislation that addresses the human and ethical implications of AI. The white paper "On Artificial Intelligence - A European approach to excellence and trust" underscores the connection between trust-building and European values, reinforcing the commitment to a socially responsible AI framework.
