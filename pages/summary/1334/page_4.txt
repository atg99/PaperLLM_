The European Commission, under the Juncker Commission, recognized robotics and artificial intelligence (AI) as critical policy areas necessitating dedicated strategies for research and development. This focus emerged alongside a growing emphasis on trust-building and ethical considerations in the governance of these technologies. The establishment of the High-Level Expert Group on Artificial Intelligence (AI HLEG) in June 2018 was a pivotal step in formulating a coherent EU strategy on robotics and AI. Comprising 52 experts, the group's mission included addressing societal issues related to autonomous systems.

One of the group’s significant outputs was the 2019 'Ethics Guidelines for Trustworthy AI,' which articulated four foundational ethical principles: respect for human autonomy, prevention of harm, fairness, and explicability. It also identified seven key requirements for trustworthy AI, emphasizing the role of regulation and standardization in fostering public trust. These included human oversight, technical robustness, privacy governance, and accountability, among others.

The European Parliament's resolution underscored the necessity of ethical frameworks in the development and application of AI-powered robots, addressing concerns like human safety, privacy, and accessibility. The resolution proposed a controversial notion of granting 'electronic personality' to sophisticated autonomous robots, suggesting a legal status that would hold them accountable for their actions. This idea sparked significant debate among experts, who argued it misrepresented the current state of technology and raised ethical and legal concerns.

Overall, the European Parliament's resolution highlighted the growing recognition of ethical implications in robotics and AI, framing these technologies as critical policy issues that demand careful consideration of their societal impacts and legal ramifications.
