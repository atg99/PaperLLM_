The paper discusses the ethical implications of human-robot interaction (HRI) and the governance of autonomous systems, emphasizing the need for interdisciplinary approaches to enhance ethical discourse. It argues that the term "ethics" should not become dogmatic but should evolve through critical inquiries into the roles of social robots and autonomous systems. 

The author contrasts the governance context with the robotics and HRI research context, highlighting their differing epistemologies. Traditional policy-making often separates these contexts, but emerging governance frameworks advocate for integrating scientific and technical expertise into policy development. This integration allows for a more proactive approach to ethical considerations in technology, particularly through what is termed "midstream" governance.

The paper points out that as autonomous systems become increasingly interactive, they raise significant concerns that influence current EU policies on robotics and AI. Ethics is positioned as a vital tool for anticipating and addressing potential issues, thereby fostering public trust in these technologies. The role of ethics extends beyond merely addressing impacts; it also aids in shaping public narratives around emerging technologies, which can ultimately enhance societal acceptance and trust.

Finally, the paper emphasizes the importance of collaboration among various stakeholders—industry, academia, and NGOs—to strengthen the ethical narrative in AI and robotics. The conclusion suggests that by connecting governance and HRI contexts, a more robust and effective ethics narrative can be developed, which is crucial for the responsible advancement of autonomous systems.
