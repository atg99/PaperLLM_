The paper discusses the role of ethical considerations in the governance of emerging technologies, particularly robotics and artificial intelligence (AI). It emphasizes the importance of understanding these technologies as rapidly developing fields that significantly impact society and the economy, thus necessitating ethical frameworks in public policy. The historical context reveals that ethical approaches in technology governance have been present since the 1970s in the US and 1980s in the EU, with applied ethics emerging in various sectors such as nanotechnology, data technology, and biomedical technology.

The paper argues that the integration of ethics into technology governance is driven by the potential high stakes and uncertainties associated with these innovations, which can foster both aspirations for economic growth and contested visions of the future. Robotics and AI are portrayed as transformative technologies, yet their commercialization raises concerns about "ethics washing," where ethical rhetoric is used to justify harmful developments.

Moreover, the paper highlights challenges in integrating ethics into technological development, including the tension between ethical and economic imperatives and the potential hindrance to innovation in competitive landscapes. It references Luciano Floridi's perspective on the EU’s unique, human-centered ethical approach, which, while noble, presents difficulties in practical implementation.

Ultimately, the paper critiques the current top-down ethics narrative, noting that it is often based on abstract principles rather than actionable frameworks. It calls for a clearer pathway to incorporate these ethical considerations into practical fields like Human-Robot Interaction (HRI), suggesting that HRI could be pivotal in realizing tangible ethical applications in robotics and AI development.
