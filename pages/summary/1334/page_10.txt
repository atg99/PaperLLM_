The paper discusses the integration of ethics into robotics through value-based design and implementation, emphasizing the importance of embedding ethical considerations within the research and development processes of social robotics. This approach, gaining traction particularly in the EU under the concept of ethics-by-design, advocates for deliberation on societal values to ensure that ethical issues are central to robotics innovation.

Key aspects include the analysis of values and the establishment of new norms during the design process, which can lead to more open, responsible, and inclusive innovation. Concepts such as Value-Sensitive Design and Participatory Design are highlighted as effective methods for integrating ethical principles. Standardization is also discussed as a means to facilitate societal engagement with robotics.

From a governance perspective, the paper stresses the need to modify research and innovation systems to promote responsible and ethical innovation, recognizing design as a form of ethical inquiry. The discussion references the trolley problem as a classic ethical dilemma that illustrates the necessity for autonomous systems, like robots, to make ethical decisions.

The paper emphasizes the potential for robots to be considered morally competent agents, capable of exhibiting ethical behavior. Research in human-robot interaction (HRI) aims to formalize ethical norms within robotic systems to enhance their trustworthiness and societal acceptance. It also highlights the importance of understanding how humans perceive and judge the moral behavior of robots, contributing to a framework for meaningful ethical governance in the development of autonomous technologies.
