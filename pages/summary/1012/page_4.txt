The IID assumption posits that training and test samples are independent and identically distributed. However, in practice, data may come from different sources, leading to performance degradation when there is a mismatch between training and test environments. The clean and big data assumption suggests that well-labeled, large datasets are necessary for effective supervised learning. In reality, collecting such comprehensive data is challenging, particularly when dealing with noisy or small datasets.

Robustness is crucial when these assumptions are violated. The paper categorizes existing research on robust pattern recognition into three types based on these assumptions and identifies twelve key issues within this taxonomy. It emphasizes the need for robust systems that maintain performance despite variations in data conditions.

The paper also critiques the closed-world assumption, which underlies many pattern recognition methods. It argues that these methods often overestimate their confidence in classifying data, particularly when encountering outliers or unknown categories. To address these limitations, the authors advocate for strategies that recognize and adapt to open and unseen data spaces, thereby enhancing the robustness of pattern recognition systems.

Overall, this paper serves as a comprehensive survey of recent advancements in robust pattern recognition, providing insights into current challenges and future research directions while emphasizing the importance of breaking traditional assumptions to improve system reliability.
