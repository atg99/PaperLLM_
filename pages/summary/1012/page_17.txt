The paper discusses advancements in semi-supervised learning (SSL), a method that leverages both labeled and unlabeled data. It highlights various approaches that have emerged, particularly within the realm of deep learning. Key strategies include:

1. **Reconstruction-based Methods**: These methods combine supervised and unsupervised losses to create a new objective function. Examples include ladder networks and stacked auto-encoders that integrate denoising and reconstruction losses.

2. **Self/co/tri-training**: This approach involves using an initial classifier to generate pseudo labels for unlabeled data, followed by iterative retraining to enhance label accuracy and classifier performance. Techniques like self-training, co-training, and tri-training are discussed, emphasizing their efficiency and effectiveness.

3. **Generative Models**: Generative models, such as Gaussian mixtures and variational auto-encoders, are employed to maximize the joint likelihood of labeled and unlabeled data. Generative adversarial networks (GANs) are also explored, with modifications to the discriminator to handle multiple classes and improve classification through synthetic sample generation.

4. **Perturbation-based Methods**: These methods utilize randomness to enhance model generalization by minimizing prediction inconsistencies from multiple passes through the network. Techniques such as the mean teacher and temporal ensembling are introduced to stabilize predictions and improve learning.

5. **Global Consistency**: The paper emphasizes the importance of global consistency, where samples with an underlying structure should yield similar predictions. This is achieved through graph-based SSL combined with deep learning to model data manifolds dynamically.

Overall, the paper illustrates the evolution of SSL methods and the innovative strategies that enhance their effectiveness, particularly through deep learning frameworks.
