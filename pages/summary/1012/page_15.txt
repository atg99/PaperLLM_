The paper discusses various methods for addressing label noise in supervised machine learning, particularly focusing on recent developments post-2014. The authors categorize approaches into several key strategies:

1. **Robust Loss**: Traditional unbounded loss functions can disproportionately emphasize noisy data, leading to suboptimal decision boundaries. Solutions involve redefining loss functions to be bounded, often resulting in non-convex functions like ramp loss and truncated hinge loss. These functions help maintain robustness in models like SVMs and deep neural networks, which are sensitive to label noise.

2. **Noise Transition**: This method models labeling noise probabilistically, assuming that the noisy label depends only on the true label. A noise transition matrix can be used to specify the probability of label misannotation. The loss function can be modified to incorporate this matrix, which can be estimated from data or with human assistance.

3. **Cleaning**: This approach involves detecting and removing noisy data. Ensemble learning can effectively identify noisy examples, improving generalization performance. Data pruning and partitioning filters are also mentioned for large datasets, while some methods incorporate noise detection directly into the learning objective.

4. **Reweighting**: This strategy assigns lower weights to noisy data rather than removing it entirely. Techniques like Siamese networks and cleanNet are used to distinguish between clean and noisy labels, helping to improve model performance on noisy datasets.

5. **Relabeling**: This involves correcting noisy labels through methods such as probabilistic graphical models and bootstrapping, where labels are updated iteratively based on model predictions.

6. **Discussion**: The paper highlights that while the methods differ technically, they share a common goal of mitigating the influence of noisy data. Robust loss, cleaning, and reweighting can be applied across various noise types, while noise transition and relabeling are specifically effective for label noise.

The paper also briefly touches upon unsupervised learning, emphasizing the shift from traditional clustering to unsupervised representation learning, particularly through self-supervised learning, where targets are generated directly from the data without the need for labels.
