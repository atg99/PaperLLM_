The paper discusses the concept of breaking the closed-world assumption in recognition systems by categorizing the open space into four segments: known known, known unknown, unknown known, and unknown unknown. 

In the "Known Known" category, empirical risk is addressed, which relates to minimizing the misclassification rate on observed samples. However, the finite nature of samples can lead to overfitting, where models perform well on training data but poorly on test data. Strategies such as k-nearest neighbors, decision trees, and multilayer perceptrons are analyzed, highlighting the importance of regularization techniques to improve generalization performance. The principle of minimizing structural risk, as suggested by VC-dimension theory, advocates balancing model complexity with training data fitting, leading to the development of support vector machines and various regularization strategies.

The "Known Unknown" category focuses on outlier risk, where outliers represent the things we recognize as unknown. A common approach is to extend classification to include a new class for outliers, but this necessitates collecting outlier samples, which can be complex. Without these samples, outlier detection becomes essential. The paper outlines several strategies for handling outliers, including pattern rejection methods that integrate rejection strategies into traditional classifiers. Examples include Bayes decision theory and modifications for specific classifiers like support vector machines and ensemble learning, emphasizing the impact of classifier structure on rejection performance. 

Overall, the paper emphasizes the need for effective strategies in both known and unknown scenarios to enhance recognition systems in open-world settings.
