The paper provides an evaluation of various robustness issues in pattern recognition, structured around different metrics and performance outcomes. 

In the open-world evaluation, classification accuracy is highlighted, with human-level accuracy on the ImageNet dataset at 94.9%, while ResNet achieves 96.43%. For the MNIST dataset, accuracy exceeds 99%. Rejection performance is assessed using the area under the curve (AUC), showing an AUROC of 85% and AUPR of 86% when distinguishing notMNIST from MNIST. Adversarial robustness is examined through perturbations, revealing that state-of-the-art networks can be misled with minimal changes. The study notes a significant drop in classification accuracy from 90% to 45% on ILSVRC as classes increase from 100 to 1000.

In the non-iid evaluation, contextual learning ability is discussed, with a notable improvement in handwritten Chinese text recognition accuracy from 69% to 91% through context integration. Adaptability and transferability metrics show a 30% error reduction in handwriting recognition via style transfer. Multi-task cooperation is highlighted, showing a reduction in labeled samples needed by about two-thirds while maintaining performance. Multi-modal fusion ability is demonstrated through performance boosts in gesture recognition competitions.

The noisy small data evaluation metrics include noisy data tolerance, where accuracy drops from 93% to 72% with 30% noise but can recover to 91% with a robust model. Self-supervised capabilities are showcased with MoCo outperforming supervised methods in several vision tasks. Semi-supervised learning achieves 91% accuracy with limited labeled data. Few-shot generalization is illustrated, with 49% accuracy in 1-shot learning on miniImageNet and 55% in 0-shot learning on CUB.

Finally, zero-shot learning (ZSL) is explored, emphasizing the use of side information to classify novel categories without examples. Various approaches for ZSL, including attribute-based classifiers and embedding-based methods, are discussed along with techniques for synthesizing class-specific samples to enhance performance.
