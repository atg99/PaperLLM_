The paper presents a comparison of conversion methods for Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) across various datasets, including MNIST, CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet. The results demonstrate that the proposed Spiking ResNet architecture achieves state-of-the-art performance, outperforming existing SNN approaches on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet. Specifically, it reports a top-1 error rate of 31.44% on CIFAR-100 and 27.25% on ImageNet, while maintaining competitive performance compared to traditional Artificial Neural Networks (ANNs).

Additionally, the paper discusses the energy efficiency of SNNs compared to ANNs. Using advanced hardware for estimation, it finds that the power consumption of ResNet is over nine times greater than that of S-ResNet across all depths. This highlights the potential of SNNs to achieve significant energy savings when implemented on neuromorphic hardware, despite the older technology used in the ROLLS chip compared to the Intel Stratix 10 TX FPGA. Overall, the findings suggest a promising future for SNN-based systems in terms of both performance and energy efficiency.
