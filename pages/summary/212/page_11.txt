In this paper, we addressed the challenges of constructing deep spiking neural networks (SNNs) within the neuromorphic computing field. We introduced an efficient method for transforming a deep residual network into a spiking version, facilitating its adaptation to energy-efficient neuromorphic hardware. Our conversion model enhances SNN performance by allowing for deeper architectures while incorporating a compensation mechanism to mitigate discretization errors that arise from increased depth. The proposed Spiking ResNet outperformed existing SNN methods on standard datasets such as CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet 2012, demonstrating its effectiveness. Additionally, our approach has the potential for adaptation to other deep artificial neural network architectures, suggesting broader applicability in advancing neuromorphic computing.
