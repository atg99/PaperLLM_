The paper discusses the feature maps of each residual block in the ResNet-44 architecture, specifically comparing standard artificial neural networks (ANNs) with spiking versions of ResNet-44. The analysis includes visual representations of feature maps from both architectures using an example input from the CIFAR-10 dataset.

The conversion model for scaling shortcuts in residual blocks is also elaborated upon. In a basic residual block, the output is defined by a specific equation involving weights and biases. To adapt this for spiking neurons, the authors propose matching the activations in ResNet with the firing rates of spiking neurons, ensuring that they do not fire multiple spikes simultaneously to avoid losing information.

To achieve this, the weights and biases are normalized based on the maximum possible activation calculated from the training set. This normalization leads to new values for weights, biases, and inputs, which are essential for the updated output of the block. The paper emphasizes that the normalized output must always reflect the original activation normalized by its maximum possible activation.

Through this analysis, the authors conclude that the shortcut connection in spiking residual networks must be scaled by a specific factor derived from the maximum input and output activations, ensuring effective information transfer within the network.
