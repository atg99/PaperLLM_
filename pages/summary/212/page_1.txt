In this paper, the authors present a novel approach to constructing a spiking version of deep residual networks, termed Spiking ResNet (S-ResNet). Spiking neural networks (SNNs) are gaining attention for their biological realism and potential energy efficiency, but training deep SNNs remains a significant challenge due to their discrete spiking mechanism. The authors propose a method to convert a pre-trained traditional residual network into an SNN, addressing issues of scaling continuous activations to spiking rates and minimizing errors from discretization through a compensation mechanism.

Experimental results indicate that the Spiking ResNet outperforms existing state-of-the-art SNN methods on benchmark datasets such as CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet 2012. This work marks the first instance of constructing an SNN deeper than 40 layers that achieves performance levels comparable to traditional artificial neural networks on large-scale datasets. The study emphasizes the potential of SNNs in emulating human-like intelligence while addressing the challenges of deep network training.
