This paper addresses the conversion of conventional artificial neural networks (ANNs) to spiking neural networks (SNNs), particularly focusing on the challenges posed by the residual network architecture. The authors propose a shortcut conversion model aimed at normalizing synaptic weights in spiking residual networks. They identify that accumulating propagation errors can hinder loss-less conversion in large-scale networks and introduce a method to mitigate this by slightly saturating neuron firing rates and enhancing response times in deeper layers. This approach demonstrates state-of-the-art performance across various datasets including MNIST, CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet. 

The paper also reviews related work in SNN learning algorithms, categorizing them into four main groups: gradient descent-based algorithms, learning with synaptic plasticity rules, statistical algorithms, and conversion methods. Gradient descent approaches, such as Spikeprop and stochastic gradient descent, aim to address the non-differentiability of SNNs. Synaptic plasticity methods leverage biological principles like spike-timing-dependent plasticity (STDP) for unsupervised learning. Statistical algorithms explore the stochastic nature of neural networks, while conversion methods involve transforming pre-trained ANNs into SNNs, often requiring adaptations for compatibility with modern architectures. Overall, the paper contributes to the ongoing development of efficient and effective methods for SNN training and conversion.
