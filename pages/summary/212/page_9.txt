The paper presents experimental results on CIFAR-10 and ImageNet datasets, demonstrating the effectiveness of error compensation in improving the performance of both plain CNNs and ResNet architectures. The findings indicate that error compensation consistently reduces propagation errors across various network depths, with more significant improvements observed in deeper networks. Notably, plain CNNs benefited more from error compensation compared to residual networks, suggesting that architectural differences influence the propagation error and overall performance of converted spiking neural networks (SNNs).

On the CIFAR-10 dataset, error rates were reported for different network depths, showing that deeper networks incur greater propagation errors. The results also highlighted that error rates for plain CNNs were notably higher than those for ResNet, emphasizing the impact of architecture on performance. 

In experiments with the ImageNet dataset, the spiking residual network achieved a top-1 error rate of 27.25% and a top-5 error rate of 9.03%, both representing the best performance reported for ImageNet 2012. The results confirm that error compensation enhances performance across various network depths.

Furthermore, a comparison with other deep SNNs across multiple datasets (MNIST, CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet) shows that the proposed methods outperform existing state-of-the-art SNNs, particularly achieving loss-less performance on MNIST with a shallower network and significantly lower error rates on CIFAR-10 with Spiking ResNet-44. Overall, the research underscores the importance of error compensation and network architecture in optimizing the performance of deep SNNs.
