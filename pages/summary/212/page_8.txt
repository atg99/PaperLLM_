The paper discusses a model for adjusting neuron weights in spiking neural networks (SNNs) to mitigate propagation errors. The objective is to minimize the sum of the error terms (Ei) across all neurons, represented by the equation argmin Wi Σ Ei(Wi; l; t). A weight update rule is proposed, using a compensation factor (θ) to simplify the parameter adjustments across layers, thereby enhancing the model's efficiency.

Experiments were carried out on four datasets: MNIST, CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet 2012, using residual networks structured as described in previous work. The evaluation focused on the impact of shortcut normalization on error rates in S-ResNets, especially on the CIFAR-10 dataset, which contains 50,000 training images across 10 classes. Results indicated that SNNs with shortcut normalization consistently outperformed those without, achieving minimal classification error rate losses across various network depths.

Additionally, the paper explores the effectiveness of error compensation in both residual networks and traditional convolutional networks, specifically a VGG-like architecture. The findings suggest that applying error compensation significantly enhances performance, paving the way for more robust neural network designs.
