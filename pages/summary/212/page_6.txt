The section discusses the compensation of propagation error in the conversion from artificial neurons to spiking neurons. The conversion relies on the one-to-one correspondence between the normalized activation of ReLU (Rectified Linear Unit) in artificial neurons and the firing rate of spiking neurons. The activation of an artificial neuron is calculated using a weighted sum of inputs and a bias, while spiking neurons reset their membrane potential after firing.

The firing rate of a neuron is influenced by its membrane potential and the weights from connected pre-synaptic neurons. The difference between the ReLU activation and the firing rate introduces a deviation termed propagation error. This error accumulates as information passes through layers of neurons, particularly in deep networks, hindering the accurate approximation of ReLU activations by firing rates. 

The section highlights the challenge of quantifying this propagation error due to its complexity, which complicates efforts to adjust firing rates to match the intended ReLU activation. Overall, the propagation error is presented as a significant obstacle in developing large-scale spiking neural networks.
