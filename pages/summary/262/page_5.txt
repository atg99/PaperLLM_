The paper discusses an advancement in capsule networks, specifically the Efficient-CapsNet, which transitions from a "place-coded" to a "rate-coded" representation of location information within capsules. The foundational unit in this network is a vector-output capsule rather than a single neuron. A capsule-wise activation function is employed to encode the existence probability of entities, necessitating the preservation of vector orientation and a length constraint between zero and one. The squash operation is introduced as a variant of the activation function to enhance sensitivity to small changes, effectively adjusting the lengths of the vector outputs to fall within the specified range.

Additionally, the paper presents a self-attention routing algorithm to connect active capsules to their respective groups. The architecture parallels a fully-connected network augmented by self-attention, where the input to a capsule in the upper layer is derived from a weighted sum of predictions from the lower layer capsules. This is facilitated by a weight matrix that captures all affine transformations between adjacent layers. Ultimately, the predictions made by capsules in one layer inform the properties of capsules in the subsequent layer, enabling a structured and efficient routing process within the network.
