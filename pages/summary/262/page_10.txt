The paper investigates the capabilities of a modified Capsule Network (CapsNet) and its ability to encode transformations of digit images. It demonstrates that the proposed self-attention routing mechanism allows CapsNet to behave similarly to the original model, effectively capturing shape, position, and orientation through a capsule structure. A Convolutional Neural Network with a vector output also shows similar encoding capabilities, unlike traditional CNNs which fail to reproduce this behavior, indicating the importance of the capsule organization for meaningful information embedding.

The study further evaluates the model's performance in capturing meaningful information by analyzing equivariance to transformations, such as translations and rotations. By generating transformed images and applying Principal Component Analysis (PCA) on the output vectors, the authors assess the linearity of the transformations encoded in the activations of the output capsules. The results reveal that the Efficient-CapsNet demonstrates higher linearity in encoding affine transformations compared to the original CapsNet, as indicated by the fraction of variance captured by the first eigenvalue in PCA.

Overall, the findings suggest that the Efficient-CapsNet architecture effectively embeds position and orientation information in the output vector components, highlighting the significance of the capsule structure for interpretable output embeddings.
