The paper by Alber et al. discusses the advancements in deep neural networks and their applications across various domains such as image and speech recognition, autonomous driving, and medical image analysis. Despite their effectiveness, these networks are often perceived as black boxes due to the complexity of their internal mechanisms and decision-making processes. The authors emphasize the importance of understanding these processes, particularly in contexts where transparency is legally required.

Several methods have been proposed to demystify neural networks, including Saliency Maps, SmoothGrad, Integrated Gradients, and Layer-wise Relevance Propagation (LRP), among others. However, there is a lack of clarity regarding which method is most effective, necessitating empirical comparisons.

To address this gap, the authors introduce iNNvestigate, a tool that provides a unified interface for a range of analysis methods applicable to various neural networks. Key contributions of iNNvestigate include:

1. A common interface that simplifies the instantiation of analysis methods, facilitating qualitative comparisons and quantitative evaluations, particularly in image classification tasks through perturbation analysis.
2. Support for multiple analysis methods, including the first reference implementations for PatternNet and PatternAttribution, along with an open-source repository for community contributions.
3. A modular implementation that utilizes layer-wise computations to minimize code redundancy and enhance integration capabilities for future methods.

iNNvestigate is accessible as a Python package, complete with documentation, and includes examples demonstrating its versatility in analyzing image classifications across different state-of-the-art neural networks.
