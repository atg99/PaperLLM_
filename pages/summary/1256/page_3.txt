Ensemble learning is a powerful machine learning technique that combines multiple models to improve overall performance. It typically employs methods such as Bagging and Stacking. In Bagging, multiple bootstrap samples are generated from the original dataset, and base learners are trained on these samples. The final output is determined by majority voting among the predictions of these learners.

Stacking, on the other hand, involves training several first-level learners using various algorithms on the original dataset. A second-level meta-learner is then trained on the predictions of these first-level learners to make the final decision. This method emphasizes the fusion of information from different models.

Empirical studies indicate that no single ensemble method consistently outperforms others. While it was once believed that more base learners would enhance performance, research by Zhou et al. introduced the “many could be better than all” theorem, suggesting that selectively choosing a subset of base learners can yield better results than using all available learners.

Ensembles generally demonstrate superior generalization capabilities compared to single learners, as noted by Dietterich. He identified three reasons for this: the training data may not provide enough information to select the best learner, multiple learners may perform equally well, and ensembles can capture a broader range of hypotheses. Ensemble methods are versatile and have been applied not only in classification and regression tasks but also in clustering and other machine learning applications.
