The paper discusses the architecture of an AI Physicist, which employs a divide-and-conquer strategy to develop and refine theories for different environments. The process begins with the training of multiple competing theories using a generalized-mean loss function, which allows for specialization of each theory to its respective domain. This approach mirrors the historical methodology of physicists, who often focus on specific aspects of complex problems rather than attempting to model everything simultaneously.

The AI Physicist utilizes a theory hub that organizes learned theories through two main strategies: applying Occam's razor to simplify neural network theories into symbolic formulas and unifying these symbolic theories into master theories. This organization enhances the proposal of new theories as the system learns from successful theories and their corresponding data.

A critical aspect of the methodology is the generalized-mean loss function, which incentivizes theories that perform well on specific data points to specialize further. The paper presents Theorem 1, which establishes that under certain conditions, the gradient of the generalized-mean loss encourages better-performing theories to improve their predictions. The authors empirically find that setting a specific parameter within the loss function, termed harmonic loss, effectively balances specialization and performance across theories.

The methodology culminates in the unsupervised differentiable divide-and-conquer algorithm, which simultaneously learns prediction functions and domain classifiers from observations. This process is likened to AdaBoost, indicating a similarity in approach to combining multiple prediction mechanisms. Overall, the paper presents a comprehensive framework for developing and refining theories in a structured manner, leveraging the strengths of various theories to enhance understanding and predictive capabilities in unfamiliar environments.
