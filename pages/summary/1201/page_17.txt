The paper discusses the challenges of integer snapping in the context of solving problems in electromagnetic regions, particularly focusing on ensuring that certain coefficients are simple fractions. This introduces complexities such as undersnapping and oversnapping that can hinder domain solving. The authors provide a resource for accessing their experimental setups online.

The main focus is on a double pendulum system comprised of two connected pendulums, each with a point charge, influenced by piecewise constant electric fields. The system's state is represented by a 4-tuple and is subjected to varying electric field strengths across seven different environments. The equations of motion (EOM) differ based on the electric field in which the lower charge resides.

To analyze this system, the authors employ Runge-Kutta numerical integration for simulating the pendulum's motion over 10,000 time steps. They aim to predict future states based on past data while simultaneously discovering the underlying domains and their respective EOMs without supervision.

The experimentation involves two neural network architectures, Baseline and Newborn, each configured with different neuron counts and activation functions for a fair comparison. The Newborn model is restricted from adding theories indefinitely, starting with a limited number of theories to enhance the experimental validity. The domain classifier is designed with a smaller architecture to classify the domains effectively.
