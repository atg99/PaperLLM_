The paper discusses the proof of a theorem related to generalized mean loss and its implications for various loss functions. It begins by reformulating the generalized mean loss, L, in terms of a specific function, `, leading to an expression that highlights the relationship between the derivative of the loss and the variables involved. The core argument establishes that the derivative of ` with respect to u is strictly decreasing, which allows for a comparison between different instances of u, specifically u(i) and u(j).

The paper then presents Corollary 1.1, demonstrating that the theorem applies to several well-known loss functions, including Mean Squared Error (MSE), Mean Absolute Error, Huber loss, and two types of description length loss. Each of these functions is shown to satisfy the theorem's conditions, such as being monotonically increasing and differentiable for u above a certain threshold.

The proof for strict convexity of these loss functions is detailed, using derivatives and specific properties of the functions involved. For instance, it is shown that the second derivative of the functions is positive in relevant intervals, confirming their strict convexity. The results affirm that the described loss functions meet the criteria laid out by the theorem, thereby validating their effectiveness in various applications.
