This paper presents Algorithm 2, titled the AI Physicist, which employs a differentiable divide-and-conquer approach with harmonic loss for training theories based on a dataset. The algorithm begins by initializing a set of theories, some of which are derived from a theory hub. It includes a training phase that iteratively refines these theories using gradient descent to minimize a defined loss function.

The training process consists of two main stages. In the first stage, the algorithm iterates through the theories to optimize their prediction functions in relation to the dataset, adjusting the median prediction error after each iteration. During this phase, theories can be added or deleted based on their performance and the coverage of the examples they explain.

In the second stage, the training focuses on fine-tuning the prediction functions for each theory, specifically using a domain classifier to allocate examples to the most relevant theory. This method aims to enhance generalization to unseen examples by ensuring that the domains are simpler and more contiguous. 

The paper outlines default hyperparameters, including the number of theories, learning rates, and optimization methods. It also describes the implementation of two regularization terms to maintain the accuracy of predictions outside their domains. The use of these techniques, including a harmonic loss function, is intended to improve model performance while adhering to principles like Occam's Razor and Minimum Description Length (MDL).

Overall, the AI Physicist algorithm aims to effectively manage multiple theories to provide robust predictions while minimizing complexity and enhancing generalization.
