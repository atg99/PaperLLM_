The paper discusses a method for data prediction utilizing the principle of Occam's razor, which favors simpler explanations. This approach is implemented through the minimum-description-length (MDL) formalism, which quantifies the description length (DL) of a dataset based on the number of bits required to describe it. The aim is to identify theories that minimize the total DL, combining the DL of the theory itself and the prediction errors over time.

The authors highlight the challenges of finding optimal data compression, a complex problem due to the vast search space involved. However, they assert that any reduction in description length is beneficial and helps avoid overfitting, a common issue in machine learning. The AI Physicist, as described, seeks to discover simple theories that efficiently explain different parts of the data, thus achieving a lower DL.

The paper outlines the calculation methods for DL concerning various types of numbers, including natural numbers, integers, rational numbers, and real numbers, emphasizing the importance of using rational approximations in physical theories. The MDL approach contrasts with other machine-learning strategies that prioritize efficiency over intelligibility, aiming instead for a deeper understanding through simplicity in predictive modeling.
