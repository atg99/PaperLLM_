In Appendix H, the focus is on addressing the challenges faced by the AI Physicist when predicting motion across different physics domains. The primary issue is determining the laws of motion in regions away from domain boundaries, which allows for automatic identification of boundaries and transition regions. The AI Physicist predicts future position vectors based on previous positions, and the complexity increases due to the presence of multiple domains with different physics.

Numerical experiments reveal that while the AI Physicist successfully handles larger domains where all position vectors belong to a single region, it struggles with smaller transition domains that involve boundary crossings or bounces due to insufficient data. To resolve this, the appendix outlines a post-processing algorithm that eliminates these transition domains.

The algorithm begins by fitting functions that predict past trajectories based on forward trajectories from big domains. When a trajectory crosses from one big domain to another, two extrapolations (forward and backward) are performed. Cubic spline interpolation is used to find the time at which the predicted positions from both extrapolations are closest. If they match closely and have the same derivative, a boundary point is recorded. These boundary points, categorized as either interior or external, can then be used to retrain domain classifiers, effectively extending big domains and eliminating transition regions.

The appendix also notes that occasional failures in boundary point determination due to multiple transitions are minor and do not significantly impact the overall fitting for domain boundaries. 

Appendix I provides additional details on the numerical experiments conducted to validate these findings.
