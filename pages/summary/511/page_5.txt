This paper discusses the applications of autoencoders and generative adversarial networks (GANs) in the field of remote sensing. Variational Autoencoders (VAE) are highlighted for their ability to map inputs to a multi-dimensional Gaussian distribution, allowing for effective reconstruction of data through sampled latent vectors. The loss function for VAE is presented, emphasizing the balance between reconstruction fidelity and the properties of the latent space.

Masked Autoencoders (MAE) are also mentioned as a recent advancement in self-supervised pre-training for vision transformers, demonstrating their potential in visual representation learning by reconstructing missing image patches.

In the context of remote sensing, autoencoders have been utilized for various data types, including multispectral and hyperspectral images. Notable contributions include the use of stacked denoising autoencoders for change detection and their incorporation in tasks such as classification, denoising, restoration, and unmixing of hyperspectral images.

The paper then transitions to GANs, explaining their adversarial training framework where a generator creates fake samples while a discriminator distinguishes them from real data. The BiGAN model is introduced, which incorporates an encoder to map data to latent representations, enhancing the training process by operating in both data and latent spaces.

Applications of GANs in remote sensing are less prevalent but include studies on hyperspectral image classification and unsupervised unmixing. The potential for GANs to generate hard-negative samples and improve classification tasks is also explored.

Overall, the paper underscores the significance of these deep learning techniques in advancing remote sensing applications, with a focus on their representation learning capabilities.
