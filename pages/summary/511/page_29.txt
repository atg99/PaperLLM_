This paper presents a comprehensive review of recent advancements in the field of remote sensing, particularly focusing on the application of deep learning techniques, including self-supervised and unsupervised learning methods. Several studies are cited, highlighting various approaches to image classification, scene segmentation, and change detection using remote sensing data.

Key concepts discussed include:

1. **Unsupervised Deep Learning**: Zhang et al. explore unsupervised representation learning for PolSAR images, enhancing classification performance with fewer labeled samples.

2. **Multi-View Learning**: Geng et al. introduce a model that leverages complementary information from multiple views for urban scene classification, demonstrating improved accuracy.

3. **Contrastive Learning**: Multiple papers, including those by Hou et al. and Zhu et al., emphasize the effectiveness of contrastive learning techniques for hyperspectral image classification, particularly under limited labeled data scenarios.

4. **Self-Supervised Learning**: Several studies, including those by Jung and Duan, focus on self-supervised methods for tasks such as change detection and oil spill detection, showcasing the potential of these techniques to enhance model performance without extensive labeled datasets.

5. **Integration of Multiple Sensors**: Research by Montanaro et al. and Saha et al. highlights the benefits of combining synthetic aperture radar (SAR) and multispectral data for land cover classification and change detection.

The overall findings indicate a significant shift towards utilizing self-supervised and unsupervised learning frameworks in remote sensing, which promise to reduce reliance on labeled data while improving classification and detection capabilities across various applications.
