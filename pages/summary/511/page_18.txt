The paper discusses the advancements in self-supervised learning (SSL) within the realm of remote sensing, emphasizing its applications across various modalities such as multispectral, hyperspectral, and SAR data. It highlights the significance of multi-sensor fusion, particularly the integration of optical and SAR images, which benefits from SSL techniques. The paper categorizes SSL into generative, predictive, and contrastive types, and analyzes their implications for different applications in remote sensing.

Applications of SSL are divided into three main tasks: image-level, pixel-level, and patch-level tasks. Image-level tasks focus on global knowledge, with scene classification being a primary example. Pixel-level tasks delve into detailed recognition at the pixel level, with semantic segmentation and change detection being notable applications. Patch-level tasks, such as object detection, require a blend of global and local representations.

The paper emphasizes that the choice of self-supervised methods should align with specific application goals, as different methods can affect outcomes differently. Additionally, it references a preliminary benchmark for large-scale unsupervised pre-training in the image domain, indicating a significant milestone in the field. The study underlines the growing interest in multimodal data fusion, including audio and social media, and encourages further exploration in this evolving area of research.
