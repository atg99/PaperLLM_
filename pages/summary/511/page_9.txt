The paper discusses the structures and methodologies of self-supervised learning (SSL), particularly focusing on generative, predictive, and contrastive methods. Generative and predictive approaches calculate loss in the output space, while contrastive methods operate in the representation space. Designing effective pretext tasks for predictive learning is challenging and can limit generalizability. To address this, contrastive methods allow networks to learn high-level representations without relying on a single pretext task.

Contrastive methods involve training models by contrasting similar inputs, such as augmented views of the same image, to ensure they are represented closely in the embedding space. However, this can lead to model collapse, where all inputs map to identical representations. To mitigate this, various strategies have emerged, including negative sampling, clustering, knowledge distillation, and redundancy reduction.

Negative sampling is a fundamental strategy that incorporates dissimilar samples to create both positive and negative pairs. The encoder is trained to maximize the similarity between positive samples while minimizing it for negative samples. This approach helps the network learn useful high-level representations, which can be transferred for downstream tasks. The paper highlights several methods based on negative sampling, including triplet loss and contrastive predictive coding (CPC), which utilizes InfoNCE loss for effective learning and classification of samples.
