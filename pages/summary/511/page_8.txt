In this paper, the authors explore various methodologies for self-supervised learning in the context of remote sensing and satellite imagery. They highlight the significant advancements in representation learning, particularly through the use of generative adversarial networks and Transformer-based approaches. Yuan et al. introduce a technique that utilizes the temporal structure of satellite time series for improved classification, demonstrating the effectiveness of predicting contaminated observations.

The paper also discusses alternative semantic contexts that enhance learning, such as counting visual primitives and adversarial artifact prediction through autoencoders. Multi-sensor and multi-modal learning are addressed, with examples including predicting sound associated with video frames and depth maps from synthetic images.

Furthermore, the authors emphasize the potential for remote sensing-specific pretext tasks, citing various studies that propose tasks like monocular depth estimation and geo-location prediction. They note that while these tasks can facilitate self-supervised learning, simpler pretexts may not always yield robust representations, necessitating their use as auxiliary tasks. The overall trend indicates that combining multiple pretext tasks generally enhances performance and accelerates training processes in self-supervised learning frameworks within remote sensing applications.
