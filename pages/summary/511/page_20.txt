The paper discusses the effectiveness of self-supervised learning in remote sensing, particularly on the BigEarthNet dataset. It highlights the importance of data augmentation techniques, specifically cropping, in improving model performance. The study compares various self-supervised methods, including MoCo-v2, SwAV, SimSiam, and Barlow Twins, noting that MoCo-v2 generally shows robust performance across different datasets. SimSiam is criticized for its instability during training, often collapsing after several epochs, suggesting that enhancements like a momentum encoder might be necessary for better results.

The paper emphasizes the critical role of cropping as a data augmentation method, revealing that other augmentations commonly used in natural images, such as color distortion, do not significantly impact performance in remote sensing applications. Furthermore, the research explores how varying the minimum cropping size affects performance, confirming that less aggressive cropping leads to poorer results, aligning with findings in computer vision.

Additionally, the study investigates the performance of self-supervised models under conditions of limited labeled data. It demonstrates that self-supervised pre-training can outperform traditional supervised learning when the number of labels is significantly reduced, highlighting the potential of these methods in scenarios where labeled data is scarce. Overall, the findings suggest that self-supervised learning, particularly with effective data augmentations, can be a powerful tool in remote sensing tasks.
