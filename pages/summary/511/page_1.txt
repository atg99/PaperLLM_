The paper "Self-supervised Learning in Remote Sensing: A Review" by Yi Wang and colleagues discusses the growing interest in self-supervised learning (SSL) within the remote sensing field, building on its significant advancements in computer vision. While SSL has demonstrated impressive results in computer vision by reducing the reliance on labeled data, its potential in earth observation is still largely untapped. 

The authors provide an overview of SSL concepts and recent developments, emphasizing the need for a comprehensive introduction tailored to the remote sensing community. They present a taxonomy of SSL methods, benchmark modern algorithms on established remote sensing datasets, and extend the analysis of SSL performance using popular datasets such as BigEarthNet, SEN12MS, and So2Sat-LCZ42. 

The review highlights the challenges of data annotation in remote sensing, noting that while open access to earth observation data is increasing, the task of labeling remains labor-intensive. The authors argue that SSL could address this challenge by leveraging unlabeled data to enhance model performance. 

Finally, the paper outlines promising avenues for future research in self-supervised learning for earth observation, aiming to foster collaboration between the computer vision and remote sensing fields. This review serves as a foundational resource for researchers interested in applying SSL techniques to remote sensing applications.
