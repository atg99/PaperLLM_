The paper discusses advancements in remote sensing image retrieval and classification using generative and predictive self-supervised learning methods. Cheng et al. introduced a perturbation-seeking GAN for scene classification, while Ozkan et al. optimized hyperspectral unmixing with Wasserstein GAN loss. The focus then shifts to predictive self-supervised methods, which utilize auto-generated labels rather than pixel-level reconstruction, addressing limitations of generative methods, such as excessive focus on low-level details and neglecting long-range correlations.

Predictive methods leverage pretext tasks to learn high-level semantic information from images. These tasks are categorized into spatial context, spectral context, temporal context, and other semantic contexts. Spatial context tasks, such as predicting the relative position of image patches and solving jigsaw puzzles, encourage global scene understanding. However, the complexity of jigsaw puzzles can lead to challenges in recognizing all permutations due to visual ambiguity.

Additionally, geometric transformations like rotation prediction and image inpainting are explored as effective pretext tasks for remote sensing applications. The paper highlights various studies applying these methods, including multi-task frameworks that combine rotation prediction with scene classification, as well as innovative approaches to few-shot scene classification.

Spectral context tasks draw inspiration from image colorization techniques. Initial work involved predicting spectral channels based on other channels, leveraging human perception in color representation. This was further refined with methods that learn cross-channel representations, enhancing the effectiveness of self-supervised learning in remote sensing.

Overall, the paper presents a comprehensive overview of self-supervised learning strategies in remote sensing, emphasizing the potential of predictive methods to improve image understanding and classification.
