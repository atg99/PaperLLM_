The paper discusses the increasing interest in self-supervised learning (SSL) within the field of remote sensing, highlighting a gap in research efforts compared to other domains. It identifies several challenges in remote sensing, such as outdated datasets and label noise, which complicate the generation of high-quality labels for machine learning models. The trade-off between the quantity and quality of annotations can lead to biased models or overfitting.

The authors emphasize the utility of transfer learning, where models pre-trained on benchmark datasets are fine-tuned for specific tasks. However, the effectiveness of this approach can be limited by the domain differences between source and target datasets. This scenario underscores the need for techniques that can leverage unlabeled data to extract valuable information applicable to multiple tasks.

Self-supervised learning is proposed as a solution to these challenges. It operates by optimizing self-produced objectives using large amounts of unlabeled data to train models without manual annotation. The paper outlines three common self-supervision strategies: reconstructing input data, predicting self-generated labels from contextual information, and contrasting semantically similar inputs. Once trained, these models can be fine-tuned for downstream tasks, benefiting from general representations and requiring fewer labeled examples.

Furthermore, self-supervised learning is linked to semi-supervised learning, where it serves as a preprocessing step to enhance model performance by extracting features from unlabeled data before adapting to specific tasks with labeled data. The paper notes that semi-supervised learning has been extensively studied in remote sensing, particularly in applications like hyperspectral and multi-spectral image recognition.
