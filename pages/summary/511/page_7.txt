The paper discusses various predictive pretext tasks relevant to self-supervised learning, particularly in the context of remote sensing and video understanding. It categorizes these tasks into three main contexts: spatial, spectral, and temporal. 

1. **Spatial Context**: This includes tasks that leverage the arrangement of spatial elements, such as relative positioning, jigsaw puzzles, rotation, inpainting, colorization, and frame order prediction. The paper highlights the need for careful design in the spectral domain due to the complexity of remote sensing imagery, which often includes multiple spectral bands.

2. **Spectral Context**: The paper cites recent work by Vincenzi et al. that utilizes spectral information for self-supervised learning, where a model predicts RGB imagery from multi-spectral bands. This approach aims to extract meaningful representations for downstream classification tasks. Wu et al. also contribute to hyperspectral dimensionality reduction through self-supervised learning.

3. **Temporal Context**: In video understanding, temporal context is critical. The paper outlines two primary approaches for designing temporal pretext tasks: shuffling timestamps and predicting missing frames. It notes that LSTM models are particularly effective for capturing the temporal dynamics in video data. The paper distinguishes between temporal order verification and recognition, emphasizing the role of frame sampling strategies to enhance model performance.

Lastly, the paper points out the potential of self-supervised learning in remote sensing, especially with respect to change detection and crop classification, despite the current limitations in satellite-based video recording. The work by Dong et al. is referenced, which focuses on optimizing models to identify changes over time in geospatial areas. Overall, the paper underscores the importance of designing context-aware pretext tasks to improve representation learning in remote sensing and video data.
