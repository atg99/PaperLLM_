The paper discusses advancements in self-supervised learning techniques, particularly focusing on contrastive learning and clustering methods in the context of remote sensing image analysis. Various approaches are highlighted, such as the integration of tile2vec sampling with SimCLR, which utilizes smoothed representations of neighboring tiles for positive sampling. Other methods explored include the application of SimCLR for segmentation training, land cover classification using Swin Transformers, and the representation of LiDAR elevation data.

Clustering-based self-supervised methods, such as DeepCluster, are examined for their iterative process of generating pseudo labels through K-means clustering. Innovations like local aggregation and self-labeling have emerged, allowing for more flexible structures without the need for a readout layer. Despite early successes, clustering methods have been overshadowed by more efficient instance discrimination techniques like SimCLR and MoCo, which eliminate slow clustering stages.

The paper also introduces the Swapping Assignments between Views (SwA V) method, which enhances clustering-based contrastive learning by assigning different views of the same image to the same prototypes. This method has shown promise in improving performance and efficiency in feature learning. Additionally, the integration of DeepCluster with other techniques for remote sensing applications, including change detection and segmentation, is discussed, indicating a growing trend towards combining contrastive learning with clustering for better representation learning in this field.
