The paper discusses recent advances in self-supervised learning techniques applied to various modalities in remote sensing. It highlights a range of methods categorized by application areas such as representation learning, scene classification, semantic segmentation, change detection, and more. A detailed table (Table II) summarizes these works, indicating the methodology used for each application.

Key concepts include the importance of different representation levels in remote sensing, where patch-level and pixel-level representations are crucial due to the spatial relationships inherent in the data. The paper also emphasizes the growing significance of temporal information in remote sensing, particularly for applications like change detection, drawing parallels with video self-supervised learning while noting the unique challenges posed by remote sensing time stamps.

The discussion covers multispectral imaging, which captures data across specific wavelengths, allowing for information extraction that extends beyond human visual perception. Compared to hyperspectral imaging, multispectral imaging typically utilizes fewer spectral bands, resulting in lower spectral resolution. Overall, the paper presents a comprehensive overview of how self-supervised learning can enhance remote sensing applications across various modalities.
