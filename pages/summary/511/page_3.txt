B. Predictive Methods
Predictive self-supervised learning methods aim to predict labels generated from the input data itself. These methods often involve creating high-level semantic labels based on the input, allowing the model to learn from the relationships within the data. This approach emphasizes the importance of understanding the context and semantics behind the data, thus enhancing the model's ability to generalize across different tasks.

C. Contrastive Methods
Contrastive self-supervised learning methods focus on maximizing the similarity between semantically similar inputs while minimizing the similarity between dissimilar ones. This technique helps the model learn robust representations by contrasting different instances, which is particularly effective in scenarios with limited labeled data.

The paper underscores the distinction between unsupervised and self-supervised learning, clarifying that while traditional unsupervised methods rely on statistical data properties, self-supervised learning leverages automatically generated labels for training. It also outlines evaluation metrics for self-supervised learning performance, including linear probing, K-nearest neighbors, and fine-tuning, along with qualitative methods for visualizing learned features.

Overall, the taxonomy of self-supervised learning presented in the paper categorizes methods into generative, predictive, and contrastive approaches, offering insights into their application in remote sensing and other fields. This structured understanding of self-supervised methodologies enhances the ability to develop effective models that can operate with minimal human intervention.
