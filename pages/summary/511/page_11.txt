This paper discusses advancements in contrastive learning methods, particularly focusing on MoCo and SimCLR. MoCo introduces a momentum-updated encoder to generate key representations on-the-fly, eliminating the need for end-to-end back-propagation or a memory bank. It maintains a queue of keys for negative samples, allowing for a larger dictionary size than typical mini-batch sizes. 

SimCLR, a significant contribution to contrastive learning, operates on an end-to-end framework with large batch sizes to enhance performance. It emphasizes the role of hard positive sampling through various data augmentation techniques, establishing a standard for future research. SimCLR also incorporates additional elements like a nonlinear transformation (projection head) and increased training steps.

The paper highlights the evolution of these methods, noting improvements in MoCo through data augmentation and the introduction of a transformer version. Similarly, SimCLR evolved into SimCLR-v2, enhancing semi-supervised learning capabilities.

In the context of remote sensing, contrastive learning has gained traction, with applications dating back to supervised contrastive regularization in CNNs for scene classification. The first self-supervised contrastive learning method for remote sensing, Tile2Vec, utilizes triplet loss to optimize the spatial relationships between tiles. Subsequent studies have refined this approach, integrating various loss functions and enhancing robustness for tasks like change detection and hyperspectral image classification. The paper underscores the effectiveness of contrastive learning in remote sensing, particularly in scenarios with limited labeled data.
