The paper presents a survey on pruning and quantization techniques in deep neural networks, focusing on the trade-offs between low precision libraries and accuracy across various frameworks. Table 3 provides empirical results on the accuracy of common networks when subjected to different quantization methods, such as Post-Training Quantization (PTQ) and Quantization-Aware Training (QAT). The results highlight the accuracy differences between Float and Quantized models for networks like AlexNet, GoogleNet, Inception v3, MobileNet, ResNet, and VGG-19.

The analysis shows that while some quantization methods maintain similar accuracy to their Float counterparts, others result in significant drops, particularly in Top-1 and Top-5 accuracy metrics. For instance, Ristretto's Pow-of-two method exhibits the largest decline in accuracy for GoogleNet and Inception v3. However, methods like PTQ without offset and QAT generally perform better in retaining accuracy.

The paper also discusses hardware implementations, particularly on FPGA and general-purpose processors, emphasizing the advancements in reducing power consumption and hardware costs through techniques like logarithmic quantization and INT8 quantization. It describes how specialized computation units and architectures have been optimized for efficient processing of quantized models, leading to faster inference times and reduced resource usage.

Overall, the survey underscores the importance of balancing accuracy and efficiency in deploying neural networks, providing insights into the performance of various methods across different frameworks and hardware platforms.
