The paper discusses advancements in pruning and quantization techniques in deep learning models, specifically focusing on Alpha-Blending (AB) as a replacement for the Straight-Through Estimator (STE). The STE sets quantization function gradients to 1, which may lead to accuracy losses in tuned networks. In contrast, AB introduces a scale coefficient, gradually increasing it to 1 during training to realize a fully quantized network while maintaining both real-valued and quantized weights.

The paper also covers low numerical precision training, highlighting mixed precision training that utilizes FP16 or INT16 for weight precision. This method maintains full precision for gradient updates while allowing other operands to use half-precision, employing loss scaling to prevent small magnitude gradients from affecting computations.

Deployment of quantization in popular frameworks and hardware is addressed, noting that large organizations often have proprietary solutions for quantization, which can lead to inconsistencies across frameworks. The discussion includes the development of multicore processors and accelerators, such as CPUs, GPUs, and FPGAs, which enhance the efficiency of DNN processing. The paper emphasizes the importance of a Heterogeneous System Architecture (HSA) that enables different processors to access shared memory, facilitating integrated processing in systems on chip (SoC).

Overall, the paper provides a comprehensive overview of the current state of pruning and quantization methods, their implementation in deep learning frameworks, and the hardware considerations necessary for efficient deployment.
