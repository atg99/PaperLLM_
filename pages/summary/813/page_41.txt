This paper presents a survey on pruning and quantization techniques in deep learning, aimed at optimizing model efficiency and performance. The authors, including Tailin Liang, John Glossner, Lei Wang, Shaobo Shi, and Xiaotong Zhang, provide insights into their academic backgrounds and research interests, highlighting their expertise in computer science, electrical engineering, and deep learning processors.

Pruning refers to the process of removing less significant weights from neural networks to reduce model size and inference time without significantly affecting accuracy. Quantization involves reducing the precision of the weights and activations, which can lead to faster computation and lower memory usage. Both techniques are critical for deploying deep learning models in resource-constrained environments.

The survey discusses various methodologies and algorithms related to pruning and quantization, examining their effectiveness and trade-offs. The authors emphasize the importance of these techniques in facilitating the deployment of deep learning applications across diverse hardware platforms, particularly in embedded systems and heterogeneous computing environments. 

Ultimately, the paper underscores the need for continued research in this area to enhance the efficiency of deep learning models while maintaining performance standards.
