This document presents a comprehensive survey on pruning and quantization techniques in neural networks, highlighting their significance in optimizing model performance and resource utilization. Pruning involves selectively removing weights or neurons to reduce model size while maintaining accuracy, while quantization refers to the process of reducing the precision of the weights and activations in neural networks. The paper discusses various methods and advancements in these techniques, including learned quantization, incremental network quantization, and the application of dual-attention mechanisms for improved predictions. It also addresses the challenges and methodologies for implementing these techniques on hardware platforms such as FPGAs. Key contributions from various studies are cited, showcasing the evolution of strategies aimed at making deep learning models more efficient and suitable for deployment in resource-constrained environments. Overall, the survey underscores the importance of pruning and quantization as pivotal strategies for enhancing the efficiency of deep neural networks.
