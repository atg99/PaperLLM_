The paper provides a comprehensive survey on various methods of pruning and quantization in neural networks, particularly focusing on techniques that maintain computational complexity similar to Binary Neural Networks (BNNs). 

Key techniques discussed include:

1. **Ternary Weight Networks (TWN)**: This method utilizes ternary logic (values of -1, 0, and +1) to efficiently implement computations in hardware. TWN achieves significant model compression of up to 16 times with a minimal accuracy loss of 3.6% on ResNet-18.

2. **Trained Ternary Quantization (TTQ)**: An extension of TWN, TTQ introduces dynamic constraints for quantization thresholds, resulting in a 0.3% improvement over full precision AlexNet and a 3% improvement over TWN.

3. **Ternary Neural Networks (TNN)**: TNN quantizes activations into ternary values, using a teacher-student framework for transfer learning. TNN achieves a classification error of 1.67% on MNIST and 12.11% on CIFAR10, although it has slightly lower accuracy than TWN.

4. **Fine-Grained Quantization (FGQ)**: Proposed by Intel, FGQ generalizes ternary weights by splitting them into groups with independent values. FGQ achieved 73.85% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet without retraining, and demonstrated significant improvements in retraining scenarios.

5. **MeliusNet**: A binary neural network that utilizes dense and improvement blocks to enhance network capacity and feature quality. It reports a top-1 accuracy of 70.7% on ImageNet while requiring fewer computations than MobileNet.

6. **AdderNet**: This technique replaces multiplications with addition in convolutions, leading to a reduction in computational complexity. It transforms billions of multiplications into subtractions with a small accuracy loss in ResNet-50.

7. **Neural Architecture Search (NAS)**: Shen's approach uses evolutionary algorithms to discover compact models, achieving a top-1 accuracy of 69.65% on ResNet-18 with ImageNet, although the search process is time-intensive.

8. **Weight Sharing by Vector Quantization**: This technique compresses parameters by clustering and sharing them, achieving significant compression ratios with minimal accuracy loss.

9. **HashNet**: Utilizes hashing to cluster weights, demonstrating a compression factor of 64 while outperforming networks of equivalent size on several datasets.

10. **Deep Compression**: A combination of weight sharing, pruning, and Huffman coding, resulting in a 49% compression on VGG-16 without accuracy loss.

11. **Hessian Method**: This method focuses on measuring parameter importance to improve weight quantization, achieving notable compression ratios with minimal accuracy loss.

Overall, these advancements illustrate the potential for significant reductions in model size and computational demands while maintaining competitive performance in neural network applications.
