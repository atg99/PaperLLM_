The paper surveys various techniques for pruning and quantization in deep neural networks (DNNs), focusing on methods that enhance model efficiency while maintaining performance. Key concepts include activation-based pruning, which identifies and removes channels with minimal contribution to outputs, achieving significant compression rates. Filter-wise pruning leverages the absolute values of filter weights, positively correlating pruning ratios with zeroed kernel weights, while penalty-based pruning modifies error functions during training to enforce sparsity.

Feature selection is highlighted as a critical technique that aids in model simplicity and reduces overfitting by focusing on relevant features. The LASSO method is discussed for its effectiveness in increasing weight sparsity by penalizing lesser-valued weights, outperforming traditional methods like ordinary least squares (OLS) in sparsity generation.

The paper also covers group LASSO, which maintains structural integrity by pruning entire groups of neurons, thereby improving execution efficiency on standard processors. Various applications of group LASSO, such as Structured Sparsity Learning, demonstrate significant performance improvements in inference times.

Additional methods like Group-wise braindamage and Sparse Convolutional Neural Networks (SCNN) are presented, showcasing their ability to enhance computational speed without sacrificing accuracy. Techniques like Network Slimming and Sparse Structure Selection further illustrate how LASSO can be applied to scaling factors and neuron groups to achieve substantial size reduction and computational efficiency.

Overall, the survey emphasizes the importance of combining pruning and quantization techniques to optimize DNNs for practical applications while minimizing accuracy loss.
