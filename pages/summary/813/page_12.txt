The paper presents a survey on pruning and quantization methods in neural networks, focusing on dynamic pruning systems. It outlines key considerations for implementing such systems, which include:

1. **Decision Components**: The nature of decision components is crucial, with options for additional connections during inference or training, characteristics learned via backpropagation, or side decision networks that are effective but challenging to train.

2. **Pruning Levels**: The paper discusses various pruning levels, including channel-wise, layer-wise, block-wise, and network-wise options, emphasizing that the chosen level impacts hardware design.

3. **Input Data Handling**: Two methods for feeding data to the decision system are highlighted: one-shot information feeding and layer-wise information feeding.

4. **Decision Score Calculation**: Different approaches to calculating decision scores are explored, including lp-norm and other methodologies.

5. **Score Comparison**: The criteria for score comparison can be based on human experience or automatic dynamic mechanisms.

6. **Stopping Criteria**: Various algorithms determine when to stop pruning, which can involve skipping pruned layers or dynamically selecting data paths.

7. **Training Decision Components**: The training of decision components can be done alongside the original network or through reinforcement learning for side networks.

The paper notes that while dynamic pruning can reduce computation and bandwidth usage, it introduces overhead as decisions about which elements to prune must be made at runtime. This necessitates a balance between the benefits of reduced computation and potential accuracy loss. The authors also mention conditional computing, which optimizes network activation by selectively activating parts of the network, thereby further reducing computation by effectively pruning inactive neurons.
