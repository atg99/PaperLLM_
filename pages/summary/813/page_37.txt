The paper presents a comprehensive survey on pruning and quantization techniques for neural networks, focusing on methods that enhance model efficiency while maintaining performance. It discusses various approaches to reducing model size and computational requirements through strategies like filter pruning, weight binarization, and low-precision training. Key references include advancements in methods such as Bi-Real Net, AutoPruner, and ThiNet, which leverage unique training algorithms and architectures to improve representational capabilities. 

The survey highlights the importance of these techniques in the context of deploying deep learning models on resource-constrained devices, such as FPGAs and edge devices. It also notes the growing need for models that can operate efficiently without significant loss in accuracy, thereby enabling broader application in real-world scenarios. Moreover, it examines recent trends in mixed precision training and ternary neural networks, which further push the boundaries of model compression and performance.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the significance of ongoing research in pruning and quantization, as well as the need for standardized frameworks to facilitate interoperability in machine learning applications.
