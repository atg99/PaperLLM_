The paper presents a survey on pruning and quantization techniques in neural networks, focusing on low precision libraries and their applications. Table 2 lists various low precision libraries that utilize quantization methods, highlighting their precision levels, methodologies, and platforms. Most libraries, including MACE, MKL-DNN, and TensorRT, employ INT8 precision for efficiency.

The paper discusses quantization-aware training (QAT) and post-training quantization (PTQ) as key methods for optimizing neural networks. It also emphasizes the importance of efficient kernels, noting that low precision inference is primarily executed on convolutional layers, with intermediate values often remaining in 32-bit floating-point format.

TensorRT, developed by nVidia, is emphasized for its high-performance inference capabilities on GPUs. It optimizes pre-trained models by converting them into 8-bit or 16-bit representations, utilizing a calibration set to adjust quantization thresholds and employing max-abs quantization to minimize storage and computation time.

Intel MKL-DNN is highlighted as a library optimized for Intel processors, supporting both FP32 and 8-bit inference in convolutional layers. It uses similar quantization techniques, including nearest integer rounding and max-abs quantization, to enhance performance.

The paper concludes that applying methods like KL-divergence calibration can significantly reduce accuracy loss during quantization, as demonstrated by minimal accuracy degradation in tested models. This survey underscores the critical role of low precision libraries in accelerating neural network processing through specialized algorithms and hardware.
