The paper discusses advances in pruning and quantization techniques for neural networks, particularly focusing on their efficiency and performance. Spatially Adaptive Computation Time (SACT) enhances residual networks by implementing an early stop mechanism, achieving around 20% acceleration without accuracy loss for ResNet-101 on the ILSVRC-2012 dataset. Multi-Scale Dense Networks (MSDNets) further improve early exit predictions and computational efficiency, yielding a 2.2x speed-up for ResNet-50 while maintaining accuracy.

To tackle the complexity in training adaptive networks, methods such as gradient equilibrium (GE) and Inline Subnetwork Collaboration (ISC) are proposed. GE mitigates gradient imbalance, while ISC improves predictions by integrating early outputs into later ones. Slimmable Neural Networks (SNN) allow dynamic width adjustments to balance accuracy and latency, solving training issues with a switchable batch normalization technique.

The paper highlights the challenges in comparing diverse pruning techniques, noting the importance of pre-trained weights. It emphasizes that while pruned models can be trained from scratch, structured pruning generally allows for better retraining outcomes compared to unstructured methods.

Quantization is defined as the process of approximating continuous signals using discrete symbols. Techniques such as k-means clustering for weight states are discussed, with low-bit quantization being emphasized for reducing model sizes and improving inference efficiency. Methods like post-training quantization and quantization-aware training are discussed, showcasing their effectiveness in retaining model accuracy while significantly decreasing storage requirements and computational costs.
