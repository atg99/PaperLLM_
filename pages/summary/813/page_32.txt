This paper presents a comprehensive survey on the techniques of pruning and quantization in neural networks, which are essential for optimizing model performance and efficiency. Pruning involves the removal of unnecessary weights or neurons in a network to reduce its size and computational requirements, while quantization aims to reduce the precision of the weights and activations, thereby lowering memory usage and speeding up inference.

The survey discusses various pruning methods, including magnitude-based pruning, where weights with the smallest absolute values are removed, and structured pruning, which eliminates entire neurons or filters. It highlights the trade-offs between model accuracy and efficiency, emphasizing the importance of maintaining performance post-pruning.

In terms of quantization, the paper explores techniques such as fixed-point representation and dynamic quantization, which can significantly decrease the model's memory footprint and improve execution speed on hardware with limited computational resources. It also addresses the challenges associated with quantizing deep learning models, such as the potential loss of accuracy.

The authors summarize key findings from numerous studies, providing insights into the current state of research and applications in pruning and quantization. They conclude by discussing future directions and the potential for integrating these techniques into real-world applications, especially in resource-constrained environments such as mobile and edge devices. This survey serves as a valuable resource for researchers and practitioners looking to enhance neural network efficiency through pruning and quantization strategies.
