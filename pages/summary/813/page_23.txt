This paper surveys methods and frameworks for pruning and quantization in neural networks, focusing on their implications for inference efficiency and accuracy. It highlights the limitations of current approaches, particularly the approximation errors introduced by rounding, which can reduce accuracy by approximately 1%. 

Key equations describe weight and activation quantization, where weights are quantized before inference, and activation factors are derived from validation datasets. Techniques such as Local Response Normalization and the use of non-negative activations are discussed to mitigate overfitting. 

TensorFlow-Lite is introduced as an open-source framework that supports both Post-Training Quantization (PTQ) and Quantization-Aware Training (QAT). The paper also details the Gemmlowp library, which employs asymmetric quantization to enhance numerical encoding and reduce computational load through optimized operations.

Ristretto, a tool for Caffe quantization, is characterized by its three-part strategy, including Dynamic Fixed Point (DFP) representation for variable ranges and efficient bit-shift operations to replace multiplication. The paper touches upon NCNN, a framework for mobile inference that employs channel-wise quantization, and Mobile AI Compute Engine (MACE) from Xiaomi, which emphasizes QAT for improved accuracy retention.

Overall, the paper presents a comprehensive overview of current methodologies for pruning and quantization, underscoring the trade-offs between computational efficiency and model accuracy.
