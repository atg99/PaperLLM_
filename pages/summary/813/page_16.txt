This paper surveys the methodologies of pruning and quantization in neural networks, focusing on their implementation and efficacy in reducing model size and improving inference speed. It discusses the process of integer quantized convolution, which mirrors traditional convolution operations but utilizes quantized values for weights and features, allowing for significant acceleration with support from third-party libraries. The de-quantization process is also outlined, converting quantized values back to floating-point format using feature and weight scales.

The paper highlights that consecutive layers can operate with quantized parameters, enabling efficient de-quantization and preserving numerical precision through careful placement of activation functions. It addresses the role of bias in neural networks, suggesting that while biases can be quantized, maintaining them in full precision is often recommended due to minimal memory impact.

Quantization approaches are categorized into post-training quantization (PTQ) and quantization-aware training (QAT), with emphasis on low bit-width quantization techniques. The use of half-precision floating-point (FP16) is acknowledged for its minimal accuracy loss in various hardware accelerators. The study references successful applications of 8-bit integer quantization during both training and inference with only slight accuracy degradation.

Additionally, it explores Integer Arithmetic-only Inference (IAI), a practical quantization scheme that enhances efficiency through the use of standard data types while managing trade-offs between accuracy and latency. The paper notes the effectiveness of fixed-point formats and dynamic scaled fixed-point representations, which can offer higher performance with specialized hardware.

Overall, this survey provides a comprehensive overview of current quantization methodologies, their practical implications, and ongoing challenges in training quantized networks.
