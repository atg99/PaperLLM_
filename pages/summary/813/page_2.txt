This paper provides a comprehensive survey on the acceleration of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) through pruning and quantization techniques, categorized into three main areas: structure design, further optimization, and specialized hardware. 

Parameter factorization is highlighted as a method that simplifies memory access and compresses model size by decomposing higher-rank tensors into lower-rank ones, applicable to both convolutional and fully connected layers. Network pruning is discussed as a technique for removing parameters that do not significantly affect network accuracy, with various methods explored in detail. Network quantization is addressed, focusing on the replacement of higher precision data types with reduced width types to maintain information integrity, with extensive discussion on various quantization methods.

The paper also reviews hardware accelerators designed specifically for network acceleration, including CPUs, GPUs, ASICs, and FPGAs. Each platform's capabilities and optimizations for neural networks are summarized, noting that CPUs are typically used for inference, while GPUs are utilized for both training and inference. Specialized ASICs are aimed at edge inference applications, and FPGAs are favored for their flexibility and lower design cycles.

The authors summarize their contributions as follows: a review of pruning and quantization techniques, classification of pruning methods into static and dynamic categories, and a quantitative analysis of current state-of-the-art quantization approaches. The paper emphasizes the importance of these techniques in enhancing the efficiency of CNNs while noting that hardware implementation details are beyond its primary focus.
