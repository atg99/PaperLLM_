This paper provides a comprehensive survey on pruning and quantization techniques in neural networks. It highlights that quantization typically reduces data representation to 8-bit integers, with lower bitwidth quantizations, such as binary neural networks, presenting challenges for retraining due to non-differentiability. The key benefits of quantization include enhanced performance, often achieving 2-3 times improvement, and significantly lowered storage requirements. The authors also discuss methodologies for implementing quantization and review popular libraries and frameworks that facilitate this process, including a comparative analysis of accuracy across various networks. 

Pruning is defined as the process of eliminating redundant computations that contribute minimally to the output, while quantization focuses on lessening computation demands by decreasing data precision. Both techniques can be applied independently or in tandem to optimize storage and accelerate inference in neural networks.
