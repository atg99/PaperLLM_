The paper provides a comprehensive survey on pruning and quantization techniques for deep neural networks, highlighting their significance in optimizing model performance and efficiency. Pruning reduces the size of models by eliminating unnecessary parameters, while quantization lowers the precision of weights, facilitating faster computation and reduced memory usage. The authors discuss various methods of pruning, such as structured and unstructured approaches, and the impact of these techniques on model accuracy and inference speed. 

Quantization strategies are also reviewed, including fixed-point and mixed-precision methods, which allow models to operate efficiently on hardware with limited computational resources. The paper emphasizes the importance of hardware-aware approaches that align model optimization with specific platform capabilities, enhancing the deployment of neural networks on mobile and embedded systems.

Key references are provided, illustrating advancements in both fields, including recent frameworks and tools designed to facilitate these optimizations. The survey concludes by stressing the ongoing need for research to balance model performance with efficiency, particularly as deep learning applications continue to expand.
