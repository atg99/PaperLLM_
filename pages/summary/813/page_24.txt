This paper surveys the advancements in pruning and quantization techniques for neural network inference, particularly emphasizing batch calibration of input data. It discusses MACE, which is optimized for ARM NEON and Qualcomm’s Hexagon DSP, and supports OpenCL acceleration. The paper introduces QNNPACK, an open-source library from Facebook tailored for low-precision neural network inference on mobile devices, which employs similar quantization methods as TensorFlow Lite, including zero-point usage. QNNPACK enhances performance through its Panel Dotproduct (PDOT) feature, which computes multiple dot products in parallel, resulting in significant speedups for MobileNet.

Paddle also implements quantization techniques, utilizing zero-points for both Quantization-Aware Training (QAT) and Post-Training Quantization (PTQ). It introduces a quantize-dequantize-convolution pipeline, termed simu-quantize, which maintains data in floating-point format during operations, thus facilitating backpropagation. Paddle employs various methods to quantize parameters based on max-absolute values, achieving a notable reduction in model size with minimal accuracy loss.

The paper further explores hardware platforms for implementing quantized networks, focusing on low-precision general and specialized hardware. It notes that FPGAs are commonly used for networks with less than 8-bit precision, with frameworks like FINN designed for Binarized Neural Networks (BNNs) on FPGAs achieving impressive throughput on datasets like MNIST. Additionally, the paper discusses techniques to enhance inference efficiency for models like Tiny-YOLO, including architectural modifications.

Overall, the survey provides a comprehensive overview of the state-of-the-art techniques and hardware implementations in the field of pruning and quantization for neural network inference.
