The paper presents a comprehensive survey on pruning and quantization techniques aimed at enhancing the efficiency of deep convolutional neural networks (CNNs). Pruning involves removing unnecessary weights or neurons from a network to reduce its size and computational demands while maintaining performance. Various methods are discussed, including filter pruning via geometric median and channel pruning, which specifically target the architecture of CNNs to optimize their operation.

Quantization, on the other hand, refers to the process of reducing the precision of the weights and activations in a neural network. This technique allows for efficient integer-arithmetic-only inference, which is beneficial for deploying models on resource-constrained devices. The paper highlights advancements in quantization strategies, including loss-aware binarization and training methodologies that aim to minimize performance degradation.

Key contributions from notable works in the field are cited, showcasing a range of approaches to both pruning and quantization. The survey underscores the significance of these methods in making deep learning models more accessible and efficient, particularly for mobile and embedded applications.

Overall, the paper serves as a valuable resource for researchers and practitioners seeking to understand the current landscape of pruning and quantization techniques, their implementations, and their impact on deep neural network performance.
