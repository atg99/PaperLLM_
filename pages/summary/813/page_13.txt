This paper surveys advancements in pruning and quantization techniques for neural networks focusing on training and inference optimizations. It discusses how conditional computing, similar to reinforcement learning (RL), helps in learning patterns to achieve computational efficiency. An approach by Bengio divides networks into blocks and formulates their selection as an RL problem, achieving a significant speed-up on the CIFAR-10 dataset without losing accuracy.

Adaptive networks are highlighted for their ability to accelerate inference by allowing early exits based on accuracy thresholds. Cascade networks, a type of adaptive network, enable early exits without computing all output layers, although they require manual hyper-parameter optimization, which can lead to challenges in automation and classification accuracy. The paper notes that a cascade network with an RL-trained Composer significantly outperforms baseline networks in accuracy while maintaining efficiency.

The BlockDrop framework enhances early exit mechanisms by enabling decisions on residual network blocks, achieving a notable speed-up without accuracy loss. Meanwhile, Runtime Neural Pruning (RNP) dynamically prunes networks based on a Markov Decision Process framework, yielding substantial reductions in execution time with minimal accuracy loss.

Differentiable methods are introduced to reduce training complexity in neural networks. Techniques like Dynamic Channel Pruning (DCP) utilize channel thresholds for pruning and have shown promising results in terms of computation speed and accuracy retention. The paper also references a cascading neural network approach that effectively reduces inference time while maintaining a balance between accuracy and efficiency.

Overall, the survey provides a comprehensive overview of various strategies to enhance neural network performance through pruning and quantization, emphasizing the trade-offs between computational efficiency and model accuracy.
