This paper surveys various techniques in pruning and quantization, particularly focusing on post-training quantization (PTQ) and quantization-aware training (QAT). It discusses the evolution of quantization methods, emphasizing the significance of KL-divergence as a measure of relative entropy between original and quantized data distributions. The paper highlights the benefits of calibrated quantization, which can enhance network acceleration without compromising accuracy, and notes that fine-tuning may further improve results.

Quantization research is categorized into QAT and PTQ based on whether the training process is considered. Additionally, quantization can be classified by data grouping methods, such as layer-wise and channel-wise approaches, as well as by the bit-width of quantized parameters. However, the anticipated speedup from reduced precision techniques is often not realized fully—INT8 inference typically achieves only a 2-3 times speedup over 32-bit floating point due to the overhead from quantization operations.

The paper introduces quantization algebra, which provides a mathematical framework for quantizing neural networks. Key equations define the quantization process, including the clamp function and methods for determining quantization bounds. The min-max and max-abs methods are elaborated upon, detailing how they affect the quantization of input features, weights, and biases.

Overall, the paper provides a comprehensive overview of the current state of quantization techniques, their mathematical foundations, and their implications for neural network performance.
