This paper provides a comprehensive survey on pruning and quantization techniques for optimizing convolutional neural networks (CNNs). It is structured into several sections, beginning with an introduction to neural networks and specifically CNNs, followed by a discussion on network optimization techniques.

Section 2 introduces CNNs, which are a type of deep neural network that utilizes convolution operations to extract features from various data sources. The section outlines the two phases of CNN deployment: training and inference. Key concepts such as coefficients, parameters, hyperparameters, activations, features, kernels, filters, and weights are defined to clarify terminology.

Section 3 focuses on static and dynamic pruning techniques, which are methods for reducing the size of neural networks by removing less important weights or neurons. Section 4 discusses quantization, explaining its impact on model accuracy and comparing various quantization libraries and frameworks. Quantized accuracy results for common networks are also presented.

The paper emphasizes that pruning and quantization techniques can help overcome constraints related to memory and processing power, especially for inference on mobile and embedded devices. The authors provide practical guidance on the application of these techniques, recommending their use for different tasks such as object detection and natural language processing.

Conclusions are drawn in Section 5, and further insights are provided in Section 7, reinforcing the significance of optimizing CNNs to enhance their performance in diverse applications.
