The paper provides a comprehensive survey on network pruning and quantization, focusing on the categorization and implementation of pruning techniques. It differentiates between static and dynamic pruning, where static pruning is conducted offline before inference, and dynamic pruning occurs during runtime. The authors categorize modern pruning techniques based on various aspects, including structured versus unstructured pruning, neuron versus connection pruning, and the granularity of pruning opportunities, which can range from element-wise to layer-wise.

Mathematically, pruning is represented as a function that modifies the neural network while assessing performance loss compared to the unpruned version. The discussion on static pruning emphasizes its three main components: the selection of parameters to prune, the method of pruning, and the optional fine-tuning or retraining process to enhance the pruned network's performance.

The paper highlights that due to network redundancy, certain neurons and connections can be removed with minimal impact on accuracy. It mentions brute-force pruning as a method for network reduction, although it is computationally intensive. The authors propose using metrics like the lp-norm to guide the pruning process effectively.

Overall, the survey aims to provide insights into the latest advancements in pruning techniques, their mathematical foundations, and their implications for improving neural network efficiency.
