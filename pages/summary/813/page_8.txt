The paper discusses pruning and quantization techniques in deep neural networks (DNNs), focusing on mathematical formulations and various methods. It begins with the mathematical description of pruning and quantization through Equation 5, which includes l1 and l2 norms, known as the Manhattan and Euclidean norms, respectively. These norms are associated with LASSO and Ridge regularization techniques. The paper highlights the differences between pruned and unpruned tensors using the l2-distance metric and introduces the l0-norm, representing the count of nonzero elements in a vector.

The discussion advances to l2-LASSO regularization, represented by Equation 6, which involves minimizing a loss function with an added penalty for nonzero coefficients. The Lagrangian form in Equation 7 simplifies this concept, translating the objective function and constraint into a manageable format.

Two main pruning methods are explored: magnitude-based and penalty-based pruning. Magnitude-based pruning relies on the principle that larger weights are more significant, leading to the removal of unimportant weights or features. Historical methods such as Optimal Brain Damage (OBD) and Optimal Brain Surgeon (OBS) utilize the Hessian matrix of the loss function to identify and prune less essential weights, achieving considerable reductions in network parameters while maintaining accuracy.

The paper notes the limitations of Hessian-based methods in modern DNNs due to their complexity, particularly in large networks like GPT-3. Consequently, simpler magnitude-based pruning techniques have gained traction.

Filter-wise pruning, which employs the l1-norm to eliminate filters that do not impact classification accuracy, is also discussed. This approach has demonstrated significant reductions in inference costs with slight improvements in accuracy for models like VGG-16 and ResNet-110.

Finally, the paper mentions that while most pruning methods focus on weights, activations can also guide pruning decisions. The Average Percentage Of Zeros (APoZ) metric is introduced to evaluate the contribution of activation maps, particularly in the context of rectified linear units (ReLU), which often yield high zero activation rates, making them suitable for pruning.
