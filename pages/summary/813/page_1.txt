The paper "Pruning and Quantization for Deep Neural Network Acceleration: A Survey" by Tailin Liang et al. discusses the challenges associated with deploying deep neural networks (DNNs) in real-time applications due to their computational complexity and resource demands. To address these issues, the authors explore network compression techniques, specifically pruning and quantization, which can reduce the size and computational requirements of DNNs while maintaining or even enhancing accuracy.

Pruning is classified into static (offline) and dynamic (runtime) categories, with a focus on various techniques for removing redundant computations. The paper examines the trade-offs involved in different pruning strategies, such as element-wise, channel-wise, shape-wise, filter-wise, layer-wise, and network-wise pruning.

Quantization, on the other hand, involves reducing the precision of data types, typically converting weights, biases, and activations into lower bit representations, often down to 8-bit integers. The authors also discuss lower bit-width implementations, including binary neural networks. Both pruning and quantization can be utilized independently or in conjunction.

The paper provides a comparative analysis of current techniques, evaluating their strengths and weaknesses, and presents compressed network accuracy results across various frameworks. Furthermore, it offers practical guidance for effectively compressing neural networks, emphasizing the importance of optimizing network structure, architecture, and hardware accelerator design to mitigate over-parameterization.

In conclusion, the survey aims to present a comprehensive overview of strategies for accelerating DNNs, highlighting the potential for significant reductions in computational cost and energy consumption without sacrificing performance.
