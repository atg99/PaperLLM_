This document presents a survey on pruning and quantization techniques in deep learning, highlighting various significant works in the field. Pruning involves reducing the size of neural networks by removing weights or neurons that contribute little to the output, while quantization focuses on reducing the precision of the weights and activations to lower computational costs.

Key contributions include Chellapilla et al.'s exploration of convolutional neural networks for document processing, Chen et al.'s introduction of AdderNet to minimize multiplications in deep learning, and the development of TVM, an optimizing compiler for deep learning. Additionally, techniques such as the hashing trick for compressing neural networks and the DianNao family of energy-efficient hardware accelerators are discussed.

Recent advancements in model compression and acceleration are summarized, including dynamic channel pruning and methods for training binary neural networks. The survey also addresses the challenge of maintaining performance while reducing model complexity and highlights the importance of mixed precision training and low-rank approximations.

Overall, the document underscores the growing need for efficient neural network architectures that balance performance and resource utilization, integrating insights from various studies to inform future research directions in model pruning and quantization.
