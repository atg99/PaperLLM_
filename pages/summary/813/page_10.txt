The paper discusses various techniques for pruning and quantization in neural networks, focusing on methods that reduce model size and improve efficiency without significantly compromising accuracy. 

Dropout is highlighted as a stochastic regularization technique that reduces parameters by randomly omitting neurons, thereby training multiple sub-networks. Although it increases training overhead, it does not impact inference time. Sparse variational dropout further enhances weight reduction in networks like VGG by identifying weights to prune based on dropout rates.

Norm-based pruning aims to eliminate zero values while maintaining a balance between retaining significant weights and reducing network size. However, challenges arise when small-valued parameters are crucial for performance, particularly in cases of saturation. Penalty-based pruning may also lead to accuracy loss by removing filters that appear redundant but are essential.

Several innovative pruning methods are described. For instance, Filter Pruning via Geometric Median (FPGM) uses L2 distances to identify filters for removal, achieving a reduction in FLOPs with minimal accuracy drop. The reduce and reuse method prunes filters with low variance, resulting in significant acceleration with a slight accuracy decrease.

Other approaches include iterative removal of redundant neurons in fully connected layers and the use of Determinantal Point Process (DPP) for categorizing and re-weighting neurons to mitigate information loss. ThiNet adopts a layer-wise greedy search for filter importance based on reconstruction costs, allowing for gradual pruning to maintain classification accuracy.

Dynamic inference-time channel pruning utilizes LASSO regression to estimate and prune channels, achieving notable acceleration with limited accuracy loss. However, it requires extensive calibration data.

The paper emphasizes that while pruning can effectively reduce computation, it often necessitates fine-tuning or retraining to recover accuracy lost during the pruning process. Techniques like deep compression illustrate static methods for pruning connections that do not contribute to classification, demonstrating the balance between efficiency and performance in neural networks.
