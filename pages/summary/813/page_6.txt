This paper surveys techniques related to pruning and quantization in neural networks, emphasizing the impact of batch normalization (BN) on these processes. When features in a channel share parameters, it simplifies to a linear transformation for each output channel. The parameters from BN can facilitate channel-wise pruning and enhance cluster-based quantization by reducing parameter dependencies. BN folding, which merges BN parameters with trained weights, can lower inference latency and computational complexity by minimizing the number of multiplications needed during inference.

Pooling techniques, introduced in the 1980s, reduce groups of values to a single value, either through averaging or selecting the maximum. While pooling can lead to a loss of spatial information, it helps in reducing computations. Global pooling methods like Global Average Pooling (GAP) can extract information from multi-channel features and can be integrated with dynamic pruning strategies. Capsule networks offer an alternative to pooling by using vectors to represent entities, preserving spatial information through dynamic routing algorithms.

The paper also discusses the growth in the number of parameters in popular neural networks, highlighting a trend of increasing complexity. For instance, AlexNet had 60 million parameters, while more recent models have exceeded 175 billion parameters. Although large networks can be deployed in data centers, resource-constrained environments necessitate reduced parameter models, such as GoogLeNet and MobileNet, which achieve competitive accuracy with significantly fewer parameters.

Network pruning is identified as a key technique for reducing memory size and bandwidth, allowing large networks to be compressed without retraining. Pruning targets redundant parameters or neurons with minimal contribution to accuracy, thus lowering computational complexity. The paper categorizes pruning research into sensitivity calculation and penalty-term methods, noting ongoing advancements in both areas.
