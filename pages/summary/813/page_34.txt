The paper provides a comprehensive survey on techniques for pruning and quantization in neural networks, which are essential for optimizing deep learning models for efficiency and performance. It discusses various methods and frameworks aimed at reducing the size and computational requirements of neural networks while maintaining their accuracy. 

Key concepts include:

1. **Pruning**: This involves the removal of unnecessary weights or neurons from the network. Techniques such as the Optimal Brain Surgeon and Dynamic Network Surgery are highlighted, which focus on minimizing performance loss during the reduction of network complexity.

2. **Quantization**: This refers to the process of reducing the precision of the weights and activations in a neural network. The survey covers methods like SYQ for symmetric quantization and differentiable soft quantization, which bridge the gap between full-precision and low-bit networks.

3. **Sparse Neural Networks**: The paper discusses the lottery ticket hypothesis, which posits that sparse networks can be as effective as their denser counterparts if they are properly initialized and trained.

4. **Resource-Efficient Inference**: Techniques such as MorphNet and Angel-Eye are explored, which focus on optimizing the deployment of neural networks on hardware platforms like FPGAs.

5. **Empirical Studies**: The survey includes references to frameworks like Ristretto that allow for empirical evaluation of resource-efficient inference methods.

The paper emphasizes the growing importance of these methods in the context of deploying deep learning models in resource-constrained environments, such as mobile devices and embedded systems. Overall, it serves as a valuable resource for researchers and practitioners looking to enhance the efficiency of deep neural networks through pruning and quantization techniques.
