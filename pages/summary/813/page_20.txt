The paper discusses the challenges and advancements in pruning and quantization techniques, particularly in the context of neural networks and the ILSVRC-2012 dataset. It highlights that weight regularization can enhance the accuracy of quantized networks by penalizing large weights, with experiments showing an 8-bit quantized MobileNet achieving a 0.23% accuracy improvement. Batch Normalization (BN) is noted for addressing internal covariate shift but may introduce numerical instabilities due to its nonlinear operations. To mitigate this, l1-norm BN utilizes linear operations, resulting in significant speedups on FPGA platforms.

The section on quantization-aware training emphasizes that traditional global quantization can lead to substantial accuracy loss, especially in binary and shallow networks. It cites specific accuracy losses for binary weights in ResNet-34 and GoogLeNet. However, retraining through backward propagation can recover accuracy losses incurred by quantization. 

The paper presents methods for training binarized networks, noting the limitations of gradient descent due to small derivatives in binary weight updates. Early approaches like Expectation Back Propagation (EBP) and BinaryConnect are mentioned, with the latter preserving floating-point weights for updates before quantization. The Straight-Through Estimator (STE) is introduced as a solution for propagating gradients through non-differentiable binary parameters.

DoReFa is highlighted as a successful approach that trains networks with low-bit gradients, achieving competitive results with k-bit quantization arithmetic. It quantizes weights, features, and gradients into multiple levels, improving dynamic range and maintaining reasonable accuracy with minimal inference acceleration. Lastly, the paper addresses the exploding gradient problem in BNNs, proposing a proximal Newton’s algorithm to optimize the loss concerning binary weights, which showed slight improvements on the MNIST dataset.
