The paper surveys recent advancements in pruning and quantization techniques to optimize deep neural networks, addressing the computational cost and resource efficiency necessary for real-time applications. It highlights the significance of quantization-aware training, which enhances accuracy by using calibration datasets and minimizing zero-point overheads. The paper advocates for 8-bit quantization as a practical compromise between accuracy and compression, emphasizing the importance of techniques like binarized networks, which have shown promising results with tailored hardware.

Key recommendations for improving post-quantization accuracy include utilizing asymmetrical quantization, prioritizing weight quantization over activation quantization, avoiding bias quantization, and implementing channel-wise quantization. Fine-tuning quantized models and initiating training with 32-bit floating-point models are also suggested to mitigate accuracy loss.

The paper identifies areas for future research, such as automatic compression methods to balance accuracy and compression ratios, exploring compression techniques across various neural network types beyond CNNs, and adapting hardware implementations to maximize the effectiveness of pruning algorithms. It also calls for global optimization methods that consider multiple layers simultaneously, enhancing overall compression efficiency.

In conclusion, the paper asserts that while deep neural networks excel in applications like computer vision, their complex architectures pose challenges for efficient deployment. Through effective pruning and quantization strategies, significant improvements in computational efficiency can be achieved with minimal accuracy loss, thus facilitating broader application of these networks in resource-constrained environments.
