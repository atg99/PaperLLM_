The paper evaluates the performance of Gradient Boosting Decision Trees with two different approaches: GBDT-SO and GBDT-MO. The experiments focus on two synthetic datasets, Friedman1 and random projection, and two real-world datasets, MNIST and Caltech101. 

In the synthetic experiments, 10,000 samples were generated for training and testing, with the Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) used for performance evaluation. Results show that GBDT-MO outperforms GBDT-SO in terms of generalization ability, as indicated by lower RMSE on test samples and a smaller performance gap between training and unseen samples. This suggests that GBDT-MO's learning mechanism is more effective at capturing variable correlations, although it suffers from slower convergence speed.

For the real-world datasets, the paper mentions the MNIST dataset, which consists of 28x28 grayscale images of handwritten digits, and the Yeast dataset, which includes eight input attributes for predicting protein localization sites. The evaluation on these datasets aims to compare the test performance and training speed of GBDT-MO against related methods.

Overall, the findings suggest that while GBDT-MO demonstrates superior generalization capabilities, it may require more time to converge compared to GBDT-SO.
