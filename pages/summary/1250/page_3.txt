The paper presents a detailed examination of Gradient Boosting Decision Trees (GBDT) tailored for multiple outputs (GBDT-MO) and discusses the computational strategies for efficient split finding in decision trees. The authors begin by illustrating the operation of GBDT-SO and GBDT-MO on a dataset with multiple output variables, emphasizing the need for efficient algorithms as the number of samples increases.

The authors delve into the mechanics of split finding, highlighting the traditional exact method’s inefficiency with large datasets. They propose approximate algorithms that utilize bucketing strategies, specifically histogram-based bucketing, which offers significant speed advantages over quantile-based methods. The paper details how samples are sorted into bins, allowing for rapid accumulation of statistical data necessary for computing optimal splits.

In discussing GBDT-MO, the authors formulate the learning objective using second-order Taylor expansion of loss, connecting it to single output objectives. They also introduce the concept of learning subsets of variables with L0 constraints. The paper concludes with an analysis of the complexity of the proposed algorithms, demonstrating their effectiveness in decision tree learning for multiple outputs.

Overall, the paper provides a comprehensive overview of GBDT techniques, particularly focusing on enhancing computational efficiency and extending GBDT to handle multiple outputs effectively.
