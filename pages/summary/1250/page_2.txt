The paper presents GBDT-MO, an extension of Gradient Boosted Decision Trees (GBDT) for multiple outputs. The authors argue that GBDT-MO improves generalization and training speed, particularly with large output dimensions. Key contributions include the formulation of a learning problem for multiple outputs, the introduction of a sparse constraint for output selection, and the adaptation of histogram approximation to enhance training efficiency. 

Section II reviews the standard single-output GBDT, detailing its workflow and objective derived from the second order Taylor expansion of the loss function, which is foundational for the subsequent extension to multiple outputs in Section III. The authors explain the significance of constructing decision trees based on gradients, the objective function for decision trees, and the split finding process, crucial for optimizing the decision tree's performance.

Overall, this work lays the groundwork for implementing GBDT in a multi-output context, addressing important aspects such as training efficiency and output selection, thereby expanding the applicability of GBDT in complex scenarios. The paper concludes with experimental results, demonstrating the advantages of GBDT-MO over traditional methods.
