The paper presents an analysis of various datasets used for classification and regression tasks, detailing their statistics in terms of training and testing samples, features, and output types. The datasets include MNIST, Yeast, Caltech101, MNIST-inpainting, Student-por, and NUS-WIDE, each with distinct characteristics and problem types.

Performance metrics on real-world datasets are compared for several machine learning models, including XGBoost, LightGBM, and different GBDT variants. The results indicate that GBDT-MO achieved the highest accuracy on MNIST, Yeast, Caltech101, and NUS-WIDE, while regression tasks on MNIST-inpainting and Student-por showed competitive RMSE values across the models.

Training time analysis reveals insights into the efficiency of these algorithms, particularly for sparse split finding, with varying parameters (k) affecting both accuracy and training duration. The results demonstrate that the choice of k significantly impacts model performance and computational efficiency.

Overall, the findings underscore the effectiveness of gradient boosting techniques in handling diverse datasets while highlighting the importance of model selection and parameter tuning in achieving optimal results.
