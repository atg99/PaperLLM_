The paper discusses the implementation and performance analysis of Gradient Boosted Decision Trees for multiple outputs (GBDT-MO), building on the existing GBDT framework. It highlights three different algorithms for split finding: non-sparse, sparse, and restricted sparse, which are illustrated with examples. The tree growth algorithm is detailed, specifying how nodes are selected based on objective gain and managed in memory to optimize performance.

The implementation is done in C++, leveraging multi-core parallelism through OpenMP to enhance speed. The authors analyze the training and inference complexities of GBDT-SO (single output) and GBDT-MO, noting that while both have similar complexities for split finding, GBDT-MO generally performs faster due to lower overhead in managing sample divisions. The paper provides complexity equations for various scenarios, including the use of exact Hessians, and compares the inference complexity, indicating that GBDT-SO requires more comparisons than GBDT-MO.

Overall, the work presents a robust framework for efficiently handling multiple outputs in gradient boosting, contributing to the field's ongoing advancements in model accuracy and computational efficiency.
