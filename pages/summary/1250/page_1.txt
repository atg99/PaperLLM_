In the paper "GBDT-MO: Gradient Boosted Decision Trees for Multiple Outputs," the authors Zhendong Zhang and Cheolkon Jung address a significant limitation of traditional Gradient Boosted Decision Trees (GBDT), which typically produce a single output variable per tree. This conventional approach fails to account for the correlations between multiple output variables, leading to redundancy in tree structures and suboptimal performance.

The proposed method, GBDT-MO, innovatively allows each leaf of the decision tree to predict multiple outputs or a subset of variables, thereby leveraging the correlations among them. This is accomplished by maximizing the summation of objective gains across all output variables during the learning process. The authors further enhance the training efficiency by extending histogram approximation to the multiple output scenario.

The paper presents experimental results on both synthetic and real-world datasets, demonstrating that GBDT-MO significantly improves accuracy and training speed compared to existing methods. The approach not only integrates the second-order gradient but also introduces an L0 constraint to automatically select relevant variables for prediction at each leaf, thereby optimizing the learning mechanism.

Overall, GBDT-MO represents a scalable and efficient advancement in the field of machine learning, particularly for applications requiring multiple outputs, such as multi-class classification and multi-label classification. The authors provide their code online for further research and application.
