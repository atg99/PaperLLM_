This paper presents algorithms for efficient split finding in decision trees when dealing with multiple outputs. It begins with the construction of histograms for each output variable using Algorithm 1, which accumulates gradient information across bins for a given column index. The histograms facilitate the identification of optimal splits by summarizing the data distribution.

Algorithm 2 outlines the process for approximate split finding, where the objective is to maximize the gain across all output dimensions. This approach overcomes inefficiencies associated with exhaustive enumeration of splits by utilizing histogram data to compute gains more rapidly.

The paper further introduces sparse split finding algorithms in Algorithms 3 and 4, which optimize the gain computation by leveraging top-k priority queues. These algorithms differentiate between non-sparse and restricted sparse scenarios, where the latter ensures that selected columns for the left and right child nodes overlap, thereby enhancing computational efficiency and promoting similarity between nodes.

Overall, the proposed methods aim to streamline the training process for multi-output decision trees, addressing the challenges of split finding in a high-dimensional output space while ensuring effective utilization of gradient statistics.
