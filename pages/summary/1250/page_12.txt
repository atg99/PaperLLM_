The paper presents an analysis of an approximated objective for optimizing weights in a machine learning context, specifically focusing on the relationship between the Hessian matrix and its diagonal elements. It establishes that the Hessian is dominated by its diagonal elements, leading to a simplified expression for the weight update. The optimal weight solution incorporates a learning rate, which allows for flexibility in practice without needing the exact value of a specific coefficient.

The paper also details hyper-parameter settings, including a grid search for maximum depth and learning rate, with specific values identified for optimal performance. L2 regularization is set to 1.0, and adjustments are made to the maximum leaves based on the depth. Additional hyper-parameters are fixed across experiments, with results summarized in tables.

Finally, the paper discusses the statistical validation of results across various datasets, utilizing random trials to estimate performance differences. It employs statistical tests to evaluate the confidence in the superiority of the proposed GBDT-MO method compared to others, concluding that the majority of confidence scores indicate a significant performance advantage.
