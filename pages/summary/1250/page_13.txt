This document presents several tables summarizing the results of a comparative analysis of different gradient boosting algorithms, specifically XGBoost, LightGBM, GBDT-sparse, GBDT-SO, and GBDT-MO, across multiple datasets including MNIST, Yeast, Caltech101, NUS-WIDE, MNIST-inpainting, and Student-por.

Table IX details the learning rates and maximum depths found through grid search for each algorithm across the datasets. The learning rates range from 0.05 to 0.25, while maximum depths vary from 4 to 10, indicating the model complexity used in each case.

Table X lists the number of trees in the models learned by each algorithm. XGBoost and LightGBM generally have the highest number of trees across most datasets, suggesting a more complex model, while GBDT-sparse shows significantly lower counts in some cases.

Table XI outlines the preliminarily searched hyper-parameters, including gain threshold, minimum samples in leaf, and maximum bins. These parameters are crucial for controlling overfitting and model performance.

Table XII provides confidence levels regarding the superiority of GBDT-MO over the other algorithms in specific datasets, indicating varying degrees of confidence in its performance, particularly high in the Caltech101 dataset.

Overall, the data suggests that GBDT-MO demonstrates competitive performance with varying confidence across different datasets, while XGBoost and LightGBM consistently show strong results in terms of the number of trees used.
