This paper presents a novel method called GBDT-MO designed for learning Gradient Boosted Decision Trees (GBDT) with multiple outputs. The approach aims to leverage the correlations between output variables while minimizing redundancy in tree structures. The authors derive an approximated learning objective for both sparse and non-sparse cases, utilizing the second-order Taylor expansion of the loss function.

The methodology includes two algorithms for sparse split finding: a restricted version that enforces uniformity in the subsets of left and right splits, and an unrestricted version without such constraints. The paper extends histogram approximation to the multiple output context to enhance training efficiency.

The authors evaluate GBDT-MO across various datasets, including MNIST, MNIST-inpainting, Caltech101, and NUS-WIDE, demonstrating that GBDT-MO consistently outperforms traditional single-output GBDT in terms of generalization ability and training speed. Statistical tests confirm the robustness of GBDT-MO's performance across different training and testing splits.

The findings indicate that while GBDT-MO achieves better accuracy and faster training times, it exhibits slower convergence, particularly in early training stages. The paper concludes with plans for future work aimed at enhancing convergence speed. Overall, GBDT-MO represents a significant advancement in GBDT methodologies for multi-output tasks.
