This paper evaluates the performance of Segmenter compared to DeepLabv3+ using a ResNeSt backbone, focusing on object size and decoder variants. The findings indicate that Segmenter shows significant improvements, particularly for large instances, with Seg-L-Mask/16 achieving a 6.39% enhancement over DeepLab. The analysis of decoder variants reveals that the mask transformer consistently outperforms the linear baseline, with gains of up to 1.6% for Seg-B†/16 and notable improvements for large objects.

When comparing Segmenter to fully convolutional networks (FCNs) and DeepLabv3+, the transformer approach demonstrates superior global scene understanding, particularly excelling with large instances. However, DeepLab performs comparably to Seg-B/16 for small and medium instances despite similar parameter counts. The performance evaluation shows that Segmenter offers a clear advantage in terms of speed and accuracy compared to state-of-the-art methods, especially with Seg-B-Mask/16, which balances good performance with computational efficiency.

The paper also emphasizes the importance of large datasets for training transformers in semantic segmentation tasks. Results on the ADE20K dataset reveal that Seg-B†-Mask/16 outperforms all FCN methods, achieving a 50.08% mean Intersection over Union (mIoU), while the best model, Seg-L-Mask/16, reaches a state-of-the-art performance of 53.63%. In Pascal Context, the Seg-L/16 model also provides competitive results, showcasing the effectiveness of the mask transformer in enhancing performance across various datasets.
