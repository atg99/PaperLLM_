The paper investigates the performance of transformer-based segmentation models, specifically focusing on the Segmenter architecture on the ADE20K validation set. Key findings highlight the effectiveness of stochastic depth as a regularization technique, which consistently enhances model performance compared to dropout, which negatively impacts results. The analysis of transformer size reveals that increasing both the token dimension and the number of layers leads to significant improvements in mean Intersection over Union (mIoU). The largest model, Seg-L/16, achieves an mIoU of 50.71% using a simple decoding scheme. 

Furthermore, the study emphasizes the importance of patch size in semantic segmentation, indicating that smaller patch sizes improve accuracy while also increasing computational demands. A notable observation is that reducing the patch size from 32 to 16 yields a 5% increase in performance for Seg-B, with further enhancement at a patch size of 8. The segmentation maps demonstrate that smaller patch sizes lead to sharper boundaries and better capture of fine details in images. Overall, the findings suggest that transformer-based methods are highly effective for semantic segmentation, with potential for further optimization through careful tuning of model size and patch dimensions.
