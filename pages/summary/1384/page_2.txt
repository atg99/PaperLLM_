This paper addresses the limitations of traditional convolutional neural networks (CNNs) in semantic segmentation, particularly their reliance on local information, which can hinder performance due to the importance of global context. To overcome this, the authors propose a novel approach using a transformer architecture, specifically the Vision Transformer (ViT), to treat semantic segmentation as a sequence-to-sequence problem. This method captures contextual information at every stage, enhancing the model's ability to understand global interactions within an image.

The proposed Segmenter model employs linear patch embeddings as input tokens for a transformer encoder, which processes these tokens and generates contextualized sequences. The decoding phase can utilize either a simple linear mapping or a transformer-based scheme to produce class masks. The authors highlight the effectiveness of their method through extensive experimentation, demonstrating that it surpasses previous state-of-the-art convolutional approaches on benchmarks such as the ADE20K dataset, achieving a mean Intersection over Union (IoU) of 53.63%.

The paper outlines four main contributions: 
1. A convolution-free semantic segmentation approach using ViT that captures global context and outperforms traditional methods.
2. A family of models offering a balance between precision and runtime.
3. A transformer-based decoder that improves class mask generation.
4. State-of-the-art results on multiple datasets, showcasing the competitive performance of the proposed method.

In summary, the work signifies a shift towards transformer-based architectures in semantic segmentation, highlighting their advantages in capturing global context effectively.
