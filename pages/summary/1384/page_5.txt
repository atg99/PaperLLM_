The paper investigates the impact of patch size on segmentation performance in models initialized with improved Vision Transformer (ViT) architectures. It highlights that models with different patch sizes, such as 32x32, 16x16, and 8x8, yield varying mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) scores, with the best performance observed at 8x8 patch size. The models are initialized using pre-trained weights from public libraries and fine-tuned for semantic segmentation tasks at higher resolutions. The training process employs standard data augmentation techniques, including mean subtraction and random resizing, to enhance model robustness.

Optimization strategies involve using pixel-wise cross-entropy loss and stochastic gradient descent with a polynomial learning rate decay. The experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of different regularization methods, with stochastic depth outperforming dropout in transformer training for segmentation tasks. Performance metrics across various model configurations reveal that smaller patch sizes and larger model backbones generally lead to improved segmentation accuracy. The paper concludes with an ablation study that explores various model parameters and their contributions to segmentation performance, emphasizing the advantages of the Segmenter models over traditional convolutional approaches.
