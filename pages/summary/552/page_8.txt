The paper introduces BayesNAS, a method designed for efficient neural architecture search (NAS) that learns sparse network architectures. It demonstrates competitive performance in image classification tasks on ImageNet, particularly in mobile settings. The results are presented in Table 2, comparing BayesNAS with various state-of-the-art models, highlighting its test error rates and parameter efficiency.

BayesNAS employs a tree-cell structure that allows for multiple paths within a single cell, which can either be chain-like or inception-style. This flexibility enhances the model's ability to adapt to different architectures during the search phase. The search process is notably efficient, requiring only one epoch to identify candidate architectures, significantly reducing the search cost compared to traditional methods.

The paper also discusses the training settings and transferability of the learned architecture from CIFAR-10 to ImageNet, achieving competitive results with a reduced number of parameters. Although there is a slight increase in test error compared to previous methods, the reduction in parameters is advantageous for both training and inference.

In conclusion, BayesNAS shows promise for efficient neural architecture search, with potential for further improvement by optimizing Hessian computation. Future work may involve enhancing implementation efficiency and scaling the approach to larger search spaces.
