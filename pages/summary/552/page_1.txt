In the paper "BayesNAS: A Bayesian Approach for Neural Architecture Search," the authors propose a novel method for Neural Architecture Search (NAS) that addresses key limitations of existing one-shot NAS approaches. They identify two main issues: the neglect of dependencies between nodes in the architecture and the questionable efficacy of pruning architecture parameters based solely on their magnitudes. To overcome these challenges, the authors employ a Bayesian learning framework using hierarchical automatic relevance determination (HARD) priors.

The proposed method allows for the training of an over-parameterized network for just one epoch before updating the architecture. This approach significantly reduces search time, enabling the identification of optimal architectures for the CIFAR-10 dataset in a mere 0.2 GPU days using a single GPU. Furthermore, the authors demonstrate that competitive performance can be achieved when transferring the learned architectures to more complex datasets like ImageNet.

Additionally, the BayesNAS method can be utilized for compressing convolutional neural networks by enforcing structural sparsity, resulting in extremely sparse networks without sacrificing accuracy. The paper highlights that while one-shot NAS reduces computational demands compared to reinforcement learning and neuro-evolutionary methods, it still faces practical challenges that BayesNAS effectively addresses.
