The paper presents BayesNAS, an efficient method for neural architecture search (NAS) that employs Bayesian learning principles. It utilizes Laplace approximation for estimating the Hessian of the log-likelihood, which is crucial for optimizing network architectures. This approach is favored due to its ease of implementation, versatility across modern neural network structures, and its relationship with network compression techniques. 

The optimization process focuses on removing redundant edges in the architecture. The cost function is defined as the maximum likelihood of the data with regularization, balancing the importance of data fit and model complexity. The BayesNAS algorithm is structured into five key steps: training the weights and architecture parameters, computing the Hessian, updating architecture-related variables, pruning under a new entropy-based metric, and retraining the pruned network.

The paper also outlines the algorithm's adaptability to different scenarios, such as proxy tasks and network compression with structural sparsity. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of BayesNAS in both proxy and proxyless NAS settings, particularly in identifying optimal convolutional cells for networks trained on CIFAR-10. The detailed calculation procedures and algorithmic steps are provided in the appendices.
