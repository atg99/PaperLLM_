The paper presents BayesNAS, a novel approach for neural architecture search (NAS) that utilizes a Bayesian framework to optimize the design of convolutional neural networks. The study evaluates the performance of BayesNAS against state-of-the-art image classifiers on the CIFAR-10 dataset, as detailed in Table 1. The results indicate that BayesNAS achieves competitive test error rates, with the most notable performance being a test error of 2.41% with a parameter count of 3.4 million and a minimal search cost of 0.1 GPU days.

BayesNAS employs a search strategy that differs from existing methods like DARTS and SNAS by utilizing a performance evaluation criterion during the search process, enabling one-shot architecture discovery. The architecture consists of convolutional cells that are stacked to form a network, with normal and reduction cells designed to optimize feature map resolutions. The search space and training settings are aligned with the methodologies of DARTS and SNAS, but BayesNAS excludes zero operations to streamline the architecture.

The experiments demonstrate that BayesNAS can efficiently discover convolutional structures with fewer parameters compared to DARTS and SNAS, validating its effectiveness in the proxyless search context. The paper concludes that BayesNAS provides a robust framework for architecture search while maintaining competitive performance metrics, indicating its potential for broader applications in deep learning tasks.
