The paper provides a comprehensive survey of pre-trained models (PTMs) in natural language processing (NLP), detailing various tasks and methodologies involved in their development and application. 

Initially, it discusses the RTD approach, which addresses the mismatch problem by allowing the network to see masked tokens during pre-training but not during fine-tuning. It also introduces WKLM, which replaces entity mentions at the entity level, training models to identify whether an entity has been substituted.

Next, the paper elaborates on the Next Sentence Prediction (NSP) task, which trains models to determine whether two sentences are consecutive. However, the effectiveness of NSP has been challenged in subsequent research, with findings suggesting that single-sentence training often outperforms sentence-pair training that includes NSP.

To improve sentence coherence modeling, the paper highlights the Sentence Order Prediction (SOP) task used in ALBERT, which replaces NSP. SOP utilizes consecutive segments from the same document, enhancing performance on downstream tasks compared to the NSP approach.

The paper further mentions other auxiliary pre-training tasks aimed at integrating factual knowledge and enhancing multilingual and multi-modal capabilities.

A taxonomy of PTMs is presented, categorizing them based on representation type, architecture, pre-training task types, and extensions. This classification helps clarify how different models relate to one another.

Lastly, the paper emphasizes the importance of understanding the types of knowledge captured by PTMs, including both linguistic and world knowledge, as evidenced by various studies on non-contextual and contextual embeddings. It highlights that static word embeddings can reveal linguistic regularities and relationships between words through vector offsets.
