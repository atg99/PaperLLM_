The paper titled "Pre-trained Models for Natural Language Processing: A Survey" by QIU XP et al. provides an extensive overview of various pre-trained models and their applications in natural language processing (NLP). The authors discuss the evolution of language models, focusing on the integration of knowledge into these models to enhance their performance.

Key concepts include the exploration of whether language models can function as knowledge bases, with references to studies like Petroni et al. that investigate the factual knowledge embedded in models such as BERT. The survey highlights efforts to improve models by incorporating external knowledge, as seen in works like K-adapter and SenseBERT.

Furthermore, the paper addresses multilingual capabilities of models, citing research on multilingual BERT and its effectiveness across languages. There is also a focus on multimodal models that combine text and visual data, such as UniViLM and UNITER, emphasizing the growing trend of cross-modal pre-training.

The authors systematically compile findings from various studies, underscoring the importance of knowledge representation and integration in advancing NLP tasks, including reading comprehension and commonsense reasoning. The survey serves as a comprehensive resource for understanding the current landscape of pre-trained models in NLP and their implications for future research and applications.
