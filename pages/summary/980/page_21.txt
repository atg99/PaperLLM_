The paper discusses the future directions for pre-trained models (PTMs) in natural language processing (NLP), highlighting five key areas for improvement. 

Firstly, the upper bound of PTMs has not yet been reached, with potential for enhancement through increased training steps, larger corpora, and deeper model architectures. However, this requires sophisticated training techniques to manage the associated costs.

Secondly, while the Transformer architecture has been effective, it faces computational complexity issues, particularly with longer input sequences. There is a need for improved Transformer designs or alternative architectures to enhance efficiency and capture longer contextual information.

Thirdly, task-oriented pre-training and model compression are essential, as different downstream tasks may require specific pre-training approaches. The challenge lies in efficiently utilizing large PTMs in scenarios with limited resources, necessitating tailored architectures and compression techniques.

Fourthly, knowledge transfer methods beyond fine-tuning should be explored, as current fine-tuning approaches are parameter-inefficient. Solutions like adaptation modules and knowledge distillation could enable shared PTMs to serve multiple tasks.

Lastly, the interpretability and reliability of PTMs are critical concerns. The complex architecture of PTMs makes decision-making processes opaque, and while efforts have been made to analyze their internal workings, challenges remain. Additionally, the vulnerability of PTMs to adversarial attacks raises significant concerns about their deployment in real-world applications.

Overall, these future directions aim to enhance the efficiency, adaptability, and transparency of PTMs in NLP.
