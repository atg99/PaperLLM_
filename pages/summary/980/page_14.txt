The paper provides a comprehensive survey of pre-trained models (PTMs) for natural language processing, focusing on various methods for compressing these models to enhance efficiency. Table 3 compares several compressed PTMs, detailing their architecture, loss functions, parameter counts, and performance on the GLUE benchmark. 

Key methods for model compression include quantization, knowledge distillation (KD), parameter sharing, and module replacing. For instance, Q-BERT and Q8BERT utilize quantization techniques, achieving significant reductions in model size while maintaining performance. ALBERT employs parameter sharing to reduce the number of parameters significantly.

Knowledge distillation is highlighted as a prevalent technique, where models like TinyBERT and MobileBERT leverage layer-to-layer distillation to transfer knowledge from larger teacher models to smaller student models. Additionally, the paper discusses the strategy of module replacing, exemplified by BERT-of-Theseus, which gradually substitutes larger modules with smaller ones, achieving a balance between size reduction and performance retention.

Early exit strategies are also explored, allowing models to terminate processing before completing all layers based on input characteristics, thereby improving inference speed. This approach has been adapted from computer vision to language models, with various methods proposed to optimize computation dynamically.

Overall, the survey emphasizes the ongoing efforts to optimize PTMs for practical applications, highlighting the balance between model size, computational efficiency, and task performance.
