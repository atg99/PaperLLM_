This paper surveys various pre-trained models for Natural Language Processing (NLP) and discusses the loss functions associated with different pre-training tasks. It provides a detailed overview of tasks such as Language Modeling (LM), Masked Language Modeling (MLM), Sequence-to-Sequence MLM (Seq2Seq MLM), Permuted Language Modeling (PLM), and others, highlighting their respective loss functions and methodologies.

The loss functions for these tasks include:

1. **Language Modeling (LM)**: Predicts the next token based on previous tokens.
2. **Masked Language Modeling (MLM)**: Involves masking tokens in a sequence and training the model to predict these tokens using the remaining context. This method, although effective, introduces a mismatch between pre-training and fine-tuning phases due to the use of a special [MASK] token.
3. **Sequence-to-Sequence MLM (Seq2Seq MLM)**: Utilizes an encoder-decoder architecture to predict masked tokens in a sequence, beneficial for tasks like question answering and summarization.
4. **Permuted Language Modeling (PLM)**: Addresses the absence of special tokens in downstream tasks by permuting the input sequence, thereby providing a more consistent training approach.

The paper also discusses enhancements to MLM, such as dynamic masking in RoBERTa, and various extensions like UniLM and SpanBERT, which incorporate additional structures and knowledge into pre-training tasks. These advancements aim to bridge the gap between pre-training and fine-tuning, enhancing the model's performance on downstream tasks.
