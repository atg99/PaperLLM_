The paper "Pre-trained Models for Natural Language Processing: A Survey" by QIU XP et al. provides a comprehensive overview of various pre-trained models (PTMs) used in natural language processing (NLP). It details the architectures, input types, pre-training tasks, training corpora, parameters, GLUE benchmark scores, and fine-tuning applicability of representative PTMs, including ELMo, GPT, BERT, RoBERTa, and others.

Key findings indicate that different PTMs excel in various linguistic and world knowledge tasks. For instance, BERT demonstrates strong performance in syntactic tasks like part-of-speech tagging but shows limitations in semantic and fine-grained syntactic tasks. The paper highlights the ability of BERT to encode syntactic structures and subject-verb agreement through probing tasks and analyses of its internal representations.

Additionally, the paper discusses the distinction between linguistic knowledge and world knowledge within PTMs. Linguistic knowledge is explored through tasks that reveal BERT's capabilities in syntax and semantics, while world knowledge is assessed using cloze statements to evaluate the model's understanding of external information. The findings suggest that PTMs like BERT not only capture linguistic properties but also store significant amounts of world knowledge derived from their training data. 

Overall, the survey emphasizes the evolution of PTMs, their architectures, and their implications for advancing NLP research and applications.
