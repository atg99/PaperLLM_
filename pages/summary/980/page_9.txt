The paper "Pre-trained Models for Natural Language Processing: A Survey" by QIU XP et al. presents a comprehensive overview of pre-trained models (PTMs) in the field of natural language processing (NLP). It categorizes these models based on their contextualization, architecture, pre-training tasks, tuning strategies, extensions, and model compression techniques.

1. **Contextual vs Non-Contextual Models**: Non-contextual models include CBOW, Skip-Gram, and GloVe, while contextual models encompass ELMo, GPT, and BERT.

2. **Architectures**: The paper highlights various architectures, including LSTM and Transformer models. Notable examples of Transformer encoders are BERT, SpanBERT, and RoBERTa, while GPT and its successors are identified as Transformer decoders.

3. **Pre-Training Tasks**: The authors discuss different tasks used for pre-training, such as supervised machine translation, unsupervised language modeling, masked language modeling (MLM), and sequence-to-sequence tasks.

4. **Tuning Strategies**: The survey outlines tuning strategies like fine-tuning, prompt-tuning (both discrete and continuous), and multi-task fine-tuning.

5. **Extensions**: It also covers extensions of PTMs that enhance knowledge incorporation, multilingual capabilities, language-specific adaptations, and multi-modal applications.

6. **Model Compression**: Techniques for model compression are examined, including model pruning, quantization, parameter sharing, distillation, and early exit strategies.

The paper effectively categorizes and illustrates the evolution and diversity of pre-trained models, providing a valuable resource for researchers and practitioners in NLP.
