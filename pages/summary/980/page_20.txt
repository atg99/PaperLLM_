The paper discusses various advancements in the application of pre-trained models (PTMs) in natural language processing (NLP), specifically focusing on machine translation (NMT), summarization, and adversarial attacks and defenses.

In the context of NMT, several studies have explored the integration of BERT into encoder architectures. Notably, Imamura and Sumita introduced a two-stage fine-tuning method where the encoder is first initialized with BERT and only the decoder is trained initially, followed by joint fine-tuning of the entire model, resulting in improved performance over one-stage methods. Zhu et al. proposed using BERT as supplementary memory to enhance NMT models, leading to improvements across various training paradigms.

The MASS framework employs a masked sequence-to-sequence pre-training approach that jointly trains the encoder and decoder, outperforming BERT-style pre-training in specific tasks. mBART extends this concept to a multilingual context, demonstrating significant enhancements in both supervised and unsupervised translation tasks.

In summarization, the paper highlights the shift towards document-level models and the introduction of tasks like Gap Sentence Generation to enhance summarization capabilities. BERTSUM is presented as a notable framework that integrates a document-level encoder for both extractive and abstractive summarization, achieving state-of-the-art results on benchmark datasets.

The section on adversarial attacks reveals the vulnerabilities of deep neural models to crafted adversarial examples. Research has shown that BERT-based models can be misled by such examples, prompting the development of effective attack strategies like BERT-Attack. The paper also emphasizes the potential for adversarial defenses, including adversarial training, to bolster the robustness of PTMs against attacks, enhancing generalization and resilience in NLP applications.

Overall, the survey illustrates the significant progress in leveraging pre-trained models for various NLP tasks and highlights the ongoing challenges and research opportunities in adversarial robustness.
