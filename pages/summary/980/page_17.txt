The paper by QIU XP et al. surveys pre-trained models (PTMs) for natural language processing, focusing on advancements in fine-tuning techniques and prompt-based tuning strategies. It highlights improvements made by Xu et al., who enhanced BERT's fine-tuning through self-ensemble and self-distillation, allowing better performance on downstream tasks without significant resource demands. The paper discusses gradual unfreezing methods for PTMs, emphasizing the progressive unfreezing of layers to optimize learning.

Prompt-based tuning is presented as a means to bridge pre-training and fine-tuning gaps, showcasing its effectiveness in few-shot, zero-shot, and fully-supervised settings. The paper categorizes prompt-based methods into discrete and continuous prompts. Discrete prompts involve inserting specific word sequences into inputs, while continuous prompts optimize prompt vectors directly in a continuous space. Techniques like GPT-3's in-context learning and automated prompt generation methods are discussed, demonstrating their competitive performance and efficiency.

The authors also note that continuous prompt tuning, such as Preﬁx-Tuning and P-Tuning, has shown to be effective, especially with larger models. The paper concludes by mentioning the availability of various resources related to PTMs and summarizes their applications across classic NLP tasks, suggesting that prompt-based tuning is a promising approach for efficiently utilizing large-scale PTMs.
