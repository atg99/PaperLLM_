The paper titled "Pre-trained Models for Natural Language Processing: A Survey" by QIU XP et al. provides a comprehensive overview of pre-trained models (PTMs) in the field of natural language processing (NLP). It discusses various types of PTMs, including multilingual and language-specific models, as well as multi-modal PTMs that incorporate visual and audio data.

XLM-RoBERTa (XLM-R) is highlighted as a significant multilingual encoder that utilizes a vast dataset of 2.5TB clean CommonCrawl data across 100 languages, achieving state-of-the-art results on several cross-lingual benchmarks such as XNLI, MLQA, and NER. The paper distinguishes between multilingual classification and generation tasks, noting that the latter requires joint pre-training of both encoder and decoder. Models like MASS and XNLG are discussed for their contributions to cross-lingual natural language generation, with mBART shown to enhance performance in machine translation tasks.

The authors also emphasize the effectiveness of language-specific PTMs, which often outperform their multilingual counterparts. They provide examples of successful monolingual models, including CamemBERT for French and AraBERT for Arabic.

In addition, the paper explores multi-modal PTMs, which integrate visual and linguistic features. Various models such as VideoBERT and VisualBERT are examined for their capabilities in tasks involving video and image-text pairs. The paper also touches on audio-text models like SpeechBERT, which focuses on speech question answering.

Overall, the survey outlines the advancements in pre-trained models across different modalities and languages, highlighting their impact on various NLP tasks and the ongoing evolution in the field.
