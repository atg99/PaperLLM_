The paper discusses the application of pre-trained models (PTMs) in various natural language processing (NLP) tasks, emphasizing their effectiveness in enhancing model performance across different domains. 

In the context of question answering (QA), models like ALBERT are utilized as encoders, with innovative approaches such as adversarial training and rationale tagging proposed to improve multi-round QA systems. For multi-hop QA, the "Select, Answer, and Explain" (SAE) model leverages PTMs effectively.

In sentiment analysis, BERT has set new performance benchmarks, particularly on the SST-2 dataset. However, its application in aspect-based sentiment analysis (ABSA) has shown limited improvement. Researchers have proposed various methods to enhance BERT's capabilities in ABSA, including transforming tasks into sentence pair classifications, utilizing post-training techniques, and integrating adversarial training. SentiLR, which incorporates linguistic knowledge, has achieved state-of-the-art results in several sentiment classification tasks.

For named entity recognition (NER), pre-trained models like ELMo and BERT have significantly improved performance in sequence-labeling frameworks. Techniques such as zero-shot learning with multilingual BERT and knowledge distillation have further advanced NER applications.

In machine translation (MT), the encoder-decoder framework benefits from PTMs, with studies demonstrating that initializing both components with pre-trained weights enhances translation quality. BERT's integration into NMT models has shown substantial improvements, particularly in unsupervised and supervised translation tasks.

Overall, the survey highlights the transformative impact of pre-trained models in improving the performance and efficiency of various NLP tasks, paving the way for further research and development in the field.
