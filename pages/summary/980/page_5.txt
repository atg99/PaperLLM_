The paper provides a comprehensive overview of pre-trained models (PTMs) for natural language processing (NLP), focusing on their architectures, fine-tuning approaches, and pre-training tasks. ULMFiT is highlighted as a significant model that fine-tunes a pre-trained language model (LM) for text classification, achieving state-of-the-art results through a three-phase process: pre-training on general data, fine-tuning on target data, and task-specific fine-tuning. The paper notes the emergence of deep PTMs like OpenAI GPT and BERT, which excel in learning universal language representations.

The authors categorize pre-training tasks into three main types: supervised learning (SL), unsupervised learning (UL), and self-supervised learning (SSL). SL involves mapping inputs to outputs based on labeled data, while UL seeks to extract knowledge from unlabeled data. SSL combines aspects of both, generating labels automatically from the input data. A key example of SSL in NLP is the masked language model (MLM), which predicts masked words based on surrounding context.

The paper emphasizes that while some PTMs in computer vision are trained on large datasets, NLP often faces challenges due to smaller dataset sizes, with machine translation (MT) being a notable exception. CoVe, a PTM trained on MT, demonstrates improved performance across various NLP tasks. Additionally, the paper discusses language modeling (LM) as a common unsupervised task, detailing its probabilistic nature and the limitations of unidirectional models, which only capture leftward context.

Overall, the paper underscores the importance of pre-training tasks in developing effective PTMs and their role in advancing NLP applications.
