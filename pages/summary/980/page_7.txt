The paper provides an overview of various pre-trained models for natural language processing, focusing on different training strategies. 

1. **Permuted Language Model (PLM)**: This model involves training on a randomly permuted sequence of tokens. The model predicts certain tokens based on the remaining ones, using a special two-stream self-attention mechanism for target-aware representations. However, it primarily focuses on predicting the last few tokens due to slow convergence.

2. **Denoising Autoencoder (DAE)**: DAE reconstructs original text from partially corrupted input using a sequence-to-sequence model. Various corruption methods are discussed, including token masking, token deletion, text infilling, sentence permutation, and document rotation, each requiring the model to recover or predict missing information.

3. **Contrastive Learning (CTL)**: This approach learns to differentiate between semantically similar and dissimilar text pairs. It minimizes a loss function that evaluates the score of similar pairs against dissimilar ones, offering a less computationally intensive alternative to traditional language modeling. Techniques such as pairwise ranking and Noise-Contrastive Estimation (NCE) are highlighted, which help in distinguishing real from fake samples.

4. **Deep InfoMax (DIM)**: Originally designed for images, DIM has been adapted for language representation learning by maximizing mutual information between sequence representations and local context.

5. **Replaced Token Detection (RTD)**: Similar to NCE, RTD focuses on predicting whether tokens have been replaced based on their context. ELECTRA enhances this by employing a generator to replace tokens, followed by training a discriminator to identify these replacements.

Overall, the paper outlines how these models leverage different strategies for effective language representation learning, contributing to advancements in natural language processing tasks.
