This paper surveys pre-trained models (PTMs) for natural language processing (NLP), focusing on their adaptation to various language understanding tasks. The authors discuss methods for early exit strategies in PTMs, which involve training internal classifiers (o-ramps) that determine whether to exit at different stages of processing. The training objective typically combines cross-entropy losses across these o-ramps, with approaches like self-distillation and ensemble learning enhancing performance by optimizing both accuracy and diversity.

During inference, strategies such as entropy-based criteria, maximum softmax scores, patience-based methods, and voting systems are used to decide on early exits. A key challenge remains in effectively transferring the knowledge from PTMs to downstream tasks, which is addressed through transfer learning. This process involves adapting knowledge from a source task to a target task, with considerations around the choice of pre-training tasks, model architectures, and the appropriateness of the training corpus. 

The paper emphasizes that different PTMs yield varying effectiveness based on their pre-training tasks, architectures, and the similarity of the data distributions to the downstream tasks. Furthermore, the selection of specific layers within a pre-trained model is crucial, as different layers encapsulate different types of information relevant to various NLP tasks. The findings underline the importance of carefully selecting PTMs and their configurations to optimize performance on targeted applications.
