This invited review paper provides a comprehensive survey of pre-trained models (PTMs) in the field of natural language processing (NLP). The authors, Xipeng Qiu and colleagues from Fudan University, begin by discussing the evolution of language representation learning and its significance in NLP. They categorize existing PTMs based on a taxonomy that considers four different perspectives.

The paper emphasizes the advantages of neural networks in NLP, particularly their ability to use low-dimensional, dense vectors to represent language features, thereby reducing reliance on handcrafted features. Despite the success of neural models, the authors note that their performance in NLP has not matched the advancements seen in computer vision, primarily due to the limited size of available datasets.

The emergence of PTMs has revolutionized NLP by enabling models trained on large corpora to produce universal language representations that can be effectively adapted for various downstream tasks. The authors highlight the progression of PTM architecture from shallow to deep models, particularly with the introduction of the Transformer architecture, which has enhanced the capabilities of PTMs.

The review serves as a practical guide for understanding, utilizing, and developing PTMs across various NLP applications, while also outlining potential future research directions in this rapidly evolving area. Key concepts discussed include deep learning, neural networks, self-supervised learning, and language modeling.
