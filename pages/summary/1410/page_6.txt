CSPNet, introduced by Wang et al., optimizes gradient flow by separating feature maps into two paths, thereby minimizing duplicate gradient information. This approach not only reduces the number of parameters but also improves the utilization of computational resources, yielding a 10% to 20% reduction in computations with maintained or enhanced accuracy. It has been successfully integrated into various architectures, including ResNet and DenseNet, making it suitable for both state-of-the-art detectors and mobile applications.

EfficientNet, developed by Tan et al., addresses the scalability of neural networks by introducing a compound coefficient to uniformly scale depth, width, and resolution. This innovative approach allows for improved accuracy without excessive increases in model size or complexity. By systematically studying the effects of scaling, they created a baseline architecture that optimizes both accuracy and computational efficiency. EfficientNet outperforms existing models in terms of speed and accuracy, marking a potential shift towards more efficient methodologies in computer vision tasks.
