The paper discusses advancements in object detection, highlighting various models and their innovations. It begins with the introduction of YOLOv2, which improved speed and recall by removing fully connected layers and utilizing learned anchor boxes. The authors also incorporated a hierarchical classification system using WordNet to enhance performance through better hypernym prediction.

RetinaNet, proposed by Lin et al., addresses the imbalance between foreground and background classes in single-stage detectors through a novel loss function called Focal loss. This model employs a dense sampling strategy and utilizes ResNet combined with a Feature Pyramid Network, allowing it to effectively detect objects at multiple scales. RetinaNet is noted for its simplicity in training, faster convergence, and superior accuracy compared to two-stage detectors.

YOLOv3 introduced incremental improvements over its predecessor, incorporating a larger Darknet-53 network and techniques such as data augmentation and batch normalization. Despite being faster, it did not significantly surpass YOLOv2 in accuracy.

CenterNet presents a unique approach by modeling objects as points rather than bounding boxes. It generates a heatmap indicating object centers and employs a multitask loss function across three heads to predict dimensions and offsets. This method utilizes a pre-trained Hourglass network for feature extraction.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the evolution of object detection techniques, focusing on innovations that enhance speed, accuracy, and training efficiency.
