The paper provides a comparison of various object detection datasets including PASCAL VOC, MS-COCO, ILSVRC, and OpenImage, highlighting differences in the number of classes, training, validation, and test images, as well as the number of objects per image. For instance, PASCAL VOC has 12 classes with approximately 5,717 training images, while MS-COCO features 80 classes with 118,287 training images. 

The discussion then shifts to VGG, a convolutional neural network architecture proposed by Simonyan and Zisserman. VGG emphasizes the impact of network depth on accuracy, utilizing small convolutional filters to create deep networks with 16 to 19 layers. This architecture effectively captures larger receptive fields while minimizing parameters and improving convergence speed. VGG outperformed the previous ILSVRC winner, GoogLeNet, and has become a prominent backbone for object classification and detection.

The paper also addresses GoogLeNet (Inception), which aimed to enhance classification networks while addressing resource intensity and computation wastage. Szegedy et al. identified that larger models could lead to overfitting due to their numerous parameters, emphasizing the need for efficient designs in deep learning networks.
