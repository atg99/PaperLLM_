This section of the paper discusses advancements in convolutional neural networks (CNNs), focusing on three key architectures: GoogLeNet, ResNet, and ResNeXt.

GoogLeNet, introduced as a 22-layer deep network, employs locally sparse connected architectures through Inception modules. These modules utilize multiple filter sizes at the same level, allowing for efficient computation and improved performance, achieving 93.3% top-5 accuracy on the ImageNet dataset without external data. This model demonstrated the effectiveness of using fewer parameters while maintaining high accuracy, paving the way for updated Inception models that further enhanced performance.

ResNets, proposed by Kaiming He et al., address the degradation of accuracy in deeper networks by introducing residual learning through skip connections. This approach allows for element-wise addition between the input and output of layers without increasing model complexity. The ResNet architecture, exemplified by a 34-layer model, includes bottleneck modules that enable deeper networks. ResNets have shown superior performance compared to traditional models, leading to the development of ResNetv2, which incorporates batch normalization and is easier to train.

ResNeXt, introduced by Xie et al., offers a more efficient alternative to increasing model depth or width by focusing on cardinality, which refers to the number of paths in a block. This architecture simplifies the design by replacing traditional ResNet blocks with inception-like modules, enhancing scalability and generalization. ResNeXt achieved higher accuracy with fewer hyperparameters than comparable ResNet models and was recognized as the first runner-up in the ILSVRC 2016 challenge.

Overall, these architectures illustrate significant progress in CNN design, emphasizing efficiency, accuracy, and the importance of innovative structural changes in deep learning models.
