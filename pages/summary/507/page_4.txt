In this paper, the authors introduce a new semi-supervised learning method, S4L, and compare it against several established semi-supervised learning algorithms that do not rely on self-supervised objectives. The paper outlines key algorithms for comparison, including Virtual Adversarial Training (VAT), Conditional Entropy Minimization (EntMin), and Pseudo-Labeling.

VAT aims to enhance robustness in model predictions by minimizing the changes in predictions around unlabeled data points. This is achieved by calculating a loss that approximates the maximal change in predictions within a defined vicinity of unlabeled data. EntMin encourages confident predictions on unlabeled data by minimizing the conditional entropy of the model's predictions. However, it is noted that EntMin alone can lead to overconfidence in predictions, necessitating its combination with VAT for effective training. Pseudo-Labeling involves training a model on labeled data and then using it to label unlabeled data, thereby expanding the training set with these confident predictions.

The experiments are conducted using the ILSVRC-2012 dataset, a standard benchmark in self-supervised learning, to evaluate the scalability of semi-supervised methods. The authors detail their methodology, including the use of a custom train/validation split and the implementation of experiments with varying percentages of labeled data (10% and 1%). They emphasize the importance of establishing strong baselines for comparison, noting that previous methods may have been unfairly weak due to inadequate tuning. The authors aim to provide a robust baseline through extensive hyperparameter tuning on the dataset.

Overall, the paper presents a comprehensive analysis of semi-supervised learning techniques, highlighting the effectiveness of S4L in comparison to existing methods while establishing a strong foundation for future research in this area.
