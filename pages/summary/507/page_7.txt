The paper discusses a three-step training approach to improve model accuracy using a method called S4L-Rotation, which integrates self-supervised learning with semi-supervised learning techniques. 

In Step 1, the model is trained using a combination of S4L-Rotation loss, V AT, and EntMin losses. Hyperparameters were fine-tuned manually, resulting in a model that achieves 88.80% top-5 accuracy on the ILSVRC-2012 dataset. The training involved a batch size of 128, a learning rate of 0.1, and specific weight decay and loss weights that were optimized for performance.

Step 2 involves retraining the model with pseudo labels generated from the predictions of the previous model. This step uses the same losses but adjusts for the fact that all examples now have pseudo labels, allowing for training on the full dataset for 18 epochs.

Finally, in Step 3, fine-tuning is performed on the original 10% labeled data, leading to a final model that achieves 91.23% top-5 accuracy, marking a new state-of-the-art performance. The paper also highlights that the model, when trained with 100% labels in an earlier experiment, slightly outperforms fully supervised baselines.

Additionally, the study includes an evaluation of the model's learned representations through transfer learning to the Places205 dataset, demonstrating that the S4L models produce more separable features than traditional self-supervised models, resulting in faster convergence in logistic regression tasks.

Overall, the paper illustrates the effectiveness of combining self-supervised and semi-supervised learning strategies to enhance model performance and representation quality.
