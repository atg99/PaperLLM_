This paper explores the effectiveness of using small validation sets in semi-supervised learning, particularly in the context of the ILSVRC-2012 dataset. The authors present a framework termed S4L, which bridges self-supervised and semi-supervised learning methods. They evaluate models trained with only 1% of labeled data and demonstrate that a tiny validation set can yield robust model selection outcomes, showing a strong correlation between performance on small and full validation sets. 

The findings suggest that the best models identified using a small validation set also perform well on larger sets, addressing concerns about the viability of small validation sets for hyperparameter tuning. The paper further introduces two instantiated S4L methods, S4L-Rotation and S4L-Exemplar, which compete favorably against established semi-supervised techniques. 

While the results are promising with 10% labeled data, the performance diminishes significantly with only 1% labeled data, suggesting a transition towards few-shot learning scenarios. The authors encourage further exploration of integrating self-supervised techniques into semi-supervised frameworks and highlight the need for new methods in low-data regimes. The study concludes by acknowledging contributions from the Google Brain Team and highlighting the potential for future research in this area.
