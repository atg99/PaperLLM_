This paper introduces the framework of self-supervised semi-supervised learning (S4L), which integrates self-supervised learning methods with semi-supervised approaches to improve image classification tasks.

The authors propose two novel semi-supervised image classification methods under the S4L framework, demonstrating their effectiveness against both baselines and existing methods in the field. The results indicate that the S4L framework, when combined with traditional semi-supervised techniques, achieves state-of-the-art performance on the semi-supervised ILSVRC-2012 dataset using only 10% of labeled data.

The paper emphasizes the importance of leveraging unlabeled data, which is easier to obtain than labeled data, and suggests that self-supervised tasks can enhance the learning of visual representations. The proposed methods utilize strategies such as image rotation to create multiple perspectives of input images, allowing the model to predict both the rotation applied and the semantic labels, which leads to competitive performance compared to existing semi-supervised learning methods.

Overall, the findings support the hypothesis that combining self-supervised learning with semi-supervised techniques can significantly improve the learning process and performance on image classification tasks, addressing the challenge of limited labeled data in real-world applications.
