The paper introduces a novel framework termed self-supervised semi-supervised learning (S4L), which integrates self-supervised learning techniques into semi-supervised learning paradigms. By bridging these two learning approaches, S4L aims to leverage the strengths of self-supervised targets to enhance semi-supervised performance, particularly for natural images. The authors evaluate their models in both semi-supervised and transfer setups, focusing on scenarios where only a small fraction of labels (10% or 1%) is available from the ILSVRC-2012 dataset. 

Key contributions of the paper include the development of new techniques that outperform baseline models trained without unlabeled data and achieve competitive results compared to existing semi-supervised methods. Additionally, the authors find that their S4L methods can be further enhanced when combined with regularization techniques from the semi-supervised literature, leading to state-of-the-art performance on ILSVRC-2012.

The paper also reviews related work in semi-supervised and self-supervised learning. Semi-supervised learning algorithms typically exploit both labeled and unlabeled data, with recent advances focusing on deep neural networks and consistency regularization methods. Self-supervised learning, on the other hand, utilizes surrogate tasks to derive useful representations from unlabeled data. The synthesis of these two approaches in S4L promises to advance the field of semi-supervised learning significantly.
