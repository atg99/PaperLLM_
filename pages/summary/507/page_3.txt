This paper discusses advancements in self-supervised learning techniques for visual representation from image databases, focusing on methods that excel in unsupervised learning. It highlights several key approaches, including those by Doersch et al., which utilize convolutional neural networks (CNNs) to predict the relative locations of image patches, and subsequent works that expand this idea to predict permutations of multiple patches. Other self-supervised techniques mentioned involve image-level tasks, such as grayscale image colorization and predicting rotation angles of images.

The paper outlines the self-supervised semi-supervised learning (S4L) framework, specifically targeting the semi-supervised image classification problem. It describes the learning objective that combines labeled and unlabeled datasets, emphasizing the flexibility in choosing mini-batch sizes and the incorporation of both supervised and self-supervised losses during optimization.

Two specific self-supervised techniques are explored within this framework: rotation prediction and exemplar learning. In the S4L-Rotation method, images are rotated, and the model predicts the degree of rotation applied, transforming the task into a four-class classification problem. The S4L-Exemplar method focuses on learning representations invariant to various image transformations, employing techniques such as cropping and color randomization, and utilizing a triplet loss to encourage similar representations for transformed images.

Overall, the paper presents a structured approach to leveraging self-supervised techniques in semi-supervised learning, with potential for future exploration of additional self-supervised losses.
