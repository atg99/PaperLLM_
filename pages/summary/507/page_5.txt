This paper investigates the impact of various hyperparameters, learning rate schedules, and model architectures on the training of deep learning models, particularly using the ResNet50v2 architecture on the ILSVRC-2012 dataset. The study systematically explores learning rates, weight decay values, and different model configurations, such as depth and width, to assess their influence on performance.

The authors emphasize that tuning weight decay and training duration is critical for achieving optimal results, while the choice of model architecture has a relatively minor effect. They establish a baseline using a standard ResNet50v2 model trained with a weight decay of 10^-3 for 200 epochs, which achieved top-5 accuracy of 80.43% using 10% of the training data, and 48.43% accuracy with just 1%. The training procedure included standard data augmentation techniques.

Further experiments revealed that color augmentation significantly enhances performance, especially with smaller datasets. The paper also evaluates semi-supervised learning techniques, including Pseudo-Label, V AT, and V AT with Entropy Minimization, reporting improved accuracy through the application of these methods.

The findings suggest that while model architecture can be optimized, the most substantial gains come from effective hyperparameter tuning, particularly regarding weight decay and training duration. The detailed results of various methods are presented, indicating that the proposed approaches can achieve competitive accuracy benchmarks on the ILSVRC-2012 validation set.
