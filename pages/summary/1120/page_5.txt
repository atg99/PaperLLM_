The proposed SparseEA algorithm is designed to address sparse multi-objective problems by integrating real and binary variable representations. The algorithm begins with an initialization phase, where a population is created with a mix of real decision variables and binary masks that control the sparsity of the solutions. The initialization involves generating random values for the real variables and setting the binary mask to an identity matrix. This allows for the selective activation of decision variables based on their scores, which are derived from non-dominated sorting. 

The algorithm employs a hybrid representation where the decision variables are updated based on both the real vector and the binary mask. Specifically, the final decision variables are calculated by multiplying the real vector by the binary mask, ensuring that only the relevant variables are considered in the optimization process. The selection of parent solutions for the next generation is conducted through binary tournament selection, emphasizing solutions with better scores and crowding distances.

Throughout the evolutionary process, the algorithm continuously refines the population by removing duplicates and ensuring that the number of solutions aligns with the desired population size. The effective assignment of scores to decision variables plays a crucial role in guiding the evolution, as it dictates the likelihood of each variable being set to zero. The methodology allows SparseEA to effectively navigate the search space of sparse multi-objective problems, demonstrating its potential in optimization tasks.
