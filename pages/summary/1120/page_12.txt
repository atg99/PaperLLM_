This paper presents an analysis of the performance of various evolutionary algorithms, including NSGA-II, SPEA2, SMS-EMOA, EAG-MOEA/D, and SparseEA, across different problem instances (FS1-FS4, PM1-PM4, CN1-CN4, and NN1-NN4) using median hypervolume (HV) values and interquartile ranges. The results indicate that SparseEA consistently achieves competitive median HV values, with several instances showing no significant difference compared to other algorithms.

Key findings include the performance of SparseEA in terms of HV across various datasets, highlighting that it achieves the best results on problems like CN4. The results are statistically compared, with symbols denoting whether the outcomes are significantly better, worse, or similar to SparseEA.

Visual representations in the form of graphs illustrate the objective values of solution sets across different algorithms, showing the trade-offs between feature selection ratios, error rates, and model complexity. Notably, SparseEA demonstrates lower model complexities but higher training error rates compared to other methods, suggesting a potential area for future improvement.

Overall, the paper underscores the effectiveness of SparseEA while providing a comprehensive comparison with established algorithms, contributing valuable insights into evolutionary computation methodologies.
