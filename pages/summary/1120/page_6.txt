The paper discusses a novel approach to feature selection using an algorithm called SparseEA, which incorporates an innovative initialization strategy and genetic operators. The proposed score assignment strategy is efficient, requiring only a limited number of function evaluations, and employs binary tournament selection to create a binary vector mask that facilitates optimization. This method contrasts with existing algorithms where probabilities of variables are optimized during the evolutionary process. Instead, SparseEA calculates scores in advance to guide the optimization.

The initial population is generated by setting elements of a real vector to random values and initializing a binary vector mask to zero. A subset of elements is then selected via binary tournament selection based on decision variable scores, enhancing the population's convergence and diversity compared to random initialization.

The genetic operators in SparseEA involve selecting two parent solutions to produce offspring. The offspring's binary mask initially mirrors one parent’s mask, followed by crossover and mutation operations that consider the scores of decision variables. These operations are designed to maintain the quality of offspring while promoting diversity in the population. Overall, the findings demonstrate the effectiveness of the SparseEA's initialization strategy and genetic processes in improving feature selection outcomes.
