The paper discusses various multi-objective problems (MOPs) and proposes a new evolutionary algorithm (SparseEA) specifically designed for large-scale sparse MOPs. It highlights four distinct MOPs: pattern mining, critical node detection, neural network training, and feature selection, each characterized by sparse Pareto optimal solutions due to their inherent constraints and objectives. 

The pattern mining problem aims to maximize both the frequency and completeness of transaction patterns. Critical node detection focuses on selecting a subset of nodes in a graph to minimize pairwise connectivity when removed. Neural network training involves optimizing weights and complexity, while feature selection seeks to identify the most relevant variables.

The paper notes that existing multi-objective evolutionary algorithms (MOEAs) such as NSGA-II and SMS-EMOA have been applied to these sparse MOPs, often enhanced by customized search strategies. However, these strategies are typically tailored to specific types of problems and may not be universally applicable. Additionally, many MOEAs designed for large-scale MOPs struggle with binary variable problems, limiting their effectiveness on certain sparse MOPs.

To overcome these challenges, the authors introduce SparseEA, which does not require prior knowledge of the objective functions or dataset structure. This algorithm is capable of addressing both real and binary variable MOPs, making it versatile for various applications. SparseEA employs a framework similar to NSGA-II, including population initialization, selection, and offspring generation, while incorporating new strategies to ensure the sparsity of solutions. The details of these strategies are elaborated in subsequent sections of the paper.
