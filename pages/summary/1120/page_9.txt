This paper evaluates the performance of various multi-objective evolutionary algorithms (MOEAs) on a set of benchmark problems known as SMOP1–SMOP8. The algorithms compared include NSGA-II, CMOPSO, MOEA/D-DRA, WOF-NSGA-II, and a proposed SparseEA. The study employs the inverted generational distance (IGD) as a performance metric, with statistical analyses conducted using the Wilcoxon rank sum test.

The experimental settings involve four different MOEAs, each with specific configurations tailored to address the challenges posed by the test problems. SparseEA consistently outperforms the other algorithms across all instances, as indicated by the median IGD values and interquartile ranges presented in Table I. The results demonstrate that while other MOEAs struggle to converge to the Pareto fronts, SparseEA achieves better convergence, highlighting its effectiveness in tackling large-scale multi-objective optimization problems.

The findings suggest that SparseEA is a robust approach for solving complex optimization tasks, outperforming established methods and indicating its potential for broader applications in evolutionary computation.
