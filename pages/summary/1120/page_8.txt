The paper discusses the formulation of sparse multi-objective problems (MOPs) and the challenges they pose for multi-objective evolutionary algorithms (MOEAs). It introduces shape functions and landscape functions that define the Pareto front and fitness landscape, respectively. The proposed MOPs consist of decision variables related to these functions, with specific conditions for achieving Pareto optimal solutions. Notably, the sparsity of these solutions is influenced by a parameter that determines how many decision variables should be set to zero.

An example problem is provided, demonstrating how the shape and landscape functions are constructed. It illustrates that as the sparsity of Pareto optimal solutions increases, the performance of existing MOEAs, like NSGA-II and SPEA2, tends to decline. This highlights the difficulty these algorithms face in finding sparse solutions.

Furthermore, the paper presents eight benchmark problems (SMOP1–SMOP8) designed to evaluate the performance of algorithms in identifying sparse Pareto optimal solutions. These benchmark problems feature various shape and landscape functions, which introduce different challenges such as low intrinsic dimensionality and multi-modality. The characteristics and mathematical definitions of these problems are included in supplementary materials, along with source codes available on the PlatEMO platform.

The section concludes with plans for experimental studies to test the proposed SparseEA against other MOEAs on the SMOPs, aiming to validate its effectiveness in solving these sparse MOPs.
