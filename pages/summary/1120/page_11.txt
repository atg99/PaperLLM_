The paper presents an evaluation of various multi-objective evolutionary algorithms (MOEAs) applied to sparse multi-objective problems (MOPs). It details the datasets used for experiments, categorized into four problem types: feature selection, pattern mining, critical node detection, and neural network training. Each dataset's characteristics, including the number of samples, features, and classes or items, are outlined in Table II.

The experimental setup includes the use of specific operators for feature selection and other problems, with defined probabilities for crossover and mutation. The stopping condition is set to 25,000 function evaluations with a population size of 50 to ensure efficient and fair comparisons among the algorithms. Performance is measured using hypervolume (HV), with statistical validation through the Wilcoxon rank sum test.

Results indicate that the proposed SparseEA significantly outperforms other MOEAs, achieving superior HV values across most test instances. This is corroborated by visualizations in figures that compare the non-dominated solution sets from different algorithms. SparseEA demonstrates better convergence and diversity compared to traditional approaches and other MOEAs, confirming its effectiveness in addressing sparse MOPs in various applications. The paper emphasizes the algorithm's potential for real-world problems, highlighting its advantages in terms of solution quality and performance metrics.
