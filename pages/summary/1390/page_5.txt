The paper introduces a novel RCCA (Recurrent Criss-Cross Attention) operation aimed at enhancing the criss-cross attention mechanism in CNNs to obtain dense contextual information from all pixels within feature maps. The RCCA module is structured into two loops, where the first loop processes feature maps extracted from a CNN model and generates new feature maps, while the second loop further refines these outputs. This mechanism allows the RCCA to capture comprehensive spatial context without significantly increasing computational cost or introducing additional parameters.

The authors conduct extensive experiments on three datasets: Cityscapes, ADE20K, and COCO, utilizing metrics such as Mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) and Average Precision (AP) for evaluation. The results indicate that CCNet, which employs the RCCA module, achieves state-of-the-art performance on Cityscapes and ADE20K, and provides a consistent performance improvement on COCO for instance segmentation.

The implementation details include using a ResNet-101 backbone for semantic segmentation, with specific training configurations such as learning rates and augmentation strategies tailored for each dataset. The paper also highlights the performance of CCNet relative to other state-of-the-art methods, noting the variability in training conditions across different approaches. Overall, the RCCA module effectively addresses the limitations of existing criss-cross attention mechanisms by enhancing contextual information extraction while maintaining computational efficiency.
