This paper presents a detailed analysis of the performance of CCNet in semantic segmentation on the Cityscapes dataset, comparing it with state-of-the-art methods. The results in Tables 1 and 2 indicate that CCNet, particularly with multi-scale testing, achieves superior mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) scores compared to other techniques, including DeepLabv3 and DPC, which utilize stronger backbones or additional datasets for training. Notably, CCNet with ResNet-101 backbone reports an mIoU of 81.4%, outperforming various methods that share the same backbone and even those with stronger architectures.

The paper also conducts ablation studies to evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed RCCA (Recurrent Criss-Cross Attention) module. Results show that incorporating criss-cross attention significantly enhances performance, with the optimal configuration being two loops, which balances improved accuracy with computational efficiency. The experiments reveal that increasing the number of loops in RCCA leads to better contextual information capture, ultimately benefiting semantic segmentation performance.

Qualitative comparisons further illustrate the advantages of the criss-cross attention mechanism, as challenging misclassified regions are progressively corrected with additional loops, underscoring the effectiveness of dense contextual information aggregation. The findings suggest that CCNet not only achieves higher accuracy but also maintains lower computational costs compared to similar methods, establishing it as a robust solution for semantic segmentation tasks.
