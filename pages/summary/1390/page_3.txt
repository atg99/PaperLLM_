The paper presents the Criss-Cross Network (CCNet) for semantic segmentation, emphasizing its ability to handle object deformation and varying scales. The CCNet architecture utilizes a criss-cross attention module that captures contextual information in both horizontal and vertical directions. This approach is contrasted with existing models such as CRF-RNN and BiSeNet, which employ graph models and real-time segmentation techniques, respectively.

Contextual information aggregation is a key focus, with references to various methods, including the Atrous Spatial Pyramid Pooling (ASPP) module from Deeplabv2 and pyramid pooling in PSP-Net. The paper highlights the importance of attention models, showcasing examples like Squeeze-and-Excitation Networks and Non-local Networks, which enhance feature representation by modeling relationships within the data.

A critical comparison is made between CCNet, Non-local Networks, and Global Convolutional Networks (GCN). The authors argue that while GCN allows only the center point to gather contextual information, CCNet and Non-local Networks enable any pixel to access information from all others. The criss-cross method employed by CCNet is noted as more efficient than the horizontal-vertical decomposition used in GCN.

The proposed CCNet architecture is designed as a fully convolutional network that processes input images to generate dense feature maps, incorporating dilation convolutions for better detail retention. The paper concludes with a discussion of the recurrent operation within the criss-cross attention module, aimed at capturing dense and global contextual information effectively.
