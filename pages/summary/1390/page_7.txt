The paper presents a comparative analysis of various context aggregation methods applied to the Cityscapes validation set, specifically utilizing ResNet-50 and ResNet-101 as backbone architectures. The results, summarized in Table 4, indicate that the ResNet101 model with the proposed Recurrent Criss-Cross Attention (RCCA) method achieves the highest mean Intersection over Union (mIOU) score of 79.8%, outperforming other techniques such as Non-Local (NL) and Pyramid Pooling (PP).

The authors argue that the superior performance of RCCA can be attributed to its sequentially recurrent operation, which effectively captures long-range dependencies and forms a more comprehensive attention map compared to the direct attention maps generated by the NL method. Additionally, the study demonstrates that criss-cross attention outperforms other attention shapes, such as horizontal and vertical attention, as shown in the results of the comparative experiments.

Furthermore, the computational efficiency of RCCA is highlighted, revealing that it requires significantly less GPU memory and reduces floating-point operations (FLOPs) by approximately 85% compared to the NL method, making it an efficient approach for full-image contextual information aggregation.

The paper also includes visualizations of the attention maps, illustrating how the RCCA effectively captures contextual information from multiple paths, especially when the number of criss-cross modules is increased from one to two. This visualization underlines the method's ability to aggregate denser contextual information, reinforcing the advantages of the proposed approach.
