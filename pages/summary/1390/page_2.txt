The paper introduces a novel criss-cross attention module aimed at improving contextual information aggregation for semantic segmentation tasks. Traditional methods, such as those using dilated convolutions and pooling, have limitations in gathering dense contextual information and adapting to varying pixel dependencies. The proposed criss-cross attention module addresses these issues by utilizing two consecutive sparse attention mechanisms that aggregate contextual information in horizontal and vertical directions. This approach significantly reduces computational complexity from O((H×W)×(H×W)) to O((H×W)×(H+W−1)), making it more efficient.

The criss-cross attention module captures full-image dependencies by allowing each pixel to gather information from all others through a recurrent operation, enhancing pixel-wise representations. The proposed method, named CCNet, integrates this module into fully convolutional neural networks, demonstrating effectiveness through extensive experiments on large-scale datasets like Cityscapes and ADE20K, where it achieved top performance in semantic segmentation tasks. Additionally, it improved the performance of existing instance segmentation methods, such as Mask R-CNN.

The paper emphasizes two main contributions: the introduction of the criss-cross attention module for efficient contextual information capture, and the development of CCNet, which leverages this module to achieve leading results in various segmentation benchmarks. Overall, the work highlights the significance of adaptive contextual aggregation in dense prediction tasks.
