The paper presents Criss-Cross Network (CCNet), a novel approach for dense prediction tasks that effectively captures contextual information through a mechanism called RCCA (Recurrent Criss-Cross Attention). The study compares CCNet to existing methods on several benchmark datasets, including Cityscapes, ADE20K, and COCO, demonstrating its superior performance.

Figure 6 illustrates the attention module's output on the Cityscapes validation set, highlighting the pixel-wise attention maps generated at different resolution levels (R=1 and R=2). The findings indicate that RCCA provides richer contextual information and captures semantic similarities across images.

In Table 5, a comparison reveals that while the baseline model has no computational overhead, the addition of the Non-local (NL) module significantly increases both GFLOPs and memory usage, whereas RCCA offers a more efficient alternative with lower resource demands while achieving better performance.

CCNet achieves state-of-the-art results on the ADE20K dataset, outperforming previous methods by over 0.6% mIoU, as shown in Table 6. The methodology contrasts with other approaches by integrating contextual information through full-image dependencies rather than relying solely on global pooling.

Experiments on the COCO dataset further validate CCNet's effectiveness in instance segmentation tasks. Results in Table 7 demonstrate that models incorporating RCCA consistently outperform both the baseline and those using the NL module across all evaluated metrics.

In conclusion, CCNet, with its RCCA mechanism, captures dense contextual information efficiently, leading to significant performance improvements in semantic and instance segmentation tasks, while minimizing computational costs. Future work may explore further optimizations and applications of this framework.
