The paper "Seeing What a GAN Cannot Generate" by David Bau et al. addresses the prevalent issue of mode collapse in Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), where certain modes of the target distribution are omitted during the training process. The authors aim to visualize and quantify these omissions at both distribution and instance levels.

To achieve this, the authors first employ a semantic segmentation network to analyze the distribution of segmented objects in generated images compared to the target distribution from the training set. This analysis reveals specific object classes that the GAN fails to generate. Secondly, the authors visualize these omissions by comparing real images with their approximate reconstructions by the GAN, focusing on individual differences to understand what the GAN is unable to replicate.

The paper emphasizes the importance of understanding the differences between real and generated images, rather than merely measuring GAN quality with metrics like Inception scores or Fréchet Inception Distance. By identifying and visualizing the omitted modes, the authors provide insights into the limitations of GANs, questioning whether these omissions are due to the exclusion of difficult images or specific objects that the GAN chooses not to learn.

Ultimately, this framework is applied to analyze various recent GANs trained on multiple datasets, identifying common failure cases and enhancing our understanding of the underlying challenges in GAN training.
