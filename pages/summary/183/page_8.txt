The paper discusses the evaluation and visualization of mode-dropping in state-of-the-art generative models, specifically focusing on Progressive GANs. It presents qualitative results from inverting layers of a Progressive GAN trained on LSUN datasets, particularly for indoor bedrooms and outdoor churches. The inversion tool is applied to assess the generators' capabilities to synthesize images outside their training sets, revealing limitations in representing certain visual concepts. 

Figures illustrate reconstructions of natural photographs compared to generated images, highlighting discrepancies where specific objects and styles are absent. The authors note that some indoor images are rendered in a bedroom style, while outdoor images show better detail recovery. The paper proposes methods to measure the quality of different generative models and their output spaces, emphasizing the importance of understanding the latent space of GANs.

Key questions are raised regarding the reasons behind a GAN's tendency to ignore certain classes and how to encourage learning without biasing the training set. The authors advocate for further exploration of mode-dropping phenomena and its implications for generative model training.
