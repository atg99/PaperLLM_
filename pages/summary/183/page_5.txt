The paper investigates the performance of different Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) in generating images from the LSUN dataset, focusing specifically on the segmentation statistics of generated images. The study compares three models: WGAN-GP, Progressive GAN, and StyleGAN, using a Unified Perceptual Parsing network to label pixels with one of 336 object classes. 

Key findings include that StyleGAN produces generated images that better match the true distribution of object classes compared to Progressive GAN and WGAN-GP, as evidenced by the Fréchet Segmentation Distance (FSD) values. Specifically, WGAN-GP scored 428.4, Progressive GAN scored 85.2, and StyleGAN achieved the best score of 22.6, indicating a closer match to the actual data distribution.

The paper also conducts a sensitivity test to assess the impact of sampling on the measurement of segmentation statistics. This test shows that differences observed between two random samples of 10,000 images reflect small measurement noise due to finite sampling rather than actual differences in the generator’s performance.

Furthermore, the analysis identifies specific object classes that are underrepresented or omitted entirely in the generated outputs, particularly in the case of Progressive GAN. The results highlight that while certain classes, like people and furniture, are not rendered, this omission is not due to poor rendering quality but rather a failure of the model to include these object classes at all.

Overall, the study emphasizes the importance of using segmentation statistics to evaluate the quality of generated images and identifies limitations in current GAN models when it comes to accurately representing diverse object classes.
