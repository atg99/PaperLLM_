The paper discusses the performance of two Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs)—WGAN-GP and StyleGAN—on generating images from the LSUN Bedrooms dataset. It employs a method called Generated Image Segmentation Statistics to analyze how well each model captures the true distribution of segmented object classes within the images.

The analysis, depicted in histograms, reveals that WGAN-GP significantly deviates from the true distribution, particularly underrepresenting classes such as beds, curtains, and cushions. In contrast, StyleGAN aligns more closely with the true distribution, although it still falls short for classes like doors, wardrobes, and people. The paper introduces the Fréchet Segmentation Distance (FSD) as a metric to quantify these differences, allowing for a summary of segmentation statistics between generated and real images.

To further investigate the shortcomings of the GANs, the authors propose a method for quantifying instance-level mode collapse by comparing real images with generated ones. They aim to find images that can be synthesized by the generator while remaining close to real images, albeit acknowledging challenges due to the complexity of recent GAN architectures.

Overall, the study highlights the effectiveness of using segmentation statistics to evaluate and improve GAN performance, providing insights into specific areas where each model needs enhancement.
