This paper presents a layer inversion method for deep networks, specifically focusing on the reconstruction of images through an encoder-generator framework. The methodology consists of several steps aimed at effectively approximating the inversion of a generator G using an encoder E.

In the first step, a neural network E is trained to invert the generator G, providing an initial estimate of the latent vector z0, derived from an input image x. This estimate is further processed through the generator's layers to yield an intermediate representation r0. The optimization process then seeks to refine this representation to minimize the difference between the reconstructed image Gf(r*) and the target image x.

The authors establish that the range of images generated by G is a subset of those generated by Gf, implying that any omissions in Gf's output will also occur in G's output. Thus, the inversion problem is approached in two stages: first, by constructing E to estimate z0 and then by optimizing to find r* that closely reconstructs x.

The paper emphasizes a layer-wise approach for training the inversion network E, where individual layers of the generator are inverted one at a time. For each layer, a small network ei is trained to approximate the inversion through minimizing both left and right inversion losses, ensuring the output of the layer is preserved. This results in a composed inversion network E* that can be fine-tuned for better performance.

After obtaining an initial guess for z, the optimization focuses on refining the intermediate representation r by making small perturbations to the early layers of the generator. A combined loss function is utilized that incorporates pixel loss and perceptual loss, balanced by a regularization parameter.

The paper concludes with implementation details and results from experiments conducted on three recent models: WGAN-GP, Progressive GAN, and StyleGAN, demonstrating the effectiveness of the proposed layer inversion method.
