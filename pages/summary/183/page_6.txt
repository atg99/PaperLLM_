The paper presents a comparison of various methods for inverting the generator of a Progressive GAN, specifically trained to generate LSUN church images. Figure 6 illustrates the performance of these methods, including baseline approaches and variants of the proposed layer-wise inversion method. The evaluation focuses on reconstruction accuracy, using mean correlation as a metric across three components: latent vector z, layer 4 features, and reconstructed pixel channels.

The first three columns of the figure showcase traditional inversion methods, while the latter columns detail the proposed approach and its ablations. The results demonstrate that the proposed method achieves nearly perfect reconstructions of GAN-generated images, outperforming the baseline methods (a), (b), and (c), which struggled with accurate reconstructions. The direct optimization of z, tested in column (a), revealed limitations in reconstructing both the latent representation and the output image for the more complex 15-layer Progressive GAN, highlighting the need for more sophisticated inversion techniques.

In addition to assessing GAN-generated images, the paper also applies these methods to real images, providing qualitative insights into their effectiveness despite the absence of ground truth for these cases. Overall, the findings underscore the advantages of the layer-wise inversion method in achieving high-quality reconstructions compared to previous approaches.
