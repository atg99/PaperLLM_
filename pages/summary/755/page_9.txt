This paper emphasizes the significance of creating meaning-preserving adversarial perturbations in natural language processing (NLP), particularly for sequence-to-sequence models. It reviews existing literature on adversarial examples in NLP, highlighting various attacks on sentiment, malware, gender, and toxicity classification. While previous studies have explored adversarial attacks in machine translation (MT), they often neglect the preservation of meaning in the source text, focusing instead on target-side evaluations.

The authors propose a comprehensive evaluation framework for assessing adversarial perturbations, comparing different automatic metrics as proxies for human judgment. Their findings indicate that traditional "naive" attacks generally fail to maintain meaning in MT. They suggest alternatives to enhance meaning preservation and demonstrate the effectiveness of adversarial training in this context. The paper aims to provide a foundation for future research to more consistently evaluate meaning conservation in adversarial settings.
