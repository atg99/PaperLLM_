The paper addresses the concept of meaning-preserving perturbations in sequence-to-sequence (seq2seq) models, proposing a method to evaluate adversarial attacks and defenses based on this concept. The authors introduce a framework to formalize and quantify the effectiveness of these perturbations, enabling both human and automated evaluations.

A key contribution is the development of a criterion for adversarial examples defined as "source-meaning-preserving, target-meaning-destroying." Through experiments, the authors find that the chrF evaluation metric correlates better with human judgments than traditional metrics like BLEU and METEOR. This suggests that chrF is more effective in distinguishing meaning-preserving perturbations across different languages and models.

The paper also explores adversarial training methods and demonstrates that aligning adversarial inputs more closely with the source semantics enhances robustness against attacks without compromising performance on the original data.

The evaluation framework emphasizes the need to measure target similarity while accounting for source meaning preservation. It introduces a target relative score decrease metric to assess the efficacy of adversarial perturbations. The authors recommend reporting both source similarity and target score decrease metrics to provide a comprehensive view of attack effectiveness, suggesting a combined success metric when a single value is preferred.

Overall, the paper provides a structured approach to understanding and evaluating the impact of adversarial attacks on seq2seq models, highlighting the importance of meaning preservation in the evaluation process.
