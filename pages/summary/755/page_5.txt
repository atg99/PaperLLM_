This paper investigates the impact of character-level perturbations on neural machine translation (NMT) models, focusing on how these perturbations preserve meaning while evaluating adversarial attacks. The authors highlight that internal character swaps do not significantly affect human readers and propose constraints that avoid producing out-of-vocabulary (OOV) words. When character swaps aren’t feasible, they use a simple method of repeating the last character. The experiments aim to assess a framework for evaluating adversarial attacks and identify automatic metrics that correlate with human judgment. 

The study uses the IWSLT2016 dataset for various language pairs and tests two NMT models: an LSTM-based architecture and a self-attentional Transformer. Both models underwent training with specific configurations, including dropout and label smoothing. The authors perform evaluations using BLEU, METEOR, and ChrF scores, aiming to determine which metrics best align with human assessments of the adversarial attacks. The results indicate that meaning-preserving attacks yield superior evaluation scores, suggesting the importance of constraints in the evaluation process. The full experimental setup and source code are made available for reproducibility.
