The paper discusses adversarial inputs generated for a word-based translation model, focusing on optimizing word substitutions to mislead the model while preserving semantic meaning. The optimization process involves identifying the best position and replacement word to maximize the likelihood of the model making an error. This is achieved through a brute-force search method that is computationally efficient.

The adversarial loss function aims to maximize the error probability of the model at the next decoding step. The paper emphasizes the importance of creating semantically similar adversarial inputs, proposing two main constraints: the kNN approach, which uses the nearest neighbors in the embedding space for replacements, and the CharSwap method, which modifies words by swapping characters. 

These methods enhance the robustness of adversarial training by ensuring that the generated inputs are not only misleading but also semantically coherent, thus providing a more effective challenge to the translation model.
