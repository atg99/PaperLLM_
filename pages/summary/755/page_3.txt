The paper discusses the evaluation of semantic similarity in the context of adversarial examples, particularly focusing on metrics for assessing similarity between sentences. It emphasizes the need for both human and automatic evaluation methods due to the challenges associated with human assessment, such as cost and accessibility, especially for low-resource languages.

For human evaluation, a six-level scale is proposed to assess semantic similarity, ranging from completely different meanings to sentences that are both well-formed and semantically equal. This scale incorporates aspects of fluency and meaning preservation, addressing the need to distinguish between coherent and nonsensical paraphrases.

In contrast, the paper also highlights three automatic metrics commonly used in machine translation evaluation: BLEU, METEOR, and chrF. BLEU measures n-gram precision and suffers from limitations in recognizing synonyms. METEOR improves upon this by using alignment and allowing for synonym recognition, though it requires language-specific resources. chrF operates at the character level, making it more adaptable across languages and better at capturing morphological variations without needing extensive resources.

The paper notes the importance of comparing these automatic metrics against human judgment to determine their effectiveness as proxies for semantic similarity. It introduces the concept of gradient-based adversarial attacks, which will be explored further in subsequent sections, focusing on substituting words in sentences to maximize adversarial loss. The overall aim is to advance the understanding of semantic similarity evaluation methods in the context of adversarial attacks.
