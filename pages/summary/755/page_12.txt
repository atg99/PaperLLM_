The supplemental material outlines methods for generating out-of-vocabulary (OOV) words through internal character swaps, details statistics on the IWSLT2016 dataset, and breaks down the correlation between automatic metrics and human judgment regarding meaning preservation in adversarial source sentences.

In the OOV word generation function, words longer than three letters undergo a scrambling process where adjacent letters are swapped. This process is repeated for a specified number of iterations. If an OOV word is successfully created that is not found in the provided vocabulary, it is returned. If the scrambling fails, the function appends the last character of the word repetitively until an OOV word is obtained.

The IWSLT2016 dataset statistics reveal a total of 220,400 training samples for French-English, 196,900 for German-English, and 114,400 for Czech-English, with corresponding validation and test samples.

Additionally, correlation coefficients between automatic metrics and human judgments of meaning preservation are presented. Results indicate that metrics such as BLEU, METEOR, and chrF exhibit varying degrees of correlation based on the number of perturbed words and the type of constraints applied. Notably, chrF consistently shows a strong correlation with human judgments across different conditions. The findings reinforce that the observed trends in correlation persist despite smaller sample sizes and varying statistical significance.
