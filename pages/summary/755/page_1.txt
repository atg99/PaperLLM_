The paper "On Evaluation of Adversarial Perturbations for Sequence-to-Sequence Models" by Paul Michel et al. addresses the evaluation of adversarial examples in sequence-to-sequence (seq2seq) models, particularly in the context of machine translation. The authors highlight that existing evaluations often overlook the importance of semantic equivalence before and after perturbations. They propose a new evaluation framework that emphasizes the need for adversarial perturbations to preserve meaning on the source side while potentially altering the target output.

The paper critiques current methodologies for not adequately maintaining semantic integrity, which can lead to misleading assessments of a model's robustness. The authors introduce a criterion for adversarial examples in natural language processing, arguing that effective attacks should preserve meaning on the source side but disrupt it on the target side. This approach contrasts with previous works that have not prioritized meaning preservation.

Additionally, the authors demonstrate that incorporating constraints on adversarial attacks can yield perturbations that maintain semantic meaning but still significantly affect the output. They conclude that employing meaning-preserving adversarial training can enhance model robustness against adversarial attacks without negatively impacting performance on standard test sets.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the need for a nuanced understanding of adversarial perturbations in NLP, advocating for evaluations that consider semantic implications to ensure more reliable assessments of model robustness.
