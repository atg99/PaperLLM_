Swap attack method demonstrates a significant impact on word-based models, particularly in terms of preserving meaning in the source text while disrupting the target output. The study compares three attack constraints: Unconstrained, kNN, and CharSwap, using a validation set with a maximum edit distance of three substitutions. Results indicate that constrained attacks, particularly kNN and CharSwap, positively affect meaning preservation in the source language, as evidenced by higher source chrF scores compared to Unconstrained attacks.

The analysis reveals that source chrF is instrumental in evaluating the effectiveness of these attacks. A manual evaluation corroborates the results, showing a strong correlation between high chrF scores and human assessments of semantic similarity. The findings suggest that while CharSwap performs well in preserving source meaning, it also exposes limitations of word-based models when faced with adversarial inputs.

Additionally, the results highlight discrepancies in performance between different model architectures. Specifically, Transformer models exhibit less robustness to small perturbations than LSTMs, as indicated by lower target RDchrF scores. This suggests that the choice of model architecture has implications for the effectiveness of adversarial attacks, warranting further investigation into the underlying reasons for these differences. Overall, the study underscores the importance of attack constraints and model selection in the context of adversarial robustness in machine translation.
