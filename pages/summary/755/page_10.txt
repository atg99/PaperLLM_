The collection of references encompasses a range of studies focusing on adversarial examples, machine translation, and natural language processing. 

Grosse et al. explore adversarial perturbations aimed at deep neural networks specifically for malware classification, highlighting vulnerabilities in machine learning systems. Hosseini et al. demonstrate methods to deceive Google's Perspective API, which is designed to identify toxic comments, emphasizing the potential for adversarial attacks in text classification.

Iyyer et al. present a deep unordered composition model that rivals traditional syntactic methods in text classification. They further investigate adversarial example generation using syntactically controlled paraphrase networks, showcasing how syntactic manipulation can create effective adversarial samples. Jia and Liang introduce adversarial examples to evaluate reading comprehension systems, stressing the importance of robust evaluation metrics.

Kingma and Ba's work on the Adam optimization algorithm has become a cornerstone in training deep learning models. Koehn et al. discuss the Moses toolkit for statistical machine translation, which has been influential in the field. Kolter and Wong propose provable defenses against adversarial examples through convex outer adversarial polytopes, contributing to the development of more secure models.

Luong et al. investigate attention mechanisms in neural machine translation, while Ma and Cieri discuss corpus support for machine translation. Moosavi-Dezfooli et al. introduce DeepFool, a method for generating adversarial examples that effectively mislead deep neural networks. 

Naik et al. focus on stress test evaluations for natural language inference, while Papernot et al. craft adversarial input sequences for recurrent neural networks, highlighting the risks associated with sequential data. The references also include significant advancements in automatic evaluation metrics for machine translation, such as BLEU and chrF, with discussions on their limitations and the need for clarity in reporting scores.

Reddy and Knight address gender obfuscation in social media writing, while Ribeiro et al. propose semantically equivalent adversarial rules for debugging NLP models. Samanta and Mehta contribute to the crafting of text adversarial samples, and Sennrich et al. tackle the translation of rare words using subword units, reflecting the ongoing evolution in machine translation techniques. 

Overall, these works collectively underscore the challenges and innovations in adversarial machine learning and natural language processing, emphasizing the need for robust, interpretable, and secure models.
