The paper explores the effectiveness of adversarial training, focusing on meaning-preserving attacks, particularly using the CharSwap constraint. It highlights that the swap constraint consistently results in attacks with an average success rate greater than one. Adversarial training is implemented by augmenting training data with adversarial examples, modifying the loss function to incorporate both original and adversarial samples. 

The study reveals that when the model is exclusively trained on perturbed inputs (with a parameter setting of 1.0), the performance of the Unconstrained-adv model declines, while the CharSwap-adv model maintains performance comparable to the original model. This suggests that the introduction of spurious training samples without constraints negatively impacts the model's ability to generalize. 

In terms of robustness, the CharSwap-adv model exhibits superior resilience to CharSwap attacks across both parameter settings (1.0 and 0.5), indicating that adversarial training with constrained attacks enhances robustness without significantly harming test performance. The findings underscore the importance of using constraints in adversarial training to balance robustness and performance on non-adversarial inputs. 

The paper also references related work in adversarial attacks and training, indicating a foundation built on previous research.
