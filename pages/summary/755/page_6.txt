The paper presents an analysis of the performance of LSTM and Transformer models in machine translation, focusing on the impact of various adversarial attacks on translation quality, measured using chrF metrics. The experiments involve two language pairs: Czech-English (cs-en) and French-English (fr-en). 

Key findings include that the original chrF scores significantly outperform scores from various attack methods, such as Unconstrained, CharSwap, and kNN across both word-based and subword-based models. The LSTM model specifically shows notable results for the French-English pair, with evaluations conducted on 900 sentence pairs subjected to different perturbations.

Human evaluations were carried out, where annotators rated sentence pairs based on translation quality. The correlation between human judgments and automatic metrics was assessed, revealing that chrF demonstrates a higher correlation with human ratings compared to METEOR and BLEU, particularly on the French source side. A paired bootstrap test confirmed the statistical significance of chrF's superiority over METEOR in French, although not in English, attributed to METEOR's access to language-specific resources.

The paper concludes that chrF is particularly effective in capturing character-level errors, which are more prevalent in the French source due to CharSwap attacks. Future results will be reported in terms of source chrF and relative decrease in target chrF, as detailed in the appendix.
