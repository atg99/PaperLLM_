The work builds on previous research by Borgonovo and Plischke, introducing new estimators for density-based sensitivity measures that are independent of sampling methods. Additionally, the method of derivative-based global sensitivity measures (DGSM) is highlighted for its ease of implementation compared to Sobol sensitivity indices, utilizing Monte Carlo sampling techniques.

The concept of adversarial examples was first introduced by Szegedy et al., revealing the vulnerability of deep neural networks to small input perturbations that can drastically alter outputs. Goodfellow et al. further developed the fast gradient sign method (FGSM) for generating adversarial examples efficiently. 

Moosavi-Dezfooli et al. proposed DeepFool, which optimizes the generation of minimum-perturbation adversarial examples, demonstrating the fragility of classifiers. The notion of universal adversarial examples shows that certain perturbations can misclassify a wide range of inputs, exposing the weaknesses inherent in deep learning models.

Papernot et al. investigated adversarial behavior and introduced algorithms to generate adversarial instances, while Kurakin et al. discussed the limitations of directly inputting adversarial examples into classifiers that interact with the physical environment. Overall, the paper underscores the critical need for developing methods to enhance the robustness of machine learning models against adversarial attacks.
