This paper discusses the balance between model explainability and performance, noting that while simpler models like linear and decision-tree based approaches offer better interpretability, they often fall short in predictive power compared to more complex models. The resurgence of interest in Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI) stems from the need for trustworthy AI systems, particularly in high-stakes sectors like healthcare and autonomous driving, where ethical considerations are paramount.

The survey aims to provide a comprehensive analysis and comparison of machine learning interpretability methods. It seeks to establish a reference for researchers and practitioners by offering a taxonomy of existing techniques, identifying suitable use cases, and linking to programming implementations. 

The paper also clarifies the concepts of explainability and interpretability, which are often used interchangeably but have nuanced differences. While there is no rigorous mathematical definition for these terms, existing literature highlights interpretability as the ability to make the workings of a model understandable to humans. The definitions provided by Doshi-Velez and Kim, as well as Miller, emphasize the human capacity to grasp the reasoning behind model decisions, underscoring the importance of clear cause-and-effect relationships in AI systems.
