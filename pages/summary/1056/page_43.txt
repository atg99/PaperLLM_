The referenced works focus on various aspects of adversarial attacks, robustness, and sensitivity measures in machine learning and neural networks. Key contributions include:

1. **Global Sensitivity Measures**: Plischke et al. and Kucherenko discuss methods for deriving global sensitivity indices from data, emphasizing the importance of understanding how variations in input affect model outputs.

2. **Adversarial Examples**: Szegedy et al. introduced the concept of adversarial examples in neural networks, demonstrating that small perturbations to input can lead to misclassification. Subsequent works by Goodfellow et al., Moosavi-Dezfooli et al., and others explore methods to generate and defend against these adversarial inputs.

3. **Physical World Attacks**: Kurakin et al. examined how adversarial examples can be effective in real-world settings, highlighting the practical implications of these vulnerabilities.

4. **Robustness Evaluation**: Carlini and Wagner proposed frameworks for evaluating neural network robustness against adversarial attacks, while Papernot et al. explored defenses such as model distillation to mitigate these threats.

5. **Optimization Techniques**: Chen et al. and others introduced various optimization strategies for crafting adversarial examples and improving attack efficiency, including black-box attacks that do not require access to the model's internal parameters.

6. **Transferability of Attacks**: Research by Liu et al. emphasizes the transferability of adversarial examples across different models, complicating defense strategies.

These studies collectively highlight the ongoing challenges in ensuring the security of machine learning systems against adversarial manipulation, underscoring the need for robust evaluation and defense mechanisms.
