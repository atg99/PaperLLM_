The study presents a comprehensive taxonomy of machine learning interpretability methods, categorizing them into four main groups: methods for explaining complex black-box models, creating white-box models, promoting fairness, and analyzing model sensitivity.

The research highlights the predominance of neural networks in interpretability literature, particularly in the fields of computer vision and natural language processing. Saliency maps, often derived from gradient information, are commonly used to illustrate the influence of various image regions on model predictions. Grad-CAM and deconvolutional neural networks are noted as significant contributors to this area.

For black-box model explanations, methods such as LIME and SHAP stand out as the most widely utilized for visualizing feature interactions and importance. They are model-agnostic and applicable to diverse data types, making them versatile tools in the interpretability toolkit. Despite the challenges in developing highly interpretable white-box models in complex domains, the study emphasizes the importance of interpretability in machine learning, especially as expectations for model transparency continue to rise.
