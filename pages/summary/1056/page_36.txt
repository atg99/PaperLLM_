The paper discusses the evolving landscape of machine learning, emphasizing the shift from white-box models, which excel in single tasks, to more versatile models capable of knowledge transfer across multiple tasks. It highlights the work of Caruana et al., who introduced generalized additive models with pairwise interactions (GA2Ms), achieving notable accuracy in healthcare applications.

Efforts to address discrimination and enhance fairness in machine learning are acknowledged, particularly through the framework proposed by Hardt et al., which quantifies and mitigates discrimination in supervised learning. However, the authors note a lack of focus on fairness in non-tabular data, such as images and text, indicating a need for further exploration in these areas.

The paper also covers sensitivity analysis, which has gained traction due to the work of Szegedy et al. on adversarial examples. Various methods for generating adversarial examples have been developed, tailored to different data types and learning tasks. Despite the growth of explainable artificial intelligence (XAI), the field remains underdeveloped, lacking formal definitions and integration into standard machine learning workflows.

Overall, the authors assert that while there has been significant progress in machine learning interpretability, there is still considerable room for improvement and exploration in this domain. They express a belief in the untapped potential of XAI to enhance machine learning practices in the future. The paper concludes with author contributions and declarations of no conflicts of interest or external funding.
