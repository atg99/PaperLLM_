The paper discusses various approaches to adversarial attacks on text classification and entailment models, particularly focusing on the BERT architecture. It highlights the robustness of BERT and examines different strategies for generating adversarial examples. Key contributions include:

1. Jin et al. propose a baseline for evaluating the robustness of BERT against natural language attacks, emphasizing the need for strong benchmarks.
2. Garg and Ramakrishnan introduce BAE, a method for creating adversarial examples based on BERT, demonstrating its effectiveness in text classification tasks.
3. Li et al. present BERT-ATTACK, which uses BERT itself to generate adversarial attacks, showcasing the model's vulnerabilities.
4. Tan et al. explore linguistic discrimination by employing inflectional perturbations to combat biases in language models.
5. Zang et al. approach word-level textual adversarial attacks through combinatorial optimization, providing insights into optimizing attack strategies.

These studies collectively underscore the critical need to evaluate and enhance the robustness of natural language processing models against adversarial manipulation.
