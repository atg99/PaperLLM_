The paper discusses the integration of machine learning tools within the context of social justice, emphasizing the need for models that address issues of social stratification and unequal access to achieve fairness. Milli et al. highlight the strategic manipulation of decision-making models by individuals seeking favorable treatment, advocating for the establishment of conservative decision boundaries to enhance robustness. However, they note a trade-off exists between the accuracy of decision-making systems and the social burden imposed on individuals, particularly those from disadvantaged groups.

Counterfactual fairness is introduced as a concept where fairness is assessed by comparing outcomes for individuals in both actual and hypothetical scenarios involving different demographic groups. The authors propose a causal inference framework to model fairness, emphasizing the importance of counterfactual causal guarantees and enabling models to account for sensitive attributes reflecting social biases.

The study further investigates the fairness of word embeddings, revealing that even widely used embeddings, such as those trained on Google News, exhibit significant gender biases. The authors present methods for quantifying these biases and propose an algorithm to debias the embeddings based on the identified properties of gender stereotypes.

Moreover, the paper argues for fairness at both segment and individual levels, framing it as a data representation challenge where similar individuals should have similar encodings while remaining insensitive to sensitive information. It also outlines three strategies to create a discrimination-free naive Bayes classifier, including the adjustment of conditional probabilities for sensitive feature values to promote fairness.

Overall, the paper underscores the complexities of achieving fairness in machine learning, advocating for careful consideration of social welfare and the implications of decision-making strategies.
