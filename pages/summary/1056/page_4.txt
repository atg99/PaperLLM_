The paper discusses the evolving field of explainable artificial intelligence (XAI), emphasizing the need for more combinatorial approaches that integrate various explanation techniques to enhance understanding. Adadi and Berrada conducted a comprehensive review of 381 papers on XAI, highlighting the necessity for formalism and increased human-machine interaction. They noted the community's focus on modeling explainability and advocated for broader consideration of explainability within machine learning.

Guidotti et al. proposed a taxonomy categorizing explainability methods into four groups based on their purpose: explaining black-box models, inspecting them, explaining outcomes, and creating transparent black-box models. They underscored the lack of formal metrics for evaluating interpretability methods and the challenges posed by latent features in black-box models, while also noting a gap in interpretability techniques for recommender systems.

Murdoch et al. introduced a framework to address the need for formal metrics in interpretability, proposing the Predictive, Descriptive, Relevant (PDR) framework with three metrics: predictive accuracy, descriptive accuracy, and relevancy. They emphasized the potential of combining transparent models with post-hoc interpretability to enhance predictive accuracy.

Arrieta et al. presented a classification that differentiates between transparent and post-hoc methods, creating sub-categories specifically for deep learning interpretability due to its complexity. They proposed four categories related to deep network processing and representation, production systems, and hybrids of the two. Additionally, they explored the concept of Responsible Artificial Intelligence, establishing criteria for AI implementation in organizations.

The paper concludes by advocating for a multifaceted classification of interpretability methods, considering various perspectives such as data type and global versus local properties, to help practitioners select the most suitable method for their specific challenges.
