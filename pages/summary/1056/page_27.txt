The advancements in sensitivity analysis methods highlight the evolution of techniques aimed at improving efficiency and accuracy. Saltelli et al. enhanced Sobol's method with better sampling techniques, while Cukier et al. introduced the Fourier Amplitude Sensitivity Test (FAST) to streamline Sobol's index calculations. Further improvements were made by Saltelli et al. for total-effect indices and by Tarantola et al. with the RBD-FAST method, which integrated random balance designs with FAST. Computational efficiency was further addressed by Plischke, and Tissot et al. contributed a bias correction method.

Morris's one-step-at-a-time (OAT) method categorizes input variables based on their significance and interaction effects, but it can be resource-intensive. Campolongo et al. proposed enhancements to Morris’s framework to improve its practicality. Additionally, the development of moment-independent importance measures, like Borgonovo's indices, offers an alternative approach to sensitivity analysis that does not depend on variance, thus expanding the toolkit available for researchers and practitioners in the field.
