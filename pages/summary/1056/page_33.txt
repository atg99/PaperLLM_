The paper discusses various methods for generating adversarial examples in natural language processing (NLP) to evaluate the robustness of models against attacks. It highlights the success of certain strategies in manipulating text to misclassify sentiment analysis and textual entailment models. Kuleshov et al. introduced a word replacement approach using greedy heuristics, which was later enhanced by Wang et al. through genetic algorithms, resulting in improved success rates and transferable adversarial examples.

DeepWordBug and Textbugger focus on identifying key text pieces for manipulation using scoring strategies, while Seq2Sick addresses adversarial attacks on seq2seq models, overcoming challenges related to discrete inputs and large output domains through innovative methods. Feng et al. introduced "input reduction" to explore model vulnerabilities by removing non-essential words without altering model predictions. Additionally, Ren et al. proposed the probability weighted word saliency (PWWS) algorithm, which substitutes words based on saliency and classification probability.

TextFooler generates adversarial examples by identifying important words and replacing them with semantically and grammatically suitable synonyms. Other studies leverage the BERT model for text perturbations, while Tan et al.'s Morpheus method manipulates word inflections to increase model loss. Zang et al. explored word substitutions based on sememes, suggesting that words with similar meanings can effectively replace each other.

The literature indicates substantial progress in adversarial attacks for computer vision and NLP, but notes a lack of research on adversarial techniques for tabular data, despite the potential applicability of existing methods from other domains.
