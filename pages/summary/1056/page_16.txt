The paper provides an overview of various machine interpretability methods designed to explain black-box models, categorizing them based on their characteristics such as local vs. global applicability, model specificity, and data types. It includes a table summarizing different tools, their categories, data types, and citation metrics, highlighting the diversity of methods available.

The methods discussed include LIVE, which shares similarities with LIME but employs original feature spaces for local exploration by perturbing individual features. The breakDown method, akin to SHAP, attributes model responses to input features but does so using a greedy approach, focusing on a single series of nested conditionings, which allows for faster computations at the cost of some theoretical robustness. Additionally, ProfWeight offers a strategy to enhance the performance of interpretable models by leveraging knowledge from high-performing pre-trained deep networks through the use of confidence scores.

The examination concludes that while a variety of methods exist, primarily targeting image and text data, a universally superior method that addresses all types of models and data has yet to be developed.
