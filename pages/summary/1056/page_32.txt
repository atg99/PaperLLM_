The paper discusses various methods for generating adversarial examples across different modalities, including images and text. Schott et al. introduced a robust image classification model that calculates class-conditional ELBOs to enhance prediction accuracy. They also developed targeted attacks to exploit the model's structure and a decision-based attack minimizing pixel perturbation.

In noise-based attacks, the addition of noise to original examples often necessitates clipping to maintain input validity. Rauber and Bethge proposed an efficient, differentiable clipping algorithm that improves the speed and effectiveness of perturbation scaling.

Adversarial vulnerabilities extend to deep reinforcement learning, as shown by Huang et al., who utilized the FGSM method to manipulate network policies with slight state perturbations.

For discrete data, Yang et al. implemented a two-step greedy approach for word perturbation in texts, leading to the Gumbel method, which parameterizes this process for optimization. Other studies introduced techniques for generating grammatically correct adversarial sentences through synonym replacement and paraphrasing.

Jia and Liang focused on reading comprehension tasks, creating adversarial examples by modifying questions related to a paragraph. Their approaches included adding words directly from questions or integrating random human-approved sentences.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the diversity of adversarial attack strategies and their implications for machine learning models, underscoring the need for robust defenses against such vulnerabilities.
