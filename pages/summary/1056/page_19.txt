The paper discusses various techniques for addressing bias in machine learning classification tasks, focusing on preprocessing methods and model adjustments. The first technique, suppression, involves removing sensitive features and their correlated attributes to reduce bias in model decisions. The second method, massaging the dataset, involves relabeling instances based on their likelihood of being favored or discriminated against, followed by training a classifier on this adjusted dataset. A third technique applies weights to instances based on the observed and expected probabilities of sensitive features and class labels, aiming to mitigate bias without changing labels.

Additionally, a convex optimization approach is proposed to balance discrimination control, individual instance distortion, and utility preservation. Adversarial debiasing is another framework discussed, where a main model predicts labels while an adversarial model attempts to predict demographic segments; this dual training seeks to enhance prediction accuracy while reducing demographic predictability.

Kamiran et al. identify limitations in existing methods that require data alterations or algorithm modifications and propose two new methods: Reject Option based Classification (ROC) and Discrimination-Aware Ensemble (DAE). ROC emphasizes cost-based classification, where misclassifying non-favored groups incurs greater penalties. DAE utilizes an ensemble of diverse classifiers to enhance fairness, allowing for control over the trade-off between accuracy and discrimination without the need for data preprocessing.
