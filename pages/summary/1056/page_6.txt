The paper discusses interpretability methods for deep learning models, categorizing them under post-hoc interpretability due to their black-box nature. It divides the methodologies into two sub-categories: those specific to deep learning and those applicable to other black-box models. 

In the context of deep learning, a significant focus is on saliency methods, which highlight unique features in images. Saliency maps represent these features, indicating visually significant locations. The paper details several gradient-based attribution techniques, notably the Gradients method, which quantifies the effect of input changes on predictions. An enhancement of this method showed a 67% increase in explained information over state-of-the-art models.

Integrated Gradients is another key technique that attributes predictions to input features while satisfying properties like completeness and sensitivity. It ensures that attributions sum up correctly, addressing limitations found in other methods. DeepLIFT, an advanced algorithm, improves upon earlier gradient methods by using a first-order Taylor approximation, enhancing saliency maps and providing better interpretability, particularly for natural images and genomics data.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the evolution of interpretability tools in deep learning, highlighting their significance in understanding model predictions and identifying weaknesses in various applications.
