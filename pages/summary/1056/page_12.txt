The paper discusses various model-agnostic interpretability methods for enhancing the understanding of predictions made by black-box models. 

Firstly, SHAP (Shapley Additive Explanations) is introduced as a method rooted in game theory that calculates the importance of each feature for individual predictions. It unifies several existing methods, including LIME and DeepLIFT, into a class of additive feature attribution methods. SHAP values are proposed as a consistent measure of feature importance that ensures local accuracy, missingness, and consistency. The authors present various estimation methods for SHAP values, demonstrating their effectiveness in distinguishing between output classes and aligning with human intuition.

Secondly, the Anchors method is discussed, which generates high-precision, if-then rules to represent local conditions for predictions. Anchors are created incrementally, starting from a general rule and refining it based on candidate rules with the highest estimated precision. This method is noted for its ability to yield shorter anchors that maintain high coverage and for leading to improved user precision and understanding compared to linear explanations.

Lastly, the Contrastive Explanations Method (CEM) is presented, which generates explanations by identifying features that are minimally necessary for a specific prediction, as well as those that should be absent. This dual focus on necessary and absent features is argued to provide a more comprehensive interpretation, aligning with natural human reasoning.

Overall, the paper highlights the advancements in interpretability methods, emphasizing their practical applications and the importance of understanding both present and absent features in model predictions.
