The paper highlights the ongoing challenges in developing interpretable machine learning models, particularly in achieving a balance between model complexity and predictive performance. The increasing reliance on deep learning models, especially in fields like computer vision and natural language processing, has overshadowed the potential of white-box models that are easier to interpret but may lack the necessary predictive power. Furthermore, as the demand for models that can perform multiple tasks grows, white-box models are becoming less favored in the literature.

In the context of fairness, the paper emphasizes the importance of evaluating machine learning systems for bias and discrimination, noting a shift towards more comprehensive and diverse approaches in recent studies. Techniques such as disparate impact testing serve as initial steps in assessing fairness but may lack depth in understanding the underlying causes of bias. Overall, the paper underscores the need for continued research and innovation in both interpretability and fairness within machine learning.
