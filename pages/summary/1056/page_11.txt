This section of the paper discusses interpretability methods for black-box models, specifically focusing on the local interpretable model-agnostic explanations (LIME) technique. LIME is widely recognized for its ability to provide explanations for individual prediction scores from any classifier. The method involves generating simulated data around the input instance, making new predictions for these perturbed instances, and training a simple interpretable model, like a decision tree, on this dataset. This process allows for interpretation of the original black-box model through the local model.

Despite its strengths, LIME has limitations, as highlighted in a 2020 theoretical analysis that pointed out that poor parameter choices could lead to the omission of critical features. Additionally, Zafar and Khan noted that the random perturbation and feature selection employed by LIME can result in inconsistent interpretations for the same prediction, raising concerns about its reliability in practical applications.

To tackle this issue, a deterministic variant called DLIME has been introduced, which replaces random perturbation with hierarchical clustering and uses k-nearest neighbors to select the relevant cluster for the instance. The authors demonstrated DLIME's superiority over LIME using three medical datasets, particularly in terms of the Jaccard Similarity metric, indicating improved stability in interpretations.
