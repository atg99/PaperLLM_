The paper discusses various approaches to integrating fairness into machine learning models while maintaining accuracy. It highlights the potential of converting fairness measures into optimization constraints during training, although it notes the limitations of these methods due to the lack of theoretical guarantees on global optimality and potential inaccuracies with small datasets. 

Zafar et al. argue that existing fairness definitions are overly stringent and can negatively impact model performance. They propose a fairness model based on collective preferences of demographic groups, introducing proxies to quantify these preferences. Their empirical evaluations demonstrate that classifiers optimizing for fairness through these group preferences achieve better predictive accuracy compared to those adhering to strict parity definitions.

Agarwal et al. present a systematic framework that encompasses various fairness definitions, treating them as special cases. Their approach breaks down fair classification into a series of sub-problems, allowing for an optimal balance between predictive accuracy and fairness criteria.
