The paper discusses various methods for visualizing and interpreting the features learned by Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs). DeepLIFT is highlighted for its ability to distinguish between positive and negative contributions, revealing dependencies overlooked by other methods like Integrated Gradients. Guided BackPropagation, also known as guided saliency, challenges the effectiveness of max-pooling in small image CNNs and suggests replacing max-pooling with convolutional layers, maintaining accuracy across image recognition benchmarks.

Deconvolution, introduced in earlier works, utilizes Deconvolutional Networks to reverse the mapping process of CNNs, providing insights into the function of intermediate layers by mapping feature activity back to the input image pixels. Class Activation Maps (CAMs) identify discriminative regions in images used by CNNs for classification but have limitations, including a requirement for specific network architectures and a focus solely on the final convolutional layers.

Grad-CAM is presented as an advancement over CAM, offering visual explanations for any CNN architecture by leveraging class-specific gradient information from the final convolutional layer. This method enhances transparency in CNN models and can be combined with pixel-space visualizations to produce high-resolution, class-discriminative outputs through Guided Grad-CAM. While Grad-CAM improves upon CAM, it still faces challenges, such as localizing multiple object occurrences and accurately determining class-region coverage due to its underlying assumptions and potential signal loss during processing.
