The paper discusses various methods for model interpretability, noting that many existing approaches either focus on specific model types or data types, or are limited in their scope. Among these methods, SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) stands out for its comprehensive applicability to any model and data type, providing both global and local explanations. However, it has limitations: KernelSHAP does not account for feature dependence, potentially skewing results, while TreeSHAP, although addressing this issue, can yield non-intuitive feature importance values due to its reliance on conditional expected predictions.

The paper also explores white-box models, which are designed to be interpretable and easily understood. It highlights several advanced models, including Supersparse Linear Integer Models (SLIM), which rely on simple arithmetic operations for predictions, enhancing interpretability. Generalized additive models with pairwise interactions (GA2Ms) are presented as effective in medical applications, striking a balance between interpretability and predictive accuracy. GA2Ms utilize modern machine learning techniques to improve performance while maintaining a modular structure for understanding feature contributions.

Additionally, the paper discusses Boolean Rule Column Generation, which employs simple Boolean rules for predictive modeling, emphasizing interpretability through rule simplicity. The authors note the computational challenges in learning rules from large datasets and propose an approximate column-generation algorithm to effectively navigate the rule space. Lastly, Generalized Linear Rule Models (GLRMs) are mentioned as interpretable models that combine rule-based features with the flexibility to capture nonlinear relationships, reinforcing their utility in the interpretability landscape.
