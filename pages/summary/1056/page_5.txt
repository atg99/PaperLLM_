The paper discusses the classification of interpretability methods in machine learning based on various criteria. It highlights the distinction between model-specific methods, which are applicable only to certain algorithms, and model-agnostic methods, which can be used across different algorithms. Furthermore, interpretability methods are categorized based on the scale of interpretation: local methods provide explanations for individual instances, while global methods explain the entire model. The type of data—such as tabular, image, or text data—also influences the applicability of these methods.

The taxonomy presented in the paper identifies four main categories of interpretability methods: 
1. Methods for explaining complex black-box models.
2. Methods for creating white-box models.
3. Methods that promote fairness and minimize discrimination.
4. Methods for analyzing the sensitivity of model predictions.

The first category focuses on methods designed to interpret pre-trained black-box models rather than creating interpretable models from the outset. This framework serves as a guide for practitioners to select the most suitable interpretability method for their specific needs, considering the purpose and approach of each method.
