The paper discusses various adversarial attack algorithms targeting neural networks, focusing on their effectiveness and methodologies. Carlini and Wagner introduced three algorithms based on L0, L2, and L∞ norms to create minimal perturbations in adversarial examples, employing margin loss for improved accuracy over previous methods. Their work includes the creation of a ground truth attack that formulates adversarial example generation as a linear optimization problem.

Xiao et al. presented a spatially transformed attack that subtly alters images through translations and rotations, while Su et al. demonstrated that modifying a single pixel can effectively mislead neural networks. Chen et al. proposed a zeroth order optimization attack (ZOO) that utilizes prediction probability scores to infer gradient information, thereby eliminating the need for a substitute model.

Narodytska et al. developed pixel-perturbing methods to generate adversarial examples without gradient information, employing random selection and a greedy local-search algorithm for improved pixel selection. The HopSkipJumpAttack algorithm introduced by another group leverages decision boundary information and Monte Carlo methods to generate adversarial examples optimized for L2 and L∞ norms.

Liu et al. explored the transferability of adversarial examples, finding that while non-targeted examples are effective across models, targeted examples are less successful. They proposed methods to enhance the transferability of both types of adversarial examples. Cisse et al. introduced Houdini, capable of generating adversarial instances across various tasks, including complex performance measures. Lastly, a novel elastic net-based regularization framework combining L1 and L2 norms was proposed, demonstrating effectiveness in generating adversarial examples across multiple image datasets.
