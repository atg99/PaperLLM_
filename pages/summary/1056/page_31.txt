The paper discusses various methods for generating adversarial examples, highlighting their effectiveness and versatility against machine learning models, particularly deep neural networks.

Papernot et al. introduced a technique for generating adversarial examples by training a model on synthetic instances and their corresponding labels from a neural network. This method exploits decision boundaries, allowing the adversarial examples to misclassify both the trained model and the original neural network.

Brendel et al. emphasized the advantages of decision-based adversarial attacks, such as their applicability to black-box models and ease of implementation. They introduced the Boundary Attack, which generates adversarial instances by starting with high perturbations and gradually reducing them, thus learning the decision boundary.

Koh and Liang proposed a method to determine the impact of altering training examples on model predictions, identifying which instances would yield the most powerful adversaries. Zugner et al. and Dai et al. explored adversarial examples in graph-structured data, utilizing different approaches, including greedy methods and reinforcement learning.

Finally, Sharif et al. demonstrated a unique attack on face recognition systems using 3D printed sunglasses, while Hayes and Danezis presented a generative framework for creating universal adversarial examples, further advancing the understanding of adversarial attacks in machine learning.
