The paper discusses the challenges and implications of generative models, particularly in the context of Deepfake technology. Wang et al. (2020a) illustrate that classifiers trained on images from a specific Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) can also detect images from other generative models, highlighting the interconnectedness of GAN performance and detection capabilities. Gulrajani et al. (2020) emphasize that traditional evaluation metrics like Inception Score (IS) and Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) may favor models that memorize training data over those that generalize well, which raises concerns about the effectiveness of these measures in assessing generative quality.

The paper underscores the urgency of developing detection tools for Deepfakes, as advancements in generative models make it increasingly challenging for humans to differentiate between real and fake content. The difficulty of detecting Deepfakes varies by category, with certain subjects like faces being easier to analyze than complex scenes. Despite these challenges, deep networks can still identify subtle artifacts in manipulated images. The authors advocate for further exploration of how GAN evaluation metrics can contribute to mitigating the risks posed by Deepfakes.
