The paper discusses various advancements in evaluating generative models, particularly through the use of Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) and its adaptations. 

1. **Class-Aware Fréchet Distance (CAFD)**: Proposed to incorporate class information into FID calculations. CAFD averages the Fréchet distances across K classes, using the means and covariance matrices of each class. This method provides a more nuanced evaluation of generative models by considering class-specific distributions.

2. **Conditional FID**: Introduced by Soloveitchik et al. to evaluate conditional generative models, this variant analyzes two classes of distributions based on a shared input, enhancing the applicability of FID in conditional settings.

3. **Fast FID**: Developed by Mathiasen and Hvilshøj to expedite FID computation, making it feasible as a loss function for training Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs). By optimizing the computation of covariance matrices, Fast FID significantly reduces the time needed for FID calculations, achieving speed increases of 25 to 500 times while facilitating backpropagation during training.

4. **Memorization-informed FID (MiFID)**: Introduced by Bai et al. in the context of a generative model competition, MiFID modifies the FID score to include a penalty for generating images too similar to the training set. This penalty is based on memorization distance, which measures how closely generated samples resemble training samples. The approach aims to encourage diversity in generated outputs by penalizing overfitting to the training data.

These methods contribute to the progression of evaluation metrics for generative models, emphasizing efficiency, class consideration, and the avoidance of memorization, thus enhancing the robustness of generated outputs.
