The paper discusses two primary metrics for evaluating generative models: perplexity and new qualitative measures for image generation.

Perplexity is a widely used metric for assessing generative text models. It quantifies the likelihood of a sentence being generated by a language model trained on a specific dataset. The formula for perplexity involves the probability of each word in a sentence, with lower values indicating better model performance.

In addition to perplexity, the paper introduces new qualitative evaluation measures for generative adversarial networks (GANs), focusing on human perception of generated images. One such measure is Human Eye Perceptual Evaluation (HYPE), proposed by Zhou et al. This method includes two variants: one that assesses visual perception under time constraints and another that evaluates human error rates on distinguishing real from fake images. While HYPE provides reliable human-centered evaluations, it faces challenges in scalability.

Another innovative measure is Neuroscore, developed by Wang et al. This approach utilizes neural signals and rapid serial visual presentation to gauge human perceptual responses to images. By employing the oddball paradigm, it captures the P300 event-related potential, which reflects the brain's response to stimuli.

Overall, the paper highlights the importance of both perplexity for text models and human-centered qualitative measures for image generation, emphasizing the need for reliable evaluation methods in generative modeling.
