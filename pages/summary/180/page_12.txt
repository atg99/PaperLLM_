The figures discussed illustrate key concepts in generative modeling and evaluation metrics. Figure 6 demonstrates the capability of the Class Activation System (CAS) to identify classes where BigGAN-deep fails to accurately represent the data distribution, contrasting real images with generated samples. Figure 7 introduces the data-copying concept from Meehan et al. (2020), showcasing how certain regions can be over-represented or under-represented in a dataset. It highlights the distinction between overfitting and underfitting through visualizations of generated samples and their nearest training neighbors, emphasizing the phenomenon of "data-copying."

In the context of GAN generalization, studies such as those by O’Brien et al. (2018) and van Steenkiste et al. (2020) are mentioned. Additionally, Sajjadi et al. (2018) propose a framework using precision and recall (P&R) to evaluate the trade-off between the quality of generated instances and their coverage of the training data. P&R curves serve as tools to identify issues like mode collapse and poor quality in generative models. Naeem et al. (2020) critique existing precision and recall metrics, citing their inadequacies in detecting matches between generated instances and real data.
