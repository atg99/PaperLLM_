The paper discusses various metrics for assessing the performance of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) in generating samples that closely resemble real data distributions. Figure 1 presents four scenarios illustrating how generated samples (triangles) compare to real data samples (circles) from a bimodal Gaussian distribution, highlighting the relationship through CrossLID scores. The desired outcome is shown in scenario (d), where generated data aligns with both modes of the real distribution, indicating a lower CrossLID score is preferable.

Figure 2 introduces the Intrinsic Multi-scale Distance (IMD) score, which distinguishes between two distributions that share the same first three moments. Unlike Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) and Kernel Inception Distance (KID), which are insensitive to higher moments, the IMD score effectively captures geometric differences, making it a valuable tool for evaluating the quality of generated data.

The paper also references the work of Barannikov et al. (2021), who propose the Cross-Barcode method to analyze topological discrepancies between distributions in high-dimensional spaces. This method leads to the introduction of the Manifold Topology Divergence score, which is applied across various domains, including images and time-series data, and is noted for being domain-agnostic and independent of pre-trained networks.

Lastly, the Perceptual Path Length (PPL) metric, introduced in StyleGAN3, is discussed as a measure of the entanglement or smoothness of the generator's latent space, emphasizing its relevance in understanding the structure of generated outputs.
