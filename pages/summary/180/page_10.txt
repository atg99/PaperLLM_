The paper discusses the evaluation of generative adversarial networks (GANs) and their ability to generalize from training data. Figure 4 illustrates the correlation between the Perceptual Path Length (PPL) and image quality generated by StyleGAN, indicating that lower PPL scores reflect better image consistency. The authors highlight that overfitted GANs produce samples that are closer to training samples, resulting in smaller average distances compared to held-out test samples. They emphasize the need to assess generative models in different regions of the instance space since their behavior varies.

Zhao et al. (2018) explore how GANs and VAEs generate novel attributes based on specific training datasets. Their findings reveal that when trained on images with a fixed number of objects, models tend to generate a range between 2 to 5 objects. In multi-modal training scenarios, models behave as if trained on separate distributions, often favoring the average number of objects present. The memorization of specific combinations is noted when the training set is limited, while greater variety leads to the generation of novel combinations. Xuan et al. (2019) corroborate these findings, indicating that models can produce a variable number of objects even when the training set is fixed. This underscores the complexities of generalization in GANs and the importance of dataset design in evaluating their performance.
