The paper discusses the limitations of current evaluation methods for Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), particularly highlighting the blind spot for image quality identified by Karras et al. (2019). Manual inspection, a common evaluation method, offers direct insights into image quality but falls short in assessing sample diversity and is influenced by subjective biases. The requirement for domain-specific realism knowledge can complicate assessments, especially in fields like medicine. Additionally, the practical constraints of manual review, such as time and variability in experimental protocols, hinder its effectiveness during model development.

The structure of the paper is organized into several sections: Sections 2 and 3 categorize recent evaluation measures into quantitative and qualitative methods, respectively. Section 4 reviews benchmark and model comparison studies, while Section 5 summarizes key findings and provides recommendations for future GAN evaluation research. The paper notes that some reviewed works may not have undergone peer review, thus warranting cautious interpretation.

In the quantitative evaluation measures, new approaches such as Spatial FID (sFID) and Class-aware FID (CAFD) are introduced. sFID, proposed by Nash et al. (2021), utilizes spatial features to enhance sensitivity to spatial variability. In contrast, CAFD, introduced by Liu et al. (2018), critiques the single-manifold Gaussian assumption inherent in FID and employs a Gaussian mixture model to improve feature fitting. These advancements aim to refine the assessment of GAN performance and address existing evaluation shortcomings.
