The paper discusses various methodologies involving Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) and their applications in image generation, manipulation, and detection. 

In the RSVP experimental protocol outlined, participants are shown a rapid image stream of target and non-target images, specifically searching for real face images. The hypothesis is that faces generated by GANs will produce different neural responses compared to real faces. A neuro-AI interface is employed to compute a Neuroscore based on the participants' neural responses to both real and GAN-generated images.

The paper also introduces a method for quantifying image manipulation through latent space exploration. This involves comparing attributes, such as luminance, between real and generated images after applying transformations in the latent space.

Additionally, the concept of GAN dissection is presented, which allows researchers to visualize the internal workings of GANs by identifying specific generator neurons responsible for certain semantic concepts in images. This technique enables targeted editing of images by activating or deactivating these neurons, thereby facilitating the addition or removal of objects within generated images.

Lastly, the paper addresses the development of a universal detector that can differentiate between real and synthetic images. This detector is trained on a dataset of synthetic images produced by multiple CNN-based generative models, aiming to enhance the ability to identify fake images across various contexts.
