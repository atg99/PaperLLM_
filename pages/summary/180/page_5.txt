The sections discussed in the paper focus on various metrics used to evaluate generative models, highlighting biases and inconsistencies in existing approaches. 

Chong and Forsyth (2020) identify that Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) and Inception Score (IS) are biased due to their expected values not accurately reflecting true values when calculated with finite samples. They propose an extrapolation method, resulting in FID1 and IS1, which are intended to provide bias-free estimates by simulating infinite sample calculations.

Parmaretal. (2021) investigate the sensitivity of the FID score and similar metrics, like Kernel Inception Distance (KID), to variations in image processing implementations across libraries. They note that different methods for image resizing can introduce artifacts, leading to inconsistencies in FID scores. To standardize evaluation, they introduce a protocol along with a user-friendly library called cleanfid, aimed at ensuring consistent FID calculations.

Unterthiner et al. (2019) extend the FID concept to video evaluation through the Fréchet Video Distance (FVD), which assesses both the quality of individual frames and the temporal coherence of video sequences. They utilize a pre-trained Inflated 3D ConvNet for feature extraction, which enhances the evaluation of generative video models. The paper also mentions other video metrics, such as Average Content Distance, Cumulative Probability Blur Detection, Frequency Domain Blurriness Measure, Pose Error, and Landmark Distance, along with references for further reading on video generation.

Additionally, Fréchet Audio Distance (FAD) and Fréchet ChemNet Distance (FCD) are mentioned as alternatives for evaluating audio and chemical data generated models, respectively.
