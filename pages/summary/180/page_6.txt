The paper discusses various methods for evaluating music enhancement and molecule generation algorithms, focusing on self-supervised learned representations and data manifold analysis. 

In section 2.2, it highlights the limitations of Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) scores when applied to non-ImageNet datasets, as they may not accurately reflect human evaluations. Morozov et al. advocate for using self-supervised representations, which are derived from contrastive or clustering-based approaches. These representations are shown to improve model ranking and sample efficiency in evaluations.

In section 2.3, the paper explores methods that analyze the data manifold to enhance model performance. Local Intrinsic Dimensionality (LID) is introduced through the CrossLID metric, which gauges the intersection of data distributions and is correlated with GAN training progress, mode collapse sensitivity, and robustness to noise. However, its applicability to complex, high-dimensional data is questioned. 

Additionally, Intrinsic Multi-scale Distance (IMD) is proposed by Tsitsulin et al. as a more comprehensive evaluation measure that considers all moments of data distributions, addressing the shortcomings of FID and Kernel Inception Distance (KID) in assessing global data structure. IMD is designed to be invariant to transformations and captures both local and global information effectively.
