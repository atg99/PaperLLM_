The paper discusses the limitations of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) in generating realistic images, emphasizing systematic flaws that hinder their performance. Bau et al. (2019) illustrate these shortcomings, showing that certain objects, such as people and fences, are frequently absent in images generated by GANs, indicating a disparity between the distribution of real and generated images. The authors reference additional studies (Chai et al. 2020; Yu et al. 2019) that echo these findings, concluding that the development of reliable GAN image detection tools remains a challenge (Gragnaniello et al. 2021).

In the section on GAN benchmarks and analysis, the authors highlight ongoing efforts to establish effective evaluation criteria for GANs. Gulrajani et al. (2020) critique existing metrics like Inception Score (IS) and Fréchet Inception Distance (FID), arguing that they can be easily manipulated by memorizing training data. They propose a new metric based on neural network divergences (NND), which assesses the performance of a discriminative model distinguishing between generated samples and a held-out test set. Their experimental results demonstrate that NND effectively captures diversity, sample quality, and generalization in GAN outputs. Additionally, Lee and Town (2020) introduce Mimicry, a lightweight library for implementing popular GAN architectures in PyTorch.
