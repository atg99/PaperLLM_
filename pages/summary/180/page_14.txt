The figures presented illustrate key metrics for evaluating the performance of generative models, specifically focusing on precision and recall. Figure 10 depicts the distributions of real images (Pr) and generated images (Pg), highlighting precision as the likelihood of a random image from Pg falling within the support of Pr, and recall as the likelihood of a random image from Pr falling within the support of Pg. Figure 11 further explores density and coverage measures, emphasizing how real outliers can skew these metrics. The figures indicate that generating samples around outliers can enhance precision, while the recall may still be high even if fake samples are distant from real sample modes due to an overestimated fake manifold. 

Additionally, the paper discusses the evaluation of precision and recall across a range of thresholds, resulting in comprehensive curves rather than single values. It also introduces an Authenticity metric to assess how likely synthetic samples are to replicate training data. The section references works that extend precision and recall methodologies, including contributions from Djolonga et al., Simon et al., and Kynkäänniemi et al. Furthermore, the Duality GAP Metric proposed by Grnarova et al. leverages game theory concepts to provide a domain-agnostic and efficient measure for model assessment, facilitating the monitoring of model progress.
