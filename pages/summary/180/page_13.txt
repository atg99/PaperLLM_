The paper discusses the evaluation of generative models using various metrics to address limitations in existing methodologies. Figure 8 illustrates how generative models can be analyzed with specific training data configurations, examining learned distributions based on the number of features present in the training images. Figure 9 highlights findings from Xuan et al. (2019), where a GAN trained on images with two rectangles can generate varying numbers of rectangles, including anomalous outputs.

The authors point out that traditional evaluation metrics have shortcomings, such as being sensitive to outliers and relying on arbitrarily chosen hyperparameters. To improve upon these issues, they propose density and coverage metrics. Precision is defined as the binary assessment of whether generated data falls within any neighborhood sphere, while density measures the number of real-sample spheres that contain generated data.

Additionally, Alaa et al. (2021) introduce a three-dimensional evaluation metric—Alpha Precision, Alpha Recall, and Authenticity—that assesses the fidelity, diversity, and generalization capability of generative models. Alpha Precision focuses on the synthetic samples that match the most typical real samples, whereas Alpha Recall measures the proportion of real samples that have similar synthetic counterparts. This approach aims to provide more interpretable and reliable evaluations for practitioners in the field of generative modeling.
