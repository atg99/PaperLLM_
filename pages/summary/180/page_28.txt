The evaluation of generative models, particularly Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), is contingent upon the specific downstream tasks they are applied to. Different tasks prioritize different aspects; for instance, image quality is crucial in graphics applications like image synthesis, while diversity is more important for generating synthetic data used in data augmentation. Therefore, evaluation metrics must be tailored to the goals of each task. 

The representation power of a GAN's discriminator or encoder does not necessarily indicate the quality or diversity of samples generated. Effective evaluation measures are essential not only for ranking generative models but also for identifying their shortcomings. This is particularly vital in fields like medical imaging, where human evaluation may not be reliable. A key consideration is whether existing evaluation metrics can be generalized across various domains.

The extent to which generative models memorize training data remains uncertain. Traditional methods for assessing memorization, such as nearest neighbor searches, have limitations. Recent research has introduced new approaches to better understand and quantify memorization in generative models.

Future research should focus on improving the evaluation of generative models to address the risks posed by fabricated content. This includes extending existing studies, benchmarking on datasets with fabricated content, and fostering collaboration between GAN evaluation research and deepfake detection efforts.
