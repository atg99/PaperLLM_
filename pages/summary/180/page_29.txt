This collection of references highlights significant contributions to the field of generative models, particularly focusing on Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) and their evaluation. Bau et al. (2018, 2019) explore methods to visualize and understand GANs, providing insights into their inner workings and limitations. Bińkowski et al. (2018) demystify Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) GANs, a variant of GANs, while Bond-Taylor et al. (2021) offer a comparative review of various deep generative modeling approaches, including VAEs, GANs, and normalizing flows.

Borji (2019) discusses the advantages and disadvantages of different GAN evaluation measures, emphasizing the need for robust metrics. Brock et al. (2018) present a framework for large-scale GAN training aimed at high-fidelity image synthesis. Buolamwini and Gebru (2018) examine intersectional accuracy disparities in gender classification systems, highlighting ethical considerations in AI.

Furthermore, van den Burg and Williams (2021) investigate memorization in probabilistic deep generative models, while Carreira and Zisserman (2017) introduce a new action recognition model with the Kinetics dataset. Casanova et al. (2020) question the progress in generating complex scenes, and Chai et al. (2020) analyze the properties that make fake images detectable. Lastly, Chen et al. (2018) focus on generating lip movements, and Chong and Forsyth (2020) propose unbiased evaluation metrics for GANs.

Overall, these works contribute to a deeper understanding of generative models, their capabilities, limitations, and the evaluation challenges they present in the field of computer vision.
