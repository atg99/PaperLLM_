The paper discusses the evaluation metrics for generative models, specifically focusing on Precision, Recall, and Authenticity metrics as illustrated in Figure 12 from Alaa et al. (2021). It highlights how generated samples can either appear unrealistic or be unauthentic, particularly when models overfit to training data. The ideal scenario is for high-quality samples to reside within the defined support of real distributions. The metrics are quantitatively defined, with an example showing values for Precision, Recall, and Authenticity.

The authors introduce the concept of duality gap as a measure of sub-optimality in the training of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), correlating it with the Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) across various modalities, including text and sound. Notably, this measure does not require labeled data or a pre-trained classifier, making it versatile across different domains. Sidheekh et al. (2021) further extend this concept to the proximal duality gap for broader applicability in GAN training.

Additionally, the paper reviews spectral methods, citing studies that demonstrate the inability of current generators to accurately replicate the spectral distributions of real data. Durall et al. (2020) identify that up-scaling operations in GANs can distort the spectral properties of images, leading to high-frequency distortions. They propose a spectral regularization term to address these issues and introduce an effective detector for generated images and videos, termed a DeepFake detector.
