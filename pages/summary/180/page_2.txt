This paper discusses the ongoing challenges in evaluating generative models, particularly focusing on advancements since 2019. It reviews various proposed measures, benchmarks, and techniques for visualizing and diagnosing errors in Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs).

The evaluation of generative models is crucial yet complex. Traditional methods, such as comparing log-likelihoods, face limitations as high likelihood does not guarantee good image quality and vice versa. The most prevalent metrics in GAN evaluation are the Inception Score (IS) and Fréchet Inception Distance (FID). IS measures the KL divergence between conditional and marginal class distributions but fails to account for intra-class diversity and is sensitive to model parameters. High IS can be misleading, as models can achieve it without generating high-quality images.

FID, on the other hand, is favored for its alignment with human judgment and its ability to detect subtle changes in real distributions. It measures the distance between Gaussian distributions fitted to the embeddings of real and generated images. Despite its advantages, FID also has drawbacks, including a high bias and the need for large sample sizes for reliable results, where smaller samples can inflate the FID score.

While single-value metrics like IS and FID capture essential aspects of generative models, they do not effectively separate quality and diversity, which is critical for thorough diagnostics. The paper emphasizes the need for more comprehensive evaluation methods that can address these limitations and better characterize the performance of generative models.
