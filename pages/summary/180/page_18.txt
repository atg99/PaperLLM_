The paper discusses various methods for evaluating the performance and capabilities of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs). 

Figure 14 presents HYPE scores, which assess the realism of images generated by different models on the CelebA and FFHQ datasets. A score of 50% indicates that the generated images are indistinguishable from real images, while scores above 50% suggest hyper-realism. The figure also includes examples of images produced by StyleGAN, with the highest HYPE scores demonstrating the best perceptual fidelity.

Wang et al. introduce Neuroscore, an approach that aligns more closely with human judgment than traditional metrics. They developed a convolutional neural network capable of predicting Neuroscore directly from GAN-generated images, eliminating the need for neural responses.

Bau et al. focus on visualizing mode collapse in GANs, utilizing a semantic segmentation network to compare the distribution of segmented objects in generated versus real images. This method highlights the classes of objects that a GAN fails to generate, allowing for a clearer understanding of its limitations.

Lastly, Jahanian et al. propose a technique for measuring GAN steerability by quantifying how well basic visual transformations can be performed through navigation in the GAN's latent space. They aim to learn an optimal path in the latent space for specific transformations, formalized through an objective function.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of evaluating GANs through various metrics and visualizations to better understand their strengths and weaknesses.
