The paper discusses various metrics for evaluating the structure of latent spaces in generative models, particularly focusing on perceptual differences and linear separability. The Perceptual Path Length (PPL) is introduced as a key measure, defined as the empirical mean of perceptual differences between consecutive images generated from interpolated latent space points. A smoother latent space is suggested to provide more coherent transitions between images, with PPL capturing both semantic and image quality aspects. The LPIPS metric is utilized to quantify perceptual distance, leveraging learned weights to align with human similarity judgments.

Additionally, the paper references a method proposed by Karras et al. to assess linear separability in latent spaces. This involves training auxiliary classifiers on binary attributes to determine how well different latent space points can be distinguished. The process includes generating a large number of images, classifying them, removing less confident samples, and fitting a linear SVM to predict labels based on latent-space vectors. A low conditional entropy value indicates effective disentanglement of factors of variation within the latent space. The final separability score aggregates these entropy measures across multiple attributes, enhancing the understanding of how well the latent space captures distinct features.
