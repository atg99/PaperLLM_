The paper discusses the manipulation of images generated by Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) through various transformations in latent space, such as brightness adjustments, rotations, zooms, and shifts in both the X and Y directions. It illustrates how navigating this latent space can lead to edited versions of generated images, highlighting the importance of comparing the distribution of these attributes in real and generated images to assess steerability.

Additionally, the authors emphasize the necessity of evaluating GANs using consistent metrics across multiple datasets to ensure reliability and reproducibility of results. They note the potential for detecting deepfakes due to their distinct spectral properties compared to authentic images.

The paper also addresses the critical issues of fairness and bias within generative models, acknowledging the growing public concern regarding these topics. It points out that bias can infiltrate models at various stages, including training, evaluation, and deployment. Recent efforts have been made to mitigate bias in GANs and utilize them to address fairness issues in other machine learning algorithms, underscoring the importance of a comprehensive evaluation when analyzing generative models.
