This section of the paper discusses various applications of Self-Organizing Maps (SOM) in deep learning and neural networks. Initially employed by Kohonen for speech recognition, SOM has evolved to serve multiple purposes, including pattern recognition, industrial diagnostics, and data mining, often in combination with architectures like RNN and CNN. A notable application is the phonetic typewriter, which identifies phonemes in real time to facilitate dictation.

The paper highlights advancements such as the Emergent Self-Organizing Map (ESOM) for clustering high-dimensional data while preserving neighborhood relationships, and the Growing Hierarchical Self-Organizing Map (GHSOM), which organizes multiple SOMs into a hierarchy for better data representation.

Specific case studies are presented, such as Potok's use of SOM for breast cancer diagnosis, classifying tumors using texture features, and Osman’s LWL-SOM technique for COVID-19 detection through chest X-ray analysis. Additionally, Jimin et al. applied SOM to visualize relationships in high-dimensional material datasets, demonstrating its superiority over traditional methods. Other studies explored the effectiveness of hybrid SOM implementations for hyperspectral data classification and proposed innovative clustering solutions in Mobile Ad Hoc Networks (MANETs) to improve performance and energy efficiency.

Furthermore, a distance-based intrusion detection system utilizing an unsupervised SOM network was introduced, showcasing the versatility and robustness of SOM in various domains. Overall, the paper illustrates the significant role of SOM in enhancing classification, clustering, and diagnostic capabilities across diverse applications.
