The paper discusses various deep learning algorithms, focusing on their applications and effectiveness in different scenarios. One notable model is proposed by Morteza Taki et al., which employs artificial network models such as Multilayer Perceptron (MLP), Radial Basis Function (RBF), and Gaussian Process Regression (GPR) to predict wheat output energy. Among these, the RBF model outperforms the others in accuracy for both irrigated and rainfed farms.

Jafari-Marandi et al. suggest an MLP architecture designed to minimize errors and packet loss in networks, consisting of an input layer, output layer, and multiple hidden layers. The MLP uses back-propagation learning, adjusting connection weights to reduce output errors during training with real data or simulated vectors. Additionally, the paper highlights the application of MLP in secure routing for the Internet of Vehicles, utilizing it to detect network intrusions and evaluate performance metrics such as precision, recall, and F1 score.

The study also introduces the Self-Organizing Map (SOM), an unsupervised neural network algorithm known for dimensionality reduction. The SOM comprises an input layer and a feature map output layer, lacking hidden layers, which allows it to convert high-dimensional data into a 2D representation. Its architecture relies on competitive learning, employing methods of competition, cooperation, and adaptation to update weights based on the input data and the distances between neurons.

Overall, the paper provides insights into the strengths and applications of different deep learning algorithms, particularly MLP and SOM, in various fields.
