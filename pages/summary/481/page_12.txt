This paper presents various applications of Deep Learning algorithms, specifically focusing on the Self-Organizing Map (SOM) across different fields. The authors summarize several studies that utilize SOM for diverse objectives, detailing their experimental designs, datasets, and performance measures.

Kohonen et al. applied SOM to speech recognition, employing preprocessing and clustering techniques on real speech signals, resulting in logical phoneme strings. Chen et al. focused on breast cancer diagnosis through RNN-SOM based autocorrelation texture classification, achieving an 85.6% accuracy in distinguishing between cancerous and non-cancerous tumors. Osman et al. utilized a locality weighted learning approach combined with SOM for identifying COVID-19 in chest X-rays, achieving an accuracy probability of 0.885.

In another medical application, Mehrbakhsh et al. employed a combination of Deep Belief Networks and SOM to remotely track Parkinson’s Disease progression, significantly improving accuracy in UPDRS predictions. Khanzadeh et al. used SOM to identify abnormal melt pools and predict porosity through ex-situ CT scan data, successfully classifying abnormal pool data.

In material science, Jafari et al. developed a cost-driven decision-making framework for laser-based additive manufacturing using a neural network-based SOM to detect pore locations and sizes. Weihua et al. introduced a semi-supervised distance-preserving SOM for machine-defect detection and classification, effectively identifying early gear-pitting failures and categorizing various levels of bearing defects.

Overall, the paper highlights the versatility and effectiveness of SOM in various domains, particularly in medical diagnostics and material science, showcasing its potential for improving accuracy in critical applications.
