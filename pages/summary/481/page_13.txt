The paper discusses various deep learning algorithms and their applications across different fields, highlighting experimental designs, datasets, and performance measures. 

Nicolas et al. conducted a study on medical applications, specifically diagnosing spinal column issues using Self-Organizing Maps (SOM) and K-Clustering. They utilized images from the UCI machine learning repository and evaluated performance through specificity, negative predictive value, precision, and Cohen’s Kappa index, concluding that SOM outperformed K-means in detecting vertebral problems.

Felix et al. focused on remote sensing, evaluating land cover classification and physical parameter regression using the SuSi Framework. They applied semi-supervised regression and SOM classification on two hyperspectral datasets, successfully addressing semi-supervised concepts through both unsupervised and supervised methods.

Srivatsa et al. investigated high-dimensional hyperspectral data classification using SOM and Cellular Self-Organizing Map (CSOM). They employed t-Distributed Stochastic Neighbor Embedding for data exploration and visualization, finding that CSOM excelled in overlapping classes while SOM was more effective for well-separated classes.

Asha Shiny and R. Jagadeesh aimed to maximize network lifespan by utilizing SOM to create energy-balanced cluster heads and members in a Mobile Ad Hoc Network (MANET). Their approach involved a random mobility model and aimed to extend network life based on energy usage.

Lastly, Abdulkadhim et al. proposed a hybrid network solution combining Software-Defined Networking (SDN) and SOM to enhance security and attack detection in a 5G-enabled Vehicle Ad Hoc Network (VANET). Their approach, utilizing the Dest_IP Database, aimed to provide a rapid, responsive network with minimized latency.

Overall, the paper underscores the versatility of deep learning algorithms across medical, remote sensing, network lifespan optimization, and security applications.
