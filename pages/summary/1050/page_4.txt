The paper discusses the relationship between learning techniques and explainability in artificial intelligence, emphasizing the importance of creating interpretable and explainable models. It categorizes learning techniques into three main types: interpretable models, deep learning, and model agnostic approaches. Interpretable models focus on developing structured and causal representations, with early examples such as Bayesian rule lists and Bayesian program learning. Deep learning can enhance explainability through careful design choices related to data selection, architecture, and optimization strategies. Model agnostic techniques treat any machine learning model as a black box to derive an approximate explainable model.

Explainable AI (XAI) aims to make AI behaviors understandable to humans by providing transparent explanations. Key principles for effective XAI systems include clarity about capabilities, actions, and the information being processed. Interpretability and explainability are context-dependent, varying by domain, and can be categorized into full or partial explanations. Fully interpretable models offer complete transparency, while partially interpretable models provide insights into their reasoning process through measures like variable importance and saliency maps.

Finally, the paper highlights user expectations regarding XAI, noting that explanations must cater to the diverse needs of end users who rely on AI outputs for decision-making. The framework suggests that understanding user context is vital for the effective deployment of XAI systems.
