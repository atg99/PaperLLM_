The paper discusses the varying needs for explanations in Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI) systems, highlighting different user groups such as intelligence analysts, judges, developers, and policymakers. Each group requires tailored explanations based on their background knowledge and specific needs. 

The section on explainability evaluation emphasizes the lack of standardized methods for measuring the effectiveness of explanations in XAI systems compared to non-XAI systems. Current measures include subjective user satisfaction and objective task performance metrics, but consistent evaluation remains a challenge. The paper outlines various frameworks for evaluation, focusing on mutual understanding and argumentation.

The paper also addresses key issues and challenges in XAI research, including the need for user-specific explanations, balancing accuracy and interpretability, simplifying explanations through abstractions, and understanding AI competencies. It calls for further exploration of how explanations can facilitate interactive learning and the need to identify AI system limitations and blind spots.
