The referenced works collectively explore various advancements in the application of attention mechanisms within deep learning, particularly in computer vision. 

Niu, Zhong, and Yu provide a comprehensive review of attention mechanisms, highlighting their significance in enhancing model performance by allowing networks to focus on relevant features. Wang et al. introduce the Residual Attention Network, which integrates attention modules to improve image classification accuracy. Hu, Shen, and Sun present Squeeze-and-Excitation Networks, emphasizing the importance of adaptively recalibrating channel-wise feature responses.

Woo et al. propose the Convolutional Block Attention Module (CBAM), which sequentially applies channel and spatial attention to refine feature maps. Park et al. introduce the Bottleneck Attention Module (BAM), which efficiently enhances feature representation with minimal computational overhead. Misra et al. innovate with the Convolutional Triplet Attention Module, utilizing a rotating mechanism to improve attention distribution.

Zhang et al. develop ShuffleNet, an efficient architecture for mobile devices, while Krizhevsky et al. discuss foundational techniques in learning features from small datasets. Sandler et al. present MobileNetV2, focusing on inverted residuals and linear bottlenecks for lightweight model design. Lastly, Fukui et al. explore the Attention Branch Network, which aims to learn attention mechanisms for better visual explanations.

These contributions collectively illustrate the evolution and impact of attention mechanisms in deep learning, particularly in enhancing the interpretability and efficiency of models in computer vision tasks.
