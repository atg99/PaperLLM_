The paper introduces a Global Attention Mechanism (GAM) aimed at enhancing cross-dimension interactions in image classification tasks. Previous attention mechanisms, such as Squeeze-and-Excitation Networks (SENet), focused primarily on channel attention but were inefficient in addressing pixel importance. Other methods like the Convolutional Block Attention Module (CBAM) and Bottleneck Attention Module (BAM) considered both spatial and channel dimensions but failed to capture channel-spatial interactions effectively.

The Triplet Attention Module (TAM) improved efficiency by incorporating attention weights across three dimensions—channel, spatial width, and height. However, it still applied attention operations on only two dimensions at a time. In contrast, GAM is designed to capture significant features across all three dimensions simultaneously.

GAM employs a sequential channel-spatial attention mechanism inspired by CBAM, with redesigned submodules. The process is mathematically defined, where input feature maps are transformed through channel and spatial attention maps. The channel attention submodule uses 3D permutation and a two-layer multi-layer perceptron (MLP) to enhance channel-spatial dependencies. The spatial attention submodule, focused on spatial information, employs convolutional layers without pooling to preserve feature maps, potentially increasing parameters, which is mitigated by using group convolution with channel shuffle techniques.

Overall, GAM aims to reduce information loss and enhance global dimension-interactive features, representing a significant advancement in attention mechanisms for image classification.
