The paper introduces a novel approach called GAM, which enhances salient cross-dimension receptive fields in convolutional neural networks (CNNs). Experimental results demonstrate that GAM outperforms the Convolutional Block Attention Module (CBAM) in various configurations, as detailed in the provided ablation studies on the ImageNet dataset. Specifically, the results indicate that GAM achieves lower top-1 and top-5 error rates compared to CBAM, showcasing its effectiveness across different architectures, including ResNet18.

The findings suggest that GAM is robust and capable of scaling with increased data, as evidenced by its performance on both CIFAR-100 and ImageNet-1K datasets. The paper emphasizes the importance of applying GAM to larger datasets for practical applications, acknowledging the challenges associated with training large models. Future research directions include exploring methods to reduce the number of parameters in larger networks, such as ResNet50 and ResNet101, as well as investigating other cross-dimension attention mechanisms that incorporate parameter-reduction techniques.

In conclusion, GAM presents a promising advancement in the field of CNNs, offering significant performance improvements and demonstrating potential for further development and application in scaling capabilities.
