In this section, the authors evaluate the Generalized Attention Mechanism (GAM) on CIFAR-100 and ImageNet-1K datasets, focusing on image classification and conducting two ablation studies. The choice of these datasets is justified by their standardization in classification tasks, with ImageNet-1K being particularly relevant for real-world applications.

The evaluation involves comparing GAM with established architectures, specifically ResNet and MobileNet V2, which represent standard and lightweight networks respectively. The performance of GAM is benchmarked against various attention mechanisms including Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE), Bottleneck Attention Module (BAM), Convolutional Block Attention Module (CBAM), Temporal Attention Module (TAM), and Attention Branch Network (ABN). All models were re-implemented under consistent conditions and trained on Nvidia Tesla V100 GPUs.

Results from the CIFAR-100 dataset indicate that GAM outperforms SE, BAM, and CBAM, as detailed in Table 1. For ResNet50, GAM achieves a Top-1 error rate of 18.67% and a Top-5 error rate of 4.54%, surpassing the other mechanisms. The experiments also explore the impact of group convolution on performance, revealing that GAM with group convolution yields a Top-1 error rate of 18.99%.

For the ImageNet-1K dataset, the authors preprocess images to a size of 224x224 and assess both ResNet18 and ResNet50 to ensure generalization across different network depths. The training regimen involves adjusting the learning rate and modifying certain architectural parameters for optimal performance. Overall, the experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of GAM in enhancing classification accuracy across both datasets.
