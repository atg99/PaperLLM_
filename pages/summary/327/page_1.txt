This editorial discusses the impact of large language models (LLMs), particularly Generative Pre-trained Transformers (GPT) 3 and 4, on medicine and surgery. Released by OpenAI, GPT-3, with 175 billion parameters, quickly gained popularity, surpassing 10 million daily users shortly after its launch. GPT-4, released in March 2023, demonstrates capabilities that exceed human performance on various professional exams, marking it as a versatile artificial intelligence tool across sectors, including healthcare.

LLMs like ChatGPT can produce coherent and contextually relevant text, making them suitable for various applications in clinical care and education. The editorial highlights potential uses in enhancing health literacy, facilitating decision-making, and generating written documentation. However, it also addresses the potential pitfalls associated with LLMs.

The authors provide a brief overview of LLMs as a subset of Natural Language Processing (NLP), emphasizing their ability to process and analyze human language. They categorize the outputs of LLMs into four main types: text classification, question answering, document summarization, and text generation. The editorial notes that while GPT-4 is currently the most advanced model, there are several others available, such as PaLM, LaMDA, and Google Bard.

The discussion also touches on the technological aspects of transformers, which enable efficient training of these models through parallel processing. This efficiency allows models like GPT-3 to be trained in a fraction of the time it would take using traditional methods. Overall, the editorial underscores the transformative potential of LLMs in the healthcare landscape while also cautioning about their limitations.
