The paper discusses the potential of Generative Pre-trained Transformers (GPT-3 and its successors) to enhance health literacy for both patients and healthcare providers. These advanced language models can efficiently abstract and present large volumes of data, making them more effective than traditional search engines. ChatGPT, as an intelligent chatbot, allows patients to ask specific health-related questions and receive clear, concise responses, thereby shaping their understanding of health and disease. This capability may lead to a shift in preference from conventional search engines, like Google, to ChatGPT for reliable healthcare information.

However, the paper also highlights several limitations of using ChatGPT for health inquiries. The model can generate factually incorrect information, produce biased content, and lacks training on recent developments, which can lead to outdated or harmful recommendations. Additionally, ethical considerations such as privacy risks, the potential for misinformation, and the legal implications of LLM outputs are discussed. Despite these drawbacks, the authors acknowledge the dynamic nature of ChatGPT as a learning tool, emphasizing the need for users to remain aware of its limitations while navigating healthcare information. 

In summary, while GPT-based technologies hold promise for improving health literacy and access to information, careful consideration of their limitations and ethical implications is essential to ensure safe and effective use in the healthcare context.
