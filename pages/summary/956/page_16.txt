The paper discusses the challenges of encoding qubit states using a continuous encoder, particularly in the context of the Bloch sphere representation. The qubit state is parameterized with two parameters, where the encoder aims to map these states through fixed binary projective measurements. However, the assumption of continuity in the encoder leads to a contradiction. This contradiction arises when considering the periodic nature of the phase parameter, which implies that the encoder cannot maintain continuity across discontinuities in the qubit representation.

The argument is structured around the mathematical representations and limits, illustrating that if the encoder were continuous, it would not be able to handle the periodicity of the phase variable effectively. The paper emphasizes the implications of such discontinuities in the context of quantum information processing and suggests that the representation of qubit states requires careful consideration of continuity and discontinuity in encoders.

Overall, the findings highlight the limitations of continuous mappings in quantum state encoding and the necessity of addressing these issues when designing quantum algorithms or protocols.
