The paper discusses the conservation of angular momentum in a classical mechanics context and its representation in a neural network model, SciNet. It describes a scenario involving a rotating body fixed on a rod and a free particle that collides with it, emphasizing the conservation of total angular momentum. SciNet is shown to effectively learn and store this conserved quantity in its latent representation.

The paper also addresses the prediction of measurement probabilities for binary projective measurements on pure n-qubit states. It introduces an operational parameterization for both states and measurements and demonstrates that SciNet can determine the minimal parameters necessary to describe a state without prior knowledge of quantum physics. The model distinguishes between tomographically complete and incomplete sets of measurements, with the latter leading to limitations in prediction accuracy. The findings highlight that increasing the dimensionality of the subspace in the two-qubit case enhances prediction accuracy and the number of latent neurons required.

In summary, the research illustrates the potential of SciNet in capturing and representing physical quantities and quantum states, providing insights into the nature of measurement and information in quantum systems.
