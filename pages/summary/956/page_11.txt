The paper explores the prediction of the position of a one-dimensional damped pendulum over time using a neural network model named SciNet. The pendulum's motion is described by a differential equation incorporating the spring constant and damping factor. The solution to the equation provides the position of the pendulum as a function of time.

The study involves training SciNet on time series data of the pendulum's positions, with fixed parameters for mass and amplitude, while varying the spring constant and damping factor. SciNet is designed with three latent neurons and aims to predict the pendulum's position at future times, achieving a root mean square error below 2% relative to the amplitude.

Key findings indicate that SciNet successfully extracts and stores the relevant physical parameters—the spring constant and damping factor—in its latent representation, demonstrating an effective learning process without prior exposure to physical concepts. The first latent neuron correlates predominantly with the damping factor, while the second is primarily linked to the spring constant. This showcases SciNet's ability to learn and represent essential characteristics of the physical system from the provided data.
