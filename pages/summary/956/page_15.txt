This paper discusses the application of SciNet in quantum tomography and celestial mechanics, highlighting its ability to predict outcomes based on tomographic data and initial conditions. In quantum tomography, SciNet processes tomographic data for one or two qubits to predict measurement outcomes, effectively identifying the minimal parameters needed to describe a quantum state. It demonstrates that with tomographically complete data, SciNet achieves high prediction accuracy, while incomplete data results in lower accuracy, which can be used to gauge the information content of the data.

In celestial mechanics, SciNet is tasked with predicting the angles of Mars and the Sun as viewed from Earth, using initial angles derived from simulated observations. The model shows impressive accuracy, predicting angles with a root mean square error below 0.4 percent.

The paper also addresses the challenges of representing cyclic parameters within neural networks, particularly in relation to data that forms closed manifolds, such as spheres. It explains that neural networks struggle with discontinuities found in such representations, which leads to errors in encoding. The discussion emphasizes the need for careful consideration of how neural networks map these complex data structures to achieve accurate predictions.
