This paper presents SciNet, a neural network model designed to predict time-dependent variables in physical systems, using the heliocentric model of the solar system as a case study. The model processes observations of angles of celestial bodies from Earth and evolves a latent representation over time to make predictions about future angles. The architecture consists of an encoder, a time evolution network, and decoders that work together to generate these predictions. 

Key findings indicate that when reference measurements are tomographically complete, increasing the number of latent neurons reduces prediction error, aligning with quantum mechanics principles. However, if measurements are incomplete, prediction accuracy cannot improve regardless of latent neuron count, revealing insights about the completeness of measurements and the degrees of freedom in the quantum system.

The paper emphasizes the importance of enforcing simple time evolution rules within the model, utilizing constant addition as a method for parameter updates. Training involves simulating weekly observations of Mars and the Sun's angles, confirming that SciNet effectively transitions to a heliocentric representation, even though training data is Earth-centric.

In conclusion, SciNet demonstrates the capability to recover physical variables from experimental data, yielding representations consistent with traditional physics frameworks. Future research aims to expand this methodology to more complex datasets with intricate underlying parameters.
