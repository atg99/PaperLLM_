The paper discusses the entanglement properties of Restricted Boltzmann Machines (RBM) and their ability to represent quantum states efficiently. It highlights that while RBMs can approximate any many-body quantum state, this often requires an impractical exponential number of hidden spins and parameters. The focus is on identifying quantum states that can be described with polynomial scaling in the number of physical spins.

The authors emphasize the significance of entanglement in understanding the representability of RBM states. Previous studies indicated that RBM states with short-range interactions adhere to an area law of entanglement, linking them to matrix-product states (MPS). Specifically, it was established that the Renyi entropies of such states are bounded, suggesting a close relationship between short-range RBM states and MPS representations. Conversely, long-range RBM states can exhibit volume-law entanglement, implying that their MPS representations would require exponential scaling.

The paper introduces a new family of variational RBM ansatzes characterized by random uncorrelated phases, aiming to explore the entanglement structure and its dependence on variational parameters. This extension to the short-range RBM ansatz incorporates independent and identically distributed random phases while maintaining local interactions between spins. The proposed ansatz wave function is designed to investigate the absence of an entanglement transition within this class of wave functions, contrasting with classical phase transitions in wave function weights.
