The paper presents a study of entanglement properties in a specific model characterized by uncorrelated random phases, focusing on the average Renyi entropies. The authors derive a lower bound for the Renyi entropies using an annealed average, which allows for analytical calculations. They show that for the second Renyi entropy, the behavior remains consistent across different system sizes, with extensive scaling observed at low values of phi.

Despite the apparent crossings in the entropy curves, the authors argue that these are artifacts of finite-size effects rather than indicative of a genuine phase transition. They provide analytical expressions for the second Renyi entropy, which depend on the system size and variational parameters, facilitating further exploration of the entanglement landscape in this model. The results point to the absence of an entanglement transition in the system being studied, with implications for understanding entanglement in quantum systems with random phases.
