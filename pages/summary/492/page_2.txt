We analyze the entanglement scaling of RBM quantum states characterized by random phases. Our findings reveal that when random phases are uncorrelated, no entanglement transition occurs; however, we observe significant finite-size effects in numerical simulations. By introducing correlated phases that decay as 1/r in real space, we identify three distinct regions with varying entanglement entropy scaling based on the correlation exponent. We provide numerical evidence for critical behavior at the transitions between these regions.

Our study highlights the essential role of long-range correlated phases in RBM-based wave functions for facilitating entanglement transitions, suggesting that such correlations are a necessary condition for observing these phenomena. This work contributes to the understanding of entangled states in quantum systems and opens avenues for further exploration of entanglement transitions beyond traditional classical mappings.
