This paper explores the use of neural-network inspired variational approaches, particularly focusing on restricted Boltzmann machines (RBMs), to model quantum wave functions in many-body quantum physics. Machine learning has significantly advanced the understanding of quantum phases and transitions, including symmetry-breaking and topological phases, and has been applied to measuring quantum entanglement and wave function tomography.

The authors investigate the ability of RBMs to capture entanglement transitions without being influenced by classical phase transitions. They specifically analyze short-range RBM quantum states, where the real part of the wave function is a local function of spin degrees of freedom. This setup intentionally avoids classical phase transitions, allowing for a more focused study on entanglement transitions.

The paper is structured as follows: after an introduction to RBMs and their entanglement properties, the authors study short-range RBM states with uncorrelated random phases, demonstrating that entanglement scales with volume-law for all variational parameters, indicating no entanglement transition. They then analyze short-range RBM states with power-law correlated phases, revealing three distinct scaling regimes of entanglement entropy and suggesting the presence of an entanglement transition in a two-dimensional phase diagram defined by the power-law exponent and inverse temperature.

The authors conclude by discussing their findings and proposing future research directions. They emphasize the representational power of RBMs and their potential in exploring the entanglement structure of quantum states, particularly under varying parameters.
