The paper investigates the scaling regimes of entanglement entropy in quantum systems using a neural-network inspired variational Ansatz, specifically a restricted Boltzmann machine (RBM) quantum state. The study focuses on how varying a parameter, denoted as beta (β), influences the entanglement properties across different system sizes.

Key findings include:

1. For β < 1, the entanglement entropy exhibits a volume-law behavior, with increasing system size leading to larger values of the parameter phi (ϕ). The crossing points of curves for different system sizes suggest a consistent volume law entanglement.

2. In the range of 1 < β < 2, there is evidence of sub-extensive scaling of entropy, particularly at β values close to 1, where crossings are observed. For larger β values, such as 1.66, the entropy aligns with a sub-extensive N^(2-β) scaling, indicating a critical region separating volume-law and sub-extensive regimes.

3. For β > 2, the entanglement entropy appears independent of system size, reflecting area-law scaling, thus confirming three distinct scaling regimes in the proposed phase diagram.

4. The analysis suggests that the observed finite-size effects may indicate a crossover instead of a true entanglement transition for certain ranges of β. This necessitates cautious interpretation of results, particularly in the context of finite-size scaling.

5. The study utilizes numerical simulations and analytical bounds to explore the behavior of the second Renyi entropy, concluding that the short-range RBM Ansatz supports volume-law entanglement across finite values of ϕ, ruling out the presence of an entanglement transition.

6. When extending the model to incorporate long-range correlated phases, the authors find that the analytical methods become inapplicable, leading to reliance on numerical simulations to explore the entangling rate.

In summary, the research highlights the complex interplay between system size, entanglement entropy, and the parameter β, contributing to the understanding of entanglement transitions in quantum systems. Further investigations are suggested to clarify the nature of the observed behaviors, especially in the context of finite-size effects.
