When considering correlated random phases, the authors propose a modified RBM Ansatz that retains locality while allowing for the introduction of power-law correlations between phases. This modification leads to a complex formulation of the wave function, with the real part maintaining local interactions and the imaginary part exhibiting distance-dependent correlations.

The high φ limit analysis reveals that the entanglement structure is influenced by the phases encoded in a Hamiltonian that is diagonal in the computational basis. This Hamiltonian features long-range power-law interactions, determined by the correlation exponent. The study aims to elucidate how these modifications affect the entanglement properties of the system, suggesting that the introduction of correlations may pave the way for a deeper understanding of entanglement transitions in RBM states. 

Overall, the paper posits that while short-range RBM states with uncorrelated phases do not exhibit an entanglement transition, the exploration of correlated phases introduces new dynamics that merit further investigation into their entanglement characteristics.
