The paper discusses the analysis of systems with power-law interactions, proposing a hypothetical phase diagram that includes three regions characterized by different scaling of entanglement entropy for large values of a parameter. Numerical simulations indicate a critical curve exists in the parameter plane for smaller values, although the limited number of spins studied prevents definitive conclusions about the nature of the transition—whether it is a genuine phase transition or a crossover.

Future research directions suggest exploring beyond Restricted Boltzmann Machine (RBM) states. Recent findings indicate that advanced deep learning architectures, like deep convolutional and recurrent networks, can effectively represent highly entangled quantum systems and support volume-law entanglement scaling more efficiently than RBMs. This motivates a study using these modern neural network-based wave functions.

The paper also includes an appendix detailing the derivation of the annealed average of the second Renyi entropy for specific quantum states. It outlines the necessary calculations involving the reduced density matrix and the average over disorder realizations, ultimately leading to a formula for the trace of the squared reduced density matrix.
