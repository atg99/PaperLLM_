The paper evaluates the performance of various LSTM architectures, particularly focusing on the proposed LSTM with Working Memory Connections (LSTM-WM) for the image captioning task using the Microsoft COCO dataset. The results, presented in Table 3, compare different models based on standard captioning evaluation metrics including BLEU, METEOR, ROUGE, CIDEr, and SPICE.

The analysis reveals that LSTM-WM consistently outperforms traditional LSTM and peephole LSTM (LSTM-PH) models across both attention and no attention settings. Specifically, LSTM-WM shows a significant improvement, achieving 2.0 CIDEr points higher than vanilla LSTM without attention and 0.8 CIDEr points higher with attention. In contrast, peephole LSTM often performs similarly or worse than the vanilla LSTM.

The study employs two captioning models: Show and Tell, which utilizes a single LSTM layer without attention, and Up-Down, which incorporates attention mechanisms with two LSTM layers. The feature representation for Show and Tell is derived from ResNet-152, while Up-Down uses multiple feature vectors from Faster R-CNN. Both models are trained using the Adam optimizer with a specified learning rate.

Overall, the findings indicate that LSTM-WM enhances image captioning performance, highlighting the effectiveness of incorporating working memory connections in LSTM architectures. Additionally, the results suggest no significant advantage of GRU over LSTM for this specific task.
