The paper "Working Memory Connections for LSTM" by Federico Landi et al. presents a novel approach to enhance Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks by integrating information from the internal cell state into the gating mechanisms. LSTMs are widely used in sequence modeling tasks due to their ability to manage long-term dependencies while addressing the exploding and vanishing gradient problems. However, traditional LSTMs do not allow the memory cell to influence the gates directly.

The authors propose a modification called Working Memory Connection, which involves adding a learnable nonlinear projection of the cell content into the network gates. This approach does not impose any specific assumptions on the tasks and is particularly effective for longer sequences. The paper identifies limitations in previous research efforts related to integrating the cell state into gating structures, which have hindered improvements over standard LSTMs.

Through extensive experimental evaluations, the authors demonstrate that Working Memory Connections consistently enhance LSTM performance across various tasks, indicating that the information contained in the cell state is valuable for the gating structure. The findings suggest that incorporating this information can lead to significant advancements in the effectiveness of LSTM networks.
