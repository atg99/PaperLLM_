This paper discusses the behavior of Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks, specifically focusing on the challenges posed by peephole connections in LSTMs compared to Working Memory Connections (WMCs). The authors highlight that the cell state (ct) can increase linearly with time steps, leading to issues in stability during training. They argue that in peephole LSTMs, the term involving the previous cell state (Wicct−1) can grow unbounded without proper regulation, causing the sigmoid activation function to potentially saturate, which hinders learning.

Equations presented in the paper illustrate how gradients associated with the peephole weights can become unstable due to their linear growth with ct. This instability can result in the saturation of both the input and forget gates, exacerbating the uncontrolled growth of ct. In contrast, the WMCs provide a more stable approach, effectively managing the behavior of the memory cell and preventing such growth.

The authors support their claims with empirical results, demonstrating that WMCs outperform peephole connections across various tasks. The experiments indicate that WMCs enhance the stability and effectiveness of LSTM networks, leading to improved performance in sequential tasks.
