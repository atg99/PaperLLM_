The paper presents results on the performance of various LSTM variants on sequential and permuted MNIST tasks, as well as character-level language modeling on the Penn Treebank (PTB) dataset. 

In Table 1, the accuracy of different models on the sequential MNIST (sMNIST) and permuted MNIST (pMNIST) tasks is reported. The proposed LSTM with Working Memory Cells (LSTM-WC) demonstrates superior performance, achieving the highest accuracy in both tasks compared to traditional LSTM variants and state-of-the-art RNNs. Specifically, LSTM-WC outperforms classic LSTMs by 0.47% on sMNIST and 1.03% on pMNIST, despite having fewer learnable parameters.

Table 2 details the mean bits per character (BPC) for the models evaluated on the PTB dataset, where lower BPC indicates better performance. The results show that LSTM-WC consistently achieves lower BPC values compared to traditional LSTMs, indicating a more efficient modeling of character sequences.

Overall, the findings suggest that the introduction of Working Memory Cells enhances the LSTM architecture, leading to improved performance across different tasks while maintaining a competitive parameter count.
