This paper discusses the behavior of recurrent neural networks, particularly focusing on Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) architectures and their components. The equations presented illustrate the relationships between input data (xt), hidden states (ht), and cell states (ct) over time, highlighting the bounded nature of xt and ht within the interval [-1, 1]. In contrast, ct can grow linearly with recursive steps, which poses challenges for stability and control within the network.

The introduction of the forget gate in LSTMs addresses the issue of unbounded growth in cell states, as it helps manage the influence of ct on the gating mechanisms, thereby preventing saturation of the sigmoid functions used in the model. This is crucial since the hyperbolic tangent non-linearity serves to mitigate the impact of ct during updates, maintaining stability in the weight matrices.

The paper also touches on peephole connections, initially introduced by Gers and Schmidhuber, which allow for direct access to the cell state in the gating equations. While these connections can enhance control over the gates, the paper identifies limitations related to gradient behavior, particularly noting that the local gradient can be affected by the structure of the peephole connections. Overall, the discussion emphasizes the delicate balance between managing cell state dynamics and maintaining stable updates in LSTM networks.
