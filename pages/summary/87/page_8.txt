The paper presents a comparison of three types of Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) models: traditional LSTM, LSTM with peephole connections (LSTM-PH), and a novel LSTM model with working memory connections (LSTM-WM). The models are evaluated on various tasks including the adding problem, copying tasks, and digit recognition using the Sequential and Permuted MNIST datasets.

In the adding problem, the input consists of pairs of real-valued numbers and corresponding markers, with the goal of predicting the sum of these numbers over a sequence. The results indicate that LSTM-WM outperforms both LSTM and LSTM-PH in terms of Mean Squared Error (MSE) across different time lengths (T=200 and T=400). 

For the copying tasks, accuracy metrics are presented, showing a consistent advantage for LSTM-WM over the other models. The performance is evaluated over multiple epochs, illustrating the effectiveness of the working memory connections in improving learning outcomes.

Overall, the analysis of these simple tasks highlights the benefits of the proposed LSTM architecture, suggesting its potential for more complex real-world applications. The findings advocate for the adoption of the LSTM-WM model, particularly when dealing with long sequences, due to its superior performance in capturing dependencies within the data.
