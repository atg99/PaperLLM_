The paper discusses the introduction of Working Memory Connections (WMCs) in Long Short-Term Memory Networks (LSTMs) to enhance their performance. WMCs improve the traditional LSTM formulation by allowing the cell state to influence gate dynamics, which contributes to increased stability during training compared to peephole connections. The authors argue that exposing different projections of the cell state without squashing its content is crucial for training stability, leading to performance gains even when peephole connections fail.

Despite the advantages of WMCs, the authors note that their benefits may diminish in stacked LSTM configurations due to increased network complexity. The influence of WMCs in complex architectures that integrate LSTMs with other components is also acknowledged as an area needing further exploration. The paper presents experimental results, particularly in image captioning, demonstrating that WMCs provide a modest improvement in performance.

In conclusion, the authors emphasize that WMCs address key limitations of traditional LSTMs and offer a stable training mechanism. They suggest that further research could explore the efficacy of WMCs across a broader range of tasks. The work was supported by initiatives aimed at advancing AI in digital humanities and data innovation in heritage areas.
