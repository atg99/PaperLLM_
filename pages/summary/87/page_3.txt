This paper investigates the implications of directly exposing the internal state of Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks without appropriate protection, demonstrating that such exposure results in unstable training dynamics that adversely affect final performance. The authors introduce a solution that effectively addresses these issues across various tasks, including those with long-term dependencies, language modeling, and image captioning.

The related work section outlines the evolution of LSTM networks, which were developed to counter the exploding and vanishing gradient problems through gating mechanisms. While several modifications to the LSTM structure have emerged, such as Liu et al.’s two-gate recurrent block and Arpit et al.’s gradient path modification, these approaches either oversimplify the model or focus on specific optimizations. The authors highlight the popularity of the Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) and acknowledge the success of self-attention models like Transformers, while also emphasizing the continued relevance of LSTMs due to their low computational demands, making them suitable for embedded systems.

In the proposed method section, the authors present their concept of Working Memory Connections (WMCs). They detail the original LSTM equations and the modifications made in their design, providing a rationale for these changes. The authors identify and address key weaknesses in prior cell-to-gate connections that impede the learning process, asserting that their proposed solution overcomes these limitations, thereby enhancing training stability and performance across diverse tasks.
