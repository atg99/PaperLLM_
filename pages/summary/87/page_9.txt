This paper investigates the performance of different LSTM architectures, particularly focusing on the proposed LSTM with Working Memory Connections (WMCs), in solving sequential tasks. The experiments include an adding problem and a copying task, where the models aim to predict sums of real-valued items or reproduce sequences of input symbols.

In the adding problem, where only two markers are set to 1, the models were tested with time steps T=200 and T=400. The performance was evaluated using mean squared error (MSE) over 200 epochs with SGD optimization, a learning rate of 0.01, and a batch size of 128. Results show that WMCs lead to improved convergence, especially in the T=400 setup, where it successfully learns the task while other models remain ineffective.

The copying task, following the setup of previous research, involved reproducing a sequence of 10 input symbols after a delay. Using a similar training methodology over 500 epochs, WMCs demonstrated significant advantages in both performance and convergence speed compared to peephole LSTM and vanilla LSTM, particularly with longer sequences.

The paper also explores the permuted sequential MNIST (pMNIST) task, which involves recognizing digits from sequentially fed permuted image pixels. The experimental setup mirrored previous studies, with 50,000 training images. Findings indicate that WMCs outperform other LSTM variants in terms of final accuracy and convergence time, achieving over 92% accuracy within 50 epochs, while other models lagged behind.

Overall, the results underscore the effectiveness of WMCs in enhancing LSTM architectures for sequential learning tasks, particularly in terms of stability and speed of convergence.
