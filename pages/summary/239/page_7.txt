The paper presents a study on the performance evaluation of two convolutional neural networks, COV19-CNNet and COV19-ResNet, in the context of COVID-19 detection. Key metrics such as accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, and precision are defined mathematically, with formulas provided for each metric. A confusion matrix is utilized to illustrate the classification outcomes of both models, highlighting true positives, true negatives, false positives, and false negatives. Additionally, training and testing curves for accuracy and loss are depicted for both networks, allowing for a visual comparison of their performance throughout the training process. The results aim to demonstrate the effectiveness and reliability of these models in detecting COVID-19, with implications for their application in clinical settings.
