The paper presents the development of two deep learning-based diagnostic inference engines, COV19-CNNet and COV19-ResNet, aimed at the early detection of COVID-19 using chest X-ray (CXR) images. The authors highlight the limitations of traditional diagnostic methods such as reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR), which can be slow and unreliable for early diagnosis, and chest CT scans, which pose risks such as excessive radiation and high costs.

The study includes a retrospective analysis of a dataset comprising 210 COVID-19 cases, 350 viral pneumonia cases, and 350 healthy controls, focusing on multi-class classification. COV19-ResNet, utilizing residual neural network architecture, achieved a classification accuracy of 97.61%, while COV19-CNNet, based on convolutional neural network architecture, attained 94.28% accuracy. Notably, both engines were developed from scratch with novel deep neural network architectures, without relying on pre-trained models.

The findings suggest that these inference engines could serve as effective tools for the early detection of COVID-19, distinguishing it from similar conditions, thereby aiding in timely treatment and reducing the burden on healthcare systems. The study underscores the potential of leveraging deep learning technology in enhancing COVID-19 diagnostic capabilities.
