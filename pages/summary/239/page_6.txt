The paper presents a comparative analysis of two deep learning models, COV19-ResNet and COV19-CNNet, for diagnosing COVID-19 using a test set of 70 samples. The results indicate that COV19-ResNet achieved an accuracy of 97.61%, while COV19-CNNet had an accuracy of 94.28%. The performance measures evaluated include accuracy, precision, recall, specificity, and F1 score, with detailed results provided in Table 1. 

For COVID-19 detection, both models showed perfect accuracy, but COV19-CNNet had a lower precision (98.36%) compared to COV19-ResNet (100%). In the normal class, COV19-CNNet demonstrated a precision of 86.42%, whereas COV19-ResNet achieved 100%. For viral pneumonia, COV19-CNNet had a precision of 100%, while COV19-ResNet's was lower at 82.86%. 

Overall, the average performance metrics across the classes highlight the superiority of COV19-ResNet in most measures, particularly in precision and recall, suggesting it may be the more reliable model for accurate COVID-19 diagnosis. Figures and tables illustrate the architectures of the models and summarize the performance metrics comprehensively.
