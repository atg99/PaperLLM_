To address this, we implemented a residual network architecture, COV19-ResNet, which incorporates skip connections to allow gradients to flow more effectively during training. This architecture enhances the learning process by enabling deeper networks without the vanishing gradient issue.

The COV19-ResNet was designed to classify CXR images into three categories: COVID-19, viral pneumonia, and normal. The model achieved remarkable performance with a perfect accuracy, precision, recall, specificity, and F1 score of 100% for COVID-19 detection. However, it recorded slightly lower accuracy for the other classes, with 95.71% for normal and 97.14% for viral pneumonia.

The study highlights the importance of using a robust dataset, which included 210 COVID-19 images, 350 viral pneumonia images, and 350 normal images, significantly larger than many previous studies. This larger dataset was critical in developing models capable of high reliability and performance.

In conclusion, our approach utilized advanced deep learning techniques to construct two neural network models, COV19-CNNet and COV19-ResNet, demonstrating significant potential in the automated detection of COVID-19 from CXR images. Future work may focus on further enhancing accuracy and generalizability through continued dataset expansion and model refinement.
