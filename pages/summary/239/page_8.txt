The paper discusses the performance of deep learning (DL) models in detecting COVID-19 from chest X-ray images, focusing on two specific models: COV19-CNNet and COV19-ResNet. Key metrics such as true positives (TP), true negatives (TN), false negatives (FN), and false positives (FP) are defined, along with recall and F1 score formulas. The study employs early stopping techniques during training to prevent overfitting, ensuring the best model weights are utilized.

A comparison of various DL applications in detecting COVID-19 is presented in a table, highlighting the accuracy, specificity, sensitivity, and F1 scores of different models across multiple datasets. Notably, the proposed models achieved an accuracy of 94.28% for COV19-CNNet and 97.61% for COV19-ResNet, outperforming several other models such as VGG19 and SqueezeNet.

The datasets used include various classes such as Normal (N), COVID-19 (C), Non-COVID-19 (NC), Pneumonia (P), Viral pneumonia (VP), and Bacterial pneumonia (BP), demonstrating the models' effectiveness in distinguishing between these categories. Overall, the findings emphasize the potential of deep learning techniques in enhancing COVID-19 detection through chest X-ray imaging.
