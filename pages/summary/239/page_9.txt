The paper discusses the application of deep learning (DL) in medical imaging, particularly for the detection of COVID-19 and other diseases such as pneumonia and cancer. DL's strong feature extraction capabilities have led to successful applications in differentiating viral pneumonia and detecting conditions from chest X-ray (CXR) images. The authors emphasize the importance of early COVID-19 detection, noting that while CXR and CT scans are used for diagnosis, CXR is preferred due to its accessibility, cost-effectiveness, and lower health risks despite its lower sensitivity compared to CT.

The paper highlights various studies that have utilized different deep learning models for COVID-19 detection. For instance, Apostolopoulos and Mpesiana achieved 97.40% accuracy in binary classification but faced challenges with multi-class classification due to data imbalance. Loey et al. used Generative Adversarial Networks (GAN) to augment their limited dataset, achieving nearly perfect accuracy in binary classification and high accuracy in multi-class tasks, although they noted discrepancies between validation and test accuracies.

Other studies referenced include those by Ozturk et al., who utilized the YOLO model, achieving high accuracy in both binary and multi-class classifications, and Ucar and Korkmaz, who improved classification accuracy significantly through data augmentation. The authors conclude that while DL methods show promise in improving COVID-19 detection, challenges such as data imbalance and the need for careful validation remain critical considerations in developing effective diagnostic tools.
