The paper highlights the importance of CT scans in identifying complications associated with COVID-19 that may not be visible on chest X-rays (CXR). It emphasizes the role of radiologists in accurately reporting findings, particularly ground glass opacities that indicate COVID-19 infection. Despite the effectiveness of CT scans, the paper discusses the risks of virus transmission during imaging procedures and the higher radiation exposure associated with CT compared to CXR. The American College of Radiology recommends cautious use of CT and suggests alternatives like portable radiography units.

The paper further explores the application of artificial intelligence, particularly deep learning (DL), in improving diagnostic accuracy for COVID-19. It reviews existing models and their performances, noting that while pre-trained models have been used successfully, they often fall short in medical imaging tasks due to differences between natural and medical image datasets. The authors argue for the development of custom models tailored specifically for medical imaging.

In contrast to previous studies that utilized pre-trained models, this research focuses on creating two deep neural networks from scratch—COV19-CNNet and COV19-ResNet—to optimize detection capabilities. The paper underscores the significance of determining the right number of hidden layers to enhance model performance, which is critical for accurately diagnosing COVID-19 from CXR images.
