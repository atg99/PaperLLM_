The paper presents the development of a deep neural network named COV19-ResNet, which is based on the ResNet architecture and aims to improve image classification in chest X-ray (CXR) images for COVID-19 detection. The COV19-ResNet network processes input images of size 224 x 224 x 1 using a series of convolutional and pooling layers, including a 7 x 7 convolution and 2 x 2 max-pooling. The architecture features two types of residual blocks: each "resnet_block" includes a combination of convolutional and identity blocks, which allow for effective residual learning.

The identity blocks consist of three layers with an identity connection, while the convolution blocks are composed of three convolutional layers and a downsampling layer. The downsampling is achieved through a stride of 2 in the convolutional layers, ensuring that the input size is appropriately reduced without mismatches.

The dataset used for training and testing the network consisted of 1000 CXR images categorized into three classes: COVID-19, normal, and viral pneumonia. The dataset was divided into training (60%), validation (20%), and testing (20%) subsets. The performance of COV19-ResNet was evaluated using confusion matrices, showing perfect classification rates for COVID-19 and normal classes, while the viral pneumonia class had some misclassifications.

Overall, the study demonstrates the effectiveness of the COV19-ResNet architecture in accurately classifying CXR images, highlighting the advantages of residual learning in deep network training.
