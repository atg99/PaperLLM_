The paper discusses the differences between Graph Convolutional Networks (GCN) and scattering channels in processing signals on graphs, particularly in terms of their ability to recognize regularity patterns. Two lemmas are presented to illustrate these differences. 

Lemma 1 shows that for a cyclic graph with a 2-periodic signal, GCN filtering results in a constant signal, while the scattering filter preserves the periodic nature of the signal. This indicates that GCNs struggle to differentiate between alternating signals on isolated cyclic structures, whereas scattering channels can distinguish them effectively. 

Lemma 2 extends this observation to bipartite graphs, where GCN filtering also leads to a constant signal for a 2-coloring input, while scattering filters maintain a non-constant 2-coloring. This highlights the limitations of GCNs in capturing specific signal regularities compared to scattering approaches.

The paper further emphasizes that graph wavelets can encode geometric information, even in the absence of varying input signals, which is beneficial for tasks like community detection. An example illustrates that while GCN filter responses may not be constant, they do not differentiate between distinct cyclic structures, unlike scattering filters, which can effectively separate these structures.

The empirical results section aims to evaluate the proposed Scattering GCN method against established semi-supervised node classification techniques, including the original GCN, noting the latter's limitations. 

Overall, the findings suggest that scattering channels offer significant advantages over GCNs in maintaining and distinguishing signal properties, particularly in complex graph structures.
