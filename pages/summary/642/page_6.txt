In this context, scattering channels enhance the model's ability to learn important frequency information by applying transformations that maintain the distinction between node-level activations. 

The architecture consists of weight matrices and bias vectors that facilitate the transformation of original node features into a hidden space. The design incorporates three GCN channels and two scattering channels, utilizing a node-level nonlinearity to ensure that each node's unique characteristics are preserved. The width of the channels is crucial, as wider channels enable the model to capture more significant regularities in the data, while narrower channels may limit the learning capacity regarding specific frequency windows.

To address the challenges posed by sparse graph sections, the proposed graph residual convolution acts as a low-pass filter, effectively managing high-frequency noise that could obscure the learning process. This innovative approach draws inspiration from residual neural networks and introduces a hyperparameter to control the cutoff frequency, allowing for flexibility in the aggregation of node-level information.

The architecture's potential is further demonstrated through an examination of the additional information provided by scattering features, which contribute to the model's overall efficacy in node classification tasks. By leveraging both GCN and scattering channels, the architecture is positioned to capture a richer spectrum of information, facilitating improved performance in understanding complex graph structures.
