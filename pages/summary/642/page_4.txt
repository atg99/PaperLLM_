The paper discusses the limitations of Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs), particularly focusing on the oversmoothing problem that arises from repeatedly applying graph convolutions. As more graph convolutions are stacked, the model increasingly smooths the signals, diminishing the ability to distinguish between nodes and leading to a loss of information. 

To address these issues, the authors introduce the concept of geometric scattering on graphs, which employs a lazy random walk matrix that allows for self-loops while maintaining a Markov process. This method performs a low-pass operation that preserves low-frequency components of signals while suppressing high frequencies. The authors augment this low-pass filtering with wavelet matrices, enabling the recovery of high frequencies through multiscale wavelet transforms. 

The geometric scattering approach originally focused on whole-graph classification by aggregating scattering features through wavelet transforms. However, this paper adapts the method for node-based classification, eliminating the aggregation step and defining a new scattering propagation rule that substitutes the GCN low-pass filter with a geometric scattering operation. This adaptation aims to enhance the representation of node features while addressing the limitations of classical GCNs.
