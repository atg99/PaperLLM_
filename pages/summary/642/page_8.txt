The paper discusses the oversmoothing problem in graph convolutional networks (GCNs) and compares a proposed hybrid approach, Scattering GCN, with existing methods. The oversmoothing issue arises in highly connected graph regions, leading to rapid mixing of node features, which degrades classification performance. The authors reference methods like partially absorbing random walks to directly address oversmoothing and the graph attention network (GAT), which uses an attention mechanism for adaptive node-wise weighting to mitigate the problem indirectly.

The study evaluates Scattering GCN against two baseline methods: a classifier based solely on handcrafted graph-scattering features and an SVM classifier that uses only node features, ignoring graph structure. The evaluation is conducted on four benchmark datasets (Citeseer, Cora, Pubmed, DBLP), with results showing that Scattering GCN outperforms other methods in three out of four datasets, particularly excelling in DBLP, where it achieves over 15% improvement over GAT.

The paper also highlights the relationship between dataset connectivity and classification accuracy, noting that increased connectivity exacerbates the oversmoothing problem and impacts performance. The authors conducted a grid search for hyperparameter tuning and included an ablation study in the supplementary material to assess the significance of each component in their architecture. Overall, the findings underscore the effectiveness of Scattering GCN in addressing oversmoothing while maintaining or improving classification accuracy across various graph structures.
