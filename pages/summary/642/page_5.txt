This paper proposes a hybrid network architecture that integrates Graph Convolutional Network (GCN) models with geometric scattering techniques. The aim is to leverage the strengths of both approaches for improved performance in processing graph data. The architecture is illustrated in Figure 2, which shows the combination of low-pass and band-pass operations.

The proposed model consists of two main components: the GCN layer and the scattering layer. The GCN layer is defined by a propagation rule that incorporates biases and powers of the adjacency matrix, enabling the model to aggregate information from k-step neighborhoods. This is expressed mathematically as H_gcn, where each filter uses a different propagation matrix.

In parallel, the scattering layer operates on the graph structure to capture frequency components through band-pass channels, allowing for the extraction of information at various scales. This is represented as H_sct, which gathers scattering information from different orders.

The final output of the hybrid model is obtained by concatenating the outputs from both the GCN and scattering components, yielding a comprehensive representation of the graph data. This architecture aims to enhance the model's ability to discern complex patterns in graph-structured data by effectively combining local and global features through the integration of GCN and geometric scattering techniques.
