This paper references a variety of works focused on graph data analysis, particularly through the lens of geometric scattering and convolutional neural networks. Key contributions include the introduction of geometric scattering methods for graph data by Gao, Wolf, and Hirn, which enhance the understanding of graph neural networks through asymmetric transforms. Zou and Lerman extend these ideas with graph convolutional neural networks, while Mallat discusses group invariant scattering, providing foundational theory for these techniques.

Hammond et al. explore wavelets on graphs via spectral graph theory, which is further developed by Coifman and Maggioni through diffusion wavelets. The concept of vertex-frequency analysis on graphs is presented by Shuman et al., which underscores the importance of localized spectral filtering in graph convolutional networks as shown by Defferrard, Bresson, and Vandergheynst.

The emerging field of signal processing on graphs is highlighted by Shuman et al., emphasizing its relevance for high-dimensional data analysis. Various approaches to deep learning on graphs, including Lanczos methods for accelerated filtering and multi-scale deep graph convolutional networks, are also discussed.

Overall, the references collectively illustrate a robust interplay between graph theory, signal processing, and machine learning, paving the way for advanced techniques in analyzing complex data structures.
