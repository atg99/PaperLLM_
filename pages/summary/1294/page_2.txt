The paper introduces Audio-MAE, a self-supervised learning framework tailored for audio representation, inspired by the Masked Autoencoder (MAE) approach used in visual tasks. The process begins with transforming audio recordings into spectrograms, which are then divided into patches. Out of these patches, 80% are masked, and only the remaining 20% are input into a Transformer encoder. The encoder processes these visible embeddings, while a decoder reconstructs the original audio spectrogram by utilizing the order-restored embeddings and masked tokens.

Audio-MAE addresses the computational challenges associated with self-attention by leveraging localized attention and a hybrid architecture in the decoder, which is particularly effective given the local correlations present in spectrogram patches. This design choice enhances the reconstruction performance. During fine-tuning, the decoder is removed, and the encoder is further refined through patch-masking.

Empirical results indicate that Audio-MAE achieves state-of-the-art performance across six audio and speech classification tasks, notably obtaining the best mean Average Precision (mAP) on the AudioSet-2M dataset without relying on external supervision. The paper also highlights visual and audible examples to illustrate the effectiveness of the Audio-MAE framework.

In the related work section, the authors discuss advancements in visual masked pre-training and how their approach extends the MAE methodology to audio spectrograms, emphasizing the connection between masked autoencoders and representation learning in both visual and audio domains.
