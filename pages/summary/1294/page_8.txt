The paper presents a comparison of various models for audio and speech classification tasks, focusing on their performance metrics such as mean Average Precision (mAP) for AudioSet and accuracy for various datasets like ESC and SPC. The results are categorized into three groups: models without pre-training, models with in-domain self-supervised pre-training, and those with out-of-domain supervised pre-training, specifically using ImageNet.

Key findings include that models trained from scratch using audio-only pre-training (AS-SSL) yield the best performance. In contrast, using ImageNet pre-training (IN-SSL) does not provide sufficient benefits and can even degrade performance when applied to self-supervised pre-training on AudioSet. The results indicate that out-of-domain pre-training is detrimental due to domain shifts, particularly when downstream datasets are smaller.

The Audio-MAE model, which relies solely on AudioSet for pre-training, outperforms other in-domain models across all tasks, achieving the highest scores on AS-20K, AS-2M, and ESC-50 datasets. It also surpasses state-of-the-art models that utilize out-of-domain data or knowledge distillation techniques. Notably, Audio-MAE is trained using audio data at a 16K sampling rate, suggesting potential for improved performance with higher sampling rates.

Overall, the findings advocate for the effectiveness of in-domain self-supervised pre-training for audio classification tasks and highlight the limitations of incorporating out-of-domain pre-training methods.
