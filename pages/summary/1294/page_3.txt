Audio-MAE employs a simple yet effective approach to learn self-supervised representations from audio spectrograms. It transforms audio recordings into Mel-spectrograms and segments them into non-overlapping patches, which are then embedded with positional information. The model uses innovative masking strategies to selectively hide portions of the spectrogram, exploring both unstructured random masking and structured masking that focuses on time and frequency components. This masking mechanism is crucial for enabling the model to learn contextualized representations by forcing it to infer information from the visible patches.

In contrast to existing methods that often leverage out-of-domain pre-training from datasets like ImageNet, Audio-MAE is designed for audio-only self-supervised pre-training. It stands apart from other in-domain methods, such as wav2vec 2.0 and SS-AST, which utilize different input types and objectives. Audio-MAE focuses solely on the non-masked spectrogram patches, enhancing its ability to generate robust audio representations.

The authors conducted experiments comparing Audio-MAE with concurrent methods, demonstrating its superior performance. The study highlights the potential of high masking rates in self-supervised audio representation learning, suggesting that significant portions of the input can be masked without hindering the learning process, thereby contributing to the advancement of audio model training techniques.
