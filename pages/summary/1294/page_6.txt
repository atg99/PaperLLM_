The paper discusses the masking strategies employed in the pre-training and fine-tuning phases of the Audio-MAE model, which is designed for audio representation learning. It presents findings that highlight the effectiveness of different masking ratios and types. In pre-training, a high masking ratio of 80% with unstructured random masking is optimal due to the continuous nature of audio spectrograms, which exhibit redundancy similar to images. This approach allows the model to extrapolate context effectively.

In contrast, during fine-tuning, structured masking (specifically time + frequency) yields better results than unstructured masking, with a recommended masking ratio of 0.3. This indicates a shift in strategy where task-specific fine-tuning benefits from more structured approaches.

The paper also examines the impact of patch size and stride on model performance, concluding that non-overlapping patches provide a better balance between computation and accuracy compared to overlapping patches, which can lead to information leakage. Additionally, the trade-off between encoder size and performance is explored, revealing that larger models generally improve accuracy, particularly on smaller datasets.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of selecting appropriate masking strategies and model configurations to enhance self-supervised learning in audio tasks.
