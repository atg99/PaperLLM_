The paper "Masked Autoencoders that Listen" introduces an extension of image-based Masked Autoencoders (MAE) to the domain of audio spectrograms, termed Audio-MAE. The proposed model utilizes a Transformer encoder-decoder structure, where it first encodes audio spectrogram patches with a significant masking ratio, processing only the non-masked tokens through the encoder. The decoder is designed to reconstruct the input spectrogram by reordering and decoding the encoded context, augmented with mask tokens. A notable enhancement in the decoder is the incorporation of local window attention, which leverages the high correlation of audio spectrograms in local time and frequency bands.

The authors fine-tune the encoder with a reduced masking ratio on specific target datasets and demonstrate that Audio-MAE achieves state-of-the-art performance across six audio and speech classification tasks. This advancement surpasses other recent models that rely on external supervised pre-training. The paper emphasizes the limitations of existing audio models that utilize ImageNet-pretrained weights, arguing that the differences between audio spectrograms and natural images may hinder performance due to label biases and the distinct nature of audio data.

By employing self-supervised learning on a large-scale dataset, AudioSet, the authors highlight the importance of extensive pre-training data for effective representation learning. The challenges associated with large-scale training, particularly the quadratic complexity of self-attention in Transformers, are also acknowledged. The paper concludes by affirming the potential of self-supervised techniques in audio representation learning, further contributing to the growing body of research in this area. The code and models are made publicly available for further exploration.
