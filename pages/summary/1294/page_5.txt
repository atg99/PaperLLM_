The paper presents an extensive evaluation of audio classification tasks using various datasets. The experiments cover audio classification on AudioSet (both AS-2M and AS-20K), Environmental Sound Classification (ESC-50), and speech classification on Speech Commands (SPC-1 and SPC-2) and VoxCeleb (SID). 

In the datasets section, AudioSet is highlighted for its extensive collection of approximately 2 million 10-second YouTube clips that feature 527 audio event types. The dataset is divided into a balanced set of 22,176 clips and an unbalanced set of over 2 million clips, with a 19K evaluation set. The ESC-50 dataset consists of 2,000 recordings across 50 sound classes, evaluated using 5-fold cross-validation. The Speech Commands datasets (SPC-1 and SPC-2) focus on keyword spotting tasks, with SPC-2 containing 35 commands. Lastly, VoxCeleb includes 150K utterances for speaker identification tasks.

The implementation details describe the use of a 12-layer ViT-B as the Transformer encoder and a 16-layer Transformer decoder with shifted local attention. The audio data undergoes preprocessing into Mel-frequency bands, resulting in a spectrogram of dimensions 1x1024x128 for a 10-second recording. The paper discusses patch embedding strategies, pre-training, and fine-tuning processes, with specific configurations for masking ratios and dataset handling to address class imbalances.

The pre-training phase utilizes AudioSet-2M for 32 epochs, employing a large batch size and distributed training across 64 GPUs. The fine-tuning phase focuses on the encoder only, implementing weighted sampling to balance class representation during training. Overall, the experiments aim to evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed methods in audio classification tasks.
