The paper presents the performance evaluation of the Audio-MAE model on audio classification tasks, utilizing spectrogram reconstruction visualizations from the AudioSet evaluation set. The study compares two masking strategies: unstructured (random) and structured (time + frequency), both with a masking ratio of 70%. The results demonstrate that Audio-MAE outperforms various models, including ERANN, PANN, AST, SS-AST, and MAE-AST, particularly in speech tasks such as SPC-1, SPC-2, and SID, without requiring pre-training. 

The authors emphasize that the model's effectiveness is attributed to audio-only from-scratch pre-training on AudioSet. Visualizations in Figure 6 illustrate the original spectrogram, masked input, and MAE output across different sound types. The reconstruction results show that Audio-MAE can effectively generate audible outputs for various sounds, including speech and music, while unstructured masking yields better alignment with ground truth compared to structured masking. The paper highlights the significance of absolute positions in spectrograms for sound understanding, noting that unstructured masking allows for more accurate predictions based on nearby patches. However, structured masking can lead to inaccuracies, such as missing words in reconstructed speech. Overall, the findings suggest that Audio-MAE is a robust model for audio classification tasks.
