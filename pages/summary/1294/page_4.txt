The paper discusses the Audio-MAE model, which applies masking techniques to improve the efficiency and accuracy of audio spectrogram processing using Transformers. It highlights that both spectrograms and images exhibit high redundancy, allowing for the effective discarding of tokens or patches. The authors found that unstructured random masking during the pre-training phase and structured time-frequency masking during fine-tuning yield the best accuracy.

The model employs a 12-layer ViT-Base Transformer as its encoder, processing only 20% of non-masked patches to minimize computational overhead. The decoder, also composed of standard Transformer blocks, incorporates local attention mechanisms to better capture the unique characteristics of audio spectrograms, which differ from images due to their reliance on the position and order of features. This includes techniques like shifted window attention and hybrid attention, facilitating better cross-window connections.

The objective of the Audio-MAE decoder is to reconstruct the input spectrogram by predicting values for masked patches, using mean squared error as the loss function. The study found that reconstruction loss alone suffices for effective learning, without needing additional contrastive objectives. During fine-tuning, only the encoder is retained, and masking is employed again to enhance learning from a limited set of spectrogram inputs, improving efficiency and computational resource usage. Overall, the paper presents a novel approach to audio processing using masking and local attention mechanisms, enhancing the performance of audio models in various tasks.
