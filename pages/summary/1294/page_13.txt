The referenced papers cover a range of topics in automatic speech recognition and audio classification, highlighting advancements in deep learning methodologies and data augmentation techniques. 

Park et al. introduce SpecAugment, a straightforward data augmentation method that enhances automatic speech recognition performance. Li et al. compare various deep learning approaches for environmental sound classification, emphasizing the importance of effective audio tagging. Hershey et al. discuss CNN architectures for large-scale audio classification and the advantages of strong temporal labels in audio event classification.

Warden presents the Speech Commands dataset, aimed at limited-vocabulary speech recognition, while Yang et al. introduce the SUPERB benchmark for evaluating speech processing models. Nagrani et al. provide insights into large-scale speaker verification with VoxCeleb, and Povey et al. detail the Kaldi toolkit for speech recognition.

Additionally, Yun et al. propose CutMix, a regularization strategy for classifiers, and Zhang et al. discuss Mixup, a technique for improving model robustness. Verbitskiy et al. and Kong et al. present efficient neural networks for audio recognition, emphasizing the performance of pre-trained models. Tan and Le focus on EfficientNet, a model scaling approach for convolutional neural networks, while Griffin and Lim contribute to signal estimation techniques, and Suzuki and Takeshima investigate equal-loudness-level contours for pure tones.

Overall, these contributions reflect ongoing efforts to enhance audio processing technologies through innovative methodologies and extensive datasets.
