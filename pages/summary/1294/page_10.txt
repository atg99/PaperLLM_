In this paper, we presented an extension of the Masked Autoencoder (MAE) framework specifically for audio data, termed Audio-MAE. Our approach focuses on learning to reconstruct masked spectrogram patches from audio recordings and has demonstrated superior performance across six audio and speech classification tasks. 

Key findings from our study include: 
1. The MAE approach effectively applies to audio spectrograms, yielding surprisingly strong results.
2. Incorporating local self-attention in the decoder enhances representation learning.
3. Masking strategies can be utilized during both pre-training and fine-tuning phases, leading to improved accuracy and reduced training costs. The effectiveness of these strategies is contingent on the data type and learning approach (self-supervised versus supervised).
4. Optimal performance is achieved when pre-training and fine-tuning occur within the same modality, avoiding the need for cross-modality transfer learning.

Future research will explore multimodal self-supervised learning by integrating audio and visual data through a joint MAE approach, leveraging the inherent relationships present in video data.
