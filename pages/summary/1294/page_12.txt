The referenced works focus on advancements in audio classification, representation learning, and self-supervised learning techniques. Key contributions include:

1. **Hierarchical Token-Semantic Audio Transformer (HTS-AT)** by Chen et al. explores a novel architecture for sound classification and detection, emphasizing the hierarchical processing of audio data.

2. **PSLA** by Gong et al. improves audio tagging through a combination of pretraining, sampling, labeling, and aggregation, showcasing the effectiveness of these strategies in enhancing tagging performance.

3. **CMKD** presents a cross-model knowledge distillation approach using CNN and transformer architectures for improved audio classification accuracy.

4. **Wav2Vec and Wav2Vec 2.0** by Baevski et al. introduce frameworks for unsupervised and self-supervised learning of speech representations, highlighting their effectiveness in speech recognition tasks.

5. **Data2Vec** offers a general framework for self-supervised learning applicable across speech, vision, and language domains.

6. **HuBERT** focuses on self-supervised learning through masked prediction of hidden units, contributing to advancements in speech representation learning.

7. **Conformer-based Self-Supervised Learning** addresses non-speech audio tasks, demonstrating the versatility of the Conformer architecture in audio processing.

8. **Masked Autoencoding Audio Spectrogram Transformer (MAE-AST)** explores the use of masked autoencoding techniques for audio representation learning.

9. **Contrastive Predictive Coding** by van den Oord et al. introduces a method for representation learning that has implications across various modalities, including audio and vision.

These studies collectively contribute to the understanding of self-supervised and unsupervised learning in audio and speech processing, providing novel architectures and methodologies that enhance performance in classification and representation tasks.
