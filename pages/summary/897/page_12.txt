The paper examines the input formulations used in EEG studies, categorizing them into two main types: calculated features and signal values. Emotion recognition, mental workload, motor imagery, and seizure tasks predominantly utilized calculated features, while motor tasks, sleep stages, and visual tasks primarily relied on signal values. The analysis is visually represented in Figure 4, illustrating the distribution of input formulations across various tasks.

Additionally, the paper discusses the role of deep learning in processing EEG data, highlighting its emergence as a transformative approach since the late 2000s. Deep learning, which leverages artificial neural networks for hierarchical data representation, has significantly improved accuracy in complex data analysis. The authors identify six categories of deep learning architectures: Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN), Recurrent Neural Networks (RNN), Multi-layer Perceptrons (MLP), Stacked Auto Encoders (SAE), Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM), and hybrid models. CNNs are the most prevalent, used in 62% of the studies, followed by hybrid models (16%), MLP (8%), SAE (6%), LSTM (6%), and RNN (2%), as depicted in Figure 5.
