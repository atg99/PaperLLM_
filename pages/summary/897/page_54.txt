The references cited discuss various advancements in utilizing deep learning techniques, particularly convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and generative adversarial networks (GANs), for tasks related to emotion recognition and EEG signal processing. Luo et al. propose a GAN-based method for augmenting data in multimodal emotion recognition, while Wei et al. focus on automatic detection of epileptic EEG signals using CNNs with enhancements in time-domain analysis. Chang and Jun present a hybrid deep-learning model to assess emotional responses to architectural designs, indicating the intersection of technology and user experience.

Yang et al. outline an optimization framework for EEG motor imagery recognition, emphasizing the importance of effective algorithmic strategies. Panwar et al. explore the generation of EEG signals through a class-conditioned Wasserstein GAN, contributing to the field of signal simulation. Gulrajani et al. improve the training processes of Wasserstein GANs, which is critical for enhancing generative models. 

Additional studies, such as those by Touryan et al. and Schirrmeister et al., delve into the capacity of deep learning for task performance estimation and EEG decoding, highlighting the growing integration of these technologies in neuroscience. Other works, like those by Ullah et al. and O'Shea et al., demonstrate the practical applications of deep learning for automated epilepsy detection and neonatal seizure identification, showcasing the potential for real-time medical diagnostics. 

Overall, these contributions signify a robust trend towards leveraging deep learning methodologies to advance both emotional and neurological signal processing, with significant implications for healthcare and user interaction design.
