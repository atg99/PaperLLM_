The paper discusses various studies focused on improving the accuracy of EEG signal classification through different data augmentation (DA) techniques and convolutional neural networks (CNN). 

N. Kwak et al. (2017) achieved a 99.28% accuracy in classifying visual evoked potentials by employing overlapping windows with varying shift lengths. They found that smaller shifts led to better performance. Schirrmeister et al. (2017) explored the effects of CNN training methods, enhancing their model through a cost function that regularized decision-making based on temporal proximity, resulting in a 95% accuracy with cropped training segments.

Ullah et al. faced challenges due to limited seizure data, leading to overfitting. They implemented two DA methods using sliding windows, achieving accuracies of 96.45% and 95.40% with different overlap percentages. N. Truong et al. (2018) utilized GANs for semi-supervised seizure prediction, generating a tenfold increase in training samples but did not specify accuracy based on shift lengths. They reported accuracies of 60.91% and 72.63% without DA, improving to 74.33% and 75.33% with DA.

Majidov et al. (2019) and Z. Mousavi et al. (2019) also explored DA methods in their work on EEG classification tasks, although details on their specific approaches were less comprehensive. Overall, the studies highlight the importance of data augmentation in enhancing the performance of CNNs for EEG signal classification.
