The table presents a comparison of various studies focused on mental workload and emotional recognition using different deep learning strategies and datasets. Each study varies in terms of the number of subjects, trials, channels, and specific tasks.

1. **University of Memphis**: Involves 13 subjects with 2670 trials using a CNN and LSTM architecture. The accuracy without data augmentation (DA) is not applicable, and no improvement was noted with DA.

2. **SDAE**: Features 8 subjects with 180 minutes of data per subject. It employs a stacked denoising autoencoder (SAE) with a reported accuracy of 93%, though accuracy with DA is not provided.

3. **AutoCAM**: This study includes 7 subjects and uses 11 channels. The SAE model achieved 76.5% accuracy without DA and improved to 85.5% with DA.

4. **SEED**: Two entries report on 14 subjects with 1890 trials each, focusing on three classes of emotional recognition. The first configuration with 62 channels and a CNN architecture resulted in 49.6% accuracy without DA and 74.3% with DA. The second configuration had a more complex CNN structure but lower accuracy of 34.2% without DA and improved to 75% with DA.

5. **MAHNOB-HCI**: Involving 30 subjects and 527 trials, this study used a CNN and achieved 40.8% accuracy without DA and 45.4% with DA.

Overall, the studies illustrate varying degrees of success in applying deep learning techniques to mental workload and emotional recognition tasks, highlighting the potential benefits of data augmentation in improving model accuracy.
