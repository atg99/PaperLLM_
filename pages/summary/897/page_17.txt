The study analyzes the impact of data augmentation (DA) techniques, particularly the addition of Gaussian noise, on the performance of various deep learning models applied to EEG signal analysis. Results from the SEED dataset demonstrated that augmenting the training dataset 30 times improved ResNet accuracy from 34.2% to 75%, outperforming LeNet, which increased from 49.6% to 74.3%. R. Hussein et al. (2018) employed RNN and LSTM networks for automatic detection of epileptic seizures, enhancing model robustness through noise addition, though they lacked specific details on the noise used and did not compare performance with and without DA. Similarly, S. Kuanar et al. (2018) used LSTM and CNN to predict cognitive load from EEG data, transforming time-series into multispectral images augmented with Gaussian noise, but also did not provide detailed comparisons. E. Salama et al. (2018) reported improved performance in a 3D-CNN for emotion recognition, achieving higher classification rates when adding Gaussian noise with a signal-to-noise ratio of 5. Parvan et al. (2019) doubled trials in the BCI competition dataset by adding Gaussian noise, resulting in a slight improvement in the kappa coefficient. Y. Li et al. (2019) highlighted the balance between increasing CNN depth for accuracy and the risk of overfitting, noting that optimal Gaussian noise parameters significantly influenced classification results. The analysis reveals a common lack of detailed reporting on noise parameters and their effects on model performance across the reviewed studies, indicating a potential gap in the literature focused on DA techniques.
