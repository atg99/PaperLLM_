The paper discusses the pre-processing and feature extraction methods used in the analysis of EEG signals, highlighting two primary approaches: event-related potentials and spectral analysis of rhythmic oscillations. It notes that EEG signals are inherently noisy due to interference from various physiological signals, necessitating filtering and de-noising techniques to enhance the clarity of neural activity. Among the 53 studies reviewed, 85% employed manual artifact removal methods, while 15% did not adequately address artifact removal. Most studies utilized frequency domain filters to concentrate on specific frequency ranges, with approximately half of the papers implementing low pass filters below 40 Hz.

The input formulations for neural networks (NNs) in these studies fell into three categories: raw EEG signals (36%), calculated features from raw signals (49%), and spectrograms processed as images (15%). The choice of input depended largely on the task type and the architecture of the deep learning model. Various techniques such as wavelet transforms, entropy calculation, and short-time Fourier transforms were used to derive features from the EEG signals. The findings indicate that most studies favored calculated features for training NNs, and many utilized spectrograms and fast Fourier transforms to convert EEG data into image formats for analysis.
