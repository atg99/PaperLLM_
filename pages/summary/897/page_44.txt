This paper presents various studies utilizing Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) for different applications in brain-computer interface (BCI) research. The studies are categorized based on specific tasks such as motor imagery (MI), emotion recognition (ER), and visual task (VT) classification, along with corresponding datasets and methodologies.

Key findings include:

1. **Motor Imagery (MI)**: The GAN models achieved classification accuracies ranging from 0.77 to 0.86, utilizing spectrogram representations and CNN architectures. The BCI Competition II and IV datasets were commonly employed.

2. **Emotion Recognition (ER)**: Different variants of GANs, including CWGAN and cBEGAN, were used. Results indicated varying performance metrics with accuracies reported as high as 0.869 for arousal and 0.73 for valence, showing the potential effectiveness of GANs in emotion classification tasks using datasets like SEED and DEAP.

3. **Visual Task (VT)**: GANs demonstrated significant potential in visual task classification, with accuracies varying based on the dataset and model configuration. Notably, models reported accuracies of up to 0.91 for specific trials and comparisons against DCGAN and VAE models highlighted GANs' superior performance.

4. **Methodologies**: Across the studies, a variety of architectures were implemented, including CNNs with different configurations (e.g., convolutional layers, fully connected layers), and activation functions like ReLU and softmax were commonly used.

5. **Performance Metrics**: The performance metrics varied significantly across studies, with some models not improving upon existing results, indicating a need for further exploration in optimizing GAN architectures for specific BCI applications.

Overall, the findings suggest that GANs hold promise for enhancing the performance of BCI systems, though challenges remain in achieving consistent improvements across different tasks and datasets.
