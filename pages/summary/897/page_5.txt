This approach is particularly beneficial in scenarios with limited data availability, such as EEG studies involving rare events.

The paper highlights the challenges of working with EEG data, particularly the issues of imbalanced datasets, subject variability, and the time-consuming nature of data collection for training machine learning models. It emphasizes the limitations of traditional EEG classifiers, which are often tailored to individual subjects and require extensive training sessions.

Deep Learning (DL) is presented as a promising avenue for overcoming some of these challenges. It utilizes hierarchical representations through neural networks to analyze complex data efficiently. Although DL has shown significant success in various fields, its effectiveness in EEG decoding is contingent on having sufficient data for training to avoid overfitting.

The paper discusses strategies to mitigate overfitting, including model regularization and data augmentation. It underscores the potential of DL for inter-subject generalization, which is crucial given the variability in EEG data across individuals. Additionally, the use of transfer learning is suggested as a method to expedite the model training process for new subjects, ultimately aiming to enhance the efficiency of EEG analysis and classification.
