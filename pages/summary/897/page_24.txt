The paper discusses the architecture and performance evaluation of a conditional Wasserstein Generative Adversarial Network with Gradient Penalty (cWGAN-GP) for generating synthetic EEG data. The generator includes two fully connected layers and two convolutional layers, while the discriminator comprises three convolutional layers and two fully connected layers. The dataset utilized for training was collected during the BCIT X2 Rapid Series Visual Presentation experiment, involving EEG signals from 10 subjects across 5 sessions, totaling 1 hour of recording per session with a 256-channel BioSemi system. The dataset consisted of two classes, target and non-target, each containing 967 samples, which were pre-processed through a series of steps including band-pass filtering and normalization.

To evaluate the generated data, the authors employed three methods: visual inspection, log-likelihood distance from Gaussian mixture models (GMMs), and classifier performance. Both visual inspection and GMM evaluations indicated high-quality synthetic data. In terms of classifier performance, synthetic data augmented the training dataset, resulting in a 5.18% improvement in cross-subject evaluation and a 3.12% improvement in same-subject evaluation when using a CNN.

Additionally, the paper references Aznan et al. (2019) who investigated EEG data generation using three methods: Deep Convolutional Generative Adversarial Network (DCGAN), gradient penalized Wasserstein GAN (WGAN-GP), and Variational Auto-encoder (VAE) for a Steady State Visual Evoked Potential (SSVEP) based BCI system. The generative models were trained on a video-stimuli dataset, while the SSVEP classifier was tested on the NAO dataset, with generated samples used for pre-training. The study found that a sample size of 500 yielded the best classification accuracy across all methods tested. The results suggest a growing trend in utilizing GANs as a data augmentation technique for EEG signals, as shown in the reported magnification factors and accuracy improvements.
