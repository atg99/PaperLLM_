The data presented summarizes various studies that utilize different convolutional neural network (CNN) architectures and techniques for analyzing electroencephalogram (EEG) data across multiple datasets. Key findings include:

1. The Freiburg Hospital intracranial EEG dataset shows varying performance metrics with models using Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT) combined with Generative Adversarial Networks (GAN) and CNN architectures, achieving accuracy rates between 0.60 and 0.72.

2. The CHB-MIT database highlights a strong performance with an accuracy of 0.97 using a spatio-temporal approach integrated with information theory in a CNN model.

3. The neonatal intensive care unit dataset achieved an impressive accuracy of 0.99 using a raw CNN approach, tailored subject by subject.

4. For BCI competition datasets, models employing spatial filters and CNN architectures yielded accuracies ranging from 0.75 to 0.84, indicating the effectiveness of these techniques in EEG signal classification.

5. The Bonn University dataset consistently demonstrated high performance with accuracy rates around 0.95 to 0.96 using CNN architectures.

6. The American Epilepsy Society Seizure Prediction Challenge dataset recorded an accuracy of approximately 0.89, showing the potential of CNNs in seizure prediction tasks.

The results underscore the importance of model architecture, preprocessing techniques, and dataset characteristics in the performance of EEG analysis, with CNNs proving to be a robust tool for this purpose.
