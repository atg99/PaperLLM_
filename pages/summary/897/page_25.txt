The table presents a summary of various studies that utilized Generative Adversarial Networks (GAN) as a data augmentation (DA) technique in different contexts, primarily focusing on EEG-based tasks. Each study is characterized by its dataset, task information, input formulation, deep learning strategy, and performance metrics.

1. **Study [61]** employed the BCI competition II dataset, focusing on motor imagery EEG from one subject with a CNN and cDCGAN strategy. The augmented dataset size doubled, resulting in an accuracy increase from 83% to 86%.

2. **Study [62]** analyzed a larger dataset with 30 subjects for EEG-based login authentication. The combination of GAN and XGBoost added 10,000 samples, enhancing accuracy from 90.8% to 95%.

3. **Study [63]** used the BCI competition IV dataset for motor imagery with GAN and CNN. The data was augmented tenfold, improving accuracy slightly from 77.6% to 79.3%.

4. **Study [65]** focused on a motor task with one subject using GAN-SWD, but did not report performance metrics.

5. **Study [69]** investigated emotion recognition with the SEED dataset, involving 15 subjects. Using CWGAN, the augmented data size doubled, leading to improved accuracies for arousal and valence from 69.02% and 53.76% to 78.17% and 73.89%, respectively.

6. Another aspect of **Study [69]** included 32 subjects, but performance metrics were not specified.

7. **Study [72]** also examined the SEED dataset with nine subjects for emotion recognition using cBEGAN. The augmentation produced an accuracy increase from 81.9% to 87.56%.

The findings illustrate the effectiveness of GAN in enhancing performance across various EEG-related tasks through data augmentation.
