The paper discusses the impact of publication bias in preclinical research, particularly regarding the predominance of positive results submitted for publication, which can skew analyses. The authors focused on key aspects relevant to data augmentation (DA) in deep learning (DL) applications on electroencephalography (EEG) data, intentionally omitting details such as normalization procedures and software tools to concentrate on more significant findings.

The conclusions highlight the successful application of DL in various EEG tasks, including sleep stages, motor imagery, mental workload, and emotion recognition. DL's ability to learn feature representations from raw data is emphasized, although its effectiveness is limited by the smaller size of EEG datasets. DA is presented as a solution to enhance training data availability, mitigate overfitting, and improve classifier accuracy and stability.

The analysis reveals that feature extraction is commonly performed outside the neural network (49%), while a notable proportion of studies (36%) utilize raw signals. Convolutional neural networks (CNN) are the predominant architecture used (62%). The authors found that DA is primarily applied in seizure detection (24%) and motor imagery (21%), with specific techniques favored for different tasks. They noted variability in result reporting across studies and provided their analysis of an open dataset, demonstrating that DA can improve accuracy in DL-based EEG analysis.

Recommendations for enhancing reproducibility and comparability in studies involving DA and DL are made, stressing the importance of clearly describing DA methods, parameters, and magnification factors, as well as reporting accuracy metrics before and after DA. The authors aim for this review to serve as a valuable resource for EEG researchers interested in applying DA to train DL algorithms, ultimately contributing to high-quality and reproducible research outcomes.
