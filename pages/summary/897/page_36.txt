The paper investigates the impact of data augmentation (DA) techniques on classification accuracy in electroencephalography (EEG) tasks, specifically focusing on motor imagery. The authors conducted an analysis using the BCI competition IV dataset 2a, which is a widely recognized dataset for motor imagery tasks. They noted that motor imagery was the predominant EEG task for DA in 2019 and selected raw signals as inputs for their neural network (NN) due to their common usage and less processing.

The study employed a convolutional neural network (CNN) architecture, specifically using a configuration of conv(2) + FC(2) with a leakyReLU activation function. Various DA techniques were tested with different magnification factors, revealing that Generative Adversarial Networks (GAN) with a magnification factor of 15 yielded the best overall accuracy. The authors compared their results with existing literature, noting that their method surpassed the accuracy reported by Dai and colleagues by over 2%, achieving an improvement score of 0.24. Additionally, the use of DA led to a more than 4% increase in accuracy compared to results obtained without DA, resulting in an improvement score of 0.41.

In conclusion, the findings indicate that DA techniques, particularly GAN, significantly enhance classification accuracy in EEG tasks, demonstrating the effectiveness of these methods in improving neural network performance for motor imagery classification.
