Schwabedal et al. emphasized that traditional methods like cost-sensitive learning and resampling techniques, while common, did not significantly enhance predictive accuracy and could introduce biases. Instead, they proposed using Fourier Transform Surrogates to create new signals that maintained statistical independence from original data. By randomizing the phases of the Fourier-transformed signals and applying the inverse transform, they generated augmented data aimed at improving model performance. The study utilized a convolutional neural network (CNN) on the CAPSLPDB sleep database, which comprises 101 overnight polysomnographies, to classify six sleep stages. 

Overall, these studies highlight the challenges associated with imbalanced data in EEG analysis, particularly in seizure detection and sleep-stage classification. Various strategies, including subsampling, feature extraction, and novel augmentation techniques like Fourier Transform Surrogates, were explored to enhance model training and improve classification accuracy.
