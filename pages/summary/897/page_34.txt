The paper investigates the effects of various data augmentation (DA) methods on classifier performance, specifically focusing on noise addition, multiplication, frequency shift, and phase shift. These methods collectively enhanced the average accuracy of classifiers by 5.3%. Mokatren et al. (2019) utilized discrete-wavelet transform to extract energy and entropy from four frequency bands—theta, alpha, beta, and gamma—in an emotion recognition task. They constructed a 3-D array representing scalp channel positioning and features, employing image augmentation techniques like horizontal and vertical shifting to boost their convolutional neural network (CNN) accuracy. Their results showed an increase in classification accuracy on the DEAP dataset from 86.47% to 90.87% for Arousal and from 88.34% to 91.33% for valence with DA applied.

The authors reviewed 53 studies, revealing no clear consensus on DA techniques but noted that sliding window, sampling, and noise addition were commonly paired with CNNs. The review concluded specific recommendations for EEG tasks: sliding windows for seizure detection, noise addition for mental workload assessment, and sampling for sleep stage classification. Figures in the paper illustrate the aggregated findings and the distribution of studies across different DA techniques.
