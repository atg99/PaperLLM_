This paper discusses the application of data augmentation (DA) techniques in enhancing the performance of EEG signal analysis, particularly in various classification tasks. It outlines key parameters for DA, such as the magnification factor, dataset characteristics, task information, preprocessing techniques, and deep learning strategies including the architecture of neural networks employed.

The research methodology identified a total of 53 relevant studies, comprising 26 journal articles, 16 conference papers, and 11 preprints, with a notable increase in publications from 2015 to 2019. The EEG classification tasks identified in these studies are categorized into seven groups: seizure detection, motor imagery, sleep stages, emotion recognition, mental workload, motor tasks, and visual tasks. 

Seizure detection studies focus on identifying upcoming seizures through EEG recordings, where DA has proven effective in amplifying rare seizure events, thereby improving algorithm accuracy. Motor imagery tasks involve subjects imagining movements, which is significant for brain-computer interfaces aimed at assisting individuals with disabilities. Sleep stage classification involves scoring EEG signals recorded overnight to determine various states of wakefulness and sleep stages, as per established guidelines.

Overall, the paper highlights the growing significance of DA in EEG research and its potential to enhance the classification accuracy across multiple neurological tasks.
