(2018) proposed a novel approach of swapping left and right electrodes to effectively double their dataset size, which consisted of data from 155 ICU patients for a 5-way classification task. Their technique aimed to help the model generalize better to new patients by maintaining three central electrodes while flipping the others, which they found did not impact classification accuracy for symmetrically patterned signals, though pre-augmentation accuracies were not provided.

Shovon et al. (2019) utilized Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT) to convert EEG signals into images for binary classification of motor-imagery signals. They applied various augmentation techniques such as rotation and flipping, which resulted in the creation of an additional 1000 images, raising the average accuracy of their CNN model to 89.19% without reporting baseline accuracy.

Freer et al. (2019) developed a convolutional LSTM network utilizing filter bank common spatial patterns for a 4-way classification task in motor-imagery scenarios. This research emphasizes the importance of data augmentation in enhancing model performance and generalization across diverse patient datasets and signal variations.
