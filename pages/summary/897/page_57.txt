The referenced works focus on advancements in motor imagery EEG signal classification and brain-computer interfaces (BCIs). Deiss et al. introduce the HAMLET technique, emphasizing interpretability in human and machine learning collaboration. Shovon et al. present a multi-input convolutional neural network for classifying motor imagery EEG signals, enhancing performance through short-time Fourier transform augmentation. Freer and Yang explore data augmentation strategies for self-paced motor imagery classification using convolutional long short-term memory networks (C-LSTM), highlighting improvements in accuracy. Mokatren et al. improve EEG classification by incorporating sensor topography, while Raza et al. discuss adaptive learning techniques that detect covariate shifts in motor imagery data. Gaur et al. propose an empirical mode decomposition filtering method to enhance classification effectiveness in BCIs. The collective studies contribute to refining methodologies for processing and interpreting EEG signals in the context of BCIs.
