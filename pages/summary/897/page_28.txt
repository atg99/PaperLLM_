The paper discusses various approaches to enhance the accuracy of classification results in different medical applications, particularly focusing on sleep stage classification and seizure detection. One study employed an overlapping technique to augment their dataset, leading to a significant increase in epochs from 7592 to 24162, resulting in a classification accuracy of 93.55% for six sleep stages. In contrast, a Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) achieved lower accuracy at 72.33% for the same classification task.

Another study by Avcu et al. developed a CNN for seizure detection using only two EEG channels. They balanced the dataset by applying different overlapping proportions, achieving a sensitivity of 93.3% for the two-channel setup compared to 95.8% for an eighteen-channel setup. However, this study did not report on data augmentation results.

Tayeb et al. implemented three deep learning models (LSTM, CNN, and RNN) for motor imagery decoding, utilizing a shifting window technique to increase the training dataset size by a factor of 25. Their CNN model achieved a mean accuracy exceeding 84% across 20 participants, yet details on data augmentation were sparse.

Other studies, such as those by Chambon et al. and Tsiouris et al., also segmented datasets to create balanced training data. Tang et al. improved their CNN's performance from 70.9% to 77.9% on a seizure dataset through segmentation. Despite the widespread use of these methods, there remains no consensus on the optimal overlapping percentage or shifting length for effective data augmentation, highlighting an area for further investigation.
