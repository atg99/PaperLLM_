The paper discusses the application of data augmentation (DA) techniques in deep learning (DL) for analyzing electroencephalography (EEG) data. It highlights that DA can enhance decoding accuracy by an average of 29%, addressing the challenges posed by low samples-to-features ratios in EEG tasks. The authors analyze various DA methods, including noise addition, generative adversarial networks, sliding windows, and others, categorizing them by EEG tasks such as seizure detection, sleep stages, motor imagery, and emotion recognition.

The study reveals that DA in DL for EEG has gained traction over the past five years, with certain techniques proving more effective for specific tasks. Notably, noise addition and sliding windows yielded the highest accuracy improvements, particularly benefiting mental workload tasks. The paper emphasizes the need for standardized reporting of DA parameters to facilitate better understanding and comparison of results across studies. Overall, the findings suggest that DA has significant potential to enhance EEG processing in the context of deep learning.
