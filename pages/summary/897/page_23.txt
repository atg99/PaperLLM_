study presents various approaches to augmenting datasets for emotion recognition and seizure detection using Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) and other techniques. The research highlights that doubling the dataset size led to the highest classification accuracy improvements, particularly noting a 2.97% rise in accuracy for the SEED dataset and significant enhancements in the DEAP dataset, where arousal classification accuracy improved by 9.15% and valence by 20.13%. The paper discusses the use of conditional Boundary Equilibrium GAN (cBEGAN) by Luo et al., which effectively generated artificial EEG features, resulting in accuracy increases for the SEED dataset from 81.9% to 87.56% with 2000 samples added. Wei et al. employed a WGAN with gradient penalty for seizure detection, achieving an 84% accuracy with data augmentation. Additionally, Chang et al. used GANs to nearly double the dataset trials, resulting in a slight accuracy improvement from 97.9% to 98.4%. Yang et al. augmented a competition dataset using a CNN-LSTM model, achieving an average accuracy of 76.4%. Lastly, Panwar et al. introduced a class conditioned Wasserstein GAN, which effectively addressed stability and frequency artifact issues during training. Overall, the findings indicate that data augmentation techniques, particularly those leveraging GANs, significantly enhance the performance of classifiers in EEG-related tasks.
