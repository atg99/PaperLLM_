The references listed highlight significant contributions to the field of generative adversarial networks (GANs) and their applications in various domains, particularly in data augmentation and brain-computer interfaces (BCIs). Bousmalis et al. introduced unsupervised pixel-level domain adaptation using GANs, while Antoniou et al. focused on data augmentation through GANs. Zhang and Liu demonstrated the enhancement of BCI performance with conditional deep convolutional GANs. Piplani et al. explored the use of GANs to improve brain-based authentication processes. 

Zhang et al. presented DADA, a method for deep adversarial data augmentation tailored for low data scenarios. Hartmann et al. applied GANs to electroencephalographic (EEG) signals, underscoring their versatility. The references also include foundational work on improving GAN training techniques by Salimans et al. and Heusel et al., which are crucial for achieving stable convergence in GANs. 

Rabin et al. discussed the Wasserstein barycenter in texture mixing, while Luo and Lu applied conditional Wasserstein GANs for EEG data augmentation aimed at emotion recognition. Zheng and Lu investigated critical frequency bands for EEG-based emotion recognition, and Koelstra et al. contributed to the establishment of the DEAP database for emotion analysis using physiological signals. Collectively, these works underscore the growing integration of GANs in enhancing data utility and performance across various applications.
