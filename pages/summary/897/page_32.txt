The paper discusses various data augmentation (DA) techniques applied to improve the accuracy of machine learning models for motor imagery classification and emotional response estimation using EEG and video data. Dai et al. (2019) employed a hybrid scaling CNN (HS-CNN) and implemented a three-stage DA method that involved segmenting trials, recombining segments, and swapping frequency bands. This technique increased the average accuracy from 86% to 87.6% for dataset 2b of BCI competition IV. They also explored other DA techniques, such as noise addition and sliding window, achieving accuracies of 86.1% and 80.1%, respectively.

Frydenlund et al. (2015) focused on estimating emotional responses to music videos using EEG data, where they downsampled signals to reduce computational costs. They utilized the discarded data from downsampling as new trials, although this was not explicitly framed as a DA method, and no accuracy comparison was made.

Sakai et al. (2017) proposed various DA methods for EEG signals, including shifting and amplifying time data and near-peak values, with multiplication factors varying between ±5% and ±50%. These approaches demonstrate the ongoing exploration of DA techniques to enhance the performance of machine learning models in analyzing bio-signals.
