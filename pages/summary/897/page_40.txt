The paper discusses the challenges of applying traditional image-based augmentation methods to spectrograms derived from EEG signals, noting the unique characteristics of EEG data, such as its temporal and frequency information, which differ from standard images. It emphasizes the necessity for caution when developing Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) for EEG, as the subjective visual assessment used in image GANs cannot be directly applied to EEG.

The paper reviews various DA techniques, including Fourier transform and intrinsic mode functions, which have shown success in augmenting EEG signals and mitigating overfitting. It notes common practices such as noise addition for mental workload assessment and sampling methods for classifying sleep stages. However, it also points out the publication bias that may skew the perception of DA effectiveness in improving classifier accuracy.

To validate the utility of DA, the authors conducted their own analysis using the BCI IV-2a dataset, achieving a notable improvement in accuracy compared to existing literature and their own non-DA model. Their findings suggest that DA techniques can significantly enhance the performance of EEG analysis models, providing valuable insights for future research in this area.
