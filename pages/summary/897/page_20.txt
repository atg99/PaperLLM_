The paper introduces Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), a framework proposed by Goodfellow et al. that consists of two competing networks: the generator (G) and the discriminator (D). The generator creates fake data, while the discriminator evaluates whether the data is real or fake, akin to a minimax game. The goal is for the generator to improve its output until the discriminator can no longer distinguish between real and fake data. The optimization process employs Jensen-Shannon Divergence to minimize the function defining this game.

GANs have found applications across various fields, including image generation from text and creating rendered images similar to existing datasets. Notably, Zhang et al. (2018) developed a conditional deep convolutional generative adversarial network (cDCGAN) specifically for augmenting EEG signals. This enhanced version incorporates label information into the model. The dataset used for training the cDCGAN consisted of EEG signals collected from subjects during imagined movements, with a convolutional neural network (CNN) trained to classify the signals as indicative of left or right movement.
