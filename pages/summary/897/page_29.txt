The section discusses various approaches to oversampling in the context of EEG classification tasks using Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) and other neural network models. R. Manor et al. (2015) utilized bootstrapping to oversample the minority class in their CNN model for single trial EEG classification, noting some overfitting but improved classification performance. Drouin-Picaro et al. (2016) balanced their dataset by sampling horizontal saccades without replacement and augmenting vertical saccades to achieve equal representation of approximately 3000 examples per direction. Supratak et al. (2017) introduced DeepSleepNet for automatic sleep stage scoring, duplicating minority sleep stages to avoid overfitting and ensure equal sample representation across stages. Dong et al. (2017) also focused on sleep stage classification, employing oversampling to create a balanced dataset. Sors et al. (2018) noted the imbalanced class distribution in their SHHS dataset and attempted cost-sensitive learning and oversampling, but found no performance improvement. Ruffini et al. (2019) balanced their classes by randomly replicating subjects from the minority class for diagnosing conditions based on brief EEG recordings. Finally, Sun et al. (2019) proposed a two-stage neural network for sleep stage classification, which utilized oversampling to address sample imbalance, though they provided limited details on data augmentation outcomes. Overall, these studies highlight the importance of oversampling techniques in addressing class imbalance in EEG data for various applications.
