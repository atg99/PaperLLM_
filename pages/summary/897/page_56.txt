This collection of references highlights significant advancements in the field of EEG signal analysis and seizure detection through deep learning and other machine learning techniques. Thodoroff et al. (2016) discuss the development of robust features for automatic seizure detection using deep learning methods. Sengur et al. (2019) introduce a neutrosophic similarity score-based entropy measure for classifying focal and nonfocal EEG signals. The issue of class imbalance in training datasets is addressed by Kubat and Matwin (1997), as well as Japkowicz (2000), emphasizing its significance and strategies for mitigation.

Chawla et al. (2002) present the SMOTE technique for synthetic minority over-sampling, which is pivotal for handling imbalanced datasets. Vega (year not specified) compares convolutional and recurrent neural networks for predicting seizures using canine EEG data. Wang et al. (2016) focus on training deep neural networks on imbalanced datasets, while Moniz et al. (2016) explore resampling strategies for imbalanced time series.

Schwabedal et al. (2018) tackle class imbalance in noisy signal classification using Fourier Transform Surrogates. Ruffini et al. (2018) apply deep learning with EEG spectrograms in the context of REM behavior disorder. Shamwell et al. (2016) utilize convolutional neural networks for single-trial EEG RSVP classification, and Said et al. (2017) propose a multimodal deep learning approach for compressing and classifying joint EEG-EMG data.

Zhang et al. (2019) investigate epilepsy seizure prediction using common spatial patterns and convolutional neural networks, while Dai et al. (2020) introduce HS-CNN, a hybrid convolution scale CNN for EEG motor imagery classification. Lastly, Frydenlund and Rudzicz (2015) examine emotional affect estimation through video and EEG data using deep neural networks. Collectively, these works contribute to the evolving methodologies in EEG analysis and seizure prediction, leveraging deep learning and addressing challenges like class imbalance.
