The paper discusses the potential of data augmentation (DA) to enhance classification accuracy in deep learning (DL) techniques for electroencephalography (EEG), highlighting its promising yet nascent nature. It notes that many studies lack clarity in reporting their methodologies, with 45% failing to state accuracy prior to DA and 38% not detailing modified parameters. To improve the reproducibility and quality of future research, the authors propose guidelines for reporting results, which include a clear description of the DA method, dataset characteristics, model architecture, and accuracy metrics before and after DA application, as well as sharing reproducible code.

The paper acknowledges limitations, such as the small number of relevant studies available, which constrains the depth of analysis. Additionally, the authors recognize that their findings depend heavily on the quality of the data reviewed, and that their search methodology may not encompass all existing literature. Despite these challenges, the authors aim to provide a transparent analysis of trends in DA for DL-based EEG research, particularly as the field continues to evolve with newer publications emerging rapidly in preprint repositories.
