The paper discusses the exploration of deep learning (DL) architectures for analyzing large datasets, particularly focusing on their hierarchical structure in natural signals. It highlights the variability in the number of hidden layers across different neural network (NN) architectures. A key figure (Fig. 7) aggregates information about these architectures, illustrating that different input formulations are used: recurrent neural networks (RNN) primarily use images, while stacked autoencoders (SAE) rely on calculated features. Long short-term memory networks (LSTM) utilize both signal and calculated features, and convolutional neural networks (CNN) and multi-layer perceptrons (MLP) often employ various input types, with signals being the most frequent.

The paper also emphasizes the significance of data augmentation (DA) in enhancing EEG processing performance through DL methods, as shown in Figure 8. It categorizes the DA techniques into seven groups, including noise addition and generative adversarial networks (GANs). Sliding window techniques were the most prevalent, although the optimal overlap percentage between windows remains debated. The paper identifies two main noise addition methods: adding noise directly to EEG signals and to their spectrogram images. It notes a lack of comprehensive reporting on accuracy improvements post-DA, complicating comparisons between studies.

Since 2018, GANs have gained traction for generating synthetic EEG signals, yet their effectiveness compared to simpler methods like noise addition is still under scrutiny. The paper presents findings indicating that noise addition often yields greater accuracy improvements (14.2% average increase) compared to GANs (5.7% average increase). Overall, while GANs have potential in EEG signal generation, traditional methods remain competitive and require more investigation to clarify their relative advantages.
