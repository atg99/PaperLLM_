Other architectures were also reviewed, but CNNs consistently demonstrated superior performance across various EEG classification tasks. The study highlighted that while deep learning methodologies are powerful, their success is highly dependent on the quality and quantity of data available, as well as the preprocessing techniques applied. 

The review systematically explored various data augmentation techniques tailored for deep learning applications in EEG analysis, aiming to address critical questions about the effectiveness of these methods. The researchers found that data augmentation significantly enhances the performance of deep learning models, particularly in scenarios with limited EEG datasets. However, the optimal data quantity and quality remain ambiguous, with the findings suggesting that lower noise levels yield better results. 

Preprocessing of EEG data was predominantly conducted using frequency-domain filters, which allowed researchers to concentrate on specific frequency ranges relevant to their tasks. Despite the common use of calculated features, the study noted a considerable reliance on raw EEG signals, emphasizing the potential for neural networks to learn features automatically. 

In conclusion, the paper advocates for further targeted research to leverage the benefits of deep learning in EEG processing, emphasizing the need for improved understanding of the interplay between data augmentation, preprocessing methodologies, and neural network architectures.
