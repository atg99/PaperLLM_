The paper evaluates the performance of the text-davinci-003 model on a set of assessments from Python programming courses, highlighting its ability to correct coding solutions based on feedback from auto-graders. The results indicate that while the model achieved scores of 56.1%, 67.9%, and 67.3% across various assessments, these scores were insufficient for course completion. The analysis revealed that the model performs well with direct feedback but struggles with tasks requiring complex reasoning, such as understanding and correcting code based on expected versus actual outputs.

The findings suggest that programming instructors may need to adapt their teaching strategies to account for advancements in AI tools like GPT, focusing on skills such as requirements formulation and debugging. The paper concludes with recommendations for future research, including exploring prompt-tuning techniques and understanding the types of tasks that challenge GPT's reasoning capabilities. Overall, the study provides insights into the integration of AI technologies in programming education while acknowledging its limitations and the need for further exploration.
