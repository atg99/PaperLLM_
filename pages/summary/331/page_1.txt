This paper investigates the ability of generative pre-trained transformers (GPT), specifically the text-davinci-003 model, to pass assessments in introductory and intermediate Python programming courses at the postsecondary level. The study involved a comprehensive evaluation of GPT's performance on a set of 599 exercises, which included multiple-choice questions (MCQs) and coding tasks. The authors aimed to understand whether GPT could successfully navigate the diverse assessments typical in programming education, as discussions around the potential uses and misuses of GPT technology in educational contexts have intensified.

The research found that GPT struggled to pass the full range of assessments, scoring below 70% even in entry-level modules. However, it was noted that a straightforward application of GPT could still enable a learner to secure over 55% of the overall score in these courses. The model demonstrated impressive capabilities, such as correcting solutions based on feedback from an auto-grader, but faced challenges with tasks requiring complex reasoning.

These findings suggest that while GPT models show promise, they are not yet reliable enough to serve as complete solutions for programming assessments. The results can inform educators on how to adapt their assessments to leverage GPT as a supportive tool for learners rather than a means to bypass learning. The study contributes to the ongoing discourse about the implications of AI in education and the necessity for evolving teaching strategies in response to emerging technologies.
