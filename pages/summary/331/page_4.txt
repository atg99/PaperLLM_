The paper discusses the performance of the text-davinci-002 and text-davinci-003 models in educational assessments, particularly focusing on multiple-choice questions (MCQs) and coding tasks within a Python programming course. Results from two performance evaluations (PE1 and PE2) are presented, showing that text-davinci-003 outperformed its predecessor, achieving a correct answer rate of 64.3% compared to 56.8%. However, despite this improvement, the model still struggled to pass the course requirements, achieving 56.1% in PE1 and 67.9% in PE2, with only partial success in module tests and summary assessments. 

The study highlights the challenges faced by the models, particularly with the prompt length constraints and the need to adapt project instructions to fit within these limits, often leading to the fragmentation of tasks. The analysis indicates no significant difference in the models' ability to handle introductory versus intermediate topics, with some complex topics being answered correctly while basic ones were not. The presence of code snippets in questions appeared to hinder performance, suggesting that the integration of natural language and code may be problematic for the models. 

Overall, the findings point to the limitations of AI models in educational settings, particularly in understanding and processing programming-related queries, while also recognizing the potential for generating reflections on learning experiences.
