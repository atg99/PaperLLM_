The paper introduces LEAStereo, a novel method for stereo matching that achieves state-of-the-art results on the KITTI 2015 test dataset while utilizing significantly fewer parameters and lower inference times. Traditional direct regression methods for stereo matching often rely on large U-shaped encoder-decoder networks that do not incorporate geometric constraints, leading to concerns regarding their generalization capabilities. In contrast, volumetric methods utilize semi-global matching and construct a 4D feature volume, employing separate networks for feature extraction and matching. Despite their success, the design of effective architectures for volumetric methods remains challenging.

The authors highlight the potential of Neural Architecture Search (NAS) to optimize network design but acknowledge the computational challenges associated with applying NAS to volumetric methods. Previous attempts at NAS in stereo matching have been limited to direct regression methods and partial architectural searches. This paper proposes an end-to-end hierarchical NAS framework that allows for the automatic selection of optimal structures for both the Feature Net and the Matching Net of volumetric networks. Unlike previous approaches, LEAStereo enables a comprehensive search over network structures, including cell arrangements and feature map sizes.

By integrating geometric knowledge into the NAS process, LEAStereo reduces the computational burden typically associated with architecture searches while improving performance. The method outperforms a wide range of existing algorithms on multiple benchmarks, including KITTI and Middlebury datasets, demonstrating its effectiveness in deep stereo matching.
