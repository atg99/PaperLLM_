This paper presents a novel stereo matching network that integrates neural architecture search (NAS) to optimize its structure for better performance in generating disparity maps from stereo images. The network comprises four main components: a 2D Feature Net for extracting image features, a 4D Feature Volume, a 3D Matching Net for computing matching costs, and a Projection Layer for generating disparity maps using a soft-argmin operation. 

The authors leverage decades of human expertise in stereo matching to guide the architecture search, avoiding the extensive computational demands typical of high-resolution dense prediction tasks. They emphasize the importance of incorporating geometric knowledge into the architecture design, which enhances convergence speed and overall performance. 

The method adopts a two-level hierarchical NAS approach that identifies both cell-level and network-level structures, diverging from traditional encoder-decoder configurations often used in dense prediction tasks. The cell-level search defines a unit as a directed acyclic graph, allowing for systematic optimization of the network's architecture. 

The results demonstrate that the proposed method, named LEAStereo, outperforms existing networks in terms of accuracy, inference time, and network size across several benchmarks, including KITTI and Middlebury datasets. Overall, the paper highlights the effectiveness of integrating human knowledge with NAS techniques for developing efficient and high-performing stereo matching networks.
