The paper presents a novel approach to stereo matching networks through a structured search space for optimizing architecture and performance. It introduces two networks: the Feature Net with six layers and the Matching Net with twelve layers, which are empirically determined to balance computational efficiency and performance, contrasting significantly with deeper architectures like GA-Net that utilize 33 layers.

The authors propose a bi-level optimization strategy that allows for joint training of the networks, using the smooth L1 loss function to ensure robustness against disparity discontinuities. The search for optimal architecture parameters is conducted over a trellis structure where each cell can receive inputs from adjacent cells, facilitating the discovery of paths that minimize loss.

To avoid overfitting during training, two separate disjoint datasets are employed for optimizing network weights and architecture parameters. The architecture search is performed on the SceneFlow dataset, consisting of synthetic images, with further evaluation on established benchmarks such as KITTI and Middlebury.

The implementation of the LEAStereo network is conducted using Pytorch, with a focus on data augmentation through random cropping. The results of the architecture search and the implications of different search strategies are explored in the experiments section, highlighting the effectiveness of the proposed methodology in stereo matching tasks.
