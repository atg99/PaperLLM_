This paper presents the architecture of LEAStereo, a deep learning model designed for stereo matching tasks, evaluated against various benchmarks including SceneFlow, KITTI 2012, and KITTI 2015. The architecture search process is outlined, involving a ten-epoch training period where the first three epochs focus on initializing the super-network's weights and the remaining epochs refine architecture parameters. The model uses a stochastic gradient descent optimizer with specific learning rate and weight decay settings, taking approximately ten GPU days for optimization on an NVIDIA V100 GPU.

The architecture, illustrated in Figure 4, features a design with a pre-defined “stem” and searchable cells, alongside manually added skip connections in the Matching Net. Evaluation metrics include average endpoint errors (EPE) and bad pixel ratios, revealing that LEAStereo outperforms state-of-the-art (SOTA) methods while utilizing significantly fewer parameters. For instance, it achieves an EPE of 0.78 pixels and a bad pixel ratio of 7.82% with only 1.81 million parameters, compared to the considerably larger parameter counts of previous methods like AutoDispNet.

In benchmark evaluations, LEAStereo ranks first among human-designed architectures in the KITTI datasets, showcasing its effectiveness through both quantitative results and qualitative visualizations. Overall, the findings indicate that LEAStereo achieves superior performance while maintaining efficiency, marking a significant contribution to the field of stereo matching.
