The paper presents an ablation study on various searching strategies for stereo matching networks, specifically analyzing their architecture and performance metrics on the SceneFlow test set. It compares different configurations, including the use of joint-search versus separate-search strategies and evaluates the impact of residual cells versus direct cells on performance.

Key findings include that the joint-search strategy outperforms the separate-search method, achieving a 9.30% improvement in end-point error (EPE) and a 10.50% reduction in parameters. Additionally, while the residual cell configuration increases parameters and FLOPS slightly, it results in a 14.29% performance boost.

The Feature Net and Matching Net serve distinct roles, with the Feature Net excelling in feature extraction and the Matching Net enhancing matching cost computations. The analysis demonstrates that using the Feature Net alone yields reasonably good disparity estimations, but the Matching Net significantly improves results.

A comparison with AutoDispNet reveals that the proposed method, LEAStereo, achieves superior performance (32.12% better on the KITTI benchmark) with significantly fewer parameters. Insights from the architecture suggest that a less deep Feature Net can still perform well, larger feature volumes are beneficial, and a cost volume at a resolution of 1/6 offers optimal results.
