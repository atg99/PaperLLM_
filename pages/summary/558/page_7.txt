The paper presents quantitative results from experiments on the KITTI 2012 and 2015 benchmarks, highlighting the performance of various stereo matching methods. The results in Table 2 indicate that the proposed LEAStereo method achieves competitive performance across several metrics, showing notable improvements in bad pixel rates compared to other state-of-the-art methods. It is particularly effective in handling large error thresholds, potentially due to its architecture and loss function design.

In Table 3, the performance of LEAStereo is also evaluated on the Middlebury 2014 Benchmark, where it ranks favorably among more than 120 stereo methods. The results emphasize LEAStereo's low error rates, particularly in the bad 4.0 metric, indicating its robustness in stereo matching tasks.

The paper further conducts an ablation study using the SceneFlow dataset to analyze the impact of various hyperparameters and operations in the algorithm. It compares two sets of candidate operations, Olarge and Oours, where Olarge includes a wider variety of convolution filters and pooling mechanisms, while Oours focuses on a more compact approach. This section aims to justify the design choices made in developing the LEAStereo network, enhancing understanding of its performance characteristics.

Overall, the findings suggest that LEAStereo not only achieves state-of-the-art results but also demonstrates a thoughtful design that balances complexity and performance in stereo matching.
