This paper discusses a method for dynamically expanding a model's capabilities by introducing new components based on a threshold-driven approach. Specifically, when a sample's log-likelihood falls below a certain threshold, it is added to a new dataset, Dnew. Once Dnew reaches a predefined size, the parameters of a new component are initialized based on the existing component that best matches the new data. This process aims to facilitate forward transfer by using the closest existing concept as a starting point for fine-tuning.

Furthermore, the paper addresses the challenge of forgetting previous tasks when learning new ones. It proposes an adaptation of Deep Generative Replay (DGR) to a mixture setting, termed Mixture Generative Replay (MGR). This method involves alternating between real data and data generated from a previous model snapshot. MGR enhances sample quality by maintaining a count of components based on their usage, allowing the model to prioritize more frequently used components during generation.

The strategy ensures that previously learned tasks are effectively reused without imposing constraints on the components used for modeling. The paper also considers when to update the model snapshot, suggesting two approaches: at fixed intervals or before dynamic expansion, which helps consolidate learned information before introducing new concepts. This method minimizes the need for additional hyperparameters related to snapshot timing.
