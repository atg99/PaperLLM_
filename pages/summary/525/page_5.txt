In the related work section, the paper discusses various approaches in generative models and continual learning. It highlights advancements in generative models like the Variational Autoencoder (VAE), where methods such as those by Nalisnick et al. and Jiang et al. have explored capturing class structures and clustering through innovative latent space designs while addressing component-collapsing issues. The section also touches on continual learning strategies, which aim to retain knowledge from previous tasks while accommodating new ones. Techniques range from regularization methods that prioritize important parameters to expansion-based models that dynamically adjust capacity for new tasks. The paper notes a shift towards task-agnostic continual learning, where recent studies have attempted to navigate learning without explicit task labels or boundaries. The approach discussed in this paper aims to tackle unsupervised continual learning problems, emphasizing the need to handle complex real-world scenarios where task identities are ambiguous.

In the experiments section, the paper outlines the empirical evaluation of its method, focusing on three main aspects: the ability to learn a class-discriminable latent space in an unsupervised setting without forgetting, the significance of dynamic expansion and generative replay techniques, and the performance of CURL on external benchmarks.
