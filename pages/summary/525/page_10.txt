The referenced papers focus on various aspects of continual learning and deep learning techniques, addressing challenges such as catastrophic forgetting and the integration of new information into existing models. 

Burda et al. introduce importance weighted autoencoders to enhance representation learning. Chaudhry et al. propose a method called A-GEM for efficient lifelong learning that mitigates forgetting. Draelos et al. extend deep networks to accommodate new classes through neurogenesis deep learning techniques. Farquhar and Gal emphasize the need for robust evaluations in continual learning approaches.

Fernando et al. present PathNet, which channels gradient descent in super neural networks for improved learning efficiency. Golkar et al. explore neural pruning as a mechanism for continual learning. Goodfellow et al. investigate the phenomenon of catastrophic forgetting in gradient-based neural networks, establishing foundational insights into this issue.

He and Jaeger propose a backpropagation method that utilizes conceptors to overcome catastrophic interference. Hsu et al. re-evaluate continual learning scenarios, advocating for strong baseline comparisons. Jiang et al. introduce a variational deep embedding approach for unsupervised clustering.

Joo et al. present the Dirichlet variational autoencoder, while Kingma and Welling's work on auto-encoding variational Bayes has become a cornerstone in generative models. Kirkpatrick et al. discuss methods to overcome catastrophic forgetting, contributing to a deeper understanding of neural network resilience.

Lake et al. explore one-shot learning of visual concepts, demonstrating the possibilities of learning from minimal examples. Li and Hoiem develop a framework for learning without forgetting, which is crucial for retaining knowledge over time. Lopez-Paz et al. introduce gradient episodic memory as a strategy for continual learning. 

McCloskey and Cohen's early work on catastrophic interference lays the groundwork for future research in this area. Milan et al. present the forget-me-not process, aiming to enhance memory retention in neural networks. Finally, Nalisnick and Smyth contribute to variational autoencoders with stick-breaking techniques, expanding the toolkit for probabilistic modeling.

Overall, these works collectively advance the field of continual learning, addressing the critical challenges of memory retention and adaptation in neural networks.
