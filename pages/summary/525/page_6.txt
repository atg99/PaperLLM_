This paper discusses the evaluation of the CURL (Continual Unsupervised Representation Learning) approach, particularly focusing on its ability to learn class-discriminative latent representations from non-stationary input data. Key metrics for evaluation include cluster accuracy and k-Nearest Neighbours (k-NN) accuracy in latent space, which help assess the model's ability to encode class-relevant information and the overall discriminability of the latent space.

The experiments utilize the MNIST and Omniglot datasets under various settings: i.i.d (independently and identically distributed), sequential (classes presented one after another), and continuous drift (gradually introducing new classes). The findings highlight the importance of dynamic expansion and mixture generative replay (MGR) in preventing catastrophic forgetting while learning representations. Results indicate that CURL effectively retains knowledge of earlier classes, with confusion primarily occurring between similar classes, as shown in the class confusion matrix.

Additionally, the paper presents ablation studies examining the impact of the expansion threshold on cluster accuracy and the number of components used, revealing that increasing the threshold leads to more aggressive model expansion and improved accuracy, peaking at 84%. Overall, the analysis suggests that while CURL addresses catastrophic forgetting, there is room for improvement in the model's plasticity for learning new concepts. Further insights and details are provided in the appendices.
