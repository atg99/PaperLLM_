The paper presents a novel approach called CURL aimed at addressing the unsupervised continual learning problem, where task labels and boundaries are unknown. CURL utilizes a mixture-of-Gaussians latent space and implements dynamic expansion along with mixture generative replay (MGR) to facilitate the instantiation of new concepts and mitigate catastrophic forgetting. 

The study evaluates CURL on benchmark datasets MNIST and Omniglot, comparing its performance with various existing models such as VAE, Dirichlet VAE, SBV AE, and VaDE using k-NN error metrics. The results indicate that CURL's performance is competitive with state-of-the-art methods, particularly excelling in learning structured and discriminable latent spaces even when trained on shuffled data. Additionally, CURL's sequential training results are noted to be on par with i.i.d. data methods, showcasing its versatility.

The findings highlight CURL’s capability to learn meaningful class-discriminative representations without significant forgetting, even in challenging settings with poorly defined task boundaries. The paper concludes by suggesting future research directions, including exploring methods to further reduce forgetting and extending the approach to reinforcement learning contexts.
