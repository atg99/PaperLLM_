The paper presents an approach called Continual Unsupervised Representation Learning (CURL), which addresses the challenges of unsupervised continual learning. Unlike traditional methods that rely on supervised or reinforcement learning with known task labels and boundaries, CURL focuses on learning representations without such prior knowledge. The authors explore various scenarios, including abrupt task changes, smooth transitions, and shuffled data, emphasizing the need for models that can infer tasks dynamically.

CURL incorporates rehearsal-based techniques to mitigate catastrophic forgetting, a common issue where new tasks overwrite previous learnings. The efficacy of this approach is demonstrated using the MNIST and Omniglot datasets, where the absence of labels prevents information leakage about tasks. Additionally, the paper highlights CURL's strong performance in both unsupervised settings and when adapted for supervised tasks like incremental class learning.

The introduction contrasts human learning capabilities with current learning algorithms, noting the latter's reliance on shuffled data and the challenges posed by sequential data. The authors argue that existing continual learning methods often fall short in real-world applications due to their focus on clearly defined tasks and class labels. This paper aims to expand the understanding of continual learning by proposing a framework that operates effectively in more realistic, unsupervised environments.
