The paper presents an ablation study evaluating dynamic expansion in mixture generative replay (MGR and SMGR) on MNIST and Omniglot datasets. The study analyzes cluster accuracy, k-nearest neighbor (k-NN) error, and the number of components used while varying the expansion threshold. Results show that dynamic expansion improves performance compared to a scenario without expansion, which yielded significantly poorer accuracy.

The findings reveal that both MGR and SMGR are sensitive to the frequency of snapshots taken during training, with optimal performance achieved when snapshots coincide with class changes. Taking snapshots too frequently leads to decreased performance and increased component usage. Interestingly, SMGR shows lower performance than standard MGR, potentially due to reduced adaptability in the model.

The analysis highlights the trade-off between cluster accuracy and memory cost when using dynamic expansion, suggesting it serves as a tuning parameter for capacity estimation. While MGR enhances performance in the MNIST case, the Omniglot case demonstrates that effective management of components can mitigate catastrophic forgetting, albeit at a higher component cost. The results underscore the importance of adjusting snapshot timing and the implications of using self-supervised methods in this context. Further details are provided in the appendix.
