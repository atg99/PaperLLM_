This paper presents a model based on a mixture of Gaussians that captures both inter- and intra-cluster variations. Due to the intractability of posterior inference for p(y,z|x), the authors propose an approximate variational posterior q(y,z|x) as the product of q(y|x) and q(z|x,y). Each component of this model is parameterized using neural networks: a shared representation for input encoding, a softmax output for mixture probabilities, and component-specific latent encoders for Gaussian parameters. The prior parameters are defined via a linear layer with a softplus nonlinearity.

The loss function is formulated as the evidence lower bound (ELBO), which includes terms for reconstruction loss and regularization. The expectation over q(y|x) can be computed exactly, while the expectation over q(z|x,y) necessitates sampling, leading to a Monte Carlo approximation. This approximation encourages the model to perform data reconstruction and clustering simultaneously, allowing it to leverage different components for various discrete structures in the data.

The paper also introduces a component-constrained learning approach, enabling the use of supervised losses when labels are available. This adapts the ELBO to focus on known labels, enhancing the model's performance by directly aligning q(y|x) with observed labels.

Additionally, the authors propose a dynamic expansion method to determine the number of mixture components, which involves adding capacity as needed by identifying poorly modeled samples and initializing new components when a critical sample size is reached. Overall, the approach aims to improve clustering and representation learning in an unsupervised context, while also accommodating supervised scenarios.
