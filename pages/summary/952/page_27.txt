The paper discusses advancements in understanding uncertainty in deep neural networks, particularly focusing on variational inference and sampling methods. Variational inference has been successfully scaled to large datasets like ImageNet, with techniques such as data augmentation and learning rate scheduling enhancing its practicality. A notable method, Monte Carlo Dropout (MC Dropout), formulates dropout layers as Bernoulli random variables, allowing for uncertainty estimation during both training and testing phases. This method's accessibility and effectiveness have been demonstrated in various studies, leading to extensions that explore different dropout strategies and representations of uncertainty.

Additionally, the paper highlights sampling methods, particularly Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC), as a non-parametric approach to Bayesian inference. These methods enable uncertainty representation without being confined to a specific distribution type, making them suitable for high-dimensional problems typical in neural networks. MCMC operates by transitioning through state space based on current states, facilitating the drawing of samples from the true posterior distribution. Overall, the paper emphasizes the significance of these methods in enhancing the understanding and representation of uncertainty in deep learning models.
