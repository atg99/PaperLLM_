The paper discusses the concept of uncertainty in deep neural networks, focusing on how various measures can assess model uncertainty. Smith and Gal (2018) highlight that mutual information (MI) is minimal when knowledge of model parameters does not enhance prediction information, thus serving as a metric for model uncertainty. The Kullback-Leibler divergence is introduced as a method to gauge the divergence between probability distributions, specifically the expected divergence among softmax outputs, which also reflects model uncertainty.

The predictive variance is examined as a means to evaluate variability in softmax outputs. The paper notes that while some Bayesian methods offer analytically defined posterior distributions, most cases require approximations, such as Monte Carlo methods, to manage the intractable propagation of parameter uncertainty into predictions. Ensemble methods and data augmentation approaches are discussed as techniques to gather multiple predictions, which can then be used to approximate the underlying distribution and apply uncertainty measures.

Furthermore, the paper addresses the challenge of measuring distributional uncertainty in classification tasks. Traditional uncertainty measures may not account for distributional shifts or out-of-distribution (OOD) examples, potentially leading to biased inferences. The authors emphasize that when predictors converge on a false class label, variability appears low, misleadingly suggesting high certainty. To mitigate this, several approaches consider the magnitude of logits, interpreting them as evidence for class predictions, either through the lens of Dirichlet distributions or by comparing logits against a constant.

Overall, the paper highlights the complexity of measuring uncertainty in deep learning models and the necessity for robust methods to ensure reliable predictions in varying contexts.
