The paper presents a comparative overview of various Bayesian neural network approaches, focusing on their properties as outlined in Table 3. It emphasizes that these properties should not be used to compare Bayesian methods against other uncertainty techniques, such as ensembles or single deterministic models, which are discussed separately in Table 1.

Key Bayesian approaches include:

1. **Variational Inference**: This method approximates the posterior distribution by optimizing a family of tractable distributions, minimizing the KL divergence. It provides an analytic expression, is deterministic, but is not unbiased and may converge to a local optimum. The computational effort is medium during training and high during inference due to the need for multiple forward passes.

2. **Sampling Approaches**: Based on Markov Chain Monte Carlo methods, these approaches allow for sampling from the target random variable but do not yield an analytic expression. They are not deterministic, can be unbiased, but face challenges with convergence assessment and require high computational effort for both training and inference.

3. **Laplace Approximation**: This method simplifies the target distribution by approximating the log-posterior and deriving a normal distribution on network weights. It provides an analytic expression, can be applied to pretrained networks, is deterministic and not unbiased, with convergence that is easy to assess. It requires low computational effort during training but high effort during inference.

The applications of these methods span various fields, including medical imaging, robotics, earth observation, and plant disease detection, highlighting their versatility and relevance in practical scenarios.
