The paper discusses various methods for evaluating the calibration of deep neural networks, focusing on calibration measures such as Expected Calibration Error (ECE), Static Calibration Error (SCE), and their adaptive counterparts (aECE and aSCE). 

The ECE is a widely used metric that involves dividing predictions into M equally spaced bins to assess calibration based on the top-label confidence scores. In contrast, the SCE considers all class predictions, computing calibration errors across bins and averaging them, which has been shown to provide a more effective assessment of calibration errors.

The adaptive calibration error differs by grouping predictions into R bins with equal numbers of predictions rather than fixed widths. Studies indicate that aECE and aSCE are more robust to the number of bins used compared to ECE and SCE. 

Additionally, the paper highlights the issue of class imbalance in multi-class settings, where larger classes can overshadow the calibration errors of smaller classes, affecting the overall evaluation of model performance.
