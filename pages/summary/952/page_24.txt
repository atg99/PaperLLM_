The paper discusses three types of Bayesian Neural Network (BNN) approaches: variational inference, Laplace approximation, and sampling methods, highlighting their differences in terms of determinism, computational costs, and capabilities. Variational inference and the Laplace approximation provide analytical expressions of uncertainty and are deterministic, while sampling methods generate samples and can capture multiple modes in the data, though they lack an analytical expression and are harder to assess for convergence.

In terms of computational costs, the Laplace approximation is efficient for normal neural network training, whereas variational inference incurs additional costs due to regularization and extra parameters for uncertainty representation. Sampling methods are the most computationally intensive during training due to their inherent reliance on sampling. The Laplace approximation can be applied to pre-trained networks without modification, but all approaches require multiple forward passes during inference, making them costly.

The paper also acknowledges advances in related areas of BNN research, including approximate inference techniques, probabilistic programming leveraging GPUs, various types of priors, theoretical advancements, uncertainty propagation techniques, and computations of aleatoric uncertainty.

Focusing on variational inference, the paper explains its goal of inferring posterior probabilities using a specified family of distributions. The variational family, such as the Multivariate Normal distribution, is parameterized, and the aim is to adjust these parameters to minimize the Kullback-Leibler divergence between the variational distribution and the posterior. Since direct minimization of the posterior is not feasible, the evidence lower bound (ELBO) is optimized instead, which relates to the KL divergence up to a constant.
