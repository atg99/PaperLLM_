The paper discusses the limitations of traditional neural network outputs, particularly in relation to uncertainty estimation in classification tasks. It highlights that while outputs from the softmax function in multiclass settings and the sigmoid function in binary classifications provide class probabilities, they often lead to over-confidence and poor calibration. This is particularly problematic when networks encounter out-of-distribution samples, which can result in misleadingly high confidence scores.

A significant example is provided: a network trained on cats and dogs may assign a higher probability to cats when presented with an image of a bird, due to feature extraction that does not accurately reflect the absence of training data related to birds. The paper cites research indicating that networks can become increasingly confident as the distance from the training data increases, further complicating uncertainty estimation.

To address these issues, the work references methods proposed by Hein et al. (2019) aimed at enforcing a uniform predictive distribution for samples outside the training set. Additionally, it discusses the use of the Dirichlet distribution in classification approaches as a way to incorporate logit magnitude for better uncertainty quantification. The Dirichlet distribution serves as a conjugate prior for categorical distributions, offering a framework to model uncertainty more effectively. The paper emphasizes the need for improved techniques that account for model uncertainty to enhance the reliability of predictions in neural networks.
