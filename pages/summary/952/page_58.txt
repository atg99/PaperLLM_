The paper discusses the challenges and advancements in uncertainty estimation methods for deep neural networks (DNNs) within the robotics and Earth Observation (EO) fields. In robotics, the unique challenges include managing computational limits for real-time applications, leveraging spatial and temporal information, selecting uncertain samples for online learning, and manipulating scenes under uncertainty. These challenges stem from the physical nature of robots as situated systems.

In the EO domain, the increasing use of EO systems for critical decision-making highlights the importance of uncertainty quantification. Despite early successes in applying deep learning to EO, the variability of data—due to differing sensor types, spatial and temporal changes—complicates this task. The paper emphasizes the need for efficient uncertainty quantification methods, particularly as many EO applications are sensitive to these uncertainties. While there has been significant research on deep learning in EO, there is comparatively less focus on measuring uncertainties.

Additionally, the paper addresses the issue of out-of-distribution (OOD) detection, noting that real-world data often diverges from training data, which can lead to misclassifications. It underscores the importance of OOD detection and uncertainty measurements in EO, especially in tasks like data fusion, where EO data can encompass a vast number of channels compared to standard optical images.
