The paper discusses the uncertainty inherent in deep neural networks, highlighting how various factors influence training outcomes. It emphasizes that the non-linear loss landscape leads to different results depending on the initializations and the order of training data points. 

Two primary strategies to manage uncertainty are bagging and boosting. Bagging involves sampling training data uniformly with replacement, allowing some samples to be seen multiple times while others may be omitted. In contrast, boosting trains models sequentially, adjusting sample probabilities based on the previous ensemble's performance. 

Data augmentation is another method mentioned, where input data is randomly altered for each ensemble member, promoting diversity. Additionally, combining different network architectures results in varied loss landscapes, enhancing prediction diversity.

Previous studies indicate that random initialization can effectively induce variety, though bagging may underperform in certain scenarios. Research by Livieris et al. (2021) shows that bagging is more effective with fewer ensemble members, while boosting excels with larger groups. Nanni et al. (2019) explored the impact of various image augmentations on ensembles for bioimage classification.

The paper also visualizes the differing evaluation behaviors of deterministic neural networks, Bayesian neural networks, and ensembles of deterministic networks, illustrating how each approaches parameter optimization and loss value differently.
