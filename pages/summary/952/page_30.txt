The paper discusses the evolution of the Laplace Approximation in the context of neural networks, particularly focusing on the challenges posed by the large number of parameters in modern architectures. The estimation of the Hessian matrix is central to the Laplace Approximation, but computing it directly is infeasible for these networks. Various methods have been proposed to approximate the Hessian, moving beyond simple diagonal approximations to include off-diagonal elements for improved accuracy, such as the layer-wise Kronecker Factor approximation.

Recent advancements have demonstrated scalable approaches to Laplace Approximation, including the re-scaling of eigenvalues for better diagonal variance representation and corrections to diagonal elements in the parameter space. The paper also highlights the use of diagonal Fisher information or Gauss-Newton matrices for weight pruning and continual learning to prevent catastrophic forgetting.

Additionally, it notes developments in low-rank approximations that leverage the sparse nature of Hessian eigenvalues, allowing for scalable applications to large datasets like ImageNet. The paper concludes by mentioning that recent research has expanded the Laplace Approximation's capabilities to optimize hyperparameters and improve inference accuracy through new hidden unit designs, addressing the limitations of traditional assumptions regarding posterior distributions.
