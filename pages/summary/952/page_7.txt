The paper discusses the uncertainties inherent in deep neural networks, identifying several key factors that contribute to these uncertainties during both the training and inference phases. 

The stochastic nature of neural network training arises from random decisions influenced by data order, initializations, and regularization techniques like dropout. This randomness leads to the emergence of different local optima, resulting in varied models for the same training data. Factors such as batch size, learning rate, and the number of training epochs also impact model performance, leading to significant differences in predictions for individual samples.

One critical source of uncertainty is errors in the training procedure. The training process involves numerous parameters that can yield different model parameterizations due to stochastic decisions such as batch generation and weight initialization. Additionally, imbalanced or poorly covered training datasets introduce further uncertainties, which can be mitigated through data augmentation and balancing strategies.

During inference, the model predicts outputs for new data samples. However, when the model encounters samples from a different domain than it was trained on, it can lead to errors. This illustrates a source of uncertainty stemming from the model's inability to generalize beyond its training data, particularly in classification tasks.

Finally, the paper emphasizes the importance of predictive uncertainty, which concerns the uncertainty propagated onto predictions. This predictive uncertainty is influenced by both the characteristics of the sample being evaluated and the underlying data acquisition model, highlighting the complex relationships between training data, model behavior, and prediction outcomes.
