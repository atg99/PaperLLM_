The paper discusses the estimation of predictive uncertainty in neural networks, emphasizing the importance of distinguishing between model-dependent and input data-dependent errors. The authors introduce a maximum a posteriori (MAP) estimation approach, relying on a sampled training dataset to predict the distribution for new samples. The predictive uncertainty is categorized into two main types: data uncertainty, which arises from the inherent noise in the data, and model uncertainty, which stems from limitations in the model itself or the training process.

Data uncertainty, also referred to as aleatoric uncertainty, is caused by the representation of real-world phenomena in data samples, such as noise in measurements or insufficient information in classification tasks. Conversely, model uncertainty, or epistemic uncertainty, relates to issues within the model structure or training methodology, including insufficient data coverage and errors during the training process.

The paper identifies various factors contributing to predictive uncertainty, noting that only one factor represents aleatoric uncertainty, while others are linked to epistemic uncertainty. The authors illustrate how these factors affect the reliability of neural network predictions, highlighting the need for careful consideration of both data and model uncertainties in the development and application of neural networks.
