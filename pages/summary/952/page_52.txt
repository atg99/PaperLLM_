It aims to assist researchers and practitioners by providing a structured summary of commonly used tasks, data sets, and baseline methods, organized in a table format for clarity.

The review identifies regression tasks as the primary focus for evaluating uncertainty estimation, with an emphasis on both samples near and far from the training distribution. It highlights the significance of calibrating uncertainty estimates in classification tasks and notes that out-of-distribution (OOD) detection and robustness against adversarial attacks are also critical areas of investigation. In medical applications, the calibration of semantic segmentation results is particularly emphasized.

Consistent data sets are noted across reviewed works, including toy data sets for regression and UCI data sets for log-likelihood comparisons. For calibration and OOD detection, popular data sets include MNIST, CIFAR10, CIFAR100, SVHN, and ImageNet. The pairing of training and evaluation data sets for OOD detection is also discussed, along with the manipulation of classification data sets to study calibration effects.

Among the baselines, Monte Carlo Dropout and deep ensembles are the most commonly employed, with the softmax output from deterministic models serving as a standard baseline. The paper also notes preferences for certain baselines within specific approaches, such as Bayesian Neural Networks (BNNs) often being compared to variational inference methods.

Overall, the section provides a useful resource for understanding the landscape of uncertainty estimation methods and the benchmarks against which they are evaluated, facilitating better experimental design and method selection in future research.
