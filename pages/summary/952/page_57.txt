The paper discusses the challenges of uncertainty in deep neural networks (DNNs) within the context of robotics, where robots operate with incomplete knowledge of their environments. This uncertainty can lead to critical failures, particularly in applications such as surgical robotics and self-driving cars. The authors highlight that traditional assumptions about training and testing conditions being from the same distribution often do not hold true in robotics, necessitating a focus on quantifying uncertainty in DNN predictions to mitigate risks.

The paper emphasizes the importance of probabilistic representations in robotics, which have been integral even before deep learning's emergence. Various methods for robot perception, such as localization and simultaneous localization and mapping (SLAM), utilize probabilistic frameworks. Advanced consumer robotics, including robotic vacuum cleaners and drones, rely on these methods for effective functioning. Additionally, the authors mention that planning and control in robotics are often framed as Bayesian sequential learning problems, with frameworks like Partially Observable Markov Decision Processes (POMDPs) incorporating probabilistic approaches to decision-making.

The text details advancements in deep learning that aim to quantify uncertainty, highlighting task-specific methods. For instance, novelty detection using auto-encoders, uncertainty estimation in rotational learning, and reinforcement learning algorithms that are uncertainty-aware have been proposed. The authors also mention various studies that have developed deep learning-based localization systems with integrated uncertainty estimates, showcasing the evolving landscape of uncertainty management in robotics through deep learning techniques.
