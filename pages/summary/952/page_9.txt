The paper discusses the significance of addressing uncertainty in deep neural networks (DNNs), particularly in the context of remote sensing classification tasks. It highlights that while model uncertainty can theoretically be reduced by enhancing the architecture, learning process, or training data, data uncertainties are inherent and cannot be eliminated. DNNs must effectively manage uncertain inputs and quantify model uncertainty to provide accurate predictions, which is crucial for mission- and safety-critical applications.

The Bayesian framework is presented as a valuable approach for reasoning about uncertainty in deep learning. In this framework, model uncertainty is represented as a probability distribution over the model parameters, while data uncertainty is depicted as a probability distribution over the model outputs. The predictive distribution over a prediction is formulated, acknowledging that the posterior distribution of model parameters is generally intractable. Ensemble methods are suggested as a means to approximate this distribution by averaging results from multiple parameter settings. The paper emphasizes the need for DNNs to incorporate these uncertainty considerations to improve their reliability and performance in real-world applications.
