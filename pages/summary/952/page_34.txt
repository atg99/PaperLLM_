The discussion highlights the dual role of ensemble methods in both enhancing predictive accuracy and quantifying uncertainty in predictions. 

While some studies advocate for the use of augmentations and diverse training strategies to enhance ensemble performance, others caution that improper application can harm the calibration of uncertainty estimations. Notably, Lakshminarayanan et al. (2017) established a foundational approach for uncertainty quantification using ensembles of neural networks, which has since gained traction. Their methodology includes a novel training pipeline that allows for the prediction of both output and data uncertainty, demonstrating competitive performance against Bayesian Neural Network approaches.

Additionally, the paper emphasizes the importance of model variety—achieved through randomization and data shuffling—in enhancing uncertainty predictions. The findings suggest that smaller ensembles, with as few as five members, can outperform other methods in robustness to data set shifts. Overall, the evidence presented underlines the effectiveness of ensemble methods in both accuracy and reliability for real-life applications, positioning them as a superior option for uncertainty quantification compared to traditional methods like Monte Carlo Dropout.
