The paper discusses methods for detecting out-of-distribution (OOD) samples and evaluating performance in classification and regression tasks. It highlights that applying the sigmoid function to logits can help derive class probabilities, making OOD sample detection more effective as low probabilities across classes can indicate such samples. Several methods provide measures of how well new data fits the training distribution, influencing prediction accuracy.

In performance evaluation, uncertainty measures differentiate between correctly and incorrectly classified samples or between in-domain and OOD samples. Common approaches include Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) and Precision-Recall (PR) curves, which visualize performance at various thresholds. The area under these curves (AUC) offers a quantitative assessment, indicating the likelihood that a randomly chosen positive sample has a higher measure than a negative one. However, a significant limitation is that performance evaluation and threshold determination rely on a specific test dataset, making them vulnerable to distribution shifts.

In regression tasks, the paper notes a different challenge, as networks typically provide pointwise estimates without uncertainty measures. To address this, a common strategy is to have the network predict parameters of a probability distribution instead of a single point estimate, allowing for better representation of data uncertainty.
