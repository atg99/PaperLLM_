This paper discusses various approaches to managing uncertainty in deep neural networks, particularly in the context of out-of-distribution detection and the efficiency of ensemble methods. A novel loss function is introduced that aims to maintain a minimum margin between the average entropy of in-domain and out-of-distribution data, significantly enhancing detection capabilities.

The paper highlights the computational challenges associated with ensemble methods, which, while improving performance, demand more memory and processing power. To address these limitations, techniques such as pruning and distillation are proposed. Pruning reduces redundancy among ensemble members using various diversity measures, thereby minimizing resource consumption without compromising performance.

Distillation involves training a single model to encapsulate the knowledge of multiple networks. Initially motivated by the challenges of large-scale classification, this approach allows for the separation of complex classification tasks into manageable sub-problems. Ensemble distillation, specifically, captures the behavior of an ensemble through a single network by averaging softmax outputs to convey predictive uncertainty. However, this method risks losing important model uncertainty information. 

To mitigate this, researchers have explored learning higher-order distributions, which maintain the diversity of outputs by modeling the ensemble members and distilled networks as prior networks predicting Dirichlet distribution parameters. This approach, generalized to other parameterizable distributions, effectively distinguishes between data and model uncertainty and demonstrates good performance in regression tasks as well. While distillation cannot fully replicate ensemble behavior, it offers a practical solution for reducing model complexity while retaining predictive capabilities.
