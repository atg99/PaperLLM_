The paper discusses key challenges and future directions in evaluating uncertainty in deep neural networks. It highlights the need for a generic evaluation framework that addresses issues such as the absence of 'ground truth' uncertainties, the inability to test on single instances, and the lack of standardized benchmarking protocols. A proposed evaluation protocol should include diverse baseline datasets and metrics, focusing on risk-averse scenarios to ensure high predicted uncertainty does not lead to failure in critical predictions, like traffic light signals.

Furthermore, the paper emphasizes the necessity for a systematic comparison of uncertainty estimation methods across real-world applications. Currently, there is a lack of structured evaluations, making it unclear which methods perform best in specific applications. The authors suggest that domain experts should engage in comparative analyses and share findings on a centralized platform, akin to Google's repository on uncertainty baselines.

The challenge of validating uncertainty methods due to the lack of ground truths is also discussed. Establishing a reliable ground truth for uncertainty, similar to benchmarks like ImageNet, would facilitate better evaluation of predictions on individual samples. Lastly, the paper mentions the importance of explainability in uncertainty estimation, which enhances trust in these methods, and references recent work aimed at making uncertainty estimation more interpretable.
