The paper discusses the calibration of neural networks in the context of data augmentation techniques, particularly focusing on mixup training and its impact on model performance. The authors highlight that mixup, which generates virtual training samples through convex combinations of training pairs, introduces a form of label smoothing that can act as an entropy-based regularization method. While mixup is recognized for improving calibration and accuracy, some researchers argue that the data uncertainty introduced by mixed inputs may actually hinder calibration.

Maroñas et al. (2020) propose a new objective function that considers calibration performance specifically on unmixed samples, addressing the miscalibration that may arise from mixup training. This approach utilizes the expected calibration error (ECE) to evaluate the calibration on unmixed samples, leading to a loss function that combines the original loss with a calibration measure.

Additionally, Patel et al. (2021) introduce on-manifold data augmentation, which involves generating out-of-domain samples through adversarial attacks, demonstrating its superiority over mixup in enhancing calibration. The findings suggest that exposing classifiers to out-of-domain examples during training can significantly improve calibration, as supported by the research of Hendrycks et al. (2019).

Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of calibration in neural networks and critiques existing data augmentation methods while proposing refined strategies to enhance model reliability.
