The document also reviews various neural network uncertainty quantification approaches, emphasizing the trade-offs between ease of application and theoretical rigor. Ensemble methods and test-time augmentation are noted for their straightforward implementation, whereas Bayesian methods provide a more comprehensive understanding of uncertainty but often require more computational resources. Among Bayesian techniques, Laplace approximations are favored for their lower computational demands and compatibility with pretrained networks.

A critical aspect of uncertainty quantification is the identification of the source and type of uncertainty, with out-of-distribution detection being a major concern to prevent erroneous network decisions. Existing research has shown promise in detecting out-of-distribution samples, but real-world scenarios present more complex challenges than those observed in standard datasets. The work by Gustafsson et al. is highlighted as a significant advancement in evaluating methods against real-world demands, revealing that ensembles may outperform Bayesian approaches in practical settings.
