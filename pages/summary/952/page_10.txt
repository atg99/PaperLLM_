The paper discusses the application of Bayesian inference in modeling, specifically using Bayes Theorem to reformulate predictive distributions. The prior distribution, denoted as p(θ), incorporates general knowledge about the model parameters without additional data influence. The likelihood, p(D|θ), indicates how probable the observed data is under a given model parameterization. Various loss functions, such as cross-entropy and mean squared error, are linked to maximizing log-likelihood.

Despite the reformulation, the predictive distribution remains intractable, prompting the exploration of approximation methods, detailed in a subsequent section. An illustrative example of a neural network pipeline for land cover classification is provided, highlighting factors that contribute to predictive uncertainty. These factors include environmental changes, insufficient measurements, label noise, uncertainties from the network structure, and the introduction of unfamiliar image types during network testing. The paper emphasizes the complexity of accurately predicting outcomes in such dynamic contexts.
