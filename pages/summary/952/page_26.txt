The paper discusses the evolution of variational methods for Bayesian Neural Networks (BNNs), tracing their origins to early works by Hinton and Van Camp in 1993, who introduced a diagonal Gaussian approximation for the posterior distribution grounded in information theory. Subsequent advancements, notably by Barber and Bishop in 1998, expanded this framework to include full covariance matrices and established methods for optimizing the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) in neural networks.

Modern approaches have built on these foundational concepts, particularly focusing on scaling variational inference for contemporary neural architectures. A significant trend is the adoption of stochastic variational inference, which optimizes the ELBO using mini-batches of data. Key contributions to this area include Graves' 2011 work on Gaussian priors and Blundell et al.'s 2015 Bayes By Backprop, which extended stochastic methods to non-Gaussian priors while ensuring unbiased stochastic gradients. Kingma et al. in 2015 introduced the local reparameterization trick to mitigate gradient variance, allowing the loss function to be reformulated as the ELBO.

Despite these advancements, challenges remain, particularly concerning the sensitivity of stochastic variational inference to initialization, prior specifications, and gradient variance. Wu et al. in 2018 proposed using hierarchical priors for more deterministic approximations of variational moments to address these issues.

Traditionally, mean-field approximations have been employed, overlooking parameter correlations. To enhance the expressiveness of variational distributions in deep networks, recent studies have explored matrix normal distributions, low-rank decompositions, and normalizing flows, which utilize a series of invertible transformations to create complex posterior distributions from simpler initial forms. Farquhar et al. in 2020 suggested that mean-field approximations may not be as limiting as previously thought, proposing that capturing depth-wise correlations could be more crucial than layer-wise correlations. Nonetheless, mean-field approaches offer lower computational complexity, which remains a practical advantage.
