The paper "A Survey of Uncertainty in Deep Neural Networks" discusses the various sources of uncertainty in the predictions made by deep neural networks (DNNs). It identifies two primary types of errors: those arising from the training procedure and those caused by unknown data. The paper outlines a four-step process that transitions from raw information to the quantification of uncertainty in DNN predictions, emphasizing how uncertainties are propagated through each step. 

The first section focuses on data acquisition in supervised learning, detailing how measurements and target variables are generated to represent real-world situations. The authors highlight that random noise and information loss can occur during this process, impacting both the input data and the corresponding target variables. This randomness introduces uncertainty, especially when the training data does not comprehensively cover the sample space.

The paper asserts that neural networks trained on limited datasets can lead to uncertainty in predictions when encountering new, unseen samples. It stresses the importance of adequately covering the sample space with training data to minimize this uncertainty. Additionally, the paper discusses the implications of the labeling process, which may vary in accuracy depending on the context, such as in tasks related to speech recognition or earth observation.

Overall, the goal of the section is to provide a clear understanding of the uncertainties associated with neural networks, emphasizing the mathematical properties relevant for applying various methodologies across different fields.
