The paper presents an overview of four general methods for predicting outcomes and quantifying uncertainty in neural networks: single deterministic networks, Bayesian methods, ensemble methods, and test-time data augmentation. 

1. **Single Deterministic Networks**: These utilize a straightforward approach, producing predictions and uncertainty through a single forward pass. They exhibit high sensitivity to initialization and training parameters, require low computational effort and memory during both training and inference, and do not account for model uncertainties.

2. **Bayesian Methods**: This approach models model parameters as random variables, allowing for stochastic predictions through sampling during inference. While it provides uncertainty quantification, it demands higher computational effort and memory, particularly during training and inference. It is less sensitive to initialization compared to deterministic networks.

3. **Ensemble Methods**: These methods involve training multiple networks and aggregating their predictions to quantify uncertainty. The process is computationally intensive, requiring significant resources during both training and inference due to the need to evaluate multiple models.

4. **Test-Time Data Augmentation**: This method enhances predictions by using augmented versions of input samples, performing several forward passes for each augmented input. It also requires considerable computational resources but is executed with a single model.

Overall, each method has distinct advantages and limitations concerning uncertainty modeling, computational demands, and memory usage, which are crucial for selecting the appropriate approach based on specific application needs.
