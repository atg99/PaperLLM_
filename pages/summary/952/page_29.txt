The paper discusses recent advancements in uncertainty estimation in deep neural networks, focusing on various methods and their theoretical foundations. It highlights the development of stochastic gradient Markov Chain Monte Carlo (SG-MCMC) techniques, which are utilized to enhance computing capabilities and memory efficiency in predictive uncertainty tasks. The authors reference significant contributions that demonstrate the convergence properties of SG-MCMC methods, including the SGLD algorithm and the application of higher-order integrators for improved accuracy.

Additionally, the Laplace Approximation is examined as a method to estimate the posterior distribution of neural network parameters. This approach involves a second-order Taylor series expansion around the maximum a posteriori (MAP) estimate, resulting in a Gaussian approximation of the weight posterior. The paper emphasizes that the Laplace Approximation can be applied to already trained networks and is compatible with standard loss functions, making it a versatile tool for modeling uncertainty.

Overall, the paper underscores the theoretical and practical advancements in SG-MCMC methods and the Laplace Approximation, contributing to the understanding and quantification of uncertainty in deep learning models.
