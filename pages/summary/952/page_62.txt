The paper discusses the significance of explainable artificial intelligence, particularly focusing on the importance of explainable uncertainty estimations in deep neural networks (DNNs). Such estimations enhance the understanding of decision-making processes in DNNs, which is crucial for their deployment in safety-critical applications that require risk aversion. The authors emphasize the potential of integrating domain-specific expert knowledge, often represented through mathematical or physical models, to improve the explainability of DNNs. This integration can be particularly beneficial in fields like earth system science, where physically guided models can incorporate explicit knowledge and practical uncertainty representations.

The work is partly funded by the German Federal Ministry of Education and Research under the AI4EO initiative, aiming to advance artificial intelligence for earth observation. The paper does not use concrete data but references publicly available datasets relevant to various problem types. The references cited provide a broader context of uncertainty quantification in deep learning and its applications.
