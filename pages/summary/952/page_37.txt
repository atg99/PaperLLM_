This paper discusses various methods for estimating uncertainty in deep neural networks, particularly focusing on ensemble approaches and test-time augmentation techniques. It highlights that while ensemble methods have been primarily evaluated based on predictive accuracy, there is a lack of attention given to how they handle predictive uncertainty. The authors argue for the need to explore strategies that encourage diversity in predictions among ensemble members to reduce overconfidence.

Test-time augmentation is presented as a straightforward method for predictive uncertainty estimation, which involves creating multiple variations of test samples through data augmentation techniques. This approach allows for the exploration of different views of the data, aiding in uncertainty measurement. The paper notes that this technique has been particularly beneficial in medical image processing, where data augmentation is commonly utilized. It emphasizes the need for valid augmentations that do not produce data outside the target distribution, as improper augmentations can lead to incorrect predictions.

The paper also references research by Shanmugam et al. and Lyzhov et al., who proposed methods to refine test-time augmentation by learning functions to aggregate predictions from augmented samples. Overall, test-time augmentation is portrayed as an accessible method for uncertainty estimation that requires no additional data and can be easily implemented. However, careful consideration of the factors influencing prediction accuracy is essential to optimize its effectiveness.
