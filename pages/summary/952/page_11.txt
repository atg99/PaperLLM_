The paper discusses different types of uncertainty in deep neural networks, specifically focusing on distributional uncertainty, in-domain uncertainty, and domain-shift uncertainty. 

Distributional uncertainty pertains to the uncertainty in the output of the network, which can be modeled using distributions such as the Dirichlet distribution for classification tasks. This type of uncertainty arises from changes in the input data distribution. It is interlinked with model uncertainty, which is related to the processes involved in building and training the neural network. The paper suggests that while many methods primarily distinguish between model and data uncertainty, specialized approaches for out-of-distribution detection explicitly represent distributional uncertainty.

Uncertainty classification is organized into three main categories based on input data domain. In-domain uncertainty occurs when the input data is from the same distribution as the training data but still presents challenges for the model, often due to design flaws or problem complexity. This uncertainty can potentially be mitigated by improving training data quality or the training process itself. 

Domain-shift uncertainty arises when inputs are drawn from a shifted distribution compared to the training data, leading to increased uncertainty due to the model's inability to generalize from seen samples. Some aspects of this uncertainty, such as occluded samples, can be modeled and reduced, although completely addressing all causes of domain shift uncertainty is complex.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of understanding and quantifying these different types of uncertainty to improve the reliability and performance of deep neural networks.
