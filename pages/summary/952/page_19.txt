The paper discusses the concept of uncertainty in deep neural networks, specifically focusing on the use of Dirichlet distributions to model class probabilities. It highlights that a higher concentration value in the Dirichlet distribution results in sharper predictions, which can be visualized in a probability simplex representing different class probabilities. The authors, Malinin and Gales, argue that high model uncertainty corresponds to a flat distribution across the simplex, indicating that the network lacks familiarity with the data. Conversely, data uncertainty is characterized by a sharper, centered distribution, suggesting the network is familiar with the data but lacks a clear class preference.

The Dirichlet distribution is employed in various frameworks, including Dirichlet Prior Networks and Evidential Neural Networks. The paper illustrates these concepts with figures showing predictions from a LeNet network trained on MNIST data, emphasizing the issue of overconfidence in incorrect classifications when data is presented in unfamiliar formats. Overall, the work underscores the importance of distinguishing between model uncertainty and data uncertainty in neural networks.
