The paper discusses various approaches to ensemble methods in neural networks, highlighting sub-ensembles and batch-ensembles as strategies to optimize computational efficiency and memory usage. Sub-ensembles divide a neural network into a trunk network for general information extraction and a task network for specific tasks, allowing for independent training of task networks while sharing a common trunk. This method reduces computational requirements significantly. Conversely, batch-ensembles connect member networks at every layer, using shared weight matrices, but require parallel training of all members, which can still be optimized similarly to mini-batch processing.

The authors emphasize that ensemble methods are straightforward to implement and allow for parallel training, but they face challenges in maintaining diversity among ensemble members. Techniques such as random initialization, data shuffling, and augmentation are suggested to enhance diversity without substantial additional effort. However, the linear increase in memory and computation with additional members poses limitations for ensemble deployment. Overall, while ensemble methods offer benefits like improved accuracy and uncertainty quantification, their scalability is constrained by resource demands.
