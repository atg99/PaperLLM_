The paper discusses methods for measuring data and model uncertainty in classification tasks. In a classification scenario with K classes, the output is a probability vector p(x), which assigns probabilities to each class, allowing for uncertainty estimates. The prediction p can be evaluated through the maximal class probability or entropy measures, where the maximal probability indicates certainty and entropy reflects the average information level of the prediction. However, a single prediction does not reveal the extent of model uncertainty affecting it.

The authors emphasize that traditional softmax predictions are often poorly calibrated and insufficient for reliable uncertainty quantification. To improve estimates, they suggest using a posterior distribution on model parameters, which transforms the softmax output into a random variable, allowing for the assessment of its variation and uncertainty. Key measures for evaluating model uncertainty include mutual information (MI), expected Kullback-Leibler Divergence (EKL), and predictive variance, all of which compare the stochastic softmax output with its expected value.

Mutual information specifically assesses the dependence between the expected outputs, highlighting the difference in information contained in the predicted versus expected softmax outputs. This approach enables a more nuanced understanding of both data and model uncertainty in classification tasks.
