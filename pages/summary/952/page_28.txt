The paper discusses the Hamiltonian Monte Carlo (HMC) method, a prominent variant of Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) sampling, which is widely recognized as a gold standard for Bayesian inference. The HMC algorithm operates by initializing parameters and then iteratively updating them using Hamiltonian dynamics, with potential and kinetic energy defined in terms of the parameter distribution. A leapfrog integrator is employed to solve the dynamics, and a Metropolis acceptance criterion is used to accept or reject samples.

However, the computational cost of processing entire datasets per iteration in HMC becomes prohibitive as dataset sizes increase. To address this, recent research has focused on scalable alternatives, notably combining Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD) with Langevin dynamics, leading to the development of Stochastic Gradient Markov Chain Monte Carlo (SG-MCMC). This approach allows for Bayesian inference on deep neural networks to be performed similarly to noisy SGD.

The paper also outlines various extensions of SG-MCMC that utilize second-order information, including preconditioning with the Fisher Information Matrix and adapting diagonal matrices. These methods aim to mitigate the stochastic perturbations in gradient estimation by employing parameter-dependent diffusion matrices, inspired by thermostat concepts to maintain a constant temperature distribution in the noise. Overall, the advancements in SG-MCMC have opened new research avenues for efficient Bayesian inference in large-scale datasets.
