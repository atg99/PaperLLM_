The paper discusses various techniques for measuring uncertainty in pixel-wise segmentation and structure estimation, highlighting methods such as confidence intervals, predictive variance, predictive entropy, and mutual information. The authors emphasize that the quality of volume uncertainties can be assessed through metrics like the coefficient of variation, average Dice score, and intersection over union, which evaluate the overlap agreement between multiple estimates. They argue that ideally, a false segmentation should increase both pixel-wise and structural uncertainty. 

The paper also addresses the importance of calibration in deep neural networks (DNNs), defining a well-calibrated predictor as one where the predictive confidence closely aligns with the actual probability of correctness. The authors outline formal definitions for calibration in classification and regression tasks, noting that under-confident DNNs fail to meet these criteria. They suggest that calibration errors often stem from model uncertainty, and achieving correct data uncertainty is crucial for a well-calibrated network. The paper concludes by noting that deeper networks are often more prone to calibration issues.
