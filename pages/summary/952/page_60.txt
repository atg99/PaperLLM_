The paper discusses significant challenges in the field of uncertainty quantification methods. A major issue is the lack of a standardized protocol for evaluating these methods, which complicates the identification of state-of-the-art techniques across various domains. This lack of standardization hinders the comparison of approaches and limits their acceptance and adoption.

Another critical challenge is the inability to assess uncertainty associated with individual decisions. Existing evaluation measures rely on entire testing datasets, which can bias the assessment of uncertainty for specific samples. For practical applications, especially in mission- and safety-critical scenarios, pointwise evaluation measures are essential for understanding the reliability of predicted confidence.

The paper also highlights the absence of ground truth uncertainties for validation purposes. Current methods are often empirically evaluated without a reliable benchmark, making it difficult to generalize results across different datasets due to potential shifts in data distribution. The use of synthetic data for training models further complicates this issue, as it can introduce artificial uncertainties that do not reflect real-world scenarios.

Lastly, the paper addresses the explainability issue within neural network uncertainty quantification methods. Although these methods provide certainty predictions, they lack transparency regarding the underlying causes of uncertainties. This obscurity complicates the transfer of methods between datasets and poses challenges for their application in safety-critical real-life situations. Understanding the mechanisms behind these methods is crucial for refining them and effectively characterizing uncertainty and potential failures.
