The paper discusses the concepts of uncertainty in deep neural networks (DNNs), specifically focusing on model uncertainty and data uncertainty. Model uncertainty encompasses in-domain, domain-shift, and out-of-domain uncertainties. Domain-shift uncertainty arises from external factors that can be mitigated by including shifted domains in the training dataset. In contrast, out-of-domain uncertainty occurs when the input data differs significantly from the training distribution, limiting the DNN's ability to make accurate predictions.

The authors highlight that model uncertainty reflects the DNN's lack of knowledge about both in-domain and out-of-domain data, while data uncertainty is linked to the inherent characteristics of the training data, such as overlapping samples and label noise. 

The paper emphasizes the challenges of completely eliminating uncertainties in practical applications due to the inherent mismatch between the DNN's training data and real-world input data. Consequently, accurately representing uncertainty in DNN predictions remains a complex task, leading to ongoing research in this area. Various methods for uncertainty estimation are categorized based on the number of DNNs used (single or multiple) and their nature (deterministic or stochastic). Single deterministic methods provide predictions through one forward pass in a deterministic network, with uncertainty quantified either externally or directly by the network.
