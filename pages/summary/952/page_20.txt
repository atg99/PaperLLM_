The paper discusses various approaches to improving the differentiation between in-distribution and out-of-distribution (OOD) samples in machine learning models, particularly through the use of Dirichlet distributions. Prior networks, as introduced by Malinin and Gales, are trained to minimize the Kullback–Leibler (KL) divergence between predictions for in-distribution data and a sharp Dirichlet distribution, while also addressing predictions for OOD data. This method enhances the separation of confidence levels for correct and incorrect predictions.

Subsequent research has built on this foundation, with Malinin and Gales reformulating the KL divergence loss to mitigate issues arising from high data uncertainty. Tsiligkaridis extended the Dirichlet network approach by introducing a new loss function aimed at minimizing an expected worst-case upper bound on errors. Wu et al. proposed a mixture of Dirichlet distributions for greater flexibility in approximating posterior distributions.

Nandy et al. highlighted that for in-domain samples with high uncertainty, the predicted Dirichlet distribution for incorrect predictions is often flatter, complicating the distinction between in-distribution and OOD predictions. They suggested a regularization term to enhance this differentiation. Evidential neural networks, introduced by Sensoy et al., optimize a single Dirichlet network's parameterization, incorporating subjective logic to interpret logits as beliefs and providing an additional "I don’t know" class.

Further advancements include Zhao et al.'s differentiation between vacuity and dissonance in evidence and Amini et al.'s adaptation of evidential neural networks to regression tasks. Charpentier et al. utilized normalizing flows for class-wise distribution learning, eliminating the need for OOD data during training. Additionally, Liang et al. proposed a simple method involving input perturbations and temperature scaling for effective differentiation between in- and out-of-distribution samples.
