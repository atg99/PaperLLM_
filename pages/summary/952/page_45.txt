The paper discusses uncertainty in deep neural networks (DNNs) and highlights the tendency of deeper networks to be more overconfident than shallower ones. It emphasizes the importance of uncertainty estimation methods that improve network calibration by quantifying model and data uncertainty separately. While some methods focus on reducing model uncertainty, a significant body of research has explored techniques to explicitly address calibration errors without reducing model uncertainty. 

An example illustrates the issue: a binary classifier that is overfitted may predict all test samples as one class with high probability, leading to unreliable confidence levels. Recalibration methods may adjust these outputs to a more moderate probability that reflects model uncertainty rather than true data uncertainty.

Calibration methods are categorized into three main groups based on their application timing: 

1. **Regularization methods**: These are applied during training to create inherently calibrated DNNs by modifying the training objective, optimization, or regularization techniques.
2. **Post-processing methods**: These are utilized after training, requiring a separate calibration dataset to adjust prediction scores, relying on the assumption that the distribution of this dataset aligns with the model's predictions.

The paper also includes reliability diagrams to illustrate different calibration scenarios: overconfident classifiers, underconfident classifiers, and well-calibrated classifiers. These diagrams visually represent the relationship between predicted confidence and actual accuracy, underscoring the significance of effective calibration in DNNs.
