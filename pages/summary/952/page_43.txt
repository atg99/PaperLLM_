The paper discusses uncertainty in deep neural networks, focusing on how to quantify this uncertainty in various predictive tasks, including regression and segmentation. It highlights two primary approaches for measuring uncertainty: predicting a mean vector and standard deviation for normally distributed uncertainty, and directly predicting prediction intervals (PIs). The first approach allows for an analytical description of the true value's location within a specific region, using quantiles to interpret predictions as expected values from a distribution. 

In contrast, the second approach defines PIs as ranges within which predictions are expected to fall, with the Mean Prediction Interval Width (MPIW) measuring average certainty. The Prediction Interval Coverage Probability (PICP) evaluates the accuracy of these intervals by calculating the proportion of actual values captured within the predicted intervals.

The paper notes that model uncertainty arises from the architecture, training process, and underrepresented data, affecting both regression and classification tasks similarly. For segmentation tasks, uncertainty evaluation mirrors that of classification, utilizing Bayesian inference techniques. Overall, the study provides insights into effectively measuring and evaluating uncertainty in deep learning models across different applications.
