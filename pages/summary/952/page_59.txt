This paper surveys the challenges and advancements in uncertainty quantification within deep neural networks (DNNs), particularly focusing on applications involving Earth Observation (EO) data. The authors highlight the complexities of integrating data from diverse sensors that vary in spatial, temporal, and semantic properties, leading to uncertainty in predictions. A significant challenge is developing methods that can accurately quantify uncertainties while assessing the contribution from individual data sources.

The paper emphasizes the unique challenges posed by EO satellites, which operate at considerable distances from subjects, introducing various uncertainties due to sensor sensitivity, atmospheric conditions, and surface reflectance. It underscores the necessity of incorporating physical knowledge of EO systems into uncertainty models, as measuring these uncertainties is crucial for applications that rely on assimilating geo-variables into process models.

In the conclusion, the authors evaluate the effectiveness of current uncertainty quantification methods in real-world applications. Despite significant advancements, the practical adoption of these methods in mission-critical scenarios remains limited. Key issues include:

1. **Lack of validation**: Many existing DNN models fail to adequately quantify uncertainty in real-world contexts, primarily because they are trained on standard datasets that do not reflect the complexities of environments such as low-resolution satellite imagery.

2. **Absence of standardized evaluation protocols**: Current evaluation methods for uncertainty estimation are often tailored to specific measurable outcomes, lacking a comprehensive framework for assessing these methods across diverse real-world applications.

The authors call for structured evaluations and standardized protocols to enhance the applicability of uncertainty quantification techniques in practical scenarios, paving the way for more reliable and robust DNN applications in critical fields.
