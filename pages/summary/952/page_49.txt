The section discusses post-processing methods for improving the calibration of deep neural networks after training. These methods utilize a held-out subset of training data as a calibration set to learn a re-calibration function that adjusts the network's outputs, such as the logit vector. Zhang et al. (2020) identify three essential requirements for effective post-hoc calibration methods: they must preserve accuracy, be data efficient, and be capable of approximating the correct re-calibration map with sufficient data. However, they note that existing approaches typically fail to meet all three criteria.

Temperature scaling, introduced by Guo et al. (2017), is highlighted as a basic yet effective post-hoc calibration method for classification tasks. This technique optimizes the temperature parameter of the softmax function to adjust the predicted confidence levels. While temperature scaling maintains data efficiency and accuracy, its expressiveness is limited. To enhance calibration, Zhang et al. (2020) proposed using an ensemble of temperature scaling models, which led to better-calibrated predictions without sacrificing accuracy or data efficiency.

Kull et al. (2019) drew inspiration from non-neural network calibration methods, employing a class-wise one-vs-all binary calibration approach. This method interprets the calibration process as a linear transformation of predicted log-likelihoods, making it straightforward to implement, though it may not preserve original predictions.

For regression networks, Levi et al. (2022) introduced standard deviation scaling (std-scaling), which resizes the predicted standard deviations. This method can be trained using the Gaussian log-likelihood function, aligning with the training objectives of regression networks that also aim to predict data uncertainty.
