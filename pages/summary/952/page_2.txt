Gawlikowski et al. discuss the significant advancements in deep neural networks (DNNs) over the past decade and their application in various fields, particularly in high-risk areas like medical image analysis and autonomous vehicle control. However, deployment in mission-critical applications is hindered by several limitations: the lack of expressiveness and transparency in DNN inference models, difficulties in distinguishing between in-domain and out-of-domain samples, unreliable uncertainty estimates, and vulnerability to adversarial attacks. These issues stem from inherent data uncertainty and model uncertainty. 

To address these challenges, the authors emphasize the necessity of providing uncertainty estimates, which can help in making informed decisions, especially in high-risk fields and situations where data is scarce. Recent research has focused on estimating uncertainty in DNNs, distinguishing between epistemic (model) uncertainty and aleatoric (data) uncertainty. Key approaches for modeling this separation include Bayesian inference, ensemble methods, test-time augmentation, and deterministic networks designed to capture both model and data uncertainty. These methods are crucial for improving the reliability and safety of DNN applications across various domains.
