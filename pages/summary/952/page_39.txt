The paper discusses the evaluation of uncertainty in deep neural networks, highlighting the effectiveness of ensemble methods and Bayesian approaches. While multi-mode evaluation through ensembles is beneficial for real-world applications, Bayesian methods offer strong theoretical foundations. The combination of ensemble strategies with Bayesian approaches is suggested to enhance model parameter variability while accommodating multiple prediction modes. Single deterministic methods, such as prior networks, yield comparable results with lower computational costs; however, they require distinct in- and out-of-distribution samples for effective training. 

The paper emphasizes the importance of developing new problem and loss formulations to deepen understanding of uncertainty in neural networks. Various methods for modeling and predicting uncertainty are presented, alongside challenges in evaluating the quality of these uncertainty estimates. Key challenges include the dependency of uncertainty quality on the estimation method, the absence of ground truth uncertainty estimates, and the lack of a unified quantitative evaluation metric across different machine learning tasks. 

In classification tasks, while the softmax output provides a measure of confidence, it is noted that this output is often unreliable and does not capture all sources of uncertainty, necessitating the development of additional approaches and evaluation measures.
