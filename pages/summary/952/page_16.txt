The paper discusses single deterministic methods for uncertainty quantification in neural networks, emphasizing that these networks produce consistent outputs for each forward pass due to their deterministic nature. It categorizes the methods into two main types: internal and external uncertainty quantification approaches. 

Internal approaches involve modeling and training a single network specifically to quantify uncertainties in predictions. These methods often utilize loss functions that minimize the divergence between the true and predicted distributions, resulting in a representation of model uncertainty. Examples include works by Sensoy et al. and Malinin and Gales.

In contrast, external approaches apply to already trained networks and utilize additional components to estimate uncertainty without altering the network's predictions. This distinction highlights the impact of the uncertainty quantification on the training process and the resulting predictions.

The paper also illustrates the basic principles of uncertainty modeling across four types of neural network approaches, indicating how each method delivers predictions alongside representations of model and data uncertainty. Overall, the focus is on the methodologies for integrating uncertainty quantification into deterministic neural networks.
