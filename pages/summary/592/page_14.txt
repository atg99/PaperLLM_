This section discusses the application of unsupervised domain adaptation (UDA) in image analysis, which is a prominent area due to the availability of extensive visual databases. Key tasks in this domain include image classification, object detection, semantic segmentation, image generation, and image captioning.

In image classification, various methods have been employed, particularly utilizing deep learning techniques. For example, Long et al. utilize an AlexNet backbone and compare their results against several other methods including DANN and MMD variations. Zellinger et al. assess their CMD methods against other discrepancy-based approaches. Face recognition is another significant area of focus, addressing variations in pose, illumination, expression, age, ethnicity, and imaging modality. Notable techniques include a bi-shifting auto-encoder framework proposed by Kan et al. for handling domain shifts in view, ethnicity, and sensor, and adversarial UDA methods for video face recognition by Sohn et al.

Several benchmarks are commonly used for testing domain shifts in natural images, such as the Office-31 dataset, which contains images from Amazon, DSLR, and Webcam. For image synthesis, the VisDA17 dataset is preferred, while DomainNet is noted as the largest dataset available for domain adaptation.

Moreover, the paper highlights the relevance of UDA in tasks that involve ordinal class labels, particularly in medical diagnosis, where labels may be discrete but follow a sequential distribution.
