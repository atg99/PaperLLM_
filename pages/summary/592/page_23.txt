the development of foundation models has influenced the landscape of unsupervised domain adaptation (UDA). These models leverage vast amounts of labeled data to capture diverse representations, which can be beneficial for adapting to new domains. The integration of foundation models with UDA can enhance the adaptation process, particularly in scenarios where labeled data is scarce.

Recent advances highlight the importance of source-free UDA methods that do not require access to the source domain data, addressing concerns related to data leakage and intellectual property. Techniques such as white-box and black-box domain adaptation are being explored. White-box methods utilize pre-trained models and adjust batch normalization statistics for the target domain, while black-box approaches enable adaptation without prior knowledge of network weights.

Additionally, the concept of test time adaptation has emerged, recognizing the need for models to adapt to single or few test samples that deviate from the source domain. This approach emphasizes low-cost training on mobile devices and aims to prevent catastrophic forgetting, thereby supporting continuous adaptation in dynamically changing environments.

Overall, the review underscores the potential for UDA to evolve under stricter data-sharing conditions, leveraging model sharing akin to federated learning. Furthermore, it advocates for the development of continuous and progressively changing UDA datasets, as well as the use of interpolation techniques to bridge gaps between disparate domains. The foundation model era presents new opportunities for UDA, enabling more effective adaptation strategies through the use of extensive and varied labeled datasets.
