Mancini et al. propose domain-specific classifiers using multiple independent models, while Ding and Fu emphasize matching low-rank structures of features. Liu et al. further refine this by aligning conditional distributions through variational inference. The challenge of achieving effective test-time adaptation for unseen domains is highlighted, particularly with label shifts that can be corrected during implementation.

Out-of-distribution (OOD) detection has gained traction as a critical area of research, especially when domain shifts hinder reliable adaptation. The paper outlines that while OOD detection in low-dimensional spaces is well-established, high-dimensional data, such as images, presents significant challenges. Hendrycks and Gimpel note that deep neural networks typically show higher maximum softmax outputs for in-distribution (ID) examples compared to OOD samples. Several proposed methods aim to enhance detection efficacy, such as incorporating both ID and OOD samples during training and employing adversarial perturbations to amplify differences in softmax outputs.

Other innovative approaches include augmenting classifiers with confidence estimation mechanisms, using generative adversarial networks (GANs) for training, and dynamically labeling training data as OOD to improve classification. However, many of these methods require retraining classifiers with altered structures or objectives. The paper concludes by emphasizing the ongoing challenge of developing robust density estimators for OOD detection on complex datasets, making it a vital area for future exploration.
