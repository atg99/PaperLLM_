This paper provides a comprehensive review of recent advancements in Unsupervised Domain Adaptation (UDA), highlighting various methodologies and their underlying principles. It discusses the use of Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) as a critical technique for aligning feature distributions between domains. MMD can be minimized between classifier outputs, leading to the development of enhanced methods such as Multiple Kernel MMD (MK-MMD) and Joint MMD (JMMD), which improve MMD's robustness.

Additionally, the paper introduces CORAL, which focuses on aligning the second-order statistics or covariances of features across domains. Various distance measures, including squared matrix Frobenius norm and log-Euclidean distances, are explored to assess covariance differences. The paper notes that CORAL has been adapted for infinite-dimensional covariance matrices in Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Space (RKHS).

Furthermore, the paper addresses class-conditioned distribution alignment through Class Distribution Divergence (CDD), which integrates class labels into MMD to manage cross-class divergence while minimizing within-class divergence. Given the challenge of missing labels in the target domain during UDA, the paper also presents Contrastive Adaptation Networks (CAN), which estimate target domain labels via clustering while minimizing CDD. Overall, the review encapsulates key methodologies and their implications for enhancing UDA performance.
