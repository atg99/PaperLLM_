The methodology section outlines the recent developments in UDA methods, driven by advancements in neural networks. Key approaches include domain alignment using statistical divergence, adversarial training, generative domain mapping, normalization statistics alignment, ensemble-based methods, and self-training. These methods can be combined to improve performance across different tasks.

A significant focus is placed on statistical divergence alignment, which aims to learn domain-invariant feature representations by minimizing discrepancies in a latent feature space. The selection of an appropriate divergence measure is crucial, with commonly used measures including Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD), Correlation Alignment (CORAL), Contrastive Domain Discrepancy (CDD), Wasserstein distance, and graph matching loss. MMD, in particular, is highlighted for its ability to compare distributions between two domains by assessing the mean of a smooth function derived from observed samples. A larger mean difference indicates greater domain discrepancy, with the methodology relying on the characteristics of reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces for analysis and comparison of distributions.
