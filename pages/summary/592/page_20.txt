The paper discusses various unsupervised domain adaptation (UDA) strategies applied to time series data analysis, particularly in the medical field. It highlights the use of UDA for tasks such as sleep classification, arrhythmia classification, and motor imagery, emphasizing the need to address distribution discrepancies across datasets and demographic features. For instance, Yoo et al. employed adversarial and self-training techniques with multiple domain discriminators to maintain local structures in sleep stages.

Additionally, the paper explores the application of UDA in emotion recognition, speech recognition, and industrial time series data analysis, including human action recognition and anomaly detection. It notes the development of models like recurrent domain adversarial neural networks and variational recurrent adversarial deep domain adaptation to better capture temporal dependencies in time series data. Other models, such as the sparse associative structure alignment and convolutional deep domain adaptation models, are also introduced to enhance UDA performance in time series contexts.
