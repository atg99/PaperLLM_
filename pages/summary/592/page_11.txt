The paper reviews recent advances in deep unsupervised domain adaptation (UDA), focusing on self-training as a key approach. Self-training utilizes unlabeled target domain data to create pseudo-labels, which are then used to retrain the network. This method has shown superior performance compared to traditional adversarial training methods in various deep UDA tasks. 

The self-training process consists of two main steps: generating pseudo-labels and retraining the model with these labels. A significant challenge is the potential noise in pseudo-labels, which can lead to unreliable predictions. To address this, it is crucial to select predictions with high confidence, often determined by the maximum value in the softmax output. Techniques such as smoothing hard labels to soft label vectors and incorporating additional supervision signals have been proposed to improve the reliability of pseudo-labels.

Moreover, self-training has been extended beyond classification and segmentation tasks to generative tasks, utilizing Bayesian uncertainty masks to control confident pseudo-labels. Overall, the paper emphasizes the flexibility and effectiveness of self-training in deep UDA while also highlighting ongoing challenges related to label noise.
