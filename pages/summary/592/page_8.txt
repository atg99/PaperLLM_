The paper discusses advanced methods for measuring distribution divergence in the context of domain adaptation (UDA). It highlights the Wasserstein distance, also known as optimal transportation distance, as a promising alternative for quantifying divergence between feature distributions. The authors introduce Joint Distribution Optimal Transport (JDOT) and its deep learning variant, DeepJDOT, which facilitate end-to-end UDA.

Additionally, the paper presents graph matching as a method for assessing divergence by establishing optimal correspondences between graphs, where nodes represent features and the distance between nodes indicates similarity. The divergence is quantified as the matching cost between source and target domain graphs.

The section on adversarial learning emphasizes a shift from predefined divergence measures, such as Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD), to dynamically learning a divergence measure through adversarial training. It references recent advancements in Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) as a foundation for minimizing domain divergence at the feature level with the aid of a discriminator. The paper outlines a framework where a feature extractor is optimized to align feature distributions from both source and target domains, supported by a classifier and a domain discriminator. The optimization objectives are articulated through a series of equations aimed at balancing the loss terms for effective training of the classifier, discriminator, and feature extractor, ensuring that the feature representations from different domains converge towards a common distribution.
