This paper evaluates various state-of-the-art universal style transfer algorithms, specifically focusing on their effectiveness and computational efficiency. The analysis includes a comparison of methods such as StyleSwap, AdaIN, WCT, and ArtFlow, among others, with results presented in both visual and quantitative formats.

In the quantitative evaluation, several metrics are assessed, including SSIM, Content Loss, Gram Loss, and computation time for 512x512 images. Notably, ArtFlow combined with AdaIN demonstrates a lower Gram loss than vanilla AdaIN, indicating improved performance in maintaining content integrity during style transfer. While ArtFlow+WCT exhibits similar Gram loss compared to WCT itself, it is noted that ArtFlow effectively mitigates the Content Leak issue without compromising stylization capabilities.

The paper discusses the phenomenon of Content Leak, where repeated style transfers can lead to the degradation of content information. It is shown that traditional methods like AdaIN and WCT are susceptible to this issue, while ArtFlow successfully prevents Content Leak when integrated with these methods. The proposed architecture, utilizing a PFN instead of a VGG19-based auto-encoder, indicates a significant improvement in preserving content across multiple style transfer iterations.

Overall, the findings suggest that ArtFlow enhances the performance of existing style transfer algorithms by addressing content bias and improving computational efficiency, thereby contributing valuable insights to the field of style transfer in computer vision.
