The paper discusses the phenomenon of Content Leak in style transfer algorithms, particularly focusing on AdaIN, WCT, and Avatar-Net. Content Leak refers to the loss of content information during the stylization process, leading to a degradation of the recognizable details of the input content image over multiple rounds of style transfer. 

The authors provide empirical evidence through experiments demonstrating that repeated style transfers result in images that bear little resemblance to the original content. They identify two primary causes of Content Leak: 

1. **Reconstruction Error**: The decoders of existing algorithms are unable to achieve lossless reconstruction of the original images due to spatial information loss from pooling operations in the encoder. This accumulated error disrupts content details.

2. **Biased Decoder Training**: The training of decoders in these algorithms is biased due to the weighted combination of content and style losses. For instance, in AdaIN, the emphasis on style loss leads to a focus on producing artistic effects rather than accurate reconstructions, further contributing to Content Leak.

Additionally, the paper discusses the role of biased style transfer modules, exemplified by the Style Decorator in Avatar-Net, which irreversibly alters content features, thus exacerbating the Content Leak issue.

The authors summarize these causes, highlighting the interplay between reconstruction errors and biased training methods as significant contributors to the Content Leak phenomenon in style transfer algorithms.
