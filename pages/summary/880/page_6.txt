The paper discusses the ArtFlow framework for unbiased style transfer, highlighting the effectiveness of its transfer modules, AdaIN and WCT. It asserts that ArtFlow achieves lossless content and style reconstruction, as evidenced by experiments comparing its performance with state-of-the-art algorithms. The framework includes theoretical and quantitative analyses that establish AdaIN and WCT as two viable options for style transfer within ArtFlow.

The experiments utilized the MS-COCO dataset for content images and the WikiArt dataset for styles, with images resized and cropped for training. The proposed network architecture comprises two flow blocks with eight neural flows each, and ArtFlow was trained using the Adam optimizer for 60,000 iterations. The training focused on minimizing content and style losses.

Results showed that ArtFlow, when combined with AdaIN/WCT, produces superior style transfer outcomes compared to other contemporary methods like StyleSwap and Avatar-Net, particularly in preserving content details and avoiding content leak. Quantitative metrics, such as SSIM and Gram matrix comparisons, further confirmed ArtFlow's ability to maintain content integrity while achieving artistic effects, marking it as a significant advancement in the field of style transfer. The paper also introduces a novel application of reverse style transfer, unique to ArtFlow, which broadens its practical utility.
