The paper presents a novel style transfer framework named ArtFlow, which employs a Projection Flow Network (PFN) based on neural flows to achieve unbiased and lossless image recovery during style transfer. The ArtFlow framework differentiates itself from traditional encoder-decoder models by utilizing both forward and backward inferences in a projection-transfer-reversion pipeline. This method enhances the feature extraction process while avoiding common issues like image reconstruction errors and biases often seen in existing algorithms.

Key contributions of the work include the identification of the Content Leak issue in popular style transfer algorithms such as AdaIN, WCT, and Avatar-Net, alongside a detailed analysis of their inherent biases. The authors propose the PFN, which allows for fair comparisons of various style transfer modules, revealing that AdaIN and WCT are unbiased, while Avatar-Net exhibits a bias towards style.

ArtFlow utilizes the unbiased transfer modules from AdaIN and WCT, demonstrating its ability to produce comparable results to state-of-the-art methods without the Content Leak problem. The paper also situates ArtFlow within the broader context of image style transfer, tracing its evolution from early methods to the current landscape dominated by deep neural networks.

Overall, ArtFlow represents a significant advancement in the field of style transfer by addressing critical shortcomings of previous models, thereby enhancing the quality and reliability of artistic style transfer techniques.
