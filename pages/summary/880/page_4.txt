The paper introduces ArtFlow, a novel unbiased style transfer framework designed to address the Content Leak issue prevalent in existing style transfer algorithms. Traditional methods typically utilize an encoder-transfer-decoder scheme, which can lead to biased image outputs. ArtFlow innovates by employing a projection-transfer-reversion scheme facilitated by a reversible neural flow model called Projection Flow Network (PFN).

In the ArtFlow framework, the process begins with the projection step, where both content and style images are input into PFN for lossless deep feature extraction. The subsequent transfer step involves applying an unbiased style transfer module, which combines the extracted features to create a stylized feature. Finally, the reversion step uses reverse propagation through PFN to reconstruct the stylized image from the stylized feature. The architecture ensures that both the information flow during the forward and reverse processes is lossless and unbiased, effectively preventing content leakage.

The PFN component of ArtFlow is constructed from three reversible transformations: additive coupling, invertible 1x1 convolution, and Actnorm. This design allows for the maintenance of information integrity throughout the style transfer process. The paper further discusses the theoretical and quantitative analyses of existing transfer modules, highlighting their inherent biases and justifying the choice of the unbiased style transfer module employed in ArtFlow.

Overall, ArtFlow represents a significant advancement in style transfer technology, promising improved output quality without the biases that have affected previous methods.
