The paper discusses the methodology and experiments conducted for voice conversion (VC) using various approaches. The study utilizes the CMU ARCTIC database, which contains recordings from four English speakers, and organizes the data into training, validation, and test sets. For training the speaker encoder, datasets such as LibriSpeech, VoxCeleb1, and VoxCeleb2 are employed, enabling generalization to unseen speakers. The synthesis models are trained using LibriTTS and VCTK corpus, focusing on any-to-any voice conversion.

Key features used in the study include 80-dimensional log mel-spectrograms for speech signals, with variations in dimensionality for input to the speaker encoder. The WaveRNN network is chosen as the neural vocoder, with training based on ground-truth mel-spectrograms from multiple speakers.

The paper compares four VC approaches, including the proposed Seq2seqPR-DurIAN and BNE-Seq2seqMoL, against two baseline methods: PPG-VC and NonParaSeq2seq-VC. The PPG-VC method employs an RNN-based architecture for PPG extraction and a multi-speaker conversion model. NonParaSeq2seq-VC utilizes disentangled content and speaker representations, allowing for flexibility in conversion settings.

Objective evaluations reveal that the BNE-Seq2seqMoL system's attention alignment matrices are nearly diagonal, indicating limited capacity for transforming duration and speaking style effectively. Overall, the study aims to advance the field of voice conversion by exploring and comparing different methodologies for enhanced speaker adaptation and synthesis quality.
