This paper presents a novel approach to any-to-many voice conversion (VC) using a location-relative sequence-to-sequence (seq2seq) modeling technique that leverages text supervision during training. The method integrates a bottleneck feature extractor (BNE) with a seq2seq synthesis module, allowing for the extraction of speaker-independent representations from spectral features through a hybrid connectionist-temporal-classification-attention (CTC-attention) phoneme recognizer. 

During training, the phoneme recognizer's encoder, which includes a bottleneck layer, is developed using a large speech recognition dataset. This enables the extraction of rich spoken content representations. A multi-speaker location-relative attention-based seq2seq synthesis model is then trained to reconstruct spectral features from the extracted bottleneck features, facilitating speaker identity control in the generated speech. To address the challenges of aligning long sequences in seq2seq models, the input spectral features are down-sampled temporally, and a discretized mixture of logistic (MoL) attention mechanism is utilized.

The proposed any-to-many VC approach demonstrates superior performance in both naturalness and speaker similarity through objective and subjective evaluations. Additionally, ablation studies confirm the effectiveness of the feature selection and model design strategies employed. The method is adaptable, allowing for extension to any-to-any VC scenarios, achieving high performance metrics. 

The paper emphasizes the significance of VC in applications such as entertainment, personalized text-to-speech, and accent correction, and positions its approach within the broader context of existing VC methodologies that traditionally require parallel training data.
