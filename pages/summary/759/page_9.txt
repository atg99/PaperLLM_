The paper presents a novel voice conversion approach called BNE-Seq2seqMoL, which utilizes bottle-neck features extracted through a Bottle-Neck Encoder (BNE) for enhanced performance in voice conversion tasks. The results indicate that BNE-Seq2seqMoL outperforms other methods such as PPG-VC, NonParaSeq2seq-VC, and Seq2seqPR-DurIAN across various objective metrics including F0-RMSE, CER, and WER. The findings suggest that frame-wise mapping in PPG-VC limits its ability to model prosody effectively, while the inclusion of a duration model in Seq2seqPR-DurIAN improves robustness.

In subjective evaluations, the BNE-Seq2seqMoL approach received the highest mean opinion scores for both naturalness and speaker similarity in an any-to-many voice conversion setting. Though it excelled in most conversion pairs, it struggled with male-to-female conversions due to limitations in modeling pitch transformation, as indicated by a high F0 RMSE. The study emphasizes the effectiveness of training with larger datasets to mitigate performance deficiencies, particularly in independent frame predictions seen in PPG-VC.

Overall, the proposed BNE-Seq2seqMoL method shows promising results in both objective and subjective evaluations, indicating its potential for robust voice conversion applications.
