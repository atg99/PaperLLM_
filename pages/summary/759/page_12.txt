The paper discusses various advancements in voice conversion technologies, particularly focusing on methods that utilize non-parallel data. It highlights significant contributions such as the use of variational autoencoders for voice conversion from non-parallel corpora and generative adversarial networks (GANs) for voice impersonation. Notable techniques include cycle-consistent adversarial networks which facilitate parallel-data-free voice conversion, as well as auxiliary classifier variational autoencoders for many-to-many voice conversion. The paper also mentions the importance of attention mechanisms and sequence-to-sequence models in speech synthesis tasks, showcasing models like Tacotron and FastSpeech for efficient text-to-speech conversion. Furthermore, it references various datasets and corpora that are essential for training and evaluating voice conversion systems, emphasizing the need for high-quality, diverse data to improve model performance. Overall, the paper emphasizes the ongoing evolution of voice conversion methodologies and their applications in real-world scenarios.
