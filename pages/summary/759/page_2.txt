This paper addresses the limitations of existing any-to-many voice conversion (VC) approaches that rely on phonetic posteriorgrams (PPGs). Traditional methods face challenges due to inaccurate phonetic alignments and the independent prediction of acoustic frames, which can lead to mispronunciations and suboptimal performance, particularly with sparse training data. 

To overcome these issues, the authors propose a novel methodology using a different content feature extractor derived from a hybrid connectionist-temporal-classification-attention (CTC-attention) phoneme recognizer. This approach employs a bottle-neck feature extractor (BNE) to provide spoken content representations. The paper introduces a sequence-to-sequence (seq2seq) model, termed BNE-Seq2seqMoL, which incorporates a mixture-of-logistic (MoL) attention mechanism to enhance synthesis quality. This model also simplifies the training process and reduces the system pipeline compared to existing methods.

Additionally, the authors explore extending the any-to-many VC approach to any-to-any conversion by integrating speaker embedding vectors that can accommodate unseen speakers. Key contributions include the introduction of a high-performing MoL attention-based seq2seq model and a straightforward methodology for expanding VC capabilities.

The paper is structured to first review related work, followed by a presentation of the baseline model and the BNE-Seq2seqMoL approach, before detailing experiments and concluding findings.
