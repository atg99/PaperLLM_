This paper presents advancements in voice conversion (VC) techniques, specifically focusing on a non-parallel, sequence-to-sequence (seq2seq) any-to-many VC approach called Seq2seqPR-DurIAN. The proposed method integrates a seq2seq phoneme recognizer (Seq2seqPR) and a multi-speaker duration-informed attention network (DurIAN) to enhance the synthesis quality of converted speech while correcting alignment errors through a local attention mechanism.

The Seq2seqPR-DurIAN approach is classified under recognition-synthesis VC methods, where an automatic speech recognition (ASR) module extracts spoken content representations, and a synthesis module predicts acoustic features based on these representations. Compared to existing methods, this approach simplifies the training process and incorporates a duration model to improve phoneme-level alignment, thereby mitigating potential errors in attention alignment.

The paper also discusses a related method, BNE-Seq2seqMoL, which utilizes pre-trained ASR and text-to-speech (TTS) models for initializing parameters of the VC model. However, the proposed BNE-Seq2seqMoL adopts a more straightforward two-stage training scheme, facilitating direct support for any-to-many voice conversion.

The Seq2seqPR model employs a hybrid connectionist temporal classification (CTC) and attention mechanism structure, where CTC serves as an auxiliary task to train the attention-based encoder. The model processes input spectral features and predicts phoneme sequences while utilizing a dynamic programming algorithm for efficient computation of posterior distributions.

In summary, the paper introduces innovative modifications to existing VC methods, emphasizing a streamlined training approach and improved synthesis quality through the integration of duration modeling, ultimately advancing the field of voice conversion technology.
