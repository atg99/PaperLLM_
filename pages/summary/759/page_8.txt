The paper presents a comprehensive evaluation of various voice conversion (VC) approaches, specifically focusing on any-to-many and any-to-any voice conversions. The evaluation includes both objective and subjective measures to assess the performance of four distinct VC methods: PPG-VC, NonParaSeq2seq-VC, Seq2seqPR-DurIAN, and BNE-Seq2seqMoL.

Objective evaluations utilize metrics such as Mel-Cepstrum Distortion (MCD), Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE), and Character/Word Error Rate (CER/WER) to quantify the quality and intelligibility of the converted voices. Results from tables indicate varied performance across different gender pairings, with average MCD, RMSE, and WER scores providing insights into the effectiveness of each approach.

Subjective evaluations focus on naturalness and similarity ratings of the converted voices. The BNE-Seq2seqMoL method shows promising results in both naturalness and similarity compared to the other methods, particularly for female-to-female and male-to-female conversions.

The paper also details the calculation of MCD and F0-RMSE, emphasizing the use of mel-cepstral coefficients for spectral evaluation and highlighting the importance of an end-to-end ASR engine for measuring intelligibility through CER and WER.

Overall, the findings suggest that while all methods have their strengths and weaknesses, the proposed BNE-Seq2seqMoL approach demonstrates superior performance in subjective assessments, indicating its potential for improved voice conversion applications.
