The paper discusses a hybrid CTC-attention model for phoneme recognition, integrating a Connectionist Temporal Classification (CTC) objective and an attention mechanism. A hyper-parameter is introduced to balance these objectives, set to 0.5 in this study.

For synthesis, the DurIAN model is introduced, which predicts mel-spectrograms from phoneme sequences while addressing alignment issues common in traditional attention-based models. This model incorporates a duration module that uses a CBHG encoder to transform phoneme sequences into hidden representations. The hidden states are then expanded based on phoneme-level duration information, allowing for accurate mel-spectrogram generation through an RNN-based TTS decoder, which supports multi-speaker generation via speaker representations.

The paper further extends the DurIAN model to support any-to-any voice conversion by incorporating a speaker encoder that generates speaker identity vectors from acoustic signals. This vector conditions the synthesis process, ensuring that the output maintains the desired speaker's vocal characteristics.

Additionally, the BNE-Seq2seqMoL approach is proposed, combining a bottleneck feature extractor with a multi-speaker mixture of logistic attention-based synthesis model. This model aims to enhance the generation of mel-spectrograms by leveraging rich content features extracted from the input data.

In summary, the paper presents innovative methods for phoneme recognition and voice conversion that enhance the accuracy and flexibility of TTS systems, particularly in multi-speaker scenarios.
