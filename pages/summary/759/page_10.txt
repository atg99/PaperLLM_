This paper presents a refined non-parallel sequence-to-sequence (seq2seq) voice conversion (VC) approach known as BNE-Seq2seqMoL, which integrates a phoneme recognizer and a multi-speaker duration-informed attention network. The study includes ablation experiments to assess the impact of various design choices on the model's performance, highlighting the importance of feature selection, such as the inclusion of Log-F0 and UV features and the use of instance normalization and location-sensitive attention.

The results from the ablation studies, summarized in Table V, indicate that the proposed model outperforms alternative configurations in terms of F0-RMSE and word error rate (WER), confirming the effectiveness of the design strategies employed. Furthermore, the cross-speaker property of the bottle-neck features is explored using t-distributed Stochastic Neighbor Embedding (t-SNE) for visualization, demonstrating strong clustering among features from different speakers and suggesting that this contributes to the model's superior performance in voice conversion.

The paper also discusses training and inference speed, noting that the BNE-Seq2seqMoL approach achieves efficient performance metrics compared to other systems. The inference process benefits from partitioning input mel spectrograms, which significantly accelerates computation without degrading conversion quality.

In conclusion, the proposed BNE-Seq2seqMoL approach effectively enhances voice conversion capabilities by leveraging robust feature extraction and model design, paving the way for advancements in any-to-many VC systems.
