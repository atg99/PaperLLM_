This paper presents a novel voice conversion (VC) approach that integrates a bottleneck feature extractor (BNE) with a mixture of logistics (MoL) attention-based sequence-to-sequence (seq2seq) synthesis model. The proposed BNE-Seq2seqMoL method addresses the limitations encountered in previous PPG-based and non-parallel seq2seq VC techniques. It enables both any-to-many and any-to-any VC configurations, demonstrating superior performance through comprehensive objective and subjective evaluations.

The study includes an analysis of inference speeds for various VC systems implemented using PyTorch on an NVIDIA Tesla M40 GPU and Intel Xeon E5-2680 CPU. Results indicate that the proposed model significantly reduces the complexity of the VC pipeline, relying solely on an automatic speech recognition (ASR) encoder and a synthesis decoder, while still utilizing spectral features such as mel spectrograms for intermediate representation. However, the reliance on an independently trained neural vocoder may impact synthesis quality, suggesting the potential for future research into end-to-end waveform training.

Ablation studies reinforce the effectiveness of the feature selection and model design strategies employed in this new approach. The paper also indicates plans to explore the application of the BNE-Seq2seqMoL model for source style transfer and emotion conversion in subsequent research.
