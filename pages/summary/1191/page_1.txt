The paper introduces CoMatch, a novel semi-supervised learning method designed to leverage unlabeled data effectively while addressing limitations of existing approaches. CoMatch operates by concurrently learning two representations of training data: class probabilities and low-dimensional embeddings. These representations interact to enhance each other, with embeddings enforcing smoothness on class probabilities to refine pseudo-labels, while pseudo-labels provide structure to embeddings via graph-based contrastive learning.

CoMatch demonstrates significant performance improvements across various datasets, particularly in scenarios with limited labeled data. It achieves a top-1 accuracy of 66.0% on ImageNet with only 1% of labels, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art method, FixMatch, by 12.6%. Additionally, CoMatch shows superior representation learning capabilities in downstream tasks, outperforming both supervised and self-supervised learning methods.

The paper critiques existing semi-supervised learning approaches for their reliance on the quality of class predictions and their inability to produce optimal representations for specific classification tasks. By unifying concepts such as consistency regularization, entropy minimization, contrastive learning, and graph-based semi-supervised learning, CoMatch effectively addresses these challenges. Experimental results confirm its efficacy, particularly in label-scarce environments, showcasing its potential for broader application in machine learning and computer vision.
