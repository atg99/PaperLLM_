This paper discusses the performance of CoMatch, a method designed for semi-supervised learning on ImageNet using only 1% labeled data. The results are presented through various plots that illustrate the accuracy of confident pseudo-labels, the ratio of these labels included in the unsupervised classification loss, and the overall top-1 accuracy on test data. CoMatch demonstrates a significant improvement of 6.1% over the best baseline, achieving 57.1% accuracy after 100 epochs of training, and further enhancements with MoCov2 pre-training, reaching 67.1% accuracy on 1% labels and 73.7% on 10% labels.

An ablation study is conducted to evaluate the impact of different components of CoMatch, focusing on parameters such as the graph connection threshold, contrastive loss weight, prediction weight in pseudo-labels, and sizes of the memory bank and momentum queue. The findings indicate that increasing the threshold T reduces the similarity between lower confidence pseudo-labels, while varying the contrastive loss weight shows that a higher weight is beneficial when fewer labeled samples are available. The prediction weight α plays a crucial role in balancing the EMA model's predictions and maintaining smoothness, with α=0.9 yielding optimal results. Lastly, adjusting the size of the memory bank and momentum queue allows for better structural constraints within the embedding space.

Overall, the study highlights the effectiveness of CoMatch and the importance of tuning specific hyperparameters to enhance the model's performance in semi-supervised learning scenarios.
