This paper discusses a novel semi-supervised learning method called CoMatch, which addresses limitations in existing techniques that rely on high-dimensional feature representations. The authors identify several issues with these methods, including the correlation of features with class predictions, the diminishing significance of Euclidean distance due to the curse of dimensionality, and high computational costs that hinder scalability.

CoMatch proposes a joint optimization of three components: an encoder, a classification head, and a projection head. The method processes batches of labeled and unlabeled samples, applying three distinct loss functions: a supervised classification loss on labeled data, an unsupervised classification loss on unlabeled data, and a graph-based contrastive loss on unlabeled data. The supervised loss is defined using cross-entropy between true labels and model predictions, while the unsupervised loss utilizes pseudo-labels derived from model predictions on weakly augmented samples.

The authors introduce memory-smoothed pseudo-labeling to refine pseudo-labels and mitigate confirmation bias, aligning the model's predictions with a moving average of past predictions. Additionally, they implement a memory bank that stores class probabilities and embeddings of past weakly augmented samples, facilitating the generation of pseudo-labels for current unlabeled samples by aggregating information from nearby samples in the memory bank.

Overall, CoMatch transforms high-dimensional features into two compact representations: class probabilities and normalized low-dimensional embeddings, enhancing both the efficiency and effectiveness of the semi-supervised learning process. The training objective combines the various losses, weighted by hyperparameters, to optimize the model's performance.
