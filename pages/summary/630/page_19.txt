The paper discusses the challenges associated with joint training of multi-modal networks in deep learning, particularly focusing on the phenomenon of modality competition. It presents a theoretical framework to understand why joint training may fail under certain conditions.

Key points include:

1. **Modality Competition**: The paper argues that when multiple modalities are trained together, they may compete for representation, leading to suboptimal performance. This competition can hinder the learning process and degrade the model's ability to generalize.

2. **Mathematical Framework**: The authors provide a series of inequalities and claims that mathematically characterize the conditions under which joint training fails. They introduce variables and terms that quantify the training dynamics and performance metrics, such as error tolerance and gradient updates.

3. **Error Tolerance**: The concept of error tolerance is explored, particularly in different stages of training. The paper establishes that error rates can be bounded and provides conditions under which these bounds hold.

4. **Gradient Updates**: The paper details how gradient updates are impacted by the competition among modalities. It highlights the importance of ensuring that the updates favor the correct modality to maintain learning efficacy.

5. **Activation Function Dynamics**: The analysis includes how the activation functions behave during training, particularly when sufficient multi-modal data is available. It emphasizes that proper representation in the linear regime of activation functions is crucial for effective learning.

Overall, the paper provides a rigorous mathematical approach to understanding the pitfalls of joint training in multi-modal networks, emphasizing the need for careful design and consideration of modality interactions to enhance performance in deep learning applications.
