This paper investigates the challenges associated with joint training of multi-modal neural networks in deep learning, particularly focusing on modality competition. It presents a detailed test error analysis, demonstrating that the likelihood of a winning modality for a given class is influenced by the network's training dynamics. 

The authors show that if a modality is winning for a class, its associated function approximation can lead to a lower test error, while other modalities may suffer from higher test errors due to insufficient data representation. Specifically, they argue that the test error can be high when the winning modality does not adequately represent the class, leading to a scenario where the predicted output for a class is less than that for another class, with a high probability.

The paper also provides a corollary that restates the conditions under which the learned multi-modal network performs poorly. It emphasizes the importance of data sufficiency for effective training and highlights the significant impact of insufficient data on overall network performance. 

In contrast, the paper briefly discusses uni-modal networks, indicating that their training does not encounter the same level of complexity due to the absence of modality competition. The proof sketch for the results related to uni-modal networks follows a similar analytical approach but simplifies the analysis by focusing solely on single modalities.

Overall, the findings suggest that modality competition can significantly hinder the performance of multi-modal networks, particularly when data representation is inadequate, thus providing insights into the design and training of such networks.
