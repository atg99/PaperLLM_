The paper "Modality Competition: What Makes Joint Training of Multi-modal Network Fail in Deep Learning?" explores the challenges associated with training multi-modal networks in deep learning. The authors argue that the competition among different modalities can lead to suboptimal feature learning and hinder the effectiveness of joint training. They emphasize that the misalignment of feature representations across modalities can result in degraded performance, particularly in tasks requiring the integration of diverse data types.

The paper references key studies, such as the foundational work on attention mechanisms by Vaswani et al., which highlights the importance of efficient information processing in neural networks. It also discusses the difficulties identified by Wang et al. regarding multi-modal classification networks, and the insights from Wen and Li on self-supervised learning processes.

Furthermore, the authors cite various methodologies and frameworks that have contributed to the understanding of multi-view learning and feature representation, including the works of Xu et al. on multi-view learning and Yang et al. on auxiliary information in feature learning. The paper underscores the necessity for better strategies to align and integrate features from different modalities to improve the performance of multi-modal networks.

In conclusion, the authors call for further research to address the modality competition issue, aiming to enhance the joint training of multi-modal networks by developing techniques that ensure more effective feature alignment and integration.
