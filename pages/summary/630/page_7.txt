The paper discusses the limitations of joint training in multi-modal networks within deep learning, emphasizing the phenomenon of modality competition. The authors establish that while training error can be minimized to zero, the test error remains high due to insufficient feature representation for certain modalities. 

Theorem 4.1 indicates that uni-modal networks can effectively learn ideal feature representations for a specific modality, while Theorem 4.2 reveals that multi-modal networks, despite achieving zero training error, often fail to generalize well, resulting in poor test performance. This occurs because at least one modality may not adequately capture the relevant features, leading to ineffective learning.

The authors argue that the intention behind joint training—leveraging information from sufficient modalities to improve accuracy—can backfire, as adding more modalities may degrade the learning of existing modalities. This phenomenon leads to a performance gap between uni-modal and multi-modal networks, as outlined in Corollary 4.3, which quantifies the failure of joint training in terms of learned feature representations.

Overall, the findings highlight the complexities and potential pitfalls of joint training in multi-modal deep learning systems, suggesting that careful consideration is needed to avoid impairing feature representation learning.
