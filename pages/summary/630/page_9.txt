The paper investigates the phenomenon of modality competition in deep learning, particularly focusing on the challenges faced by joint training of multi-modal networks. The authors establish a theoretical framework to explain why uni-modal networks often outperform multi-modal networks trained together under late-fusion settings.

The analysis is structured in two main phases for both multi-modal and uni-modal training processes. In the first phase for multi-modal networks, a slight difference in initialization can lead to one modality dominating the learning process, which the authors refer to as "winning modality." This winning modality maintains its advantage throughout training, while other modalities struggle to catch up. The authors introduce Property 5.1 to quantify how initialization differences can impact modality performance.

In contrast, the uni-modal training process allows the network to focus solely on learning features from a single modality without competition, leading to faster convergence and feature extraction.

The paper highlights practical implications, suggesting that practitioners can improve late-fusion training by dynamically adjusting modality participation or introducing regularization to ensure comprehensive feature exploration from all modalities. Previous works that adaptively blend supervision signals during training are supported by the findings of this study.

Limitations include focusing on a simplified model, with a call for future research to explore joint training's sub-optimality in other fusion frameworks and relax certain data assumptions.

In conclusion, the paper provides insights into the modality competition phenomenon, offering a theoretical basis for the observed performance gaps between uni-modal and joint multi-modal networks, supported by empirical evidence. The research contributes to a deeper understanding of feature learning in multi-modal contexts.
