The paper "Modality Competition: What Makes Joint Training of Multi-modal Network Fail in Deep Learning?" explores the challenges associated with the joint training of multi-modal networks in deep learning contexts. It discusses the phenomenon of modality competition, where different modalities (such as text, audio, and visual data) compete for representation in a shared model, leading to suboptimal performance. The authors argue that this competition can result in the degradation of learning efficiency and the effectiveness of the model.

The paper provides a theoretical framework to analyze why joint training may fail, emphasizing the importance of understanding the interactions between modalities. It identifies factors such as modality laziness, where certain modalities may dominate the learning process while others become underutilized, and the implications of this imbalance on model performance.

Through empirical experiments and theoretical insights, the authors suggest strategies to mitigate these issues, such as improving modality representation and balancing contributions during training. The findings contribute to the ongoing discourse in multi-modal machine learning, highlighting the necessity for careful design and training strategies to harness the full potential of multi-modal networks.
