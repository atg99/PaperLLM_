The paper investigates the challenges associated with joint training of multi-modal networks in deep learning, focusing on the concept of modality competition. It establishes a framework where each class has an associated feature vector in various modalities. A key observation is that for sufficient classes, the true label's coordinate in the feature vector is significantly larger than others, while for insufficient classes, the target coordinate is smaller than competing off-target signals. This distinction underlines the real-world scenario where different modalities contribute varying levels of importance to classification tasks.

The authors introduce a noise model that incorporates both Gaussian noise and feature noise, leading to a more realistic representation of input data. They define a multi-modal network architecture that employs a late-fusion approach, processing each modality through a single-layer neural network with a smoothed ReLU activation function. This design allows for the integration of features from multiple modalities before classification.

The paper also contrasts multi-modal networks with uni-modal networks, highlighting that the latter omits the fusion step and processes each modality independently. The theoretical analysis suggests that the effectiveness of multi-modal learning hinges on the proper learning of the encoder layer, and the framework accommodates scenarios where the classifier layer is also trainable.

Overall, the work sheds light on the pitfalls of multi-modal network training and emphasizes the importance of understanding modality significance and noise in achieving effective classification outcomes.
