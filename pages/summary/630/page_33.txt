The paper investigates the challenges associated with joint training of multi-modal networks in deep learning, particularly focusing on modality competition. It explores whether a single-modal model, trained with a fixed encoder initialized from a multi-modal model, can outperform a single-modal model trained independently. The study employs an empirical approach, utilizing a linear classifier atop a frozen encoder to avoid parameter updates during training. 

The optimization is conducted using the AdamW algorithm with specific hyperparameters, including a peak learning rate of 1e−4 and a cosine decay schedule, across a batch size of 256 on NVIDIA V100 GPUs. 

Results from experiments on an internal product dataset illustrate the limitations of modality-specific performance. Figures presented in the study highlight that certain classes show significant accuracy discrepancies between text-only and RGB-only uni-modal training, indicating that the RGB modality has not been effectively learned and that text modality struggles in the competition, leading to suboptimal exploration of its potential. Overall, the findings suggest that fixed modality encoders initialized from multi-modal training may not yield the expected improvements in performance, emphasizing the complexities of modality interactions in deep learning frameworks.
