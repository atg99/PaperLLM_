The paper discusses the challenges in joint training of multi-modal networks within deep learning, particularly focusing on modality competition. The authors provide a mathematical analysis of the training phases, establishing claims regarding the behavior of the network parameters over iterations.

Claim A.7 asserts that under certain induction hypotheses, the update rule for the parameters of a specific class j can be expressed in terms of previous values and the gradient of the loss function. The proof demonstrates that the parameters can either increase or decrease based on the interaction of various factors, such as the learning rate and the classification outcomes.

The training process is divided into two main phases for each class j. Phase 1 occurs until a threshold iteration Tj is reached, where the parameter Γ(t) for class j approaches a significant level. Phase 2 is further divided into two stages: the first stage continues until all classes reach a certain parameter level, while the second stage signifies when the network has learned sufficient patterns, leading to significant outputs.

The analysis emphasizes that until the parameters reach a certain threshold, their contribution to the network's output is minimal. The critical point T0, where all parameters achieve a significant level, is shown to be dependent on the number of classes K, the learning rate η, and other factors. Overall, the findings highlight the complexities involved in effectively training multi-modal networks and the need to understand the dynamics of parameter updates across different phases.
