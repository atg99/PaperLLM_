The paper discusses the challenges associated with joint training of multi-modal networks in deep learning, focusing on modality competition and its impact on training and test error performance. 

In the proof section, the authors provide a rigorous analysis of training error, demonstrating that for each data pair, the training error can be bounded using a specific mathematical approach. They conclude that the training error is sufficiently small after a complete training iteration, indicating effective learning of the model parameters.

The test error analysis highlights that under sufficient data conditions, the model can learn to approximate the target function effectively. However, in scenarios of insufficient data, the model struggles to generalize, leading to suboptimal performance. The authors emphasize that the probability of insufficient data affects the model's ability to learn effectively.

In the experimental section, the authors validate their theoretical claims through empirical tests on the Kinetics-400 dataset, which involves action recognition, and an internal product classification dataset. For Kinetics-400, they utilize video frames and audio spectrograms as inputs, employing Transformer models for late fusion of multi-modal data. The internal product dataset involves images and text, and similarly, Transformer architectures are applied to process both data types.

Overall, the paper presents a comprehensive analysis of the limitations of joint training in multi-modal networks, supported by theoretical proofs and empirical experiments, highlighting the importance of sufficient data for effective model training.
