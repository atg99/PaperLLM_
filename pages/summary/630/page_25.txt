The paper explores the challenges associated with joint training in multi-modal networks within deep learning, particularly under the framework of modality competition. The authors analyze the mathematical properties of the training dynamics, focusing on error tolerances and the behavior of gradients across different modalities.

Key concepts include the definitions of various parameters such as dErrTol and the implications of the gradient inequalities on the convergence of the model. The analysis shows that when certain conditions hold, particularly for the maximum values of the function Φ(t), the model can become ineffective due to negligible loss contributions from the correct classes. This leads to the conclusion that if the score for the best-performing modality surpasses a certain threshold, it can become trivial, preventing effective learning.

The paper also introduces Lemma A.17, which asserts that under specific induction hypotheses, the weight updates remain nearly non-negative across iterations, despite fluctuations in loss values. This highlights the potential pitfalls of modality competition where competing signals can undermine the overall learning process.

In summary, the paper provides a rigorous examination of the mathematical underpinnings of multi-modal training failures in deep learning, emphasizing the need to balance modality contributions to ensure effective learning outcomes.
