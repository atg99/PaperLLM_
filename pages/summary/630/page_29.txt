The paper explores the challenges associated with joint training of multi-modal networks in deep learning, specifically focusing on the phenomenon of modality competition. It presents a series of mathematical proofs and lemmas that establish conditions under which certain training errors can be minimized. 

Key points include:

1. **Induction Hypothesis**: The paper employs an induction hypothesis to demonstrate that the winning modality for each class can be bounded under specific conditions, ensuring that the training error approaches zero after a sufficient number of iterations.

2. **Main Theorem**: Theorem A.21 outlines that for a sufficiently large number of classes K and after a polynomial number of iterations, the training error for a multi-modal network is effectively zero. However, it also notes that the test error can be high with a certain probability, indicating that while training may succeed, generalization to test data remains a challenge.

3. **Training Error Analysis**: The analysis shows that the training error can be bounded by considerations of the loss function, revealing that as the number of iterations increases, the likelihood of misclassification decreases. 

4. **Gradient Descent**: The paper emphasizes the non-increasing nature of the gradient descent algorithm when applied to Lipschitz continuous objective functions, allowing for a controlled reduction in training error.

Overall, the findings suggest that while multi-modal networks can achieve low training error, the competition between modalities can complicate their performance on unseen data, highlighting the need for careful consideration in model design and training strategies.
