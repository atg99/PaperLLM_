The paper discusses the challenges of joint training in multi-modal networks within deep learning, particularly focusing on modality competition. The analysis is divided into two phases, with Phase 1 addressing the conditions under which the model parameters evolve when sufficient multi-modal data is available. The authors derive equations that represent the updates to the model weights, showing that the influence of the target modality significantly affects the learning process. 

In Phase 1, they establish bounds on the weight updates based on the data characteristics and the model's performance, leading to the conclusion that certain conditions must be met for effective learning. Specifically, they note that when the model is trained on data where the correct modality is present, the updates are more favorable, while insufficient data can lead to suboptimal learning outcomes.

Phase 2, Stage 1 shifts focus to scenarios where the model encounters insufficient multi-modal data. The authors detail how the model's performance is still bounded even under these conditions, showing that the network can still converge to a certain extent despite challenges posed by modality competition. They provide bounds on the expected values of the model weights, indicating that careful management of the learning process can mitigate some of the adverse effects of modality competition.

Overall, the paper presents a thorough theoretical framework that elucidates the mechanisms behind joint training failures in multi-modal networks, emphasizing the importance of data sufficiency and modality relevance in achieving successful learning outcomes.
