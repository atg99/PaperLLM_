The paper investigates the challenges associated with joint training of multi-modal networks in deep learning, specifically focusing on modality competition. The authors present a mathematical framework to analyze why such joint training often fails. 

Key findings include a detailed examination of the error bounds during different training phases and stages. The analysis highlights that the error tolerances in successive stages are influenced by factors such as the learning rate and the distribution of data across modalities. The authors derive specific inequalities that govern the behavior of the network's weights over iterations, emphasizing the role of noise and the interaction between modalities.

The proof involves several claims that establish relationships between the error terms and the parameters of the model, indicating that under certain conditions, the training process can be effectively controlled. The paper also discusses Gaussian noise correlation and its implications for the weight updates in the network, providing insights into how noise can affect the learning dynamics of multi-modal networks.

Overall, the work contributes to a deeper understanding of the limitations and potential pitfalls of multi-modal joint training in deep learning, offering a theoretical foundation for future advancements in this area.
