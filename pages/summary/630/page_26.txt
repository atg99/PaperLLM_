The paper investigates the challenges of joint training in multi-modal networks within deep learning, particularly focusing on modality competition. It presents a mathematical framework to analyze the performance of these networks under different training conditions. 

Key findings include the identification of conditions under which the training of multi-modal networks fails, specifically highlighting the role of error tolerance and off-diagonal correlation in the optimization process. The authors establish bounds for various parameters, demonstrating that the error tolerance in the third stage of training can be significantly influenced by the modalities' interaction.

The proof structure relies on induction hypotheses and employs gradient inequalities to derive results about the weights associated with different modalities. The paper delineates phases of training, analyzing the behavior of the networks at different time intervals and under various assumptions about the input data and labels.

Overall, the findings suggest that careful management of the interaction between modalities is crucial for successful joint training, and the paper provides theoretical insights that can guide future research in multi-modal deep learning systems.
