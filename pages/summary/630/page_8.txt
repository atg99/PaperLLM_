The paper discusses the challenges associated with joint training of multi-modal networks in deep learning, specifically focusing on the phenomenon of modality competition. It establishes that the test error of a joint training approach can be influenced by the strengths and weaknesses of different modalities, characterized by two sets of factors: the probability of a modality prevailing during training and the structural insufficiency of that modality.

The authors outline a proof sketch for their theoretical results, providing an overview of the joint and uni-modal training processes. They introduce a key quantity that characterizes feature learning in modality encoders and detail the training dynamics of multi-modal networks, identifying two phases. The first phase involves modality competition arising from random initialization, where only one class-associated feature is quickly learned, while others are neglected. This competition results in sub-optimal performance of naive joint training.

The authors further explore the implications of random Gaussian initialization, demonstrating that for each class, a winning modality emerges based on the correlation between the modality and the neurons in its encoder. They show that the winning modality's feature representation improves faster than the other, leading to persistent challenges for the weaker modality.

Overall, the paper highlights that the dynamics of multi-modal joint training can lead to significant inefficiencies due to competition between modalities, ultimately affecting the feature learning process and the model's performance.
