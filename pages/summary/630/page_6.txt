The paper "Modality Competition: What Makes Joint Training of Multi-modal Network Fail in Deep Learning?" investigates the challenges associated with training multi-modal networks compared to their uni-modal counterparts. It discusses the framework used for late fusion and outlines the training process for both multi-modal and uni-modal networks, utilizing gradient descent to optimize empirical cross-entropy loss.

The authors define training data consisting of multi-modal pairs and categorize them into sufficient and insufficient data pairs. They present a structured training algorithm that initializes network weights and employs a gradient descent update rule for both network types. 

The main results include theoretical guarantees for uni-modal networks, demonstrating that they can achieve global minimum training loss and exhibit good test performance after sufficient iterations. In contrast, the paper highlights that naive joint training of multi-modal networks often leads to unfavorable feature representations, particularly under conditions of insufficient data. This results in a performance gap between the best uni-modal and multi-modal configurations.

The findings suggest that the inherent competition among modalities in joint training can hinder the learning process, prompting the conclusion that careful consideration is necessary when employing multi-modal networks in deep learning applications.
