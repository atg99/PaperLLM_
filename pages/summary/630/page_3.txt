The paper investigates the phenomenon of modality competition in joint training of multi-modal networks in deep learning, specifically analyzing why such training often fails to outperform the best uni-modal models. The authors present experimental findings using the Kinetics-400 dataset, highlighting that feature representations from joint training degrade compared to those from directly trained uni-modal models. 

The results, illustrated in error curves and accuracy metrics, indicate that while video modalities perform well, audio modalities struggle significantly, leading to a loss in competition. The authors provide evidence that video features remain under-explored despite joint training, as shown in their comparative analysis of accuracy changes for both improved and dropped classes.

The paper also discusses the broader context of multi-modal applications in deep learning, acknowledging the success of combining modalities like text and vision in various tasks. However, the focus is on the theoretical exploration of late-fusion multi-modal networks, aiming to understand why joint training does not yield expected performance improvements. The authors note that theoretical advancements in multi-modal learning are limited, suggesting a gap in understanding the interactions between modalities in joint training scenarios.
