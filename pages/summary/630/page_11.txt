The paper explores the challenges of joint training in multi-modal networks within deep learning, particularly focusing on modality competition. The authors argue that while combining multiple modalities can enhance performance, certain factors can lead to failures in joint training. Key reasons for this include the competition between modalities, which can cause one modality to dominate the learning process, resulting in suboptimal representations. The paper references various studies on multi-modal learning, highlighting the need for effective integration strategies to balance the contributions of different modalities. Additionally, it discusses the implications of these findings for future research and practical applications in multi-modal deep learning. The insights provided aim to inform the development of more robust models that can leverage the strengths of diverse data sources without succumbing to competition-related pitfalls.
