We prove that during the joint training of a late-fusion multi-modal network, modalities compete against each other, leading to a scenario where only a subset of modalities is effectively learned. This phenomenon, termed modality competition, results in the underutilization of certain modalities, contributing to the overall suboptimal performance of the multi-modal network compared to the best uni-modal network.

The paper identifies the fundamental issues in multi-modal learning, particularly focusing on how each modality's encoder learns its representations and why naive joint training is often ineffective. The authors provide a theoretical framework supported by empirical results that demonstrate the intrinsic behavior of modality competition in late-fusion joint training.

In summary, this work reveals that the competition among modalities during joint training can hinder the effectiveness of multi-modal networks, leading to performance that may not meet expectations given the potential information from multiple signals. The findings underscore the need for a deeper understanding of the training dynamics in multi-modal networks, marking a significant contribution to the theoretical discourse in the field.
