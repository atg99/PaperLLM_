This paper discusses the challenges faced in joint training of multi-modal networks in deep learning, specifically addressing the issues of modality competition. It presents a mathematical framework to analyze the performance of these networks under varying conditions of data availability and modality interaction.

The authors derive conditions under which the training of multi-modal networks can fail, particularly when there is insufficient multi-modal data. They highlight that for certain classes of data, the output of the network can be naively bounded, leading to ineffective learning. The paper provides rigorous mathematical expressions to describe the relationships between the model parameters, data characteristics, and the resulting learning outcomes.

Key findings include:

1. The lower bound for model output can be zero under specific conditions, indicating potential failures in learning when data is sparse.
2. The relationship between different modalities is quantified, revealing how the learning rate and data distribution influence overall model performance.
3. The authors introduce metrics such as ErrTol and gErr to assess the error tolerance and generalization error of the model, respectively.

The paper concludes with a lemma that establishes the conditions for effective training over time, emphasizing the importance of managing modality interactions and data distribution to enhance the robustness of multi-modal networks. Overall, the study provides a theoretical foundation that underlines why joint training can fail and suggests directions for improvement in future research.
