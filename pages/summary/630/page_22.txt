The paper examines the challenges associated with joint training of multi-modal networks in deep learning, particularly focusing on modality competition. It introduces a mathematical framework to analyze this competition, using parameters such as Λ and Γ(t) to quantify the performance of different modalities. Key findings include that certain conditions must be met for successful training, and that the winning modality for each class can be determined through a defined set of parameters.

The authors present lemmas that establish the relationship between data-dependent parameters and the performance of the network. They leverage classic results from tensor power analysis to demonstrate that under specific conditions, the performance of competing modalities can be analyzed and bounded, leading to insights on why joint training might fail. 

The paper emphasizes the importance of understanding modality interactions and provides a theoretical basis for improving multi-modal network training by addressing the competition between modalities. The results contribute to the broader understanding of deep learning architectures and their optimization.
