The paper investigates the challenges associated with joint training of multi-modal networks in deep learning, specifically focusing on the phenomenon of modality competition. The authors present a formal proof detailing how gradient updates can lead to failures in learning when multiple modalities are involved.

The proof begins by analyzing the behavior of weights during updates, showing that certain conditions can lead to suboptimal convergence. It establishes that for a given input-output pair, the weight adjustments depend on the classification accuracy of the current model and the gradients derived from the modalities. The analysis reveals that if the model is not adequately representative of all modalities, the updates can either reinforce incorrect classifications or fail to improve learning altogether.

Key findings highlight that if the gradient updates are not balanced among modalities, it can lead to a situation where the model overfits to a dominant modality while neglecting others. This imbalance is quantified through inequalities that relate the weight updates to the performance of the model across different modalities.

Furthermore, the paper provides an induction hypothesis that asserts under specific conditions—namely, a sufficiently small learning rate and adequate parameter settings—the proposed methodology can hold for all iterations. The authors utilize Gaussian analysis to validate their claims and ensure that the model's performance remains robust across training iterations.

In conclusion, the paper systematically addresses the reasons behind the failures in multi-modal network training, emphasizing the need for careful management of modality contributions during the training process to achieve effective learning outcomes.
