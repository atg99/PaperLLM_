The paper discusses the challenges of joint training in multi-modal networks within deep learning, particularly highlighting the phenomenon of modality competition. It notes that while some classes, such as "auction," exhibit low accuracy, this indicates an insufficient structure in the uni-modal data. The findings corroborate previous research by Wang et al. (2020), which shows that the best-performing uni-modal models often surpass multi-modal counterparts.

The study reveals that only certain modalities can effectively learn feature representations. For instance, classes like "fabric" and "business" initially demonstrate higher accuracy but ultimately see their performance drop to zero in joint training, suggesting that images for these classes are not effectively learned. A similar trend is observed in the text modality. The analysis further illustrates that the feature representations from joint training degrade compared to those from directly trained uni-modal models, as shown in the error curves presented in the figures.
