The paper discusses the challenges of joint training in multi-modal networks in deep learning, specifically focusing on modality competition and the conditions under which such training may fail. It introduces a formal framework by defining a data set Xr and a sparse vector zr, identifying the indices of non-zero coordinates as S(Xr). The analysis distinguishes between sufficient and insufficient uni-modal training data, denoted as Drs and Dri, respectively.

The Induction Hypothesis B.1 outlines conditions for training performance based on data sufficiency. For sufficient data, it establishes that the learned model's output closely aligns with the true labels for relevant indices while remaining bounded for irrelevant ones. For insufficient data, the model's performance is less reliable, with certain bounds provided for the outputs.

The training process for uni-modal networks is divided into two phases for each class. Phase 1 focuses on early training iterations, while Phase 2 is divided into stages that monitor the model's performance as training progresses.

The main theorem (Theorem B.2) asserts that for sufficiently large K, after a polynomial number of iterations, the learned uni-modal network achieves zero training error and a bounded test error close to the expected performance, indicating a successful learning outcome under the specified conditions. The findings highlight the significance of data sufficiency in the effectiveness of multi-modal network training.
