The paper examines the challenges of joint training in multi-modal networks within deep learning, particularly focusing on the phenomenon of modality competition. It highlights how insufficient data across modalities can hinder the training process, leading to suboptimal model performance.

In the analysis, the authors denote specific error tolerances for different stages of training and provide mathematical formulations to represent the relationships between various parameters. They argue that when multi-modal data is inadequate, it impacts the learning dynamics, leading to a degradation in performance, which is captured through the equations involving error tolerances and data distributions.

The paper also discusses regularization techniques, particularly through the lens of diagonal correlations. It establishes that under certain induction hypotheses, the correlations remain bounded, which supports the stability of the training process. The authors provide proofs that demonstrate how gradient updates and the behavior of the model parameters evolve over time, particularly under conditions of insufficient data.

Ultimately, the findings suggest that careful consideration of data sufficiency across modalities is crucial for effective joint training in multi-modal networks. The paper argues that without addressing these data limitations, the training may fail to converge to an optimal solution, thereby highlighting the importance of balancing modality contributions during the learning process.
