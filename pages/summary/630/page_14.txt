The paper discusses the concept of modality competition in deep learning, specifically focusing on the challenges faced during the joint training of multi-modal networks. The authors introduce a data-dependent parameter to characterize modalities at initialization, denoted as dj,r(D). This parameter is calculated based on the distribution of data pairs across different classes.

The winning modality is defined for each class at the initialization stage, where one modality is considered winning if it satisfies a specific condition involving the parameters Γ(t) and dj,r(D). The authors also present a lemma that characterizes the probability of each modality being the winning one, demonstrating that at least one modality will likely win and providing bounds on this probability.

The proof of this lemma illustrates that under certain conditions, the probabilities of the modalities winning are non-trivial and can be quantified. An induction hypothesis is also introduced to facilitate further analysis, defining a set Sr(X) that captures the indices of non-zero coordinates in the sparse vector representation of the data.

Overall, the section emphasizes the importance of modality characterization and the probabilistic foundations that influence the success of multi-modal training in deep learning contexts.
