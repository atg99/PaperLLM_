The paper investigates the challenges associated with joint training of multi-modal networks in deep learning, particularly highlighting the phenomenon of "Modality Competition." The authors argue that the typical structure used in multi-modal applications is often insufficient, leading to sub-optimal performance compared to uni-modal networks. They present theoretical findings indicating that when training with multiple modalities, the network tends to focus on features from only a subset of modalities that align well with their initialization, while others remain underutilized.

Key findings include:

1. **Uni-modal networks excel at learning modality-specific features** when trained individually, leading to better performance.
2. **Naive joint training** of multi-modal networks results in inefficient learning, where only certain modalities capture significant feature representations.
3. The authors provide empirical evidence to support their claims, showing that uni-modal networks can outperform multi-modal networks in specific tasks and that certain classes exhibit low accuracy due to insufficient structure in the uni-modal setup.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the need for improved training strategies that address the competition among modalities to enhance the efficacy of multi-modal networks in deep learning applications.
