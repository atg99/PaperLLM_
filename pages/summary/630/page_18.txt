The paper discusses the phenomenon of modality competition in the context of joint training of multi-modal networks in deep learning. It focuses on the conditions under which joint training may fail, providing a mathematical framework to analyze the performance of such networks.

The authors present a series of inequalities and claims that establish bounds on the error rates of the model as a function of various factors, including the number of modalities and the complexity of the data. For instance, it is shown that the error rate can be bounded by an exponential function of the logarithm of the number of modalities, indicating that as complexity increases, the performance of the model may degrade.

The core argument revolves around the relationship between the error rates across different modalities and how they interact during training. The paper provides a detailed proof structure to demonstrate that under certain conditions, specifically when the distributions of data across modalities do not align, the overall error can be significantly high.

Key findings include that the joint training may not effectively leverage the multi-modal data, leading to suboptimal performance. The paper concludes with a discussion on the implications of these results for designing multi-modal networks and suggests areas for further research to improve the robustness of such models against modality competition.
