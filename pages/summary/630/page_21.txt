The paper discusses the challenges associated with joint training of multi-modal networks in deep learning, specifically focusing on the phenomenon of modality competition. The authors analyze gradient updates within the context of a multi-class classification problem, where they derive conditions under which certain classes (j) may fail to improve during training due to competition from other classes.

Key concepts include the formulation of a function \( D \) representing the loss associated with class \( j^* \) and how updates to this function are influenced by data points belonging to different classes. The authors establish a relationship between the loss function and the behavior of model predictions, indicating that the contributions from insufficient data are minimal.

The analysis further shows that for certain classes \( j \), the prediction function \( f(t) \) can be bounded above by a polynomial function of the complexity of the problem, denoted as \( \Phi(t) \). They derive an upper bound for the expected prediction error, which leads to implications on the growth of \( \Phi(t) \) over iterations. 

The authors conclude that if the exponential growth of \( \Phi(t) \) reaches a particular threshold, it will be constrained in subsequent iterations, suggesting that the competition among modalities can hinder the effective learning of the model. This insight is crucial for understanding why joint training may sometimes fail in multi-modal networks and highlights the importance of managing modality interactions during training.
