The paper titled "Modality Competition: What Makes Joint Training of Multi-modal Network Fail in Deep Learning?" addresses the challenges in joint training of multi-modal networks in deep learning, particularly in situations with varying data sufficiency. 

The authors present a detailed analysis of the performance of the network under two conditions: sufficient and insufficient data. For sufficient data, they establish that the output of the network converges to a certain value with respect to the winning modality. For insufficient data, the output exhibits a different behavior, revealing the dependency on the winning modality and introducing additional error margins.

The paper introduces several key claims and proofs, particularly focusing on the training phases of the network and the implications of the induction hypothesis. The authors characterize the function approximation and provide bounds on the learning error, demonstrating that the network's performance is heavily influenced by the modality competition and the amount of available data.

Key results include the conditions under which one modality may outperform others and the implications of these conditions on the overall training process. The analysis shows that the training dynamics vary significantly depending on data sufficiency, which can lead to failures in joint training of multi-modal networks.

In conclusion, the paper emphasizes the importance of understanding modality competition and its impact on network training, providing insights into the conditions that can lead to failure in multi-modal deep learning contexts.
