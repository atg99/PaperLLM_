This paper surveys the various models of trust in Human-Robot Interaction (HRI), emphasizing the significance of appropriately calibrated trust for effective collaboration between humans and robots. As robotic systems evolve to function more as teammates rather than mere tools, the nature of their interaction with human collaborators becomes increasingly important. 

Trust plays a crucial role in HRI, impacting performance and influencing how humans engage with robots. Misplaced or inadequate trust can lead to suboptimal interactions, ultimately harming team performance. Therefore, it is essential to model trust accurately to achieve a suitable level of trust that fosters effective collaboration. 

The authors highlight the absence of comprehensive reviews focused specifically on trust modeling in HRI, motivating their exploration of various techniques and methods in this domain. They also outline potential research directions and challenges that need to be addressed in future studies related to human-robot trust modeling. This work aims to contribute to the understanding and advancement of trust in HRI, which is vital for the successful integration of robots into collaborative environments.
