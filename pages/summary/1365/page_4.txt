The paper discusses the factors influencing trust in human-robot interaction (HRI), categorizing them into three main areas: robot-related factors, human-related factors, and task and environment-related factors.

1. **Robot-Related Factors**: These are identified as having the most significant impact on trust in HRI. They are further divided into three subcategories:
   - **Performance-related factors**: These include aspects such as reliability, frequency and timing of faults, transparency, feedback, situation awareness, false alarms, and the level of autonomy.
   - **Behavior-related factors**: Advances in robotics have led to a perception of robots as teammates rather than mere tools. Factors such as likeability, proximity, engagement, reliability, and the robot's personality in relation to its tasks influence trust. Specific behaviors like apologies or explanations can aid in trust repair after failures.
   - **Appearance-related factors**: The physical appearance of robots, including anthropomorphism, gender, task harmony, and similarity to humans, affects how trust is formed.

2. **Human-Related Factors**: Although deemed to have the least impact on trust, these factors play a crucial role in trust development and fluctuation. Key elements include individual gender, feelings towards robots, initial expectations, prior experiences with robots, cultural influences, and understanding of the robotic system.

3. **Task and Environment-Related Factors**: These factors are noted as having the second greatest effect on trust. They encompass the nature of tasks, risk and safety considerations, workload, task duration, and the physical presence of the robot, among others.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the complexity of trust in HRI, highlighting the interplay between robot characteristics, human perceptions, and contextual factors.
