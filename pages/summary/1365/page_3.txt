The paper provides a comprehensive overview of trust in human-robot interaction (HRI), highlighting its significance in various robotic applications. It emphasizes that the definition of trust varies depending on the context, with Lee and See's definition being widely accepted. They define trust as the attitude that an agent will assist in achieving an individual's goals amidst uncertainty and vulnerability. Wagner et al. expand on this by defining trust as a belief that the trustee will act to mitigate the trustor's risk when outcomes are at stake.

The authors identify three main categories of factors affecting trust in HRI, which are derived from previous studies on human-automation trust but tailored to account for the unique characteristics of robots. These categories include:

1. Robot-related factors: encompassing performance, appearance, and behaviors of the robot.
2. Human-related factors: focusing on the abilities and characteristics of the human interacting with the robot.
3. Task and environment-related factors: addressing aspects of team collaboration and task requirements.

The paper also outlines that while many of these factors are consistent with earlier research, some have been updated or reclassified based on recent studies, reflecting the evolving understanding of trust in HRI. The classification serves as a framework for further research in this area.
