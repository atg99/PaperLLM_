Hancock et al. conducted a meta-analysis identifying human-based, robot-based, and environment-based factors that affect trust, leading to the development of a comprehensive trust model that also incorporates training and design implications.

Recent research emphasizes the use of both subjective and objective trust measurement techniques to enhance the accuracy of trust assessments. Psycho-physiological measurements have been utilized in studies across different domains like human-computer interaction and human trust in artificial intelligence. For instance, Khalid et al. proposed a trust model that integrates facial expressions, vocal features, and heart rate data, alongside self-reported trust, to classify trust levels using a Neuro-fuzzy trust classifier.

Trust models vary based on application domains, with input factors reflecting situational risk and agent behavior in emergency scenarios, or task performance in supervisory roles. Some models, like the OPTIMo model, consider robot failure rates and human interventions as inputs to gauge human trust in robotic teammates. Overall, the paper underscores the complexity of trust dynamics in HRI, highlighting the importance of collaboration performance and contextual factors in trust assessment and modeling.
