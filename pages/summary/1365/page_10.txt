The referenced works explore various aspects of trust in human-robot interaction and automation. Gao et al. model the influence of operator trust on performance in multiple robot control scenarios. Goodrich et al. discuss adjustable autonomy in human-agent interactions. Groom and Nass examine the viability of robots as teammates, while Gulati et al. focus on empirical assessments and the design of a human-computer trust scale. Hancock et al. provide a meta-analysis of factors influencing trust in human-robot interaction. Hoff and Bashir integrate empirical evidence on trust in automation.

Itoh and Tanaka present a mathematical model addressing trust dynamics, while Johnson and Mislin conduct a meta-analysis of trust games. Keri et al. investigate the psycho-physiological aspects of trust, particularly in the context of schizophrenia. Lazanyi and Maraczi assess dispositional trust towards autonomous vehicles. Lee and Moray explore trust and control strategies in human-machine systems, emphasizing adaptation to automation. Lee and See focus on designing systems for appropriate reliance.

Li et al. conduct a cross-cultural study on the effects of robot appearance and task on trust, and Lucas et al. investigate the role of social dialogue in error recovery among social robots. Martelaro et al. highlight the design of human-robot interactions to foster trust and companionship. Collectively, these studies contribute to understanding the complex dynamics of trust in interactions involving robots and automation.
