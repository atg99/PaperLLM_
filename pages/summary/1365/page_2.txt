The paper highlights the importance of trust in human-robot interaction (HRI), emphasizing that trust influences the acceptance and utilization of robotic agents in various disciplines, including computer engineering and psychology. The authors argue that inadequate trust calibration can lead to misuse, where users over-rely on robots, or disuse, where they fail to recognize the robots' capabilities. To mitigate these issues, it is essential to model trust effectively.

While many reviews examine factors affecting trust, this paper specifically focuses on trust modeling in HRI. It begins by defining trust, detailing the factors influencing it, and reviewing existing studies on trust models. The paper also discusses the input and output components of these models and concludes by addressing current challenges and future research directions in trust modeling.

In summary, the authors advocate for the development of methodologies to measure and calibrate trust within HRI to enhance the effectiveness and safety of robotic agents in various applications.
