The current trust models in Human-Robot Interaction (HRI) are often tailored to specific scenarios or robot types, limiting their comparability and general applicability. A comprehensive trust model is necessary to evaluate existing models' accuracy and facilitate the integration of trust assessments in emerging robotic fields.

Research in other disciplines, such as psychology and sociology, provides alternative indicators for measuring trust, such as physiological responses and behavioral assessments. These methods could enhance HRI trust models by offering a broader understanding of trust dynamics independent of numerous influencing factors.

While there has been some investigation into the impact of robot failures on trust, the field lacks extensive modeling of trust violations and their recovery. Future research should delve deeper into the nuances of trust loss and repair across various robot types and interaction contexts, considering factors like task nature and risk levels. Understanding how trust fluctuates post-failure is crucial for estimating recovery times and formulating effective trust repair strategies.

In summary, advancing trust modeling in HRI requires a shift toward generalizable models that can accommodate diverse factors influencing trust, ultimately leading to more robust and adaptable human-robot collaborations.
