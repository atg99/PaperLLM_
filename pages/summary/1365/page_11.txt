This survey paper focuses on the modeling of trust in human-robot interaction, exploring various dimensions and factors that influence trust between humans and robotic systems. It reviews a range of studies that investigate theoretical issues, empirical findings, and practical applications related to trust in automated systems.

Key themes include the impact of anthropomorphism and accountability on trust levels, the importance of communication styles and cultural factors, and the role of system transparency in fostering reliance on robotic systems. The paper also discusses how physical and psychological distances, such as proxemics, affect human-robot interactions.

Several studies highlighted in the survey examine the antecedents of trust in collaborative settings, including emergency scenarios where trust dynamics are critical. Findings suggest that timing and the nature of robot errors significantly influence trust repair and maintenance.

The paper emphasizes the need for robust trust models that can adapt to various contexts and user interactions, ultimately aiming to improve the efficacy and safety of human-robot collaborations in diverse applications, including manufacturing and emergency response.
