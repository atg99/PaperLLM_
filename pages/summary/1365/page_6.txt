Studies indicate a strong correlation between trust levels and the performance of robotic agents, highlighting that high trust fosters synergy where human decision-making complements the robot's capabilities. Conversely, low trust can lead humans to hesitate in delegating tasks or even disable the robot.

Performance-based trust models have been developed, primarily serving as feedback mechanisms to align a robot's performance with human expectations, thus promoting trust. Some models focus on assessing robot reliability to appropriately assign tasks, while others adjust robot behavior to enhance overall collaboration performance.

In supervisory collaborations, where humans supervise robots, trust models have evolved to measure the compatibility of robot performance with human expectations. Significant advancements include the incorporation of factors like failure rates and intervention frequencies into trust models. The Online Probabilistic Trust Inference Model (OPTIMo) uses Bayesian beliefs to provide real-time estimates of human trust based on robot performance.

Real-time trust modeling, such as the trust-POMDP model, integrates trust measurement into the robot's decision-making process, allowing for adaptive responses to maintain or restore human trust. This approach maximizes collaboration effectiveness by ensuring that trust dynamics are actively managed throughout the interaction.
