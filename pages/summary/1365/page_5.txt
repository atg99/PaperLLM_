The paper surveys the factors influencing trust in human-robot interaction (HRI) and compares it to trust in human-automation interaction (HAI). It categorizes factors affecting trust into four main groups: robot-related, task and environment-related, human-related, and performance-related. Key aspects include the robot's dependability, reliability, appearance, and behavior, as well as the human's personality, culture, experience, and subjective feelings.

Current research on trust modeling highlights that while there are similarities between HRI and HAI, existing HAI models often overlook crucial elements pertinent to HRI, such as situational awareness and the robot's physical presence. Notable contributions to trust modeling in HRI include works by Muir, Lee and Moray, and Desai, who have developed various models that account for these unique factors. Desai et al. presented a schematic model integrating aspects of both HRI and HAI, while Yagoda et al. introduced an early model focusing on task dimensions and expert assessments. The paper emphasizes the need for tailored models that reflect the complexities of HRI, ultimately advocating for a nuanced understanding of trust in robotic contexts.
