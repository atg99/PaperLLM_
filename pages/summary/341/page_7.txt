The paper presents a comprehensive analysis of various methods for table-to-text generation and question answering (QA) using the ToTTo dataset and multiple QA datasets. Key results are summarized through tables showcasing performance metrics like BLEU and Parent scores for different methods, including Random, kNN with RoBERTa, and KATE with various configurations (RoBERTa, NLI, and NLI+STS-B).

In table-to-text generation, KATE consistently outperforms other methods, achieving a BLEU score of 40.3 for the overall overlap subset, indicating its effectiveness in generating accurate textual representations from tabular data. The KATE method focuses on detailed information extraction, as demonstrated through examples comparing ground-truth sentences with outputs from random selection and KATE, highlighting its superior attention to relevant details.

For QA tasks, the results indicate KATE's strong performance across datasets like NQ, WQ, and TriviaQA, with scores of 41.6, 50.2, and 62.4 respectively for KATE NLI+STS-B, surpassing other methods including RAG and T5 models. The analysis further explores the impact of the number of in-context examples on KATE's performance, revealing that even a small number of examples can lead to significant improvements over random selection.

Additionally, the study examines the effect of the training set size on retrieval performance, demonstrating that larger training sets enhance KATE's ability to retrieve relevant examples, thereby improving the accuracy of GPT-3's responses. Overall, the findings underscore KATE's potential in leveraging in-context examples to enhance performance in both table-to-text generation and QA tasks.
