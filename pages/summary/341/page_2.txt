This paper investigates the impact of using different in-context examples on the performance of GPT-3 in natural language understanding and generation tasks. The key finding is that in-context examples that are closer to the test sample in the embedding space lead to better performance. Building on this observation and the success of retrieval-augmented models, the authors propose a method to utilize the nearest neighbors of a test sample from the training data as in-context examples. 

The proposed approach is evaluated across various tasks, including sentiment analysis, table-to-text generation, and open-domain question answering. The results indicate that retrieval-based in-context examples significantly enhance GPT-3's few-shot learning capabilities compared to a random sampling baseline. Even with fewer examples, this method improves performance, highlighting the importance of the specific sentence encoders used for retrieval. Fine-tuning these encoders on relevant datasets further boosts performance, particularly in question answering tasks.

The contributions of the paper include: 
1) A first step in understanding GPT-3's sensitivity to in-context example selection.
2) Introduction of a retrieval module that identifies semantically similar examples, outperforming random sampling.
3) Evidence that fine-tuning retrieval models on task-specific data yields better results.
4) Improvement in GPT-3's performance with an increased number of retrievable examples.

The methodology involves treating in-context learning as a conditional text generation problem, where the model generates predictions based on a context string formed by concatenating training instances and their labels. The paper emphasizes the computational challenges of fine-tuning GPT-3 directly, advocating instead for its use in an in-context learning framework to leverage its few-shot capabilities effectively.
