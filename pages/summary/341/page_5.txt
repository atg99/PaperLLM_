The paper discusses various methods for enhancing the performance of models in tasks such as table-to-text generation, question answering, and sentiment analysis using in-context examples and retrieval techniques. The authors focus on using a specific number of in-context examples, finding that three examples suffice for optimal performance in certain scenarios.

For table-to-text generation, the evaluation relies on the ToTTo dataset, with performance metrics including BLEU and PARENT. The authors implement a preprocessing step to accommodate the token limit of GPT-3, which is set at 2048 tokens.

In the question answering task, the model generates answers to factual questions, evaluated using the Exact Match score across three benchmarks: Natural Questions, Web Questions, and TriviaQA. The number of in-context examples varies based on the benchmark to fit within the token limit.

The paper introduces baseline methods like random sampling and k-nearest neighbor (kNN) approaches to compare against the proposed KATE method. The random sampling selects examples from the training set, while kNN leverages the closest retrieved examples for predictions.

In sentiment analysis evaluations, KATE consistently outperforms the random selection baseline. However, fine-tuning the sentence encoder on dissimilar tasks, such as NLI and STS-B datasets, may degrade performance, indicating that task alignment is crucial for optimal results. The findings underscore the importance of carefully selecting in-context examples and the impact of model training on performance in various language tasks.
