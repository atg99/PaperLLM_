Results show that increasing the number of in-context examples correlates with improved Exact Match (EM) scores, indicating that more examples generally enhance model performance. However, the specific impact varies based on the selection method used, with KATE demonstrating better results than random selection.

Furthermore, the exploration of example order reveals that while the default ordering typically performs well, the reverse order yields the best results for the NQ dataset. This suggests that proximity in the embedding space may aid the model in leveraging relevant information more effectively. However, the findings are not universally applicable across datasets, as performance can differ in others like WQ and TriviaQA.

Overall, the study concludes that while the ordering of in-context examples can influence performance, the choice of examples and their relevance to the task remains paramount for achieving optimal results in retrieval-based question answering.
