The paper presents a method for selecting in-context examples using the k-nearest neighbors (kNN) approach, specifically focusing on the selection of semantically similar training instances to improve various natural language processing tasks. The algorithm outlined employs a sentence encoder to map sentences into a latent semantic space, where similarities are calculated to identify the most relevant examples.

Two categories of sentence encoders are discussed: 
1. Generally pre-trained encoders like BERT, RoBERTa, and XLNet, which are trained on diverse tasks and provide rich semantic embeddings.
2. Task-specific fine-tuned encoders, which are optimized on specific datasets, enhancing their ability to assess semantic similarities effectively.

The experimental setup applies the kNN in-context selection method to sentiment classification, table-to-text generation, and question answering, utilizing various datasets as outlined in a provided table. The paper emphasizes the significance of selecting in-context examples from the training set, with evaluations conducted on development or test sets depending on the dataset.

For sentiment classification, the study adopts a transfer learning approach, treating one dataset as the training set while evaluating on another. Specifically, in-context examples are selected from the SST-2 dataset for predictions on the IMDB test set. The performance is benchmarked using accuracy metrics, with a focus on the impact of using fine-tuned encoders on the overall performance of the kNN method.

Overall, the paper highlights the importance of selecting appropriate sentence encoders and demonstrates the effectiveness of the kNN in-context example selection in enhancing the performance of natural language processing tasks.
