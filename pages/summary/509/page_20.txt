The paper discusses various aspects of graph-level tasks, particularly focusing on graph classification and regression using graph neural networks (GNNs). The objective function for training is formalized, where the model minimizes a loss function based on the performance of node embeddings aggregated into graph embeddings. The paper highlights that while graph classification often uses inductive supervised learning, current self-supervised learning (SSL) methods fall short of achieving the performance levels of supervised methods, such as the Graph Isomorphism Network (GIN) baseline.

Key observations from the performance comparison indicate that methods in the unsupervised representation learning (URL) group tend to underperform compared to supervised counterparts. Moreover, cross-scale contrast-based methods demonstrate better results than same-scale methods, suggesting an improvement in global representations. The involvement of graph labels does not consistently enhance encoder performance, raising questions about negative transfer phenomena in graph-level SSL. The need for unified benchmarks for evaluating graph-level SSL methods is emphasized due to the inconsistency of datasets used across different studies.

The paper categorizes commonly used datasets into citation networks, co-purchase networks, social networks, and bio-chemical graphs, providing descriptions and statistics for each type. Citation networks represent academic relationships, co-purchase networks indicate product purchasing behavior, and social networks illustrate user interactions on platforms like Reddit and IMDB. Each dataset is characterized by node features and labels relevant to their respective domains, underscoring the diversity and application of these datasets in graph learning tasks.
