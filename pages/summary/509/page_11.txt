The paper discusses graph contrastive learning, focusing on subgraph sampling techniques and various methods for maximizing mutual information (MI) between graph instances. It identifies several strategies for generating subsets of nodes (V0), including uniform sampling, random walks, and importance-based sampling. Hybrid augmentations are emphasized, with methods like GRACE and MVGRL employing edge dropping and node feature masking to create diverse contrastive views.

Graph contrastive learning is categorized into same-scale and cross-scale approaches. Same-scale methods are further divided into node-level and graph-level contrasts. Node-level methods aim to learn representations by pulling nodes with similar contextual information closer together, often utilizing random walks for context extraction. Examples include DeepWalk, node2vec, and GraphSAGE, which extend these ideas to attributed graphs.

Modern node-level contrastive methods leverage richer semantic information through graph augmentations. Techniques such as GRACE, GCA, and GROC explore various augmentation strategies to enhance representation learning. Additionally, approaches like GraphCL(N) adapt the successful SimCLR framework to graph data, while BGRL utilizes knowledge distillation to focus on graph instances, reducing the need for negative sampling. Overall, the paper emphasizes the evolution of graph contrastive learning methods and their reliance on innovative augmentation strategies to improve performance.
