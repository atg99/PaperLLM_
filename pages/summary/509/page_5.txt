The paper discusses various schemes for self-supervised learning (SSL) in graph representations, outlining three main approaches: Pre-training and Fine-tuning (PF), Joint Learning (JL), and Unsupervised Representation Learning (URL). 

In the PF scheme, the encoder is initially pre-trained on unsupervised tasks, setting the stage for fine-tuning with labeled downstream tasks, where the encoder's parameters are optimized based on specific objectives. The PF approach allows for flexibility in using either the same or different datasets for pre-training and fine-tuning.

The JL approach involves simultaneous training of the encoder with both pretext and downstream tasks, integrating their loss functions to enhance performance on downstream tasks while leveraging self-supervised learning as a form of regularization. 

The URL scheme also begins with pre-training but differs in that the encoder's parameters are frozen during the downstream task learning phase, posing additional challenges due to the lack of supervision in the initial stage. 

Additionally, the paper categorizes downstream tasks based on prediction targets into node-level, link-level, and graph-level tasks, highlighting the different objectives and methodologies associated with each level. 

Overall, the paper emphasizes the diverse properties and applications of various SSL methods, their implementation complexities, and the ongoing challenges in developing effective joint learning frameworks.
