This section of the paper outlines the main characteristics of various contrast-based graph semi-supervised learning (SSL) approaches, categorized into node-level same-scale (NSC), graph-level same-scale (GSC), patch-global cross-scale (PGCC), and context-global cross-scale (CGCC) methods. Each approach is detailed with attributes such as the pretext task, downstream task level, training scheme, data type of the graph, graph augmentation techniques, and objective functions used.

Key approaches in NSC include DeepWalk and node2vec, both employing the SkipGram objective function. Other notable methods such as GraphSAGE and GRACE utilize different training schemes and objective functions like JSD and InfoNCE. The GSC category features approaches like GraphCL(G) and DACL, which emphasize graph augmentations and maximize mutual information (MI) between node embeddings and graph representations.

In PGCC, methods like DGI and InfoGraph focus on contrasting node embeddings with graph-level representations to enhance learning. The paper also highlights the role of hard negative sampling in improving the contrastive learning tasks.

Finally, the section introduces context-global contrast, which is a prevalent design within cross-scale graph contrastive learning, emphasizing the need for effective representation learning techniques that can handle diverse graph structures and augmentations.
