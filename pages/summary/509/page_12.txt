The paper discusses contrast-based methods for self-supervised learning (SSL) in graph representations, focusing on two primary categories: same-scale contrast and cross-scale contrast. 

In same-scale contrast, various augmentations of an input graph are generated, allowing for discrimination at node or graph levels. Techniques like SelfGNN utilize different graph augmentations, including graph diffusion and node feature splitting, while methods such as Barlow Twins and G-BT minimize dissimilarity between representations of augmented views without negative samples. The graph-level same-scale contrast involves optimizing graph representations through contrastive frameworks like GraphCL and DACL, which enhance representation learning by pulling closer graph-level embeddings of different views.

Cross-scale contrast distinguishes representations across varying graph structures, such as node versus graph levels. This category is divided into patch-global and context-global contrasts. Patch-global contrast focuses on contrasting node-level embeddings with graph-level representations, illustrated by methods like DGI, which maximizes mutual information between different scales. GIC extends this concept by clustering nodes and optimizing their proximity to cluster summaries.

Overall, the paper highlights the evolution and effectiveness of contrastive learning methods in capturing both local and global semantic information from graph data, illustrating their applicability in various settings, including heterogeneous and multiplex graphs.
