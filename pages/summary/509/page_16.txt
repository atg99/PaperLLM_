The paper discusses advancements in graph self-supervised learning (SSL) methods, particularly focusing on their empirical evaluation in node and graph classification tasks. It highlights various approaches, including bi-level mutual information maximization and multi-task pretraining of Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) to enhance representation learning.

In the empirical study section, the authors compare the performance of different SSL methods across benchmark datasets like Cora, Citeseer, Pubmed, and PPI. Two learning paradigms are examined: semi-supervised transductive learning and supervised inductive learning. The results indicate that early generative methods and random walk-based approaches underperform compared to more recent graph SSL methods that utilize well-designed pretext tasks. The paper also notes that methods employing advanced contrastive learning techniques do not consistently outperform traditional models, suggesting that cross-domain methods may not always yield improvements.

The analysis observes that hybrid methods, which integrate multiple pretext tasks, often outperform supervised baselines, implying that diverse supervision signals enhance learning. The authors conclude that access to label information significantly improves performance, highlighting the importance of integrating SSL approaches in practical applications like recommender systems, where graph SSL can address challenges such as cold-start issues and enhance model pre-training. Additional resources for benchmark datasets and open-source implementations are provided to facilitate further research.
