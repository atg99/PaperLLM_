The paper discusses various methods for learning graph representations through contrastive learning, focusing on mutual information (MI) estimation and the optimization of graph-level embeddings. It introduces a formula for minimizing a contrastive loss based on contextual subgraphs sampled from an augmented input graph. The representations of these subgraphs are denoted as ~hs, while the overall graph representation is ~g. 

Several methods are highlighted, including BiGI, which employs a node-level representation approach to maximize MI between local contextual and global representations. HTC aims to maximize MI between full-graph and contextual embeddings derived from sampled subgraphs. MICRO-Graph introduces a motif learning-based sampling strategy to create semantically informative subgraphs.

SUGAR is discussed as a method that utilizes reinforcement learning to select informative subgraphs from a larger candidate set and contrasts their embeddings with a generated graph representation. 

The paper also elaborates on MI estimation techniques used in contrastive learning, including Jensen-Shannon divergence (JSD), which provides an efficient lower bound for MI, and the noise-contrastive estimator (InfoNCE) that incorporates both positive and negative pairs. A triplet loss is mentioned, although it does not guarantee maximization of MI. Additionally, BYOL-inspired methods that do not rely on negative samples are described.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of MI estimation in contrastive learning frameworks for graph representations, detailing various strategies and their applications in enhancing graph-level embeddings.
