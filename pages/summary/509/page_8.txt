In clustering-based methods, nodes are grouped into clusters based on their features or structural characteristics. The mapping function assigns pseudo labels to nodes, allowing models to classify them accordingly. Representative approaches include Node Clustering, which uses feature-based clustering, and Graph Partitioning, which minimizes inter-cluster connections. These methods leverage algorithms like K-means and attention-based aggregation to refine clusters and generate pseudo labels.

On the other hand, pair relation-based methods utilize the relationships between node pairs as a supervision signal. These approaches focus on measuring distances and similarities between nodes, employing functions such as pairwise distances and centrality scores to inform the learning process.

The paper emphasizes the importance of these auxiliary property methods in enhancing the performance of graph SSL by providing meaningful pseudo labels and regression targets, which are crucial for training models effectively. Overall, the proposed frameworks aim to improve understanding and representation of graph properties through self-supervised techniques.
