This paper discusses generation-based semi-supervised learning (SSL) approaches for graph data, categorizing them into feature generation (FG) and structure generation (SG) methods. Key characteristics of various approaches are summarized in a table, highlighting their pretext tasks, downstream task levels, training schemes, data types, input data perturbation, and generation targets.

The Graph Autoencoder (GAE) is presented as a fundamental structure generation method, utilizing a graph convolutional network (GCN) to generate node embeddings and recover the adjacency matrix through a binary cross-entropy (BCE) loss function. To mitigate issues related to sparse adjacency matrices, strategies such as re-weighting and sub-sampling are recommended. Variations of GAE, including Variational GAE (VGAE) and others like SIG-VAE and ARGA, introduce enhancements such as variational inference and adversarial regularization to improve generative representation learning.

In contrast to full graph reconstruction, some methods focus on reconstructing masked edges. Techniques like Denoising Link Reconstruction and EdgeMask employ edge perturbation strategies to recover missing connections, while Zhu et al. implement dual perturbation methods to enhance recovery tasks. The paper notes that structure generation methods tend to capture more detailed pair-level information, whereas feature generation methods focus on node-level knowledge.

Additionally, auxiliary property-based methods are introduced, which derive supervision signals from graph properties. These methods align closely with supervised learning paradigms but rely on self-generated pseudo labels rather than human-annotated ones, thus reducing costs. Two sub-categories under auxiliary property-based methods are identified: classification-based and regression-based methods, with the former employing discrete pseudo labels and cross-entropy loss for training.

Overall, the paper highlights the diversity and evolution of SSL techniques in graph-based learning, emphasizing the balance between generative tasks and the utilization of auxiliary properties for enhanced learning outcomes.
