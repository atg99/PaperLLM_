The paper discusses various self-supervised learning (SSL) methods, particularly focusing on hybrid approaches that combine multiple pretext tasks. The Barlow Twins Loss is highlighted as a simpler alternative to existing methods like BYOL, emphasizing redundancy reduction in representations derived from two views of data. The paper outlines how hybrid methods integrate diverse pretext tasks in a multi-task learning framework, allowing for a combination of different supervision signals. 

Key examples of hybrid methods include GPT-GNN and Graph-Bert, which merge feature and structure generation tasks for graph neural networks (GNNs). These methods utilize multiple loss functions to enhance the training process. GMI and CG3 are mentioned for their joint learning objectives that combine generative and contrastive tasks, optimizing the agreement between node embeddings and their neighbors' features, as well as minimizing adjacency matrix reconstruction errors.

The paper concludes by noting the effectiveness of integrating various contrast-based tasks, as seen in approaches like GraphLoG and HDMI, which utilize multiple contrastive objectives to enrich the learning signals and improve representation learning across different graph structures.
