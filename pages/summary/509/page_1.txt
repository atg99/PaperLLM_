This paper presents a comprehensive survey on graph self-supervised learning (SSL), addressing the limitations of traditional (semi-) supervised learning methods that heavily rely on labeled data. The authors emphasize the challenges associated with manual label collection, including high costs, poor generalization, and vulnerability to adversarial attacks. In contrast, SSL leverages pretext tasks that allow models to learn from unlabeled data, thus enhancing performance, generalization, and robustness.

The authors categorize existing graph SSL approaches into four types based on their pretext task objectives: generation-based, auxiliary property-based, contrast-based, and hybrid approaches. They also discuss various applications of graph SSL across different fields, highlight commonly used datasets and evaluation benchmarks, and provide performance comparisons along with available open-source codes.

The paper concludes by identifying ongoing challenges in graph SSL and proposing potential future research directions, underscoring the unique complexities of applying SSL techniques to graph-structured data compared to traditional domains like computer vision and natural language processing.
