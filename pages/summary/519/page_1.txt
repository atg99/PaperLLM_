The paper presents a framework for understanding self-supervised learning (SSL) from a multi-view perspective, highlighting its effectiveness in representation learning without the need for labeled data. The authors argue that SSL leverages self-defined signals, such as augmented images, as additional views of the data, allowing the model to extract relevant information while discarding irrelevant details.

The study builds on existing SSL methods like SimCLR, which use contrastive loss between original and augmented images. The authors introduce an information-theoretical framework that emphasizes the importance of task-relevant information extraction and the elimination of task-irrelevant information. They propose a composite objective that merges contrastive and predictive learning objectives, adding a term specifically designed to filter out irrelevant information.

To validate their theoretical insights, the authors conduct controlled experiments assessing the impact of the proposed composite objectives on learning efficacy. They also explore the applicability of their framework beyond scenarios where cross-view redundancy is apparent, suggesting broader implications for SSL methodologies.

The paper contributes to the understanding of SSL by providing a formal analysis of its mechanisms and proposing new objectives that enhance its effectiveness in various downstream tasks, thereby reinforcing the potential of self-supervised approaches in machine learning.
