This paper, presented at ICLR 2021, explores the generalization error of self-supervised learning (SSL) representations through theoretical and empirical analyses. It emphasizes the challenge of quantifying conditional information and proposes a Lagrangian relaxation approach to learn representations with minimal self-supervision. The study features two sets of experiments: one focused on visual representation learning using the Omniglot dataset, and the other on visual-textual representation learning with the MS COCO dataset.

In the first experiment, the authors demonstrate how self-supervised signals are constructed from images of different characters, ensuring that the shared information contributes to task-relevant labels. They employ various SSL objectives, particularly examining the impact of the inverse predictive learning objective alongside contrastive learning. Results indicate that integrating the inverse predictive objective can enhance performance, although it is sensitive to hyper-parameter tuning. Additionally, comparisons between different learning objectives reveal that contrastive learning outperforms forward predictive learning in terms of accuracy and training efficiency.

The second experiment shifts to a more complex setting involving multi-labeled images, suggesting that achieving similar performance on larger datasets requires significantly more computational resources. The findings underline the effectiveness of SSL objectives in improving representation learning while providing insights into their interactions and generalization capabilities across different modalities.
