In this paper presented at ICLR 2021, the authors explore a framework for self-supervised learning (SSL) that focuses on efficiently extracting task-relevant information while discarding task-irrelevant information. The key contributions are outlined through information diagrams that illustrate the minimization of entropy and maximization of mutual information between the input data and self-supervised signals. The authors argue that learned representations can achieve two objectives: extracting all task-relevant information with a potential loss and discarding task-irrelevant information with a fixed gap.

The analysis connects previous SSL methods, particularly in contrastive and predictive learning, and suggests that combining these approaches can lead to improved performance and reduced overfitting. The paper introduces a new objective aimed at discarding task-irrelevant information that can be integrated with existing SSL objectives.

The authors conduct experiments across two sets: one where the multi-view assumption holds, comparing various SSL objective compositions, and another where inputs and self-supervised signals come from different modalities, challenging the multi-view assumption. This investigation aims to assess the generalization capabilities of different SSL objectives.

The framework employs various notations for random variables and mutual information, laying the groundwork for understanding the relationship between inputs and self-supervised signals in different SSL contexts. The authors provide a table that illustrates how different frameworks define inputs and self-supervised signals, highlighting the diversity within SSL methodologies. Overall, the paper aims to advance the understanding and application of self-supervised learning by addressing the extraction of relevant information in a structured manner.
