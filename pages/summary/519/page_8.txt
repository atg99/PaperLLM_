This paper presents research on cross-modality self-supervised learning (SSL) using the MS COCO dataset, focusing on the effectiveness of different configurations of BERT and ResNet models for visual-textual representation training. The study evaluates the performance of these configurations based on Micro ROC-AUC and Subset Accuracy metrics on the MS COCO validation set, reporting results from multiple trials.

Key findings indicate that SSL can effectively bridge different modalities, as evidenced by pre-trained ResNet models paired with BERT outperforming ResNet alone. Notably, the representation learned from a pre-trained ResNet combined with a raw BERT shows improved performance, suggesting that visual understanding benefits more from pre-trained models. The research also highlights that combining the LCL and LIP objectives enhances representation quality by filtering out task-irrelevant information.

The paper discusses the sensitivity of the hyper-parameter ϕIP in the learning process, advocating for an optimization perspective to optimize performance. It suggests a Lagrangian Relaxation approach for balancing the objectives of maximizing mutual information while minimizing conditional entropy, with empirical evidence showing that optimal performance is achieved when the scale of LIP is one-tenth that of LCL.

The authors relate their findings to prior work in the field, noting that previous studies have established the benefits of contrastive learning and representation learning for downstream tasks. They differentiate their approach by emphasizing the interaction between input and self-supervised signals as corresponding data views, while acknowledging assumptions made in earlier works regarding independence between these views.

Overall, this research contributes to the understanding of self-supervised learning across modalities, offering insights into optimizing representation learning through strategic model configurations and hyper-parameter tuning.
