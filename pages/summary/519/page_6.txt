The paper presents a theoretical analysis of the Bayes error rate for downstream classification in the context of self-supervised learning (SSL). The Bayes error rate represents the irreducible error when inferring labels from learned representations. The authors establish a relationship between this error rate and mutual information, using empirical samples to estimate mutual information from the distribution of representations and labels.

Proposition 1 outlines that a neural network can approximate the mutual information with a high probability given a sufficient number of samples. The main result, Theorem 3, states that the Bayes error rate is minimized when the estimated mutual information is large, when more samples are used, and when the task-irrelevant information is low. This suggests that maximizing mutual information is beneficial for learning representations that enhance downstream task performance.

Theorem 4 extends this analysis to self-supervised learned representations, indicating that a smaller upper bound on the Bayes error rate is achievable when the redundancy between the input and the self-supervised signal is minimal. This implies that self-supervised representations can perform better when the redundancy is low.

Finally, the paper sets the stage for controlled experiments to empirically support the theoretical findings and to evaluate different SSL objectives. The authors emphasize the challenges in accurately quantifying information, referencing existing literature on the topic.
