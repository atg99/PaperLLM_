The paper discusses the effectiveness of various self-supervised learning (SSL) objectives and their compositions in training visual representations, specifically using datasets like Omniglot. The results indicate that combining different objectives can enhance test accuracy. For instance, the combination of LCL (Latent Contrastive Learning) with LFP (Latent Forward Prediction) and LIP (Latent Information Prediction) achieved the highest accuracy of 86.17 after 9000 epochs.

The authors emphasize the practical implementation of contrastive and predictive learning objectives, recommending Contrastive Predictive Coding (CPC) and exploring different mutual information approximations. They also highlight the use of Gaussian distributions for LFP/LIP, while discussing alternatives like JS divergence for contrastive learning.

The paper introduces various reconstruction loss functions, including Mean Square Error (MSE), Binary Cross Entropy (BCE), and Reverse Binary Cross Entropy (RevBCE). The experiments reveal that RevBCE outperforms the others, leading to its selection as the primary reconstruction loss in their methodology.

Moreover, the authors propose a novel self-supervised signal construction strategy that distinguishes between input and self-supervised signals based on drawing styles and image augmentations. This approach contrasts with traditional methods that rely solely on augmentations. Experimental results demonstrate that their strategy significantly improves generalization performance on test sets compared to conventional methods.

Overall, the paper underscores the importance of selecting appropriate SSL objectives and construction strategies to enhance the generalization capabilities of visual representation models.
