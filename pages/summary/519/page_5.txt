This paper discusses advancements in self-supervised learning (SSL) techniques, focusing on two key strategies: Forward Predictive Learning (FPL) and Inverse Predictive Learning (IPL). FPL aims to extract task-relevant information by maximizing the log conditional likelihood of self-supervised signals given learned representations. This is framed as a predictive learning objective, ensuring that the learned representation can reconstruct the self-supervised signal effectively. 

In contrast, IPL is concerned with discarding task-irrelevant information by maximizing the log conditional likelihood of learned representations given the self-supervised signals. This approach ensures that the learned representations focus on relevant features while minimizing noise from irrelevant data.

The paper introduces a composite loss function that combines these two learning objectives along with a contrastive loss to simultaneously extract relevant information and discard irrelevant data. This composite loss leverages hyper-parameters to balance the contributions of each component, facilitating improved performance in downstream tasks.

Overall, the authors emphasize the importance of designing SSL frameworks that effectively manage the trade-off between relevant and irrelevant information to enhance learning outcomes, especially in high-dimensional data scenarios.
