The paper discusses the objectives of learning minimal and sufficient representations in the context of machine learning. It outlines how previous methods, such as those by Tishby et al. and Achille & Soatto, aimed to maximize the mutual information between the learned representation and the downstream task while minimizing the mutual information between the representation and the input data. The authors propose an alternative approach that focuses on minimizing the conditional entropy of the representation given the task, thereby establishing a relationship between mutual information minimization and entropy minimization.

The paper emphasizes that, under the constraint of maximizing mutual information with the task, minimizing the mutual information between the representation and the input is equivalent to minimizing the conditional mutual information. The authors further relate this concept to self-supervised signals, noting that similar interchangeability exists when the transformation from input to representation is deterministic.

Additionally, the authors critique the approach of directly minimizing conditional mutual information, which can lead to optimization instability. They reference prior work that uses Gaussian assumptions to simplify this process and present upper bounds on the objectives.

The paper includes proofs for key theorems that reinforce the deterministic nature of their proposed framework and the implications for the relationships between the variables involved in their model.
