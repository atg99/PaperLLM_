The paper presents two theorems regarding the information captured in supervised and self-supervised learned representations. 

Theorem 5 establishes that supervised learned representations contain all task-relevant information present in the input data, while self-supervised representations may contain all task-relevant information but with a potential loss. This is formalized as I(X;T) = I(Zsup_X;T) = I(Zsupmin_X;T) ≥ I(Zssl_X;T) ≥ I(Zsslmin_X;T) ≥ I(X;T) - ϵ_info. The proof is divided into two parts: for supervised representations, the Data Processing Inequality (DPI) implies that these representations maximize the mutual information with the task (I(ZX;T)), equating it to the input's mutual information (I(X;T)). For self-supervised representations, similar reasoning shows that they retain less task-relevant information due to the potential loss ϵ_info.

Theorem 6 addresses task-irrelevant information, asserting that the sufficient self-supervised representation (I(Zssl_X;T)) contains more task-irrelevant information than the sufficient and minimal self-supervised representation (I(Zsslmin_X;T)). The latter retains a fixed amount of information (I(X;SjT)) that is essential and cannot be discarded. This relationship is formalized as I(Zssl_X;X|T) = I(X;SjT) + I(Zssl_X;X|S;T) ≥ I(Zsslmin_X;X|T) = I(X;SjT) ≥ I(Zsupmin_X;X|T) = 0.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the distinctions in information retention between supervised and self-supervised learning paradigms, particularly in terms of task relevance and irrelevance.
