The paper investigates the effectiveness of multi-view learning in self-supervised representation learning, specifically focusing on the relationship between input data and self-supervised signals. It highlights that frameworks treating the input and self-supervised signal as corresponding views yield better downstream performance compared to those that do not. The authors introduce a multi-view assumption, asserting that the self-supervised signal is largely redundant for task-relevant information, allowing for effective representation learning. They provide examples illustrating cases where this assumption holds, such as using image augmentations that maintain content while altering style. However, they also acknowledge scenarios where the assumption fails, particularly when drastic augmentations significantly alter content or context.

The paper further explores the concept of learning minimal and sufficient representations for self-supervision, building on the Information Bottleneck method. It defines objectives for obtaining such representations, emphasizing the importance of minimizing the randomness in representations while maximizing mutual information with the task or self-supervised signal. The authors propose a framework to associate supervised and self-supervised learned representations, asserting that while supervised representations capture all task-relevant information, self-supervised representations may incur a potential loss of information. The findings suggest that effective representation learning hinges on the balance between maintaining relevant information and reducing complexity.
