The paper presents two significant theorems regarding Bayes error rates for learned representations in machine learning, specifically focusing on arbitrary and self-supervised learned representations.

Theorem 7 establishes a relationship for the Bayes error rate Pe in the context of arbitrary learned representations ZX. It restates a prior theorem, showing that Pe is bounded by an exponential function involving entropy H(T) and mutual information terms I(X;S|T) and I(Z;X|S,T). The proof utilizes inequalities from Feder & Merhav (1994) to derive that Pe is constrained between 0 and 1 - 1/jTj, ultimately concluding that Pe can be approximated using the provided expression that incorporates a perturbation term O(sqrt(d + log(1/epsilon))/n).

Theorem 8 focuses on self-supervised learned representations, comparing their Bayes error rates Psup_e, Pssl_e, and Psslmine. It demonstrates that the Bayes error rates for self-supervised representations are bounded by terms involving the supervised error rate and the size of the target set jTj. The proof combines results from previous theorems and inequalities, leading to upper and lower bounds for the self-supervised learned representations’ Bayes error rates, indicating that these rates are influenced by the supervised error rate and the logarithm of the target set size.

Overall, the paper provides a theoretical framework for understanding the performance of different learned representations, highlighting the interplay between supervised and self-supervised learning paradigms in terms of their Bayes error rates.
