This paper explores self-supervised learning, emphasizing the extraction of task-relevant information while discarding task-irrelevant data. The authors critique previous assumptions regarding independence between tasks and views, arguing that these assumptions may not hold in practice. They propose a method that minimizes conditional entropy to reduce redundant information across views, contrasting it with existing approaches like the Multi-view Information Bottleneck and InfoMin, which focus on mutual information.

The paper highlights that self-supervised learning can effectively extract relevant information, albeit with some loss, and emphasizes the importance of understanding the balance between relevant and irrelevant information for improving performance in contrastive and predictive learning frameworks. The authors plan to connect their findings with recent self-supervised learning methods that do not fit neatly into their analysis.

In conclusion, the work provides both theoretical and empirical insights into self-supervised learning, suggesting that it can enhance our understanding of its effectiveness. The study is supported by various funding sources, including the NSF and NIH, and acknowledges NVIDIA for GPU support.
