The paper evaluates the effectiveness of Graph Transformer Networks (GTNs) for node classification on heterogeneous graphs using three datasets: DBLP, ACM, and IMDB. Each dataset has multiple types of nodes and edges, with DBLP and ACM focusing on citation networks and IMDB on movies. The datasets are characterized by various statistics, including the number of nodes, edges, edge types, features, and data splits for training, validation, and testing.

The implementation details specify that an embedding dimension of 64 was used across all methods, with the Adam optimizer employed to maximize performance through optimized hyperparameters. Random walk-based models were configured with specific settings for walk length and negative samples, while GCN, GAT, and HAN parameters were optimized using validation data. For GTN, the number of layers varied by dataset.

The paper compares GTNs with traditional random walk-based methods such as DeepWalk and metapath2vec, as well as state-of-the-art graph neural network (GNN) methods like GCN, GAT, and HAN. While DeepWalk operates on the entire heterogeneous graph, metapath2vec leverages meta-path based random walks. The GNN methods were adapted to account for the directed nature of the datasets, with GCN and GAT applied to the entire graph and HAN tested on sub-graphs formed by meta-paths.

Results demonstrate that GTN outperforms all other methods across the datasets in node classification tasks, affirming the effectiveness of the representations learned from the new graph structures. This supports the research questions regarding the performance comparison of GTN against existing methods.
