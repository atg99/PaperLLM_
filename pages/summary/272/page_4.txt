The paper discusses the Graph Transformer Networks (GTNs), which are designed to automatically learn meta-paths from heterogeneous graphs, contrasting with previous methods that relied on manually defined meta-paths. The core innovation lies in the Graph Transformer Layer, which facilitates the soft selection of adjacency matrices from a set of candidate matrices, enabling the generation of new meta-path graphs through matrix multiplication.

The process involves two main components: first, the GT layer selects two graph structures, Q1 and Q2, from the candidate matrices. Then, it computes a new graph structure through the multiplication of these two matrices, normalizing the result using the degree matrix for stability. The soft selection is achieved through 1x1 convolutions with parameters learned via a softmax function.

The study highlights that GTNs can learn arbitrary-length meta-paths by employing multiple GT layers, where the adjacency matrix for these paths is derived from a weighted sum of all possible edge types. However, a challenge arises as increasing the number of GT layers can lead to longer meta-paths, which may overlook the significance of original edges. To address this, the authors propose incorporating identity paths to capture both short and long meta-paths, thus enhancing the flexibility and utility of the GTN architecture in various applications.
