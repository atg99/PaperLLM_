The paper introduces Graph Transformer Networks (GTNs), a novel architecture designed to enhance node classification by learning multiple meta-path adjacency matrices through GT layers. GTNs generate new graph structures, enabling graph convolution similar to Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs). By stacking l GT layers, GTNs can learn meta-paths of varying lengths, with the flexibility to adaptively produce these paths based on the dataset.

The architecture integrates multiple node representations derived from different meta-path graphs, which are concatenated to improve classification performance. The model utilizes intermediate adjacency tensors to compute the meta-paths and applies GCNs across each channel of the generated meta-path tensor. The final node representations are used for classification, with a cross-entropy loss function employed for nodes with known labels.

The experimental section aims to assess the effectiveness of GTNs compared to state-of-the-art models, focusing on three research questions: the efficacy of the new graph structures for node representation learning, the adaptability of GTNs in producing variable-length meta-paths, and the interpretability of the importance of each meta-path from the generated adjacency matrices.
