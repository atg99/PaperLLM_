In this paper, we introduced Graph Transformer Networks (GTNs) aimed at learning node representations in heterogeneous graphs. Our method reformulates a heterogeneous graph into multiple new graphs based on meta-paths, accommodating various edge types and lengths, thereby enhancing node representation through convolution on these newly constructed meta-path graphs. This approach achieves state-of-the-art performance in node classification across three benchmark datasets without relying on predefined meta-paths from domain knowledge.

Furthermore, the versatility of our Graph Transformer layers allows for integration with existing Graph Neural Networks (GNNs), suggesting that our framework can autonomously optimize graph structures for specific data and tasks without manual intervention. Future research directions include examining the effectiveness of GT layers in conjunction with different GNN architectures beyond Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) and exploring the application of GTNs to other network analysis tasks like link prediction and graph classification.
