The references listed encompass a range of significant contributions to the field of graph representation learning and network analysis. Key works include:

1. **Node2vec** (Grover and Leskovec, 2016) introduces a scalable method for learning node features in networks, focusing on flexible random walks.
2. **Inductive representation learning** on large graphs (Hamilton et al., 2017) presents techniques that allow models to generalize to unseen nodes.
3. **Graph convolutional networks** (Kipf and Welling, 2017) provide a framework for semi-supervised classification on graph-structured data, making use of local graph structures.
4. **DeepWalk** (Perozzi et al., 2014) explores online learning of social representations through random walks and skip-gram models.
5. **Variational graph auto-encoders** (Kipf and Welling, 2016) propose a probabilistic approach to graph representation learning.

Additional contributions include hierarchical models for stock movement prediction (Kim et al., 2019), geometric deep learning methods (Monti et al., 2016), and advancements in attention mechanisms for graph pooling (Lee et al., 2019). The references collectively highlight the evolution of techniques for learning from graph-structured data, emphasizing scalability, inductive learning, and the integration of neural architectures tailored for relational data.
