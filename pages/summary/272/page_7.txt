The evaluation results for the node classification task, presented in Table 2, indicate that GNN-based methods such as GCN, GAT, HAN, and GTN outperform random walk-based methods like DeepWalk and metapath2vec. Among these, GAT generally surpasses GCN due to its ability to assign varying weights to neighboring nodes, unlike GCN which averages them. Notably, despite being a modified version of GAT, HAN often underperforms, suggesting that the predefined meta-paths may detract from its effectiveness.

The proposed GTN model achieves the highest performance across all datasets, even with a single GCN layer, in contrast to other models that utilize multiple layers. This suggests that GTN is effective at learning a new graph structure that incorporates useful meta-paths for enhanced node representation. The GTN's flexibility in assigning variable weights to edges stands in contrast to the constant weights used in HAN.

An ablation study revealed that removing the identity matrix from the candidate adjacency matrix (resulting in the GTN-I model) consistently leads to poorer performance, particularly on the IMDB dataset. This indicates that shorter, effective meta-paths, such as MDM, are more beneficial in certain contexts.

The paper also delves into the interpretability of Graph Transformer Networks (GTNs), detailing how to calculate the importance of each meta-path through the GT layers. The adjacency matrix generated in each layer is a weighted combination of previous outputs and input adjacency matrices, allowing GTNs to consider all meta-paths, from original edges to longer paths. The attention scores assigned to edge types at each layer help determine the contributions of different meta-paths, enhancing the interpretability of the model's outputs.
