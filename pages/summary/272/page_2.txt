In this paper, we introduce the Graph Transformer Network (GTN), which automates the transformation of heterogeneous graphs into task-specific meta-path graphs, allowing for end-to-end learning of node representations. GTNs draw inspiration from Spatial Transformer Networks, which learn spatial transformations for images. A key challenge addressed by GTNs is the arbitrary length and types of edges in meta-paths, exemplified by author classification in citation networks where paths such as Author-Paper-Author (APA) or Author-Paper-Conference-Paper-Author (APCPA) can be beneficial.

Our contributions are threefold: (i) we present a framework for GTNs that identifies useful meta-paths and multi-hop connections to enhance node representation learning, (ii) our model provides interpretable graph generation that sheds light on effective meta-paths for predictions, and (iii) we demonstrate that node representations learned through GTNs outperform state-of-the-art methods that incorporate domain knowledge across three benchmark datasets for node classification on heterogeneous graphs.

In the context of related works, we discuss the evolution of Graph Neural Networks (GNNs), which can be categorized into spectral and non-spectral methods. While traditional methods often rely on hand-crafted features or solely on graph structure for node classification, recent advances have shifted towards representation learning through random walks and deep learning techniques. However, most existing GNNs are designed for homogeneous graphs, necessitating a two-stage approach for heterogeneous graphs that often relies on manual meta-path selection. Our GTN framework addresses these limitations by learning transformations automatically, thus enhancing performance and flexibility in heterogeneous graph analysis.
