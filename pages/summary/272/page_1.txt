The paper introduces Graph Transformer Networks (GTNs) as a novel approach to address the limitations of traditional Graph Neural Networks (GNNs), which typically operate on fixed and homogeneous graphs. GTNs are designed to learn effective node representations while simultaneously generating new graph structures by identifying valuable connections among previously unconnected nodes. A key component of GTNs is the Graph Transformer layer, which facilitates a soft selection of edge types and composite relations to create multi-hop connections known as meta-paths.

The authors argue that existing GNNs struggle with noisy or heterogeneous graphs, where the predefined structures can lead to ineffective learning and representation. GTNs, however, operate in an end-to-end manner, learning new graph structures based on the data and specific tasks at hand, without requiring domain-specific knowledge. Through extensive experiments, the paper demonstrates that GTNs outperform state-of-the-art methods in benchmark node classification tasks, achieving superior performance without the need for pre-defined meta-paths. This highlights the potential of GTNs to enhance representation learning in complex graph scenarios, making them a significant advancement in the field of graph-based learning.
