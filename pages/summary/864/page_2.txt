The paper discusses advancements in language model pretraining, particularly highlighting the significance of cross-sentence pretraining and a novel cloze-driven training regime. The authors emphasize that their extensive experimental analysis reveals three key findings: first, cross-sentence pretraining is essential for various tasks; second, performance continues to improve with larger datasets, up to 18 billion tokens; and third, their cloze-driven approach is more effective than traditional methods of predicting tokens separately.

The related work section reviews previous efforts in learning sentence-specific representations, referencing models like ELMo and GPT, which have laid the groundwork for contextualized word representations. ELMo combines different layers of language models for improved task performance, while GPT employs a left-to-right approach with a projection layer for each task. The authors note that their model, while similar to GPT, also integrates an ELMo module effectively for tasks like named entity recognition and constituency parsing.

The paper contrasts their model with BERT, which uses a transformer encoder to capture both left and right contexts through multi-task learning. Unlike BERT, which employs a next sentence prediction task and focuses on masked token predictions, the authors' model predicts every token in a sequence, optimizing a single loss function that utilizes information from all surrounding tokens.

In the proposed two-tower model, the authors describe a self-attentional architecture with forward and backward towers for token prediction. Each tower processes the input in opposite directions, ensuring that target tokens are not included in their representations. The model is pretrained using individual examples from diverse datasets, including News Crawl and Wikipedia, with boundary markers added to delineate sentences. This innovative approach aims to enhance the effectiveness of language models in understanding and generating human language.
