For tasks involving sentence pairs, the approach is to concatenate the sentences with a separator token, <sep>, and the output of this token is integrated into the final projection. In structured prediction tasks, such as named entity recognition and parsing, task-specific architectures are fine-tuned alongside the language model, albeit with different learning rates. The input representations for these architectures come from the pretrained language model.

During fine-tuning, no masking is applied to the current token in the final layer that aggregates outputs. This allows access to information necessary for token-level classification tasks, such as named entity recognition, and improves performance for sentence classification as well. However, masking is retained below the combination layer for optimal results.

Optimization processes involve using larger learning rates for new parameters compared to those of the pretrained model. For GLUE tasks, the output of the language model is scaled before projections. The Adam optimizer is employed, and dropout rates are adjusted based on task type. A cosine learning rate schedule is utilized, with specific tuning for GLUE tasks and structured predictions based on validation performance.

The experimental setup includes training on datasets such as Common Crawl, News Crawl, and a combination of BooksCorpus and Wikipedia, with a total of about 9 billion tokens used in most experiments. Hyper-parameters are adapted from existing transformer models, with three model sizes explored. The CNN models feature an adaptive softmax for output, and the BPE model utilizes a shared vocabulary for input and output embeddings.
