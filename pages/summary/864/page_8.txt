This paper investigates the impact of different data sources and their volumes on the performance of a bi-directional transformer model for natural language processing tasks, specifically on the GLUE benchmark. The results demonstrate that larger datasets enhance performance across various tasks, with a particular advantage observed when pretraining on corpora that preserve paragraph structure over those consisting of individual sentences. For instance, a significant accuracy gap was noted in the RTE task when comparing News Crawl and Common Crawl datasets, underscoring the importance of multi-sentence training examples.

Additionally, the study explores the effectiveness of concatenating training examples into single strings and cropping them for training, finding that this approach did not outperform the existing strategy of using data as is. BooksCorpus and English Wikipedia exhibited strong performance on certain tasks like QNLI and MNLI, but less so on others.

The authors conclude that their cloze-style pretraining architecture, which predicts every token in context, outperforms prior models and leads to state-of-the-art results for parsing and named entity recognition. The analysis highlights key findings, including that cross-sentence pretraining is crucial, performance continues to improve with increased data, and their cloze-driven training method is more effective than traditional token prediction methods. Future research will focus on architectural variations, particularly parameter sharing to enable deeper model training without increasing parameter counts.
