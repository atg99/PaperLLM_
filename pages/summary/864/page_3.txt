The paper discusses the architecture and structure of a two-tower model based on the Transformer framework described by Vaswani et al. (2017). Each block in the model contains two sub-blocks: a multi-head self-attention module with 16 heads that employs masking for managing forward and backward states, and a feed-forward network (FFN) that utilizes a ReLU activation function. Notably, the authors implement layer normalization before the self-attention and FFN blocks, diverging from the original method, which they argue enhances training effectiveness. Residual connections and fixed sinusoidal position embeddings are incorporated, alongside character CNN encoding for input tokens.

The model combines forward and backward representations to predict an ablated word. This is achieved through another self-attention module followed by an FFN. The output is projected into a vocabulary space, with attention inputs drawn from the relevant forward and backward states of the sequence. The authors detail how attention queries are constructed and emphasize that the model can utilize comprehensive context around the target token.

For fine-tuning, the authors propose strategies tailored to different downstream tasks. In classification tasks, outputs from boundary tokens are concatenated and projected to match the number of target classes, followed by softmax normalization and cross-entropy optimization. Regression tasks are handled differently, focusing on a single output and using mean squared error for training. The paper highlights the importance of adapting the masking strategy during fine-tuning, especially for tasks like named entity recognition, to leverage full contextual information.
