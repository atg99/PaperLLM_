The paper discusses the performance of various models on the GLUE benchmark, focusing on different loss functions and pretraining strategies. It presents results from the CNN base model, highlighting that fine-tuning significantly enhances performance across tasks such as CoLA, SST-2, and MRPC. The authors explore the efficacy of the cloze loss compared to the bidirectional language model (bilm) loss, noting that the cloze loss yields better results. They propose a triplet loss combining both cloze and bilm losses but find that it does not surpass the performance of the cloze loss alone, suggesting that the learning signals from the two tasks are not complementary. 

Additionally, the paper examines the impact of training data size and domain on model performance. Results indicate that larger training corpora, particularly from Common Crawl, significantly improve accuracy across tasks, with CoLA and RTE showing the most benefit. In contrast, pretraining on News Crawl data yields poorer results despite being newswire-focused. The findings underscore the importance of both loss function selection and the volume and type of training data in optimizing model performance on NLP tasks.
