The paper presents results on various natural language processing tasks evaluated using the GLUE benchmark, comparing the performance of different models including OpenAI GPT, CNN configurations, BERT variants, and STILTs. Key metrics include Matthews correlation, accuracy, and F1 scores. The findings indicate that while the CNN base model performs comparably to STILTs overall, STILTs excels in specific tasks such as MRPC and RTE. The paper emphasizes the importance of task-specific training data, suggesting that training on entire paragraphs rather than just sentence pairs can enhance model performance.

In addition to GLUE tasks, the paper explores structured prediction tasks like Named Entity Recognition (NER) and constituency parsing. For the NER task, various configurations were tested, including ELMo and CNN models, with fine-tuning yielding the best results. The results demonstrate that the proposed architectures, particularly the CNN large model with fine-tuning, achieve superior performance compared to previous benchmarks, showcasing advancements over ELMo and BERT models.

Overall, the paper highlights the effectiveness of tailored training approaches and fine-tuning in improving performance across multiple NLP tasks, contributing to the ongoing evolution of language model capabilities.
