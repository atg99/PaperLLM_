The paper presents a lightweight ReﬁnerNet designed to enhance the accuracy of box predictions in 3D detection and Bird’s Eye View (BEV) scenarios, particularly for bounding boxes with higher Intersection over Union (IoU). The training process for the Fast Point R-CNN involves two main steps: first, training the Voxel Region Proposal Network (VoxelRPN) until convergence, followed by training the ReﬁnerNet using features and inferred bounding boxes from the first step.

In VoxelRPN, anchors are distributed across the global feature map, with positive samples identified by an IoU greater than 0.6 and negative samples by an IoU below 0.45. The training employs a multi-task loss function combining classification and regression losses. The classification loss is calculated using binary cross-entropy, with a focus on balancing the loss contributions from positive and negative samples. The regression targets are defined based on the differences between anchor and ground-truth box parameters.

ReﬁnerNet aims to refine the quality of predicted boxes, trained on positive proposals with an IoU above 0.5. The regression targets are set as offsets from the proposal center to the corners of the target box.

Experiments were conducted on the KITTI dataset, which contains 7,481 training images and point clouds, with evaluations split into easy, moderate, and hard categories based on occlusion and truncation levels. The model's performance is primarily assessed using Average Precision (AP) at an IoU threshold of 0.7 under moderate difficulty.

Implementation details include voxelizing point clouds into a 3D cuboid, with specific anchor sizes for different object categories and applying Non-Maximum Suppression (NMS) to eliminate duplicate predictions. The paper highlights the effective performance of both VoxelRPN and ReﬁnerNet, particularly for car detection, demonstrating their capabilities in enhancing detection accuracy.
