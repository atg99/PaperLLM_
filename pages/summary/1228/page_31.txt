This paper presents a statistical result concerning the convergence of a normalized sum involving random variables. It establishes that under certain conditions, specifically when the variance condition σ2 + (1−ρ)κ2 is greater than zero, the normalized sum converges in distribution to a normal distribution with mean zero and variance σ2 + (1−ρ)κ2.

The proof begins by noting that the expected value of the sum of the random variables, denoted as Nnρn, equals one, while its variance tends to zero as n increases. The continuous mapping theorem is then applied to demonstrate that the square root of the normalized sum converges in probability to one.

To further support the main result, the paper defines a sequence of variances and shows that for sufficiently large n, the variance s2n remains positive. The expected value of the adjusted random variable is shown to equal zero, and the variance is calculated to establish the convergence of the sum of variances.

Overall, the findings emphasize the conditions under which the central limit theorem can be applied to a sequence of random variables, providing a foundation for further statistical analysis.
