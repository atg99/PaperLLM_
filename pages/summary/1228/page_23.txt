The paper discusses the estimation of asymptotic variances in a statistical context, specifically focusing on components such as ρ, Γ, ∆ehw, and ∆cond. The authors provide formulas for estimating these components, with ρ estimated as /hatwideρn=N/n, given that the population size is known. The estimate for Γ is then derived from the average of outer product matrices over the sample.

Estimating Vcausal and Vcausal,sample is noted to be more complex due to the involvement of ∆cond. The challenges arise from the inability to consistently estimate certain terms related to potential outcomes, leading researchers to often adopt conservative estimators. However, the authors argue that in the presence of attributes, improvements can be made to the variance estimator, referencing previous works by Abadie and Imbens, and Fogarty.
