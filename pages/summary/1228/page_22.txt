Theorem 4 asserts that if Assumptions 3-10 are satisfied, the estimator of θn converges in distribution to a specified limit irrespective of the correlation parameter ρ. Notably, this theorem's result holds true even when substituting θcausal,sample_n for θcausal_n, due to their near equivalence under Assumption 10.

A critical takeaway from Theorem 4 is that the asymptotic variance of the estimator does not depend on the ratio of the sample size to the population size when the regression function is appropriately specified. This implies that the standard EHW variance matrix is valid for the estimator under these conditions. The paper further notes that for a specific case where Xn,i is binary and consists only of an intercept, the theorem's result can be derived from Neyman's work on randomized experiments. The extension to non-binary regressors is achieved through Assumption 10.

However, the asymptotic variance of the least squares estimator for the attributes still relies on the sample-to-population size ratio, with conventional robust estimators potentially overestimating this variance. The section concludes by addressing the variance estimation for both descriptive and causal estimates, introducing shorthand notation for the causal variance matrix.
