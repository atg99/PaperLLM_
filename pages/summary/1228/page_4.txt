This article addresses the sources of uncertainty in causal estimands and variance estimators in multiple linear regression, building on foundational work by Eicker, Huber, and White. The authors present a new framework that generalizes existing results by allowing for sampling from a finite population and explicitly incorporating design-based uncertainty. This contrasts with traditional methods that assume infinite populations and do not account for the complexities of finite sampling.

The authors demonstrate that the commonly used robust standard errors from the EHW framework are conservative and only accurate in limited scenarios. They propose improved variance estimators that leverage fixed attributes of units in repeated sampling, enhancing the precision of estimates.

Additionally, the article clarifies the distinction between internal and external validity by separating sampling-based from design-based uncertainty. The findings are particularly applicable in empirical contexts where researchers utilize random samples from finite populations, such as large-scale experiments or public-use census data. The proposed methods are also relevant when the entire population is available, providing a coherent interpretation of standard errors in such cases. Overall, the contributions of this research offer valuable insights for empirical analysis in various settings.
