The proof of Lemma 2 establishes the convergence of various terms related to the estimator Λn and its asymptotic behavior. It begins by showing that under certain conditions, Λn converges to Bn with high probability. The difference between the estimated and true values of Λn approaches zero in probability due to Lemma 1 and Assumption 6. This leads to the conclusion that the estimator Γn also converges to Γ, which has full rank. The convergence of the parameters θn and γn to their causal counterparts is derived from these results.

The proof introduces the definitions of various terms, including ∆ehw n and its related components, and demonstrates that the expectations of these terms converge. By employing similar arguments as in Lemma 1 and leveraging Assumption 5, the proof shows that the differences between estimated and true values of these terms vanish in probability. The final step aggregates these results to conclude that the overall difference converges to zero, largely due to Assumption 9.

Lemma 3 extends this analysis by showing that the estimated difference ∆Z n is bounded by ∆ehw n. It introduces the expected value of Gn and demonstrates that the estimator converges to this expected value under the same assumptions as in Lemma 2. Consequently, the convergence of the projected differences is established, reinforcing the results from Lemma 2.
