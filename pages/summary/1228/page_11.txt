The paper discusses the interpretation of coefficients in regression analysis, emphasizing the distinction between causal variables and attributes. A causal interpretation of a coefficient requires that the corresponding regressor is a cause, while if a regressor is merely an attribute, the coefficient estimates a population difference among subpopulations. For instance, in regressing earnings on years of education, years of education may be the causal variable. Conversely, when earnings are regressed on a job search program indicator, age, and years of education, the program indicator is the causal variable, with age and education as attributes.

The setup involves a sequence of finite populations, each characterized by fixed attributes and a potential outcome function that connects causal variables to outcomes. The realized outcomes vary across samples due to changes in the sampled units and causal variable values. The paper introduces a joint independence assumption for treatment assignments, allowing for non-identical distributions of causal variables. This contrasts with previous sections where marginal distributions were fixed to derive exact finite sample results. The focus here is on asymptotic results, which permits some dependence in the assignment mechanism.
