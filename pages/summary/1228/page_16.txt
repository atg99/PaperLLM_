Assumption 7 introduces a modified framework for analyzing treatment effects, diverging from conventional exogeneity conditions which typically require residuals to be independent of regressors. Under random assignment of treatment, the mean of the treatment variable is constant, and Assumption 7 is satisfied if the model includes an intercept. This assumption allows the treatment's distribution to depend on individual characteristics, but constrains this dependence to a linear relationship with the observed attributes. When treatment is binary, this assumption aligns with the idea of the propensity score, combining unconfoundedness with a linear propensity model.

The paper proceeds to establish that under specific conditions, including Assumption 7, the estimands can be interpreted as weighted averages of unit-level causal effects. This connection reflects previous findings regarding the linearity of the propensity score and its implications for interpreting population regression coefficients as averages of heterogeneous causal effects.

Assumption 8 introduces a linear model for potential outcomes, where the outcomes depend on the treatment variable and a non-stochastic error term. This assumption implies that any influence of observed or unobserved attributes on potential outcomes is captured by fixed parameters, with each parameter indicating the causal effect of a unit change in the treatment variable. While this linearity is a strong assumption, it may hold in contexts where the treatment variable is binary or takes on a limited number of values.
