The paper discusses the application of statistical theorems to establish convergence properties of certain sums involving random variables. It begins by establishing inequalities using Minkowski's inequality and properties of expectations, leading to the conclusion that a specific normalized sum converges in distribution to a standard normal distribution. 

In particular, it shows that under certain assumptions, the sum of products of random variables converges to a normal distribution with mean zero and a variance that depends on parameters defined in the study. The results are formalized in Lemma A.2, where three distinct convergence results are presented. Each result involves sums of products of random variables and their respective variances, highlighting different conditions under which convergence occurs.

The proofs involve checking conditions of previously established lemmas and utilizing properties of expected values and norms. The paper emphasizes the importance of certain assumptions to ensure the validity of the results, ultimately demonstrating the robustness of the convergence claims through careful mathematical reasoning.
