This paper discusses the limits of population variance and the expected outer product, defining two key concepts: ∆cond, which represents the limit of the population variance, and ∆ehw, the expected outer product. The difference between these two, ∆µ, is established as positive semidefinite. The paper makes an assumption regarding the existence of these limits, affirming that both ∆cond and ∆ehw are positive definite.

Theorem 3 outlines the asymptotic behavior of the estimators under specific assumptions. It presents three results regarding the convergence of the estimators of the causal and descriptive parameters as the sample size approaches infinity. Specifically, the results indicate how the estimators relate to the limits of the variances and the outer products, with detailed expressions for each case.

The commentary emphasizes that when the correlation parameter ρ equals zero, the asymptotic variance aligns with the standard EHW variance. However, when the sample size is significant relative to the population, the difference between the EHW variance and the finite population causal variance is noted to be positive semidefinite, reflecting the relationship between the variances and the correlation parameter.
