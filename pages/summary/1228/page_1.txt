The paper examines the interpretation of estimated parameters and standard errors in regression analysis when using data from entire populations, such as all U.S. states or website visits. Researchers often assume that their samples are randomly drawn from larger populations and report standard errors that reflect sampling variation. However, the authors argue that this approach may be inadequate, especially when the population of interest is poorly defined.

To address this, the paper introduces a design-based inference approach, where values of some regressors can be manipulated through interventions. This design-based uncertainty arises from the lack of knowledge about how regression outcomes would differ under alternative interventions. The authors derive standard errors that account for this design-based uncertainty, either in place of or alongside sampling-based uncertainty.

The findings suggest that the proposed standard errors are typically smaller than traditional infinite-population sampling-based standard errors, with specific conditions outlined under which they align. The paper highlights the importance of considering both types of uncertainty in regression analysis to improve the reliability of parameter estimates.
