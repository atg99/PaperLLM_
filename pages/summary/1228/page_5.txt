In this paper, the authors explore the implications of design-based uncertainty on regression estimators, particularly focusing on least squares estimation with a single binary regressor. They make four key points. First, they illustrate how design-based uncertainty impacts the variance of regression estimators. Second, they assert that the standard Eicker-Huber-White (EHW) variance estimator remains conservative even when accounting for design-based uncertainty. Third, they identify a finite-population correction to the EHW variance estimator applicable to descriptive estimands, but not to causal estimands. Fourth, they discuss the relationship between the two sources of uncertainty and the concepts of internal and external validity.

The analysis is set within a finite population framework, where sampling from this population introduces a binary indicator for whether a unit is sampled. The authors use the example of U.S. states and a binary regressor representing state regulations, such as right-to-carry (RTC) laws. They emphasize that the regressor is treated as a policy variable rather than a fixed characteristic, leading to missing data and design-based uncertainty. Utilizing the Rubin causal model, they define potential outcomes for each unit based on the presence or absence of the regressor, exemplified by state-level crime rates with and without RTC laws. The realized outcome reflects the observed state-level crime rate based on the regressor's value.
