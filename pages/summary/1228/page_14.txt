In this section, the paper defines descriptive and causal estimands, extending previous concepts to a regression context. 

Causal estimands are characterized as functionals dependent on potential outcome functions and are not expressible solely in terms of observed variables. Descriptive estimands, on the other hand, can be expressed using only realized outcomes and attributes, without relying on potential outcomes or sampling indicators.

The key distinctions are that descriptive estimands can be inferred from observed data, while causal estimands require knowledge of potential outcomes that go beyond what is observed. The paper introduces three specific estimands, each defined mathematically, which exist with high probability under specified conditions. These estimands include θdescr, θcausal,sample, and θcausal, which are formulated using various matrices that represent relationships among variables in the population.
