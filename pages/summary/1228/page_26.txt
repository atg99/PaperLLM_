The paper presents a series of simulation designs aimed at evaluating the behavior of various estimators of treatment effects under different conditions. Seven distinct designs are outlined, each with specific parameter adjustments, such as population size, sampling rates, and relationships between treatment effects and regressors.

The first panel of Table 4 details the parameters for each design, while the second panel reports standard deviations across simulation iterations for different estimators. The simulations utilize 50000 iterations and 1000 bootstrap samples to assess the performance and coverage rates of 95 percent confidence intervals.

In the basic design, with a small sampling rate of 0.01, both EHW and bootstrap standard errors provide accurate estimates for certain standard deviations, though the sample average causal effect exhibits a conservative bias. Subsequent designs show that increasing regressors has minimal effect on results, while a high sampling rate significantly overestimates variability. Designs with no explanatory power from regressors yield similar results across variance estimators, and designs where regressors fully explain treatment effects show closely approximated standard deviations. The final design, where the treatment effect is constant, results in similar variances across the board.

Overall, the findings underscore the impact of design parameters on the accuracy of variance estimators in treatment effect analysis, highlighting the importance of carefully considering sampling rates and the relationships among variables.
