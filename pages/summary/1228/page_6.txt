The paper discusses the distinction between descriptive and causal estimands in the context of sampling and observed data. It notes that while certain attributes remain fixed during repeated sampling, others are stochastic, leading to variability in observed outcomes. The authors define key population vectors, including realized outcomes and potential outcomes, and highlight the difference between estimands that can be expressed solely in terms of realized values and those that require potential outcomes for their definition.

Descriptive estimands are identified as functions of realized values that can be known with certainty if all data is observed, while causal estimands depend on potential outcomes and cannot be fully expressed with just observed data. The paper provides three specific estimands related to a binary regressor, including one descriptive and two causal forms.

The focus then shifts to a particular estimator, which is derived from a least squares regression approach, emphasizing the randomness inherent in the estimator due to the stochastic nature of the variables involved. The discussion sets the stage for further exploration of these concepts in subsequent sections of the paper.
