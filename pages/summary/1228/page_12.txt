The paper presents a transformation method for removing correlation with attributes in a statistical analysis context. It starts by defining a transformation \( X_{n,i} = U_{n,i} - \Lambda_n Z_{n,i} \), where \( \Lambda_n \) is a specific matrix derived from the expected values of \( U_{n,i} \) and the population matrix is assumed to be full-rank. This transformation ensures that the resulting variables \( X_{n,1}, \ldots, X_{n,n} \) remain independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.).

The paper further discusses the properties of the estimator \( \hat{\theta}_n \), which is obtained by minimizing the least squares errors of the sample, leading to a focus on the regression residuals \( e_{n,i} = Y_{n,i} - X'_{n,i} \theta_n - Z'_{n,i} \gamma_n \). The nature of the estimands \( (\theta_n, \gamma_n) \) influences the properties of these residuals. It is noted that the transformation is typically unfeasible due to unknown expected values, but it does not affect the least squares estimator \( \hat{\theta}_n \), allowing the analysis to concentrate on the regression with transformed variables \( X_{n,1}, \ldots, X_{n,n} \) rather than the original \( U_{n,1}, \ldots, U_{n,n} \).

The paper also establishes a random sampling assumption that ensures the sample size increases with the population size, detailing the conditions on sampling probabilities and rates. Specifically, it requires that the sequence of sampling rates \( \rho_n \) satisfies \( n \rho_n \to \infty \) and converges to a value within the interval [0,1]. This framework sets the stage for analyzing the properties of the estimators in a rigorous statistical context.
