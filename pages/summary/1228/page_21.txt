The paper discusses the importance of random sampling in research, particularly within the context of development economics. It highlights various studies that utilized random samples from larger populations, including works by Muralidharan and Niehaus (2017), Keels et al. (2005), and DellaVigna et al. (2017). These examples illustrate how random sampling can be employed for practical reasons such as cost and computational efficiency while still yielding valuable insights.

The authors emphasize the significance of understanding the variance in sampling, particularly when considering different ratios of sample size to population size (ρ). They argue that presenting variance for general cases, including situations where ρ equals 0 or 1, bridges two main perspectives on uncertainty: sampling-based and design-based. This connection demonstrates that their causal perspective does not conflict with traditional views on uncertainty but rather adds an additional layer.

Furthermore, the paper outlines the implications of assuming constant treatment effects for correctly specifying a linear regression model. This assumption is crucial for accurately describing potential outcomes in research analysis. Overall, the findings underscore the relevance of random sampling and variance considerations in enhancing the robustness of empirical studies.
