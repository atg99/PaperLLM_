The paper discusses the asymptotic behavior of statistical estimators under certain conditions, particularly focusing on the convergence of sums of random variables. It establishes that as the sample size increases, the variance of the estimators approaches a specific limit, denoted as a′, provided that certain conditions regarding the underlying distributions are met.

The authors utilize the Cramer-Wold device to demonstrate that the distribution of a normalized sum of random variables converges to a normal distribution characterized by its mean and variance. Specifically, they derive results related to the convergence of linear combinations of random variables and their implications for causal estimators.

The proof of Theorem 3 is outlined, showing that under the assumption of invertibility of certain matrices, the difference between the estimated and causal parameters converges in distribution. The authors provide a detailed mathematical formulation, including the relationship between various components of the estimators and the asymptotic behavior of the residuals.

Overall, the findings contribute to the understanding of the statistical properties of estimators in econometric models, particularly in the context of large sample sizes and the implications for causal inference.
