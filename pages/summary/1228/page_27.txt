In this article, we examine the interpretation of standard errors in regression analysis when the assumption of random sampling from a larger population is not valid. Utilizing a potential outcome framework, we differentiate between descriptive and causal estimands, providing a coherent interpretation of standard errors that accounts for uncertainty from both random sampling and conditional random assignment. The proposed standard error estimators may differ from conventional ones and depend on three factors: the nature of the estimand, the sample's representation of the population, and the degree to which measured attributes explain variation in treatment effects. While our focus is on linear regression models, the issues discussed are relevant in various contexts and require further exploration in future research. This work serves as an initial step in a broader research agenda.
