The paper discusses the properties of causal least squares estimators, particularly in the context of potential outcome functions and their relationship to unit-level causal effects. It highlights that even when all potential outcomes are constant with respect to a variable x, the causal least squares estimand can still yield non-zero values depending on parameters a and b.

In the section on the asymptotic distribution of the least squares estimator, the authors define population residuals in relation to causal estimands. These residuals, while similar in definition to those in conventional regression, differ conceptually as they may not be stochastic. The paper emphasizes that the definitions of residuals are based on causal parameters, and in asymptotic analysis, the distinction between causal and descriptive parameters diminishes, leading to similar results regardless of the parameter type.

The paper also states that under the assumption of joint independence among the variables Xn,i, the products of Xn,i and the residuals εn,i are also independent, though not identically distributed. Importantly, the expectations of these products may vary across units, indicating that averages of these expectations across the population do not necessarily equal zero.
