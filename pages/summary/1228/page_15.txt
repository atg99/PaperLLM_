The paper discusses the definition of estimands as coefficients corresponding to orthogonality conditions related to residuals in a regression context. Specifically, it focuses on three types of estimands: descriptive, causal-sample, and causal estimands, represented by equations (3.3) to (3.5). The residuals are defined as the difference between the observed outcomes and the predicted outcomes based on the model parameters.

The least squares estimator is introduced as a method for estimating parameters, represented as a matrix equation. It is emphasized that while the causal estimands have implications for causal inference, not all causal estimands necessarily represent average causal effects.

The paper also addresses the interpretation of the descriptive estimand as the coefficients of the best linear predictor. It further explores the causal estimands and their relationship to potential outcome functions, raising the complexity of interpreting these causal effects.

An important assumption is presented, which generalizes random assignment of treatments to allow for dependence on certain attributes. This assumption includes the existence of functions and matrices that relate the expected assignment to these attributes, setting the stage for a deeper investigation into causal interpretations.
