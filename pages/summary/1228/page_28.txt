In this appendix, the authors present a Bayesian approach to modeling inference, emphasizing the need to differentiate between descriptive and causal questions in statistical analysis. They redefine the variables involved, introducing Y∗n(1) and Y∗n(0) as vectors representing potential outcomes, and assume the rows of the associated matrix are exchangeable. This leads to modeling the data as independent and identically distributed quadruples based on an unknown parameter β.

The authors propose a prior distribution for β and specify a model where the variables Xi and Ri follow Binomial distributions. The potential outcomes are assumed to be jointly normally distributed, forming a comprehensive parameter vector that includes the probabilities q and ρ, means µ1 and µ0, variances σ²1 and σ²0, and the correlation κ.

They modify the observational scheme from a previous section to facilitate the derivation of posterior distributions, allowing for the observation of the outcomes Yi based on the indicator Ri. The appendix defines the descriptive estimand and causal estimand, highlighting the differences in the estimands used for descriptive analysis versus causal inference.

Overall, this Bayesian perspective underscores the importance of formal modeling in drawing distinctions between types of inference, enhancing the understanding of the underlying statistical processes involved.
