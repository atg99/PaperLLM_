This paper discusses the importance of accurately defining the estimand in research, particularly in the context of causal inference. The author argues that focusing solely on the super-population estimand θcausal=µ1−µ0 may not be suitable, as posterior inferences for this estimand can differ significantly from those of other estimands, such as θcausal n and θdescr n.

The paper provides proofs for key lemmas and theorems, establishing foundational concepts necessary for understanding asymptotic normality in statistical analysis. For instance, Theorem 1 demonstrates that under certain conditions, the estimand θcausal n can be expressed in terms of expected values, leading to important implications about the relationships between variables involved in the analysis.

The proofs rely on various mathematical principles, such as the mean value theorem and properties of independent variables, to derive results that reinforce the claims made about the estimands. Lemma A.1 further supports the discussion by outlining conditions under which a specified boundedness and variance hold for a triangular array of independent rows, providing a framework for establishing asymptotic normality.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the necessity of precise definitions in statistical estimands and the implications of these definitions for causal inference methods.
