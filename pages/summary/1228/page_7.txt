The paper discusses the concepts of sampling-based uncertainty and design-based uncertainty in the context of statistical estimation. It differentiates between two sources of randomness: the sampling component, which arises from the random selection of samples, and the design component, which stems from the assignment of treatments. 

Two key assumptions are established: 

1. Random Sampling or External Validity, which posits that the probability of observing a specific sample configuration is uniform across all possible configurations.
2. Random Assignment or Internal Validity, which suggests that the probability of a specific assignment of treatments is uniform, given the sample.

The paper proceeds to examine the first moments (expected values) of an estimator under these assumptions, stating that the expected value of the estimator conditioned on sample sizes (N1, N0) is equal to the descriptive parameter theta. It also notes that the expected values conditioned on either random sampling or random assignment yield specific causal interpretations of the estimator.

Furthermore, the paper analyzes the variance of the estimator, integrating both random assignment and random sampling assumptions. It defines population variances related to the outcomes under different treatments, contributing to the understanding of variability in the estimator's performance.

Overall, the findings illustrate the relationship between sampling and design in statistical estimation, highlighting how both uncertainties impact the interpretation of results.
