In this paper, we analyze the variance of the estimator /hatwideθ and present two conditional forms of this variance: the sampling variance conditional on X and the design variance conditional on R. The total variance, Vtotal, is defined to encompass both sampling and design uncertainties. It is expressed mathematically as:

Vtotal(N1,N0,n1,n0) = var(/hatwideθ|N1,N0) = S²1/N1 + S²0/N0 - S²θ/(n0+n1).

The sampling variance, Vsampling, focuses solely on sampling uncertainty, while the design variance, Vdesign, accounts for design uncertainty. Their formulations are given by:

Vsampling(N1,N0,n1,n0) = E[var(/hatwideθ|X,N1,N0)|N1,N0] = S²1/N1(1 - N1/n1) + S²0/N0(1 - N0/n0),

Vdesign(N1,N0,n1,n0) = E[var(/hatwideθ|R,N1,N0)|N1,N0] = S²1/N1 + S²0/N0 - S²θ/(N0 + N1).

The paper includes several comments on these variances. Comment 1 notes that Vtotal has been derived by Neyman for randomized experiments. Comment 2 highlights that Vsampling and Vdesign cannot be directly ranked, as their values depend on the sampling rate and treatment effect variance. Comment 3 states that in the case of infinite populations, the total variance and sampling variance converge, indicating that design uncertainty becomes negligible. Comment 4 emphasizes the importance of considering finite population effects, regardless of whether the estimand is causal or descriptive. Overall, the paper aims to provide a nuanced understanding of the variances associated with different experimental designs and sampling methodologies.
