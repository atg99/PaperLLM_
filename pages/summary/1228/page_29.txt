The paper discusses the distinctions among three estimands: the descriptive estimand (θdescr), the causal estimand (θcausal), and the super-population average treatment effect (θcausal). It highlights that these estimands generally possess unique posterior distributions, although they may converge in certain large sample scenarios. 

In a simplified case where parameters such as variances and sample sizes are known, the posterior distributions for the means (µ1 and µ0) are derived using independent, improper priors. This leads to specific posterior distributions for θcausal and θdescr, which are mathematically expressed. The paper notes that the posterior distribution for θdescr resembles the confidence interval based on normal approximations when sample sizes are large. Conversely, if the sample sizes equal the population sizes, the posterior distribution for θdescr becomes degenerate.

Further analysis reveals the posterior distribution for θcausal under constant treatment effects, where the relationship between the treatment outcomes simplifies the calculations. The results indicate that as the population size increases relative to the sample, the posterior distributions of θdescr, θcausal, and θcausal become similar. However, in smaller populations, these distributions diverge significantly. 

Overall, the findings underscore the impact of sample size on the estimation and interpretation of treatment effects, emphasizing the importance of understanding the underlying distributions in statistical analysis.
