The paper discusses a special case of adjusted variance that emerges when Zn,i consists of exhaustive and mutually exclusive dummy variables. In such cases, the residuals from regressing the demeaned versions of the causal variable and error term on Zn,i can be utilized to derive a conservative estimator of ∆cond, referencing variance formulas from Wooldridge (2001) for standard stratified samples.

The subsequent section presents simulations designed to highlight the differences between the conventional EHW variance estimator and the newly proposed variance estimators. The simulations focus on a single causal variable, Xn,i, alongside an outcome variable, Yn,i, and a vector of attributes, Zn,i. The attributes include a constant and k values drawn from a standard normal distribution. The potential outcome function follows a specified form, with population values generated from a normal distribution.

The data-generating process results in specific relationships among parameters, revealing that certain values can be derived with certainty. The simulations involve random sampling from the population, leading to a random sample size with an expected value determined by the sampling probability. Various designs are tested, with the basic design featuring a small sampling probability and a large population size, while subsequent designs explore variations in dimensionality and population size.

Results from the simulations are presented in a table, showcasing the performance of different designs and their impact on variance estimation.
