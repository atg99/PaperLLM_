The results, presented in Table XI, indicate that self-supervised learning methods generally surpass deep clustering and reconstruction-based techniques, highlighting their superior representation learning capabilities. This advantage is attributed to the diverse pretext tasks employed in self-supervised models, which enhance the model's ability to learn meaningful representations by capturing various data aspects.

Notably, contrastive learning methods emerged as particularly effective, as they allow for the differentiation of samples from multiple views without explicit labels, leading to robust representations. In contrast, adversarial methods like TimeGAN and TimeVAE often face challenges in convergence and computational expense, requiring specific data distributions and larger datasets. Predictive methods, such as EEG-SSL and TST, tend to underperform compared to contrastive methods due to their reliance on extensive context for accurate predictions.

Further analysis of different contrast levels, detailed in Table XII, reinforces the effectiveness of contrastive methods across the datasets. For instance, InstanceSimCLR and BYOL consistently performed well, achieving high accuracy, macro-averaged F1 scores, and Cohen's Kappa coefficients across all datasets. This comparative analysis underscores the importance of contrastive learning approaches in enhancing performance in unsupervised learning tasks, especially in time series data analysis.
