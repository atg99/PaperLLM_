SimCLR emphasizes learnable transformations and larger batch sizes, while TimeCLR adapts these principles to time series data. MoCo introduces a dynamic queue for negative samples, and BYOL innovates by eliminating negative samples entirely. CPC combines autoregressive modeling with noise-contrastive estimation to encode future observations, whereas SimSiam employs a stop-gradient mechanism to prevent mode collapse. MCL leverages noise injection and mixup techniques to create new training samples.

The paper highlights the challenges of using negative samples due to potential hard negatives and discusses alternative strategies that have emerged. It categorizes contrastive learning into instance-level and prototype-level approaches, with a focus on methods specifically designed for time series data. The analysis provides a comprehensive understanding of various contrastive models' contributions, architectures, augmentation techniques, datasets, evaluation methods, and performance metrics, illustrating the effectiveness of contrastive learning in discovering informative representations in time series data without explicit reconstruction tasks. Further details on the models are provided in accompanying tables, with subsequent sections exploring these methods in greater depth.
