These models seek to mitigate the issues faced by instance-level methods, particularly in their treatment of semantically similar samples as negatives, which can lead to poor representation learning.

The paper discusses various methods in contrastive learning, emphasizing the shift from instance-level approaches to non-contrastive and prototype-level models. Notable instance-level methods include SimCLR, which enhances representation quality through a learnable projection layer, and TimeCLR, which adapts data augmentation techniques to better suit time series data. MoCo improves negative sampling by maintaining a dynamic queue of samples, while BYOL and SimSiam introduce innovative architectures that rely on positive pair training without negative samples.

In summary, the paper highlights the evolution of contrastive learning techniques, showcasing the effectiveness of non-contrastive methods and the potential of prototype-level approaches to enhance representation learning by leveraging semantic information from clusters of similar samples.
