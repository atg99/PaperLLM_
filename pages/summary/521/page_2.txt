This paper addresses this gap by providing a comprehensive review of unsupervised representation learning methods, particularly focusing on contrastive learning techniques. The study emphasizes the importance of alignment in representation learning, which enhances the robustness of learned features by effectively mapping positive pairs close together while distancing negative pairs.

The review categorizes existing surveys on time series representation learning, highlighting their focus on specific tasks and models. It identifies a need for a unified approach to representation learning, which is satisfied by the development of a standardized library named ULTS. This library facilitates empirical evaluations and comparisons of various representation learning methods.

The paper's main contributions include a detailed classification of unsupervised representation learning methods and an exploration of their advantages across different data types, with a strong emphasis on time series data. The discussion on contrastive learning is structured around three levels: instance-level, prototype-level, and temporal-level contrast, each contributing to the effectiveness of representation learning by leveraging the inherent structures in data.

Overall, this work provides a significant advancement in understanding unsupervised representation learning, paving the way for improved methodologies in time series analysis and related fields.
