This paper presents an empirical evaluation and analysis of multivariate time series datasets, focusing on nine commonly used datasets. The authors reorganize datasets sourced from UCI, UEA, and MTS archives, emphasizing the importance of diversity in application domains, sequence lengths, sample sizes, variables, and classes. The selected datasets include PhonemeSpectra, DuckDuckGeese, EigenWorms, PenDigits, Epileptic Seizure Recognition, FingerMovements, Human Activity Recognition Using Smartphones, Smartphone and Smartwatch Activity and Biometrics Dataset, and UniMiB SHAR, chosen primarily from UCI and UEA due to their prevalence in existing research.

The experimental setup involves linear evaluation of state-of-the-art unsupervised learning models, particularly contrastive methods, using the PyTorch framework on an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3090 GPU. A standard linear benchmarking evaluation scheme is followed, where pre-trained representations are frozen and a logistic regression classifier is applied. The classification performance is measured using three metrics: Accuracy (ACC), macro-averaged F1 score (MF1), and Cohen’s Kappa coefficient (κ), with all models trained for 200 epochs. The paper provides detailed statistical information on the selected datasets, including the number of samples, variables, classes, and the length of each time series.
