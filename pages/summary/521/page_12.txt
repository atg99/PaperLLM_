b) T-Loss [50]: T-Loss focuses on leveraging temporal information through the construction of positive and negative samples based on the neighborhood of each timestamp. This approach enhances the contrastive learning process by ensuring that the temporal dependencies are taken into account, allowing for better representation of the sequences.

c) TNC [33]: TNC employs a similar strategy to T-Loss, utilizing neighborhood information to create contrasting pairs. This model emphasizes the importance of local contexts in the time series data, improving the overall learning process.

d) TS-TCC [51]: TS-TCC introduces time-series-specific augmentations for pair constructions, facilitating cross-view temporal contrasting. This method enables the model to learn robust representations by considering various augmented views, thus capturing more intricate details within the time series.

e) Additional Methods: Other models emphasize timestamp-level or segment-level representations, targeting the extraction of detailed temporal patterns. These approaches aim to provide a nuanced understanding of the data, but may overlook the broader semantic information inherent in the entire time series.

In summary, recent advancements in contrastive learning for time series data have led to the development of various models that address both instance-level and temporal-level representations. These models aim to capture the unique characteristics of time series data while balancing the need for detailed, localized information and higher-level semantic understanding.
