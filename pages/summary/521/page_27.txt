The paper discusses the limited advancements in automatic augmentation selection strategies for improving contrastive learning, particularly in the context of time series data. It highlights that these strategies often do not yield performance improvements over original models. A summary of classification accuracy results across different datasets and augmentation methods is provided. For the HAR dataset, the original method achieved an accuracy of 81.83, while RandAugment and W-Augment produced lower results. In the WISDM dataset, the original method had an accuracy of 83.71, with W-Augment slightly outperforming other strategies. The Epilepsy dataset showed the highest accuracy across methods, with the original method reaching 92.97. The findings indicate that optimizing augmentation strategies remains an unresolved challenge in enhancing model performance for time series data.
