This paper addresses the significant gap in the systematic analysis of unsupervised representation learning methods, particularly in the context of time series data. It highlights the limitations of existing surveys that primarily focus on traditional unsupervised techniques or supervised models, neglecting the rapidly advancing field of self-supervised learning and contrastive learning approaches. The authors conduct an extensive literature review of unsupervised representation learning methods, categorizing them into deep clustering, reconstruction-based methods, and self-supervised learning techniques.

The paper emphasizes the challenges in fairly comparing existing methods due to the rapid evolution of techniques and varying evaluation criteria, which include differences in augmentation methods, model architectures, and datasets. To address this issue, the authors introduce a standardized library, ULTS, designed for the efficient evaluation of unsupervised representation learning methods for time series. They conduct empirical evaluations of state-of-the-art methods across nine diverse real-world datasets, providing insights into the strengths and applicability of different models.

Key contributions of the paper include a comprehensive literature review with an updated taxonomy of unsupervised representation learning methods, the development of the ULTS library, and discussions on practical considerations and open research challenges in the field. The organization of the paper includes definitions and terminology in Section II, a review of related works in subsequent sections, and a conclusion discussing future research directions. Overall, this work serves as a valuable resource for researchers aiming to understand and advance the field of unsupervised time series representation learning.
