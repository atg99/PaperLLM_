c) CCL [64]: CCL focuses on refining representations by discovering clusters with high purity. It leverages these cluster assignments as potentially noisy supervision, enhancing the quality of learned features. This method emphasizes the importance of having a sufficient number of samples for each cluster.

d) SCCL [80]: SCCL employs a dual approach combining top-down clustering and bottom-up instance-wise contrastive learning. This method aims to improve the separation between clusters and reduce dispersion within clusters, effectively optimizing inter-cluster and intra-cluster distances.

e) CC [81]: CC integrates deep clustering with representation learning by relating instances and clusters through a feature matrix. This approach projects instances into a subspace whose dimensionality matches the number of clusters, providing a coherent representation of the data.

f) SLIC [82]: SLIC enhances video representation learning by combining iterative clustering with multi-view encoding and temporal discrimination. The method focuses on learning view-invariant representations and fine-grained motion features, utilizing clustering assignments to inform the sampling of positive and negative pairs.

g) MHCCL [60]: MHCCL enriches contrastive learning by incorporating hierarchical clustering information. It employs downward and upward masking techniques to refine prototypes and filter out misleading negatives, guiding the construction of effective contrastive pairs.

The paper highlights the challenges faced by existing methods that require predefined cluster numbers and struggle to capture multi-level semantics in unlabeled time series data. The discussed models leverage hierarchical structures to improve semantic understanding and representation quality in time series analysis.
