The paper presents a comprehensive taxonomy of unsupervised representation learning methods specifically for time series data, dividing them into three main categories: deep clustering, reconstruction-based methods, and self-supervised learning methods. Deep clustering methods integrate traditional clustering with deep learning, utilizing clustering results as labels for subsequent representation learning. Reconstruction-based methods typically use auto-encoders or sequence-to-sequence models for reconstructing time series data. Self-supervised learning has emerged as a significant technique for extracting representations without manual labeling, relying on pretext tasks that can be categorized as adversarial, predictive, or contrastive.

Adversarial methods aim to distinguish between real and generated data, predictive methods focus on forecasting future inputs or reconstructing corrupted views, while contrastive methods employ distance discrimination through self-supervised signals derived from data augmentations. Within contrastive methods, there are further subdivisions based on the level of contrast: instance-level, prototype-level, and temporal-level approaches.

Additionally, the paper introduces the ULTS library, a standardized framework designed in PyTorch for evaluating and comparing various unsupervised representation learning models, thereby providing a unified environment for research in this domain.
