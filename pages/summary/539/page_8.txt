The paper investigates the effectiveness of using pixel-level pretext tasks for learning dense feature representations in visual representation learning. The proposed method, named PixPro, leverages contrastive learning at the pixel level, yielding significant transfer performance improvements on downstream tasks that require dense predictions. 

Key findings include the performance metrics from various datasets, such as ImageNet, COCO, and Pascal VOC, where PixPro achieved notable accuracy improvements compared to previous supervised and unsupervised methods. Specifically, it reported 60.2 AP and 41.4 / 40.5 mAP accuracy on these tasks, surpassing the best existing methods by 2.6 AP and 0.8 / 1.0 mAP. 

The paper also highlights the benefits of combining pixel-level tasks with instance-level methods, showcasing that this combination leads to enhanced linear evaluation accuracy and transfer performance across multiple datasets. Additionally, it emphasizes the importance of head network pre-training, indicating that more pre-training layers correlate with better downstream task accuracy.

In the semi-supervised object detection results, the method outperformed prior instance-level techniques, showing improvements of +3.9 mAP and +2.3 mAP with only 1% and 10% labeled data, respectively. The findings suggest that aligning the pre-training network with downstream tasks is crucial for achieving better performance.

In conclusion, the paper establishes the strong potential of pixel-level pretext tasks in unsupervised visual representation learning, proposing a pathway for future research that could extend to video and multi-modality signal learning.
