The referenced papers contribute to advancements in the fields of contrastive learning, object detection, self-supervised learning, and unsupervised feature learning. 

Tian et al. (2020) explore the factors that enhance the effectiveness of views in contrastive learning, emphasizing the importance of view selection for optimal model performance. Tian et al. (2019) present FCOS, a fully convolutional one-stage object detection framework that streamlines the process by eliminating the need for anchor boxes. Trinh et al. (2019) introduce Selfie, a self-supervised pretraining method aimed at improving image embeddings, showcasing the potential of unsupervised learning techniques.

Detectron2, developed by Wu et al. (2019), is a robust framework for object detection that offers flexibility and modularity. Wu et al. (2018) propose a method for unsupervised feature learning through non-parametric instance discrimination, highlighting the effectiveness of instance-based approaches. Ye et al. (2019) focus on unsupervised embedding learning by utilizing invariant and spreading instance features, further contributing to the field.

Zhang et al. (2016) and Zhang et al. (2017) investigate unsupervised learning techniques, with the former addressing colorization of images and the latter introducing split-brain autoencoders for cross-channel prediction. Lastly, Zhuang et al. (2019) present a local aggregation method for unsupervised learning of visual embeddings, which adds to the understanding of embedding techniques within the context of visual data. 

Overall, these works collectively push the boundaries of unsupervised and self-supervised learning, offering new methodologies and frameworks that enhance model performance in various applications.
