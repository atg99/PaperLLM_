The paper discusses advancements in unsupervised feature learning, particularly focusing on missing part prediction and pixel-level contrastive learning. It highlights the decline in interest for unsupervised tasks due to their complexity and inferior performance. The authors propose a method called PixContrast, which utilizes pixel-level discrimination for learning visual representations, achieving state-of-the-art performance in dense prediction tasks like object detection and semantic segmentation.

PixContrast operates by sampling two augmented views from the same image, which are processed through encoder networks to compute feature maps. Unlike previous methods that generate a single feature vector, PixContrast computes feature maps that allow for pixel-level pretext tasks. The paper outlines how the distances between pixels in the feature maps are calculated and normalized, facilitating the creation of positive and negative pairs for contrastive loss computation.

Additionally, the paper introduces a pixel-to-propagation consistency task aimed at enhancing spatial sensitivity and smoothness in learned representations. This involves a pixel propagation module that filters pixel features by leveraging similar pixels, promoting coherence in pixel-level predictions. The proposed methods are designed to simplify training and avoid the need for specialized architectures, marking a significant contribution to unsupervised learning techniques in computer vision.
