In the paper "Propagate Yourself: Exploring Pixel-Level Consistency for Unsupervised Visual Representation Learning," the authors Zhenda Xie, Yutong Lin, Zheng Zhang, Yue Cao, Stephen Lin, and Han Hu propose a novel approach to enhance unsupervised visual representation learning through pixel-level pretext tasks. They argue that existing contrastive learning methods primarily focus on instance-level tasks, which may not yield optimal representations for downstream applications that require detailed pixel predictions.

The authors introduce two main pixel-level tasks: the first applies contrastive learning directly at the pixel level, while the second, termed pixel-to-propagation consistency, significantly improves performance, surpassing state-of-the-art methods. Their experiments demonstrate that this approach achieves impressive results on various benchmarks, including 60.2 Average Precision (AP) for Pascal VOC object detection, 41.4/40.5 mean AP for COCO object detection, and 77.2 mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) for Cityscapes semantic segmentation, all using a ResNet-50 backbone. These results exceed previous best methods based on instance-level contrastive learning by notable margins.

Furthermore, the pixel-level pretext tasks are shown to effectively pre-train both standard backbone networks and head networks for dense downstream tasks, indicating their complementary nature to traditional instance-level methods. The findings highlight the potential of pixel-level tasks in advancing unsupervised visual representation learning and suggest a promising direction for future research. The authors provide their code for further exploration of their proposed method.
