The paper presents a comparison of the proposed pixel-level pre-training method, PixPro, against various supervised and unsupervised pre-training methods across different downstream tasks, including object detection and semantic segmentation. The results, summarized in Table 1, highlight the performance of PixPro on datasets such as Pascal VOC, COCO, and Cityscapes, where it consistently achieves superior results compared to previous methods. 

Specifically, on Pascal VOC object detection with the R50-C4 backbone, PixPro achieves an average precision (AP) of 60.2, which surpasses the best previous unsupervised method by 2.6 AP. In COCO object detection, it accomplishes a mean average precision (mAP) of 41.4 and 40.5 for R50-FPN and R50-C4 respectively, improving upon previous results by 0.8 and 1.0 mAP. For Cityscapes semantic segmentation, PixPro achieves a mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) of 77.2, which is a 1.0 improvement over prior methods.

The paper details the configurations and architectures used for each task, including the use of Faster R-CNN for Pascal VOC, Mask R-CNN for COCO, and FCN for Cityscapes. It also outlines the training settings, such as synchronized batch normalization and fine-tuning strategies. The results indicate that PixPro is a competitive approach for unsupervised pre-training, demonstrating its effectiveness across different applications in computer vision.
