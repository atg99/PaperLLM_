This paper presents an ablation study on the hyper-parameters for the PixPro method, which is evaluated on the Pascal VOC and COCO object detection tasks. The study focuses on the sensitivity of various hyper-parameters, including distance thresholds, sharpness exponent, number of transformation layers, feature maps, and training length.

The key findings from the ablation study are as follows:

1. **Distance Thresholds**: Using a distance threshold of T=0.7 yields optimal performance across different feature maps. Higher resolution feature maps (e.g., P3) showed more stability in results compared to C5.

2. **Sharpness Exponent**: A sharpness exponent of γ=2 was found to provide the best transfer performance, indicating that both overly smooth and overly sharp similarity functions can negatively impact results.

3. **Transformation Layers**: The number of transformation layers in the pixel-to-propagation module (PPM) was examined, with l=1 yielding slightly better outcomes than other configurations. Notably, the absence of learnable parameters (l=0) still performed reasonably well, but removing the PPM altogether led to model collapse.

4. **Feature Maps**: The choice of feature maps significantly impacted transfer accuracy. While using higher resolution feature maps (P3 and P4) improved performance on COCO, they were less effective on Pascal VOC, suggesting that architectural consistency between pre-training and downstream tasks is beneficial.

5. **Training Length**: Increasing the training duration generally enhanced transfer performance, with a maximum training length of 400 epochs yielding a noticeable improvement.

The paper also compares the PixPro method with the PixContrast method, demonstrating that PixPro consistently outperformed PixContrast by notable margins in both datasets. The inclusion of the PPM in PixPro improved performance while avoiding hyper-parameter tuning issues present in PixContrast.

Overall, the findings emphasize the importance of hyper-parameter selection and architectural consistency for optimizing object detection performance in the PixPro method. Future work will explore further enhancements, particularly regarding training duration.
