The paper discusses the cloning, expression, and production of the AP205-RBM vaccine, which targets the receptor-binding motif (RBM) of the SARS-CoV-2 Spike protein. The process began with the genetic fusion of codon-optimized AP205 monomers, which were subcloned into a modified pETDuet-1 vector to create the pETDuet-1-AP205 dimer plasmid. The RBM gene, encoding residues 437-508 of the Spike protein, was amplified using PCR and subsequently ligated into the plasmid, resulting in the pETDuet-1-AP205-RBM construct. E. coli XL1-Blue cells were used for cloning, and after sequencing, the plasmid was transferred to T7 Express Competent E. coli for protein expression.

For expression and purification, E. coli cultures were grown in lysogeny broth supplemented with ampicillin. The expression was induced with IPTG, and cells were harvested and lysed. The lysate was subjected to centrifugation to separate soluble and insoluble proteins, which were analyzed via SDS-PAGE. The insoluble proteins were solubilized in a specific buffer and underwent a series of dialysis steps for refolding, ultimately leading to the purification of the VLP fusion protein using a HisTrap column.

Electron microscopy was utilized to visualize the purified AP205-RBM protein, which was stained for imaging. This comprehensive methodology highlights the key steps in vaccine development, emphasizing genetic engineering, protein expression, and purification techniques essential for producing effective vaccines against SARS-CoV-2.
