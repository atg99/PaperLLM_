The paper discusses the immunogenic properties of a vaccine candidate utilizing bacteriophage-derived AP205 virus-like particles (VLPs) designed to present the receptor-binding motif (RBM) of the SARS-CoV-2 spike protein. The authors propose that coronaviruses, unlike other viruses with repetitive epitopes, employ a structural strategy that limits strong antibody responses. This is attributed to the large particles and long spike proteins of SARS-CoV-2, which result in poor complement recruitment and suboptimal B cell stimulation.

The study highlights that the vaccine candidate effectively induces high-avidity antibodies targeting both the RBM and the entire spike protein. However, the ability of these antibodies to recognize emerging variants of concern, such as B.1.1.7, B.1.351, and P.1, remains uncertain, given previous findings that convalescent sera show reduced recognition of these variants.

The authors also note the significance of the IgG2a subclass in antibody responses, emphasizing its superior functional properties, such as complement activation and antibody-dependent cell-mediated cytotoxicity. The study found that RNA packaged in VLPs triggers an isotype switch to IgG2a through TLR7/8 signaling, suggesting that the E. coli-based production system is optimal for generating protective antibody responses.

Furthermore, strong serum IgA responses were observed, indicating potential local neutralization of SARS-CoV-2 on mucosal surfaces. However, the study did not assess Th cell responses directly, although previous work suggests that VLPs can induce Th cell-dependent isotype switching and Th1 responses.

In conclusion, the AP205 VLPs serve as an effective vaccine platform for the RBM domain, demonstrating high immunogenicity and the ability to generate neutralizing antibodies that inhibit virus replication in independent assays.
