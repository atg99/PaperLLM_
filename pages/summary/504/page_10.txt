The study investigates the efficacy of the AP205-RBM vaccine candidate in inducing high levels of IgG antibodies against the receptor-binding domain (RBD) and Spike protein of SARS-CoV-2. The vaccine was evaluated using ELISA to measure RBD-specific and Spike-specific IgG titers from vaccinated mice, demonstrating significant antibody responses. Avidity tests indicated that the antibodies produced were of high avidity, as shown by their resistance to dissociation in high urea concentrations.

Additionally, the vaccine's capability to neutralize SARS-CoV-2 was assessed through two types of neutralization assays: one utilizing a pseudotyped Vesicular Stomatitis Virus and the other employing the cytopathic effect method with actual SARS-CoV-2. Results showed that the antibodies induced by the vaccine effectively neutralized the virus, confirming its potential as a protective measure against COVID-19.

The discussion highlights the development of the vaccine using recombinant RBD coupled to CuMV TT virus-like particles (VLPs) and suggests that genetic fusion techniques may enhance large-scale production. The study underscores the importance of targeting the RBD, particularly the receptor-binding motif (RBM), which is minimally glycosylated, allowing for effective immune response without the virus evading detection. The AP205-RBM vaccine candidate demonstrated promising results, inducing robust antibody responses even after a single dose, with potential for further enhancement through booster immunization.
