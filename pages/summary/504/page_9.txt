The study evaluates the immunogenicity of the AP205-RBM vaccine, focusing on the induced IgG antibodies against the receptor-binding domain (RBD) and spike protein of SARS-CoV-2. It employs statistical analysis to compare different IgG subclasses, with a significance threshold of p < 0.05. The results show that the antibodies elicited by the vaccine exhibit high avidity, as determined by modified immunoassays using 7 M Urea washes to assess binding strength. The ELISA tests demonstrated strong signals for RBD- and spike-specific antibodies, indicating robust affinity.

Furthermore, the vaccine candidate was shown to induce neutralizing antibodies against SARS-CoV-2. Two neutralization assays were conducted: one with pseudotyped Vesicular Stomatitis Virus and another with live SARS-CoV-2. Both assays confirmed that the sera from vaccinated mice could effectively neutralize the virus, indicating the vaccine's potential effectiveness in providing protection against SARS-CoV-2. Overall, the findings highlight the promising immunogenic profile of the AP205-RBM vaccine candidate.
