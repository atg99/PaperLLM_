The study investigates the development of a fusion vaccine against SARS-CoV-2 using bacteriophage AP205 virus-like particles (VLPs). Previous research demonstrated the effectiveness of AP205-VLPs for fusing various epitopes without compromising their integrity. In this study, the receptor binding motif (RBM) of the spike protein of SARS-CoV-2 was fused to the C terminus of AP205 dimers, allowing for the production of a vaccine candidate through expression in E. coli. The resulting AP205-RBM fusion protein was confirmed via SDS-PAGE and characterized by electron microscopy and mass spectrometry, showing successful formation of VLPs approximately 25-30 nm in size.

To evaluate the immunogenicity of the AP205-RBM vaccine, BALB/c mice were immunized with 100 µg of the vaccine, followed by a booster dose on day 28. Antibody responses were monitored, revealing significant increases in both receptor binding domain (RBD) and spike-specific IgG titers after the priming and boosting, while no RBD-specific antibodies were observed in control mice vaccinated with AP205 alone. Statistical analysis confirmed the significance of the results, indicating that the AP205-RBM vaccine effectively induces a robust immune response.
