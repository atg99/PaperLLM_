The paper discusses the development and immunogenicity of a fusion vaccine against SARS-CoV-2 utilizing bacteriophage AP205 virus-like particles (VLPs). Key findings include the successful expression and purification of the AP205-RBM fusion construct, evidenced by SDS-PAGE analysis which confirms a molecular weight of approximately 37.4 kDa. Electron microscopy revealed the VLPs to be around 30 nm in size.

Immunological studies demonstrated that the AP205-RBM vaccine effectively induced the production of RBD-specific immunoglobulin A (IgA) antibodies and promoted class-switching to immunoglobulin G (IgG), particularly enhancing IgG2a and IgG1 responses. The study notes the importance of IgG2a and IgG2b in pathogen defense due to their roles in opsonization and complement activation. Additionally, the vaccine induced high-avidity IgG antibodies against the receptor-binding domain (RBD) and spike protein, indicating strong binding strength due to multiple interaction points.

Overall, the results suggest that the AP205-RBM vaccine is a promising candidate for further development in the fight against SARS-CoV-2, demonstrating robust immunogenicity and the potential for effective immune responses.
