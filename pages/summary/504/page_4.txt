This paper details a study on the immune response elicited by the AP205-RBM vaccine in Balb/c mice, focusing on various methodologies employed to assess antibody production and functionality. The experimental protocol included sample preparation for transmission electron microscopy and mass spectrometry analysis. Mice were vaccinated subcutaneously with AP205-RBM and boosted after 28 days, with serum samples collected at multiple intervals for analysis.

The enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) was utilized to quantify total IgG antibody titers against AP205-RBM, using spike proteins as a coating agent. The process involved multiple dilutions of serum samples and subsequent incubation with HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies to measure optical density, which allowed for the determination of antibody concentrations.

Furthermore, the study assessed antibody avidity against SARS-CoV-2 spike and receptor-binding domain proteins through a series of wash steps involving urea to evaluate the strength of the antibody-antigen interaction. Neutralization assays were also performed using a vesiculoviral pseudotyped virus to determine the vaccine's effectiveness in preventing viral infection. This involved incubating the virus with heat-inactivated serum from immunized mice before adding HEK293T/17 cells expressing ACE2 and TMPRSS2, followed by measuring reporter gene activity to assess neutralization efficacy.

Overall, the methodologies employed in this study provide a comprehensive approach to evaluating the immunogenicity of the AP205-RBM vaccine, contributing valuable insights into its potential as a protective measure against SARS-CoV-2.
