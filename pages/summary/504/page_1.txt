The paper discusses the development of a promising vaccine candidate for COVID-19 based on virus-like particles (VLPs) derived from dimerized capsid proteins of AP205. This candidate incorporates the receptor binding motif (RBM) of the SARS-CoV-2 spike protein, which is a key target for neutralizing antibodies in recovered patients. The researchers genetically fused the RBM domain to the C-terminus of AP205 and expressed the modified VLPs in E. coli. Initial expression resulted in insoluble aggregates, which were then denatured and refolded to achieve proper VLP formation, confirmed through electron microscopy.

Immunization studies in mice showed that the resulting antibodies could effectively recognize both the eukaryotically expressed receptor binding domain (RBD) and the spike protein of SARS-CoV-2. Notably, these antibodies also demonstrated the ability to neutralize the virus. The authors highlight the potential for large-scale production of this vaccine candidate, which could significantly aid in the global effort to achieve herd immunity and restore normalcy in the face of the ongoing pandemic. The paper emphasizes the urgency of vaccination as a critical strategy to combat COVID-19, especially given the rapid spread of the virus compared to previous coronaviruses.
