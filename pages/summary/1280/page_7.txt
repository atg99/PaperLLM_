The paper compares various dimensionality reduction (DR) methods in terms of their detection accuracy across different shifts in datasets MNIST and CIFAR-10. It presents results in two main tables: Table 1 focuses on the detection accuracy of different DR techniques and their performance on univariate and multivariate tests, while Table 2 assesses shift detection performance based on shift intensity and perturbed sample percentages.

In Table 1, BBSDs is identified as the best-performing method for univariate testing, while UAE excels in multivariate contexts. The results indicate that as the number of samples increases, the accuracy of detecting shifts improves significantly. For univariate tests, the accuracy of BBSDs reaches 79% at 10,000 samples, while the other methods show lower performance. In multivariate tests, UAE also shows superior accuracy, especially with larger sample sizes.

Table 1b reveals that certain shifts, such as lgn and mimg, are easily detectable, even with few samples, while others like sgn and ko are harder to detect. The paper categorizes shifts into harmless (green bold) and harmful (red italic) based on their detection accuracy. 

Table 2a highlights that detection accuracy improves with shift intensity, with large shifts being significantly more detectable than small shifts. Specifically, large shifts can be detected with better than chance accuracy at just 20 samples. Table 2b shows that shifts with 50% and 100% anomalous samples can be detected effectively, whereas only 10% anomalous samples present a challenge.

Overall, the findings suggest that the choice of dimensionality reduction technique greatly influences shift detection capabilities, with BBSDs and UAE leading in performance. The paper emphasizes the importance of sample size and shift intensity in accurately detecting shifts, indicating potential applications in real-world scenarios where data distributions may shift over time.
