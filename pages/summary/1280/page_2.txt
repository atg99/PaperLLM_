This paper addresses the problem of detecting dataset shift, which occurs when the distribution of source data (used for training) differs from the target data (representing real-world scenarios). The authors aim to achieve three primary goals: (i) detect distribution shifts with minimal examples, (ii) characterize the nature of the shift, and (iii) assess whether the shift is detrimental. The focus is primarily on the first goal, with preliminary exploration of the latter two.

The authors utilize statistical two-sample testing to evaluate the equivalence of the source and target distributions. While univariate distribution testing is well-established, high-dimensional testing (e.g., in image data) presents challenges, particularly concerning scalability and statistical power. They reference a method called black box shift detection (BBSD) proposed by Lipton et al., which enables detection of distribution differences using classifiers with invertible confusion matrices. The authors build upon this by exploring various dimensionality reduction techniques in conjunction with two-sample testing.

Empirical results indicate that BBSD is effective under diverse shifts, even when the label shift assumption is violated. The paper also analyzes the performance of domain-discriminating classifiers, which are specifically trained to differentiate between source and target samples, in the context of complex high-dimensional data.

In the related work section, the paper discusses various approaches to anomaly detection and change point detection, highlighting the existing literature on dataset shifts in domain adaptation. It reviews methods involving covariate and label shifts, as well as recent advancements in out-of-distribution sample detection techniques. Overall, the paper contributes to the understanding of dataset shift detection and the effectiveness of different methodologies in high-dimensional settings.
