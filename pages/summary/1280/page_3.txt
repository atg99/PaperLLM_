3.2 Two-Sample Testing Techniques
Following dimensionality reduction, we explore various two-sample testing techniques to assess the distributional shift between the labeled and unlabeled data. The choice of test can significantly impact the detection performance. Common techniques include statistical tests like the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test, the Wasserstein distance, and more recent machine learning-based methods. Each method offers distinct advantages, such as robustness to different data distributions or computational efficiency.

3.3 Evaluation and Combination of Results
When applying multiple dimensionality reduction techniques and two-sample tests, it is crucial to evaluate their performance comprehensively. We propose a framework for combining the results from different tests and representations to improve overall detection reliability. This may involve voting schemes, ensemble methods, or meta-analysis approaches to synthesize the information from various sources, ultimately leading to a more informed decision about the presence of a shift.

In summary, the paper discusses three primary considerations in shift detection: the representation of data, the choice of two-sample tests, and the methodology for combining results. It highlights several dimensionality reduction techniques, including PCA, Sparse Random Projection, Autoencoders, and various classifier outputs, followed by evaluations of different statistical tests for detecting distributional shifts. The goal is to establish a robust framework that effectively identifies shifts while considering computational efficiency and accuracy.
