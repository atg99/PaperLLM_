The paper presents results on shift detection in image datasets, specifically focusing on the MNIST and COIL-100 datasets. It evaluates the performance of various dimensionality reduction (DR) methods under different levels of perturbation in the test data. 

Figures illustrate the evolution of p-values and classification accuracy across varying percentages of perturbed data. Subfigures in each figure show how the p-values change as the number of samples increases, with different DR methods being compared. The results indicate that the domain classifier can effectively identify the most different and most similar examples, aiding in the characterization of shifts. 

A key finding is the ability to distinguish between malignant and benign shifts. The paper acknowledges the challenge of relying on external labeling functions for accuracy assessments but demonstrates that fewer labeled examples—two to three orders of magnitude less than the total sample size—can still yield a reliable approximation of target accuracy. This suggests the potential for efficient shift detection in practical applications while minimizing labeling requirements.
