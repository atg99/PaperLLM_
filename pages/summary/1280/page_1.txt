The paper "Failing Loudly: An Empirical Study of Methods for Detecting Dataset Shift" by Rabanser, Günnemann, and Lipton addresses the challenge of machine learning systems failing silently when faced with unexpected inputs due to their reliance on the i.i.d. assumption. The authors aim to enhance the reliability of ML systems by developing methods for detecting dataset shifts, identifying representative examples of these shifts, and quantifying their severity.

The study explores various datasets and perturbations affecting both covariates and label distributions, revealing that a two-sample-testing-based approach utilizing pre-trained classifiers for dimensionality reduction yields the best results in detecting shifts. Additionally, the authors find that domain-discriminating methods are effective in qualitatively characterizing shifts and assessing their potential harm.

The introduction highlights the widespread application of deep neural networks in critical industries and emphasizes the importance of reliable software testing. The paper underscores the difficulty of detecting distribution shifts in ML systems, which can lead to significant performance degradation, particularly in high-stakes scenarios. The authors' three-fold objective is to advance methods for detecting and characterizing distribution shifts to facilitate the safe deployment of machine learning in real-world applications.
