The paper discusses various statistical hypothesis testing techniques used to evaluate classifier performance against random chance. It highlights different methods based on the type of data representation derived from Dimensionality Reduction (DR) techniques.

1. **Multivariate Kernel Two-Sample Tests**: The Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) is introduced as a kernel-based approach for comparing multi-dimensional distributions. It utilizes the mean embeddings of distributions in a reproducing kernel Hilbert space to calculate the squared MMD statistic. An unbiased estimate of this statistic is derived, and a p-value is obtained through permutation testing.

2. **Multiple Univariate Testing**: The Kolmogorov-Smirnov (KS) test is proposed as a simpler alternative to MMD for continuous data. It assesses the largest difference between cumulative density functions of two distributions. Due to multiple testing, the Bonferroni correction is applied to control the false discovery rate, adjusting the significance level based on the number of tests performed.

3. **Categorical Testing**: For categorical data, Pearson's chi-squared test is utilized to evaluate the consistency of observed frequency distributions with expected distributions. The test assesses homogeneity across class distributions through a contingency table, calculating a test statistic that follows a chi-squared distribution under the null hypothesis.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of selecting appropriate statistical tests based on data characteristics to accurately evaluate classifier performance.
