The referenced works encompass a variety of significant contributions to the fields of deep learning, image recognition, anomaly detection, and statistical methods for data analysis. 

Kaiming He et al. introduced Deep Residual Learning, which has become foundational in image recognition tasks by facilitating the training of very deep networks through residual connections. Hendrycks and Gimpel established a baseline for detecting misclassified and out-of-distribution examples in neural networks, emphasizing the need for reliable detection mechanisms. In another contribution, Hendrycks et al. proposed a method for deep anomaly detection with outlier exposure, enhancing the robustness of models against unusual data points. 

Geoffrey Hinton and colleagues highlighted the application of deep neural networks in acoustic modeling for speech recognition, showcasing their versatility beyond image data. Additionally, Krizhevsky and Hinton's work on learning multiple layers of features from small images laid the groundwork for advancements in convolutional neural networks.

The literature also addresses confidence calibration in classifiers, particularly for out-of-distribution sample detection, with contributions from Lee et al. and Liang et al. who focused on enhancing reliability in neural networks. The use of ensemble methods for combining p-values, as examined by Loughin and others, reflects ongoing efforts to improve statistical inference methodologies.

Overall, these studies collectively advance the understanding and capabilities of machine learning, particularly in the context of image and signal processing, while also addressing challenges such as anomaly detection and data reliability.
