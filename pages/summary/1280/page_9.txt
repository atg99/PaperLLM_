The paper presents an empirical investigation into detecting distribution shifts in machine learning systems using dimensionality reduction and two-sample testing. Key findings include the effectiveness of black-box shift detection with soft predictions across various shifts, the comparable performance of aggregated univariate tests to multivariate two-sample tests, and the ability to characterize the type and malignancy of shifts through domain-discriminating classifiers. 

Two case studies illustrate the findings: the first involves a synthetic image shift on the MNIST dataset, where methods, particularly BBSDs, successfully detect shifts, and accuracy on anomalous instances deviates from source data performance. The second study on COIL-100 shows that while a partitioned dataset indicates a shift, it does not adversely affect classifier performance. 

Additionally, analysis of the MNIST dataset revealed a significant distribution shift, particularly among instances of the digit 6, supported by a two-sample KS test with a highly significant p-value. The study concludes that the original MNIST split is not independent and identically distributed, contradicting its assumed randomness.

The paper suggests future research directions, including shift detection for online data and application of the proposed framework to other domains such as natural language processing and graph analysis.
