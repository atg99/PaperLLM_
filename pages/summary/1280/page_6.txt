The methodology involves a systematic approach where data is split into training, validation, and test sets, with shifts applied only to the test set. This allows for a consistent evaluation across multiple random data splits. The analysis includes different shift types such as adversarial samples, class imbalances, Gaussian noise, image transformations, and original dataset evaluations. 

Results indicate that multiple univariate tests, when aggregated appropriately, perform comparably to multivariate tests, despite the latter being favored in certain scenarios. Among dimensionality reduction methods, BBSDs emerged as the most effective for shift detection, while UAE excelled in multivariate contexts. Furthermore, the domain classifier showed poor performance in low-sample situations but improved with larger datasets. 

Overall, the findings highlight the importance of selecting appropriate shift detection methods and dimensionality reduction techniques, providing valuable insights for machine learning practitioners focused on model robustness and reliability.
