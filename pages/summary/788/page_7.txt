The paper "Algorithm Selection Using Deep Learning Without Feature Extraction" presents an approach to predicting the best algorithm for solving bin packing problems using Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks. The study evaluates the predictive performance of the LSTM on various test sets (DS1 to DS5) using metrics such as Kappa, accuracy, and average precision.

Results indicate that the LSTM outperforms the Simple Best Selector (SBS) across all datasets, achieving significant accuracy and precision. Notably, the LSTM's performance is close to that of the Virtual Best Selector (VBS) in some cases, with a pairwise comparison showing no significant difference in performance on DS4 (p-value of 0.31). The findings suggest that instances with longer sequences provide more information, enhancing the LSTM's ability to identify patterns.

Confusion matrices from experiments on DS4 and DS5 highlight that the LSTM correctly classified most test instances, particularly excelling in distinguishing instances labeled as NF. The study also emphasizes the importance of Falkenauer’s performance metric for evaluating solution quality, demonstrating that the LSTM uses fewer bins compared to the SBS, while remaining close to the VBS in terms of bin usage.

Overall, the paper concludes that the LSTM model effectively generalizes across different instance lengths and weight distributions, providing a promising method for algorithm selection in bin packing problems without the need for feature extraction.
