This paper presents an evaluation of various algorithms for solving a bin packing problem, specifically using Falkenauer's performance metric to assess their effectiveness. The study involved generating four balanced datasets, each containing 4000 instances, where each algorithm from a set A was tasked with solving 1000 instances optimally. The fitness of the algorithms was determined by the formula provided, with a fixed bin capacity and specific performance metrics.

The algorithms were subjected to a tournament selection process with a fixed population size, applying single-point crossover and mutation techniques to evolve solutions over one million iterations. The experiments utilized a high-performance computing cluster, and results were implemented in Java.

Figures in the paper illustrate that the target algorithm consistently outperformed others across various instances, with statistical significance confirmed through a Wilcoxon Signed-Rank Test. Notably, the Next Fit algorithm exhibited the poorest performance, requiring significantly more computational resources to evolve its instances compared to others. Principal Component Analysis (PCA) was employed to visualize the performance diversity among the algorithms, revealing clear distinctions in their effectiveness.

Overall, the findings indicate that the study successfully generated a diverse set of instances that effectively capture the performance landscape of the investigated algorithms. The Best Fit algorithm emerged as the top performer, while the Virtual Best Solver demonstrated the potential for achieving optimal results by selecting the best algorithm for each instance.
