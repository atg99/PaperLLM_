The paper discusses the application of deep learning techniques for algorithm selection in the context of the Bin Packing Problem (BPP). It focuses on four simple approximation algorithms: First Fit (FF), Best Fit (BF), Worst Fit (WF), and Next Fit (NF), which are commonly used for the BPP variant where the order of items is fixed. Each algorithm has a distinct strategy for placing items into bins, and their performance is evaluated in terms of the worst-case performance ratio (WCPR) against the optimal solution.

The authors highlight the limitations of existing benchmark problems, which often favor certain algorithms due to their structure. To create a balanced dataset for evaluating algorithm performance, they propose using an Evolutionary Algorithm (EA) to generate instances. This EA generates 4000 instances per dataset, with each instance being specifically tailored to favor one of the four algorithms based on their performance.

The paper details the EA's process, which includes initializing a population of candidate sequences, selecting parents, performing crossover and mutation, and evaluating fitness. The goal is to evolve item orders such that the target algorithm outperforms the others. The authors provide specific parameters for the datasets, including item counts and weight distributions.

By utilizing this approach, the paper aims to address the challenges of algorithm selection in the BPP context and to ensure a fair comparison across different algorithms. The findings intend to contribute to the understanding of algorithm performance and selection in combinatorial optimization problems.
