The authors employ Keras to implement the LSTM in a sequence-to-one configuration, where the input is a sequence of item weights and the output is a four-bit one-hot encoding representing the best heuristic among four options: Best Fit (BF), First Fit (FF), Next Fit (NF), and Worst Fit (WF).

The experiments are conducted using Google Colab with TPU support, and they include tuning the LSTM architecture and hyperparameters such as the number of layers, batch sizes, and learning iterations. The selected LSTM architecture consists of two layers with 32 neurons each, using the Adam optimizer and categorical cross-entropy as the loss function. The results from the validation sets indicate that datasets with a wider range of item weights yield better classification performance, as reflected in the Kappa statistics presented in Table 2. 

The Kappa values achieved across datasets DS1 to DS5 range from 71.50% to 93.79%, with values above 75% indicating excellent classifier performance. The study highlights that the complexity of the problem, particularly with average weights around a specific threshold, can influence the classifier's ability to generalize. The best-performing models from each dataset are further evaluated on unseen test sets, establishing the robustness of the LSTM approach in this classification task.
