The paper discusses an innovative approach to algorithm selection using deep learning, specifically through the use of Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks. Traditional machine learning methods for algorithm selection typically utilize feature vectors that lack consideration for the order of features. However, the authors argue that, particularly for the algorithms studied, the order of items significantly impacts the performance of different solvers in packing problems.

The study emphasizes the necessity of considering temporal information, which is often overlooked in conventional approaches. By generating balanced datasets that maximize the complementary performance of a set of algorithms, the authors demonstrate the importance of the sequence in which items are presented. Using LSTM networks, which are adept at learning from sequences and temporal data, the research aims to map information encoded in these sequences to identify the best-performing algorithm.

Initial exploratory experiments using conventional machine learning techniques, such as Multi Layer Perceptron (MLP) and Random Forest (RF), yielded satisfactory results with accuracy rates around 67%. However, the authors hypothesize that employing a deep learning approach, particularly an LSTM, would yield more robust outcomes due to its design for handling sequential data.

The paper details the architecture of recurrent neural networks (RNNs) and their ability to learn from time-series data, highlighting the application of Back Propagation Through Time (BPTT) for training these networks. The use of LSTMs is presented as a solution to effectively learn long-term dependencies within ordered data sequences, further enhancing the potential for successful algorithm selection in packing problems.
