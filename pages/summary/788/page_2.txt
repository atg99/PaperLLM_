This paper discusses the Algorithm Selection Problem (ASP) within the context of one-dimensional bin packing (BPP), focusing on the challenges and advancements in selecting the most effective algorithm for given problem instances. The authors outline the components of the ASP, including the problem space, feature space, algorithm space, and performance space, aiming to establish a mapping that maximizes performance.

The paper reviews various approaches to ASP, emphasizing the complexity of feature identification and the common reliance on machine-learning techniques to predict optimal algorithms based on extracted features. Traditional methods often involve hand-crafted features and require feature-reduction techniques like Principal Component Analysis to simplify learning. Some innovative approaches are mentioned, such as evolving generic feature parameters and using learning classifier systems to derive features from the state of problem instances dynamically.

The authors highlight a shift towards using deep learning in ASP, noting the success of deep neural networks in predicting heuristic performance based on extensive datasets. However, they propose a novel approach that eliminates the need for explicit feature extraction by employing a recurrent neural network with long-short-term memory (LSTM). This method is designed to analyze temporal information in the sequence of items presented to the solver, thereby enhancing the algorithm selection process without the typical challenges associated with feature identification.

The paper specifically addresses the offline variant of the BPP, where all items to be packed are known upfront, and aims to predict the best heuristic based solely on the temporal sequence of these items. This approach is positioned as a significant advancement in the field, potentially leading to improved algorithm selection for complex problem instances.
