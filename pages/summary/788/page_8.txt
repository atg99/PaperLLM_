The paper presents a novel approach to algorithm selection for combinatorial problems using a recurrent neural network (RNN) with long short-term memory (LSTM) architecture. This method directly utilizes the sequence of items in an instance as input, eliminating the need for feature design and selection. The model achieved an accuracy between 82% and 96% across different datasets, demonstrating a 7% to 11% improvement over the single best solver (SBS) and performing similarly to the virtual best solver (VBS). 

The evaluation was conducted on four large datasets with varying item counts and distributions, supported by a newly created database that features instances with distinct best-solvers, facilitating balanced training. This database is made available for further research. Future work aims to expand this approach to more complex algorithms and applications in temporal domains, such as job-shop scheduling, and to adapt the method for online scenarios where items are continuously presented. The ultimate objective is to derive insights into the relationship between ordering and predicted outcomes from the trained models.
