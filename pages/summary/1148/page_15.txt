This paper, presented at ICLR 2021, investigates the performance of hierarchical classification models, specifically comparing WideResNet28x10 and ResNet10 on the CIFAR10 dataset. The authors analyze the induced hierarchies of classes and the accuracy of these models, noting that WideResNet achieves a higher accuracy of 97.62% compared to ResNet's 93.64%. The hierarchy generated by WideResNet is more coherent, grouping all animals separately from vehicles, while ResNet shows a less logical grouping, combining disparate classes like airplanes and cats.

The paper also examines path traversal frequency as a means of understanding model behavior. Three cases are analyzed: a seen class (Horse), an unseen class with seen context (Seashore), and an unseen class with unseen context (Teddy Bear). The results indicate that the model effectively identifies the Horse class due to its presence in training data, while it relies heavily on context for the Seashore class, misclassifying it as a vehicle. For the Teddy Bear, the model generalizes to the broader category of Animals, showing a preference for furry animals like Dogs.

Additionally, the discussion includes the use of hierarchical softmax and conditional execution in neural networks to enhance computational efficiency in large-scale classification tasks, referencing various prior works. The findings underscore the importance of context and hierarchical structure in improving classification accuracy and model interpretability.
