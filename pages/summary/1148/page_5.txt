This paper presents NBDT, a novel decision-tree-based method that achieves state-of-the-art performance in image classification while maintaining interpretability. The results indicate that NBDT outperforms existing decision-tree-based methods by up to 18% and shows competitive performance against traditional neural networks, surpassing them by approximately 1% in some cases. The method retains interpretable properties such as pure leaves and sequential decisions, which are highlighted in the results table.

The training methodology incorporates a tree supervision loss that is carefully calibrated to enhance learning without hindering early training speeds. This approach includes progressive adjustments to the loss weights, which grow from zero to specified values over time, ensuring effective learning from the hierarchical structure of the data. The experiments demonstrate that NBDTs not only match or exceed the accuracy of leading neural networks on various datasets, including CIFAR10, CIFAR100, and TinyImageNet, but also improve generalization to unseen classes by up to 16%.

In larger-scale evaluations, NBDTs achieved a top-1 accuracy of 76.60% on ImageNet, significantly outperforming the closest competitor, NofE, by 15%. The paper emphasizes that while competing methods may achieve high accuracy, they often sacrifice interpretability through ensemble techniques, which NBDT successfully avoids. Overall, the findings highlight NBDT's potential as a robust and interpretable alternative in the realm of image classification.
