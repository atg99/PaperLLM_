This paper presents a novel decision-tree-based model called NBDT that demonstrates significant improvements in image classification tasks, particularly on the ImageNet dataset. NBDT outperforms existing decision-tree methods by at least 14% while maintaining accuracy close to that of EfficientNet, achieving an original accuracy of 77.23%. The analysis indicates that NBDT excels in distinguishing higher-level concepts, such as differentiating between animals and vehicles.

The paper compares various hierarchical approaches, showing that the induced hierarchy from NBDT surpasses alternatives like the WordNet hierarchy and classic decision tree structures based on information gain. The results highlight that data-dependent hierarchies tend to overfit, while existing hierarchies do not adequately capture visual similarities.

Additionally, the authors challenge the necessity of hierarchical softmax for hierarchical classifiers, finding that using tree supervision loss yields better accuracy. The original neural network can also benefit from NBDT's training methodology, leading to performance improvements of up to 2% on certain datasets.

The concept of zero-shot superclass generalization is explored, demonstrating NBDT's capability to classify unseen classes from different datasets accurately into their respective superclasses, outperforming standard neural networks by over 8%.

Finally, the paper discusses the interpretability of decision trees, emphasizing their ability to provide insights into the decision-making process by breaking down complex decisions into simpler intermediate ones. Despite the advantages, the study notes that using fully-trained weights for hierarchy construction is preferred for optimal performance.
