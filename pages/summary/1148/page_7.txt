The paper presents a comparison of various training methods for neural networks, particularly focusing on the Neural Binary Decision Tree (NBDT) combined with tree supervision loss. The results indicate that using a linearly increasing weight for tree supervision loss (TreeSup(t)) yields better performance compared to a constant weight (TreeSup), hierarchical softmax (HrchSmax), and no additional loss terms (None). For instance, in the CIFAR10 dataset with a ResNet18 backbone, TreeSup(t) achieved an accuracy of 94.82%, surpassing the other methods.

Additionally, the study highlights the importance of constructing and utilizing hierarchies early in training, as this approach consistently leads to higher accuracy across datasets. The paper also explores interpretability in model predictions, demonstrating that users can identify misclassifications more effectively with NBDT explanations compared to saliency maps. This is attributed to the discrete decision-making process of NBDT, which allows for clearer attribution of predictions.

Further analysis reveals that the introduction of tree supervision loss enhances the original neural network's accuracy across different datasets, with increases observed in both ResNet18 and WideResNet models. Finally, the NBDT's ability to generalize to unseen classes is evaluated, showing significant improvements in superclass classification when compared to baseline ResNet18 models.

Overall, the findings underscore the effectiveness of tree supervision loss and the NBDT framework in enhancing model performance and interpretability in computer vision tasks.
