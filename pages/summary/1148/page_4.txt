The paper discusses a novel approach to building induced hierarchies for decision-tree-based models, focusing on visual similarity rather than traditional data-dependent heuristics or existing hierarchies like WordNet. The method involves several key steps: loading weights from a pre-trained model, normalizing weights for leaf nodes, averaging weights for parent nodes, and calculating ancestor node weights based on leaf node averages. This process helps create a hierarchy that reflects visual relationships among classes.

The authors argue that existing methods tend to overfit to data or fail to capture visual similarities, as seen in the example comparing the visual relationship of a bird to a plane versus a cat. The proposed approach uses hierarchical agglomerative clustering on normalized class representatives, which allows for a more accurate reflection of visual similarity in the induced hierarchy.

Furthermore, the paper addresses the challenge of labeling decision nodes with WordNet. While WordNet provides a structure for nouns, it lacks coverage for non-object concepts and abstract visual ideas. The authors propose using the earliest common ancestor in WordNet to assign meanings to nodes, acknowledging the limitations of this approach and suggesting that more advanced methods could be explored in future research.

To enhance the model's performance, the authors introduce a tree supervision loss that fine-tunes the model to better separate representatives for inner nodes, which is traditionally not addressed by standard cross-entropy loss. Overall, the paper presents a structured methodology for building decision hierarchies that align more closely with visual instances and proposes improvements for training these models effectively.
