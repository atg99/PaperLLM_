The paper "NBDT: Neural-Backed Decision Tree" presents a novel approach to combine the strengths of decision trees and neural networks, aiming to improve both accuracy and interpretability in machine learning applications, particularly in fields like finance and medicine where justifiable predictions are essential. Previous attempts to merge these two paradigms often resulted in either a loss of accuracy or interpretability. The authors introduce Neural-Backed Decision Trees (NBDTs), which replace the final linear layer of a neural network with a differentiable sequence of decisions and a surrogate loss. This structure encourages the model to learn high-level concepts while minimizing reliance on uncertain predictions.

The results demonstrate that NBDTs can match or exceed the performance of state-of-the-art neural networks on datasets such as CIFAR and ImageNet, with improvements in generalization to unseen classes by up to 16%. Additionally, the surrogate loss contributes to an accuracy enhancement of up to 2%. Beyond accuracy, NBDTs enhance interpretability by clearly delineating the model's decision-making process, thereby fostering greater human trust and aiding in the debugging of datasets.

The paper critiques existing explainable AI methods like saliency maps for their inability to elucidate the model's decision-making process and highlights the limitations of previous integrations of deep learning with decision trees. NBDTs address these challenges by maintaining properties such as sequential, discrete decisions and pure leaves, which contribute to a clearer understanding of the model's credibility. The authors adopt a specific definition of interpretability that emphasizes the ability of humans to validate model predictions, which is crucial for debugging and trust-building. The proposed method is flexible, allowing for the integration of NBDTs with any existing classification neural network without requiring architectural changes.
