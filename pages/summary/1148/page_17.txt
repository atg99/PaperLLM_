This paper presents a comparison of different inference modes and training methodologies for neural networks, particularly focusing on the performance of their proposed models, NBDT-H and NBDT-S, against traditional neural networks (NN) across various datasets including CIFAR10, CIFAR100, and TinyImageNet. 

The results indicate that soft inference outperforms hard inference across all tested models, as evidenced by the accuracy metrics shown in Table 7. Specifically, while the NN model with a WideResNet28x10 backbone achieved the highest accuracy on CIFAR10 (97.62%), the NBDT models closely followed, with NBDT-S achieving 97.55%. For CIFAR100, NBDT-S also outperformed NBDT-H, achieving 82.97% compared to 82.21%. In TinyImageNet, soft inference methods also demonstrated superior performance.

In addition, the paper highlights the effectiveness of tree supervision loss (TSL) compared to hierarchical softmax (HS) and no extra loss terms (None). Table 8 shows that TSL consistently yields higher accuracy across datasets and inference modes, with notable improvements especially in hard inference scenarios.

The experimental setup emphasizes the importance of hyperparameter tuning, where the soft loss terms are weighted differently based on the dataset. The authors utilized a variety of training techniques, including SGD with specific momentum and weight decay parameters, and adjusted training durations and batch sizes. For cases where baseline accuracy could not be achieved, they fine-tuned pretrained models with modified learning rates.

Overall, the findings suggest that implementing tree supervision loss and soft inference can significantly enhance model performance in neural network training and evaluation.
