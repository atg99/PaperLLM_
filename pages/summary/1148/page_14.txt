The paper presented at ICLR 2021 discusses the interpretability of neural network models through the lens of node hypotheses and their meanings. The authors explore how different models, specifically WideResNet28x10 and ResNet10, induce class hierarchies based on their weights, which may not always align with visual attributes of the classes.

The authors propose a four-step method to assess a node's visual meaning. First, a hypothesis regarding the node's meaning is established, such as distinguishing between Animals and Vehicles. Second, a dataset containing unseen classes relevant to the hypothesis is collected. Third, samples from this dataset are passed through the node to check if they align with the hypothesis. Finally, the accuracy of the hypothesis is determined by the proportion of samples correctly classified.

The findings demonstrate that the WideResNet model achieves high classification accuracy (80-95%) when predicting unseen classes from the CIFAR100 validation set, validating the hypothesis that it correctly distinguishes between Animals and Vehicles. In contrast, the ResNet model's hierarchy does not accurately group visually similar classes, indicating that lower accuracy may lead to less interpretable results. The authors argue that higher accuracy in models like WideResNet contributes to better interpretability of decisions, suggesting that improved model performance does not compromise interpretability but enhances it.
