The paper discusses a series of surveys aimed at evaluating the effectiveness of different explainability techniques in image classification tasks, particularly under challenging conditions such as blurred images from the CIFAR10 dataset. The authors highlight that when participants were presented with saliency maps and class probabilities, only a small fraction of incorrect predictions were recognized. In contrast, the NBDT (Non-Parametric Bayesian Decision Tree) approach allowed respondents to identify nearly three times as many misclassifications due to its comprehensive output of predicted classes and probabilities leading to the final decision.

Despite this, a significant number of NBDT predictions remained unrecognized as incorrect, primarily due to errors occurring at the final binary decision stage, which were not visible to participants. In a follow-up survey involving severely blurred images, human performance was generally poor, with only 27.2% accuracy without model guidance. However, providing predictions from both the original neural network and the NBDT improved accuracy to 42.5%. Participants exhibited a greater trust in NBDT predictions compared to those based on saliency maps, with 52% of responses favoring NBDT.

Further analysis revealed that a majority of respondents preferred NBDT explanations over saliency maps when determining which model to trust, particularly in cases of misclassification. The study also addresses issues of ambiguous labels in datasets such as ImageNet, emphasizing the need for careful identification of faulty labels to enhance model performance. Overall, the findings suggest that NBDT offers more reliable and trusted explanations for model predictions compared to traditional saliency techniques.
