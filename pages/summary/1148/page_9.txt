The paper presents Neural-Backed Decision Trees (NBDTs), which demonstrate significant improvements in both accuracy and interpretability compared to traditional neural networks like ResNet18. NBDTs achieve over 16% better generalization and outperform or match state-of-the-art models on several datasets, including CIFAR10, CIFAR100, TinyImageNet, and ImageNet. 

A key contribution of the paper is the introduction of "path entropy," which effectively identifies ambiguous labels in images. The authors show that NBDT's approach to entropy highlights instances where intermediate decisions reveal uncertainty among multiple plausible classes, in contrast to standard neural networks that tend to assign high certainty to one class even in ambiguous situations. This ability to distinguish ambiguous labels enhances the model's interpretability, as it allows for deeper insights into decision-making processes.

The findings suggest that NBDTs can bridge the gap between accuracy and interpretability, challenging the notion that improving one comes at the expense of the other. The paper concludes that NBDTs not only enhance classification performance but also provide a framework for more trustworthy AI systems in practical applications.
