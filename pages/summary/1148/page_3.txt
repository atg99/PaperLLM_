This paper presents Neural-Backed Decision Trees (NBDTs), a novel approach that integrates decision trees within neural networks to enhance classification accuracy, particularly in scenarios with uncertain decisions. The key distinction between hard and soft decision trees is highlighted, where hard trees make definitive choices at each node, leading to irrecoverable errors if an early mistake occurs. In contrast, soft trees utilize probabilities to navigate paths, allowing them to overcome initial misclassifications by relying on more certain decisions later on.

NBDTs replace the final linear layer of a neural network with a decision tree structure that leverages path probabilities for inference. The method involves several steps: first, samples are featurized using a neural network backbone; then, the final layer operates as an oblique decision tree with modified decision rules. These rules include seeding tree weights from neural network weights and calculating node probabilities via softmax inner products. The final class prediction is derived from the probabilities of reaching each leaf through its path from the root, allowing for better handling of uncertainty.

The authors argue that this architecture improves performance by reducing overfitting through the hierarchical nature of decision trees and by effectively learning high-level decisions. The experimental results demonstrate that NBDTs outperform traditional decision trees, achieving significantly better accuracy while maintaining the benefits of neural network architectures.
