In this paper, the authors present a novel recurrent neural network architecture termed AntisymmetricRNN, which is derived from a continuous-time model represented as an ordinary differential equation (ODE). The core of this model relies on an antisymmetric weight matrix, Wh - WT_h, which contributes to the stability of the system by ensuring that the Jacobian matrix remains with purely imaginary eigenvalues. This characteristic fulfills the critical stability criterion outlined in the paper.

The authors derive a discrete version of the ODE using a naive forward Euler discretization, leading to the formulation of the AntisymmetricRNN. They emphasize that the antisymmetric matrix has reduced complexity, allowing for a parameter-efficient representation by constraining Wh to be a strictly upper triangular matrix.

However, the authors recognize a potential instability in the forward Euler method due to the nature of the eigenvalues. To address this, they introduce a diffusion mechanism by modifying the transition matrix to include a small positive value, thereby ensuring that the eigenvalues possess slightly negative real parts, which enhances stability.

Additionally, the paper discusses the integration of gating mechanisms within the AntisymmetricRNN framework. The authors highlight the importance of maintaining the critical stability condition while implementing gating, as demonstrated in prior works on LSTM networks.

Overall, the study contributes to the understanding of stability in recurrent neural networks and proposes a method to enhance both parameter efficiency and numerical stability in training these models.
