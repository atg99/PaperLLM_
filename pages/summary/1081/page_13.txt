The paper discusses the properties and experimental setup of Antisymmetric Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs). It establishes that certain mathematical relationships imply that all eigenvalues of a specific matrix related to the model are imaginary. The experimental section details the initialization of input and hidden matrices, the choice of biases, and the optimization methods used, including SGD with momentum and Adagrad. The parameters for the models, such as step size and diffusion, are specified, and training iterations differ based on the dataset. The performance is measured by classification accuracy on standard datasets like MNIST and CIFAR-10.

Additionally, the paper presents visualizations that compare the dynamics of vanilla RNNs with Antisymmetric RNNs, emphasizing the random behavior of the former and the predictable nature of the latter. The results indicate that while the dynamics of Antisymmetric RNNs can become noisier with independent Gaussian input, the overall trend remains consistent.
