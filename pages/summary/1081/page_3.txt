This paper, presented at ICLR 2019, introduces an initialization scheme for recurrent neural networks (RNNs) by aligning the initial gate bias with the range of time dependencies to be captured. The authors build upon the dynamical systems perspective of residual networks (ResNets), as explored by previous works that interpret ResNets as ordinary differential equations (ODEs). This interpretation allows for parameter learning in networks to be viewed as a parameter estimation problem related to ODEs.

The paper provides a concise overview of ODEs, focusing on first-order ODEs where the solution involves a single variable, time. The authors discuss the initial value problem associated with ODEs and highlight that analytic solutions are often unattainable, necessitating numerical methods such as the forward Euler method for approximating solutions. The method involves discretizing the ODE and iteratively updating the hidden state of the RNN, effectively transforming the ODE into a recurrent network framework.

The forward Euler method is shown to converge under certain conditions, and the authors illustrate its application through an example involving the hyperbolic tangent activation function. This approach allows for the design of RNN architectures that inherit desirable properties from the underlying dynamical systems, such as stability.

The paper further delves into the stability of ODE solutions in the context of antisymmetric RNNs, emphasizing the importance of stability in addressing challenges like vanishing and exploding gradients, which are common issues in deep learning. The authors suggest that their framework can lead to more robust RNN designs by leveraging the theoretical foundations of dynamical systems.
