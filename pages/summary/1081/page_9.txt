The paper presents a novel perspective on the trainability of recurrent neural networks (RNNs) by linking them to ordinary differential equations (ODEs). It introduces AntisymmetricRNNs, a new architecture derived from ODE discretization, which aims to address the exploding and vanishing gradient problems commonly faced by traditional RNNs like LSTMs.

Experiments were conducted using noise-padded CIFAR-10 datasets to analyze the performance of AntisymmetricRNNs compared to LSTMs. The results, illustrated in Figure 2, show that LSTMs exhibit vanishing gradients as indicated by eigenvalues approaching zero with increased time steps, leading to training failures. In contrast, AntisymmetricRNNs maintain eigenvalues centered around 1, demonstrating their robustness against gradient issues, although high diffusion constants can lead to similar challenges.

An ablation study further validated the importance of the antisymmetric weight matrix in performance, revealing that replacing it with unstructured matrices resulted in decreased effectiveness. The paper concludes by emphasizing the theoretical advantages of AntisymmetricRNNs and suggests that future research could explore other stable ODEs and numerical methods to develop improved recurrent architectures. Overall, the work aims to bridge the gap between RNNs and dynamical systems, promoting advancements in both fields.
