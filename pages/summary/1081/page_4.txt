The paper discusses the relationship between the stability of ordinary differential equations (ODEs) and the trainability of recurrent neural networks (RNNs). It introduces a new RNN architecture designed to maintain stability while effectively capturing long-term dependencies in data. Stability is defined formally, indicating that the long-term behavior of an ODE solution should not be significantly influenced by small perturbations in initial conditions. The stability condition is connected to the eigenvalues of the Jacobian matrix, with a specific focus on ensuring that the real parts of these eigenvalues do not exceed zero.

The paper argues that while stability is necessary, it is not sufficient for maintaining long-term dependencies, as systems with non-positive real eigenvalues can lead to information loss over time. To address this, the authors propose a "critical criterion," which requires that the real parts of the eigenvalues be non-negative, thereby preserving information while ensuring stability.

The authors further explore how stability impacts the trainability of RNNs through sensitivity analysis of the ODE solutions. By establishing that the Jacobian of the hidden state with respect to the initial state remains approximately constant under the critical criterion, they demonstrate that this architecture avoids issues related to exploding or vanishing gradients.

To construct RNNs that meet the critical criterion, the paper highlights the use of antisymmetric matrices, which inherently have imaginary eigenvalues, making them suitable for stable recurrent architectures. The proposed ODE formulation for the RNN incorporates these principles, aiming to enhance both stability and the ability to learn from long-term dependencies effectively.
