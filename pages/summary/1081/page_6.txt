The paper introduces AntisymmetricRNN, an architecture that incorporates an input gate (zt) to manage the flow of information into hidden states. The model's equations show that zt is defined using a sigmoid function and a Hadamard product, which allows it to function similarly to the input gate in LSTM and the update gate in GRU. This design choice helps maintain a modest increase in model parameters by sharing an antisymmetric weight matrix, rather than doubling them.

The authors highlight that the Jacobian matrix of this gated model retains a structure akin to a diagonal matrix multiplied by an antisymmetric matrix, which is crucial for maintaining the stability of the network. As a result, the eigenvalues of the Jacobian matrix remain close to zero, satisfying the critical criterion for stability.

The paper also includes a simulation section that visualizes the dynamics of various RNN configurations, both vanilla and with feedback, using different weight matrices. The visualizations illustrate the behavior of these networks over time with different initial states, emphasizing the unpredictability in the behavior of RNNs with random weight matrices. Overall, the findings suggest that AntisymmetricRNN can effectively manage information flow while preserving stability characteristics similar to existing gated architectures.
