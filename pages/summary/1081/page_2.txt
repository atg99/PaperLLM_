The paper presents a novel perspective on the trainability of Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) by exploring their relationship with ordinary differential equations (ODEs). It critiques existing methods that enforce orthogonality in weight matrices, noting their computational overhead and limited effectiveness in addressing exploding and vanishing gradients. The authors argue that these issues stem from the dynamics of the underlying systems, highlighting that exploding gradients indicate instability while vanishing gradients arise from lossiness.

Key contributions include the introduction of the AntisymmetricRNN architecture, designed by discretizing ODEs, which effectively captures long-term dependencies while being simpler than traditional RNN enhancements. The AntisymmetricRNN demonstrates improved performance over standard Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks in tasks requiring long-term memory and matches their performance in short-term tasks with fewer parameters.

The paper reviews related work on RNN trainability, emphasizing various approaches to mitigate gradient issues, including gating mechanisms and specialized neural architectures. It also discusses efforts to constrain weight matrices for better conditioning of the back-propagated error signals, noting that orthogonal weights alone do not suffice to prevent gradient issues due to nonlinear activations.

Furthermore, the connection between dynamical systems and RNNs is explored, with references to previous studies demonstrating chaotic dynamics in LSTMs and GRUs. The authors build on this foundation to establish a clearer understanding of RNN behavior through the lens of dynamical systems theory. Overall, this work provides a significant advancement in RNN architecture design and the theoretical understanding of their dynamics.
