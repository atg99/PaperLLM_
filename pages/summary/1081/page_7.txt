This paper presents a study on the dynamics of hidden states in recurrent neural networks (RNNs), particularly focusing on antisymmetric RNNs. The authors explore the behavior of various weight matrices, including identity, orthogonal, and antisymmetric matrices, and their effects on the convergence of hidden states. The unique properties of the tanh activation function, characterized as a contractive mapping, lead to predictable trajectories of hidden states based on the eigenvalues of the weight matrices.

Key findings include the observation that while orthogonal matrices can lead to convergence towards the origin, antisymmetric matrices can generate circular trajectories that may exhibit numerical instability. This instability arises from the outward spiraling of states, which the authors address by introducing a small diffusion term to the weight matrix, stabilizing the trajectories.

The paper further evaluates the performance of antisymmetric networks on image classification tasks, specifically the MNIST dataset, where images are presented pixel-by-pixel. The network's ability to learn long-range dependencies is tested through a permuted version of the MNIST dataset. The authors compare the performance of their proposed models, AntisymmetricRNN and AntisymmetricRNN with gating, against established benchmarks like LSTM, highlighting the effectiveness of their approach in handling complex dependencies within the data.

Overall, the research demonstrates the potential of antisymmetric RNNs in maintaining stable dynamics while effectively addressing the challenges associated with long-range dependencies in sequential data.
