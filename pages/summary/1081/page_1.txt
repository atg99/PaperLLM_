In the paper "Antisymmetric RNN: A Dynamical System View on Recurrent Neural Networks," the authors Bo Chang, Minmin Chen, Eldad Haber, and Ed H. Chi explore the challenges of recurrent neural networks (RNNs) in capturing long-term dependencies due to issues like exploding and vanishing gradients. They propose the AntisymmetricRNN, a novel architecture that leverages the stability properties of ordinary differential equations to effectively model sequential data.

The authors argue that existing RNN improvements, such as gated architectures like LSTMs and GRUs, often add significant computational overhead. In contrast, the AntisymmetricRNN is designed to enhance trainability without such overhead. Through extensive simulations and experiments, the paper demonstrates that AntisymmetricRNNs exhibit more predictable dynamics and outperform traditional LSTM models in tasks requiring long-term memory while maintaining comparable performance in short-term dependency tasks. This work contributes to the understanding of RNNs by framing them within a dynamical systems perspective, which offers a promising direction for future research in sequential data modeling.
