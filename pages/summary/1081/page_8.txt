The paper presents the AntisymmetricRNN, a recurrent neural network architecture that demonstrates superior performance on both pixel-by-pixel MNIST and CIFAR-10 datasets compared to existing methods, particularly LSTM models. Table 1 shows that AntisymmetricRNN achieves higher accuracy with significantly fewer parameters, outperforming LSTMs while requiring only one-seventh of their parameters. The study indicates that imposing orthogonal weight constraints limits model capacity, while softening these constraints leads to modest performance improvements, yet AntisymmetricRNNs excel without such computational overhead.

In experiments on pixel-by-pixel CIFAR-10, the AntisymmetricRNN performs comparably to LSTMs, and its gated variant slightly surpasses LSTM accuracy, utilizing about half the parameters. The analysis reveals that short-term dependencies dominate this task, with LSTM achieving 48.3% accuracy by considering only the last eight rows of input.

The paper also introduces a more challenging task, noise padded CIFAR-10, which incorporates long-range dependencies by inputting Gaussian noise after the initial rows of the image. LSTMs struggle with this task, failing to train effectively, while AntisymmetricRNNs manage to perform reasonably well, further demonstrating their efficiency and effectiveness in handling complex temporal dependencies with fewer parameters.
