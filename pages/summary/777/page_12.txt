This paper presents a novel deep neural network designed for diagnosing Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD) using pre-processed fMRI time-series data. The proposed network operates in an end-to-end manner, integrating feature extraction, selection, and classification into a single model, which eliminates the need for hand-crafted features. This marks the first instance of a fully end-to-end deep learning approach utilizing functional connectivity for classifying a neurological disorder based on fMRI data.

The research aims to determine whether a deep learning model can effectively classify ADHD and outperform traditional machine learning methods. The authors highlight the advantages of deep learning, particularly its ability to learn discriminative features during training through multiple modules that progressively abstract representations.

The study utilizes resting state fMRI data from the NeuroBureau ADHD-200 competition, which includes 250 MRI datasets and accompanying phenotypic information such as age, gender, and IQ. The datasets were gathered from eight imaging sites, though the authors focused on data from three specific sites: NeuroImage, New York University Medical Center, and Peking University. The model is designed to process time-series signals of length 172, leading to the exclusion of data from sites that did not meet this criterion.
