The paper discusses the implementation of the Adam optimizer for training a deep learning model over 50 epochs, resulting in stable training loss convergence. The feature extractor and similarity measure networks are initialized with weights from a pre-trained FCNet, which undergo fine-tuning during training. The loss function used is cross-entropy, with the model distinguishing between ADHD subjects and healthy controls.

Different learning rates are applied for the feature extraction and classification networks, specifically 10^-5 and 10^-4, respectively. To assess the effectiveness of the proposed method, comparisons are made with various network architectures and state-of-the-art techniques, including an end-to-end model without functional connectivity, FCNet, a clustering method, and a correlation method.

The importance of functional connectivity in cognitive processes is highlighted, referencing studies that suggest altered connectivity can serve as a biomarker for identifying and classifying brain disorders. This foundation supports the integration of functional connectivity in the proposed model architecture, emphasizing its relevance in the research.
