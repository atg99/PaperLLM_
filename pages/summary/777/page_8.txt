The paper discusses the application of various imaging modalities, specifically MRI and fMRI, in classifying disorders such as Autism and ADHD. It highlights two studies that utilize multiple imaging techniques, employing methods like histogram of oriented gradients and minimum redundancy maximum relevance for feature selection, followed by support vector machine classifiers for predictions. Both studies, however, do not incorporate functional connectivity, which is a crucial aspect of fMRI data.

In contrast, the proposed work focuses solely on fMRI and aims to leverage functional connectivity for ADHD prediction. The results achieved are comparable to those of the previous studies while using only a single imaging modality. The paper also critiques independent component analysis (ICA) methods, noting their limitations, such as the difficulty in interpreting independent components and the challenges associated with selecting the number of components and threshold values, particularly in fMRI applications where prior information is lacking.
