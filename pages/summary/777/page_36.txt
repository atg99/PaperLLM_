The paper presents a novel end-to-end network model that incorporates functional connectivity to classify Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD), marking the first application of such a model in this context. The analysis reveals that only a small fraction, less than 10%, of the top 100 feature maps from healthy individuals correspond to the top 500 feature maps in the ADHD classification. This highlights a significant distinction between the two groups. Furthermore, the findings indicate that the proposed model, despite having a large number of parameters, outperforms an alternative model that lacks functional connectivity and has fewer trainable parameters. This supports the conclusion that functional connectivity serves as a critical biomarker for identifying neurological disorders, with experimental results on the ADHD-200 dataset demonstrating superior performance compared to existing state-of-the-art methods.
