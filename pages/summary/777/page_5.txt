Resting state fMRI data is represented as a 4D tensor, where the brain's 3D volume is segmented into voxels, capturing the activity of each region over time. Functional connectivity refers to the synchronous activity between anatomically separated brain regions, indicating the strength of their communication. This connectivity has emerged as a crucial biomarker for distinguishing various brain disorders, including Alzheimer’s disease, epilepsy, and ADHD, as alterations in functional connectivity are indicative of underlying mechanisms associated with these conditions.

Resting state fMRI has become a valuable neuroimaging tool for exploring the functional activity of brain regions and identifying connectivity changes linked to disorders such as epilepsy, schizophrenia, ADHD, Alzheimer’s, and Parkinson’s disease. ADHD affects 5-10% of young children and can lead to significant lifetime impairment and a poor quality of life for affected individuals and their families. The mechanisms underlying ADHD remain unclear, and the diagnosis process can be lengthy and reliant on subjective observations.

In response to these challenges, the paper proposes a deep learning architecture designed for diagnosing ADHD. This end-to-end trainable network utilizes pre-processed time-series signals as input and generates predicted labels as output, incorporating a functional connectivity network to enhance diagnostic accuracy.
