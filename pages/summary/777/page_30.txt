The paper discusses the comparison between two end-to-end models for classifying ADHD: one with functional connectivity and one without. The model with functional connectivity has 502,751 trainable parameters compared to 386,665 in the model without it. Despite the higher parameter count, the model with functional connectivity demonstrates superior performance, indicating its significance as a biomarker for ADHD classification.

The study also evaluates the necessity of the classification network. An experiment was conducted using a pre-trained feature extractor alongside a functional connectivity network, without the proposed classification network, to assess its importance. Elastic Net was utilized to extract discriminant features from functional connectivity, followed by an SVM classifier for accuracy evaluation.

Additionally, the paper explores the functional connectivity network's role through clustering and correlation methods. Clustering was employed to calculate functional connectivity between brain regions, which was then fed into the classification network for performance evaluation. Similarly, correlation was used to calculate functional connectivity, followed by the application of the classification network. Overall, these experiments underscore the critical role of functional connectivity in enhancing ADHD diagnosis and classification accuracy.
