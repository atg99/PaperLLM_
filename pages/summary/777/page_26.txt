The proposed method demonstrates superior performance compared to average accuracy results from competitive teams, as well as the highest accuracy recorded for individual sites. It also outperforms the state-of-the-art FCNet method. However, the distribution of healthy controls and ADHD cases in the training and testing datasets varies, which is crucial for classifier performance. If the majority class shifts in the testing data, performance can significantly decline. The baseline accuracy for various sites indicates that the proposed method, DeepFMRI, surpasses these baselines, particularly for Peking and NYU, while showing similar results to FCNet for Peking. Despite the overall improvements, classification accuracy remains low for ADHD datasets, with competing studies averaging around 50%. This reduced accuracy may be attributed to the heterogeneous nature of the data and differing scan parameters across sites, such as conditions during imaging.
