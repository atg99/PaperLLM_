This paper presents DeepFMRI, an end-to-end deep learning architecture designed for the diagnosis of Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD) using resting state fMRI data. The motivation behind this research stems from the challenges associated with ADHD diagnosis, which currently relies heavily on behavioral analysis and lacks comprehensive understanding of its underlying mechanisms.

The proposed DeepFMRI framework consists of three sequential networks: a feature extractor, a functional connectivity network, and a classification network. It processes pre-processed fMRI time-series signals and is trained using back-propagation to classify subjects as either ADHD or healthy controls. The paper emphasizes the significance of functional connectivity in enhancing classification accuracy and providing interpretable results.

Experimental results from the ADHD-200 dataset reveal that DeepFMRI outperforms existing state-of-the-art methods, achieving a classification accuracy of 73.1% for data from the New York University imaging site, with a specificity of 91.6% and sensitivity of 65.5%. The findings suggest that DeepFMRI could be a valuable tool for improving ADHD diagnosis through automated neuroimaging analysis.
