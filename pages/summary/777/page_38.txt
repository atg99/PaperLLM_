The proposed method aims to assign varying weights to individual functional connectivity features based on their significance in predicting class labels, specifically distinguishing between healthy controls and individuals with ADHD. The findings indicate that the frontal lobe exhibits the most discriminative power in ADHD classification, aligning with previous research linking this brain region to cognitive functions such as attention, memory, and mood regulation.

However, the study acknowledges limitations, notably the small sample size and heterogeneity across different imaging sites, which complicates the training of the network and affects classification accuracy. The distribution of class labels between healthy controls and ADHD patients varies across sites, presenting additional challenges for classifiers. While baseline classifier accuracy is relatively high at certain sites, the DeepFMRI method demonstrates marginally improved accuracy compared to this baseline, particularly in the Peking and NI datasets.

Future research will focus on applying this network to explore ADHD sub-groups based on factors like treatment response and clinical outcomes, without necessitating a redesign of the network. Additionally, there is interest in assessing the method's applicability to other disorders, such as epilepsy and Alzheimer's, while also addressing variability across imaging sites.
