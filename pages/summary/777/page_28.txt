The paper presents a comparison of classification accuracies for ADHD using imaging data from three sites: NYU, NI, and Peking. It highlights the performance differences when models are trained on individual sites versus a combined dataset. The accuracy results show that training on individual sites yields higher accuracies (NYU: 73.1%, NI: 67.9%, Peking: 62.7%) compared to training on the combined dataset (NYU: 65.8%, NI: 60.0%, Peking: 43.1%).

The proposed end-to-end method, which integrates a feature extractor, functional connectivity analysis, and a classification network, outperforms existing state-of-the-art methods. Although a statistical significance test could not be performed due to a limited number of subjects, the focus is on understanding the contributions of each subnetwork to the overall accuracy. Additional experiments were conducted to assess the impact of various components, including the functional connectivity network, and to compare different modeling approaches.

Overall, the findings suggest that the end-to-end method with functional connectivity is crucial for achieving better classification performance in ADHD diagnosis.
