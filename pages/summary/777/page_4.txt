In this paper, we introduce a novel end-to-end deep learning method that leverages functional connectivity for the classification of Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD), an approach not previously explored in existing literature. Our findings indicate that this architecture outperforms other state-of-the-art classification methods, with the frontal lobe identified as the most significant region contributing to the classification accuracy of ADHD.

The introduction highlights the complexity of the human brain as a network of interconnected regions that coordinate various bodily functions. Abnormalities in this connectivity are linked to different neurological disorders, making brain connectivity a valuable area for research in diagnosis and characterization. Among various imaging techniques, functional Magnetic Resonance Imaging (fMRI) stands out due to its non-invasive nature and excellent spatial resolution, making it ideal for studying brain activity and connectivity. Recent studies have demonstrated the effectiveness of fMRI in investigating brain networks relevant to neurological conditions, thereby reinforcing its relevance in our research on ADHD classification. 

In summary, our proposed deep learning model presents a significant advancement in the classification of ADHD through the analysis of functional connectivity, with promising implications for future research and clinical applications.
