This paper presents a deep learning architecture designed for the classification of Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD) based on functional connectivity measures from brain regions. The key contributions include the development of an end-to-end trained model, highlighting the significance of functional connectivity in achieving improved classification results, and achieving a new state-of-the-art accuracy on the ADHD-200 dataset. 

The authors note that this work extends a preliminary version published in a peer-reviewed conference by incorporating additional experimental results, exploring the impact of functional connectivity within their deep learning framework, and conducting anatomical analyses of the findings. Although the current study focuses solely on ADHD data, the proposed method is applicable to other neurological disorders such as schizophrenia and epilepsy.

The structure of the paper includes a review of related work in Section 2, an overview of the fMRI data and preprocessing steps in Section 3, a detailed description of the multi-stage network in Section 4, experimental validation and results in Section 5, anatomical analysis in Section 6, and concludes with Section 7. The related work section discusses various methodologies previously employed in ADHD research, including correlation methods, dimensionality reduction techniques, graph-based methods, clustering approaches, and deep learning strategies.
