The study investigates the differences in feature importance between healthy individuals and those with ADHD, as depicted through several visualizations. Figure 6 presents the top 100 feature importance maps for both classes, revealing distinct variations. Further analysis includes brain maps for each class shown in Figures 7 and 8, indicating that the network assigns different importance values to functional connectivity for each group.

A quantitative comparison was conducted by extracting the top 100 feature maps from the healthy class and cross-referencing them with the ADHD feature maps, with results illustrated in Figure 9. The findings indicate that fewer than 10% of the top 100 healthy feature maps appear in the top 500 ADHD feature maps. A similar analysis was performed in reverse, as shown in Figure 10, confirming that less than 10% of the ADHD top 100 feature maps overlap with the healthy class.

These results suggest significant differences in functional connectivity between the two groups, indicating that certain brain network structures are weighted differently based on the class. Additionally, the learned feature importance maps were analyzed in relation to inter-lobe and intra-lobe distributions, with results displayed in Figure 11. The analysis shows that the frontal lobe contains a higher number of discriminant features for both classes, while the distribution across other lobes varies, highlighting the unique neural characteristics associated with each class.
