The paper describes a method for enhancing the classification of brain functional connectivity using a deep learning framework. The key operation utilized is M(i) = w1vi1 / (1 + w2vi2), where vi1 and vi2 are outputs from similarity measure networks, and w1 and w2 are weights constrained by w1 + w2 = 1. To simplify the model and reduce training parameters, the authors set w1 = 1 and w2 = 0, ensuring that the functional connectivity is directly passed to the classification network. 

The output is interpreted as the functional connectivity mapping of all brain regions, utilizing features from a pre-trained feature extractor network. Instead of random weight initialization, the authors leverage weights from a pre-trained FCNet, maintaining architectural consistency between the feature extractor and similarity measure networks. 

During training, a small learning rate of 10^-5 is employed to preserve the original characteristics of the functional connectivity network while allowing for necessary adaptations to the classification task. The effectiveness of this approach is evaluated by comparing the weights of the pre-trained functional connectivity network with those after training, resulting in a maximum weight difference of 5% across three sites, indicating that the original characteristics are preserved. The training of FCNet is conducted without the inclusion of test data, ensuring the integrity of the evaluation process.
