The paper presents an evaluation of a proposed end-to-end network for classifying imaging data across three different sites: NYU, NI, and Peking. The results indicate classification accuracy, specificity, and sensitivity for each imaging site, with NYU achieving the highest accuracy of 73.1%, followed by NI at 67.9% and Peking at 62.7%. The study utilized the ADHD-200 dataset, which provided separate training and testing datasets for each site, ensuring that the test data was independent from the training data. 

The metrics are defined as sensitivity being the ratio of true positives to the total of true positives and false negatives, while specificity is the ratio of true negatives to the total of true negatives and false positives. Despite concerns regarding the small number of subjects at each site (226 for NYU, 48 for NI, and 85 for Peking), the model demonstrated strong generalization capabilities on independent test datasets. This aligns with recent literature suggesting that deep neural networks can effectively generalize even with limited data.

Furthermore, the paper intends to compare the proposed method's performance with state-of-the-art techniques, with results to be detailed in subsequent sections.
