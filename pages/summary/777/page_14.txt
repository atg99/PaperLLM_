This paper presents an end-to-end deep learning model designed for classifying Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD) using pre-processed functional Magnetic Resonance Imaging (fMRI) time-series signals. The study details scan parameters utilized across three imaging sites: NYU, NI, and Peking, specifying the number of slices, repetition time (TR), echo time (TE), slice thickness, field of view (FoV), and flip angle.

The preprocessing steps undertaken include the removal of the first four time points, slice time correction, motion correction, registration to Montreal Neurological Institute space at a resolution of 4x4x4 voxels, followed by bandpass filtering and smoothing with a Gaussian filter. The brain is segmented into 90 regions using the Automated Anatomical Labeling (AAL) template, which is favored over other atlases due to its lower dimensionality, despite the existence of alternatives that segment the brain into 351 regions.

The proposed model's architecture is inspired by FCNet, which is utilized to extract functional connectivity from the fMRI data. The paper emphasizes the use of the AAL template for brain parcellation, supported by citations of previous studies that have successfully implemented this method. Further details on the data and preprocessing can be found on the competition website referenced in the study.
