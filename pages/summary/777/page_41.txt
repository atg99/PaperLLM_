The cited works discuss various aspects of neuroimaging and its implications for understanding neurological conditions. 

M. D. Dez-Cirarda et al. (2018) investigate dynamic functional connectivity in Parkinson's disease patients, differentiating between those with mild cognitive impairment and normal cognition, highlighting how connectivity patterns can inform clinical understanding of the disease. 

Wu et al. (2009) explore changes in the functional connectivity of the motor network in Parkinson's disease during resting state, suggesting alterations that could impact motor function. 

Harpin (2005) and Nigg (2013) analyze the effects of Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD) on individuals and their communities, detailing the long-term implications of the disorder on health outcomes and quality of life. 

Riaz et al. (2018) present a deep learning approach for classifying fMRI data, emphasizing the potential of advanced machine learning techniques in neuroimaging research. 

Nuñez-Garcia et al. (2015) focus on identifying functional-anatomical discriminative regions for resting state fMRI characterization, contributing to the understanding of brain function and structure relationships. 

Zou and Hastie (2005) introduce the elastic net method for regularization and variable selection, which has applications in analyzing complex neuroimaging data. 

Lastly, Tabas et al. (2014) apply spatial discriminant independent component analysis to resting state fMRI, providing insights into brain activity patterns. 

Together, these studies underscore the importance of neuroimaging in understanding neurological disorders and the potential of machine learning to enhance analysis and interpretation.
