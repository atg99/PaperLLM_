The study presents an overview of a dataset used for analyzing brain imaging data related to healthy controls and individuals with ADHD, as detailed in Table 1. The dataset comprises training and testing groups from different imaging sites, including NYU, NI, and Peking, with varying numbers of subjects and time-series signal lengths. Notably, the NYU site had the highest number of subjects (226) and a signal length of 172, which was established as the fixed input length for the deep learning methodology employed in this study. Signals shorter than 172 were discarded, and longer signals were truncated to maintain uniformity.

Additionally, the study acknowledges the variability in scan parameters and equipment across imaging sites, which introduces complexity to the dataset. For instance, different conditions were applied during data acquisition, such as whether subjects were instructed to keep their eyes open or closed. The experiments utilized pre-processed data from the connectome project, ensuring consistency in the analysis. The preprocessing was conducted using established tools like AFNI and FSL, highlighting the rigorous methodology behind the data preparation.
