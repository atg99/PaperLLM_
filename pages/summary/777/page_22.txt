This paper discusses various methods for calculating functional connectivity in brain regions using pre-processed fMRI signals. The correlation method is employed as a baseline for comparison, where Elastic Net feature selection is applied to identify discriminant features, followed by classification using a Support Vector Machine (SVM). 

Additionally, a clustering method is highlighted, which is deemed more sophisticated than correlation due to the sparsity of the networks it produces. The authors use the Synthetic Minority Over sampling Technique (SMOTE) to tackle dataset imbalance, also applying Elastic Net for feature extraction and SVM for classification of healthy versus ADHD subjects.

A significant focus of the study is on the interpretability of functional connectivity models, addressing common criticisms regarding deep networks being "black boxes." The authors emphasize the importance of understanding the differences in connectivity between healthy controls and patients, which can provide insights into neurological conditions. They demonstrate this by ranking individual functional connectivity values based on their contribution to predicting class labels, thus establishing a measure of feature importance that reveals the strength of each connection in the context of the classification task.
