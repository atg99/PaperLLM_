This paper discusses various neuroimaging markers relevant to epilepsy, focusing on the use of functional and structural magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) techniques. It highlights the importance of these imaging modalities in identifying neurophysiological characteristics associated with epilepsy. 

The authors reference several studies that have explored similar methodologies in different contexts, such as the identification of attention deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) and autism using MRI data combined with personal characteristics. For instance, Ghiassian et al. and Sen et al. developed models to detect ADHD and autism through structural and functional MRI analysis.

Additionally, the paper mentions advancements in deep learning techniques, as highlighted by LeCun et al., and their applications in predicting diseases like Alzheimer's through neuroimaging. The work of Payan and Montana on 3D convolutional neural networks for Alzheimer's prediction is also noted, demonstrating the trend towards utilizing AI in neuroimaging for clinical diagnosis.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the potential of combining neuroimaging data with advanced computational models to enhance diagnostic accuracy in neurological disorders, including epilepsy, ADHD, and autism, paving the way for improved clinical decision-making.
