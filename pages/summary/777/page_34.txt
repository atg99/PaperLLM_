This paper explores the involvement of the frontal lobe in cognitive functions, including attention, executive function, mood, memory, self-awareness, and personality. Alterations in the frontal lobe can lead to abnormal behaviors in these areas, particularly in individuals with ADHD. The study highlights connectivity changes in the frontal, temporal, and occipital cortices in ADHD patients, supporting previous research findings.

The authors present a novel end-to-end deep neural network designed for classifying ADHD using fMRI data. The model processes pre-processed time-series signals from fMRI to predict classification labels, aiming to determine if such classification tasks can be effectively addressed using this approach. The study includes visualizations comparing feature importance maps between healthy individuals and those with ADHD, emphasizing the significant differences observed in brain activity patterns associated with each class.
