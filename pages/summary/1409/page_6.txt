The paper presents DiffusionDet, a novel approach to object detection that demonstrates significant flexibility in using varying numbers of proposal boxes and iterative refinement steps. The experiments were conducted using the COCO 2017 dataset, comparing DiffusionDet with DETR under different configurations.

In the first section of the results, the impact of the number of boxes during evaluation was analyzed. DiffusionDet consistently improved performance with an increased number of boxes, achieving a 1.0 Average Precision (AP) gain when transitioning from 300 to 4000 boxes. In contrast, using cloning strategies with DETR resulted in a performance drop, highlighting the limitations of fixed queries in DETR. Alternative strategies such as concatenating random queries also led to significant performance declines as the number of evaluation boxes increased.

The iterative evaluation further showcased the advantages of DiffusionDet. Performance enhancements were noted as the number of iteration steps increased, with models using fewer random boxes benefiting more from additional refinements. For instance, using 100 random boxes, the AP improved from 41.9 to 46.1 over eight steps, indicating the model's capacity for iterative learning.

Additionally, the paper explores zero-shot transfer learning capabilities of DiffusionDet by evaluating COCO-pretrained models on the CrowdHuman dataset. It was observed that DiffusionDet outperformed previous methods with substantial AP gains when increasing the number of evaluation boxes or iteration steps, demonstrating its robustness in densely populated scenarios.

Finally, the benchmarking results in comparison to state-of-the-art detectors on the COCO dataset affirm DiffusionDet's competitive performance, solidifying its potential as a versatile tool for various object detection tasks without the need for additional fine-tuning. The findings underline DiffusionDet's adaptability to different environments, confirming its value in both sparse and crowded settings.
