The paper introduces DiffusionDet, a novel approach to object detection utilizing diffusion models. It frames the detection task as a generative process focused on predicting the positions and sizes of bounding boxes in images. During training, Gaussian noise is added to ground truth boxes, resulting in noisy boxes that are used to extract Region of Interest (RoI) features from a feature map. The detection decoder is then trained to predict the original boxes from these noisy inputs. 

DiffusionDet stands out for its flexibility, allowing it to handle varying numbers of evaluation boxes and iterative evaluation without needing additional fine-tuning. This is demonstrated through experiments on the CrowdHuman dataset, where DiffusionDet shows significant performance improvements compared to traditional methods when adjusting evaluation parameters. Furthermore, on the COCO dataset, DiffusionDet achieves competitive results, outperforming Faster R-CNN and DETR, and matching Sparse R-CNN.

The contributions of the paper include the introduction of a generative denoising process for object detection, the ability to decouple training and evaluation stages, and extensive evaluations across different datasets, showcasing the method's effectiveness in zero-shot transfer scenarios. Additionally, the paper reviews related work in object detection and diffusion models, highlighting the gap in applying diffusion models to discriminative tasks, which DiffusionDet aims to fill.
