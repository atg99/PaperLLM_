The paper discusses DiffusionDet, a novel object detection framework that utilizes a multi-stage detection decoder to refine box predictions through iterative sampling. After each sampling step, predicted boxes are categorized into desired and undesired predictions. The desired predictions accurately represent objects, while undesired predictions are randomly distributed. To enhance inference alignment with training, the authors propose a box renewal strategy that replaces undesired boxes with new random samples from a Gaussian distribution.

DiffusionDet distinguishes itself from previous multi-stage detectors, such as Cascade R-CNN, by allowing the entire detection head to be reused multiple times, leading to improved performance. In contrast, prior methods typically processed each stage only once without parameter sharing.

The experimental evaluation is conducted on datasets including COCO, LVIS, and CrowdHuman. The authors report metrics such as box average precision (AP) over various IoU thresholds, demonstrating DiffusionDet's flexibility in adapting to different inference scenarios without the need for retraining. The implementation details outline the training process, including the use of pre-trained backbones, optimization settings, and data augmentation strategies.

Overall, DiffusionDet showcases significant advantages in flexibility and performance through its unique design and methodology, making it suitable for diverse object detection tasks.
