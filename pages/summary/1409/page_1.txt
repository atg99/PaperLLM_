The paper introduces DiffusionDet, a novel framework that approaches object detection as a denoising diffusion process, transforming noisy bounding boxes into accurate object boxes. During training, the model learns to reverse the diffusion of object boxes from ground-truth to a random distribution. In the inference phase, it progressively refines a set of randomly generated boxes to yield the final detection results. This framework is characterized by its flexibility, allowing for a dynamic number of boxes and iterative evaluation. Experimental results demonstrate that DiffusionDet outperforms established detectors, achieving significant gains in average precision, particularly in a zero-shot transfer setting from COCO to CrowdHuman. The authors emphasize that DiffusionDet simplifies the detection process by eliminating the need for heuristic object priors and learnable queries, instead relying on a random box initialization that is iteratively refined. The paper positions this work within the broader context of advancements in object detection and the emerging use of diffusion models in perception tasks.
