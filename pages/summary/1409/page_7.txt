The paper presents a comparative analysis of various object detection methods on the COCO 2017 validation set and LVIS v1.0 validation set, highlighting the performance of the proposed DiffusionDet model. The results show that DiffusionDet, particularly with the ResNet-50 backbone and using a single iteration with 300 evaluation boxes, achieves an Average Precision (AP) of 45.8, outperforming established methods like Faster R-CNN, RetinaNet, and Sparse R-CNN. Increasing the number of iterations and evaluation boxes consistently enhances performance, with the model achieving an AP of 52.5 when using the Swin-Base backbone.

The analysis also details performance improvements across different configurations, indicating that DiffusionDet benefits from larger backbone architectures, evidenced by the model's performance with ResNet-101 and Swin-Base. Notably, the paper acknowledges that while DiffusionDet shows strong results, it still lags behind more advanced methods like DINO, suggesting potential for incorporating additional techniques to enhance its capabilities.

In the LVIS experiments, DiffusionDet demonstrates significant performance gains compared to traditional methods, particularly with iterative evaluations. The use of federated loss further boosts the performance of other detectors, showing the effectiveness of this approach in addressing challenges posed by sparse annotations in the dataset. Overall, the results underscore the robustness of DiffusionDet across different datasets and configurations, indicating its potential as a leading object detection framework.
