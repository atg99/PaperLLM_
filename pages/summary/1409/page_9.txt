The paper presents DiffusionDet, a novel object detection paradigm that approaches the task as a denoising diffusion process, transforming noisy boxes into accurate object boxes. The method demonstrates significant advantages, including flexibility in the number of boxes and iterative evaluation, allowing the use of the same network parameters for different evaluation scenarios without the need for retraining.

In comparative experiments, DiffusionDet outperforms established models on standard benchmarks. Specifically, it achieves an average precision (AP) of 90.1 on the CrowdHuman dataset using a single step with 1000 boxes, surpassing Sparse R-CNN. Increasing the number of boxes and iteration steps further enhances performance, indicating that DiffusionDet benefits from these adjustments.

The paper also highlights the robustness of DiffusionDet against variations in random seeds, showing consistent performance across multiple independent training instances. Despite its promising results, the paper acknowledges potential challenges with sampling speed due to the fundamental diffusion strategy employed. Future work will explore advanced diffusion strategies to improve efficiency.

In conclusion, DiffusionDet represents a significant advancement in object detection, demonstrating strong performance and flexibility, and is supported by research funding from the National Key R&D Program of China and the General Research Fund of Hong Kong.
