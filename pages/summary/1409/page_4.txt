The paper presents DiffusionDet, a novel approach to object detection that employs a diffusion process for training and inference. The training process begins by encoding image features and padding ground truth boxes to a fixed number of instances. The ground truth boxes are then corrupted by adding Gaussian noise, with the noise scale managed by a monotonically decreasing schedule. The model is trained to reverse this corruption, predicting box locations and classifications.

Key aspects of the training algorithm include the use of a detection decoder that operates iteratively, differentiating it from Sparse R-CNN, which relies on a fixed set of learned boxes. The decoder is designed to process noisy boxes and refine predictions through a series of cascading stages.

During inference, DiffusionDet starts with randomly sampled boxes from a Gaussian distribution and progressively refines these predictions through multiple sampling steps. The model's ability to estimate box locations and classifications is enhanced by incorporating a time-step embedding that guides the iterative evaluation process.

Overall, the proposed method demonstrates improved performance in object detection by effectively leveraging the principles of diffusion and noise management, which are critical for enhancing the signal-to-noise ratio in the detection task.
