This paper investigates the spectral bias of neural networks, revealing their tendency to prioritize learning low-frequency functions. The authors utilize Fourier analysis to demonstrate that deep networks, while capable of fitting arbitrary functions, exhibit a frequency-dependent learning speed that favors global patterns over local fluctuations. This preference aligns with the idea that over-parameterized networks learn simple, generalizable patterns more readily.

The paper introduces the concept of spectral bias, highlighting how lower frequency components are learned faster and are more resilient to parameter perturbations. Interestingly, the authors also explore the relationship between the complexity of the data manifold and the learning of higher frequencies, finding that increased manifold complexity can facilitate the learning of these higher frequencies.

Key contributions of the work include an analysis of the Fourier spectrum of ReLU networks, empirical evidence supporting the spectral bias, and a theoretical framework that explains how complex manifold shapes influence learning dynamics. The findings suggest that while neural networks can approximate a wide range of functions, their learning process is inherently biased towards simpler, low-complexity solutions.
