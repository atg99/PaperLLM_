The paper discusses the spectral bias of neural networks, exploring how neural networks can exhibit varying biases towards different frequencies of the functions they approximate. The authors thank several individuals for their feedback, indicating a collaborative effort in refining their research. 

Key references are provided, highlighting foundational works in neural network theory and approximation, including the universal approximation capabilities of neural networks and the role of activation functions. The research emphasizes the importance of understanding how neural networks process information in the frequency domain, which can influence their performance in tasks such as sampling, optimization, and generalization.

The authors provide insights into the implications of spectral bias for the design and training of neural networks, suggesting that certain architectures may favor specific spectral characteristics, which can affect their learning dynamics and the types of functions they can effectively approximate. This understanding could guide future developments in neural network architecture and training methodologies.
