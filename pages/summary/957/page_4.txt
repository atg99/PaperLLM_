The paper investigates the spectral bias of neural networks during training, focusing on how these networks learn to prioritize different frequency components of a target function. It presents two scenarios: one where all frequency components have equal amplitudes and another where higher frequencies have larger amplitudes. The findings indicate that even when higher frequencies dominate, neural networks tend to learn lower frequencies first. This behavior is supported by an observed increase in the spectral norm of the weights as the model fits higher frequencies, consistent with theoretical expectations.

Additionally, the paper examines the robustness of the learned model's spectrum to parameter perturbations. It shows that lower frequencies exhibit greater resilience to these perturbations compared to higher frequencies. This phenomenon is further explored in the context of noise added to the target function, particularly in experiments involving the MNIST dataset. The results reveal that validation performance is adversely influenced by low-frequency noise, while high-frequency noise has a negligible impact on validation accuracy.

The paper includes visual representations of the learning process, showing the evolution of the learned function in relation to the target function over multiple iterations. These insights contribute to a deeper understanding of how neural networks interact with different spectral components during training and highlight the implications of noise frequency on model performance.
