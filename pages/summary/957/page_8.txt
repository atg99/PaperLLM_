The paper investigates the spectral bias of deep ReLU neural networks, particularly focusing on their behavior in approximating functions on an interval [0,1]^m using Fourier series representation. It establishes a relationship between the frequencies of coordinate functions and the network's capacity to express high frequencies on data manifolds. The findings indicate that as the frequency mode of the coordinate functions increases, the neural network can express larger frequencies in the latent space while maintaining lower frequencies in the input space. 

The paper builds on existing literature that highlights the universal approximation capabilities of neural networks, showing that while these networks can approximate complex functions, they exhibit a preference for simpler, low-frequency functions. This phenomenon, termed spectral bias, suggests that deep networks prioritize learning smooth patterns from data during training. 

Additionally, the paper contrasts deep neural networks with other algorithms like kernel machines and K-nearest neighbors, noting that DNNs strike a balance between smoothness and expressivity, learning smoother functions than KNNs due to their faster spectrum decay.

In conclusion, the authors advocate for further exploration of neural networks in the Fourier domain, as it provides valuable insights into function sensitivity and model complexity. This research opens avenues for understanding the underlying mechanics of neural network learning biases and their implications for function approximation.
