The paper explores the spectral bias of ReLU neural networks by analyzing their structure through Fourier representation. ReLU networks are characterized as continuous piece-wise linear (CPWL) functions, which can represent a wide range of functions due to their universal approximation properties. The authors present a detailed decomposition of the Fourier transform of ReLU networks, revealing that it can be expressed in terms of the linear regions defined by the network's activation patterns.

Key findings include the formulation of the Fourier components of a ReLU network, which indicates that the spectral decay is anisotropic in high dimensions. Most directions exhibit a decay rate of k^(-d+1), while specific directions orthogonal to the faces of the linear regions can show slower decay rates, such as k^(-2). Furthermore, the numerator of the Fourier expression is bounded by the number of linear regions and the Lipschitz constant of the network, which is influenced by the spectral and max norms of the weight matrices across layers.

The paper concludes that the spectral properties of ReLU networks are complex and highlight the impact of the network's architecture on its behavior in the Fourier domain. This understanding of spectral bias is crucial for improving the design and training of neural networks.
