The paper explores the spectral bias of neural networks, particularly focusing on how noise frequency and amplitude impact training and validation performance. It presents findings from several experiments, notably highlighting that low-frequency noise significantly affects network performance more than high-frequency noise. Early in training, networks tend to fit low-frequency components of the target function, leading to a dip in validation loss, while learning higher-frequency components occurs later. This observation aligns with prior research, indicating that networks exhibit robustness to high-frequency noise amplitude, as increasing it does not substantially degrade performance.

Experiment 4 introduces a method to analyze the frequency response of the network by projecting its function onto the space of orthonormal eigenfunctions of the Gaussian RBF kernel. This approach allows for a deeper understanding of the spectral characteristics of the learned functions. The results illustrate that the validation loss dip corresponds to the point at which the network has yet to learn the higher-frequency components of the noise.

The discussion emphasizes that these findings on spectral bias are relevant even with complex, high-dimensional data, suggesting that not all data manifolds are learned equally by neural networks. Overall, the research underscores the importance of frequency characteristics in the training dynamics of neural networks.
