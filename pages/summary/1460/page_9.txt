This paper introduces Task-Oriented Dialogue BERT (TOD-BERT), which is trained on a diverse set of nine human-human and multi-turn task-oriented datasets encompassing over 60 domains. The evaluation results demonstrate that TOD-BERT significantly outperforms BERT across four dialogue-related tasks: intention classification, dialogue state tracking, dialogue act prediction, and response selection. Notably, in response selection tasks, TOD-BERT-jnt achieves 65.8% accuracy for 1-to-100 and 87.0% for 3-to-100 metrics on the MWOZ dataset, surpassing BERT by substantial margins. The results also indicate that TOD-BERT excels in few-shot scenarios, making it advantageous when limited labeled data is available.

The paper further includes a visualization of embeddings using t-distributed stochastic neighbor embedding (t-SNE), showing that TOD-BERT-jnt provides clearer group boundaries compared to BERT and TOD-BERT-mlm. Quantitative analysis using K-means clustering reveals higher normalized mutual information (NMI) scores for TOD-BERT compared to BERT, indicating better alignment with true domain labels.

In conclusion, TOD-BERT is an effective model for various dialogue tasks, offering ease of deployment and accessibility for the NLP research community, as it will be open-sourced for further applications and fine-tuning in task-oriented conversational problems.
