The paper presents an evaluation of dialogue act prediction and dialogue state tracking across various models and datasets, specifically focusing on the performance of different variations of the BERT architecture. The results are detailed in tables comparing micro-F1 and macro-F1 scores for dialogue act prediction on MWOZ, DSTC2, and GSIM datasets, as well as joint goal accuracy and slot accuracy for dialogue state tracking on MWOZ 2.1.

Key findings indicate that TOD-BERT consistently outperforms traditional BERT and other baseline models across different datasets and evaluation metrics, particularly in full data scenarios. In few-shot learning contexts, TOD-BERT-mlm shows significant improvements over BERT, achieving a 3.5% increase in micro-F1 and a 6.6% increase in macro-F1 on the MWOZ corpus with just 1% of the training data. Notably, performance remains robust even with 10% of training data, nearing that of full data training.

The paper also includes a tSNE visualization to illustrate the representation differences among BERT, TOD-BERT-mlm, and TOD-BERT-jnt in relation to system responses, indicating distinct clustering based on dialogue acts and domains.

In addition, the response selection task is assessed using k-to-100 accuracy, aligning with community standards for evaluating task-oriented dialogues. Overall, the findings underscore the effectiveness of TOD-BERT models in advancing dialogue systems, particularly under limited training data conditions.
