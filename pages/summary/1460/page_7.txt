The paper presents an evaluation of various models for intent recognition, dialogue state tracking, and dialogue act prediction, specifically focusing on the performance of TOD-BERT variants compared to traditional models like BERT, GPT2, and others. 

In the intent recognition task, results indicate that TOD-BERT-jnt outperforms other models in all categories, achieving a notable 13.2% improvement in all-intent accuracy and 16.3% in-domain accuracy in the 1-shot setting compared to BERT. The evaluation metrics include accuracy for both in-domain and out-of-scope intents, demonstrating the effectiveness of the TOD-BERT models.

For dialogue state tracking, the paper highlights the superiority of TOD-BERT-jnt over BERT, showing a 2.4% increase in joint goal accuracy on the MWOZ 2.1 dataset. The researchers created a new ontology to address gaps in the original dataset, further validating the performance of TOD-BERT.

In dialogue act prediction, the experiments across multiple datasets reveal that TOD-BERT consistently outperforms baseline models, including MLP and RNN. The micro-F1 and macro-F1 scores are provided to illustrate the models' efficacy in multi-label classification tasks.

Overall, the findings underscore the advantages of task-oriented dialogue pre-training with TOD-BERT across various dialogue processing tasks, establishing it as a leading model in the field.
