The paper introduces TOD-BERT, a pre-trained model specifically designed for task-oriented dialogue, addressing the limitations of existing language models when applied to this domain. The authors highlight the significant differences in linguistic patterns between general text and task-oriented dialogue, which can hinder the performance of traditional pre-trained models like BERT in dialogue applications.

To enhance the modeling of dialogue behavior, TOD-BERT incorporates user and system tokens into its masked language modeling. A contrastive objective function is proposed to better simulate the response selection task, which is crucial for task-oriented dialogues that have clear goals, such as booking a restaurant or purchasing tickets. The model is trained on a unified dataset that combines nine multi-turn task-oriented dialogue datasets, resulting in approximately 100,000 dialogues and 1.4 million utterances across more than 60 domains.

The experimental results demonstrate that TOD-BERT outperforms strong baseline models, including BERT, on four key downstream tasks: intention recognition, dialogue state tracking, dialogue act prediction, and response selection. Furthermore, TOD-BERT exhibits a stronger few-shot learning capability, which helps mitigate issues related to data scarcity in task-oriented dialogue scenarios. The findings support the hypothesis that self-supervised pre-training on task-oriented dialogue corpora yields better representations for related downstream tasks compared to existing pre-trained models.
