The paper evaluates the performance of PiTree, a method designed to convert advanced Adaptive Bitrate (ABR) algorithms into decision trees while maintaining Quality of Experience (QoE). Three QoE metrics are considered: linear, logarithmic, and hierarchical, with specific parameters defined for each. The evaluation utilizes network traces from various sources, including Norway's HSDPA, US FCC broadband, and Oboe, ensuring a broad comparison across different network conditions.

The paper compares PiTree against state-of-the-art ABR algorithms: RobustMPC, which uses Mixed Integer Linear Programming; Pensieve, which employs Reinforcement Learning; and HotDASH, an extension of Pensieve using neural networks. The performance of PiTree is measured by the QoE ratios between the original algorithms and those generated by PiTree, revealing that the average performance degradation is minimal, averaging around 3%. This indicates that PiTree effectively mimics the original algorithms while simplifying their implementation.

Results show that PiTree can accurately predict bitrate selection at an individual level, with accuracy rates of 80% for RobustMPC, 90% for Pensieve, and 82% for HotDASH. The paper also discusses the overhead of implementing PiTree, highlighting a significant increase in page size when using original ABR algorithms, which can lead to impractical loading times.

Overall, the findings demonstrate that PiTree can convert sophisticated ABR algorithms into efficient decision trees with negligible performance loss, making it a promising approach for enhancing video streaming applications.
