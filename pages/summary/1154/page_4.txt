To mitigate this issue, PiTree leverages imitation learning, where the original ABR algorithm (the teacher) provides corrective feedback for the decisions made by the decision tree (the student). This process allows the decision tree to learn from its mistakes and gradually improve its decision-making capabilities across the entire state space.

The methodology involves three main steps: 
1. Initialization, where state-action pairs are gathered through simulations of the original ABR algorithm using a virtual player.
2. Training the decision tree with these pairs using the CART algorithm, which focuses on minimizing a normalized square loss to ensure that predictions align closely with the teacher's actions.
3. Continuous simulation of the decision tree, where the teacher's feedback helps refine the student’s decision-making, allowing the decision tree to adapt to new states it had not previously encountered.

Overall, PiTree aims to create an effective decision tree representation of complex ABR algorithms, enhancing their performance by enabling them to learn from real-world scenarios and adapt to changing conditions in the streaming environment. This process not only improves decision accuracy but also helps maintain seamless video playback for end-users.
