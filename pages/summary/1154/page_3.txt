This paper discusses the limitations and challenges of Adaptive Bitrate (ABR) algorithms, particularly in the context of offloading computation to remote servers. The authors highlight that while many current ABR solutions offload complex computations to remote servers, this leads to increased operational expenses for content providers, making large-scale deployment impractical. The paper presents load testing results indicating that the capacity of ABR servers is limited, supporting the argument that the introduction of remote servers can significantly raise operating costs, potentially reaching millions of dollars.

To address these challenges, the authors propose a method called PiTree, which aims to implement sophisticated ABR algorithms directly on client-side video players. The design choices for PiTree focus on using decision trees due to their ability to approximate complex decision boundaries effectively, their lightweight nature for implementation, and their alignment with the decision-making processes inherent in ABR algorithms. 

The paper outlines the decision tree training algorithm based on imitation learning and discusses the theoretical analysis of the optimization loss associated with PiTree. It emphasizes the need for a scalable and high-performance solution that allows ABR designers to focus on optimizing performance without being hindered by implementation practicality. 

Overall, the authors argue for a shift towards local implementations of ABR algorithms to enhance scalability and reduce costs, while providing a detailed exploration of the design and training processes of PiTree as a potential solution.
