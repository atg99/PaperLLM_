This paper addresses the challenges of implementing sophisticated Adaptive Bitrate (ABR) algorithms for video streaming, particularly in client-side video players. Current methods, such as FastMPC, which attempts to pre-compute solutions for all network states and use a lookup table, suffer from significant performance degradation compared to baseline algorithms. Additionally, offloading computation to remote ABR servers, as seen in systems like Pensieve and HotDASH, increases operational costs and complicates scalability for content providers.

To tackle these issues, the authors propose PiTree, a general framework that utilizes decision trees to convert complex ABR algorithms into lightweight, efficient representations. This approach aims to maintain high performance while enabling scalable implementation. The authors leverage imitation learning, where the original ABR algorithm serves as a teacher to guide the decision tree, ensuring a faithful conversion of sophisticated algorithms. They provide a theoretical analysis of the optimization loss associated with using decision trees for online video processing.

The key contributions of the paper include:

1. Identifying the practical challenges in implementing sophisticated ABR algorithms for content providers.
2. Introducing PiTree as a scalable framework that bridges offline algorithm design and online implementation.
3. Presenting an imitation learning-based training algorithm for decision trees and analyzing its optimization loss.
4. Evaluating PiTree through simulations and real-world implementations, showing that it can effectively convert advanced ABR algorithms into decision trees with minimal performance loss.

PiTree is positioned as the first framework enabling the practical deployment of state-of-the-art ABR algorithms on client-side players, potentially facilitating the adoption of new sophisticated algorithms that enhance streaming performance.
