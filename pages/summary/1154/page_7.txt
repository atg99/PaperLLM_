The paper investigates the performance and efficiency of PiTree, an adaptive bitrate (ABR) decision-making framework, in comparison to traditional rate-based ABR algorithms. Key findings are organized into three main areas: page size, decision latency, and memory utilization.

1. **Page Size**: The introduction of PiTree leads to a modest increase in page size, with a maximum increase of only 13% even when using 2000 leaf nodes, which is negligible. For decision trees with 100 leaf nodes, the increase is approximately 0.6%, adding only 0.01 seconds to page load time, thus maintaining user experience.

2. **Decision Latency**: The decision-making latency of PiTree is significantly lower than that of original algorithms, measured at less than 1 millisecond compared to 1 second for traditional methods. This reduction is critical as high latency can degrade quality of experience (QoE) by delivering outdated information, potentially stalling video playback.

3. **Memory Utilization**: The runtime memory increase for PiTree is less than 7% across various decision trees, which is minimal compared to other video player components. This indicates that PiTree maintains efficient memory management while implementing its enhanced decision-making capabilities.

Additionally, the paper discusses the operational expenses (OPEX) associated with server-based ABR solutions. PiTree, being client-based, avoids these costs, offering substantial savings for large content providers. The offline training process for PiTree, while taking up to 2.3 hours for initial setup, is deemed acceptable as it is performed only once during the design phase.

Finally, a sensitivity analysis highlights that the prediction accuracy of PiTree's decision trees remains robust as the number of leaf nodes is varied from 100 to 2000, affirming its effectiveness across different configurations. Overall, PiTree presents a promising alternative to traditional ABR algorithms, balancing performance with operational efficiency.
