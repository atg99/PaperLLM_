This paper introduces PiTree, a framework designed to enhance the practical implementation of sophisticated Adaptive Bitrate (ABR) algorithms in real-world scenarios. PiTree employs offline imitation learning to convert various ABR algorithms into decision trees, achieving high performance while maintaining low runtime overhead and minimal deployment efforts.

The analysis includes experiments on three representative ABR algorithms: RobustMPC, Pensieve, and HotDASH. Results indicate that while the accuracy of PiTree can initially decline due to a lack of samples, it stabilizes as the number of leaf nodes increases, demonstrating a robust performance that is largely unaffected by the number of nodes beyond a certain threshold. Additionally, the generalization capability of PiTree is highlighted, showing that decision trees trained on different traces maintain consistent performance during testing, with a median normalized Quality of Experience (QoE) ratio exceeding 97%.

The paper also discusses related work in ABR algorithms, noting the diversity in methodologies, including buffer-based and ML-based approaches. It emphasizes that PiTree can integrate with existing techniques for further improvements without incurring significant costs or complexities typically associated with deploying more sophisticated models, such as neural networks.

In conclusion, PiTree represents a promising advancement in making ABR algorithm implementations more efficient and accessible, potentially accelerating the development of new ABR strategies. The authors acknowledge contributions from peers and funding support from notable research programs.
