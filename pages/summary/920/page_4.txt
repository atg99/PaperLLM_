The paper proposes a novel deep metric learning architecture, referred to as DML-ALA, which integrates a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) with a Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) module to enhance the training process of a metric model. The architecture includes a fully connected layer following the CNN, acting as a metric for assessing tuples of data.

A key feature of the approach is the introduction of a sequence-aware learnable sample assessment strategy, denoted as SA. This strategy generates adaptive weights for each tuple based on the current model's status, allowing the training to dynamically adjust as learning progresses. The authors argue that traditional sampling strategies, such as hard mining, often rely on prior knowledge and fail to adapt throughout the training stages, potentially harming model generalization.

The learnable assessor takes input tuples and a state variable, outputting a weight that influences the training process. The state variable encodes information from previous training steps, facilitating a more informed assessment and interaction with the metric. The LSTM network processes concatenated embeddings of tuples, refining knowledge at each step to produce real-valued weights.

The training is framed as a meta-learning problem, where the assessor not only guides the metric training but also learns simultaneously to enhance its generalization capability. The authors emphasize that the assessor acts as an optimizer for the metric, addressing common issues of overfitting in deep metric learning by maintaining robustness against variations in real-world data.

In conclusion, the DML-ALA architecture aims to improve the adaptability and performance of deep metric learning through a sophisticated, learnable assessment strategy that evolves during training.
