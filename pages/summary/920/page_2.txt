This paper addresses the challenges of sampling strategies in deep metric learning, particularly the limitations of fixed methods like hard mining, which may not be effective throughout the entire training process. The authors introduce an Adaptive Learnable Assessment (ALA) method that dynamically adjusts sampling to enhance the generalization ability of the trained metric. ALA employs a soft weighting scheme for tuples, allowing for a range of weights between 0 and 1, rather than binary weights based on hardness.

The proposed method incorporates a sequence-aware assessor that utilizes knowledge from previous inputs and the current model status. The authors posit that overfitting in existing deep metric learning methods hampers their success, and they frame the learning of the assessor as a meta-learning problem aimed at maximizing generalization. They implement an episode-based training scheme that simulates the training and testing process, which allows the metric and assessor to work together towards improving generalization.

The paper reviews relevant literature on deep metric learning, highlighting trends in loss function design and sampling strategies. It emphasizes the impact of sample quality on training convergence and performance, noting that many existing methods rely on pre-defined sampling strategies that lack flexibility.

Experimental results on benchmark datasets, including CUB-200-2011, Cars196, and Stanford Online Products, demonstrate that the ALA method improves upon traditional approaches in both image retrieval and clustering tasks. Overall, the paper presents a novel framework for adaptive sampling in deep metric learning, aiming to enhance model performance through a more sophisticated and flexible approach to training data selection.
