The paper presents experiments conducted on three benchmark datasets: CUB-200-2011, Cars196, and Stanford Online Products, focusing on evaluating a proposed method under a disjoint training/test set configuration. 

In the CUB-200-2011 dataset, consisting of 11,788 images of 200 bird species, the training set includes the first 100 species (5,864 images), while the test set includes the remaining 100 species (5,924 images). The Cars196 dataset contains 16,185 images of 196 car models, split into 98 models for training (8,054 images) and 100 models for testing (8,131 images). The Stanford Online Products dataset features 120,053 images of 22,634 products, with the training set comprising the first 11,318 products (59,551 images) and the test set the remaining 11,316 products (60,502 images).

For evaluation, metrics such as Recall@Ks for retrieval tasks and Normalized Mutual Information (NMI) and F1 for clustering tasks were employed. NMI measures the agreement between clusters and ground truth classes, while F1 is the harmonic mean of precision and recall.

Results indicate that the proposed method, Adaptive Loss Assessment (ALA), improves performance over traditional triplet loss approaches, particularly when using disjoint labels. ALA demonstrates a significant reduction in overfitting, achieving better test results despite similar training performance. The analysis of tuple weights reveals that ALA initially treats samples equally but adapts weights as training progresses, enhancing the model's ability to handle challenging tuples.

Overall, the findings suggest that both adaptive assessment and disjoint label usage contribute to improved performance in deep metric learning tasks.
