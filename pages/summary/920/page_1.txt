In this paper, the authors introduce a novel approach called Deep Metric Learning via Adaptive Learnable Assessment (DML-ALA) aimed at enhancing image retrieval and clustering tasks. The key innovation of DML-ALA lies in its adaptive sampling strategy, which replaces conventional fixed sampling methods, such as hard negative mining, with a sequence-aware learnable assessor. This assessor dynamically re-weights training examples to optimize the metric's generalization capabilities.

The learning process for the assessor is framed as a meta-learning problem, utilizing an episode-based training scheme. Each episode is constructed by sampling two subsets of disjoint labels, simulating both training and testing phases. The performance of a one-gradient-updated metric on the validation subset serves as the meta-objective for the assessor, allowing it to adapt continuously to the model's current status.

The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of DML-ALA through experiments conducted on established datasets, including CUB-200-2011, Cars196, and Stanford Online Products. The results indicate that the proposed method significantly improves the performance of deep metric learning compared to traditional approaches by leveraging an adaptive assessment strategy that optimally weights training examples.
