The paper argues that while human-imitative AI has garnered significant attention due to recent successes in areas like computer vision and robotics, the reality is that we are still far from achieving true human-imitative AI. Despite the media hype, the progress made thus far is limited. The authors contend that the excitement surrounding human-imitative AI can distract from addressing more pressing challenges within intelligent automation (IA) and intelligent infrastructure (II).

The paper emphasizes that achieving advancements in areas like self-driving cars will require tackling complex engineering problems that may not necessarily relate to human abilities. It posits that the future transportation system will resemble a sophisticated, data-driven control system rather than a direct imitation of human driving. Therefore, the authors suggest that focusing on human-imitative AI may not be the most effective path forward for solving significant IA and II issues.

Moreover, the notion that human-imitative AI is essential for progressing in IA and II lacks historical support. The authors argue that engineering disciplines have made strides without needing to replicate human workers. They also point out that human intelligence has inherent limitations, and thus, creating AI that mimics human reasoning may not be the optimal approach to address the complexities of modern decision-making and uncertainty in II contexts. 

In conclusion, the authors advocate for a broader perspective on AI development, one that prioritizes solving complex problems over merely imitating human intelligence.
