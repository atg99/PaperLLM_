The paper argues that while machine learning (ML) has significantly impacted various industries since the 1990s, the term "artificial intelligence" has been rebranded and often misrepresented. Historically, AI aimed to replicate human-level intelligence, a vision that remains largely unfulfilled. Current AI developments have primarily originated from engineering disciplines focused on low-level pattern recognition and statistical analysis rather than high-level cognitive reasoning.

The paper traces the roots of key algorithms, such as the backpropagation algorithm, back to control theory, suggesting that many so-called AI advancements are not aligned with the original aspirations of human-like intelligence. While ML and data science have transformed business operations and decision-making, the paper emphasizes that the anticipated revolution in AI, characterized by human-imitative capabilities, has yet to materialize. The author calls for a critical examination of the rebranding of AI and its implications for understanding the true nature of the technology and its capabilities.
