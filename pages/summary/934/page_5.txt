The paper discusses the current state of artificial intelligence (AI) and the ongoing developments in related fields. It emphasizes that while significant advancements have been made in specific engineering challenges—such as document retrieval, text classification, and recommendation systems—these are often grouped under the broad and vague label of AI. This simplification can obscure the complexities involved in understanding both the intellectual and commercial issues at play.

Over the past two decades, there has been notable progress in what is referred to as Intelligence Augmentation (IA), which utilizes computation and data to enhance human intelligence and creativity. Examples include search engines and natural language translation, which serve to complement human capabilities rather than replicate high-level reasoning.

The concept of Intelligent Infrastructure (II) is also introduced, describing a network of computation, data, and physical entities designed to improve human environments across various sectors like transportation and healthcare. This infrastructure seeks to facilitate deeper interactions between humans and systems, moving beyond mere connectivity to more sophisticated data analysis and interaction.

An illustrative example is provided, envisioning a comprehensive medical system that integrates data from various sources—such as genetics and environmental factors—to aid in diagnosis and treatment, thereby enhancing the collective intelligence of medical practitioners and improving patient care. This approach highlights the importance of understanding relationships within broader contexts rather than focusing solely on individual interactions.
