The paper discusses the current state of artificial intelligence (AI) and the necessity for a new engineering discipline that integrates computer science, statistics, and human-centered design. It emphasizes the need for a planetary-scale medical system capable of functioning without extensive human oversight, highlighting that existing educational frameworks lack the principles needed for such systems. The author argues that these principles are crucial not only in medicine but also across various sectors like commerce and transportation.

The text draws parallels between the development of civil and chemical engineering and the emerging field of AI, suggesting that this new discipline should build upon concepts such as information, algorithms, data, and optimization, while also incorporating insights from social sciences and humanities due to its focus on human-related data. 

Currently, the integration of these concepts is being done in an ad-hoc manner, leading to societal-scale systems that may exhibit significant flaws, akin to early engineering projects that lacked foundational principles. This situation is compounded by the public's vague understanding of AI, which often conflates it with machine learning (ML). The paper calls for a structured approach to the analysis and design of AI systems to better anticipate and mitigate potential flaws, ultimately enhancing human life through effective human-computer collaboration.
