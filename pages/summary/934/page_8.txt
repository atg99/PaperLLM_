The paper discusses the evolution and current state of artificial intelligence (AI), emphasizing the historical contributions of figures like John McCarthy and Norbert Wiener. It argues that the contemporary discourse around AI is often too narrow, focusing on specific industry applications rather than the broader implications and opportunities that AI presents. The author stresses the importance of incorporating diverse perspectives, particularly from social sciences, cognitive sciences, and humanities, into the conversation about technology and its societal impact.

The paper highlights that while the engineering aspects of AI are crucial, there is a need for a new engineering discipline that is human-centric. This discipline should ensure that the technologies developed are reliable and effective, particularly in critical areas such as healthcare and transportation. The author calls for a shift in how we conceive of engineering in the context of AI, advocating for an approach that prioritizes human values and societal needs. The piece concludes with a recognition of the potential for creating a new branch of engineering that aligns with these principles, while cautioning against the risks of overlooking the broader implications of AI development.
