It begins by contrasting the traditional encoder-decoder architecture with an attention-based architecture, highlighting how attention scores are computed to relate source tokens to target tokens during decoding.

The paper formulates the attention mechanism mathematically, defining a sequence of vector elements and detailing the steps for computing attention scores, normalizing them, and encoding the sequence into a context vector. Attention scores, derived from a task-specific pattern vector, indicate the quality of match between elements in the sequence, with different methods such as multiplicative, additive, and MLP used to measure pairwise similarity.

It emphasizes the importance of attention scores in determining the relevance of source tokens to target tokens, which is crucial for effective machine translation. The notation used throughout the paper is clarified, distinguishing between scalars, vectors, matrices, and tensors.

The discussion extends to variations of attention mechanisms, noting that while the basic form is widely used due to its simplicity, it may not suffice for more complex tasks. The paper introduces several advanced attention types, such as multi-dimensional, hierarchical, self-attention, memory-based, and task-specific attention, indicating that as task complexity increases, the mechanisms become more specialized.
