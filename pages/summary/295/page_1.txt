This paper provides a comprehensive survey of attention mechanisms in natural language processing (NLP), highlighting their evolution and application across various tasks. Initially inspired by human cognitive processes, attention mechanisms were adapted for machine translation to enhance token alignment, resulting in significant improvements in tasks such as sentiment classification, text summarization, question answering, and dependency parsing.

The introduction presents neural machine translation as a primary example, illustrating the limitations of traditional encoder-decoder architectures based solely on recurrent neural networks (RNNs). These limitations include the forgetfulness of RNNs and the lack of explicit alignment during decoding, leading to scattered focus across input sequences. The introduction of the attention mechanism addressed these issues by allowing the model to compute attention scores for input tokens, thereby creating a context vector that emphasizes relevant information while diminishing the impact of irrelevant data.

The paper outlines the mathematical formulation of the attention mechanism, detailing how context vectors are derived from attention scores and integrated into the decoding process. This mechanism enables improved memory retention and soft alignment, allowing the model to focus on critical elements of the input sequence.

The subsequent sections of the paper aim to detail the basic form of attention, discuss its variants tailored to specific tasks, and explore its relationship with other machine learning techniques, such as pre-training and ensemble methods. Overall, this survey serves as an introductory guide to understanding attention mechanisms and their transformative role in enhancing NLP applications.
