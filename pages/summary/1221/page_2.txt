This paper discusses the role and interpretation of control variables in regression analyses aimed at estimating causal effects. The authors argue that the estimated effect sizes of these control variables should not be interpreted causally, as they may be endogenous and represent a blend of various causal mechanisms impacting the outcome. Consequently, the paper recommends that researchers avoid interpreting the marginal effects of control variables and instead concentrate on the primary variables of interest, which can be more reliably identified. To mitigate the risk of misleading managerial or policy implications, it is suggested that the coefficients of control variables be explicitly labeled as non-causal or omitted from regression tables. Additionally, the authors caution against using estimates from control variables for further theory development or meta-analyses.
