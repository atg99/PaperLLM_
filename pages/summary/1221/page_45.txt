The paper discusses the impact of control variables in understanding the relationship between renewable portfolio standards (RPS) and state-level incentives on a dependent variable. It highlights that no significant relationship was found, potentially due to the timing of RPS implementation, which often occurred post-2004 with goals set for after 2020, while the data concluded in 2009. This suggests insufficient time to observe effects. Furthermore, the paper notes that provisions of the production tax credit (PTC) may limit the benefits of state incentives for certain projects.

Additionally, the paper examines the behavior of banks regarding dormancy in relation to control variables. It finds that older banks are more likely to become dormant, possibly due to past experiences with unfavorable policies. Conversely, banks that have performed well under such policies may be overconfident, leading to dormancy. Notably, banks with a higher deposits-to-liability ratio are less inclined to enter dormancy, indicating that a reliance on branch banking affects their operational decisions.
