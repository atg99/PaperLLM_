The paper discusses the implications of including control variables in empirical research, particularly in the context of career choice and participation in ATP (Association of Tennis Professionals) events. It highlights that while control variables are often used to account for potential confounding factors, their inclusion can sometimes obscure the true relationships being studied. The authors argue that careful consideration must be given to which variables are controlled for, as unnecessary controls may introduce bias or lead to misleading conclusions. The causal diagram presented in the paper illustrates the empirical strategy used in previous research by Azoulay et al. (2021), emphasizing the interconnectedness of various factors such as academic achievements, school reputation, and research potential in influencing career choices. Overall, the paper calls for a more nuanced understanding of the role of control variables in empirical analysis.
