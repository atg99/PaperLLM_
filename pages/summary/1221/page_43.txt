The excerpts from the papers highlight the role of control variables in understanding various organizational and economic phenomena. 

In Zhou (2015), it is noted that subsidiaries in higher GDP countries tend to experience local supervision, with telecommunications positively influencing this supervision, while tax rates have a negative, albeit statistically insignificant, effect. The inclusion of institutional quality showed a significant positive impact on local supervision.

Chen, Qian, and Narayanan (2017) emphasize the importance of competition, revealing that an increase in the number of competitors correlates with a 41% increase in the hazard of technology exit, indicating the competitive landscape's critical role in technology sustainability.

Lungeanu, Stern, and Zajac (2016) discuss how business line diversification negatively impacts the diversity of technology-sourcing portfolios, possibly due to resource allocation issues. They also observe that the size of the top management team negatively relates to diversification and dissimilarity.

Lastly, Liu and Wezel (2015) report that population age has a significant negative effect on growth rates, while market share positively influences growth, aligning with existing theories on resource partitioning and competition.

Overall, these studies illustrate the nuanced insights provided by control variables in analyzing organizational behavior and market dynamics.
