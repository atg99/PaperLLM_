The paper discusses the implications of controlling for various covariates in estimating the causal effect of participation in an Academic Training Program (ATP) on the choice of pursuing a research career. It emphasizes that controlling for factors such as prior research activities, academic achievements, experience, and school reputation is essential for obtaining valid causal estimates. However, the authors caution that the latent variable of research potential must not directly influence ATP participation, otherwise the results may be biased.

The analysis employs inverse probability weighting, where covariates are used solely for estimating the propensity score and not in the outcome regression. The paper highlights the endogeneity of prior research activities, suggesting that while they are valid controls, they are also influenced by the unobserved research potential. Therefore, a positive correlation between prior research activities and career choices should not be interpreted as evidence of causation, as both may be confounded by an individual's overall ability.

The authors stress that the research design is limited to making causal inferences about ATP participation, urging researchers to avoid overinterpretation of their findings, even if they seem to offer valuable insights into related research topics. The focus is primarily on career choice, intentionally setting aside other aspects of scientific career success explored by Azoulay et al.
