The paper discusses the implications of using control variables in research, particularly within the context of strategic management studies. It highlights the potential nuisance of control variables, arguing that their inclusion may not always enhance the validity of research findings. The authors provide a detailed analysis of various articles published in the Strategic Management Journal across different volumes and issues, showcasing the diversity in research approaches and the varying roles of control variables. The data presented in the tables indicates the number of articles, their authors, and the specific issues in which they were published, reflecting the ongoing discourse in strategic management literature regarding methodological rigor and the appropriate use of control variables. The paper ultimately calls for a critical examination of the necessity and impact of control variables in empirical studies, advocating for greater transparency and justification in their application.
