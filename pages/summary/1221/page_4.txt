The paper discusses the common expectation among reviewers and researchers that control variables should be treated with the same importance as primary independent variables. It cites recommendations from various scholars advocating for the reporting of control variable coefficients and their significance levels to enhance research transparency.

The authors highlight a consensus in organizational research suggesting that interpreting control variable estimates contributes positively to understanding effect sizes. However, they introduce a cautionary perspective based on causal diagrams, which can assist in identifying relevant control variables. The authors contend that, while control variables are crucial in causal research designs, they do not hold the same analytical status as main variables. They argue that valid controls can often be endogenous, which complicates their interpretation and potentially undermines the conclusions drawn from empirical analyses. This nuanced view challenges traditional methodological practices and calls for a more discerning approach to the role of control variables in research.
