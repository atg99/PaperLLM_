The paper discusses the implications of control variables in research, particularly in the context of strategic management. It highlights how the inclusion of control variables can sometimes obscure the primary relationships being studied, potentially leading to misleading conclusions. The authors emphasize the need for careful consideration when deciding which variables to control for, suggesting that unnecessary controls may introduce bias rather than mitigate it. The analysis is supported by data from various articles published in the Strategic Management Journal, showcasing trends and patterns in the use of control variables across multiple studies. The overall argument is that while control variables can be useful, their misuse can detract from the clarity and validity of research findings.
