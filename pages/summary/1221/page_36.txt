The paper discusses the implications of control variables in research, particularly in strategic management studies published in the Strategic Management Journal. It presents a summary of articles from Volume 36 of the journal, showcasing the number of articles published in different issues, alongside the authors of each article. The data indicates a varied number of contributions per issue, reflecting the diverse topics explored within the field. The mention of specific authors and their works highlights the ongoing discourse regarding the use of control variables and their potential nuisance in empirical research. The analysis suggests that while control variables are often deemed necessary for accurate modeling, they may complicate interpretations and introduce biases if not handled appropriately.
