The paper discusses the challenges and implications of using control variables in organizational research. It highlights that while control variables are often utilized to mitigate confounding effects, their misuse can lead to misleading conclusions and complicate the interpretation of results. The authors argue for a more judicious approach to the selection and reporting of control variables, emphasizing that researchers should critically assess their necessity and the potential for introducing bias. The paper advocates for best practices in research design, underscoring the importance of clear rationale and transparent reporting when employing control variables. This ensures that findings are robust and meaningful, contributing to more reliable organizational research outcomes.
