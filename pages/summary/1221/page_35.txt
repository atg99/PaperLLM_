The paper discusses the implications of using control variables in empirical research, particularly in the context of strategic management studies published in the Strategic Management Journal. It presents a detailed analysis of various articles across different volumes and issues, highlighting the total count of articles and the authors involved. The data indicates a consistent number of contributions from various authors over the years, reflecting the ongoing discourse on the role of control variables in research methodologies. The authors emphasize the potential nuisance that control variables can introduce, suggesting that their use may complicate analysis and interpretation of results. This critique aims to encourage researchers to carefully evaluate the necessity and impact of control variables in their studies to enhance the clarity and validity of their findings.
