The paper discusses the selection process for the Accelerated Training Program (ATP) during the Vietnam War era, highlighting how young physicians sought to fulfill their military service obligations. The selection involved a first screening followed by interviews at the NIH, where candidates were evaluated based on observable characteristics such as prior research activities, academic achievements, experience, and the reputation of their training institutions. Azoulay et al. contend that despite the highly selective nature of the applicant pool, the interview stage relied solely on these observable traits, making it challenging to assess candidates' future research potential. This setting enabled the authors to utilize a selection-on-observables design. Their findings indicate that ATP participants were significantly more likely to pursue research careers, resulting in higher publication and citation rates, increased grant funding, and greater recognition through prestigious awards, including the Nobel Prize and membership in the National Academy of Sciences.
