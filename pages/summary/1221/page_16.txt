The paper discusses the role and potential issues of control variables in research designs aimed at estimating causal effects. It argues that while treatment variables should not be endogenous to avoid biased estimates, control variables often are endogenous, complicating their interpretation. Researchers should select control variables to eliminate backdoor paths between treatment and outcome based on theoretical models, rather than attempting to account for all influencing factors of the outcome. The authors suggest that it may be more effective to control the treatment assignment mechanism when researchers have better institutional knowledge of what influences treatment take-up. The paper emphasizes that interpreting the effects of control variables can lead to misleading conclusions and recommends against making such interpretations in published research. This caution stems from the risk of deriving incorrect managerial advice and the potential for subsequent studies to inaccurately build theory on flawed empirical results.
