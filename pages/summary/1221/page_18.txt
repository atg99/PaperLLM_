The paper addresses the issue of control variables in research, emphasizing their potential to lead to invalid causal inferences despite their predictive power. It critiques the practice of comparing models with and without control variables, as suggested by previous studies, arguing that this approach can be misleading. The authors align with Westreich and Greenland's concept of the "table 2 fallacy," which highlights the misinterpretation of results when control variables are not causally identified. They recommend that researchers focus on a single causal factor at a time and treat control variables as nuisance parameters, thereby avoiding complications arising from omitted variables. Additionally, the paper warns against including potentially biased control estimates in meta-analyses, as this can hinder the aggregation of scientific knowledge. The authors reference Becker's work to illustrate how the systematic reporting of control variables is essential for effective meta-analysis, as non-reporting can limit the understanding of relationships between variables.
