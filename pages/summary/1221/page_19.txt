The paper discusses the limitations and challenges associated with the use of control variables in meta-analyses, particularly in the context of organizational research and economics. It highlights that while meta-regression can filter confounding influences, control variables such as extraversion and agreeableness do not possess causal interpretations. This limitation undermines their contribution to understanding effect sizes, as their coefficients may reflect a mix of various causal mechanisms affecting the outcome, such as citizenship behavior. 

The variability of coefficients based on different adjustment sets further complicates their utility. Consequently, the paper recommends that meta-analyses focus solely on main treatment variables for which a clear identification argument exists, emphasizing the distinction between treatment and control variables in regression analysis.

The authors conclude that researchers should not be concerned if control variable coefficients do not align with expected signs, as they are likely biased. Instead, the focus should be on the marginal effects of primary variables of interest. Control variable results can be minimized or included in appendices to avoid misleading causal interpretations, streamline discussion sections, and conserve manuscript space.
