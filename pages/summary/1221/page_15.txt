The paper emphasizes that attributing substantive meaning to the effects of biased control variables can lead to incorrect intuitions and misguided managerial or policy decisions. It argues for a more cautious approach, suggesting that researchers should refrain from discussing the results of control variables unless they can guarantee that all relevant influencing factors have been accounted for in their regression models. Given the practical challenges in achieving this, the authors recommend treating control variables as nuisance parameters, included solely for identification purposes. This perspective aligns with practices in non-parametric matching estimators and modern machine learning techniques, which typically do not report estimates related to control variables. The paper ultimately calls for a shift in how control variables are approached in organizational research to avoid misinterpretation and enhance the integrity of causal conclusions drawn from regression analyses.
