The paper discusses the role of control variables in causal analysis, highlighting their potential nuisance. It presents various causal diagrams to illustrate different scenarios where a control variable, Z1, is valid for controlling confounding effects in the relationship between variables X and Y. The diagrams demonstrate how the inclusion or exclusion of additional variables (Z2, Z3, Z4, Z5) can impact the validity of Z1 as a control variable. The analysis emphasizes the importance of carefully selecting control variables to avoid biases and ensure accurate causal inferences.
