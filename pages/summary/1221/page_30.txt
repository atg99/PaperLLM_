The paper discusses the challenges associated with the use of control variables in research, particularly in the context of strategic management. It highlights that while control variables aim to isolate the effects of primary variables of interest, they may introduce complexities and potential biases that can undermine the validity of research findings. The authors argue for a more nuanced understanding of when and how to use control variables, suggesting that their indiscriminate application can lead to misleading interpretations. The paper emphasizes the need for researchers to critically assess the necessity and impact of control variables in their analyses, advocating for a more thoughtful approach to enhance the robustness of empirical studies in strategic management.
