The paper discusses a structured approach to testing autonomous systems, divided into three stages: early-phase testing, transitional testing, and confirmatory testing. 

In early-phase testing, the focus is on verifying that integrated modules implement intended mappings and developing base models for offline testing. This stage includes identifying potential anomalies and ensuring that the system performs adequately in controlled environments.

Transitional testing follows, where the system is deployed in real-world settings under close oversight. The goals are to identify contexts of real-world failures and characterize environmental variations. This phase emphasizes active engagement and systematic exploration to understand system boundaries and improve performance.

Finally, confirmatory testing involves deploying the system with oversight to assess its reliability and alignment with user values. This stage is critical as many failures arise from discrepancies between the system's operational success and user expectations. Monitoring during this phase helps identify and address performance failures and value divergences, refining the system until it aligns with both the formal model and real-world contexts.

Overall, the paper advocates for dynamic certification throughout the system's lifecycle, enabling iterative testing and adjustments to ensure acceptable and safe behavior.
