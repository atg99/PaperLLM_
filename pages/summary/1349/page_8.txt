The paper discusses the implementation of dynamic certification for autonomous systems, emphasizing the integration of stakeholder values into modeling and testing processes. Unlike traditional approaches that rely on formal methods for guarantees post-construction, dynamic certification utilizes these methods to simulate values and constraints in real-world deployment scenarios. The authors argue for the necessity of feedback loops among formal models, simulation environments, and open-world deployments to ensure stakeholder values are upheld.

A key point made is that scalability concerns are alleviated in dynamic certification. Instead of requiring comprehensive testing of all system behaviors, the focus can be narrowed to behaviors that risk violating stakeholder values. This approach allows for partial proofs of safe deployment without needing to scale indefinitely. The paper suggests that certifying autonomous systems in socio-technical contexts requires an iterative process where early-phase models inform acceptable behaviors, even before hardware or software implementation.

By integrating modeling with testing, dynamic certification aims to provide credible assurances of correct operation and adapt design decisions based on exploratory testing outcomes. This proactive approach facilitates adjustments to design and context before finalizing the system, ultimately leading to improved decision-making and requirements. The authors conclude that while no certification can guarantee infallibility, the continuous testing inherent in dynamic certification can help identify and correct errors in formal models.
