The paper discusses the concept of dynamic certification for autonomous systems, highlighting the unique challenges posed by artificial intelligence. It emphasizes the need for structured approaches to requirements elicitation to overcome communication barriers among stakeholders. Key proposals include the development of modular methods to clarify the relationship between requirements and system behavior, and the enhancement of causal models to improve counterfactual reasoning during system design and certification.

Dynamic certification diverges from traditional methods by promoting exploratory testing and partial specification at each stage, allowing for better-informed decision-making among stakeholders. The paper advocates for interdisciplinary research that integrates robotics, control, learning, safety, security, resilience, testing, and formal methods to facilitate this approach.

Recent advancements such as compositional verification and operational data acquisition—like high-definition maps—are identified as critical to developing effective dynamic certification mechanisms. Ultimately, the paper argues that dynamic certification can lead to more robust, safer, and secure autonomous systems by fostering a common language among various models, simulations, and regulatory frameworks, despite being an under-explored area in the field.
