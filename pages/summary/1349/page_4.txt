The paper discusses the concept of dynamic certification for autonomous systems, emphasizing the need for a flexible approach that incorporates both modeling and testing throughout the system's life cycle. The strategy involves defining acceptable use-context pairs, thereby allowing designers to abstract complex behaviors and focus on high-level system interactions. This abstraction facilitates the identification of potential hazards and simplifies the design process.

Dynamic certification is framed around two core operations: modeling, which helps to document and visualize design choices and interactions, and testing, which assesses the real-world performance of the system. This approach addresses the limitations of traditional static certification, which can struggle when operational assumptions fail. By intertwining modeling and testing, dynamic certification enables continuous refinement of the system's model based on real-world feedback, thus enhancing the assurance of system behavior.

The paper outlines four essential components for specifying the base system model: system modules, operational contexts, behavior mappings for successful performance, and environmental variations for robustness. Dynamic certification is structured into three stages, allowing for iterative adjustments to these components based on testing outcomes. The first stage, early-phase testing, focuses on assessing the risks and benefits of deploying autonomous systems in various contexts, highlighting the importance of stakeholder interaction in establishing effective testing procedures. Overall, dynamic certification aims to ensure that autonomous systems can adapt and function reliably in unpredictable environments.
