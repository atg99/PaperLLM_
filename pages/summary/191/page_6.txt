The paper presents a comparative analysis of various colorization and image processing methods, specifically focusing on the proposed multi-code inversion approach, termed mGANprior. The qualitative results, illustrated in Figure 6, encompass several techniques: optimizing feature maps, Deep Image Prior (DIP), Zhang et al.'s method, and the authors' mGANprior. The quantitative evaluation, detailed in Table 2, measures the area under the curve (AuC) of cumulative error distribution over the ab color space for different methods applied to bedroom and church images. The results indicate that mGANprior outperforms the other methods in both scenarios, achieving AuC scores of 90.02% and 89.43%, respectively.

The paper emphasizes the advantages of using multiple latent codes, which specialize in inverting distinct meaningful regions of an image, thereby enhancing overall image composition. Furthermore, the mGANprior method is shown to facilitate various image processing tasks such as colorization, super-resolution, inpainting, denoising, semantic manipulation, and style mixing. 

In the image colorization task, the authors contrast their method with others, noting that while discriminative models like DIP struggle with low-level tasks, generative models provide richer and more satisfying results. Their method demonstrates comparable results to Zhang et al.'s colorization-focused approach, highlighting the potential of GANs for specific image categories.

Regarding image super-resolution, the authors conduct experiments with a significant scale factor of 16, comparing their method against DIP, RCAN, and ESRGAN. They utilize multiple metrics, including PSNR, LPIPS, and NIQE, to evaluate performance quantitatively. The results affirm the effectiveness of mGANprior in generating high-fidelity images across different applications, reinforcing the benefits of leveraging generative models in image processing tasks.
