The paper presents a quantitative comparison of different Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) inversion methods, focusing on their performance in reconstructing images from latent codes. The methods evaluated include optimizing a single latent code, learning an encoder, using the encoder for initialization, and the proposed mGANprior. The evaluation metrics used are Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR) and the Learned Perceptual Image Patch Similarity (LPIPS), where higher PSNR and lower LPIPS values indicate better performance.

Results from the comparison show that mGANprior outperforms all other methods across different models trained on various datasets, including LSUN bedroom, LSUN church, and CelebA-HQ. The superior performance of mGANprior is attributed to its ability to utilize multiple latent codes, which enhances detail recovery in the reconstructed images. The paper also discusses the trade-off between the number of latent codes and the quality of inversion, revealing that while increasing the number of codes improves performance, there is a limit beyond which no significant gains are observed.

Furthermore, the paper analyzes the impact of different composition layers within the GAN architecture on inversion quality. It finds that using higher layers generally yields better results, though these layers may not effectively capture high-level semantics. The authors visualize the contributions of individual latent codes, demonstrating that each code can focus on specific semantic regions within the image, thereby complementing each other in the reconstruction process.

Overall, the findings underscore the effectiveness of mGANprior in achieving high-fidelity GAN inversion through the strategic use of multiple latent codes and careful selection of composition layers.
