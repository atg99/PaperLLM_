The paper presents a comparison of various image inpainting and restoration methods, including single latent code inversion, feature map optimization, Deep Image Prior (DIP), and the authors' proposed method, mGANprior. The results indicate that mGANprior demonstrates comparable or superior performance to advanced learning-based methods. Specifically, it offers greater flexibility regarding the super-resolution (SR) factor, leveraging the PGGAN model's learned knowledge to enhance low-resolution images effectively.

In qualitative assessments, the authors highlight improved detail in reconstructed images, particularly in human eye features. Quantitative evaluations are conducted using PSNR and Structural Similarity Index (SSIM) metrics across different methods. For super-resolution tasks with an SR factor of 16, mGANprior achieves a PSNR of 26.93, outperforming DIP and ESRGAN, while maintaining a lower LPIPS score, indicating better perceptual quality.

In terms of image inpainting, mGANprior also excels, achieving higher PSNR and SSIM scores compared to other methods, both under center and random cropping conditions. These results collectively underscore the efficacy of mGANprior in various image restoration tasks, suggesting its potential for broader applications in the field.
