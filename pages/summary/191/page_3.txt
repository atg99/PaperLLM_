The paper presents a novel approach for GAN inversion utilizing multiple latent codes to enhance image reconstruction quality. Traditional GAN inversion, which aims to retrieve a suitable latent code to recreate a target image, often struggles due to the non-convex nature of the optimization problem. The authors propose leveraging multiple latent codes, denoted as {zn}Nn=1, to capture different image details and regions effectively.

The methodology is structured around two main components: feature composition and adaptive channel importance. Instead of directly combining images generated from each latent code in the image space, which can be ineffective, the authors suggest performing the combination in an intermediate feature space. The generator is divided into two sub-networks, allowing the extraction of spatial features from a selected intermediate layer for further integration.

The paper also emphasizes the importance of adaptive channel importance, represented by αn, which assigns different significance to each channel of the feature maps based on their role in generating specific visual elements. This results in a more precise alignment of the generated features with the target image's semantics.

The optimization objective is reformulated to simultaneously optimize both the latent codes and their corresponding channel importance scores, leading to a more effective reconstruction of the target image. The proposed method demonstrates improved performance in recovering intricate details, thus addressing the limitations of previous single latent code approaches.
