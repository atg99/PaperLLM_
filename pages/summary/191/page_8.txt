The paper introduces mGANprior, a method that utilizes multiple latent codes to enhance the reconstruction of real images using pre-trained Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs). The authors demonstrate that leveraging GANs as prior knowledge significantly improves performance in various image processing tasks, including image restoration, semantic manipulation, colorization, and inpainting.

In the experiments, the authors compare inversion results across different GAN models trained on specific datasets, highlighting the effectiveness of their approach in filling missing pixels and removing noise. They note that existing methods struggle with these tasks, while mGANprior successfully repairs corrupted images with meaningful content.

The paper also explores the concept of knowledge representation within GANs, emphasizing that single latent codes can be limiting, especially when there is a domain gap. By using multiple latent codes, the method can generate images that deviate from the original training domain, allowing for more versatile applications. The authors analyze the performance of their method at different layers of the GAN, concluding that higher layers provide better reconstructions for certain tasks due to their representation of abstract semantics and content details.

The results indicate that the best outcomes for colorization occur at the 8th layer, whereas the 4th layer is optimal for inpainting. This aligns with the observation that colorization is a low-level rendering task, while inpainting requires more detailed content filling.

In conclusion, mGANprior effectively employs multiple latent codes and pre-trained GANs to advance various image processing applications, showcasing its potential for future research. The work is supported by grants from the Research Grants Council of Hong Kong and SenseTime.
