The paper presents an innovative approach to improve image reconstruction quality by defining an objective function that utilizes both low-level pixel-wise reconstruction error and high-level perceptual feature differences. The objective function is mathematically expressed as L(x1, x2) = ||x1 - x2||² + ||φ(x1), φ(x2)||₁, where φ(·) represents the perceptual feature extractor. The authors employ gradient descent to optimize latent codes and channel importance scores.

The proposed method, termed multi-code GAN prior (mGANprior), is applied to various image processing tasks, including image colorization, super-resolution, and inpainting. Each task modifies the objective function to align with specific requirements, such as maintaining the gray channel for colorization or downsampling the inversion result for super-resolution. 

Extensive experiments were conducted using state-of-the-art GAN models like PGGAN and StyleGAN on datasets such as CelebA-HQ and LSUN. The study compares the effectiveness of mGANprior against several baseline methods, including optimizing a single latent code, learning an encoder for generator reversal, and a hybrid approach using encoder outputs for optimization. The results demonstrate the superiority of the proposed multi-code inversion approach in enhancing image quality across various tasks.
