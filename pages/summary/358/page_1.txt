The paper introduces XL-Editor, a novel framework designed to enhance the post-editing capabilities of the XLNet model in natural language processing tasks. Traditional autoregressive models generate sequences in a left-to-right manner, which does not reflect the human ability to revise sentences flexibly. XL-Editor addresses this by enabling variable-length insertion probabilities, allowing it to estimate the likelihood of inserting sequences at specific positions within a sentence. 

The framework supports three main post-editing operations: insertion, deletion, and replacement, which collectively improve text generation quality. Empirical results demonstrate that XL-Editor outperforms XLNet in tasks focused on text insertion and deletion, validating its effectiveness. Additionally, the framework extends to unpaired text style transfer, where it successfully modifies sentences to adopt a target style while preserving their original semantic coherence.

The authors highlight the limitations of existing models like BERT and the initial shortcomings of XLNet in text generation, particularly its reliance on fixed sequence lengths, which complicates variable-length sequence sampling. XL-Editor incorporates a novel insertion-based relative positional encoding scheme to overcome these challenges, thus broadening XLNet's applicability across various NLP tasks. Overall, this work showcases significant improvements in both style transfer accuracy and post-editing capabilities, indicating the potential of XL-Editor in enhancing neural sequence generation models.
