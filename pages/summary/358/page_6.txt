The paper discusses advancements in style transfer through a post-editing approach, particularly utilizing the XL-Editor model. The challenge in this domain arises due to the lack of parallel training pairs, requiring models to learn from instance-wise style labels. The authors propose a method to estimate the need for replacements or insertions in a sequence based on the conditional probabilities of subsequences belonging to a source and target style.

The core idea is to identify subsequences that have a high likelihood of belonging to the source style but a low likelihood for the target style, suggesting they should be replaced. The post-editing process allows for three operations: insertion, deletion, and replacement, guided by a probabilistic framework. The method is summarized in an algorithm that iteratively modifies the input sequence until no further improvements can be made.

Training objectives for XL-Editor focus on maximizing the conditional insertion probability and auxiliary style prediction. The authors also incorporate task-specific strategies, such as biased sampling and forced insertion, to enhance the model's performance, particularly in handling incomplete sentences in the Yelp dataset.

Experiments were conducted on the Yelp and Amazon datasets, which involve flipping sentiment between positive and negative styles. The results indicate that the proposed methods and enhancements improve the model's ability to effectively transfer styles in text. The paper provides a comprehensive evaluation of various models, highlighting the efficacy of XL-Editor in the style transfer task.
