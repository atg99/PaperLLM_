The framework focuses on variable-length insertion probability estimation, enabling effective post-editing operations such as insertion, deletion, and replacement. The datasets used for evaluation include Yelp and Amazon, featuring positive and negative sentiment samples, with statistics provided for training, development, and test splits.

The paper categorizes baseline approaches into four groups: rule-based systems, disentangled representation learning systems, sequence-to-sequence systems, and reinforcement learning systems. Evaluation metrics include BLEU scores, style accuracy, and G-scores, with results demonstrating XL-Editor's capability to significantly improve style accuracy while maintaining BLEU performance.

The findings reveal that XL-Editor outperforms various baselines, particularly when applied to the outputs of top-performing systems. The study highlights the potential of incorporating additional techniques, such as adversarial loss, for future enhancements in text style transfer. In conclusion, XL-Editor proves effective for post-editing tasks and contributes positively to the text style transfer domain, with implications for further research and development.
