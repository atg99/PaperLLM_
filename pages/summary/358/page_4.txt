The paper presents a model that predicts the insertion of tokens in a sequence using an insertion-based relative encoding scheme. This approach effectively removes the encoded distance between left and right contexts while pinpointing the exact insertion position. The model operates similarly to a sequence-to-sequence model, treating both contexts as the source and the insertion sequence as the target, but it uniquely encodes the positional relationships between these contexts.

For estimating variable-length insertion probabilities, the model defines a method to adjust relative positional encodings based on the indices of the tokens being inserted. This ensures that predictions remain consistent regardless of the length of the inserted sequence, eliminating implicit length assumptions and avoiding the computational bottlenecks of earlier models since inserted tokens do not affect hidden states during training or inference.

The training objective for the proposed XL-Editor model utilizes an unsupervised framework, maximizing the likelihood of predicting tokens based on surrounding contexts. The paper also describes strategies for executing post-editing operations—locating insertion positions, estimating insertion probabilities, replacing subsequences, and deleting tokens.

To validate the model's effectiveness, the authors conduct experiments on various post-editing tasks using a dataset of CNN news articles. They compare the XL-Editor model against two baselines: one using a left-to-right factorization without insertion-based encoding and another employing a transformer architecture for sequence-to-sequence tasks. Each model is evaluated under similar configurations, with the XL-Editor demonstrating promising results in the post-editing tasks.
