In the Locate task, a test set of 5,000 sentences is created by deleting a subsequence from each sentence. The model must identify the correct insertion position among five given options. The evaluation metric is the accuracy of position prediction, with different models employing various methods for predicting insertion points.

For the Text Infill task, the approach narrows down plausible insertions by providing context from surrounding sentences. The model is tasked with infilling a deleted subsequence from a middle sentence within a set of three consecutive sentences. Predictions are made using greedy decoding, with XLNet employing a strategy to consider multiple potential insertion lengths. The outputs are evaluated based on BLEU scores compared to the original deleted subsequence.

In the Text Deletion task, the model identifies a randomly inserted sentence among five consecutive sentences. The evaluation involves comparing perplexity ratios to determine which sentence should be deleted to maintain coherence. A ranking strategy is also tested, where the entire sequence's perplexity is computed after deletion.

The results indicate that XL-Editor performs comparably or better across all tasks, and fine-tuning the XLNet BASE model with the XL-Editor objective enhances its capabilities for post-editing tasks.

The paper also touches on Unpaired Text Style Transfer, where the goal is to transform text sequences into a desired style while retaining semantic meaning. This task involves using a training corpus with predefined styles to train a model capable of performing style transformations effectively.
