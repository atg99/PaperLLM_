The paper presents XL-Editor, a model designed for effective post-editing through two sets of experiments. The first experiment evaluates XL-Editor's performance on three tasks: Locate, Text Infill, and Text Deletion, demonstrating superior results compared to traditional XLNet baselines. The second experiment explores its application in unpaired text style transfer, framing the task as a post-editing process where the target style is applied to a given sentence. The authors emphasize that XL-Editor significantly improves style accuracy while maintaining content integrity.

The core of XL-Editor is its ability to estimate variable-length insertion probabilities during the post-editing process. The paper discusses the formulation of this probability, q(y|x(l)↓x(r)), where y is the sequence to be inserted between two contexts x(l) and x(r). The authors critique existing sequence modeling techniques, such as masked sequence modeling and permutation sequence modeling, for their limitations in handling variable-length insertions. They highlight the challenges of methods like BERT and XLNet, which either assume fixed lengths or impose independence assumptions on tokens.

Additionally, the paper reviews Insertion Transformer models, which predict insertion positions but face challenges in determining optimal training orders. The authors argue that their approach, focusing on one insertion position at a time, leverages variable-length insertion probabilities for more effective post-editing, including deletion and replacement tasks. This approach circumvents the computational bottlenecks faced by existing models, positioning XL-Editor as a more efficient and versatile tool for post-editing applications.
