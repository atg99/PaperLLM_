This paper discusses advancements in network quantization and knowledge distillation (KD) as essential techniques for model compression in neural networks. It highlights various quantization strategies tailored to specific hardware structures, emphasizing the joint proposal by Y-Tech and Seattle AI Lab in 2020 for automatic compression that integrates pruning and quantization based on model size. Notably, the Samsung Artificial Intelligence Center's binary architecture search (BATS) and Gwangju Institute of Science's binary network learning architecture (BNAS) are presented as significant contributions that reduce the accuracy gap in binary neural networks (BNNs).

The paper categorizes quantization into binary, ternary, and cluster quantization, noting that while binary quantization offers minimal resource usage, it suffers from low accuracy. Ternary quantization improves accuracy but demands more resources, while cluster quantization balances resource requirements with the need for clustering knowledge. The authors discuss ongoing research addressing precision recovery and gradient mismatch issues associated with quantization, underscoring its importance in enhancing computational speed and model size reduction.

In addition, the paper delves into KD, introduced in 2006, which facilitates knowledge transfer from a complex teacher network to a simpler student network. It categorizes KD into output transfer, feature transfer, and relation transfer, noting the growing body of research with 26 papers identified. The application of KD is particularly emphasized in object detection and semantic segmentation tasks, reflecting its significance in optimizing neural network performance while conserving computational resources.
