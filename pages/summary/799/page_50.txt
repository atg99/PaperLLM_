This paper analyzes the effectiveness of various low-rank decomposition methods for compressing different neural network architectures, specifically VGG-16, AlexNet, GoogLeNet, and ResNet-32, across multiple datasets including ImageNet, CIFAR10, and CIFAR100. 

The findings highlight that while the accuracy of compressed models can match that of larger networks like GoogLeNet, the compression ratio remains limited due to the original model's size. For instance, VGG-16 shows a range of parameter reductions and accuracy scores when compressed using different methods, with the Tai and Tucker methods yielding significant reductions in FLOPs but varying in accuracy. 

In the case of AlexNet, multiple low-rank decomposition techniques also demonstrate reductions in parameters and FLOPs, with results suggesting that some methods like GreBdec maintain competitive accuracy levels. 

For GoogLeNet, the compression methods reveal varying impacts on accuracy, with the Tai method achieving a notable reduction in parameters while improving Top-5 accuracy. 

ResNet-32's performance on CIFAR datasets is particularly highlighted, where the TT method outperformed others in both accuracy and compression efficiency. The overall results indicate that while compressing neural networks can be effective, the choice of method significantly influences the balance between model size and accuracy retention.
