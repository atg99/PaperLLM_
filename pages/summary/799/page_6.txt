The paper discusses network pruning methods, categorizing them into structured and unstructured pruning. Unstructured pruning focuses on removing unimportant weights from each layer of a deep neural network (DNN) to reduce model parameters without significantly altering the network's overall structure. The principle involves pruning weight edges deemed unimportant, as illustrated in the figures provided.

Since 2015, various research teams have made notable advancements in unstructured pruning. The Han Song team from Stanford University, in collaboration with NVIDIA, pioneered a weight pruning method based on removing weights with absolute values below a specified threshold, which led to a sparse network capable of learning the remaining weights effectively. This approach achieved a high sparsity rate at the weight level.

The paper also highlights contributions from other institutions, including Stanford and Tsinghua University's work on deep compression and deep gradient compression, as well as efforts from various universities and research organizations exploring topics such as constrained optimization, frequency-domain dynamic pruning, and layer-adaptive magnitude-based pruning. These advancements reflect ongoing research and innovation in the field of model compression through unstructured pruning techniques.
