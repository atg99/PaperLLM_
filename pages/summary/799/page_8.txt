The paper discusses structured pruning in deep neural networks (DNNs) as an advancement over unstructured pruning, which faces limitations in effectively reducing model complexity. Structured pruning aims to eliminate unimportant structures within the entire DNN while minimizing reconstruction errors. Various methods are categorized by their granularity: neuron pruning, filter pruning, channel pruning, and layer pruning. 

Neuron pruning focuses on deactivating neurons that do not contribute to the output, thus reducing redundancy. This method has gained significant attention since 2016, with contributions from several institutions. Notable advancements include iterative pruning techniques proposed by Hong Kong University of Science and Technology and SenseTime, and a dynamic remedy method by Guo et al., which adjusts neuron connection importance dynamically.

The paper highlights a timeline of significant contributions from various research teams, emphasizing that filter pruning has been the most prevalent approach. The discussion includes various methods introduced by institutions like the University of Maryland, DeepMind, and others, showcasing a collaborative effort across academia and industry to enhance the efficiency of DNNs through structured pruning techniques.
