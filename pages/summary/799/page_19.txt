SqueezeNet, developed by Stanford University, DeepScale, and UC Berkeley, is a pioneering lightweight network that utilizes 1 × 1 convolutions and grouped convolutions, introducing the Fire module for parameter compression. Subsequent iterations, such as SqueezeNext and SqueezeDet, further optimized this framework by incorporating separation convolution and adapting design principles from YOLO for improved feature prediction.

Other notable contributions include ChannelNet, which employs a combination of channel-wise convolutions to minimize model parameters, and SENet, which integrates an attention mechanism to enhance channel feature acquisition, achieving top performance in the 2017 ImageNet competition. Lite-HRNet, developed by Huazhong University and Microsoft, introduced efficient shuffle blocks, while PeleeNet and MobileNeXt focused on utilizing conventional convolutions and novel modules, respectively, to enhance existing architectures.

Further advancements were made with RSANet, which combines a lightweight backbone with a residual semantic-guided attention feature pyramid network to optimize detection performance. Additionally, the introduction of versatile filters and the PP-PicoDet model by Baidu emphasizes the importance of lightweight structures in real-time object detection.

The ongoing research into lightweight convolutional structures highlights their significance across various deep learning applications, making them a focal point for future developments in the field.
