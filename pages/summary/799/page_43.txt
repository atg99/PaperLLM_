The paper discusses the performance of various neural architecture search (NAS) methods and their effectiveness in achieving a balance between model size, accuracy, and search time. Key findings include the performance metrics of different models like ResNet152 and EfficientNetB0, with specific emphasis on the CP-NAS method, which achieved a notable accuracy of 96.35% with a model size of 2.9 million parameters on the CIFAR10 dataset. The CP-NAS method utilizes a parent model to guide the search for a binary child model, leading to improved compression and accuracy.

In terms of model comparisons, the best NAS method was found to compress to 2.5 million parameters but only achieved an accuracy of 69.8%. Additionally, the AutoFormer method demonstrated superior accuracy of 99.1%, indicating the advantages of leveraging trained supernetworks for high-quality transformers.

The paper also reviews classic network quantization methods, applying them to various architectures such as ResNet and VGG on datasets like ImageNet and CIFAR10. The evaluation metrics focus on compression performance, illustrating the effectiveness of lightweight model design methods. Overall, the findings highlight the trade-offs between model complexity, accuracy, and computational efficiency in neural network design.
