The paper discusses Average Precision (AP) as a key metric for evaluating the performance of object detection and image classification tasks. AP quantifies the balance between precision and recall across different categories, with values ranging from 0 to 1, where higher values indicate superior model performance. It is essential to balance categories based on the least represented ones in the dataset to prevent any category from skewing the overall evaluation. The AP is calculated as the area under the Precision-Recall curve.

In the performance comparison section, the authors summarize the compression effects of various deep neural network (DNN) compression methods on multiple public datasets, including COCO test-dev2015, VOC 2007, ImageNet ILSVRC 2012, CIFAR10, and CIFAR100. The performance of models is influenced by factors such as input image size, feature extractor, GPU architecture, number of proposals, training methods, and loss functions. The authors assume that the results in original papers reflect the best experimental outcomes for a fair comparison.

The performance of classic lightweight structure design models and original object detection methods is evaluated using metrics like model parameter size, top-1 accuracy, mean average precision, average precision, and operation speed. A wide range of models is compared, including VGG-16, SSD, YOLO variants, MobileNets, EfficientDet, and many others, highlighting the diverse approaches to achieving efficient and effective object detection.
