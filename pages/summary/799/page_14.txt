The paper discusses various methods of neural network pruning, specifically focusing on channel and layer pruning techniques. Channel pruning, which has gained traction in recent years, utilizes approaches such as L1 regularization, sensitivity analysis, and the coupling of channel correlations to eliminate redundancy. Huawei's Noah's Ark Lab has explored combining channel pruning with search algorithms and tensor decomposition to enhance pruning efficiency. Additionally, a recent contribution from National Yang Ming Chiao Tung University has developed a real-time object detector that effectively uses structural pruning, feature distillation, and neural architecture search to operate under edge device constraints.

Layer pruning is noted as a more aggressive method that directly removes entire layers from the network, leading to substantial compression but often at the cost of accuracy. Recent advancements from Nanyang Technology University and MIT have proposed innovative layer pruning techniques that focus on independent layer analysis and energy consumption metrics to guide the pruning process. Notably, the collaboration with Google led to the development of Netadapt, which automates the pruning process based on hardware constraints.

The application of these pruning methods is exemplified in a project by the Guangdong Key Laboratory, which successfully applied channel and layer pruning to optimize the YOLOv3-SPP model for garbage detection on resource-limited edge devices.

Lastly, the paper mentions emerging training-based approaches that allow for the maintenance of a fixed parameter count and computational cost during the training of sparse networks. This method, developed by Google Brain and DeepMind, focuses on dynamically updating the network topology based on parameter magnitudes and infrequent gradient calculations, thus achieving better efficiency in terms of FLOPs and accuracy.
