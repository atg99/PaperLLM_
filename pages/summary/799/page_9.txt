This paper discusses advancements in pruning techniques for deep neural networks, particularly focusing on neuron and filter pruning. The TuSimple Team introduced a parameter-scale factor for data-driven and end-to-end pruning, while the NVIDIA team proposed methods to assess neuron contributions to final loss for effective neuron removal. Khakzar from the Technical University of Munich presented a pathway selection method and a feature attribution approach to reveal critical input features. Wang from Northeastern University addressed pruning schedule and weight importance criteria through novel regularization algorithms.

Additionally, structured weight pruning has gained attention, with the DiDi AI research team proposing an automatic framework called AutoCompress using ADMM, and Ma et al. introducing a sparse structure called partial convolution. Alibaba's Dharma Academy developed a pruning method utilizing block-max weight masking and density-adaptive regular blocks.

The paper highlights that neuron pruning research mainly centers on evaluating neuron contribution rates, with limited studies on the topic. Filter pruning, which targets the redundancy in convolutional neural networks, is emphasized as a key structured pruning method. Various techniques, including group sparsity and structural sparse regularization, have been explored to achieve sparse structures in networks, improving computational efficiency while preserving output accuracy.
