The references listed pertain to various methodologies for pruning deep neural networks, aimed at enhancing their efficiency and performance. Key concepts include:

1. **Holistic Filter Pruning**: Enderich et al. propose an approach that focuses on removing entire filters rather than individual weights to streamline deep neural networks.

2. **Thinet and Factorized Convolutions**: Luo et al. and Li et al. present filter-level pruning and factorized convolution methods, respectively, which significantly compress network architectures while maintaining performance.

3. **Global and Geometric Median Pruning**: You et al. introduce a global filter pruning method, while He et al. utilize geometric median techniques to accelerate deep convolutional networks.

4. **Heterogeneous Convolutions**: Singh et al. explore kernel-based convolutions to optimize network performance through varying kernel sizes.

5. **Learning Efficient Networks**: Liu et al. and Ding et al. focus on techniques like network slimming and lossless pruning to improve the compactness and functionality of CNNs.

6. **Channel Pruning**: Various studies, including those by Hu et al., Zhuang et al., and Guo et al., delve into channel pruning strategies that consider classification loss and feature importance, aiming to reduce model size without sacrificing accuracy.

7. **Collaborative and Dynamic Pruning**: Wang et al. and Kang et al. discuss methods for collaborative compression and dynamic network pruning, which adaptively modify network structures based on performance metrics.

8. **Unified Frameworks**: Guo et al. propose a multi-dimensional pruning framework that integrates various pruning strategies for model compression.

These methodologies collectively address the challenges associated with deep neural network deployment, focusing on reducing computational overhead while retaining or enhancing performance.
