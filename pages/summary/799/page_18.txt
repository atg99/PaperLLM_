The paper discusses advancements in lightweight neural network architectures aimed at reducing computational complexity and improving efficiency. It highlights the development of DenseNet and its improved version, CondenseNet, which utilizes learnable grouped convolution to decrease video memory usage and enhance speed. The interleaved group convolution network (IGCNets) series, proposed by Microsoft Research, focuses on widening networks without increasing parameters, with subsequent versions improving upon the original by integrating low-rank and sparse convolutions.

Pointwise convolution, often underutilized in previous models, is addressed through ShuffleNetV1 and its successor ShuffleNetV2, which incorporate group pointwise convolution and channel splitting to minimize computational demands. The ThunderNet model further builds on these principles by integrating context enhancement and spatial attention modules.

Additionally, the efficient spatial pyramid network (ESPNet) is introduced for semantic segmentation, employing a combination of pointwise and dilated convolutions to optimize computational efficiency. Finally, Huawei's Noah’s Ark Lab explores plug-and-play network models, culminating in the GhostNet architecture, which effectively partitions convolutional layers to generate intrinsic and ghost feature maps with fewer kernels. These innovations collectively aim to enhance the performance of lightweight neural networks while maintaining low resource consumption.
