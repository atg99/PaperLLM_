The primary focus has been on low-rank decomposition of convolution kernels to reduce computational demands and the number of parameters in CNNs. This approach involves decomposing large convolution kernel tensors into smaller approximations, thereby maintaining performance while enhancing efficiency.

Research in this area has seen a significant uptick since its introduction, with a peak in studies around 2018. The foundational work by the University of Oxford and Facebook in 2013 established the theoretical framework for applying matrix factorization to convolution kernels, allowing for the prediction of remaining values from partial weights. This was further advanced by the introduction of the CP decomposition method by the Skolkovo Institute of Science and Technology in 2014.

Although low-rank decomposition methods have gained traction, recent years have seen a decline in the number of studies, suggesting a potential shift in research focus. The paper highlights the need for further exploration into knowledge distillation (KD) techniques, particularly in scenarios with limited data and applications beyond image classification, such as target detection. Overall, the discussion emphasizes the potential of combining different KD methods to enhance model performance while addressing the limitations of current approaches.
