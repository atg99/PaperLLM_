The paper evaluates the performance of various lightweight object detection models, particularly focusing on YOLO-LITE and PP-PicoDet, using the COCO test-dev 2015 dataset. YOLO-LITE is noted for its minimal parameters and FLOPs, but it exhibits a significant drop in average precision (AP). Its main contribution lies in demonstrating the feasibility of shallow networks for real-time object detection without the need for batch normalization. In contrast, the PP-PicoDet model outperforms other lightweight models with a size of only 0.99 M and an AP of 27.1%, attributed to its enhanced backbone structure and effective label assignment strategy.

The paper also discusses the performance of other lightweight models, such as Ghost-ResNet-56, which achieves a parameter reduction to 0.43 M while maintaining high accuracy on the CIFAR-10 dataset. Notably, models like EfficientDet and MobileNet series demonstrate a good balance between parameter efficiency and accuracy, underscoring advances in lightweight model design since the introduction of MobileNetV1-V3.
