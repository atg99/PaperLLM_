The section discusses the concept of output transfer in knowledge distillation (KD), which utilizes the outputs of a teacher network to enhance the learning of a student network. The idea gained traction in 2014 when it was established that the knowledge capacity of shallow networks can match that of deeper networks. The Google team coined the term KD during this period. The process involves initially training a teacher model using hard targets, followed by training the student model with both hard and soft targets from the teacher's softmax layer outputs. This dual approach allows the student network to develop similar expressiveness while compressing the model.

Traditionally, KD has been characterized by a one-way transfer from the teacher to the student. However, a new method called deep mutual learning, proposed by Zhang et al., introduces a collaborative framework where multiple networks can learn from each other, enhancing the overall learning process.
