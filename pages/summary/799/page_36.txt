The paper discusses recent advancements in low-rank decomposition methods aimed at improving model compression and acceleration in neural networks. Various institutions have employed different techniques to enhance efficiency while maintaining performance. Microsoft Research used generalized singular value decomposition (GSVD) for acceleration with a slight precision trade-off. The Skolkovo Institute focused on Tensor-train Decomposition for parameter compression, while the University of Central Florida introduced LASSO sparsity constraints to further optimize convolution kernels post-decomposition.

Research from Princeton University and the Chinese University of Hong Kong highlighted a low-rank decomposition method yielding a closed global optimal solution, leveraging batch normalization for training deep low-rank networks. The University of Sydney combined low-rank decomposition with sparsity for feature reconstruction, and the Hikvision Research Institute developed a filter bank-based low-rank decomposition method to reduce redundancy. Other teams, including the University of Electronic Science and Technology and Purdue University, explored combinations of CP and Tucker decomposition as well as Tensor-ring decomposition for weight tensor reduction.

The paper emphasizes that traditional singular value decomposition (SVD) has limitations, particularly in recognizing the varying importance of neurons across layers. A study by Sridhar Swaminathan et al. proposed a sparsified SVD approach to enhance image recognition models. While existing tensor compression methods have effectively compressed large networks like VGG, they face challenges with advanced architectures, such as MobileNet, which utilize depthwise separable convolutions.

Notably, a collaboration between the University of California and Meta introduced a novel approach combining low-rank tensor factorization with sparse pruning, achieving superior results compared to using either method alone. This work marks a significant step in exploring low-rank and sparse weight compression for state-of-the-art architectures reliant on efficient convolution techniques. Despite the benefits, the implementation of low-rank decomposition methods remains complex and resource-intensive.
