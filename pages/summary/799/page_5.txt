The paper discusses the significance of model compression methods in object detection, highlighting that network pruning is one of the most prevalent techniques. Network pruning aims to enhance model efficiency by eliminating redundant parameters, thus making the network sparse. This method has a historical background dating back to 1989, with numerous advancements occurring since then. The paper references a compilation of 90 recent papers related to network pruning, indicating a growing interest in this area within the last six years. Additionally, it categorizes various network pruning approaches, including unstructured and structured pruning, as well as specific techniques like channel pruning, filter pruning, weight pruning, neuron pruning, and layer pruning. The findings illustrate the increasing popularity and diversification of network pruning methods in the context of deep neural networks.
