The paper discusses advancements in binary and ternary quantization techniques for neural networks. Several innovative algorithms and frameworks are highlighted, each aiming to enhance the efficiency and accuracy of binary networks (BNNs) and ternary quantization methods.

In binary networks, various methods have been proposed. The LAB algorithm focuses on optimizing binarization weights using the approximate Newton algorithm. Bi-Real Net addresses limitations of XNOR-Net by incorporating shortcut connections and fitting the activation function's sign, allowing for effective binarization while preserving inference accuracy. The BWNH approach transforms the learning of binary parameters into a hashing problem, while Diffenderfer et al. introduced a method that prunes and quantizes full-precision networks to yield accurate BNNs, supporting the multi-prize lottery ticket hypothesis.

Further enhancements include the FINN framework for efficient FPGA mapping of BNNs, the binary ensemble neural network (BENN) for improved performance, and channel-wise interaction based binary convolutional neural networks (CI-BCNN) for efficient inference. The circulant binary convolutional network (CBCN) enriches the expressive capability of binary features by rotating filters, and the BNN distribution loss (BNN-DL) framework aims to minimize negative impacts from forward binarization and backpropagation.

In ternary quantization, the introduction of a third threshold (0) reduces quantization error. The trained ternary quantization (TTQ) method and the ternary weight network (TWN) are notable contributions, improving accuracy with minimal degradation. To address accuracy loss in existing ternary methods, the ternary residual quantization (TRQ) framework combines quantization with refined reconstruction, enhancing precision and flexibility.

Overall, the paper presents a comprehensive overview of ongoing research and developments in binary and ternary quantization, emphasizing their potential to improve neural network performance.
