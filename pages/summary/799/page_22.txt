The paper discusses lightweight network design methods, emphasizing three main approaches: convolution structure-based design, convolution operation method-based design, and YOLO model structure-based design. The convolution structure approach is simple and deployable but sacrifices feature representation. The convolution operation method offers a balance between model expressiveness and compression but can complicate training efficiency. The YOLO model structure is tailored for object detection, leveraging the YOLO algorithm's real-time detection capabilities.

Additionally, the paper introduces Neural Architecture Search (NAS) as a solution to the challenges of designing neural networks amidst increasing complexity. NAS automates the design process through three components: search space, evaluation method, and search method. This approach alleviates the time and cost associated with heuristic and rule-based strategies in model compression. The paper notes a significant rise in NAS research since 2018, highlighting the growing importance of this method in lightweight model design. A total of 34 papers on NAS for model compression are reviewed, showcasing its evolution and relevance in recent research.
