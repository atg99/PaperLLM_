The paper discusses various methods for neural network pruning, particularly in scenarios with limited data and without the need for retraining or fine-tuning. Traditional approaches often require extensive original data for retraining after pruning, which can raise data privacy concerns as only a small portion of data may be accessible. To address this, the SenseTime team introduced a data-driven technique utilizing synaptic strength parameters to create a nuclear-level sparse convolutional neural network (CNN). In collaboration with Tsinghua University, they developed a method for network compression under limited training data conditions.

Additionally, researchers from Nanjing University proposed a residual block-based pruning method tailored for small datasets. The Tsinghua Ding team sought to eliminate redundant filters safely but faced challenges with large datasets due to the need for complete retraining. They then introduced approximated oracle filter pruning (AOFP), which uses a binary search to identify and prune less important filters while fine-tuning the model through a multipath framework.

The paper also contrasts hard filter pruning, which directly removes filters and can degrade model performance, with soft filter pruning (SFP) proposed by Southern University of Science and Technology. SFP allows for updates to pruned filters during training, minimizing reliance on pretraining. Collaborative efforts with Huawei led to an end-to-end pruning method addressing dimensional mismatches without iterative processes.

Furthermore, some researchers have aimed to create pruning methods that allow for precise control over model accuracy post-pruning. The IIT Kanpur team introduced a play and prune (PP) framework with auto-balanced filter pruning (AFP) and a pruning rate controller (PRC), facilitating direct specification of error tolerance. The holistic filter pruning (HFP) method by Robert Bosch GmbH also enables accurate control over pruning rates, leveraging batch-normalization channelwise scaling factors. Lastly, the Thinet architecture proposed by Nanjing University employs a greedy approach for filter pruning. 

Overall, these advancements highlight a shift towards more efficient and data-conscious pruning techniques in deep learning, facilitating better model performance and reduced reliance on extensive datasets.
