The paper discusses various methods for filter and channel pruning in convolutional neural networks (CNNs), emphasizing their optimization and efficiency. It highlights key contributions from multiple research teams, including Tsinghua University and Tencent AI Lab, which introduced a factorized convolutional filter structure optimized through the ADMM method. Peking University’s GateDecorator addresses the global filter importance ranking problem, while He et al. proposed a geometric median-based approach to prune redundant filters.

Additionally, innovations such as Perforatedcnns leverage masking strategies to enhance efficiency, and IIT Kanpur's hetconv structure reduces computational load without sacrificing accuracy. UC Berkeley's tied block convolution (TBC) method shares filters across blocks, allowing for multi-response generation.

The paper notes that filter pruning often employs sparse constraint regularization and cross-layer correlation to assess importance and eliminate redundancy. It also differentiates between filter and channel pruning, with the latter directly removing entire channels to address dimensional mismatches and improve model efficiency.

Tsinghua University’s application of L1 regularization for channel identification and their layer grouping algorithm to evaluate importance are discussed, alongside Ding’s ResRep method for lossless channel pruning. Overall, the research indicates a trend toward global dynamic pruning as a promising future direction in model optimization.
