The paper discusses advances in sparse architectures and lightweight network design, focusing on various pruning techniques and their effectiveness in neural network training. Le et al. from VinAI Research highlight the benefits of random pruning combined with learning rate restarting over traditional fine-tuning methods, showing improved performance recovery in pruned networks. Liu et al. examine the efficacy of different random pruning methods against non-random approaches, concluding that random pruning is particularly effective for larger models. Hu proposes a novel one-shot pruning quantization method that simplifies the compression allocation problem during fine-tuning. Tiwari et al. introduce ChipNet, a deterministic pruning strategy, while Chang et al. emphasize that pruning over-parameterized models yields better results.

The paper categorizes network pruning into structured and unstructured methods. Unstructured pruning maintains the original network structure but requires specialized hardware, whereas structured pruning removes parts of the network structure, making it more suitable for general hardware acceleration. Further, structured pruning can be divided into various granularities: neuron, kernel, channel, and layer pruning, each with its specific applications and effectiveness.

In addition to pruning, the paper discusses lightweight network design methods, which aim to reduce network parameters by creating more compact convolution kernels or methods. This approach has gained significant attention since 2015, emphasizing the need for improved network architecture to enhance accuracy in lightweight models.
