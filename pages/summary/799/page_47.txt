The paper discusses various methods for knowledge distillation (KD) and low-rank decomposition within the context of neural network optimization, specifically focusing on their performance on datasets such as ImageNet, CIFAR10, and CIFAR100.

In evaluating KD methods, the results presented in Tables 9, 10, and 11 demonstrate that the accuracy of student networks achieved through KD is comparable to that of teacher networks. Notably, the AIN method shows a minimal accuracy drop of only 0.15%, indicating its effectiveness in transferring statistical information from the teacher to the student networks.

The paper also analyzes classic low-rank decomposition methods, comparing them with pruning algorithms using various neural network architectures (AlexNet, VGG-16, ResNet-32, and GoogLeNet). The evaluation metrics include model parameter size, top-1 and top-5 accuracy, and operational speed (FLOPS). The GreBdec method stands out in the comparison for VGG-16 and AlexNet, achieving significant compression (7.0% and 9.8% of the original size, respectively) while slightly improving accuracy. This method combines feature reconstruction with low-rank decomposition and sparsity, outperforming traditional methods like Tucker and Tai.

Overall, the findings highlight the effectiveness of certain KD and low-rank decomposition strategies in enhancing model performance and efficiency without substantial accuracy loss.
