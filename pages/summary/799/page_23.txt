The paper discusses various methods for Neural Architecture Search (NAS), categorizing them into reinforcement learning (RL), neuro-evolutionary methods, and gradient-based methods. It highlights the application of RL in NAS, beginning with the pioneering strategy developed by MIT and the University of Cambridge, which frames NAS as a Markov decision process and employs Q-learning optimization alongside experience replay to determine optimal network architectures. 

The paper also mentions advancements made by other institutions, such as Google, which introduced NASNet by leveraging prior knowledge to design blocks with varied architectures and create a local search space for neural network architecture. Additionally, the Chinese Academy of Sciences and SenseTime developed a NAS method utilizing Q-learning in a block-based search space, while Shanghai Jiaotong University implemented RL for designing structures with complex path topologies and proposed an efficient architecture search framework that reuses weights from existing networks, significantly reducing computational costs. The research situations of various teams and institutions in this domain are visually represented in the figures provided.
