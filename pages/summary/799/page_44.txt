The paper presents a comparative analysis of various neural architecture search (NAS) methods based on their performance in terms of model size (parameters), accuracy (Top-1 percentage), and computational efficiency (FLOPs for ImageNet and GPU days for CIFAR10). 

For the ImageNet ILSVRC 2012 dataset, results reveal that ResNeXt-101 achieves the highest accuracy of 79.3% with a significant number of FLOPs at 7800 million. In contrast, ProxylessNAS, with only 4.1 million parameters, demonstrates a lower accuracy of 74.6% but offers a more compact model. EfficientNetB0, with 5.3 million parameters, delivers a near-optimal accuracy of 77.3% while being computationally efficient at 390 million FLOPs. Other notable methods include FBNetV3, which balances a smaller model size of 3.7 million parameters with an accuracy of 78%.

In the CIFAR10 dataset analysis, the method AutoFormer stands out with an impressive accuracy of 99.1%, although it requires 23 million parameters. DARTS, with a minimal model size of 3.1 million parameters, achieves a competitive accuracy of 97.06% while requiring only 1.5 GPU days, demonstrating efficiency in training time. Other methods like ENAS and BNAS also show strong performance with high accuracies and relatively low parameter counts.

Overall, the findings highlight the trade-offs between model complexity, accuracy, and computational resources across different NAS methodologies, providing valuable insights for future model design and optimization in deep learning applications.
