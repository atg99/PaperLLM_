The paper discusses advancements in knowledge distillation (KD) techniques, focusing on methods that enhance student learning from teacher models. Kim et al. from Seoul National University introduced a convolution-based approach to encode and decode teacher model outputs, improving student comprehension. Borui et al. from Megvii Technology Limited identified the underutilization of output KD and proposed Decoupled Knowledge Distillation (DKD), which separates target class knowledge distillation (TCKD) from non-target class knowledge distillation (NCKD). This decoupling allows for independent control over both aspects, leading to improved performance across various visual tasks.

In the realm of feature transfer, the paper highlights several key contributions. Romero et al. introduced FitNet, which leverages hidden layer features from teacher networks to train students. Attention Transfer (AT) by Komodakis and Zagoruyko encourages students to replicate the attention distributions of teachers. TuSimple framed knowledge transfer as a domain adaptation challenge, matching activation distributions between models. An information-theoretic framework, proposed by institutions like KAIST and Amazon, aims to maximize mutual information during knowledge transfer. Tung et al. emphasized maintaining similarity in activations between teacher and student networks, while Heo et al. designed a novel feature distillation method that synergizes various transformations and distance functions. Lastly, a method based on feature statistics was developed by the University of Nottingham and Samsung AI Center, which incorporates adaptive instance normalization to feedback learned statistical information to the teacher model.
