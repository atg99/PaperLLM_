The objective of these strategies is to reduce model parameters or computational complexity to enhance inference speed, although they often result in decreased model accuracy. 

Network pruning's effect on accuracy is contingent on the chosen strategies and levels, with excessive pruning leading to significant accuracy losses. Lightweight architecture design's success relies on specific design choices and expert experience. Notable advancements in network quantization, such as post-training quantization and quantization-aware training, have reduced accuracy impacts, enabling compressed models to maintain levels close to the original. Neural Architecture Search (NAS) can yield models that balance accuracy and compression but is typically time-consuming. Knowledge Distillation (KD) can achieve accuracy comparable to a teacher model if the right teacher model and data augmentation are selected. Low-rank decomposition demands careful strategy selection to ensure reasonable accuracy retention.

The influence of these techniques can vary based on implementation, datasets, and application contexts. The paper outlines future directions and application scenarios for six compression methods: network pruning is suitable for low-memory devices; lightweight architectures are ideal for real-time embedded platforms; network quantization fits high-speed, low-memory devices; NAS is best for mobile devices with specific requirements; KD is effective for small datasets; and low-rank decomposition is primarily for compressing fully connected layers due to its computational demands.

In conclusion, the development of lightweight models enhances the efficiency of neural networks, broadening their applicability in areas such as virtual reality, augmented reality, smart security, and wearable devices. Their real-time performance is crucial for online learning and autonomous driving technologies. The paper provides an overview of neural network compression technology, details current strategies and their comparative effects, and discusses implications for future research and application.
