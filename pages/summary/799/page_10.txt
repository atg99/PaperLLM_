The paper discusses various strategies for filter pruning in convolutional neural networks (CNNs) to enhance computational efficiency. It highlights methods that utilize sparse learning frameworks, adaptive techniques, and dynamic pruning strategies. 

Notable contributions include:

1. **Sparse Learning Frameworks**: Université de Sherbrooke's approach allows for the pruning of learned filters while considering neuron budgets. A Bayesian algorithm from the University of Amsterdam constructs sparse structures for pruning but requires sensitivity analysis.

2. **Global Rescaling Method**: Proposed by NVIDIA, this method simplifies pruning by eliminating the need for sensitivity estimation across layers.

3. **Adaptive and Dynamic Pruning**: Tsinghua University has developed runtime neural pruning (RNP) that adapts pruning based on input and feature maps. Their auto-balanced filter pruning (AFP) method further prunes weak filters along with feature maps. Additionally, a momentum-based SGD optimization is introduced to reduce network complexity through dynamic pruning.

4. **Layer-Adaptive Approaches**: The University of Tennessee's method builds graphs for convolutional layers to measure redundancy and prune less significant filters effectively. Peking University and Huawei's ManiDP method leverages input relationships to remove redundant filters dynamically.

5. **Dynamic and Progressive Filter Pruning**: A scheme by the Chinese Academy of Sciences learns structured sparsity directly from scratch, imposing regularization on expected pruning parameters in a dynamic manner.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the evolution of filter pruning techniques, focusing on improving efficiency while maintaining network performance.
