The paper discusses advancements in Neural Architecture Search (NAS) and network quantization, focusing on their applications in model compression and acceleration. It highlights the development of LightTrack by Microsoft Research, a lightweight object tracker utilizing one-shot NAS specifically for object tracking, which is optimized for real-time performance on resource-constrained platforms.

The discussion categorizes NAS methods into three types: reinforcement learning-based, evolutionary algorithm-based, and gradient-based. While reinforcement learning methods are effective in identifying optimal neural network structures, they require extensive training time. In contrast, evolutionary algorithms, despite their tendency to converge on local optima, offer shorter search times. The gradient-based approach is noted for its novelty and efficiency, as it does not rely on maintaining a full population, thereby reducing computational costs, though it is still under development. The compatibility of NAS with other model compression techniques is emphasized, with a mention of recent MIT research that integrates hardware performance and resource constraints into the search process, indicating potential future trends.

The section on network quantization explains its purpose of converting floating-point operations to integer fixed-point operations to minimize model size and enhance training speed. The industry predominantly uses INT8 quantization, with major deep learning frameworks like TensorRT, TensorFlow, and PyTorch implementing this approach. Academic efforts focus on minimizing accuracy loss associated with quantization, categorizing methods into binary, ternary, cluster quantization, and hybrid approaches. The paper reviews 33 significant research contributions in network quantization, showcasing the ongoing research landscape and developments from various institutions.
