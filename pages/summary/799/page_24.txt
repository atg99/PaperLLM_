The paper discusses advancements in Neural Architecture Search (NAS) methods, highlighting key developments aimed at overcoming the limitations of traditional manual heuristic and rule-based strategies. The AutoML model compression method (AMC) was introduced by the MIT Han Song team in collaboration with Google, utilizing reinforcement learning to automatically learn compression strategies, yielding higher compression ratios while maintaining neural network performance. This has inspired further research, including methods for network acceleration through knowledge transfer and the MnasNet method for mobile neural network design, which considers layer diversity.

The paper also reviews NAS methods based on evolutionary algorithms, with Google’s team achieving optimal image classification network architectures through this approach. They introduced AmoebaNet, surpassing manual classifiers. Carnegie Mellon University and DeepMind explored hierarchical search spaces, while Megvii Research Institute proposed a single path super network to alleviate weight coupling issues.

Additionally, gradient-based NAS methods are discussed, such as budget super networks (BSNs) from Sorbonne University and Crito Research Institute, which address computational and memory challenges. The FBNet series from UC Berkeley and Facebook, which employs differentiable neural architecture search and resource constraints, is also highlighted.

Finally, the paper mentions other NAS approaches developed by Google's AI team, including PNASNET, MorphNet, and Efficient Neural Architecture Search (ENAS), which leverage different optimization and scaling strategies to enhance neural network design.
