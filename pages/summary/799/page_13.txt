The paper discusses various advancements in channel pruning methods aimed at optimizing convolutional neural networks (CNNs) while maintaining performance. Hu et al. proposed a genetic algorithm-based channel pruning method that prunes networks layer by layer based on channel sensitivity, followed by fine-tuning. Researchers from South China University of Technology introduced a recognition perception loss to aid in selecting beneficial channels and optimizing reconstruction error. Tencent AI Lab developed a collaborative channel pruning method that leverages channel dependencies to minimize computational costs with minimal performance loss.

Huawei Noah's Ark Lab has made significant contributions, including a variational technique for estimating channel saliency and a binary search strategy for channel configuration. They also proposed a model with discrete weights for improved interpretability and a collaborative compression scheme that integrates channel pruning with tensor decomposition.

The University of Sydney's Guo et al. introduced a classification loss-guided channel pruning method to minimize the impact of unimportant features. They also developed multi-dimensional pruning, considering various dimensions for pruning. Another team, including Gao et al., addressed loss metric mismatch and incorporated episodic memory to manage sub-networks during pruning.

Seoul University's approach emphasized the risk of poor local minima post-pruning and proposed a data-driven pruning algorithm utilizing random gradient descent without requiring fine-tuning. The University of Pittsburgh's Gao et al. suggested a discrete optimal structural pruning method for compact CNNs. Joo et al. introduced a linearly replaceable filter method, allowing for filter removal while compensating for output differences through weight modifications.

In 2022, Hou et al. proposed the Channel Exploration methodology, which prunes and regrows channels during training to mitigate premature loss of important channels, achieving notable results on the ImageNet dataset with a compressed ResNet-50 model.
