To overcome the limitations of existing multi-view clustering methods, we propose the Multi-VAE framework, which employs a Variational Autoencoder (VAE) approach to learn disentangled visual representations. Our framework distinguishes between a view-common variable, which captures the shared cluster information across multiple views, and multiple view-peculiar variables, which account for unique visual factors inherent to each view. The view-common variable is modeled using a discrete Gumbel Softmax distribution, facilitating the extraction of common cluster factors. In contrast, the view-peculiar variables are represented using a continuous Gaussian distribution, allowing for the representation of specific characteristics of each view.

By strategically controlling the mutual information capacity, we effectively disentangle the shared and unique visual representations, enabling the extraction of more robust clustering information from the multi-view data. Our experimental evaluations demonstrate that the Multi-VAE framework not only yields explainable and disentangled visual representations but also achieves superior clustering performance compared to state-of-the-art methods.

In summary, Multi-VAE addresses the challenges of multi-view clustering by leveraging the strengths of a VAE-based approach to separate common and unique visual factors, leading to enhanced clustering accuracy and interpretability.
