This paper discusses a generative model utilizing a variational inference framework to learn disentangled representations from multi-view data. The model incorporates latent variables that are divided into view-common and view-peculiar components. The view-peculiar variable follows a standard normal distribution, while other visual information is treated as continuous. The variational distribution is parameterized using a factorized Gaussian approach, enabling the use of the reparameterization trick for efficient computation.

The generative process combines the latent variables to generate observations, and the likelihood of each view is expressed through a decoder. The parameters of the model are partially shared across views, which simplifies subsequent derivations. The core objective is to maximize the likelihood of observed data, which is framed as maximizing the evidence lower bound (ELBO) using Jensen's inequality.

The ELBO is further decomposed, allowing for the separation of KL divergence terms corresponding to the view-common and view-peculiar variables. This separation is crucial for disentangling the representations. The paper introduces controlled capacities for the KL divergence terms, which are adjusted to promote the effective encoding of information in the latent variables. 

Specifically, the ELBO is modified to include trade-off coefficients that manage the capacities of the KL divergence terms. The analysis shows that the KL divergence serves as an upper bound for the mutual information, and the controlled capacities ensure that each latent variable progressively encodes more variance. The paper provides a detailed mathematical formulation of the model and its learning objectives, emphasizing the importance of disentangled representations in understanding multi-view data.
