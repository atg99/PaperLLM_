The paper discusses advancements in multi-view clustering using a variational autoencoder (VAE) framework, highlighting its unique approach to disentangled representation learning. The authors build upon existing clustering methodologies, particularly focusing on VAE and Gaussian mixture models. They note that while there are various VAE-based multi-view learning methods, their work is the first to propose a disentangled multi-view VAE framework that distinguishes between view-common and view-peculiar representations.

Multi-view clustering has traditionally utilized methods such as spectral clustering and non-negative matrix factorization, which often assume shared subspaces across different views. Recent techniques have incorporated deep learning models to enhance clustering effectiveness. The paper emphasizes the importance of disentangled representation learning, which aims to extract explainable factors from data. Prominent methods in this domain include InfoGAN and β-VAE, with the latter focusing on balancing reconstruction quality and disentanglement by adjusting the KL divergence term during training.

The proposed method addresses the challenge of clustering multi-view image datasets by utilizing independent view-common and view-peculiar variables within a generative model. The view-common variable captures shared cluster information across all views, while view-peculiar variables represent unique visual characteristics of each view. The inference process combines embeddings from all views to learn the common information, employing techniques such as Gumbel Softmax distributions to manage the non-differentiability of discrete random variables in neural networks.

Overall, the paper presents a novel approach to multi-view clustering that leverages the strengths of VAE while aiming to achieve clarity and disentanglement in the representations learned from diverse visual information.
