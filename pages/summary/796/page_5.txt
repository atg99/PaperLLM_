The paper discusses the relationship between JPEG compression quality factors (QFs) and classification accuracy in deep neural networks (DNNs). It highlights that classification accuracy is generally a group measure, but examining individual images reveals that lower QFs can sometimes enhance the predicted rank and probability of the ground truth (GT) label. This is illustrated with examples from the ImageNet validation set using the Inception V3 model. Specifically, for Image #651, the original image has a GT label rank of second with a 37% probability, which improves to first with a 72% probability when compressed at QF=10. A similar trend is observed for Image #37 and when using the ResNet-50 V2 architecture. Overall, the findings suggest that JPEG compression may not always negatively impact DNN performance on specific images, as lower quality factors can lead to improved classification outcomes.
