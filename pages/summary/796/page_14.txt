The paper discusses a new network architecture designed to improve the classification accuracy of deep neural networks (DNNs) when working with the ImageNet dataset. The architecture consists of 11 parallel main architectures that share identical weight parameters to mitigate overfitting and reduce training complexity. This approach differs from traditional data augmentation, which modifies the dataset without altering the network architecture itself. Instead, the proposed design fundamentally changes the network structure while still training on the original image dataset.

The paper also contrasts this design with Siamese networks. While both share weights in early layers, the proposed architecture aims to classify inputs by concatenating embeddings from the original image and its 10 JPEG compressed versions, using cross-entropy loss as the objective function. In contrast, Siamese networks focus on differentiating between input samples using Euclidean distance.

Training was conducted using stochastic gradient descent (SGD) on two NVIDIA GeForce RTX 2080 Ti GPUs. The methodology involved initializing a learning rate, freezing earlier layers, and gradually unfurling modules while applying weight decay and batch normalization.

Experimental results indicate that the proposed topology significantly enhances Top 1 and Top 5 accuracy for both Inception V3 and ResNet-50 V2 models when compared to their default configurations, demonstrating the effectiveness of the new architecture.
