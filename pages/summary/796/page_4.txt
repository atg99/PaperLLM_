The paper discusses improvements in classification accuracy for deep neural networks (DNNs) using a new convolutional neural network (CNN) topology and a selector method. The study utilizes 11 parallel identical main architectures that are pre-trained on the ILSVRC 2012 dataset, leading to enhanced Top 1 and Top 5 classification accuracies—by 0.4% and 0.3% for Inception V3, and 0.4% and 0.2% for ResNet-50 V2, respectively. 

The proposed selector maintains classification accuracy even when the ground truth labels are unknown, achieving an average compression ratio of 3.1 compared to the original images. The organization of the paper includes a motivation case study, a detailed description of the HRS method, and analyses of both the new CNN topology and the selector's performance. The motivation section challenges the conventional belief that JPEG compression negatively impacts DNN classification performance, demonstrating through experiments that using a constant quality factor (QF) for all images leads to accuracy degradation. Overall, the research highlights significant advancements in both accuracy and efficiency in image classification tasks.
