The paper discusses a method for selecting compressed image versions to maximize classification accuracy in deep neural networks (DNNs), specifically through the introduction of the Highest Rank Selector (HRS). The HRS operates under the premise that the ground truth (GT) label of each image is known to the selector but not to the DNN itself. 

In the HRS framework, each original image is associated with 11 JPEG compressed versions. The selector ranks the GT label based on the prediction vector generated by the DNN for each compressed image. The image with the lowest rank for the GT label is chosen as input for the DNN. If there are multiple images achieving the same minimum rank, the selector chooses the largest index among them.

The paper provides an empirical analysis of the HRS's performance in improving classification accuracy and compression ratios for pre-trained models such as Inception V3 and ResNet-50 V2 on the ILSVRC 2012 dataset. 

A key result is presented in Theorem 1, which establishes that the HRS achieves the highest Top k accuracy for any given image set compared to all other selectors, demonstrating its optimality. The proof shows that the rank of the GT label under HRS is always less than or equal to that of any other selector, thus ensuring superior classification performance.
