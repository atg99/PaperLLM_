This paper presents a new framework for understanding how JPEG compression affects deep learning in image classification. By using various JPEG compressed versions of images, the framework allows for the selection of an optimal input that enhances classification accuracy while significantly reducing the image size.

Key findings show that a selector, termed the Highest Ranking Selector (HRS), can improve the Top 1 and Top 5 accuracy of popular models like Inception V3 and ResNet-50 while achieving high compression ratios. Specifically, HRS improved accuracy by 5.6% and 1.9% respectively, with a compression ratio of 8 across the ImageNet validation dataset.

Additionally, a new CNN topology was introduced for scenarios where the original image's label is unknown, which also yielded consistent improvements in accuracy. Other selectors were developed that maintained accuracy levels while achieving significant compression ratios.

The results suggest that advancements in compression algorithms could lead to improved performance in deep learning applications. The paper encourages further exploration of new compression theories that balance human perception and machine learning accuracy, indicating that current CNNs may not fully leverage the potential of image compression.
