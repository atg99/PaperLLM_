The paper discusses the impact of JPEG compression on feature extraction in images, specifically using Inception V3 as the model for analysis. Example 3 illustrates the process with Image #37, demonstrating how the Hierarchical Representation Selection (HRS) chooses certain features based on their relevance, with the original image and its compressed version both analyzed. 

Figures 9 and 10 highlight the feature maps extracted from the original image labeled "Brambling" and its compressed counterpart at a quality factor (QF) of 10. The analysis reveals that JPEG compression improves contrast in the feature maps while removing extraneous details, such as small spots on the frog's body, which can lead to misclassification with similar species. 

The findings suggest that while compression can enhance certain key features and reduce noise, it also affects how subsequent layers of the model interpret the image. The overall argument emphasizes the dual role of compression in potentially clarifying essential features while also altering the original content's classification potential.
