The analysis of Image #651 from the ImageNet validation set illustrates that a JPEG compressed image with a low quality factor (QF=10) can lead to a higher classification probability for its ground truth label compared to the original image. Specifically, the original image had a probability of 37% for the ground truth label "Brambling," while the compressed version achieved 72%. 

This phenomenon is attributed to the feature maps generated by the Inception V3 model, which show that the feature map from the compressed image is clearer and exhibits better contrast between the foreground and background. The JPEG compression process employs unequal quantization of discrete cosine transform coefficients, which diminishes background noise more effectively than foreground details. Consequently, this selective energy reduction enhances the DNN's focus on relevant features, as further processed through the rectified linear unit (ReLU) function.

The findings underscore the potential benefits of optimizing input image quality through compression techniques to enhance the performance of deep neural networks in image classification tasks.
