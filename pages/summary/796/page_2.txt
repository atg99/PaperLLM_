The research highlights that traditional approaches typically utilize a constant QF across all images in a dataset, which can lead to a uniform degradation in classification accuracy as compression quality decreases.

The study references previous work by Dodge et al., which demonstrated that as the QF decreases, both Top 1 and Top 5 classification accuracies for different deep neural network (DNN) models decline significantly. This underscores the negative impact of compression on model performance, especially with low-quality JPEG images. Efforts to mitigate this impact, such as data augmentation and stability training, have shown some success but still result in notable accuracy loss.

In contrast, this paper proposes a novel approach where each image in the dataset is compressed using different QFs before being fed into the DNN. This method aims to identify which version of compressed raw data is optimal for enhancing DL performance. By allowing for diverse QF values, the study seeks to better understand the relationship between compression quality and classification accuracy, suggesting that a more tailored approach to data compression may yield improved outcomes in DL applications.
