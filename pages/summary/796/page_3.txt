Deep Neural Network (DNN) in a novel way. The paper investigates the potential of image compression to enhance classification accuracy while reducing input size for DNNs using the ILSVRC 2012 dataset. Each original image is paired with ten compressed versions, created at different quality factors (QF). The key question addressed is whether a selector can optimize the choice of compressed image to improve both Top 1 and Top 5 classification accuracy.

The authors introduce the Highest Rank Selector (HRS), which leverages the prediction vector from the DNN to determine the rank of the ground truth label for each compressed version. HRS selects the version that ranks the highest for the ground truth label, prioritizing lower QF in case of ties. This approach demonstrates that, contrary to traditional beliefs, appropriate use of compression can significantly enhance classification accuracy while reducing the bit size of the input image.

Empirical results show that applying HRS to DNN architectures like Inception V3 and ResNet-50 V2 leads to an average increase in Top 1 accuracy by 5.6% and Top 5 accuracy by 1.9% across the ImageNet validation set. Furthermore, the selected compressed version by HRS achieves an average compression ratio of 8 compared to the original image.

In scenarios where the ground truth label is unknown, HRS cannot be applied. To address this, the authors propose a new convolutional neural network (CNN) architecture that incorporates the original image and its compressed versions in parallel, demonstrating that compression can still enhance classification accuracy even without knowledge of the ground truth. This work highlights the effectiveness of strategic compression in improving deep learning performance.
