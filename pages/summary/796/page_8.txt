The paper presents a framework for optimizing image selection for deep neural networks (DNNs) using a method called High-Resolution Selection (HRS). The proof of Theorem 1 indicates that the average HRS accuracy can be represented as a function of the cardinality of selected images and their associated labels. An application scenario illustrates the practical use of this method, where an organizer can verify participants' identities at a secure gathering using a DNN that only has access to JPEG compressed images, while the organizer retains the ground truth labels.

Empirical results demonstrate significant accuracy improvements for HRS when applied to the ImageNet validation dataset, with Inception V3 achieving a Top 1 accuracy of 83.37%, up from 77.6%, and ResNet-50 V2 improving from 75.58% to 80.95%. The average improvement in Top 1 accuracy is 5.6%, while Top 5 accuracy sees a 1.9% increase.

The analysis further reveals that HRS tends to select JPEG images with lower quality factors (QFs) more frequently, particularly favoring the lowest QF of 10. This selection strategy results in a substantial reduction in image size, achieving an average compression ratio of 8 compared to the original images. Overall, the findings highlight HRS's effectiveness in enhancing DNN classification accuracy while significantly reducing data size.
