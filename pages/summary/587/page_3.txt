This paper examines the performance of various neural network architectures for diagnosing referable diabetic retinopathy (DR) and thoracic pathologies using transfer learning from ImageNet. It categorizes DR into referable (grades 3 and up) and non-referable (grades 1 and 2), and evaluates the models' performance using AUC-ROC metrics.

The study includes two datasets: retinal fundus images for DR and chest X-rays from the CHEXPERT dataset for diagnosing conditions like atelectasis and cardiomegaly. The authors compare the performance of standard ImageNet architectures, specifically ResNet50 and Inception-v3, against smaller convolutional networks they designed, referred to as CBR models.

The findings reveal that transfer learning from ImageNet does not significantly enhance model performance, particularly for smaller architectures. Despite their inferior accuracy on ImageNet tasks, the CBR models performed comparably to the larger architectures on medical tasks. This suggests that standard ImageNet models may be overparametrized for medical applications with fewer classes.

The paper emphasizes the importance of exploring transfer learning effects on smaller architectures, especially in medical contexts where computational efficiency is crucial. It concludes that smaller, lightweight convolutional networks can achieve competitive performance on medical datasets without the need for large, complex models. The results indicate that transfer learning strategies may need to be re-evaluated for specific medical imaging tasks.
