The paper investigates the impact of pretraining on model representations and convergence speed in deep learning architectures, specifically focusing on Resnet and CBR-Small. Through visualizations of conv1 filters before and after training on the RETINA task, the authors highlight that Resnet filters exhibit minimal change post-training, indicating limited adaptation. In contrast, CBR-Small shows significant filter movement and fails to learn Gabor filters when trained from scratch, even erasing some pretrained Gabors.

The study further explores how transfer learning influences convergence speed, revealing that it offers feature-independent benefits primarily through improved weight scaling. The authors find that utilizing pretrained weights, especially from the lowest layers, markedly enhances convergence speed. They suggest hybrid approaches that incorporate only a subset of pretrained weights alongside a redesigned network top, allowing for greater flexibility in applications. The paper concludes by demonstrating that initializing weights based on the mean and variance of pretrained weights (Mean Var init) can significantly accelerate convergence, underscoring the importance of weight scaling in transfer learning.
