The paper investigates the impact of different initialization strategies on the convergence speed of deep learning models, specifically in the context of transfer learning for medical tasks. It compares standard transfer learning methods with a Mean Var initialization scheme and demonstrates that using only the scaling of pretrained weights significantly enhances convergence speed on datasets like RETINA and CHEXPERT.

Figures illustrate the results, showing that the Mean Var initialization outperforms training from scratch, leading to faster achievement of high AUC scores. The study also explores weight transfusion, where only a subset of pretrained weights is reused, particularly focusing on the lowest layers of the network. Results indicate that reusing weights from the first convolutional layer (conv1) yields the most substantial speedup in convergence, even when additional layers are included.

The findings suggest a hybrid approach to transfer learning, proposing the idea of reusing pretrained weights up to certain layers while redesigning the higher layers to be lighter and initialized randomly. Additionally, the potential benefits of using synthetic Gabor filters for the initial layer are discussed, indicating that such strategies can match the performance of traditional transfer learning methods.

In summary, the paper advocates for flexible and innovative transfer learning strategies that leverage pretrained weights effectively, particularly emphasizing the importance of lower layers for feature reuse and convergence efficiency.
