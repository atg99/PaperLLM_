This paper investigates the effects of transfer learning on the hidden representations learned by neural networks, particularly focusing on the differences between models initialized with pretrained weights and those trained from random initialization. Using Canonical Correlation Analysis (CCA) to quantify the representational similarities, the authors find that larger models, such as Resnet and Inception, demonstrate significant differences in their learned representations compared to those trained from random initialization. In contrast, smaller models show less distinction. 

The study highlights that representations learned from pretrained weights are less similar to each other than those from random initialization, suggesting that transfer learning results in unique feature reuse patterns. The authors employ Singular Vector Canonical Correlation Analysis (SVCCA) to analyze the complex latent representations and overcome challenges associated with their mapping to inputs and outputs.

Through experiments, the paper concludes that transfer learning leads to different hidden representations, particularly in higher layers of larger models, which can inform new hybrid approaches to transfer learning. Overall, the findings emphasize the importance of model size in the transfer learning process and the varying representational characteristics between different initialization strategies.
