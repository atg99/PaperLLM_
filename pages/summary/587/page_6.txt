The paper investigates the representational similarity of various neural network architectures, specifically Resnet50 and two CBR models (Large and Small), before and after fine-tuning on a medical task. The analysis focuses on Canonical Correlation Analysis (CCA) similarity scores across different layers of the networks, comparing random initialization and pretrained weights.

Key findings indicate that lower layers maintain a higher similarity to their initial states, particularly in the Resnet50 model, suggesting that these layers exhibit limited change during training. The results show that the similarity scores for the lowest layers remain significantly higher for pretrained weights compared to random initialization, highlighting a tendency for feature reuse primarily in these layers.

In contrast, smaller models like CBR-Small demonstrate less stability in their lower layers, as indicated by lower similarity scores. The analysis reveals a strong correlation between initial and converged similarity scores for larger models, suggesting that larger networks tend to undergo less change during training, which may indicate overparameterization for the specific task. This trend was less pronounced in the smaller models, where functions learned from different initializations were more similar.

Overall, the findings emphasize that larger models, such as Resnet50, display a unique behavior in their training dynamics, particularly in the lowest layers, while smaller models adapt more significantly, reflecting their different capacities for feature learning and reuse.
