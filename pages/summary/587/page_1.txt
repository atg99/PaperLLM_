This paper investigates the use of transfer learning in medical imaging, particularly the common practice of utilizing pretrained models from natural image datasets like ImageNet. The authors, Maithra Raghu, Chiyuan Zhang, Jon Kleinberg, and Samy Bengio, argue that despite the widespread adoption of this approach, transfer learning may not significantly enhance performance for medical tasks. Their evaluation of two large-scale medical imaging tasks reveals that simpler, lightweight models can achieve performance comparable to more complex ImageNet architectures.

The authors highlight key differences between natural image classification and medical imaging, such as variations in data size, feature characteristics, and task specifications. They attribute some of the limited benefits of transfer learning to the over-parametrization of standard models rather than effective feature reuse. The paper further isolates instances where feature reuse is beneficial and discusses the implications for more efficient model exploration in medical imaging.

Additionally, the authors explore feature-independent advantages of transfer learning, particularly related to weight scaling. Overall, the findings challenge the conventional wisdom surrounding transfer learning in medical imaging and call for a reevaluation of its application in this critical field.
