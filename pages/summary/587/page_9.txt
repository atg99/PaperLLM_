In this study, we explored key aspects of transfer learning in medical imaging, focusing on both standard ImageNet architectures and lightweight models across two large-scale medical tasks. Our findings indicate that transfer learning provides limited performance improvements, with smaller architectures performing comparably to larger ImageNet models. We observed significant correlations between the initialization similarities and post-training outcomes, suggesting that standard models may be overparameterized for specific medical tasks. Additionally, we found that effective feature reuse is predominantly observed in the lower layers of the network.

To leverage these insights, we investigated hybrid approaches that involve reusing a portion of pretrained weights while slimming the rest of the network. Our experiments demonstrated that these hybrid methods preserve the advantages of transfer learning while offering new opportunities for enhanced model performance. We also highlighted the feature-independent benefits of transfer learning, which include improved weight scaling and faster convergence rates.

Overall, our research contributes to understanding transfer learning's applicability in medical imaging, revealing that alternative strategies may yield comparable or superior results to conventional methods.
