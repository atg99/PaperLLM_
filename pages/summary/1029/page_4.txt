The paper discusses the integration of AI ethics into the computing curriculum, highlighting the current shortcomings in education regarding ethical responsibilities for AI developers. It argues that while there have been efforts to introduce ethics, such as familiarizing students with professional codes, these measures alone are insufficient to influence behavior. The Mozilla Foundation's support for developing ethics pedagogy is noted, with a specific example from Georgia Tech where students engage in role-playing scenarios related to autonomous vehicles to enhance moral sensitivity and perspective-taking.

To effectively prepare students for emerging ethical challenges in AI, the paper proposes three key elements: 

1. Teaching ethical design principles for AI algorithms, emphasizing participatory design and the importance of ethical considerations.
2. Incorporating data science fundamentals and ethical data acquisition, requiring students to confront issues of privacy and fairness using real-world data.
3. Providing ethics education throughout the curriculum, reinforcing its significance in both technical and non-technical courses.

The paper also stresses the importance of interdisciplinary collaboration in creating and teaching AI ethics content, as the complexities of AI challenges require diverse expertise. Furthermore, it emphasizes cultivating critical thinking and ethical reasoning skills that can be applied across various professional contexts.

Looking ahead, the authors call for a commitment to AI ethics education to ensure responsible technological development. They note the increasing relevance of jobs in AI ethics, suggesting the possibility of roles such as chief AI ethics officer, and express hope that these developments represent genuine efforts to prioritize ethics rather than mere "ethics washing." Ultimately, the paper advocates for a central role of ethics in AI education to promote a socially responsible approach to technology.
