The paper discusses the profound impact of Artificial Intelligence (AI) on society, highlighting both its benefits and potential harms. As AI integrates into various aspects of life, ethical concerns arise regarding its design, deployment, and use. The authors stress the importance of educating future AI developers and stakeholders about these ethical implications, suggesting that a systematic inclusion of AI ethics in educational curricula is essential.

The authors note that while AI can provide observable benefits, it also poses risks associated with data collection and algorithmic decision-making. Examples include employers monitoring employees and automated tools potentially disqualifying job candidates based on social media data. The paper emphasizes the human element in AI, arguing that algorithms, designed by fallible humans, can perpetuate biases and make flawed decisions.

Addressing these ethical challenges is complex, as defining fairness in AI is subjective and can evolve over time. The authors argue that simply fixing biases in data or algorithms is insufficient; a more comprehensive approach is necessary to mitigate the societal ills exacerbated by AI. The paper ultimately calls for a deeper commitment to AI ethics education to prepare individuals for the responsibilities that come with developing and implementing AI technologies.
