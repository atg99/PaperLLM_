The paper discusses the ethical challenges posed by artificial intelligence (AI) and emphasizes the need for proactive measures to address these issues. It highlights that the expansion of AI usage brings forth various ethical dilemmas, such as assumptions about device capabilities that may not exist. To counter these challenges, numerous initiatives have emerged, including the Montreal Declaration and the IEEE's ethical standards for intelligent systems. Companies are also beginning to articulate their stance on ethical issues like fairness and transparency.

Despite these efforts, the paper argues that the root problems stem from human fallibility and biases that influence technology design and use. It stresses the importance of fostering a professional mindset among AI developers, encouraging them to recognize the ethical dimensions of their work. Developers often view ethics as external to their responsibilities, but their design choices carry significant ethical implications that affect individuals and society.

The paper draws a parallel to the medical profession's Hippocratic Oath, suggesting that AI developers should similarly acknowledge their ethical obligations to the public. Ultimately, it calls for a deeper understanding of these responsibilities within the AI community to ensure that technology is developed with ethical considerations at its core.
