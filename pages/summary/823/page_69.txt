The paper discusses various approaches to achieving sparsity in deep learning models, particularly focusing on BERT and RoBERTa. The findings indicate that these models can maintain over 90% of their original accuracy while being pruned to 40-65% sparsity on GLUE benchmark tasks. It highlights that even random structured removal can yield good performance at low sparsity levels.

McCarley et al. (2020) explore multiple pruning techniques, comparing random pruning, a first-order gain metric, a leave-one-out score, and sampled L0-regularization. Their results suggest that L0-regularization is the most effective, allowing for the pruning of 40-75% of model elements with only a minimal drop in performance.

Wang et al. (2020a) propose a modified L0-regularization approach that utilizes low-rank factorization for weight matrices, incorporating a diagonal pruning matrix to retain the most significant components.

Further, Lin et al. (2020) and Fan et al. (2020) investigate pruning larger structures within transformers. Lin et al. focus on entire residual blocks based on activation levels, while Fan et al. introduce LayerDrop, a method that randomly drops entire layers during training to optimize model size for inference.

In terms of unstructured sparsification, Prasanna et al. (2020) find that iterative magnitude pruning often results in smaller networks with better accuracy compared to structured pruning. Gordon et al. (2020) demonstrate that a pretrained BERT model can be pruned to 40% sparsity without significant performance loss, while Chen et al. (2020) further contribute to the discussion on pruning strategies. Overall, the paper underscores the effectiveness of various pruning methods in enhancing model efficiency without substantial accuracy loss.
