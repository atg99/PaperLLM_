The paper discusses various methods for achieving sparsity in deep learning models, focusing on regularization techniques and pruning strategies. It highlights the use of regularization terms, specifically L1 and L2 penalties, to enhance sparsity after an initial magnitude pruning of weights. Liu et al. (2017) propose a method that incorporates scaling factors related to gating variables, which can be integrated into batch normalization layers, allowing for a more efficient pruning process. This method has been adapted by other researchers, such as Gordon et al. (2018) and Kang and Han (2020), to prune neurons and account for the effects of activation functions like ReLU.

Huang and Wang (2018) extend this approach by applying scaling factors to various components of convolutional networks, while Ye et al. (2018) use similar scaling in the context of batch normalization. These methods leverage techniques like Accelerated Proximal Gradient and Iterative Shrinkage-Thresholding Algorithm (ISTA) for effective sparsification.

Additionally, the paper touches on alternative regularization techniques aimed at promoting low-rank weight matrices or weight similarity, although these are noted as being outside the main focus of the work. It mentions the neuron energy penalty introduced by Chauvin (1989), which reduces neuron magnitudes to facilitate sparsity, and Tartaglione et al. (2018)'s sensitivity-based regularization that allows for gradual setting of low-sensitivity parameters to zero during training.

Furthermore, the paper addresses the relationship between pruning and weight quantization, detailing a model that quantizes weights while considering the associated errors. It concludes with a cautionary note on the limitations of grouped L1 and L2 regularization in the presence of batch normalization layers, as these can obscure the regularization effects by rescaling outputs.

Overall, the paper provides a comprehensive overview of current strategies and challenges in promoting sparsity within deep learning models.
