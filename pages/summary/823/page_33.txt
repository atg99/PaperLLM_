The paper discusses the concept of sparsity in deep learning, focusing on the Tri-state ReLU (Srinivas and Babu, 2016), which generalizes element gating for neuron pruning. The Tri-state ReLU is defined to include learnable binary parameters, allowing for the removal of entire layers or neurons based on specific conditions. The parameters are constrained to the interval [0, 1] and can be viewed as learning the parameters of a binomial distribution, indicating the selection of weights.

Srinivas et al. employ maximum likelihood for weight gating during training, while You et al. (2019) utilize a first-order approximation of the loss function to prune filters structurally. The exploration of the Jacobian matrix reveals that some weights are correlated, leading to redundancy. Zhou and Si (1999) and Xu and Ho (2006) apply QR factorization to identify redundant weights and neurons, benefiting from the nonlinearity of activation functions.

Pruning during transfer learning leverages first-order gradient information. Molchanov et al. (2017) prune feature maps based on the magnitude of gradients, while Sanh et al. (2020) introduce movement pruning, which considers the direction of gradient changes to identify less important weights. Ding et al. (2019b) propose a global sparse momentum method that classifies weights into important and unimportant sets based on gradient magnitude, allowing for dynamic updates during training. This "re-selection" process is noted to be crucial for model accuracy but occurs infrequently. Overall, the paper highlights various strategies for effectively implementing sparsity in deep learning models through parameter pruning and weight management.
