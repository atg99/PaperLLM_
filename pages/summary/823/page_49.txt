The paper discusses various methods for achieving sparsity in deep learning through pruning techniques. One approach is based on KL-divergence, as outlined by Engelbrecht, which suggests that parameters with low sensitivity variance across the training set can be pruned. Another method, proposed by Baykal et al., involves sampling to estimate the "empirical sensitivity" of neuron inputs, leading to sparse weight matrices with theoretical guarantees on sparsity and generalization. 

Additionally, genetic algorithms can be applied to pruning, where multiple pruned network versions are trained and evolved based on parameter count and generalization performance. However, this method is noted to be impractical for large-scale models due to its complexity. 

Diversity networks utilize Determinantal Point Processes to select diverse neurons, eliminating the need for fine-tuning after initial training. Quantized neural networks leverage rounding errors to inform pruning decisions. Lastly, Tang et al. introduce "knockoff" features to identify and prune filters that are sensitive to irrelevant inputs, thus enhancing model efficiency. Overall, these methods highlight the ongoing exploration of efficient network architectures in deep learning through innovative pruning strategies.
