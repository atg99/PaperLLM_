The paper discusses advancements in sparsity within deep learning, particularly focusing on the development of accelerators that enhance training efficiency and reduce energy consumption. Accelerators like those mentioned by Zhang et al. (2019) utilize early structure adaptation and iterative pruning schedules to improve training speed by up to 40%. More generic accelerators, such as SparTen and SIGMA, are designed for sparse matrix-vector products and support flexible matrix reuse through bitmap storage, although their efficiency varies based on the sparsity level.

Nvidia's Ampere micro-architecture supports structured sparsity, optimizing the processing of dense blocks, while current architectures are primarily tailored for the modest sparsity levels found in deep neural networks. Future advancements may lead to accelerators capable of handling significantly higher sparsity levels, akin to those in scientific computing.

The paper provides an overview of existing accelerators supporting sparse deep learning, highlighting that most are optimized for inference, with some specifically designed for training. The accelerators target various layer types, including fully connected, convolutional, and recurrent layers, with varying efficiency.
