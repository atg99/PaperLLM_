The paper discusses the importance of sparsity in deep learning, particularly focusing on the frequency of updates for structural changes and input feature selection. It highlights that while high-frequency updates can enhance performance, tuning the right frequency is essential and may be influenced by factors such as minibatch size and gradient noise. There is a lack of structured research on this hyperparameter, but studies suggest that a higher update frequency benefits training, especially in the context of ephemeral weight and activation sparsity.

The discussion on input feature selection reveals that many datasets contain irrelevant input dimensions. Techniques like sensitivity analysis, as proposed by Engelbrecht et al., allow for the pruning of input neurons based on their relevance. This involves calculating the sensitivity of each input with respect to the output, followed by pruning and retraining. Castellano and Fanelli further conceptualize feature selection as network pruning, iteratively removing less significant features. QuickSelection introduces a method using a sparse denoising autoencoder for unsupervised feature selection by evaluating the salience of features based on the weights of their connections.

The paper also explores the use of sparsity in creating ensemble models, which allows for multiple smaller models to be trained within a limited parameter budget. This approach can enhance performance in tasks such as image recognition, as demonstrated by Collins and Kohli, who found that ensembles can outperform single models with the same parameter constraints. Overall, the paper emphasizes the potential of sparsity to optimize deep learning models through careful tuning and innovative feature selection methods.
