The paper discusses the role of sparsity in deep learning, highlighting its Bayesian interpretation and its function as a method of data compression. Historically, earlier research has emphasized improved generalization and interpretability through sparsification, particularly in smaller models. However, the impact of sparsity on larger models, which now contain millions or billions of parameters, remains uncertain. Recent studies have suggested that sparsity can enhance robustness against adversarial attacks and can be modeled as noise, similar to dropout techniques.

A significant focus of modern research is on improving computational efficiency while preserving model accuracy. As deep learning models become increasingly resource-intensive, with examples like Inception-V3 and GPT-3 requiring immense computational power and costs, exploring sparsity during training has become critical. Current sparsification methods can achieve substantial reductions in model size (10-100 times) without significantly sacrificing accuracy, leading to improvements in computational, storage, and energy efficiency.

The authors provide an overview of key techniques in sparsification, addressing which components of neural networks can be sparsified, the timing of these changes, and the methods used. They also discuss the need for maintaining model complexity by re-adding connections during training. The paper acknowledges the diversity of techniques and evaluation settings in the community, which complicates comparisons and assessments of state-of-the-art approaches. To address this, the authors aim to summarize existing techniques and emphasize qualitative aspects of model design.
