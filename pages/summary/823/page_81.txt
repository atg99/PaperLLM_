The paper by Renda et al. (2020) examines different strategies for sparsity in deep learning, specifically comparing standard fine-tuning after pruning to a method known as weight rewinding. Weight rewinding involves resetting weights to a previous iteration's values before retraining with the same learning rate schedule. While Renda et al. suggest that this method, termed Iterative Magnitude Pruning, has merit, other studies by Savarese et al. and Chen et al. indicate that fine-tuning from the most recent weights is generally more effective for tasks in image recognition and natural language processing. They found that while weight rewinding can be beneficial, it is outperformed by simply resetting the learning rate to the initial iteration. Furthermore, Evci et al. provide evidence that lottery ticket networks tend to relearn the pruning solution, while Ding et al. demonstrate that using first-order information can outperform magnitude-based pruning methods. Morcos et al. also highlight the transferability of lottery tickets across various datasets and optimizers.

The paper discusses the differences between structured and unstructured sparsification. It notes that unstructured pruning typically maintains better accuracy per element, but structured pruning offers enhanced computational performance on modern hardware. This suggests that structured sparse models may require more training iterations and operations during inference to achieve similar efficiency. Additionally, random pruning at initialization is shown to be more effective for neurons and filters compared to weights, leading to a recommendation for different pruning strategies based on the structure.

The paper further explores the distinction between sparsification and manual design in neural network architectures. It points out that sparsifying older architectures may not yield the same improvements as newer designs, which often incorporate manual sparsification techniques such as bottleneck layers or depthwise separable convolutions. These optimized networks can become more challenging to sparsify, suggesting a need for a balance between induced biases and the flexibility inherent in deep learning architectures. Overall, the findings underscore the nuances of sparsity in deep learning, highlighting the importance of tailoring approaches to specific architectures and tasks.
