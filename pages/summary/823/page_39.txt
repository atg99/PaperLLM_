The paper discusses the concept of sparsity in deep learning, particularly focusing on the Kronecker-Factored Approximate Curvature (K-FAC) method for optimizing neural networks. It begins by establishing the gradient of the objective with respect to pre-activations and simplifies the expression for the gradient with respect to weight matrices. The Fisher information matrix is then formulated using the expectations of these gradients, which allows for the derivation of the K-FAC approximation.

The K-FAC method is advantageous as it facilitates efficient computation of the inverse of the approximated Fisher matrix by leveraging the properties of Kronecker products. This approach primarily applies to fully-connected layers, but adaptations are necessary for convolutional or recurrent neural networks. The paper cites various applications of K-FAC in optimization and reinforcement learning, specifically in the context of pruning techniques.

Wang et al. (2019) introduces the EigenDamage technique, which involves reparameterizing the neural network in the Kronecker-factored eigenbasis and applying a Hessian-based structured pruning framework. The paper emphasizes the need to consider correlations between weights within the same structure for effective pruning. Overall, the work highlights the analytical benefits of K-FAC in enhancing the efficiency of deep learning models while addressing challenges related to layer types and weight correlations.
