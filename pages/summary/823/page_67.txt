The paper discusses sparsity in deep learning, particularly focusing on transformer networks, which have revolutionized natural language processing and are expanding into areas like computer vision. Transformers, introduced by Vaswani et al. in 2017, utilize a multi-head attention mechanism to process sequences effectively, allowing for deeper and more parallelizable architectures compared to recurrent neural networks.

The architecture consists of multiple transformer layers that include multi-head attention, feedforward networks, layer normalization, and residual connections. Large transformer models, such as BERT and GPT-3, contain millions to trillions of parameters, making them challenging to deploy in practical applications. As a result, model compression techniques, including pruning, have become crucial. Research indicates that compressing a large, over-parameterized model can lead to better accuracy than training smaller models from scratch.

Various strategies for sparsifying transformer networks are examined, including first-order and magnitude-based pruning methods. The paper highlights the importance of compression in maintaining model performance while reducing the computational and memory demands of transformers. Comprehensive reviews of compression techniques for models like BERT are also referenced, underlining the ongoing advancements in this area.
