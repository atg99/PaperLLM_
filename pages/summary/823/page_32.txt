In this section, T. Hoefler et al. explore a pruning process that aims to enhance or maintain the accuracy of pruned neural network models. They propose the utilization of first-order Taylor expansion of the training loss function to determine the importance of weights during training. By leveraging gradients computed in the optimization process, the authors suggest that weight selection for pruning can be efficiently achieved.

The authors introduce a method that considers the total weight change over the training period, advocating for the removal of weights that exhibit minimal changes. This approach aligns with previous works, such as those by Karnin and Golub, and is complemented by the squared gradient-weight product method proposed by Molchanov et al., which serves as an approximation for the importance of neurons or filters.

Additionally, a gradient-based scheme using a binary gating function is described, allowing for the iterative pruning of less significant neurons. The relevance of a neuron is determined by the gradient of a gating function that regulates its inclusion. This method, inspired by Mozer and Smolensky, necessitates two backpropagation stages—one for weight adjustments and another for gate perturbation.

Overall, the authors emphasize that these techniques can effectively integrate pruning into the model optimization process, enabling the selective removal of weights or neurons based on their contribution to the overall loss function.
