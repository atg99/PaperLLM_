The paper discusses the concept of sparsity in deep learning, highlighting various approaches and techniques aimed at optimizing neural networks through reduced complexity. Key contributions include the exploration of pruning versus clipping methods for neural network efficiency, as examined by Janowsky. Jayakumar et al. introduce Top-K Always Sparse Training, a method designed to maintain sparsity during training. Jiang and Agrawal analyze the benefits of sparse and quantized communication in distributed learning, while Jin et al. propose error-bounded lossy compression for neural networks in their DeepSZ framework. 

The paper also references iterative hard thresholding methods for training compact neural networks, as presented by Jin et al. and discusses cognitive and neural plasticity relevant to aging. The hierarchical mixtures of experts method by Jordan and Jacobs provides insights into network optimization. Kameyama and Kosugi's work on automatic fusion and splitting of neural elements contributes to network size optimization, while Kang and Han's operation-aware soft channel pruning introduces differentiable masks for enhanced efficiency. Lastly, Kanjilal et al. emphasize reduced-size neural networks through singular value decomposition and subset selection. Overall, the paper consolidates various strategies and research findings that aim to improve the performance and efficiency of deep learning models through sparsity.
