The paper discusses the selection of the least significant weights to remove from a fully-trained model, utilizing a second-order Taylor expansion of the training loss function. This approach builds on earlier work by Le Cun et al. (1990) and is further developed by Hassibi and Stork (1992), who introduced a more general framework. Both frameworks focus on minimizing the loss increase associated with removing parameters, often neglecting first-order terms in their analysis.

The Taylor expansion of the loss function is expressed as a function of model perturbation, specifically targeting a single weight's effect on the loss. By assuming the model is at a local minimum, the zero gradient term can be disregarded, simplifying the problem to finding the weight whose removal minimizes a specific expression involving the Hessian matrix of the loss.

The resulting "saliency measure" for each weight is defined as a function of the weight's squared value and the inverse Hessian matrix's diagonal elements. Weights can be ranked by this saliency statistic, with lower values indicating better candidates for removal.

The method also suggests optimal adjustments to the remaining weights post-pruning, which can be calculated using a defined formula. The initial derivations and numerical methods for this metric were established for small networks, consisting of tens or hundreds of parameters.

Additionally, the paper mentions Optimal Cell Damage (OCD) by Cibas et al. (1996), which applies a similar saliency calculation to prune input features. However, this method is reported to be less effective than regularization-based approaches. The paper indicates that further assumptions and practical limitations will be explored in subsequent sections.
