In the paper by T. Hoefler et al., the authors discuss the critical aspect of selecting elements for removal in sparsification schemes, which is essential for optimizing neural networks. They highlight that while evaluating the network's performance with and without certain elements is a precise method, it is not scalable due to the computational burden of training multiple networks. Random removal of elements is another approach, linked to compressive sensing, but it may not yield optimal results.

The authors propose that using metrics of importance to guide element removal generally leads to better performance in creating compressed models with high sparsity. They provide a comprehensive overview of various selection methods without quantifying their effectiveness, as this can vary significantly based on network architecture, hyperparameters, and learning tasks. Instead, they focus on the rationale behind each method and reference specific results for different architectures.

Additionally, the paper differentiates between structured and unstructured element removal. Structured sparsity allows for the removal of elements in defined patterns, which can simplify processing and storage, while unstructured sparsity enables the removal of arbitrary elements but complicates the management of non-zero weights. Overall, the authors aim to clarify the landscape of sparsification methods and their implications for deep neural network optimization.
