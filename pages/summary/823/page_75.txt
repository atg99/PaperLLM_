The paper discusses advancements in deep learning architectures that leverage sparsity to enhance efficiency in inference. Han et al. (2016a) introduce the Efficient Inference Engine (EIE), which is designed for sparse models in convolutional neural networks (CNNs), utilizing parameter sharing and supporting sparse matrices and activation vectors. The architecture employs fine-grained parallelism by distributing weight matrix columns across processing elements (PEs) and efficiently handling non-zero activation values through a queuing system. The silicon implementation of EIE demonstrates significant speed and energy efficiency improvements compared to traditional GPUs.

Kim et al. (2018) present a method that capitalizes on both weight and activation sparsity in convolutional layers, alongside other sparse deep neural network (DNN) accelerators like Cambricon-X and SCNN, which achieve notable speedups and reduced energy consumption. Niu et al. (2020) design an FPGA-based accelerator that processes sparse convolutional layers in the frequency domain, facing challenges related to limited on-chip memory.

Further, the paper explores accelerators for sparse recurrent neural networks (RNNs). Han et al. (2017) introduce the Efficient Speech Recognition Engine (ESE), an FPGA accelerator that improves performance and energy efficiency for LSTM models by balancing load with block-balanced sparsity. Gupta et al. (2019) propose an ASIC design for sparse RNNs, utilizing a bitmask scheme to manage weight and activation sparsity.

The paper also highlights the potential of predicting output activation sparsity to enhance performance. SparseNN (Zhu et al., 2018) shows that predicting non-zero output patterns can lead to significant performance gains and energy savings. Additionally, LRADNN (Zhu et al., 2016) employs Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) to predict sparsity, resulting in faster computations compared to traditional methods.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of exploiting sparsity in deep learning to achieve high performance and efficiency in various architectures.
