In this section, T. Hoefler et al. discuss the role of convolutional layers in deep learning, particularly highlighting their designed sparsity. Convolutional operators perform weighted averages over local neuron regions, which effectively reduces the number of weights while incorporating local information. Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) have shown significant success in tasks such as image classification and segmentation. The convolution operator can be viewed as a sparse alternative to fully connected layers, as it limits connections to local regions determined by parameters like kernel size and strides.

The authors introduce the concept of Locally Connected Networks (LCNs), which utilize unique filters for each output neuron to specialize in different spatial areas. They reference the work of Olshausen and Field, who suggest that sparsity is crucial for vision processing. To enhance translational equivariance, the authors describe weight sharing, where local filters are reused across output neurons.

The convolutional operation is defined mathematically, illustrating how input channels are processed through unique filters to produce output features. The text indicates that additional sparsity can be integrated into CNNs and other deep neural network classes.

The overview of sparsity in deep learning emphasizes its benefits in both performance and model robustness. The authors plan to explore the trade-offs between sparsity and accuracy, various sparse storage formats, methodologies for element removal, and schedules for sparsification, applicable to both inference and training scenarios.
