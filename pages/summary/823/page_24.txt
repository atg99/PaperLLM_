In the paper by T. Hoefler et al., the authors investigate the effects of SGD fine-tuning on the performance of various pruning methods in neural networks. They highlight that while pruning can lead to immediate gains in accuracy, these improvements may diminish after fine-tuning. The authors present a gradual pruning schedule applied to ResNet-50 on the ImageNet dataset, illustrated in Figure 9, which shows the evolution of validation accuracy for two methods: global magnitude pruning and the second-order Wood-Fisher method.

The pruning schedule begins with a sparsity target of 5% and progresses to 95%, incorporating fixed-length fine-tuning periods between pruning steps. The results demonstrate a drop in accuracy immediately following each pruning step, with the Wood-Fisher method initially outperforming the global magnitude method. However, this performance gap narrows significantly after fine-tuning, leading to a final accuracy that is only 0.4% higher for the Wood-Fisher method compared to the magnitude-based approach.

Additionally, the paper discusses the update frequency of sparse model structures, noting that while ephemeral sparsification can be applied to each minibatch, structural changes may benefit from delays to minimize noise and optimize the costly rearrangement of data structures. This observation aligns with biological processes, such as neurotransmitter activation, suggesting a potential parallel in model training strategies.
