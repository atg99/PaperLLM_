The paper discusses advancements in the application of natural gradient methods for optimizing deep neural networks, highlighting significant contributions from various researchers. Initially, Amari's discovery in 1998 focused on small-scale, single-layer networks. Singh and Alistarh revisited this in 2020, adapting the method for large-scale networks by proposing a block-diagonal approximation. Their approach showed that it provides a more accurate local prediction of loss during pruning compared to existing methods, resulting in reduced accuracy drops and improved outcomes after fine-tuning.

The paper also reviews extensions to the OBD/OBS framework introduced in the early 90s, such as those by Pedersen et al., who improved the method by estimating generalization error instead of just loss, incorporating weight decay, and addressing nuisance parameters that can skew pruning results.

Additionally, it explores alternative uses of the Fisher matrix, noting how researchers like Tamura and Fletcher utilized singular value decomposition to determine optimal neuron counts in hidden layers. The discussion includes the Kronecker-Factored Approximate Curvature (K-FAC) method, which serves as a pre-conditioner for gradient updates, further illustrating its application in fully-connected networks through a gradient derivation example.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the evolution and refinement of methods for optimizing neural networks, particularly in the context of pruning and generalization, while also considering the implications of the Fisher matrix and K-FAC in these processes.
