The paper discusses the process of sparsification in deep learning, outlining a structured approach to optimize network performance through weight management. The process consists of several key steps: 

1. **Initialization**: Create a random network structure or use a sparse network construction method like SNIP. Weights are initialized, often randomly, or through specialized techniques for sparse networks.
   
2. **Training**: The network undergoes an initial training phase using either a standard or sparsity-inducing training schedule until it converges or is stopped early.

3. **Pruning and Regrowing**: The network is pruned to remove less important elements, followed by regrowing techniques to adjust the network structure.

4. **Retraining**: After pruning, the network is retrained, which may improve accuracy, although this step is sometimes skipped. The importance of retraining is emphasized as it helps maintain model quality post-pruning.

5. **Iterative Process**: The steps can be repeated, allowing for multiple rounds of pruning and retraining to refine the network further.

The paper highlights that while pruning may remove less critical weights, the resulting model can suffer from quality degradation. Therefore, retraining is crucial to recover or enhance the model's performance. The correlation between pre- and post-pruning accuracy is discussed, indicating that effective retraining strategies can lead to well-performing sparse networks.
