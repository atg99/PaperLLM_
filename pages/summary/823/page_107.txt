The references listed focus on the theme of sparsity in deep learning, highlighting various approaches to optimize neural networks for efficiency in training and inference. Key contributions include methods for model compression, filter pruning, and dynamic model adjustments. 

Zhuohan Li et al. propose a strategy to train large models initially and then compress them for efficiency in Transformers. Lucas Liebenwein and colleagues discuss provable filter pruning as a means to enhance neural network performance. Timothy P. Tao Lin et al. investigate dynamic model pruning with feedback mechanisms to improve efficiency.

Yujun Lin et al. focus on deep gradient compression to reduce communication bandwidth in distributed training. Zi Lin and colleagues address pruning redundant mappings in transformer models using spectral-normalized identity priors. The work by Pierre Lison et al. involves statistical rescoring for sentence alignments in noisy parallel corpora, while Baoyuan Liu and his team discuss sparse convolutional neural networks for enhanced performance in computer vision tasks. Lastly, Lanlan Liu and Jia Deng propose dynamic deep neural networks that optimize accuracy and efficiency through selective execution.

Overall, these works contribute to the understanding and implementation of sparsity in deep learning, emphasizing the balance between model size and computational efficiency.
