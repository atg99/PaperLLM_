The paper discusses the concept of sparsity in deep learning, focusing on various methods for pruning neural networks to achieve competitive performance while maintaining efficiency. It highlights that while some schemes yield competitive results, they often fall short in accuracy compared to fully-trained ResNets. A notable finding is that random initialization serves as a strong baseline, leading to the exploration of data-free initialization methods.

Data-free initialization, as explored by Lee et al. (2020), enhances signal propagation across layers, which is crucial for effective training. The SNIP method incorporates this idea by adjusting initial weights to create a layer-wise orthogonal sparse topology, potentially achieving accuracy comparable to dense networks. Theoretical backing for this approach is provided by Hayou et al. (2021), and it is noted that this method can be adapted for networks pruned in a data-dependent manner.

The paper also discusses the need for fine-tuning pruning ratios per layer and mentions the "smart-ratio" approach introduced by Su et al. (2020), which adjusts sparsity based on layer size. Additionally, Tanaka et al. (2020) propose strategies to combat layer collapse and demonstrate that iterative magnitude pruning can avoid this issue while achieving extreme sparsity.

However, limitations exist with one-shot pruning methods, as analyzed by Frankle et al. (2021) and Su et al. (2020), which indicate that a data-independent random sparsity structure with appropriate density ratios can outperform more complex methods. Ultimately, the paper suggests the integration of both ephemeral and model sparsity during training, as indicated by the findings of Raihan and Aamodt (2020), which highlight the challenges of sparsifying activations and gradients.
