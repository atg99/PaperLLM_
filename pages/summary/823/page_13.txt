The section on sparsity in deep learning emphasizes the critical role of generalization performance in model effectiveness. Generalization indicates how well a model can perform on unseen data from the same distribution as the training set. The paper discusses the concept of Occam's Hill, which illustrates that as models are sparsified, accuracy can initially improve by reducing noise, a phenomenon observed in both natural language processing and computer vision tasks. This is attributed to smaller models acting as stronger regularizers, allowing the learning algorithm to concentrate on essential features.

However, as sparsity increases, performance typically stabilizes before declining significantly with excessive sparsity. The early exploration of sparsification focused on enhancing generalization, but the field has shifted towards optimizing computational efficiency. The analysis of computational performance reveals that while low sparsity may lead to slow performance gains due to overheads in managing sparse structures, moderate to high sparsity generally results in improved performance. Yet, extreme sparsity can lead to diminishing returns as storage and control overheads become more significant. Overall, maximizing performance at a specific sparsity level is complex, as most sparsity exploitation techniques are efficient only within specific sparsity ranges or distributions of non-zero elements.
