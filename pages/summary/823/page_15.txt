In the context of deep learning, sparsity is a crucial consideration for optimizing model performance and storage efficiency. The paper discusses various storage schemes for sparse representations, highlighting that the overhead for bitmaps is minimal in dense scenarios, while no sparse scheme is advantageous below 10% sparsity. The bitmap index performs optimally between 10% and 70% sparsity, whereas the delta encoded scheme is preferred for sparsity levels exceeding 80%. The paper suggests that offset indices and dimension-aware methods may excel in high sparsity environments, though such high levels may not be typical for deep models, with the highest recorded sparsity being up to 99.9%.

The authors categorize sparsification into model (structural) and ephemeral types. Model sparsification, akin to neural architecture search, alters the model without changing the sparsity pattern across multiple passes. Key elements that can be sparsified include weights and neurons, with weight sparsification being particularly effective but potentially leading to unstructured models that require additional indexing. This unstructured nature can hinder computational efficiency on hardware designed for dense operations. Consequently, structured weight sparsification techniques have been developed to mitigate these issues by storing contiguous blocks of weights.

The paper also addresses the possibility of sparsifying components in specialized layers, such as filters in convolutional layers and heads in attention mechanisms, which can significantly reduce model parameters and enhance efficiency. The overview of sparsification techniques is further elaborated in subsequent sections of the paper.
