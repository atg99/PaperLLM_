This section discusses sparse training in deep learning, which involves training models that can dynamically add or remove elements. The fully-sparse training schedule begins with a sparse model and allows for changes during training. Research by Narasimha et al. demonstrates that this approach can outperform traditional growing or pruning methods for neuron-sparse training in simple multilayer perceptrons. Evci et al. achieved performance on ResNet-50 comparable to fully-dense training, albeit with more iterations. Sparse training can facilitate the training of high-dimensional models that would be too large for dense representations.

Initialization of sparse networks is critical, as standard dense initialization schemes do not hold true once sparsity is introduced. This issue can be addressed with techniques like batch normalization and skip connections. Additionally, specific initialization methods for sparse networks have been proposed by Evci et al. and Liu et al.

The paper distinguishes between static and dynamic sparsity. Dynamic sparsity involves the iterative pruning and regrowth of elements throughout training, while static sparsity involves a one-time pruning before training begins. Techniques such as SET and NeST exemplify dynamic sparsity, with NeST following a three-stage process that mimics brain development. RigL also updates the topology during training based on weight magnitudes and gradients.

Conversely, fixed sparsity involves predetermined non-zero element structures before training, which can be hand-tuned, determined in a pre-training phase, or randomly initialized. The section concludes with a note on the feasibility of constraining shallow neural networks to sparse small-world graphs without significant accuracy loss.
