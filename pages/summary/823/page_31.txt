The paper discusses the concept of sparsity in deep learning, focusing on techniques for neuron pruning based on their contributions to the output variance. It highlights the use of large Fourier amplitudes at specific frequencies to identify sensitive weights and remove neurons contributing less than 5% to total output variance. The methodology requires simulation runs that scale linearly with the number of weights. Han and Qiao (2013) extended this approach to prune neurons in hidden layers by analyzing output variance across input samples and using Fast Fourier Transforms (FFTs) to assess output changes.

A significant advantage of the FAST method is its reliance on upper and lower bounds of feature values, allowing for a rough approximation of input distribution without needing extensive data. Afghan and Naumann (2020) also utilize input interval sizes and maximum partial derivatives to establish neuron significance for pruning.

The paper further explores selection strategies based on neuron activity and correlation. It notes that neurons often activate together, aligning with Hebbian theory. Sparsification schemes can merge similar-output neurons, thereby adapting biases and rewiring the network. Sietsma and Dow (1988, 1991) identified and removed redundant neurons producing similar outputs, while Kameyama and Kosugi (1991) proposed fusing these neurons to minimize the impact on the network's performance.

Additionally, Suau et al. (2019) applied principal component analysis to determine the optimal number of filters in a layer, removing the most correlated neurons. An alternative approach is to strengthen connections between correlated neurons by dropping weights between weakly correlated ones, which Sun et al. (2015) found effective for refining fully trained networks and enhancing generalization.

Overall, the paper emphasizes that while sensitivity-based pruning methods focus on minimizing input-output behavior impact, they may not be beneficial for networks with low accuracy, suggesting a need to consider the training loss function in these pruning strategies.
