The collection of papers presented addresses the theme of sparsity in deep learning, focusing on techniques for optimizing neural networks through methods such as parameter pruning, dropout, and gradient compression. 

Srinivas and Babu's works from 2015 and 2016 explore data-free parameter pruning and the optimization of neural network architectures using backpropagation. Their studies emphasize reducing neural network complexity while maintaining performance. The 2016 paper on training sparse neural networks further contributes to this theme by discussing strategies for effective training in the presence of sparsity.

The concept of dropout, introduced by Srivastava et al. in 2014, provides a method to prevent overfitting in neural networks by randomly deactivating neurons during training, thus promoting a more robust model.

Stich et al. (2018) investigate sparsified stochastic gradient descent (SGD) with memory, proposing techniques to enhance efficiency in distributed training settings. Strom's contributions in both 1997 and 2015 discuss sparse connections and pruning in neural networks, alongside scalable training methods using GPU cloud computing.

Recent advancements include Su et al.’s 2020 work on pruning methods that validate the effectiveness of random tickets in sparse networks, and Suau et al.’s 2019 study on filter distillation for network compression, which aims to reduce model size while preserving accuracy.

Sun et al. (2019) address sparse gradient compression in distributed SGD, highlighting communication efficiency in training large models. Xu et al. (2017) introduce meProp, a method for accelerated deep learning that leverages sparsity to mitigate overfitting. Lastly, Sun et al. (2015) focus on sparsifying connections for enhanced performance in face recognition tasks.

Overall, these contributions highlight the importance of sparsity in improving the efficiency and effectiveness of deep learning models, addressing both theoretical and practical aspects of network optimization.
