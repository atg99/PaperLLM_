The collection of works cited emphasizes advancements in neural network optimization, particularly focusing on pruning techniques and efficient architectures. 

Hoefler et al. present fast algorithms for convolutional neural networks, enhancing computational efficiency. Le Cun et al. introduce the concept of optimal brain damage, which lays the groundwork for network pruning by identifying and removing less critical connections. Lebedev and Lempitsky further this idea with group-wise brain damage methods to improve ConvNet performance.

Lee et al. provide a signal propagation perspective for pruning networks at initialization, indicating that early-stage pruning can be beneficial. Their work on SNIP presents a single-shot pruning method based on connection sensitivity, optimizing network capacity while maintaining performance. 

Lepikhin et al. discuss GShard, which allows for scaling large models through conditional computation and automatic sharding, addressing the challenges of model size and resource allocation. Li et al. measure the intrinsic dimensionality of objective landscapes, offering insights into the optimization landscape of neural networks.

Additionally, works by Hao Li and colleagues focus on pruning filters for efficient ConvNets, while Jiajun Li et al. introduce SqueezeFlow, an accelerator that takes advantage of sparse convolution rules. Other notable contributions include the SAC model, which accelerates self-attention mechanisms, and research by Yunqiang Li that examines how pruning binary networks can enhance weight capacity. Lastly, Yuanzhi Li et al. explore the regularization effects of using a large initial learning rate in training.

Overall, these studies collectively advance the understanding and implementation of efficient neural network architectures and training methodologies.
