The section discusses magnitude pruning in deep learning, particularly when the Hessian is treated as the identity matrix, suggesting that pruning based on weight magnitude becomes equivalent to using saliency measures proportional to the square of each weight. This approach is widely adopted in practice, although the assumption of a uniform Hessian is strong and often unrealistic.

The OBD/OBS pruning method presents a mathematical framework but is constrained by several key assumptions and limitations. Firstly, it presumes pruning occurs on a well-trained model with a negligible loss gradient, which may not be valid in real scenarios. Research has indicated that pruning metrics and updates can be adapted for non-zero gradients.

Secondly, the method requires the Hessian to be invertible and constant in the pruning direction, necessitating gradual pruning or recalculation of the Hessian, which is detailed in subsequent sections. The method is designed for single weight removals, and applying it at scale assumes negligible correlations between weights, a condition that may not hold true, leading to potential inaccuracies in pruning decisions.

Additionally, the framework neglects the post-pruning fine-tuning process, which is critical for accuracy recovery, highlighting an area for future research. Finally, it references earlier work that operates under the assumption of a diagonal Hessian matrix, indicating the foundational nature of these assumptions in pruning methodologies.
