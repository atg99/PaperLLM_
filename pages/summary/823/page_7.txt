The paper discusses the concept of sparsity in deep learning, particularly in the context of training multilayer perceptrons (MLPs). It begins by explaining the process of a backward pass in neural network training, where the loss is propagated backward through layers to compute gradients and update parameters iteratively. This process aims to improve the function's accuracy on unseen examples, which is crucial for generalization.

The paper introduces notation and mathematical concepts necessary for understanding various pruning schemes, drawing on previous research by Molchanov et al. and Singh and Alistarh. It describes a typical MLP structure with input, hidden, and output layers, using ReLU activation functions. The forward pass is represented mathematically as a series of matrix-vector products, indicating how the network function is parameterized by weight matrices and bias vectors.

The discussion highlights the potential overparameterization in networks with many neurons, leading to inefficiencies in storage and computation. A visual comparison is made between a dense MLP and a sparsified version, showcasing how the removal of certain neurons and weights can streamline the model.

The paper also touches on the deterministic formulation of training, framing it as a minimization problem of a loss function across training data, which is essential for effective model training. This sets the groundwork for exploring various methods of inducing sparsity in neural networks to enhance their efficiency and performance.
