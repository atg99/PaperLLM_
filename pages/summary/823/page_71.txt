The paper discusses the concept of sparsity in deep learning, emphasizing its potential to enhance performance and interpretability in attention mechanisms. It references various methods such as entmax, sparsemax, and fusedmax that aim to induce sparsity and improve model efficiency.

Despite the advantages of sparse models, the paper highlights that they do not necessarily execute faster than dense models on current hardware. Studies indicate that small dense models can outperform sparse models in terms of speed, even when sparse models demonstrate higher accuracy. For instance, it was shown that 90% sparse workloads can execute slower on GPUs compared to dense computations of the same sparsity level. The paper notes that unstructured sparsity is poorly supported by existing architectures, while some structured sparsity approaches can leverage optimizations available in frameworks like cuDNN.

Training sparse models can be costly in terms of resources, as regularization and gating methods often increase the number of parameters significantly. Variational methods also tend to be resource-intensive during training. The authors advocate for a thorough evaluation of memory and computational overheads when assessing the performance of sparse methods.

The paper further distinguishes between the characteristics of sparsity in deep learning and traditional scientific computing, noting that deep learning sparsities are generally lower. It suggests that existing libraries optimized for high sparsity may not be directly applicable to deep learning. Therefore, the focus is on developing algorithmic and software solutions tailored to the sparsity levels typical in modern deep learning tasks.
