The paper discusses various approaches to filter and neuron pruning in convolutional neural networks (CNNs) aimed at improving efficiency while maintaining performance. Luo et al. (2017) frame filter pruning based on output sensitivity to subsequent layers, employing a greedy optimization strategy. Yu et al. (2018) introduce an importance metric focused on minimizing errors in the input to the final classification layers, thereby considering multi-layer information flow. Ding et al. (2019a) propose a method called "centripetal SGD" to align filter weights for effective pruning.

The concept of sensitivity is further explored, with Zeng and Yeung (2006) measuring neuron output sensitivity to input perturbations, and Engelbrecht and Cloete (1996) using a sensitivity matrix to evaluate the impact of earlier neurons on later ones. Tartaglione et al. (2018) adapt this by penalizing weight magnitudes based on output sensitivity during training.

Another pruning strategy involves contribution variance, as noted by Thimm and Fiesler (1995), where connections with low output variance across training examples can be pruned. Hagiwara (1993, 1994) suggests pruning based on "energy consumption," targeting low-activation neurons, while Hu et al. (2016) propose the "Average Percentage of Zeros" (APoZ) criterion for neurons with many zero activations, particularly effective for ReLU activations. DropNet (Tan and Motani, 2020) enhances this by using average activation magnitudes for pruning decisions.

Lastly, Lauret et al. (2006) introduce the "Fourier Amplitude Sensitivity Test" (FAST) to assess the relevance of weights based on their contribution to output variance, suggesting a spectral approach to understanding weight importance in model performance.
