In summary, the paper discusses various methods for compressing neural networks through soft weight sharing and Bayesian pruning. Ullrich et al. propose a soft weight sharing technique that clusters weights using a mixture of Gaussians, emphasizing sparsity. Louizos et al. introduce hierarchical priors for Bayesian pruning based on the minimum description length principle, promoting structured sparsity and quantization of weights. The application of Bayesian pruning extends to recurrent neural networks, with techniques from Lobacheva et al. and Kodryan et al. demonstrating effective sparsity induction. Additionally, Dai et al. present an information-theoretic approach to prune neurons by minimizing inter-layer mutual information, further contributing to the body of work on network compression. Overall, these methods highlight the potential of probabilistic frameworks in enhancing the efficiency of neural networks while preserving performance.
