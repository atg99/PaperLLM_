The paper discusses the concept of sparsity in deep learning, highlighting its significance for enhancing the efficiency and reducing the complexity of neural networks. Several key contributions in the field are referenced, showcasing various approaches to implement sparsity effectively.

Kundu et al. (2020) focus on predefined sparsity for low-complexity convolutional neural networks, while Kundu and Sundaresan (2021) introduce AttentionLite, an efficient self-attention model tailored for vision tasks. Kung et al. (2019) explore packing sparse convolutional neural networks for systolic array implementations, emphasizing column combining under joint optimization.

Kurtz et al. (2020) discuss the induction and exploitation of activation sparsity for fast inference, and Kusupati et al. (2020) present soft threshold weight reparameterization for learnable sparsity. Kuzmin et al. (2019) provide a taxonomy and evaluation of structured compression techniques for convolutional neural networks.

Furthermore, the work of Lample et al. (2019) on large memory layers with product keys and Larsson et al. (2017) on FractalNet, an ultra-deep network without residual connections, are also notable. Lastly, Lauret et al. (2006) introduce a node pruning algorithm based on Fourier amplitude sensitivity, contributing to the broader discourse on network optimization through sparsity.

These references collectively underline the ongoing research efforts to leverage sparsity for improving the performance and efficiency of deep learning models across various applications.
