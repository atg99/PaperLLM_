The paper discusses the concept of sparsity in deep learning and its impact on model accuracy. It emphasizes the importance of tuning sparsity across different layers for effective model sparsification. An extensive literature overview from 1988 to 2020 is presented, categorizing various approaches based on three criteria: the candidate elements for removal, the methods used for selection, and the focus on optimizing inference versus improving training.

The analysis reveals that the majority of studies concentrate on weight sparsification, followed by neuron sparsification, with structured schemes and inputs being less common. Among weight sparsification methods, magnitude pruning is the most prevalent, followed by first and second-order techniques. The literature also indicates that over 60% of the papers focus on inference, with an increasing trend towards training improvements.

The findings show that regularization is a dominant technique for inference pruning, while training primarily utilizes magnitude pruning. Additionally, the combination of magnitude pruning and regularization is frequently employed, highlighting their complementary benefits. The paper concludes with a statistical summary of the different methods and their applications in the context of deep learning sparsity.
