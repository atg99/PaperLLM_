The paper by T. Hoefler et al. discusses advancements in network design through automated methods, particularly in the context of convolutional layers and transformers. It contrasts traditional hand-crafted biases with automated approaches that leverage the learning process to optimize network architecture.

The authors highlight stochastic gradient descent (SGD) as the standard algorithm for training deep neural networks, noting its sensitivity to parameters and network structure. They reference studies indicating that training larger models tends to be more computationally efficient than training smaller ones, attributing this to the iterative nature of optimization processes that can navigate local minima in high-dimensional loss landscapes.

A key observation is that sparsifying a model can restrict SGD's effectiveness, as it operates in a reduced-dimensional space, potentially leading to convergence at suboptimal minima. This limitation stems from SGD's linear first-order descent characteristics. The authors propose that integrating iterative pruning with SGD creates a guided nonlinear search process. Each pruning step perturbs the function in a directed manner, allowing the model to escape local minima that SGD alone might not surpass. This approach, when well-tuned, approximates an efficient learning algorithm, enhancing the quality of pruned models significantly.
