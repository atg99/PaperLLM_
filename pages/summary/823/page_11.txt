The paper addresses the challenges of optimizing variational lower bounds in deep learning, particularly for complex models where direct computation is intractable. It highlights the use of sampling techniques and reparametrization tricks to derive unbiased, differentiable Monte-Carlo estimators for expected log-likelihood terms in large-scale models.

A key concept introduced is Variational Dropout, which applies to a fully-connected neural network layer. In this context, dropout is a regularization method that incorporates multiplicative noise into the input, with the noise drawn from a specified distribution. The paper discusses the effectiveness of Gaussian dropout, which interprets the weights of the network as random variables rather than fixed parameters, allowing for a Bayesian perspective on weight sampling.

The authors emphasize that Variational Dropout allows for the optimization of dropout rates as variational parameters, rather than treating them as static hyperparameters. This enables the training of distinct dropout rates for individual layers, neurons, or weights, enhancing the model's ability to sparsify deep neural networks. The foundational work by Kingma et al. (2015) is acknowledged, with Molchanov et al. (2017) credited for further exploring the implications of training individual dropout rates.
