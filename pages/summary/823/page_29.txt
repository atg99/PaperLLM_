The paper discusses the concept of sparsity in deep learning, highlighting methods that do not rely on specific examples and can serve as pre- or post-processing steps for data-driven techniques. A basic approach involves comparing weights across neurons in a fully connected layer, allowing for the merging of similar neurons, which has been shown to be effective in smaller networks but less so in larger ones. Coreset pruning presents a way to balance sparsity and approximation error, achieving improved accuracy even at high sparsity levels.

While data-free methods like magnitude pruning are effective, they often require extensive retraining to maintain accuracy. To enhance precision, the paper suggests incorporating the influence of training data into the pruning process, leading to data-driven pruning schemes. These methods assess the sensitivity of neuron outputs to training data changes, allowing for the removal of low-relevance elements without significantly impacting network performance.

The first proposed scheme eliminates neurons with minimal output variation across inputs, adjusting the biases of remaining neurons accordingly. Further developments include generalizing this method to optimize weight changes post-neuron removal through solving linear equations, minimizing output changes using least-squares metrics. This approach accommodates networks with skip layers and does not necessitate hyperparameter tuning. Additionally, similar strategies have been explored for pruning input features based on their linear combinations with other neurons’ outputs, indicating a robust framework for achieving sparsity while preserving model accuracy.
