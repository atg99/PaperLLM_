This paper discusses the effects of pruning neural networks on their accuracy and generalization, particularly in the context of various architectures and strategies. It presents data illustrating the relative accuracy drop across different networks like ResNet-50, AlexNet, and EfficientNet-B0 when subjected to varying levels of sparsity. The findings indicate that neural networks can be pruned significantly without a notable loss in overall accuracy. However, this raises concerns about whether top-level accuracy is sufficient to evaluate the impact of pruning, considering the substantial changes to the network's representation.

Research by Hooker et al. reveals that while iterative magnitude pruning can degrade accuracy for specific underrepresented classes, quantization tends to have a lesser effect. Furthermore, pruned models exhibit increased brittleness under distribution shifts, leading to higher error rates on certain classes, particularly those with limited training data. The study highlights that this brittleness and the introduction of biases can exacerbate existing algorithmic biases, as seen in the CelebA dataset, where pruning disproportionately affected underrepresented demographic groups.

The paper concludes that the biases and vulnerabilities introduced by pruning may restrict the applicability of pruned models in sensitive areas like facial recognition and healthcare, where fairness is crucial. It emphasizes the need for a deeper understanding of the nuanced impacts of pruning methods and calls for the development of more robust pruning techniques to address these issues.
