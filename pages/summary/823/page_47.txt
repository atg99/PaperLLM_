The paper discusses the implications of sparsity in deep learning, focusing on the Sparse Variational Dropout (Sparse VD) technique. Sparse VD achieves high sparsity in models but exhibits substantial variance in final accuracy and average model sparsity. A key unresolved question is whether the variances of pruned weights differ from those of unpruned weights. Following the intuition of Molchanov et al. (2017), the paper notes that for weights with large values, it is beneficial for their variances to be close to zero to minimize noise that could degrade model quality.

Experiments reproduced on the CIFAR-10 dataset revealed that the log-variances of weights remained near the small initial value chosen to prevent divergence during training. Similar results were observed with ResNet-50 on ImageNet, where weights showed minimal variance change throughout training, leading to behaviors akin to global magnitude pruning. In contrast, Transformer networks exhibited different variance behaviors, suggesting that large weights had smaller log-variances compared to pruned weights, which adjusted significantly during training.

The paper highlights that while Sparse VD allows for immediate pruning of a significant portion of weights post-training with minimal accuracy loss, traditional magnitude pruning methods necessitate fine-tuning to recover from performance drops. This suggests Sparse VD provides a robust regularization effect not always reflected in the final weight variances.

The discussion transitions to structured Bayesian pruning, noting that while Sparse VD results in unstructured sparsity, structured sparsity is preferred for inference acceleration. Referencing Neklyudov et al. (2017), the authors propose using a truncated log-normal distribution for variational parameters to achieve structured sparsity. This method allows for the non-negative propagation of noise and offers a closed-form solution for the KL divergence in the variational lower-bound. Neurons are thresholded based on their signal-to-noise ratio (SNR), with low SNR neurons set to zero. The authors demonstrate acceleration with this method on smaller datasets like MNIST and CIFAR-10, reinforcing the potential benefits of structured sparsity in deep learning models.
