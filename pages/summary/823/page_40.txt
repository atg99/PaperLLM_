This paper by T. Hoefler et al. discusses methods for pruning residual networks, validated experimentally on CIFAR-10 and Tiny-ImageNet datasets. The authors reference concurrent work by Zeng and Urtasun, which employs a K-FAC-based approximation of the Hessian for unstructured pruning, introducing a technique called MLPrune. This method offers a global saliency metric, allowing users to specify a global average sparsity percentage, which the technique then distributes across layers based on their pruning sensitivity.

The paper also explores regularization techniques for model sparsification, where penalty terms are added to the loss function to guide the optimization process towards sparse weights. Various penalty functions can be defined, impacting model complexity and generalization. The choice of penalty term is critical, as it can complicate the optimization landscape, potentially introducing local minima and requiring careful tuning.

One foundational penalty term discussed is weight decay, which enhances generalization by reducing weight magnitudes during updates. The paper mentions that weight decay can be combined with magnitude pruning for improved sparsification.

Additionally, the L0 norm is highlighted as a straightforward penalty for generating sparse weights by counting non-zero elements. However, optimizing the L0 norm directly is challenging due to its discrete nature, leading to NP-completeness. To address this, Louizos et al. proposed approximating the L0 norm with differentiable non-negative stochastic gating variables, facilitating weight optimization.
