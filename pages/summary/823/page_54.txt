The paper by T. Hoefler et al. discusses various approaches to enhancing sparsity in neural networks, drawing inspiration from biological brains. It begins by introducing top-(k+d) gradients, which incorporate additional gradients as a "halo zone" around the most significant ones. The authors highlight the potential benefits of locality-based and greedy regrowth strategies, emphasizing that biological brains exhibit hierarchical sparsity. These strategies include the exponential decay of weight addition probability based on neuron distance and greedy schemes that selectively add beneficial neurons after removing all.

The paper also explores ephemeral sparsification, which distinguishes between model sparsity and activity sparsity in biological systems. It notes that only a small percentage of neurons are active at any given moment, crucial for maintaining the brain's energy efficiency. The authors propose that deep neural networks can mirror this behavior through activation functions like ReLU, which selectively inhibit signals and enable a form of ephemeral sparsity during both inference and training.

The discussion on inference sparsification addresses how neural activations can be zeroed out during inference and the forward pass of training. The paper then examines dropout techniques that promote generalization by sparsifying networks during training. It also highlights gradient sparsification to alleviate communication overhead in distributed training and considers less common methods for sparsifying back-propagated errors and optimizer states. Overall, the paper provides a comprehensive overview of sparsification techniques aimed at improving efficiency in deep learning models.
