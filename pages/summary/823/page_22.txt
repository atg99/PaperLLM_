The paper discusses the SWAT method, which employs a simple top-k selection strategy to eliminate small weights during the forward pass and both small weights and activations during the backward pass. It also explores the application of sparsity in transfer learning and fine-tuning, where large pre-trained networks are adapted for more specific tasks. This process presents an opportunity for pruning, where parameters can be reduced in number during fine-tuning, as evidenced by the works of Molchanov et al. and Mehta.

The pruning schedule during fine-tuning typically follows a train and prune approach, with the key difference being the use of a distinct dataset that often reflects a narrower focus or a shift in distribution compared to the original training data. This allows the pre-trained network to serve as an effective initialization for faster learning. Various pruning methods are highlighted, including first-order gradient-based pruning and magnitude-based pruning, which have been shown to achieve significant sparsity in models like BERT during fine-tuning.

Research indicates that task-specific fine-tuning can lead to 40-90% sparsity in final weights without compromising performance. Additionally, the DI pruning method allows for task-specific parameters to be defined as differences from pre-trained weights, resulting in high levels of sparsity while maintaining the density of pre-trained parameters. This approach supports multiple tasks with minimal inference overhead.

Moreover, the paper references findings from Manessi et al., which demonstrate that moderately sparse models can effectively transfer knowledge to other tasks with little to no accuracy loss. Lastly, it outlines a general framework for sparse deep learning schedules, detailing a series of steps that can be adapted or iterated upon for training pruned networks.
