The paper discusses the impact of parameter budgets on sparsity across different layers in neural networks. Various hyperparameters define sparsity, either based on element values or a target sparsity level. The choice between global sparsity, where a single sparsity level is applied across the entire model, and layer-specific sparsity is examined. Previous studies have shown that global selection may be effective for certain architectures, but different layer types, such as convolutional versus densely connected layers, require distinct treatments. Additionally, the same layer type might necessitate varying sparsity levels depending on its position within the network.

Recent approaches aim to simplify hyperparameter tuning by automatically determining appropriate parameter budgets for each layer. Linking sparsity to layer characteristics, such as the weight-to-neuron ratio, has been proposed. Moreover, some methods adjust parameter allocation dynamically during training based on saliency metrics, prioritizing layers with larger loss gradients for re-adding pruned parameters.

The paper highlights that operational requirements can significantly differ even with the same overall sparsity due to how parameters are distributed across layers. The distribution of sparsity in a ResNet-50 model is illustrated, showing that many global schemes tend to allocate more parameters to the earlier layers. This leads to a general observation that practitioners often avoid pruning the initial layers due to their critical role in network performance.
