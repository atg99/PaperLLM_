The paper by T. Hoefler and colleagues discusses the concept of parameter efficiency in deep neural networks (DNNs), particularly in relation to sparsity and model quality. Parameter efficiency is defined as the contribution of the average parameter to the overall model quality, taking into account the complexity of the learning task and the dataset. The authors note that pruning DNNs can increase parameter efficiency while potentially decreasing overall model quality.

They review previous metrics for parameter efficiency, such as accuracy density, which is calculated by dividing validation accuracy by the number of parameters. While this metric shows advantages for certain models like MobileNet over ResNet-50, it can lead to misleading rankings, favoring sparse but less accurate models over denser ones.

To address these limitations, the authors propose a modified measure called SotA-normalized parameter efficiency. This metric adjusts accuracy based on the complexity of the dataset by leveraging historical state-of-the-art accuracy data. They estimate the difficulty of achieving a certain level of accuracy and use this to normalize correct predictions against the number of parameters.

The findings indicate that dense networks like ResNets and MobileNets exhibit increased parameter efficiency compared to older models like AlexNet and VGG. However, VGG variants demonstrate better parameter efficiency than AlexNet despite being larger. Interestingly, Vision Transformers, despite their state-of-the-art status, show low SotA-normalized parameter efficiency due to their high parameter count and moderate accuracy improvements.

For sparse networks, pruned models generally exhibit higher parameter efficiency than their best dense counterparts, with a pruned ResNet being the top performer. Overall, the paper highlights the need for more nuanced metrics to accurately assess parameter efficiency in the context of model sparsity and accuracy.
