The paper discusses various methods and theoretical analyses related to sparsity in deep learning, particularly focusing on the convergence of sparsified gradient methods. Wangni et al. (2018) introduce rand-k sparsification, which retains a random selection of k gradients based on their absolute values while zeroing the rest and rescaling to maintain unbiasedness. They propose algorithms for optimal sparsification strategies within a variance budget, akin to top-k sparsification.

Wang et al. (2018) further address minimizing variance under a sparsity constraint and explore sparsifying arbitrary atomic decompositions. Concurrently, Tsuzuku et al. (2018) identify variance as a key metric for sparsification, utilizing the variance of gradients in a mini-batch as a criterion, which proves theoretically beneficial and effective in practice against existing methods like threshold sparsification.

Additional methods for gradient sparsification include Ivkin et al. (2019) using count sketches for gradient approximation, Lim et al. (2019) combining sparsification with ternary quantization, and Basu et al. (2020) analyzing the convergence of sparsity, quantization, and local updates, demonstrating consistent rates with SGD in certain contexts. Wang et al. (2020b) apply top-k sparsification in the frequency domain after FFT on gradients.

The convergence of sparsified gradient methods has been theoretically analyzed by several researchers, including Stich et al. (2018) and Alistarh et al. (2018), who showcase that these methods converge comparably to standard SGD when error feedback is employed. Their findings vary in assumptions and guarantees, with Stich et al. focusing on convex objectives and error correction, while Alistarh et al. address non-convex objectives in a multi-node scenario. Karimireddy et al. (2019) and Dutta et al. (2020) extend these results, highlighting the advantages of layer-wise compression, though practical exceptions exist where full model sparsification may outperform layer-wise methods. Tang et al. (2019) provide an additional convergence analysis for scenarios involving individual gradient sparsification by workers.
