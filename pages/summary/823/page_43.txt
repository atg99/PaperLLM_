The paper discusses sparsity in deep learning and critiques traditional L1 norm regularization for its limitations, such as uniform parameter shrinkage and invariance to parameter scaling. To address these issues, Yang et al. (2020b) propose the squared Hoyer regularizer, which offers a scale-invariant measure that differentiates between L1 and L2 norms. This regularizer can also be used for structured pruning.

Additionally, the paper introduces a shrinkage operator that outperforms simple thresholding after L1 regularization. This operator zeros out weights that may change sign and involves a weight threshold for effective parameter adjustment.

The concept of layer-wise regularization is also explored, where Aghasi et al. (2017) apply L1 regularization to individual layers while maintaining the layer outputs close to the original outputs. They utilize a "cut-off" method to create a convex relaxation for optimization, particularly for ReLU activations.

Finally, the group lasso is presented as a generalization of the lasso operator, designed for scenarios where variables are grouped. It ensures that all members of a group are either non-zero or zero together, enhancing the sparsity strategy in deep learning models.
