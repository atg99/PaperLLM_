The paper discusses various methods for pruning neural networks, particularly focusing on variational selection schemes that utilize Bayesian approaches and the minimum description length principle. One key method highlighted is Sparse Variational Dropout (Sparse VD), which is based on the idea of using variational inference to define the relevance of neural network weights based on their variance during training. Sparse VD allows for the optimization of individual dropout rates for each weight, enabling a more tailored pruning process.

The authors reference the work of Molchanov et al., who introduced a method to approximate posterior probability distributions for weights and optimize variational parameters through stochastic variational inference. The paper also notes that large values of the dropout rate correspond to weights that can be effectively set to zero during testing, thus improving model performance.

While Sparse VD offers advantages such as eliminating the need for additional hyperparameter tuning and allowing for one-shot weight removal post-training, it also presents challenges, including an increase in the number of parameters and difficulties in training from scratch. The authors recommend either starting with a pretrained model or using a "warm-up" strategy to gradually incorporate regularization during training. Although initial results were reported on smaller datasets, subsequent work has demonstrated the applicability of Sparse VD to larger models, including those trained on ImageNet.
