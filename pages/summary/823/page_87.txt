The section on best practices for sparsity in deep learning outlines several recommendations drawn from existing literature. It begins by noting that simple pruning methods can achieve moderate sparsity levels of 50 to 90 percent while maintaining or even improving accuracy. However, reaching higher sparsity levels, exceeding 95 percent, requires more sophisticated techniques, as traditional gradient-based optimization may reach its limits.

The best practices are categorized into five key areas:

1. **Pruning Strategy**: To achieve the highest sparsity, a combination of regularization methods and iterative pruning with growth schedules is recommended. While these methods can significantly increase training costs, magnitude pruning is highlighted as a simpler and more controllable approach for managing target sparsity and accuracy.

2. **Retraining/Fine-tuning**: For sparsity aimed at improving inference, retraining and fine-tuning are crucial. Gradual pruning schedules tend to perform best, with each iteration starting from the most recently trained weights.

3. **Structure**: Structured pruning is identified as an effective approach that balances accuracy and performance, particularly in architectures optimized for dense computations. This method can leverage powerful mechanisms like locally connected layers and weight sharing.

4. **Distribution**: Careful consideration of sparsity distribution across layers is essential, based on memory size and computational intensity. It is often beneficial to fine-tune sparsity levels for different operators, recognizing that dense layers can generally tolerate more pruning than convolutional layers.

5. **Combined Ephemeral and Model Sparsity**: A successful sparse deep neural network should integrate both ephemeral and model sparsity. Techniques like dropout can act as a pre-regularizer, enhancing generalization when sufficient data is available, and combining these forms of sparsity can reduce the computational burden.

These recommendations aim to guide practitioners in effectively implementing pruning techniques to enhance the performance of deep learning models.
