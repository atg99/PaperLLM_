The paper discusses various methods for gradient sparsification in deep neural network training to reduce communication bandwidth without sacrificing accuracy. It highlights the approach of accumulating compression errors locally and integrating the residuals into subsequent iterations, as demonstrated by Strom in 2015, achieving significant bandwidth reduction.

The challenges of selecting absolute cut-off magnitudes for gradient sparsification are addressed, as different networks may have varying gradient magnitudes. Dryden et al. (2016) introduced top-k selection, retaining a fixed proportion of positive and negative gradients through efficient sampling. They also employed quantization and entropy-based compression techniques.

Subsequent advancements included Aji and Heafield's (2017) global selection of a single proportion for all gradients, while Sun et al. (2017) and Chen et al. (2017) explored further refinements in sparsification techniques, including binning and self-adjusting thresholds. Lin et al. (2018) introduced multiple enhancements to convergence in top-k sparsification, achieving up to 99.9% sparsity, though with sensitivity to hyper-parameters. 

The paper contrasts gradient sparsification with weight or activation pruning, noting that gradients are more conducive to achieving higher sparsity without accuracy loss, exemplified by Lin et al.'s results on ResNet-50.

Furthermore, the impact of variance on stochastic gradient descent (SGD) convergence is discussed, with Alistarh et al. (2017) noting that stochastic quantization can lead to high variance in sparse gradients, potentially hindering convergence. Overall, the paper illustrates the evolution of gradient sparsification techniques and their implications for efficient deep learning training.
