The paper discusses the concept of sparsity in deep learning, specifically focusing on sparse optimizer states and their potential benefits for memory-efficient training algorithms. It introduces dynamic networks utilizing conditional computation, where previous layer outputs dictate the computational path, enhancing model capacity without increasing computational costs. This method allows for structured activation sparsity, exemplified by approaches like PackNet and Supermasks, which enable the encoding of multiple tasks in a single dense network while preventing catastrophic forgetting through task-specific sparse subnetworks.

Additionally, the paper highlights mixture of experts (MoE) models that have shown success in natural language processing. The MoE layer consists of several expert subnetworks and a gating network that outputs a sparse vector to select relevant experts. Techniques such as noisy top-k gating improve load balancing among experts. The implementation of these concepts in transformer networks has facilitated the training of massive models, achieving significant speed improvements during pretraining.

The paper also addresses the necessity for efficient decision-making during inference in conditional computation. Methods like Runtime Neural Pruning utilize reinforcement learning to determine which network channels to activate based on input, while other approaches employ gating modules or routing techniques. The discussion of dynamic memory networks, while significant, is noted as being outside the primary focus of the paper.
