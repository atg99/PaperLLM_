The paper discusses the concept of sparsity in deep learning, particularly focusing on various sparse matrix storage formats and their practical implementations. It highlights different structures, such as unstructured, blocked, and strided formats, emphasizing their efficiency in representing non-zero elements. The strided format, for instance, fixes every fifth element as non-zero, offering a compact yet constrained representation.

The practical application of tuned block sparsity is exemplified through the work of Elsen et al. (2020), who achieved significant speedups for inference on mobile devices by optimizing convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for sparsity levels between 70% and 95%. Their approach emphasizes optimizing cache usage and data movement, which are critical bottlenecks in deep learning systems. The study reveals that block shape is less important than block size, with larger models being less affected by block size variations.

Further advancements include Scalpel (Yu et al., 2017), which combines weight-blocking and neuron pruning tailored for SIMD platforms, achieving notable reductions in required sparsity for speedup on different architectures. Additionally, Han et al. (2017) introduced block-balanced pruning, ensuring uniform sparsity across blocks to facilitate parallel processing without load imbalances, while Dey et al. (2019) and Kundu et al. (2020) explored fixed neuron degrees and periodicity in convolutional filters, respectively.

Lastly, PruneTrain (Lym et al., 2019) focuses on accelerating training processes through group-lasso regularization and pruning during training, highlighting the potential for memory optimization. Overall, the paper underscores the importance of various sparse structures and their optimizations in enhancing the performance of deep learning systems.
