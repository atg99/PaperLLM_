The paper discusses advancements in sparse neural network (SparseNN) architectures and their optimization for various hardware implementations. It highlights how output masks can prevent unnecessary computations of zero elements, with SparseNN improving this through backpropagation and a straight-through estimator for the sign function.

The concept of fixed block sparsity is introduced, which allows for reduced overhead in managing indices and control logic, making it suitable for optimizing software across different hardware types. The Cambricon-S accelerator is noted for its explicit support of block sparsity, enabling the skipping of both zero neurons and weight blocks. Observations indicate that large weights tend to cluster in specific dimensions in fully-connected and convolutional layers, leading to the definition of 2D and 4D block-sparse weight formats. The authors report improved performance and energy efficiency with smaller permissible block sizes for ResNets compared to larger networks.

Furthermore, the paper examines the challenge of utilizing systolic arrays efficiently for sparse computations. It references Kung et al.'s method of packing sparse filters into a dense structure to leverage systolic arrays, which involves selecting columns with minimal overlap in their non-zero elements, resulting in a more efficient representation. This approach, alongside moderate retraining, is noted to be effective for small networks. The SqueezeFlow technique is also mentioned for compressing sparse filters by decomposing convolutions into effective and ineffective parts.

The section on training accelerators emphasizes that while inference accelerators can be used for the forward pass in training, they introduce complexities such as the need to store activations and access specialized formats during the backward pass. To address these challenges, specific accelerators like Procrustes are designed for sparse training, particularly in conjunction with the Dropback pruning algorithm. Procrustes is noted for exploiting structural weight sparsity while managing the issues posed by batch normalization layers that tend to diminish sparsity in gradients.
