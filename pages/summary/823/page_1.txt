The paper "Sparsity in Deep Learning: Pruning and Growth for Efficient Inference and Training in Neural Networks" reviews the growing significance of sparsity in deep learning due to the increasing energy and performance costs associated with neural networks. The authors argue that sparse networks can generalize as well as, or even better than, their dense counterparts, making them suitable for applications on mobile devices and for reducing training times.

The paper provides a comprehensive survey of existing research on sparsity, detailing various methods for pruning and adding components within neural networks. It covers training strategies for achieving model sparsity and practical mechanisms to exploit sparsity. Drawing insights from over 300 research papers, the authors aim to guide both practitioners and researchers in effectively utilizing and advancing sparsity in deep learning.

Key topics discussed include mathematical methods related to sparsification, phenomena like early structure adaptation, and the relationship between sparsity and the training process. The authors also introduce a metric for pruned parameter efficiency to facilitate comparisons among different sparse networks. Finally, the paper speculates on the potential impacts of sparsity on future workloads and identifies significant open problems in the field, emphasizing the importance of developing efficient deep learning models.
