The paper discusses the challenges and methodologies associated with achieving sparsity in deep learning models. It highlights that certain methods, such as those proposed by Srinivas et al., may struggle due to the stochastic nature of parameter selection, which can introduce noise and lead to discrepancies between training and inference performance. Verdenius et al. note the difficulty of tuning hyperparameters in L0-based schemes, which limits their applicability to different networks.

The paper also addresses the complexity of estimating discrete functions, particularly the selection of non-differentiable binary gating variables. One common approach is the use of Straight-through Estimators, which treat the derivative of binary functions as if they were identity functions during backpropagation. While this method is utilized in several works, some researchers argue it can be unstable, suggesting alternatives like variants of ReLU.

Another approach involves creating continuous approximations to discrete functions, such as using the sigmoid function to approximate the Heaviside step function, with a temperature term to control smoothness. This method allows for continuous sparsification during training, balancing the trade-off between approximation quality and smoothness. The paper illustrates these concepts with figures showing various approximations for non-differentiable step functions.
