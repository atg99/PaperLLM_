The paper discusses various strategies for enhancing the training of neural networks through structured sparsity and optimization techniques. It highlights a three-step training scheme introduced by Han et al. that includes dense training, magnitude pruning, and retraining, which collectively improve generalization performance. Similarly, Carreira-Perpinan and Idelbayev propose a method that uses a learned mask to selectively enable weights, allowing for increased sparsity and better weight selection than traditional magnitude pruning.

The authors draw parallels between deep neural network training dynamics and biological processes, suggesting that the importance of network elements is established early in the training phase. Two distinct phases of training are identified: a drift phase for minimizing empirical risk and a diffusion phase for refining internal representations. Empirical evidence supports this phased approach, with studies showing that critical connections are identified early in training.

You et al. and Golub et al. build on the concept of early structure adaptation, advocating for the freezing of sparsity patterns after initial training iterations. Their findings emphasize the effectiveness of using large learning rates early on to help the model capture simpler patterns, which can later be refined. This approach is linked to learning rate schedules that adapt as sparsity increases.

Furthermore, the paper notes that sparsity can emerge organically from the optimization process, particularly when using adaptive optimizers like Adam in conjunction with regularization techniques. This phenomenon, however, is not observed with standard SGD or momentum methods. Overall, the paper underscores the importance of structured sparsity and adaptive training techniques in improving neural network performance.
