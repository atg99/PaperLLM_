The paper discusses the concept of sparsity in deep learning, focusing on different approaches to implement model sparsification effectively. Two primary methods are highlighted: the train-then-sparsify approach and the sparsify-during-training approach.

The train-then-sparsify method begins with a dense model, allowing for the reuse of existing hyperparameters and learning schedules. However, it can lead to reduced final accuracy if pruning occurs before convergence, as noted by Engelbrecht and Cloete.

In contrast, the sparsify-during-training method initiates the sparsification process before the model reaches convergence. This approach is generally more cost-effective than the previous method but may lead to less efficient convergence and requires careful hyperparameter tuning. Early studies suggest that a fixed pruning schedule could negatively impact the network's generalization capabilities. Prechelt's observations on weight distribution during training emphasize the importance of dynamically adjusting the pruning rate based on generalization loss.

Additional techniques, such as using binary masks to manage pruned weights and the Iterative Hard Thresholding (IHT) method, are discussed. IHT combines dense and sparse training iterations to optimize model performance. The paper underscores the significance of the sparsification schedule in achieving effective model performance while maintaining generalization ability.
