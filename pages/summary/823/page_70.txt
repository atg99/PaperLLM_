The paper discusses advancements in model pruning and sparse attention mechanisms in neural networks, particularly in the context of the Lottery Ticket Hypothesis and transfer learning. Hoefler et al. demonstrate that magnitude pruning can achieve up to 70% sparsity in pretrained BERT models without performance loss on the pretraining objective, although such networks may not transfer effectively to downstream tasks. In contrast, Guo et al. highlight the challenges of using L1 or L2 regularization during training, advocating for Reweighted Proximal Pruning, which employs reweighted L1 minimization and proximal algorithms instead of traditional backpropagation.

Sanh et al. propose movement pruning, which identifies and removes parameters that decrease during fine-tuning, achieving higher performance at extreme sparsity levels (up to 97%) compared to traditional methods. The paper also addresses the limitations of standard attention mechanisms in transformers, which require significant time and memory for computing alignment matrices. Recent research has focused on efficient transformers, with Yun et al. providing theoretical backing for sparse attention mechanisms that maintain essential connectivity.

Various approaches to achieve sparse attention include local neighborhood restrictions, star topologies, and hybrid sparsity patterns. Techniques such as sliding windows and task-specific sparsity structures further enhance performance. Additionally, innovations in SoftMax computation allow for ranking preservation while inducing sparsity, exemplified by Zhao et al.'s application of top-k sparsification to attention weights. Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of sparsity in improving efficiency and performance in neural network architectures.
