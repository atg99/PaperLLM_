The referenced papers address various aspects of sparsity in deep learning, focusing on techniques for optimizing neural networks. Wei Wen et al. (2016) explore structured sparsity in deep neural networks, providing methods to enhance model efficiency. David White and Panos A. Ligomenides (1993) introduce a genetic algorithm, GANNet, for optimizing neural network topology and weights. Whitley and Bogart (1990) examine the evolution of connectivity through genetic algorithms to prune neural networks.

Williams et al. (2018) present a challenge corpus aimed at improving sentence understanding via inference, which can be relevant for training sparse models. Peter M. Williams (1995) discusses Bayesian regularization and pruning with a Laplace prior, contributing to the understanding of model complexity.

Recent works by Wortsman et al. (2019, 2020) investigate neural wirings and the concept of supermasks in superposition, shedding light on how sparsity can be effectively harnessed in neural architectures. Wu et al. (2017) propose a scalable trust-region method for deep reinforcement learning, enhancing performance while maintaining sparsity.

Xiao et al. (2019) introduce AutoPrune, an automatic network pruning method that regularizes auxiliary parameters, while Xu and Ho (2006) present a training and pruning algorithm based on node dependence and Jacobian rank deficiency. Lastly, Yaguchi et al. (2018) highlight how the Adam optimizer induces implicit weight sparsity in rectifier neural networks, and Yang et al. (2020) propose Procrustes, a data flow and accelerator designed for sparse deep neural network training.

Together, these studies contribute to the growing body of knowledge on achieving efficient and effective deep learning models through sparsity techniques.
