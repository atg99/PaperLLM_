The paper by T. Hoefler et al. outlines ten significant challenges and open questions in the field of sparsity in deep learning to guide future research. The challenges include:

1. Sparse Training: Investigating methods to train large models using sparsity without compromising accuracy.
2. Structured vs. Unstructured: Analyzing how structural biases affect the trade-off between accuracy and model size.
3. Hardware Co-design: Exploring the co-design of hardware architectures alongside pruned models, focusing on cost, accuracy, and structured sparsity.
4. Multi-objective Pruning: Identifying effective strategies for simultaneous pruning aimed at multiple objectives, such as minimizing energy consumption within a specific memory size.
5. Architecture Design: Evaluating whether neural architecture search (NAS) should be used to find efficient networks or if pruning can serve as a replacement or complement.
6. Theory of Sparse Learning: Understanding the interplay between sparsity, learning dynamics, and generalization.
7. Sparse Representations: Assessing the representational power of sparse neural networks and establishing a rigorous definition for parameter efficiency.
8. Method Generalization: Determining which pruning methods applicable to MLPs or CNNs can be generalized to transformers or other architectures.
9. Data-free Sparsity: Developing one-shot or data-free methods that match the accuracy of data-dependent methods.
10. Fairness, Bias, and Robustness: Creating robust sparse models and sparsiﬁcation techniques while safeguarding against adversarial attacks.

The authors note that their focus is primarily on engineering aspects of sparsity, rather than directly addressing brain-related research challenges. In the conclusions, they highlight that sparsity can lead to significant efficiency improvements, with larger networks offering greater pruning opportunities. They emphasize the potential for training large models with sparse methods and advocate for hardware-software co-design to leverage these efficiencies in next-generation deep learning architectures.
