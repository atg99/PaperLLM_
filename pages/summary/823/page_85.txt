The paper discusses the concept of sparsity in deep learning, particularly focusing on the parameter efficiency of state-of-the-art deep neural networks (DNNs) applied to the ImageNet dataset. It highlights a trend over the years from 2012 to 2020, showcasing how various networks have achieved significant validation accuracy with relatively few parameters. For instance, a network developed by Savarese et al. in 2020 reached 66% validation accuracy with only 281,000 parameters, while a pruned MobileNet (v1) achieved 68% accuracy with 423,000 parameters.

The paper introduces the concept of "parameter slack," which measures the level of sparsity achievable with a fixed decrease in accuracy relative to a dense baseline. It notes that architectures like AlexNet and VGG-16 exhibit higher parameter slack compared to more recent models like MobileNet or Inception. This suggests that older architectures can tolerate more parameter reduction without significantly impacting accuracy.

Additionally, the paper discusses the impact of weight data types on model information content and parameter efficiency, particularly in binarized and ternary networks. It raises questions about the trade-offs involved in storing non-zero structures versus the benefits gained from sparsity.

The authors conclude that despite the diversity of methods in the literature, many achieve similar accuracy levels at given sparsity thresholds, indicating potential inherent limits to compression that are challenging to surpass.
