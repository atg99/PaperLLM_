The paper by T. Hoefler et al. discusses various approaches to model sparsification in neural networks, focusing particularly on ephemeral sparsification and its implications for performance. Ephemeral sparsification is applied during the processing of individual examples and primarily affects activations, as seen in well-known functions like ReLU and SoftMax, which naturally induce sparsity by setting values to zero based on thresholds. The paper also highlights techniques such as random activation sparsity (dropout) and top-k sparsification that enhance model efficiency.

In addition, the authors explore how gradient-based training can incorporate sparsity during the back-propagation phase of stochastic gradient descent (SGD) by partially updating weights, which can lead to performance improvements, especially in distributed training environments. Techniques such as delaying the communication of small gradient contributions until they are significant are also discussed.

The paper differentiates between ephemeral sparsity, which is dynamically updated during training and inference, and model sparsity, which requires a more complex training schedule often involving a pruning process. The authors outline three training schedules, emphasizing the common "train-then-sparsify" approach, which involves training a dense model to convergence before applying sparsification and subsequently fine-tuning the model to enhance accuracy. This method serves as a baseline for evaluating the effectiveness of sparsification in improving model performance and generalization.
