The paper by T. Hoefler et al. discusses the results of pruning the BERT-base model, which consists of 110 million parameters and is evaluated across four tasks from the GLUE benchmark: SST-2, MRPC, MNLI, and CoLA. The findings reveal that, unlike CNN pruning, BERT pruning shows fewer instances of significant accuracy degradation and high sparsity levels. This is attributed to research focusing on understanding the model's learning or the Lottery Ticket Hypothesis, where iterative pruning is limited to maintaining accuracy close to the original model.

The paper highlights several trends in pruning methods. For low sparsity levels, structured head pruning is effective but declines rapidly as more heads are pruned. At moderate sparsity levels (40-80%), unstructured magnitude pruning outperforms structured methods. However, for sparsity levels exceeding 90%, only first-order movement pruning shows promising results, achieving high accuracy on MNLI.

The authors also delve into "BERTology," examining the importance of various transformer components. Studies indicate that many attention heads can be pruned with minimal loss in accuracy. Voita et al. (2019) identify specific roles for heads, allowing for significant pruning without substantial quality degradation. Michel et al. (2019) corroborate these findings, demonstrating that head importance can be evaluated and that early training influences head significance. However, they note that multi-head attention layers only constitute about one-third of BERT's parameters, which limits overall compression. Prasanna et al. (2020) build on this by extending the importance metric to prune entire feedforward networks, indicating a comprehensive approach to model optimization.
