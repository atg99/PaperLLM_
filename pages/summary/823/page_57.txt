The paper discusses various methods of introducing sparsity in deep learning, particularly focusing on dropout techniques and gradient sparsification. Variational dropout, as proposed by Kingma et al., employs Bayesian principles to adapt dropout probabilities for each neuron based on training noise, which helps in reducing gradient variance. Molchanov et al. utilize this approach for weight pruning.

Gomez et al. enhance the dropout mechanism by implementing a targeted dropout that selectively drops weights or neurons based on their importance, determined by their activation magnitudes. This approach ensures that less significant elements are dropped during training, preparing the network for subsequent pruning.

The paper also delves into gradient sparsification, which seeks to reduce the size of gradients communicated during distributed training. This method is part of a broader communication compression strategy that includes techniques like quantization. The rationale behind gradient sparsification is that since gradients produced by stochastic gradient descent (SGD) are often noisy, removing less important components has minimal impact on convergence and can even regularize the training process.

Magnitude-based gradient sparsification methods are highlighted, starting with Strom's 2015 work, which establishes a fixed threshold for gradient selection. Gradients above this threshold are utilized, while smaller ones are quantized and packed efficiently. Error feedback is also introduced as a technique to enhance performance by compensating for removed gradient components. Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of sparsity in improving model efficiency and performance in deep learning.
