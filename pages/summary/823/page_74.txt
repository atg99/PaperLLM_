The paper discusses the integration of structured sparsity in convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and the implications for hardware acceleration. It builds on previous work analyzing the effects of different levels of sparsity, including unstructured weight sparsity and various structured forms, on CNN accuracy. Notably, increasing the block size of sparsity leads to a degradation in top-5 accuracy at certain sparsity levels.

The authors highlight the significant role of hardware accelerators designed specifically for sparse computations in deep learning, noting that typical workloads exhibit high levels of activation and weight sparsity. These accelerators are optimized for latency-sensitive tasks, particularly in sparse matrix-vector multiplication, achieving impressive performance metrics.

The overview of sparse CNN inference accelerators illustrates various techniques to enhance efficiency. For instance, Minerva employs a thresholding method to prune low-activation values, resulting in reduced power consumption. Eyeriss optimizes energy usage by clock-gating processing elements that handle zero activation values. The paper emphasizes that while different architectures can support a variety of layer types, specialized tuning for specific network architectures is crucial for maximizing performance.
