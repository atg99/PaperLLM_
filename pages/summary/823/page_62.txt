The paper by T. Hoefler et al. discusses the application of sparsification methods in neural network architectures, particularly focusing on their effectiveness in enhancing performance, reducing memory consumption, and improving energy efficiency. Various techniques, such as regularization and magnitude pruning, are often combined to achieve specific goals. The authors emphasize the importance of a structured methodology that includes a combination of sparsifying elements, a sparsification schedule, and removal methods, which can lead to significant performance improvements.

However, the authors also note the inconsistency in experimental practices across different studies, which can lead to ambiguous results regarding accuracy and performance. They advocate for careful interpretation of results, highlighting that while comparisons can be made, they should be viewed as rough estimates due to varying computational costs and implementations.

The paper further narrows its focus to convolutional neural networks (CNNs), which have become the primary target for sparsification. It describes how the architecture of CNNs, which includes convolutional and fully connected layers, affects the choice of sparsification strategies. The authors illustrate the historical development of accuracy and sparsity in CNNs over time, noting the trade-offs between achieving high validation accuracy and maintaining a high compression ratio. Overall, the results presented offer a valuable overview of the current state of sparsification methods in deep learning.
