The paper discusses the concept of sparsity in deep learning, particularly in the context of deep convolutional neural networks (CNNs). It highlights various methods and approaches to enhance the efficiency of these networks through filter and channel pruning. 

Key works cited include Yang He et al.'s research on filter pruning via geometric median, aimed at accelerating CNNs, and Yihui He et al.'s channel pruning technique designed to speed up very deep networks. These methods focus on reducing the number of parameters and computations while maintaining performance.

The foundational theories of neural network behavior, as proposed by Donald O. Hebb, are referenced to explain the underlying neuropsychological principles. Additionally, advancements in hardware acceleration for sparse tensor algebra are discussed, with Kartik Hegde et al. presenting ExTensor as a solution.

The paper also addresses the robustness of neural networks to various perturbations, referencing work by Dan Hendrycks and others on benchmarking and natural adversarial examples. The implications of sparsity on neural network learning and memory retention are examined, particularly in the context of compressed networks.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of sparsity for improving the efficiency and effectiveness of deep learning models, drawing from a range of studies and technological advancements in the field.
