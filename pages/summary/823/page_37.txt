The section discusses large-scale pruning techniques in deep learning that utilize second-order information, particularly focusing on the challenges of applying these methods to deep neural networks with millions or billions of parameters. A significant hurdle is the estimation of the Hessian inverse, which is difficult to store and invert.

One notable approach is the Layerwise Optimal Brain Surgeon (L-OBS), introduced by Dong et al. in 2017. This method refines the classical pruning techniques by creating separate objectives for each layer and approximating the Hessian using neuron-structured blocks, yielding better results than traditional layer-wise magnitude pruning.

Another method involves the empirical Fisher approximation to the Hessian, initially suggested by Hassibi and Stork in 1992. This approximation is valid under certain conditions, such as the model being well-optimized for classification tasks. The empirical Fisher matrix can be approximated using gradients from the loss function across samples.

The section also highlights a specific application known as Fisher pruning, where Theis et al. in 2018 employed a diagonal approximation of the empirical Fisher matrix for structured pruning in gaze prediction models.

Lastly, approaches utilizing low-rank inversion are mentioned, which leverage the structure of the approximation as a sum of rank one matrices. The Sherman-Morrison formula aids in estimating the inverse of the Fisher matrix iteratively, starting with an identity matrix and incorporating gradients over samples. This method, which enhances computational efficiency, traces back to the work of Hassibi and Stork.
