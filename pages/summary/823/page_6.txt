The paper by T. Hoefler et al. provides a structured overview of various sparsification approaches in deep learning, catering to a broad audience. It is organized into multiple sections, starting with a mathematical background in Section 1.2, which can be skipped by those familiar with the concepts. Section 2 offers an executive summary of pruning schemes, while Sections 3 and 4 delve into methods for weight removal and addition during training. Section 5 discusses ephemeral sparsification schemes, and Section 6 presents examples of pruning in convolutional and transformer architectures. Section 7 covers performance enhancement strategies for sparse models across software and hardware. The paper concludes with an overview of significant insights in Section 8 and outlines ten research challenges in Section 9.

For readers seeking a quick overview, Sections 2 and 8 are recommended, with selective reading of Sections 3, 4, 5, and 7. Hardware engineers should focus on Section 7, while neural network architects might find value in Sections 6 and the executive overview. Researchers are encouraged to review the entire document to engage with all aspects and identify challenges in Section 9.

The background in Section 1.2 explains deep learning inference and training, defining networks as graphs of parameterizable layers that implement complex functions. The learning process involves designing the network structure and training weights, with the forward pass being critical for evaluating predictions against a loss function. This foundational understanding is essential for comprehending the subsequent discussions in the paper.
