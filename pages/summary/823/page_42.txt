The paper discusses various methods for weight pruning in neural networks, focusing on techniques that enhance model sparsity and regularization during training. T. Hoefler et al. highlight the use of a differentiable L1 norm for regularization, while Azarian et al. introduce a sigmoid-based "soft pruning" estimator combined with layer-wise threshold learning, emphasizing the need for a gradual pruning process. Manessi et al. propose a soft version of the threshold linear function, allowing the threshold to be learned through stochastic gradient descent (SGD). 

Additionally, Yu et al. and Collins and Kohli advocate for a top-k pruning strategy, where only the top-k weights by magnitude are retained, reporting superior performance compared to soft regularization methods. Zhuang et al. introduce a polarization approach that uses a regularizer to distinguish between small and large weights, effectively separating them during pruning.

The paper also discusses the L1 norm as a convex relaxation of the L0 norm, which is widely used in lasso regression for sparsity. While L1 norms lead to small weights, they do not typically reduce weights to zero, necessitating the use of magnitude-based thresholding. Various studies cited explore different strategies for achieving effective weight pruning, including logarithmic penalties and proximal gradient algorithms for directional pruning, ultimately aiming to enhance model efficiency and performance.
