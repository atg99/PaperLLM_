The section discusses performance and model storage in the context of sparsification, which involves removing nonessential elements from a model to reduce its size and the operations needed for evaluation. When entire neurons or filters are eliminated, linear algebra properties can be leveraged to convert a sparse structure into a smaller dense one. However, removing elements from a weight matrix without structural constraints necessitates indexing the remaining non-zero elements.

Storage methods for indexing non-zero elements vary significantly, utilizing schemes ranging from bitmaps to absolute coordinate systems. The optimal indexing scheme depends on factors such as sparsity, structure, and access patterns, with various techniques existing for integer compression problems. The paper emphasizes a selection of illustrative indexing formats.

The discussion includes different storage schemes, such as bitmaps (BM), which are efficient for dense structures, and coordinate offset (COO) schemes, which are more suited for hyper-sparse structures. Additionally, run-length encoding can enhance efficiency by storing only the differences between indices, while zero-padded delta offset schemes can optimize storage based on expected distances between elements. In high-sparsity conditions, methods like compressed sparse row (CSR) and compressed sparse column (CSC) are highlighted for their effectiveness in managing indices in matrices and tensors, with CSR specifically demonstrating how to represent indices using column and row index arrays.
