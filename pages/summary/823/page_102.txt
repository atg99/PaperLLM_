The referenced works collectively address various aspects of machine learning, particularly focusing on model efficiency, bias, and optimization techniques. 

Hoefler et al. (2020) explore bias in compressed models, highlighting the implications of model compression on fairness. Howard et al. (2017) introduce MobileNets, which optimize convolutional neural networks for mobile applications, emphasizing efficiency in design. Hoyer (2004) presents non-negative matrix factorization with sparsity constraints, which is significant for feature extraction in high-dimensional data.

Hu et al. (2016) propose a data-driven neuron pruning method known as network trimming, aimed at enhancing the efficiency of deep architectures. Huang et al. (2016) discuss deep networks with stochastic depth, demonstrating improved training and performance by varying layer depth during training. Huang and Wang (2018) focus on data-driven sparse structure selection for deep neural networks, contributing to model robustness.

Huang et al. (2019) present optimal sparsity-sensitive bounds for distributed mean estimation, which is crucial for efficient data handling in distributed systems. Hubara et al. (2016) investigate binarized neural networks, reducing model size while maintaining accuracy. Iandola et al. (2016) introduce SqueezeNet, achieving AlexNet-level performance with significantly fewer parameters, highlighting the importance of model compactness.

Ioffe and Szegedy (2015) propose batch normalization to accelerate deep network training by minimizing internal covariate shift. Ivanov et al. (2021) focus on optimizing transformer architectures, underscoring the significance of data movement efficiency. Lastly, Ivkin et al. (2019) address communication-efficient distributed stochastic gradient descent with sketching methods, enhancing the scalability of learning algorithms.

Jacobs et al. (1991) present the concept of adaptive mixtures of local experts, which is foundational in understanding model adaptation and specialization in neural networks. Collectively, these contributions advance the understanding of model efficiency, bias, and optimization in machine learning.
