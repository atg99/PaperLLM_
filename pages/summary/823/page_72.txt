The paper by T. Hoefler et al. discusses the storage techniques for unstructured sparse matrices, highlighting the use of run-length and delta-encoding methods combined with quantization to optimize data alignment for efficient processing. It presents an example where sparse vectors are packed into 16-bit elements to fit DRAM and PCIe boundaries, facilitating operations such as sparse matrix-vector multiplication through the compressed sparse column (CSC) format.

The authors reference various studies, including Gale et al. (2020), which explore optimization techniques for GPU architectures, and Park et al. (2017), who focus on tuning unstructured sparsity in convolutional layers. They find that while high sparsity can improve storage efficiency, it can lead to performance degradation on CPUs due to increased overhead in managing sparse data structures and reduced compute load. The paper emphasizes the need for careful tuning and co-design between data science and engineering aspects to maximize performance.

In discussing structured sparsity, the authors differentiate between unstructured and structured storage formats, noting that structured formats can reduce storage overhead significantly. They introduce concepts such as blocked formats, which store offsets for blocks of elements, and block-balanced formats, which specify fixed block sizes and non-zero counts per block. These innovations aim to enhance the efficiency of sparse matrix storage and operations in deep learning contexts.
