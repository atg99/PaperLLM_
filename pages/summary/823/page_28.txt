The paper by T. Hoefler et al. discusses magnitude pruning of weights in the context of deep neural networks, specifically focusing on the ResNet-50 architecture and its performance on the ImageNet validation set. The authors illustrate the weight distribution before and after pruning, revealing that weight values typically follow a normal distribution centered around zero. The figures presented show the weight distributions in three scenarios: before pruning, immediately after pruning, and post-retraining.

An important consideration in magnitude pruning is selecting the pruning threshold, denoted as x. The authors discuss various strategies for determining this threshold, including fixing a weight budget, retaining a certain number of top weights, or learning layer-specific sparsification thresholds during training. One method proposed by Kusupati et al. involves replacing original weights with thresholded weights while learning the pruning thresholds via stochastic gradient descent (SGD). Another approach mentioned utilizes reinforcement learning to optimize pruning thresholds based on different resource constraints or target accuracies.

Magnitude pruning is often integrated into sparse training schedules to maintain consistent connection density. The paper references several notable works that have contributed to the popularity and application of magnitude pruning, including methods for pruning entire filters in convolutional layers and its implications in recurrent neural networks.

The authors also note that there are data-free pruning methods that do not rely on training examples but instead base their decisions on network structure. These approaches contribute to the broader understanding of weight pruning and network compression techniques in deep learning.
