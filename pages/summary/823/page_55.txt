The section discusses the phenomenon of sparsity in neuron activations within ReLU-based neural networks. It notes that while the theoretical expectation is that around 50% of output values would be zero, empirical studies have shown that actual sparsity can reach up to 90% in certain layers. This has significant implications for hardware architecture and has led to various strategies for compressing activation maps to improve memory usage and computational efficiency.

Key techniques for sparsifying activations include stochastic pruning for defense against adversarial attacks, and converting fixed-point activations to vectors over finite fields, allowing for reduced memory requirements with minimal accuracy loss. However, these methods often require network structure modifications and retraining.

Recent research by Georgiadis introduced L1-regularization for activation maps, achieving a notable increase in sparsity, while Kurtz et al. highlighted Hoyer regularization as more effective, yielding higher sparsity with less accuracy degradation. They also presented thresholding methods to enhance sparsity further and proposed algorithms for faster inference on CPUs. Concurrently, Dong et al. developed a framework for speedups in models with high input sparsity, applicable in specific tasks like LiDAR detection and character recognition. Overall, these advancements in activation sparsity are crucial for optimizing neural network performance.
