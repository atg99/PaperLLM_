The paper discusses the concept of sparsity in deep learning, focusing on structured and strided sparsity methods. Structured sparsity, while offering high performance and reduced storage needs, may restrict model flexibility and performance. An example is the removal of entire neurons in fully-connected layers, which simplifies computations to dense matrix multiplications. Similar strategies apply to convolutional filters and transformer heads.

Strided sparsity involves structured weight sparsification at the channel level, where entire feature maps or kernels can be removed, leading to efficient storage through offset and stride representations. The convolution operator can also be sparsified, allowing for different non-zero structures across input maps, which has been shown to effectively reduce filter complexity.

The paper also highlights data-free selection based on weight magnitude as a simple yet effective pruning technique, where weights with the smallest magnitudes are removed. This method has historical precedent and is often combined with retraining and gradual sparsity increases. It can be applied to individual weights or groups, supporting structured pruning approaches. The authors suggest that this technique is theoretically justified under certain assumptions.
