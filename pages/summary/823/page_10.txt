The paper by T. Hoefler et al. discusses the relationship between the Fisher matrix and empirical Fisher, emphasizing the approximation of the Fisher matrix with empirical data. The authors note that this approximation is common but not always valid, referencing works by Martens and Grosse, Ly et al., Kunstner et al., and Singh and Alistarh for further reading.

The paper also introduces Bayesian inference as a framework for understanding variational pruning methods. In Bayesian learning, the goal is to determine a set of parameters that best approximates the output distribution given a dataset. This involves establishing a prior distribution over the model parameters and updating it to a posterior distribution using Bayes' rule. However, calculating the posterior is often impractical due to the complexity of the models, leading to the need for approximations.

Variational Inference is presented as a technique that approximates the posterior distribution with a parametric form. The quality of this approximation is evaluated through the Kullback-Leibler divergence. The optimization problem is framed to maximize a variational lower bound of the marginal log-likelihood, combining the expected log-likelihood of the model and a regularization term that encourages the approximate distribution to remain close to the prior. This approach facilitates efficient computation and understanding of Bayesian inference in the context of machine learning.
