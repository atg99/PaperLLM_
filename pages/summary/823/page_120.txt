The cited works encompass significant contributions to the fields of neural networks, natural language processing, and machine learning. 

Hoefler et al. introduced the Transformer model in "Attention Is All You Need," which revolutionized sequence-to-sequence tasks by leveraging self-attention mechanisms. This model eliminated the need for recurrent layers, improving parallelization and performance.

Verdenius et al. focused on pruning neural networks by using iterative ranking of sensitivity statistics to enhance model efficiency without sacrificing accuracy. Voita et al. further explored pruning in self-attention mechanisms, demonstrating that specialized heads in multi-head attention are crucial for performance, allowing less significant heads to be pruned.

Wan et al. proposed DropConnect as a regularization technique for neural networks, enhancing generalization. Wang et al. introduced the GLUE benchmark, facilitating the evaluation of natural language understanding across multiple tasks.

Additionally, Chaoqi Wang et al. presented EigenDamage, a structured pruning method that operates in the eigenbasis, while Hongyi Wang et al. developed ATOMO, which focuses on communication-efficient learning through atomic sparsification.

Linnan Wang et al. applied FFT-based gradient sparsification to improve the efficiency of distributed training of deep neural networks. Ziheng Wang et al. investigated structured pruning specifically for large language models.

Wangni et al. addressed communication efficiency in distributed optimization with gradient sparsification techniques. Warstadt et al. examined neural network acceptability judgments, contributing to understanding language models' interpretability. Lastly, Wei et al. introduced minimal effort backpropagation for convolutional neural networks, streamlining the training process.

These works collectively advance the understanding and implementation of efficient neural network architectures and training methodologies, highlighting the interplay between model complexity and computational efficiency.
