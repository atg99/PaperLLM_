The document discusses various metrics used to evaluate the performance of classification models, particularly in the context of anomaly detection. Key metrics include:

1. **False Positive Rate (FPR)**: Measures the percentage of normal instances incorrectly identified as positive, calculated as FPR = FP / (FP + TN).

2. **Accuracy (AC)**: Represents the overall correctness of the model, defined as AC = (TP + TN) / (TP + TN + FP + FN). It is most effective when the costs of false positives and false negatives are similar.

3. **Precision (P)**: Indicates the proportion of predicted anomalies that are actual anomalies, calculated as P = TP / (TP + FP) * 100%.

4. **Recall (R)**: Reflects the ability of the model to identify actual anomalies, computed as R = TP / (TP + FN) * 100%. A high recall indicates effective detection of the positive class.

5. **F-Score**: The harmonic mean of precision and recall, providing a balanced measure of the model's performance when the costs of false positives and negatives differ, calculated as F = (2 * P * R) / (P + R) * 100%.

6. **Confusion Matrix**: A table summarizing the performance of a classification model, showing true positives (TP), false negatives (FN), false positives (FP), and true negatives (TN).
