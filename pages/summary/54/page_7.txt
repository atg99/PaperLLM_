The paper discusses the distribution of normal and attack records in the NSL-KDD dataset, presenting data on the total instances, including normal and various attack types. It highlights the effectiveness of RNN-based intrusion detection systems (RNN-IDS) as reported by previous researchers, demonstrating superior performance over traditional machine learning methods like J48, SVM, RF, and ANN in classifying both binary and multi-class instances. The authors reference various studies that employed LSTM and CNN-LSTM models, showing their capabilities in classification and feature selection.

The paper introduces a new approach using an LSTM-RNN NIDS combined with a Genetic Algorithm for feature selection, aiming to enhance detection rates. The research involved training the model on the NSL-KDD dataset and evaluating its performance through binary and multi-class classifications. Key metrics employed for assessment included accuracy, precision, recall, f-score, true positive rate, and false-positive rate, utilizing a confusion matrix for performance visualization. The LSTM-RNN models were tested with varying neuron configurations in hidden layers, and the implementation was carried out using Python 3.7 with relevant libraries.
