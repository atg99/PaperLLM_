The paper discusses the development of an LSTM-RNN model for classification tasks, specifically focusing on hyper-parameter selection and optimizers for both binary and multi-class classification. Key hyper-parameters defined include batch size, epochs, learning rate, dropout rates, and activation functions. Batch size refers to the number of training samples processed in one iteration, while epochs indicate a complete pass through the training dataset. The learning rate controls the weight updates during training, and dropout serves as a regularization technique to prevent overfitting by randomly ignoring certain neurons during training.

For optimization, the paper selects stochastic gradient descent (SGD) for multi-class classification due to its lower computational cost compared to other methods, which require all training samples per iteration. The Adam optimizer is chosen for binary classification for its ease of implementation and efficiency.

The model's performance is evaluated by calculating testing accuracy and loss values after fitting with training and testing datasets. The prediction matrix is generated using Keras, and classification metrics such as precision, recall, F-score, true positive rate, false-positive rate, and confusion matrix are computed to assess model accuracy.

The experimental setup employs TensorFlow and Keras for the LSTM-RNN model and scikit-learn for SVM and RF classifiers. Two classification types are conducted: binary classification for normal and anomaly detection, and multi-class classification for detecting various types of attacks. The optimal feature set was determined using a genetic algorithm, consisting of 99 features out of an initial 122.

To mitigate overfitting, the study implements varying dropout rates across different layers of the LSTM-RNN model, adjusting the percentage based on the number of hidden layers used. Each experiment was repeated multiple times to ensure reliability in results.
