The paper discusses the implementation of various machine learning models, specifically focusing on the LSTM-RNN classifier for both binary and multi-class classification tasks. The hyper-parameters for the experiments are detailed in Table 4, which outlines settings such as learning rate, dropout rates, activation functions, optimizers, epochs, batch sizes, and loss functions tailored for binary and multi-class classification scenarios. 

Additionally, the architecture of the LSTM-RNN is explored, with varying numbers of neurons in the hidden layers as shown in Table 5. The experimental results evaluate classification performance based on metrics including accuracy, precision, recall, f-score, true positive rate (TPR), and false positive rate (FPR) across different configurations of hidden neurons. 

The classification tasks involve distinguishing between normal and anomalous data for binary classification, and identifying five different types of attacks (normal, DoS, probe, U2R, and R2L) for multi-class classification. Furthermore, the performance of the LSTM-RNN is compared with traditional machine learning models such as Support Vector Machine (SVM) and Random Forest (RF), utilizing feature sets derived from genetic algorithms. The results aim to demonstrate the effectiveness of the LSTM-RNN model in comparison to these conventional methods.
