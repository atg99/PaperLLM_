Anomaly-based network intrusion detection systems (NIDS) utilize heuristic methods to identify unknown attacks by classifying deviations from normal traffic, although this approach often results in a high false-positive rate. Despite this drawback, anomaly-based NIDSs are favored in research for their potential to detect novel attacks. Various machine learning techniques, such as support vector machines (SVM) and decision trees (DT), have been applied to established datasets like the 1998 DARPA and NSL-KDD datasets. Additionally, methodologies including random forests, k-means, naïve Bayes, multilayer perceptrons, and artificial neural networks have been explored.

Deep learning methods, specifically deep neural networks (DNN), recurrent neural networks (RNN), convolutional neural networks (CNN), deep belief networks (DBN), and long short-term memory networks (LSTM), have shown promise in various domains including intrusion detection. Research indicates that LSTM-RNN outperforms several other models, such as SVM and k-nearest neighbors, particularly in detecting attacks within the KDD Cup'99 datasets.

Feature selection is critical in developing effective NIDS, as features relevant to one attack class may not apply to another. Techniques for feature selection include filter, wrapper, and embedded approaches, each with distinct advantages and drawbacks. This research presents a new NIDS that integrates genetic algorithms (GA) for optimal feature selection with LSTM-RNN for classification on the NSL-KDD dataset. The study involved both binary and multi-class classification, with an emphasis on establishing an optimal set of hyper-parameters for the LSTM-RNN model.

Results demonstrate that the combination of GA and LSTM-RNN significantly enhances accuracy compared to using the full feature set. The model effectively learns to identify various attack classes, resulting in improved detection rates and reduced misclassification. Performance metrics, including accuracy, precision, recall, and confusion matrix, were utilized to evaluate the model, which consistently outperformed SVM and random forest classifiers.
