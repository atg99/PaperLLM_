In the binary classification results, the initial accuracy with 122 features was 96.51% for KDDTest + and 99.88% for KDDTrain +, with TPR and FPR values of 0.944 and 0.007, respectively. After implementing the GA to select an optimal set of 99 features, accuracy increased to 99.91% for KDDTest + and 99.99% for KDDTrain +, achieving a TPR of 0.999 and FPR of 0.003. This demonstrates a more than 3% enhancement in accuracy and highlights the efficiency of a smaller LSTM-RNN network, which also minimized running time.

Furthermore, the Random Forest (RF) classifier outperformed the LSTM-RNN in binary classification, attributed to its effectiveness in constructing decision trees from well-defined feature classes. The RF's structure allowed it to combine multiple decision trees effectively, leading to high testing accuracy.

In the multi-class classification scenario, the initial accuracy with 122 features was 82.68% for KDDTest + and 99.87% for KDDTrain +, with TPR and FPR values of 0.707 and 0.015, respectively. Following the GA's application, accuracy improved to 93.88% for KDDTest + and 99.0% for KDDTrain +, with TPR and FPR values of 0.896 and 0.005, respectively, reflecting a notable increase of over 10% in accuracy using fewer features.

Confusion matrices for both binary and multi-class classifications demonstrated a reduction in misclassifications after the implementation of the GA, underscoring its effectiveness in enhancing classifier performance while lowering complexity. Overall, the research emphasizes the benefits of using genetic algorithms for feature selection in improving classification outcomes in both binary and multi-class scenarios.
