In this paper, the authors conducted a comparative analysis of various classifiers, specifically SVM, random forest, and LSTM-RNN, on the NSL-KDD dataset for binary and multi-class classification tasks. The results showed that the proposed model achieved an impressive 99.99% training accuracy and 99.91% testing accuracy for binary classification, while the five-class classification yielded 99% training accuracy and 93.88% testing accuracy. Notably, the LSTM-RNN outperformed SVM in binary classification but was surpassed by random forest in overall performance. However, with a reduced feature set of 99, the LSTM-RNN produced testing accuracy comparable to that of random forest.

For multi-class classification, the LSTM-RNN demonstrated superior performance, achieving 82.68% accuracy with 122 features and 93.88% with the 99-feature set. The LSTM-RNN enhanced by a genetic algorithm (GA) achieved a 10% higher accuracy than random forest in this context, highlighting the effectiveness of GA in boosting LSTM-RNN performance, particularly on larger datasets.

The authors acknowledged limitations in their approach, such as not recording training time and the need for future experiments on GPU systems and with updated datasets like UNSW-NB15. They also proposed developing a framework for real-time network data collection and labeling, alongside comparisons of their LSTM-RNN-GA model with other deep learning techniques.

The paper concludes that while LSTM-RNN is suited for large datasets, its advantages are less pronounced in smaller datasets with fewer features.
