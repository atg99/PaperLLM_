The paper presents an analysis of multi-class classification performance using genetic algorithms and various feature sets on the KDDTest dataset. It highlights that the application of a genetic algorithm reduced misclassifications, resulting in a higher number of correctly classified records. 

Tables 15 and 16 showcase confusion matrices and evaluation metrics for models using 122 features. The results indicate that while accuracy rates for the Normal, DoS, and Probe classes are relatively high, the R2L and U2R classes exhibit significantly lower accuracy due to their limited training records. 

Table 17 further illustrates the performance of models using 99 features, which improved classification accuracy for the R2L and U2R classes when compared to the 122 feature model. Particularly, the use of genetic algorithms contributed to better results for these classes.

Additionally, the paper references a self-taught learning approach achieving high accuracy rates for both binary and multi-class classifications, and it compares these results with those obtained from LSTM-RNN models previously applied to the KDD Cup' 99 dataset. Overall, the findings emphasize the effectiveness of genetic algorithms in enhancing classification performance across different feature sets.
