The paper discusses the development of a novel intrusion detection system (IDS) that utilizes a Long Short-Term Memory Recurrent Neural Network (LSTM-RNN) combined with a genetic algorithm (GA) for optimal feature selection to classify network attacks. The study specifically focuses on the NSL-KDD dataset, which is commonly used for evaluating IDS performance. The researchers found that employing the LSTM-RNN classifiers with an optimal feature set significantly enhances the accuracy of intrusion detection.

Key metrics such as accuracy, recall, precision, f-score, and confusion matrix were used to assess the performance of the IDS. The LSTM-RNN was effective in classifying the dataset into binary (normal vs. abnormal) and multi-class categories (Normal, Denial of Service (DoS), Probing, User to Root (U2R), and Remote to Local (R2L)). The results demonstrated that the GA improved the classification accuracy of the LSTM-RNN for both binary and multi-class classifications when compared to traditional classifiers like Support Vector Machine (SVM) and Random Forest (RF). For multi-class classification, the LSTM-RNN with GA outperformed both SVM and RF, while for binary classification, it achieved similar accuracy to RF and better accuracy than SVM.

The introduction highlights the increasing prevalence of cyber offenses and the necessity for effective NIDS to safeguard network systems. It outlines the two primary types of NIDS: signature-based, which relies on known attack patterns, and anomaly-based, which detects deviations from normal behavior. The paper underscores the advancements in IDS technology since its inception and the ongoing need for robust solutions to combat evolving network threats.
