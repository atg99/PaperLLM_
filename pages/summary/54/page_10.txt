The paper discusses the use of a Genetic Algorithm (GA) for feature selection in a dataset, focusing on the optimization of predictive features for classification tasks. The GA employs a uniform crossover method to combine feature sets from a population of individuals, while mutation is applied with a low probability of 0.05 to maintain diversity and avoid premature convergence. After processing 10 generations, the GA selects the individual with the highest predictive feature set and terminates, or continues if further generations are needed.

In the experiment, the GA identified 70 unique features from the training dataset and 66 from the testing dataset, leading to a combined set of 99 features. The majority of these features belong to service and flag categories, as well as protocol type, indicating their relevance in predicting attack types. Other unique features were also selected for their potential value.

The paper then outlines the construction of an LSTM-RNN model for classification, detailing the selection of hyper-parameters such as batch size, number of epochs, learning rate, dropout, and activation function. These parameters are crucial for optimizing the training process of the LSTM-RNN model.
