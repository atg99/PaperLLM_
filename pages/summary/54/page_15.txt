The paper evaluates the performance of three classification methods—LSTM-RNN, SVM, and Random Forest (RF)—on binary and multi-class classification tasks using different feature sets. 

In binary classification, LSTM-RNN achieved a testing accuracy of 96.51% with a True Positive Rate (TPR) of 0.944, outperforming SVM but falling short of RF, which was the best performer. 

For multi-class classification, using all features, LSTM-RNN attained the highest testing accuracy at 82.68%, with SVM at 67.20% and RF at 80.70%. LSTM-RNN also demonstrated a superior TPR of 0.707 compared to SVM and RF.

When using an optimal feature set of 99 features, the performance of SVM, RF, and LSTM-RNN in binary classification was comparable, with testing accuracies around 99.9%. However, in multi-class classification with the same optimal feature set, LSTM-RNN excelled with a testing accuracy of 93.88%, while SVM and RF achieved 68.10% and 84.90% respectively, again showing the highest TPR at 0.896. Overall, LSTM-RNN consistently performed well across various classifications, particularly in multi-class scenarios.
