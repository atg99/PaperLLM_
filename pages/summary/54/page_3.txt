This paper investigates the effectiveness of Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks combined with Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) for binary and multi-class classification tasks, achieving an accuracy of 99.91% for binary classification and 93.88% for multi-class classification. The methodology integrates genetic algorithms (GAs) to enhance performance metrics, including high True Positive Rate (TPR) and low False Positive Rate (FPR).

The structure of the paper is as follows: Section 2 provides a background on deep learning, LSTM, RNNs, and GAs, along with an overview of the NSL-KDD dataset utilized for the experiments. Section 3 reviews prior research on LSTM and RNN applications in Network Intrusion Detection Systems (NIDS). Section 4 details the methodology employed for the LSTM-RNN with GA model. Section 5 presents experimental results and a comparison of classifier performances. Section 6 discusses the implications of the results, and Section 7 concludes with insights and directions for future research.

In the background section, deep learning is described as a method leveraging multi-layer artificial neural networks, including various architectures such as DNNs, CNNs, RNNs, and DBNs. Specifically, RNNs are highlighted for their structure, which mimics human brain neuron connections, allowing for signal transmission and weight adjustments that facilitate learning. RNNs typically consist of input, output, and hidden units, with a necessary configuration of at least three hidden layers to process data effectively. The architecture allows for a directional flow of information and error comparison across hidden layers, enhancing the learning process.
