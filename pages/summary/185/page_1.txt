This paper by Lilian Weng discusses the mathematical foundations of generative adversarial networks (GANs) and the challenges associated with their training. GANs consist of two competing models, a generator and a critic, which aim to improve each other's performance. Despite their success in generating realistic content, GANs often face issues such as instability and convergence difficulties.

The author reviews two key metrics for measuring the similarity between probability distributions: Kullback–Leibler (KL) divergence and Jensen–Shannon (JS) divergence. KL divergence is an asymmetric measure that can lead to misleading results when comparing distributions of differing significance. In contrast, JS divergence is symmetric and smoother, making it a more reliable option for quantifying similarity.

The paper suggests that the transition from KL divergence to JS divergence in the loss function may have contributed to the success of GANs. The author introduces Wasserstein GAN (WGAN) as a modification designed to address the training challenges encountered with traditional GANs. Overall, the work aims to provide insights into the mathematical principles underlying GANs and the rationale for adopting WGANs to enhance training stability.
