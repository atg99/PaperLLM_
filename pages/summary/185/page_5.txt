The paper discusses challenges in training Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), specifically focusing on issues related to low-dimensional supports and the vanishing gradient problem. 

In the context of low-dimensional supports, the authors explain that real-world data often lies on lower-dimensional manifolds despite appearing high-dimensional. This phenomenon is crucial for understanding how real images conform to specific characteristics, such as the expected features of objects. The paper references prior work that highlights how the supports of the generator and real data can become disjoint on these manifolds, leading to the ability to create a perfect discriminator that can separate real from fake samples.

The vanishing gradient problem arises when the discriminator becomes too effective, leading to a scenario where the loss function approaches zero. In this case, the generator receives no meaningful feedback to learn from, resulting in stagnation of the training process. The paper outlines a dilemma faced during GAN training: if the discriminator performs poorly, the generator lacks accurate feedback; conversely, if the discriminator excels, the gradient becomes negligible, hindering learning progress.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the inherent difficulties in GAN training due to the interplay between the structures of data supports and the gradients during the learning process.
