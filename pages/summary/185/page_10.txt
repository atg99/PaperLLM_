The paper discusses the use of various distance metrics to compare probability distributions, specifically focusing on the Kullback-Leibler divergence (DKL), Jensen-Shannon divergence (DJS), and Wasserstein distance (W). 

The Kullback-Leibler divergence is noted for producing infinite values when distributions are disjoint, while the Jensen-Shannon divergence exhibits a sudden jump and is not differentiable at the point where distributions overlap. In contrast, the Wasserstein distance offers a smooth measure, crucial for stable learning processes, particularly in gradient descent algorithms.

The authors propose leveraging the Wasserstein distance as a loss function in Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs). They highlight the challenge of exhaustively computing all possible joint distributions to derive infimum values and suggest a transformation based on Kantorovich-Rubinstein duality to simplify the computation. This results in a form that requires the function to be K-Lipschitz continuous, meaning it maintains a bounded rate of change defined by a Lipschitz constant.

The paper emphasizes that while functions that are continuously differentiable meet the Lipschitz condition, not all Lipschitz continuous functions are differentiable everywhere. The modified Wasserstein-GAN utilizes a discriminator model to learn and optimize the Lipschitz continuous function, thereby measuring the Wasserstein distance between the real and generated distributions as the loss function. 

Overall, the work illustrates the advantages of using Wasserstein distance in GANs for more stable and effective training.
