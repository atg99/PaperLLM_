The paper discusses the challenges faced in training Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), particularly highlighting two significant issues: Mode Collapse and the lack of proper evaluation metrics. 

In the context of Mode Collapse, the generator may converge to producing a limited set of outputs, failing to capture the diversity of the real-world data distribution. Despite potentially deceiving the discriminator, this results in a loss of variety in the generated outputs, indicating a significant limitation in the generator's learning process.

Additionally, the paper points out the absence of an effective evaluation metric for GANs, which complicates the training process. Without a reliable objective function, it becomes difficult to gauge progress, determine when to cease training, or compare the effectiveness of different models. This lack of clarity can lead to inefficient training and suboptimal outcomes. 

Overall, the discussion emphasizes the need for improved strategies to address these challenges in GAN training to enhance their performance and reliability.
