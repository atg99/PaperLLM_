The paper discusses the concept of Wasserstein distance in the context of continuous probability distributions. It introduces the formula W(pr;pg) = inf{E(x,y)[kx-yk]} where ϕ(pr;pg) represents all possible joint probability distributions between pr and pg. Each joint distribution corresponds to a dirt transport plan in continuous probability space, indicating the percentage of dirt to be moved from point x to point y to align with the target distribution pg. The marginal distributions confirm that the total dirt moved from x matches the distribution of y and vice versa.

The total dirt moved is denoted as ϕ(x,y), with the cost calculated as ϕ(x,y) * kx - yk. The expected cost across all (x,y) pairs is computed, and the Wasserstein distance is defined as the minimum cost among all transport solutions. The infimum in the definition signifies the focus on finding the smallest cost.

The paper argues that Wasserstein distance is superior to Jensen-Shannon (JS) or Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence, as it provides a meaningful representation of distance even when two distributions exist in lower-dimensional manifolds without overlap. An example illustrates this point, showing that even when distributions P and Q do not overlap, Wasserstein distance can still yield a smooth representation of their separation.
