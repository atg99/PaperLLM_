The Wasserstein metric is introduced as a replacement for Jensen-Shannon (JS) divergence due to its smoother value space. The Wasserstein Distance, also referred to as Earth Mover's Distance (EM distance), quantifies the distance between two probability distributions by estimating the minimum energy cost required to transform one distribution into another. This cost is calculated based on the amount of "dirt" moved and the distance it is moved.

In a discrete case with two distributions P and Q, each containing four piles of dirt, the process of transforming P into Q involves several steps. The distributions are defined as follows: P consists of piles with 3, 2, 1, and 4 shovelfuls, while Q has 1, 2, 4, and 3 shovelfuls respectively. The transformation involves moving shovelfuls between the piles to match the distributions. Each move incurs a cost, which is tracked through a series of calculations leading to the total Earth Mover’s distance, calculated as the sum of all movement costs. In this example, the final Wasserstein Distance is determined to be 5.
