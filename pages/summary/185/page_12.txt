The referenced papers focus on advancements in training generative models, particularly Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs). 

Huszár discusses the challenges of training generative models, emphasizing techniques like scheduled sampling and the role of likelihood and adversarial training. Salimans et al. propose improved techniques for GAN training, addressing common pitfalls and enhancing model performance. Arjovsky and Bottou highlight the need for principled training methods for GANs, advocating for theoretical foundations to guide practices. They introduce the Wasserstein GAN, which redefines the loss function to improve training stability and performance. Gulrajani et al. build upon this by presenting enhancements to the Wasserstein GAN training process, focusing on regularization methods that further stabilize training and improve convergence.

Collectively, these works contribute to the understanding and practical approaches for effectively training generative models, particularly emphasizing the importance of theoretical frameworks and innovative techniques to overcome previous limitations.
