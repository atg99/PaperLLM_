This paper discusses advancements in automatic speech recognition (ASR) using end-to-end methods, achieving state-of-the-art results on the L IBRISPEECH dataset. It highlights the limitations posed by using 10k word-piece tokens during training, which restrict the model's ability to stride effectively. The authors note that as the relative word error rate (WER) gains from language models diminish, there is potential for integrating acoustical models (AMs) that can output words with an arbitrary lexicon. The paper advocates for semi-supervised learning techniques that do not require external language models while demonstrating the effectiveness of a straightforward training pipeline.

The authors emphasize that methods such as sequence-to-sequence models, connectionist temporal classification, and differentiable decoding can yield competitive results without relying on external language models. The discussion suggests that future research could explore joint training of AMs and language models to further enhance performance. The paper concludes with acknowledgments for contributions to the audio recordings used in the study and provides a comprehensive list of references for further reading on the topic.
