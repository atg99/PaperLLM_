The paper discusses the implementation of a decoding framework for automatic speech recognition (ASR) using a combination of acoustic models (AM) and language models (LM). It utilizes pseudo-labels generated from the LIBRISPEECH dataset to improve performance on a filtered corpus. The perplexity metrics indicate that the 4-gram LM trained on the filtered dataset shows only a slight regression in word error rate (WER) compared to the original LIBRISPEECH corpus.

Decoding is performed through a one-pass beam-search process, which selects the best transcription by considering the posteriors from the acoustic model alongside the language model's perplexity. The authors employ both lexicon-based and lexicon-free beam-search decoders, which differ based on the model type (CTC or S2S) and the input they can handle. The decoding process involves tuning parameters such as the language model weight and word insertion penalty on validation datasets.

Additionally, a rescoring step is introduced after obtaining N-best hypotheses from the initial decoding, where external language models are used to evaluate and reorder the hypotheses based on their log-probabilities. This strategy enhances the likelihood of identifying the correct transcription by diversifying the hypotheses.

The experiments described in the paper utilize standard splits from the LIBRISPEECH dataset for training and validation. The models are trained using the wav2letter++ toolkit, with hyper-parameters being cross-validated to optimize performance. The paper emphasizes the significance of careful tuning and methodology in achieving effective ASR results.
