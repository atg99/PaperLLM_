The paper discusses various advancements in automatic speech recognition (ASR) technologies, highlighting significant contributions from different studies. Key topics include data augmentation techniques such as SpecAugment, which improves model robustness, and the application of fully neural network-based methods for speech recognition on mobile and embedded devices. The research also emphasizes the importance of large-scale acoustic models, noting lessons learned from building models with extensive speech datasets. 

Sequence-to-sequence models are compared, showcasing their effectiveness in ASR tasks. The paper references several state-of-the-art systems, including wav2letter++, which is recognized for its speed, and transformer-based acoustic modeling, which has gained traction for hybrid speech recognition. Other notable contributions include work on neural speech recognizers and advancements in semi-supervised training techniques. 

The collective findings from these studies illustrate the ongoing evolution of ASR technologies, driven by innovations in neural networks and model training methodologies. The references cited provide a comprehensive overview of the landscape of speech recognition research, underscoring the collaborative efforts in improving machine understanding of human speech.
