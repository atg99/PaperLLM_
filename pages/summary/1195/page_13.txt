The paper presents a detailed analysis of word error rates (WER) on the LibriSpeech dataset, focusing on models trained with Connectionist Temporal Classification (CTC) and Sequence-to-Sequence (Seq2Seq) losses. The results are organized by model architecture, lexicon type, and decoding strategy, with performance evaluated on both development and test sets for clean and other data.

Key findings indicate that models trained with larger architectures, such as ResNet and TDS, generally achieve lower WER, particularly when using advanced decoding techniques like GCNN and rescoring methods. For instance, the ResNet model with a 500M parameter size and GCNN decoding achieved a WER of 2.19 on clean test data, showcasing effective performance. The Seq2Seq models also demonstrate competitive results, with the TDS model reaching a WER of 2.08 using GCNN decoding.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of model architecture and decoding strategy in optimizing performance on speech recognition tasks, highlighting significant improvements in word error rates through careful selection of these parameters.
