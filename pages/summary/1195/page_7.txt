This paper presents a detailed analysis of various acoustic models (AMs) and their performance on the LIBRISPEECH dataset, specifically focusing on word error rates (WERs) across different configurations and training conditions. Key findings include:

1. **Performance Metrics**: The results are summarized in tabular format, with the best-performing models highlighted. The WERs vary based on the type of model, lexicon usage, and whether the data is clean or includes noise.

2. **Model Variants**: Different architectures are compared, including Fully Convolutional networks, Convolutional Transformer models, and Transformers with varying amounts of parameters and configurations. Notably, models trained with Seq2Seq loss consistently show lower WERs.

3. **Impact of Pseudo-labeling**: The study examines the effect of using pseudo-labeled audio from LIBRIVOX to improve model performance. Increasing the amount of pseudo-labeled data leads to improved results, demonstrating the value of leveraging additional unlabeled data.

4. **Overlap in Training Data**: The paper discusses the implications of using a language model (LM) that includes overlapping text when generating pseudo-labels. While some improvement in performance was noted, it was minimal, suggesting that the impact of overlap may not be significant.

5. **Training Exclusively on Pseudo-labels**: When evaluating models trained solely on pseudo-labels from LIBRIVOX, results indicated that these models can outperform those trained exclusively on LIBRISPEECH data. This highlights the potential effectiveness of pseudo-labeling in enhancing model training.

Overall, the findings underscore the importance of model architecture, training data quality, and the innovative use of pseudo-labeling to improve speech recognition systems. Further details and comprehensive results are provided in the appendices.
