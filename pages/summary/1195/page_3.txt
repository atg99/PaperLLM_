This paper discusses advancements in acoustic modeling techniques, particularly focusing on two primary architectures: Time-Depth Separable (TDS) Convolution Acoustic Models and Transformer-Based Acoustic Models.

In the TDS models, the architecture is enhanced by increasing the number of channels in the feature maps and employing a series of TDS blocks, which consist of 2-D convolution layers and fully-connected layers with ReLU, LayerNorm, and residual connections. The model is designed to accommodate both labeled and unlabeled data, with different configurations that adjust the number of blocks and channels to optimize performance. The encoder structure is consistent across both Connectionist Temporal Classification (CTC) and Sequence-to-Sequence (Seq2Seq) frameworks, integrating self-attention mechanisms for improved context processing.

The Transformer-based models feature a compact front-end consisting of multiple layers of 1-D convolutions followed by GLU activation functions. After the front-end processing, the architecture employs Transformer blocks with attention heads and feedforward networks. Two configurations are presented: one for CTC-trained models and another for Seq2Seq models, which includes an additional decoder layer. The self-attention mechanism and normalization processes are thoroughly defined, ensuring effective feature representation.

The paper also addresses the use of external language models (LMs) for beam-search decoding, including n-gram, convolutional, and Transformer-based LMs, highlighting their integration into the overall acoustic modeling framework. The combination of these methodologies aims to enhance the accuracy and efficiency of speech recognition systems.
