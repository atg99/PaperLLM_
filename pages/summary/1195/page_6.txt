This paper presents a comprehensive analysis of language models (LMs) trained on the L IBRISPEECH dataset, detailing the methodologies and results achieved across various configurations. The perplexity results for different LMs, including word-level 4-grams, GCNNs, and Transformers, are reported in Table 1, showcasing significant performance differences. 

Training procedures for the models are meticulously outlined, indicating that ResNet and TDS models were trained using plain stochastic gradient descent with momentum, while Transformers employed Adagrad. The models were trained on multiple GPUs, with batch sizes varying depending on the architecture. Learning rate strategies are specified, with warm-up schedules for Transformers and varying dropout rates applied for regularization.

Results from L IBRISPEECH indicate that the best-performing models, particularly those based on Transformers, achieved a word error rate (WER) of 6.98% without decoding and 5.17% with decoding and rescoring. The paper also addresses the integration of pseudo-labeled data from L IBRIVOX, which led to further performance enhancements. Acoustic models trained on this combined dataset reached state-of-the-art WERs of 4.88% on test-other and 2.28% on test-clean without decoding, with improvements noted upon decoding and rescoring.

Overall, the findings suggest that end-to-end training methods can rival traditional bootstrapped systems, highlighting the efficacy of the proposed models and training strategies in speech recognition tasks.
