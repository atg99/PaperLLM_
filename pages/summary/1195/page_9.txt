The paper discusses the performance of various acoustic models (AMs) in automatic speech recognition (ASR), particularly focusing on the impact of language models (LMs) during decoding. It compares models trained on the LIBRISPEECH dataset with those trained using pseudo-labels from the LIBRIVOX dataset. The results indicate that the gain from LM beam-search decoding is significantly smaller for models trained on LIBRIVOX compared to those trained solely on LIBRISPEECH.

Experimental evaluations were conducted on a "shuffled transcription" task, where both unshuffled and shuffled utterances were analyzed across three audio settings to minimize bias. The findings reveal that models utilizing LIBRIVOX pseudo-labels exhibit improved acoustic representations, leading to better performance on shuffled inputs, despite both CTC and Seq2Seq models struggling with these inputs.

Further analysis shows that when sufficient unlabeled audio is available, decoding with an LM does not enhance performance, underscoring the importance of LM quality in the self-training process. The study demonstrates that while decoding with an LM provides minimal improvements for models trained on LIBRIVOX pseudo-labels, notable gains are observed for models trained exclusively on LIBRISPEECH, indicating that information from the LM has been effectively assimilated into the models trained with LIBRIVOX labels.

The paper also references related work in ASR, highlighting the evolution of deep neural networks in the field, including advancements in end-to-end models and various training methodologies. The authors employed techniques such as SpecAugment for improved regularization in their experiments, ultimately demonstrating that transformer-based models yield strong results in Seq2Seq settings.
