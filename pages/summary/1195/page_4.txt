This paper discusses the development of language models for pseudo-labeling in speech recognition, specifically utilizing word-based and word-piece models. It highlights the architecture of the models employed, including the GCN N-14B for word-piece and word-level models, and a Transformer language model structured similarly to the Google Billion Words model.

The dataset preparation outlines the use of the LIBRIVOX audio collection, which includes 72,000 hours of read English speech. After preprocessing, which involved filtering out duplicates and corrupted audio, the authors retained 53.8K hours of audio. Pseudo-labels were generated using a Transformer acoustic model trained on the LIBRISPEECH dataset, achieving a word error rate of 6.20% when decoded with a 4-gram word language model.

For the language model training, the authors utilized a text corpus from 14,500 public domain books, ensuring no overlap with the unlabeled audio to prevent embedding ground truth information. They implemented a detailed filtering process using Levenshtein distance metrics to eliminate titles that were too similar to those in LIBRIVOX. Ultimately, 13% of the original LIBRISPEECH language model corpus was filtered out. The resulting corpus was normalized and used to train a 4-gram language model with a vocabulary of 200,000 words, which will be applied during beam-search decoding alongside the acoustic model.
