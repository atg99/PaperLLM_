The paper discusses the effectiveness of a Transformer acoustic model (AM) trained with pseudo-labels generated by a decoder that integrates a language model (LM) with overlapping text from unlabeled audio. The results show that the model with overlap achieves lower word error rates (WERs) compared to one without overlap, indicating that the quality and diversity of pseudo-labels contribute significantly to performance improvements. Specifically, the pseudo-label-only model outperforms the LIBRISPEECH-only baseline, demonstrating the advantages of leveraging additional audio data.

Furthermore, the paper explores the concept that end-to-end acoustic models inherently learn language modeling capabilities. Experimental results reveal that acoustic models trained on additional audio data yield better acoustic representations, which reduces reliance on external LMs during decoding. The discussion highlights that modern convolutional architectures can effectively learn internal word representations from audio, supporting the argument that a robust LM can diminish the need for external LMs in many scenarios.

The findings suggest that the combination of well-trained acoustic models and ample pseudo-labeled data leads to significant improvements in transcription accuracy, underscoring the importance of integrating diverse training data and leveraging the inherent language modeling abilities of acoustic models.
