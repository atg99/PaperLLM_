The paper investigates the performance of various acoustic models (AMs) in automatic speech recognition (ASR) using different datasets and training methodologies. It highlights the significant improvements achieved by Transformer architectures compared to other models when trained on the L IBRISPEECH dataset. Notably, models trained on the L IBRIVOX dataset consistently outperform those on L IBRISPEECH, with a smaller performance gap between Transformer AMs and other architectures.

A key finding is that when a model trained on L IBRISPEECH is combined with a language model for generating pseudo-labels from unlabeled audio, it achieves state-of-the-art results, demonstrating a Word Error Rate (WER) of 2.28% on test-clean and 4.88% on test-other without an external language model. With language model beam-search decoding, these rates improve further to 2.09% and 4.11%, respectively.

The study emphasizes the effectiveness of semi-supervision through pseudo-labeling in bridging performance gaps among various architectures and loss functions. It suggests that with sufficient unlabeled data, different AMs, including Transformer, ResNet, and depthwise-separable convolution-based models, can achieve comparable performance regardless of the loss function used.

In the section on acoustic models, the paper details the architecture of ResNet models, which utilize multiple convolutional layers with skip connections to mitigate vanishing gradient issues. The ResNet encoder features 42 convolutional layers designed for processing audio input, emphasizing the end-to-end training capability with either Connectionist Temporal Classification (CTC) or Sequence-to-Sequence (Seq2Seq) loss functions. Overall, the findings advocate for the potential of semi-supervised learning to enhance ASR performance across diverse model setups.
