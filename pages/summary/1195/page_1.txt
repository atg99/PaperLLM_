The paper explores the application of pseudo-labeling for semi-supervised training of various modern architectures, specifically ResNet, Time-Depth Separable ConvNets, and Transformers, in the context of automatic speech recognition (ASR). The study utilizes the L IBRISPEECH dataset and incorporates additional unlabeled data from L IBRIVOX to enhance model performance.

The authors demonstrate that while Transformer-based acoustic models excel with supervised datasets, semi-supervised approaches significantly enhance all model types across different architectures and loss functions. This method effectively narrows the performance gap among the models. Notably, the paper reports achieving a new state-of-the-art for end-to-end acoustic models when decoded with an external language model in the supervised learning setting, and an absolute state-of-the-art through semi-supervised training.

The research also investigates the impact of utilizing varying amounts of unlabeled audio and proposes evaluation methods for assessing the characteristics of unlabeled audio, which contribute to improved acoustic modeling. Findings indicate that acoustic models trained with more unlabeled audio exhibit a reduced dependency on external language models.

The introduction emphasizes the advantages of end-to-end speech recognition systems, highlighting their simplicity in implementation and training compared to traditional bootstrapped systems, which often require more complex training processes. Despite challenges in optimizing acoustic models using CTC or Seq2Seq methods, the study shows that end-to-end models can achieve competitive performance, reaching a word error rate of 5.17% on the test-other dataset. Overall, the paper underscores the potential of semi-supervised learning to enhance ASR model performance without necessitating significant algorithmic changes.
