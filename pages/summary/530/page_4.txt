The paper discusses a method for optimizing clustering in unsupervised learning, specifically through a framework termed ODC (Online Distributed Clustering). It focuses on the management of pseudo labels derived from sample features stored in memory and the use of a momentum coefficient to update these features. The approach involves assigning new labels by finding the nearest centroids of classes, ensuring that centroids are updated periodically based on the features of associated samples.

To handle clustering distribution, the paper introduces loss re-weighting to prevent the training process from collapsing into a few large clusters, which is a common issue in clustering tasks. This method ensures that smaller clusters contribute more significantly to the learning process, effectively pushing the decision boundary to include more samples. Additionally, to combat the potential collapse of small clusters, the paper proposes a strategy to eliminate or merge these clusters before they become empty, enhancing the overall stability of the clustering process.

Dimensionality reduction is also addressed, where the paper notes that traditional methods like PCA are not applicable due to the varying timestamps of sample features in ODC. Instead, a non-linear head layer is introduced to reduce high-dimensional features to a manageable size, which is dynamically tuned throughout the ODC iterations.

The paper positions ODC as a complementary approach to self-supervised learning, enhancing the performance of existing methods by focusing on inter-image relationships rather than just intra-image semantics. The effectiveness of ODC is evaluated as an unsupervised fine-tuning strategy, leveraging initializations from various self-supervised learning methods.

Lastly, implementation details highlight the use of ImageNet for training, with specific data augmentation techniques to prevent the model from relying on color as a shortcut. The experiments are conducted using ResNet-50 and AlexNet as backbone architectures, providing a comparative analysis of performance.
