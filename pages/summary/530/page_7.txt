The paper presents an evaluation of the ODC (Our Deep Clustering) model in the context of low-shot SVM classification on the VOC2007 dataset, utilizing ResNet-50 as the backbone architecture. The results, as shown in Table 3, indicate that ODC achieves a mean Average Precision (mAP) of 57.1% in low-shot classification, surpassing DeepCluster (DC) by 10.2%. This highlights ODC's effectiveness in generalizing learned features for low-shot scenarios.

Further analysis reveals that ODC serves as an efficient unsupervised fine-tuning scheme, significantly enhancing the performance of various self-supervised models. Improvements in mAP for models such as Colorization, Jigsaw, and Rotation are documented, with ODC providing notable increases in performance compared to DC.

The paper also investigates the influence of hyper-parameters on ODC's performance. It demonstrates that the frequency of centroid updates does not significantly affect performance within a reasonable range. However, an excessively high threshold for minimal cluster size leads to performance degradation, indicating the importance of balancing cluster size to maintain feature learning stability.

Overall, the findings suggest that ODC not only excels in low-shot classification but also enhances the fine-tuning of self-supervised models while maintaining stability and robustness in training.
