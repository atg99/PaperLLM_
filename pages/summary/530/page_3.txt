The paper introduces Online Deep Clustering (ODC), a novel unsupervised fine-tuning scheme aimed at enhancing self-supervised learning methods. ODC differentiates itself from the conventional Deep Clustering (DC) approach by eliminating the requirement for an additional feature extraction step and allowing for the simultaneous evolution of labels and network parameters throughout the training process.

The methodology consists of four main steps per iteration: obtaining compact feature vectors from input images, retrieving pseudo-labels from a samples memory, updating the network via back-propagation, and recalculating centroids memory that represents class features. The proposed framework employs samples and centroids memories to store feature data and class centroids, which facilitates a more integrated learning process.

Additionally, the paper discusses strategies to maintain stable cluster sizes during ODC operations and outlines implementation details. ODC is presented as a flexible and unified approach to improve self-supervised learning, capable of performing unsupervised representation learning from scratch. The findings indicate that ODC can effectively boost the performance of self-supervised models by addressing challenges like label permutation and trivial shortcuts in learning.
