The paper discusses the implementation and evaluation of the Online Deep Clustering (ODC) model using AlexNet and ResNet-50 architectures. The authors modified the AlexNet architecture by removing Local Response Normalization and incorporating batch normalization layers. They trained both models from scratch with a batch size of 512 across 8 GPUs, maintaining a constant learning rate of 0.01 for AlexNet and 0.03 for ResNet-50 over 400 epochs, followed by a decay for an additional 80 epochs. The number of clusters was set to 10,000, and the centroids were updated every 10 iterations to balance learning efficacy and efficiency.

The evaluation of unsupervised features was conducted on various downstream tasks, including ImageNet classification, Places205 classification, VOC2007 SVM classification, and VOC2007 Low-shot classification. The authors re-implemented Deep Clustering (DC) for ResNet-50, ensuring fair comparisons by aligning hyper-parameters.

In ImageNet classification, ODC consistently outperformed DC across all convolutional layers, achieving a notable improvement of 6.7% in the conv1 layer. ODC achieved a top-1 accuracy of 41.4% in the conv4 layer. For ResNet-50, ODC reached 57.6% accuracy in the conv5 layer, surpassing the re-implemented DC by 5.4%.

In the Places205 classification task, ODC continued to show superior performance, especially in deeper layers, and achieved a best-performing accuracy of 49.3% in the conv5 layer. The results indicate that ODC's learned representations generalize well across different domains.

Lastly, in VOC2007 SVM classification, ODC also demonstrated significant improvements over previous methods, reinforcing the effectiveness of the proposed approach in extracting valuable features for various applications. Overall, the findings suggest that ODC provides a robust method for enhancing unsupervised representation learning in deep neural networks.
