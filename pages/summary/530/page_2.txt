The paper introduces Online Deep Clustering (ODC), a novel approach to unsupervised representation learning that addresses the limitations of Deep Clustering (DC). DC, while pioneering in scaling clustering-based representation learning, faces challenges due to the alternating mechanism of deep feature clustering and CNN parameter updates. This results in instability as label assignments may change randomly between epochs, requiring parameters to be randomly initialized, which hinders the continuity of learning.

ODC aims to enhance stability by integrating clustering and feature learning within the same iteration cycle, allowing label updates to occur in a mini-batch manner during the network's forward and backward propagation. This integration avoids the need for separate feature extraction processes, thus promoting a more consistent training flow similar to supervised learning. ODC utilizes two dynamic memory modules: one for storing sample labels and features, and another for evolving centroids, ensuring that both labels and network parameters progress together, leading to a steadier loss curve.

The authors highlight that ODC not only excels in unsupervised representation learning but also serves as an effective fine-tuning tool for models trained using other unsupervised methods. The results indicate that ODC outperforms DC in various benchmarks, showcasing its potential in jointly optimizing clustering and feature learning.

In summary, the contributions of the paper are threefold: the introduction of ODC for stable unsupervised representation learning, its role as a unified fine-tuning scheme, and the demonstration of its effectiveness across different datasets. The paper also contextualizes ODC within the broader landscape of unsupervised representation learning, including generative models and self-supervised learning approaches, while emphasizing the significance of joint clustering and feature learning methods.
