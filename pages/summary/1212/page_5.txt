The paper presents experimental results on the CelebA, Waterbirds, and Colored-MNIST datasets, focusing on the performance of different models in the presence of spurious correlations between target and bias attributes. 

In CelebA, results are shown for unbiased and worst-group accuracy across various attributes, with the proposed BPA method outperforming others in several cases. The results indicate that the model's performance varies significantly depending on the target and bias attributes, with BPA achieving notable improvements in the presence of bias.

The Waterbirds dataset, which combines bird images with background scenes, also shows BPA's effectiveness in handling spurious correlations, achieving high unbiased and worst-group accuracy.

For Colored-MNIST, BPA demonstrates strong performance, particularly in scenarios where digit and color attributes are correlated.

The implementation uses a ResNet-18 backbone pretrained on ImageNet for both CelebA and Waterbirds datasets. Overall, the findings highlight the challenges posed by spurious correlations and the effectiveness of the BPA method in addressing these issues across different datasets.
