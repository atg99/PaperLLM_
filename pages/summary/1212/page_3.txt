The paper discusses an unsupervised debiasing framework aimed at mitigating hidden stratification in machine learning models, particularly in real-world datasets like CelebA. The authors critique existing unsupervised methods that rely on clustering for subclass label estimation, noting limited performance improvements in complex datasets compared to simpler ones like MNIST. 

The proposed method focuses on bias pseudo-attribute estimation and sample reweighting. It begins by defining a target attribute and identifying potential bias attributes that may introduce spurious correlations. The model aims to predict the target attribute without these biases by estimating the intended causation. The authors highlight that traditional empirical risk minimization (ERM) can overlook minority groups and lead to biased predictions. 

To address this, they explore distributionally robust optimization (DRO) techniques, which aim to maximize unbiased generalization accuracy by considering uncertainties in the training distribution. The group DRO approach is introduced, which uses prior knowledge of group information to create more realistic test distribution sets. The formulation leads to minimizing the worst-case risk by focusing on group-specific distributions, thus improving model robustness against biases in training data. 

Overall, the paper presents a novel unsupervised debiasing strategy that leverages clustering to identify bias pseudo-attributes, ensuring more equitable model performance across diverse groups.
