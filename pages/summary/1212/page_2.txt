This paper addresses the challenge of dataset bias in machine learning, particularly in computer vision tasks, where biases can lead to spurious correlations between true labels and features. Traditional supervised debiasing methods require explicit bias annotations, which can be difficult to obtain. In contrast, this work introduces a novel unsupervised debiasing technique that leverages feature clustering and cluster reweighting to mitigate bias without the need for supervision.

The proposed method identifies bias pseudo-attributes through clustering examples with similar labels for non-target attributes. By reweighting these clusters based on their size and task-specific accuracy, the approach encourages minority clusters to contribute more significantly to the learning process, thereby enhancing worst-group generalization. 

The authors claim that their unsupervised method is competitive with supervised debiasing techniques, achieving strong performance in unbiased and worst-group accuracies. The paper is structured to first review related research, then present the proposed framework, followed by extensive empirical analysis, and concludes with a summary of findings.

Key contributions of the paper include:
- An unsupervised debiasing approach that does not require explicit bias supervision.
- A method for learning debiased representations through clustering and reweighting.
- Comprehensive experimental results demonstrating the effectiveness of the approach compared to supervised methods. 

The paper highlights the significance of addressing dataset bias to improve machine learning models' robustness and fairness in real-world applications.
