The paper discusses a method for addressing algorithmic bias in machine learning models, specifically focusing on datasets like CelebA, WaterBirds, and Colored-MNIST. The models are trained using the Adam optimizer with specified parameters, and k-means clustering is utilized for feature extraction. The evaluation protocol measures accuracy across different groups defined by target and bias attributes, reporting average and worst-group accuracies.

In the CelebA dataset analysis, the authors identify spurious correlations between gender (the bias attribute) and other target attributes, noting that 26 out of 32 attributes show such correlations. The proposed method, referred to as BPA (bias pseudo-attribute), significantly outperforms baseline models and existing methods like LfF and Group DRO in terms of unbiased and worst-group accuracies. The results indicate that BPA is competitive with supervised approaches while effectively reducing bias.

The method is also evaluated on the WaterBirds dataset, where it again achieves superior accuracy compared to unsupervised methods and is comparable to supervised methods. For the Colored-MNIST dataset, BPA maintains consistent accuracy in digit classification while also performing well in color classification, indicating robustness to algorithmic bias.

Additionally, the authors test their algorithm on unbiased datasets to confirm its reliability in the absence of bias. They find that their approach consistently performs well across multiple bias attributes, demonstrating its applicability in various contexts without prior knowledge of dataset bias. Overall, the proposed method shows promise in mitigating algorithmic bias in machine learning applications.
