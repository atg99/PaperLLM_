This paper presents an unsupervised debiasing framework using pseudo-attributes, focusing on the CelebA dataset. The authors evaluate their model's performance against Group Distributionally Robust Optimization (Group DRO) in a setting where group information is not fully available during training, specifically using gender as the specified bias attribute. Results indicate that their model outperforms Group DRO in terms of unbiased accuracy, particularly in scenarios with unspecified bias attributes, highlighting Group DRO's limitations in handling worst-case generalization.

A sensitivity analysis on the number of clusters reveals that unbiased accuracy stabilizes when the number of clusters (K) is 4 or more. Feature visualization using t-SNE demonstrates that the authors' method effectively mixes samples from different gender groups within the same target class, which is beneficial for debiasing.

The conclusion emphasizes the effectiveness of the proposed cluster-based weighting strategy for learning debiased representations, achieving competitive results compared to supervised methods. The paper also discusses the societal implications of addressing bias in machine learning, acknowledging potential blind spots in bias identification and suggesting further research into hidden biases. 

The research received support from various grants, indicating its significance in advancing AI reliability and robustness.
