The CHiME-5 dataset is designed to enhance robust automatic speech recognition (ASR) systems, comprising 50.12 hours of recorded conversations in real home environments. It includes a training set with 40.33 hours and nearly 80,000 utterances, a development set of 4.27 hours with over 7,000 utterances, and a testing set of 5.12 hours containing 11,000 utterances.

The TED-LIUM Corpus offers a substantial resource with 452 hours of TED talks and their transcriptions, serving as an open-source speech dataset.

Common Voice, initiated by Mozilla, is an open-source project that collects speech data through community contributions, amassing 2,400 hours of multilingual data, with 1,900 hours validated. It includes datasets in English, German, French, Welsh, Turkish, and 13 other languages.

The Spoken Wikipedia dataset contains 1,005 hours of audio in English, German, and Dutch. The English portion is the largest, with 395 hours from 1,339 pages, while the German dataset includes 386 hours from 1,014 pages, and the Dutch dataset has 224 hours covering 3,171 pages.

The CSTR VCTK Corpus features 400 sentences from 109 native English speakers of various ages, genders, and accents, totaling nearly 9 hours of audio data.

AISHELL-1 is an open-source dataset with 170 hours of Mandarin speech, featuring 400 unique speakers across different genders and ages, covering topics such as finance, science, technology, entertainment, and sports.

ASR architecture functions by converting sound waves from spoken speech into text, utilizing inputs from microphones or audio files.
