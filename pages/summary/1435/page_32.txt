The paper discusses various classification methods for data, particularly in the context of speech recognition. It highlights the use of Adaptive Neuro-Fuzzy Inference System (ANFIS) for recognizing isolated Persian words, where clustering was initially performed using Self-Organizing Maps (SOM) and Linear Vector Quantization (VQ). The results demonstrated that ANFIS outperformed conventional Feedforward Neural Networks (FNN). Similarly, another study achieved an accuracy of 85.24% in recognizing Malay digits using ANFIS.

The paper also addresses Support Vector Machines (SVM), noting their recent adoption in speech recognition tasks. SVM constructs a decision hyperplane that maximizes the distance between classes, utilizing various kernel functions to map input features into higher-dimensional spaces. Common kernel functions include polynomial and Gaussian radial basis functions. Despite their effectiveness as classifiers, SVMs face limitations in Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) due to their inability to handle variable input lengths and high computational costs for multi-class classification. To manage multi-class problems, two techniques are mentioned: the one-against-all method, which divides the multi-class SVM into multiple binary classifiers, and the one-against-one technique.
