The cited papers cover a range of topics related to speech recognition and processing technologies. Makino et al. (2019) discuss a recurrent neural network transducer for audio-visual speech recognition, highlighting the integration of visual input. Malekzadeh et al. (2018) focus on Persian vowel recognition using Mel-frequency cepstral coefficients (MFCC) and artificial neural networks on a specific speech dataset. Mallat (1989) introduces wavelet representation as a method for multiresolution signal decomposition, which is fundamental to various signal processing applications.

Mehla and Aggarwal (2014) provide a survey on automatic speech recognition, summarizing the advancements and methodologies in the field. Messaoud and Hamida (2010) explore parameter selection for continuous speech systems using context-dependent hidden Markov models (CDHMM). Meyer (1993) emphasizes the algorithms and applications of wavelets, while Milone and Di Persia (2008) present learning hidden Markov models with hidden Markov trees.

Modic et al. (2003) compare wavelet and MFCC features in speech recognition across different languages. Mohamadpour and Farokhi (2009) propose a novel approach for Persian speech recognition. Molau et al. (2001) detail the computation of MFCCs from power spectra, a crucial step in speech recognition. Morgan and Bourlard (1990) describe continuous speech recognition utilizing multilayer perceptrons alongside hidden Markov models.

Mporas et al. (2007) examine various speech features for recognition tasks, while Muller et al. (2006) discuss a connectionist approach to speech understanding. Nataraj et al. (2011) work on enhancing vocal tract shape estimation consistency. Nehe and Holambe (2009, 2012) investigate feature extraction techniques for digit and isolated word recognition using discrete wavelet transform and linear predictive coding.

Nguyen et al. (2010) present flat direct models for speech recognition, and Nouza et al. (2010) discuss a system for automatic collection and indexing of Czech broadcast speech. O'Shaughnessy (2008, 1988, 2003) reviews the history and challenges of automatic speech recognition and linear predictive coding techniques. Pallett et al. (1990) address resource management in speech and natural language processing.

Panayotov et al. (2015) introduce Librispeech, a corpus for automatic speech recognition based on public domain audiobooks. Paul et al. (2009) develop a Bangla speech recognition system using LPC and artificial neural networks. Paulson (2006) discusses the transition of speech recognition technology from software to hardware, while Picone (1993) examines signal modeling techniques in speech recognition. Ping et al. (2009) propose a speech recognition algorithm using wavelet analysis and neural networks, and Polikar (1996) provides a comprehensive wavelet tutorial.
