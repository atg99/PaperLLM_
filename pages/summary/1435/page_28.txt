The paper discusses various approaches to Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) using different neural network architectures, particularly focusing on Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP), Radial Basis Function (RBF) networks, and Recurrent Neural Networks (RNN). 

The equation presented describes the output of an RBF network, where H is the number of hidden layers, wh are the weights, x is the input, and Фh is the Gaussian function defined by its center ch and width σh. A comparison of MLP and RBF networks revealed that RBF achieved a higher accuracy of 98.69% compared to MLP's 96% when identifying six words spoken by different speakers. Additionally, RBF demonstrated faster training and testing speeds.

Further research integrated RBF with Hidden Markov Models (HMM) to improve continuous speech recognition. This hybrid approach resulted in an 80% accuracy rate for recognizing ten words, utilizing a specially created HMM for each word and optimizing the number of neurons in the hidden layer.

Another study combined wavelet transformation with RBF, leading to a robust ASR model. This hybrid model outperformed a simple RBF network in various environments and speaker conditions. However, it was noted that the hybrid model's accuracy declined with larger vocabularies, suggesting that simple RBF networks might be preferable for extensive word sets.

Additionally, a model utilizing temporal RBF features for Arabic letter recognition was presented, achieving a recognition rate of 98.175% after extensive preprocessing and feature extraction steps.

Lastly, the paper describes RNNs, which can operate without phonetic dictionaries, processing input sequences to generate output and hidden state vectors. The equations governing RNNs illustrate how they compute these vectors based on input and previous hidden states, showcasing their capability in handling sequential data effectively.
