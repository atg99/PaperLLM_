The paper discusses the architecture and functions of recurrent neural networks (RNNs), particularly focusing on Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) cells and the advantages of using bidirectional RNNs in speech recognition tasks. The mathematical formulations for LSTM cells are provided, detailing the roles of input, forget, and output gates, as well as the cell activation function.

A key limitation of standard RNNs is their inability to consider future context, which is crucial in applications like speech recognition. Bidirectional RNNs address this issue by processing input data in both forward and backward directions and maintaining separate hidden state vectors for each direction.

The paper also highlights the use of Hidden Markov Models (HMMs) and Connectionist Temporal Classification (CTC) as methods to align audio input with transcribed output. CTC, in particular, allows for training the model without needing pre-defined alignments, and two decoding methods are suggested: selecting the highest probability output at each time step or employing beam search, which can enhance efficiency by integrating a dictionary and language model.

Additionally, an Attention-based Transducer model is presented, incorporating pyramid LSTM layers, standard LSTM layers, and multi-head self-attention. This model demonstrated a word error rate (WER) of 10.3% and a real-time factor (RTF) of 0.19 using 10,000 hours of English speech data.

Lastly, the paper references the application of RNNs in recognizing Bengali speech through a similar layered architecture, emphasizing the effectiveness of these neural network designs in various speech recognition tasks.
