The paper discusses an algorithm used in the training phase that employs backpropagation and lateral inhibition concepts. It highlights the limitation of this model, which requires fixed-length inputs, making it less effective for dynamic speech signals and small vocabularies, thus functioning well for phoneme recognition but not for word recognition. 

Research referenced in the paper includes a study that utilized a Multilayer Perceptron (MLP) for recognizing Urdu digits, achieving 94% accuracy with a dataset from a single user in a clean environment, using FFT and MFCC for feature extraction. Another study on Persian digit recognition also employed MLP, achieving 98% accuracy after denoising with MFCC and feature extraction via Discrete Wavelet Transform (DWT), again using data from a single male speaker. Additionally, a deep MLP network was used for speech emotion recognition, utilizing the IEMOCAP database and achieving mean scores of 0.453 and 0.469 for speaker-independent and speaker-dependent data, respectively.

The paper also introduces Sparse Multilayer Perceptrons (SMLP), which are structurally similar to MLPs but require one hidden layer to produce a sparse matrix output.
