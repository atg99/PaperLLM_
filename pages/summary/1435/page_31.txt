The paper discusses the application of convolutional neural networks (CNN) and fuzzy neural networks (FNN) in speech recognition, highlighting their architectures and performance metrics. 

In CNNs, the convolutional function is represented mathematically, where the input signal is convolved with a filter to extract features. This process allows CNNs to effectively capture essential features from signals while minimizing the number of weights, thus reducing model complexity and the risk of overfitting. The pooling layer further down-samples the data by retaining dominant values, aiding generalization.

The research cited utilized a combination of CNN and Bidirectional LSTM (BLSTM) to recognize Mandarin speech, achieving a word error rate (WER) of 19.2% with the AISHELL-1 dataset. The architecture comprised multiple CNN blocks, each including convolutional, batch normalization, ReLU activation, and max pooling layers. Another study experimented with different input types (MFCC, power spectrum, and raw waveform) in a model with over 12 convolutional layers, reporting varying WERs based on the input type: 7.2% for MFCC, 9.4% for power spectrum, and 10.1% for raw waveform using the LibriSpeech dataset.

Fuzzy neural networks (FNN) integrate fuzzy logic with neural networks, employing membership functions that are particularly beneficial for mapping speech signals due to their ambiguous boundaries. FNNs show promise in achieving effective training with smaller datasets compared to traditional artificial neural networks (ANN). A study demonstrated that a hybrid model using wavelet transforms, CDHMM, and FNN outperformed CDHMM in noisy conditions by 15.2% in accuracy, although CDHMM was more effective in clean environments.

The Adaptive Neuro-Fuzzy Inference System (ANFIS) is highlighted as a prominent FNN-based approach, leveraging various fuzzy inference techniques for speech recognition tasks.
