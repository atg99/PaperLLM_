The paper discusses the application of Discrete Wavelet Transform (DWT) for analyzing speech signals through multi-resolution analysis. DWT allows for the examination of input signals at various frequency levels and resolutions, reflecting the behavior of the human auditory system. It utilizes different window sizes for low and high-frequency components; narrower windows are used for higher frequencies, while wider windows are applied to lower frequencies.

DWT separates the input speech signal into detail coefficients, which capture high-frequency components, and approximation coefficients, which represent low-frequency components. This separation is achieved through a fast pyramidal algorithm known as Mallat tree decomposition, involving high-pass and low-pass filters followed by down-sampling. The mathematical representation of this process is provided, indicating how the filters are applied to the input signal.

The study indicates that speech signals predominantly exist in the lower frequency range, suggesting that even when high-frequency components are removed, the speech remains intelligible. Research cited in the paper shows that using approximation coefficients instead of detail coefficients can lead to improved accuracy in generating octaves. DWT coefficients are formed by concatenating these coefficients from the last decomposition level, with the number of decomposition levels determined by the frame size, typically ranging from 3 to 6 octaves. The filters used in DWT computations should be quadrature mirror filters (QMF).
