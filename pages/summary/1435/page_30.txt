The paper discusses advancements in speech recognition systems, focusing on models utilizing different neural network architectures. One study employed a model with a softmax activation function, trained on 33 hours of data from 508 speakers, achieving a word error rate (WER) of 34 through techniques like dropout and connectionist temporal classification (CTC) combined with a language model. Another research introduced an audio-visual speech recognition system using a recurrent neural network (RNN) transducer, featuring five bi-directional long short-term memory (LSTM) layers in the encoder and two unidirectional LSTM layers in the decoder, with a joint space of 640 dimensions. This model, leveraging a five-layer convolutional neural network (CNN) named V2P for feature extraction from video, was trained on a dataset of 31,000 hours of transcribed YouTube videos, achieving WERs of 21.5% for audio-only and 20.5% for audio-visual systems.

Furthermore, the paper compares various RNN types, including gated recurrent units (GRU) and LSTM, against a simple RNN using the TED-LIUM Corpus for training and testing. The results indicate that LSTM outperformed other architectures with WERs of 77.55% and 65.04% for 500-node and 1000-node configurations, respectively, while noting that RNNs were the fastest to train and LSTMs required more time.

The paper also details the structure of convolutional neural networks (CNNs), typically employed in computer vision but increasingly utilized in natural language processing due to their effective feature generation and discrimination capabilities. A standard CNN architecture consists of alternating pooling and convolutional layers, culminating in fully connected layers, with convolutional layers made up of neurons acting as kernels that process input signals into smaller, manageable receptive fields.
