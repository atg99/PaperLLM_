The introduction of the paper discusses the significance of speech as a natural mode of human communication and the role of Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) systems in facilitating machine interaction through spoken input. An ideal ASR system is characterized by its ability to perceive, recognize, and process spoken words into actionable commands. However, achieving effective ASR faces challenges due to the vast variations in human speech, particularly among bilingual or multilingual speakers, and the limited availability of training resources for the extensive number of world languages.

The paper references various survey studies that have explored different facets of ASR, including the challenges associated with speech recognition such as speaker variability, vocabulary size, and channel differences. It highlights three classification approaches: acoustic-phonetic, pattern recognition, and artificial intelligence. Additionally, the text reviews the efficiency of feature extraction techniques like perceptual linear prediction and discusses the practical challenges in implementing optimal ASR systems. The importance of addressing under-resourced languages and their preservation is also emphasized, alongside an overview of hybrid ASR models and diverse recognition approaches. Overall, the paper sets the stage for a comprehensive examination of ASR systems and the ongoing research aimed at overcoming existing limitations.
