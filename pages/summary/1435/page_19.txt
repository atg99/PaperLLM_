The paper discusses the process of deriving Perceptual Linear Prediction (PLP) coefficients from an input signal, X, using Bark-scaled frequency. This frequency scale aligns with the critical band frequency selectivity of the human cochlea. After calculating the Bark-scaled frequency, it is adjusted according to the equal-loudness curve, followed by the application of the intensity-loudness power law. The process involves several signal processing techniques, including Inverse Fourier Transform (IFT), linear predictive analysis, and cepstral analysis, culminating in the extraction of PLP features, as illustrated in the provided block diagram.

The effectiveness of PLP as a feature extraction method is highlighted through two key studies. The first study utilized a Hidden Markov Model-Artificial Neural Network (HMM-ANN) system to recognize English phonemes, achieving 64.9% accuracy with the TIMIT corpus. However, accuracy decreased to 34.4% when tested on the HTIMIT corpus, which includes telephone channel speech data. The second study compared the performance of PLP with Mel-Frequency Cepstral Coefficients (MFCC) in noisy environments, specifically with white and street noise. This research demonstrated that PLP outperformed MFCC by 0.2% in accuracy when recognizing words across six languages: Hungarian, English, French, Italian, Spanish, and German.
