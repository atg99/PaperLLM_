This collection of papers focuses on various methodologies and advancements in speech recognition technology, particularly employing artificial neural networks (ANNs), hidden Markov models (HMMs), and support vector machines (SVMs). 

Sukumar et al. (2010) explore isolated question word recognition using ANNs, while Tang (2009) discusses a hybrid approach combining HMMs and ANNs for automatic speech recognition. Tang et al. (2010) make initial strides in phoneme recognition with structured SVMs. Tavanaei et al. (2011) utilize Mel-scaled discrete wavelet transforms for Persian phoneme recognition, and Thubthong and Kijsirikul (2001) apply SVMs for Thai phoneme recognition.

Toshniwal et al. (2018) present a single end-to-end model for multilingual speech recognition, while Tóth (2011) proposes a hierarchical neural network architecture for enhanced phone recognition. Trentin and Gori (2001, 2003) provide surveys and robust combinations of hybrid ANN/HMM models for speech recognition.

Umarani et al. (2009) investigate implementing HMMs and radial basis functions, and Vadwala et al. (2017) survey various speech recognition algorithms, highlighting challenges and techniques. Vapnik (2013) discusses statistical learning theory, foundational for understanding these models.

Veaux et al. (2016) introduce a multi-speaker corpus for voice cloning, and Veisi and Sameti (2011) integrate principal component analysis with cepstral mean subtraction for robust recognition. Velichko and Zagoruyko (1970) document early efforts in automatic word recognition.

Recent works by Wang et al. (2012, 2019, 2020) explore monaural features, end-to-end systems combining CNN and BLSTM, and attention-based transducers for online speech recognition. Weston and Watkins (1998, 1999) focus on multi-class SVMs, enhancing pattern recognition capabilities. Wijoyo and Wijoyo (2011) apply linear predictive coding with ANNs for mobile robot movement control.

Overall, these contributions advance the field of speech recognition through innovative combinations of established techniques, addressing various languages and applications.
