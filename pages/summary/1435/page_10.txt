The paper discusses the functionality of Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) systems, focusing on the process of converting an input sequence of audio signals, denoted as X, into an output sequence of words, Y. The primary objective is to maximize the posterior probability P(Y|X), which can be calculated using the provided formula. This formula involves three probabilities: P(W), the occurrence probability of a word; P(X), the likelihood of the input signal; and P(X|W), the probability of the acoustic signal corresponding to the word.

ASR systems are structured into four main modules: a pre-processing module, a feature extraction module, a classification model, and a language model. The process begins with capturing audio through a microphone, which often includes background noise. The pre-processing module aims to minimize the signal-to-noise ratio using various techniques such as framing, normalization, end-point detection, and pre-emphasis. The choice of pre-processing methods can vary depending on the feature extraction algorithms employed.

Following pre-processing, the clean audio signal is directed to the feature extraction module, which plays a crucial role in the performance of the classification model. The effectiveness of this module is significantly influenced by the quality of the extracted features. The paper highlights that the overall architecture of an ASR system is essential for accurate speech recognition, emphasizing the interdependence of each module in achieving optimal results.
