This collection of references covers a wide range of topics related to automatic speech recognition (ASR) and related methodologies. 

1. The Aishell-1 corpus provides an open-source resource for Mandarin speech recognition, establishing a baseline for further research (Bu et al. 2017).
2. The IEMOCAP database focuses on emotional speech recognition, offering a dataset for interactive emotional analysis (Busso et al. 2008).
3. WSOM explores adaptive wavelet construction using self-organizing maps, contributing to signal processing techniques (Campos & Carpenter 1998).
4. Chan et al. (2016) introduce a neural network model, "Listen, Attend and Spell," aimed at enhancing large vocabulary conversational speech recognition.
5. Chang et al. (2008) propose a convex optimization method for estimating parameters in large-margin continuous density hidden Markov models (CDHMM).
6. Chen et al. (2005) discuss speech feature smoothing to improve ASR robustness.
7. The performance of front-end processing for speech recognition systems is evaluated by Cheng et al. (2005).
8. Chiu et al. (2018) present state-of-the-art results in speech recognition using sequence-to-sequence models.
9. Chow et al. (1987) detail the BYBLOS continuous speech recognition system and its performance metrics.
10. The n-best algorithm for efficient sentence hypothesis generation is introduced by Chow and Schwartz (1989).
11. Clarkson and Moreno (1999) investigate the application of support vector machines in phonetic classification.
12. Coifman et al. (1992) discuss wavelet analysis within the context of signal processing.
13. Collobert et al. (2016) present Wav2letter, an end-to-end convolutional network-based ASR system.
14. Cortes and Vapnik (1995) introduce support-vector networks, foundational to machine learning applications in speech.
15. Crouse et al. (1998) explore wavelet-based statistical signal processing using hidden Markov models.
16. Cutajar et al. (2013) conduct a comparative study on various ASR techniques.
17. Davis et al. (1952) discuss the automatic recognition of spoken digits, marking an early milestone in ASR.
18. Deshmukh and Picone (1995) focus on methodologies for language modeling in continuous speech recognition.
19. Du and He (2006) examine clustering solutions for speech recognition models using self-organizing maps.
20. Dumitru and Gavat (2006) compare feature extraction methods for continuous speech recognition in Romanian.
21. Fontaine et al. (1996) utilize nonlinear discriminant analysis with neural networks for speech recognition.
22. Forsberg (2003) addresses the challenges in speech recognition, providing insights into its complexities.

Overall, these studies collectively advance the field of speech recognition, highlighting various methodologies, datasets, and theoretical frameworks that contribute to improved ASR systems.
