The paper discusses various methods for feature extraction in audio processing, highlighting Mel-frequency Cepstral Coefficients (MFCC) as a prominent technique. MFCC is essential due to its alignment with human auditory perception, which is non-linear, particularly in the frequency range of 0 to 1000 Hz. The Mel-scale, developed in the 1940s, translates frequencies to a linear representation suitable for audio applications.

To process audio signals, they are segmented into small overlapping frames, typically 25 ms or 30 ms in duration. Each frame undergoes multiplication with a Hamming window, followed by a Discrete Fourier Transform (DFT) or Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) to expedite processing. The Fourier transform results are used to compute a filter bank, which in turn generates log energy outputs.

The final step involves applying the Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) to these log energy outputs, yielding the MFCCs that effectively reduce dimensionality while preserving significant energy information. The paper emphasizes the importance of accounting for temporal correlations between frames, as the influence of phonemes can span multiple frames, which is crucial for achieving accurate results in audio analysis.
