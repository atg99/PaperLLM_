The paper discusses various techniques for implementing Support Vector Machines (SVM) in multi-class classification, particularly in speech recognition. It highlights two primary approaches: one-against-all and one-against-one. The one-against-all technique generates a smaller number of binary SVM classifiers but requires extensive training data for each. This method faces the unclassifiable region problem, which can be addressed using continuous decision functions or fuzzy SVM. While both methods are comparable in performance evaluation, continuous decision functions are simpler to implement.

A comparative study between SVM and Artificial Neural Networks (ANN) is presented, where SVM, utilizing the one-against-all technique with the Radial Basis Function (RBF) kernel, outperformed a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) network in recognizing isolated vowels in Thai, achieving an accuracy of 87.08% compared to 82.72% for the MLP.

The one-against-one technique, in contrast, creates binary SVMs for every possible class pair. Although it results in a higher number of classifiers, it requires less training data and is computationally efficient as classifiers can be disregarded if their corresponding classes are rarely distinguished. An example of this technique is illustrated in a phoneme classification system that utilized Mel-Frequency Cepstral Coefficients (MFCC) for feature extraction and achieved an accuracy of 77.6%, surpassing a Hidden Markov Model (HMM) which recorded 73.7% accuracy.

In summary, while HMM has been a prevalent method for modeling speech due to its effectiveness in capturing temporal information, SVM and ANN have emerged as viable alternatives or enhancements, often employed either independently or in hybrid configurations with HMM. The paper notes a shift from Multi-Layer Perceptron to more advanced models like Recurrent Neural Networks (RNN) and RBF in contemporary applications.
