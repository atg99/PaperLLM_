The section on decoding techniques outlines methods used to determine the most likely word sequence from spoken utterances by employing an acoustic model, a language model, and the spoken input. The text highlights the impracticality of exhaustive enumeration due to the exponential growth of possible outputs and mentions pruning algorithms as a solution for efficiency.

Viterbi search is discussed as a common technique that compares hypotheses directly but is computationally expensive. Consequently, Viterbi beam search is preferred as it narrows the search space by focusing on hypotheses within a specific likelihood radius. An example from research demonstrates the use of the Viterbi algorithm in an HMM-based speaker-independent ASR system, achieving a performance improvement from 60.1% to 92.2% through effective use of a dictionary and word pair techniques.

N-best search is another technique that offers multiple hypotheses, but it is noted that shorter hypotheses may be favored, leading to potential inaccuracies. To mitigate this issue, search and pruning methods are commonly applied.

In conclusion, the text emphasizes the importance of language models for large vocabulary systems and notes ongoing research aimed at optimizing these models, with n-gram and n-best search being prevalent methods.
