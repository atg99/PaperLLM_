The paper discusses the integration of Hidden Markov Models (HMM) with wavelet transforms to enhance performance in applications such as speech recognition. It introduces the Hidden Markov Tree (HMT) model, which establishes Markov structures among wavelet coefficients to account for dependencies that are typically overlooked when wavelets are treated independently. The HMT model demonstrated superior performance compared to traditional wavelet-based algorithms, particularly in tasks like speech signal de-noising and classification.

The paper also highlights an enhanced version of the HMT for feature extraction and introduces the Continuous Density Hidden Markov Model (CDHMM), which employs a maximum likelihood algorithm for training and recognizing HMMs. CDHMM can capture variations within and between phonemes more effectively and can be improved further with large margin classifiers, resulting in reduced error rates compared to conventional machine learning techniques.

Moreover, the paper examines Artificial Neural Networks (ANN) as powerful classifiers for pattern recognition, noting their effectiveness with unknown data. However, it points out challenges such as overfitting and local minima issues, which can be mitigated through Hybrid HMM-ANN models that leverage the strengths of both approaches. Among the ANNs discussed, Multilayer Perceptrons (MLP) are emphasized for their efficiency and widespread use in various applications.
