This paper discusses the use of cepstral coefficients, specifically Linear Predictive Cepstral Coefficients (LPCC) and Perceptual Linear Prediction (PLP), in the context of speech recognition. The equations presented describe the calculation of LPCC coefficients based on the number of samples in the analysis frame. 

Recent studies highlighted in the paper compare the performance of LPCC with Mel-frequency Cepstral Coefficients (MFCC). One study demonstrated that both LPCC and MFCC performed similarly in identifying twelve Hindi words from five different speakers. Another research indicated that LPCC was 10% more efficient and 5.5% faster than MFCC.

PLP is introduced as an algorithm that leverages characteristics of human auditory perception. It features three main elements: spectral resolution of critical bands, the intensity-loudness power law, and equal loudness curve reduction. By transforming the frequency axis to the Bark scale, PLP achieves a critical band spectrum approximation, which effectively integrates energy in these bands. The algorithm enhances sensitivity to mid-frequency ranges in human speech by suppressing extremes in high and low frequencies. 

The process for calculating PLP coefficients involves windowing the input signal, applying a Fast Fourier Transform (FFT), and converting the resulting signal into the Bark scale. The paper concludes with a block diagram illustrating the LPCC process, which includes steps such as framing, windowing, auto-correlation, LPC analysis, and cepstral analysis.
