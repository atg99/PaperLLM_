The paper discusses the process of feature extraction and classification in automatic speech recognition (ASR) systems. The feature extraction module generates coefficients from speech signals using methods such as Mel-frequency cepstral coefficients (MFCCs), linear predictive coding (LPC), and discrete wavelet transform (DWT). This module must be robust against noise and echo.

The classification model predicts text based on the features extracted. Two main approaches are outlined: the generative approach, which uses joint probability distributions with models like Hidden Markov Models (HMM) and Gaussian Mixture Models (GMM), and the discriminative approach, which involves parametric models like Support Vector Machines (SVM) and Artificial Neural Networks (ANN). Hybrid models combining these approaches, such as HMM and ANN, are also mentioned.

The paper further elaborates on the language model, which consists of rules and semantics necessary for recognizing phonemes and forming coherent words or sentences. Although modern ASRs can function without language models, their efficiency is enhanced when such models are incorporated.

ASR systems can be classified based on several factors, including speaker models, vocabulary, channel variability, and speaking style. Specifically, speaker-independent models are designed to recognize speech from multiple speakers without prior training on specific users, making them complex yet flexible for real-world applications.
