The paper discusses various feature extraction techniques used in Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR), focusing on the advantages of Discrete Wavelet Transform (DWT) and Wavelet Packet Transform (WPT). DWT is highlighted for its robustness to noise, allowing for effective signal decomposition without significant loss of information. Research shows that DWT outperforms traditional spectral analysis methods like Mel Frequency Cepstral Coefficients (MFCC) in phoneme recognition, achieving a notable reduction in word error rates.

WPT, while similar to DWT, offers a more detailed decomposition but has shown lower performance in specific language recognition tasks compared to DWT. The paper emphasizes the shift towards incorporating temporal analysis techniques like wavelet transforms, as spectral analysis alone is insufficient for optimal information extraction from speech signals.

Storage space considerations lead to a preference for DWT due to its smaller feature vector size. Other techniques, such as Principal Component Analysis (PCA), Vector Quantization (VQ), and Linear Discriminative Analysis (LDA), can enhance feature extraction when combined with DWT or MFCC to improve system robustness and reduce dimensionality.

The choice of feature extraction technique also depends on the deployment environment. While MFCC performs well in clean conditions, DWT and related techniques excel in noisy environments. Combining MFCC with DWT or employing RASTA-PLP can enhance robustness in varying conditions. The paper concludes with a summary table outlining the pros and cons of the discussed techniques.
