This paper discusses a robust policy gradient method for reinforcement learning, focusing on convergence properties and the derivation of gradients for a smoothed version of the policy. 

The authors establish that the sequences generated by their robust policy gradient algorithm converge almost surely to a stationary point, demonstrating that any sequence {θt} generated by their approach will ultimately belong to a set of global optimal points. Specifically, they reference Lemma A.4 to conclude that the sequence {θt} converges to the global optimum of the objective function Jρ. 

The paper then transitions to the proof of Theorem 5.1, where the authors derive the gradient of the smoothed objective function Jσ. They begin by relating the value function Vπθσ to the action-value function Qπθσ. Through a series of calculations involving the gradient of these functions, they arrive at an expression for the gradient of the objective function that incorporates both the policy and the LSE (Log-Sum-Exp) function, which is critical for ensuring robustness in the learning process.

Overall, the paper provides a rigorous framework for understanding the behavior of robust reinforcement learning algorithms and their optimization properties, emphasizing the theoretical underpinnings that guarantee convergence to optimal solutions.
