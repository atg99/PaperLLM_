The paper discusses the development and evaluation of robust reinforcement learning algorithms, specifically focusing on policy gradient methods. It presents empirical results comparing robust policy gradients with non-robust counterparts across various problems, such as the Garnet and Taxi problems.

Figures illustrate the performance of these algorithms under different settings, highlighting that the robust policy gradient performs better as the noise parameter σ increases, confirming that a larger σ approximates the robust policy gradient more effectively. The robust actor-critic algorithm is also introduced, demonstrating superior performance in terms of accumulated discounted rewards compared to traditional actor-critic methods.

A comparison with other robust reinforcement learning approaches, specifically robust adversarial reinforcement learning (RARL) and adversarially robust policy learning (ARPL), shows that the proposed robust actor-critic algorithm outperforms RARL in terms of resilience to model mismatches. The results indicate that the robust methods provide significant advantages in scenarios with potential state transition perturbations, establishing their effectiveness in enhancing the robustness of reinforcement learning agents.
