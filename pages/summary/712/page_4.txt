The paper introduces a robust policy gradient method for reinforcement learning, emphasizing the importance of the Fréchet sub-differential to handle non-differentiable scenarios in the robust context. The authors assume a differentiable and Lipschitz policy class, which allows for the derivation of the robust policy gradient through Theorem 3.3. This theorem provides a way to compute the (sub-)gradient of the robust objective function Jρ(θ), leveraging the robust Q-function, which differs from traditional approaches that rely solely on the vanilla Q-function.

The paper highlights the challenges of exploration in policy gradient methods, particularly when the initial state distribution is overly concentrated. To address this, the authors propose an optimization measure that enables effective exploration while still converging to the desired optimal solution.

Algorithm 1 is presented as a robust policy gradient algorithm that can be applied to any differentiable and Lipschitz parameterized policy class, designed to iteratively update the policy parameters based on the computed robust policy gradient.

The authors further assert the global optimality of their approach under direct parameterization, showing that the robust objective function satisfies the PL condition. This provides a theoretical foundation for the convergence of their algorithm to stationary points and suggests that proximity to these points correlates with closeness to the global optimum.
