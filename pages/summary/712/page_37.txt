This paper discusses a policy gradient method aimed at achieving robust reinforcement learning. The authors derive bounds for specific terms in their equations using the mean value theorem and properties of stochastic processes. They establish a relationship between different components of the method, specifically focusing on the behavior of the bias term and its implications for convergence.

The main results indicate that the bias of the gradient estimator can be bounded, which is crucial for understanding the convergence properties of the algorithm. The authors demonstrate that the convergence of their proposed algorithm can be framed as a projected stochastic gradient descent, with bounds on bias that depend on certain parameters such as the estimate of the policy and the exploration noise.

Furthermore, the paper highlights the global convergence of the robust actor-critic approach in a tabular setting. By defining specific stochastic gradient maps and leveraging properties of smoothness in the cost function, the authors derive conditions that ensure the algorithm steadily decreases the objective function over iterations.

Overall, the findings contribute to the understanding of robust reinforcement learning methods, particularly in how to manage bias in policy gradient estimates and ensure convergence in practical applications.
