This paper presents a robust actor-critic algorithm for reinforcement learning, focusing on enhancing the robustness of policy gradient methods. The key algorithm detailed, Algorithm 4, involves initializing parameters and iteratively refining the policy through a series of sampled trajectories and gradient updates. The algorithm incorporates a smoothed robust actor-critic approach, promising convergence to a global optimum with specific sample complexity bounds.

Theorem 6.1 establishes that under certain parameter settings, the difference between the expected objective function and the optimal value is bounded above by a small constant, indicating the effectiveness of the method in achieving robust performance. The sample complexity required for the robust temporal difference learning (Algorithm 5) is also highlighted, with implications for the overall algorithm's performance.

Numerical experiments are conducted to demonstrate the convergence and robustness of the proposed methods, comparing the robust policy gradient with traditional vanilla policy gradient approaches. Results from testing on the Garnet problem and the Taxi environment indicate that while both methods perform similarly when robustness parameters are zero, the robust policy gradient significantly outperforms the vanilla method under various robustness conditions. This is illustrated through graphical representations of their performance across multiple iterations.

Additionally, the paper discusses the smoothed robust policy gradient's performance, demonstrating its effectiveness in approximating the robust policy gradient with varying smoothing parameters. The findings underscore the advantages of the robust approach in reinforcement learning settings, particularly in achieving stability and improved performance in uncertain environments.
