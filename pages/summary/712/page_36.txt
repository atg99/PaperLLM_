The paper discusses a Policy Gradient Method aimed at enhancing robustness in reinforcement learning. The focus is on deriving bounds for specific terms within the policy gradient, particularly those involving the behavior policy and the target policy. 

The first term evaluated is the difference between the expected values under the behavior policy and the target policy. This term is bounded using a combination of the distribution of states and the expected rewards, leading to an inequality that incorporates the action space size and an estimate of the robustness parameter.

The second term involves a more complex expression that requires breaking it down into components. It highlights the differences between the expected returns of the target policy and the behavior policy. By applying the mean-value theorem, the authors derive an upper bound for one of the components, which shows that the difference can be controlled by a function of the robustness parameter and the expected value of returns.

Overall, the paper presents a systematic approach to bounding policy gradients in the context of robust reinforcement learning, emphasizing the importance of these bounds in ensuring stability and performance in reinforcement learning algorithms.
