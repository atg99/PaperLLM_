The paper presents a detailed examination of policy gradient methods in the context of robust reinforcement learning, focusing on the sub-gradient of the value function and its implications for the optimization of the policy. 

The analysis begins with the derivation of the limit involving the value function, showing that the sub-gradient \( \hat{\phi}_s(\theta) \) belongs to the sub-differential of the value function \( V^\pi_\theta(s) \) for any policy parameter \( \theta \). The objective function \( J_\rho(\theta) \) is defined as the expected value of the policy across state distribution \( \rho(s) \). The paper derives the sub-gradient \( \partial J_\rho(\theta) \), demonstrating that it encompasses contributions from different states and actions, ultimately leading to the expression \( \psi_\rho(\theta) \) which is shown to be part of the sub-gradient of \( J_\rho(\theta) \).

In the derivation of the sub-gradient \( \hat{\phi}_s(\theta) \), the authors leverage the Lipschitz continuity of the maximum value function and Rademacher's theorem, establishing that the maximum value function is differentiable almost everywhere. This leads to the conclusion that the sub-gradient can be expressed in terms of the gradients of the policy and the action-value function \( Q^\pi_\theta(s, a) \).

The paper emphasizes the recursive nature of the sub-gradient calculations, revealing how the contributions from future states and actions are integrated into the overall sub-gradient of the value function. This comprehensive approach enables robust reinforcement learning by effectively capturing the nuances of policy optimization in uncertain environments.
