The paper discusses the Policy Gradient Method for Robust Reinforcement Learning, focusing on establishing the Lipschitz smoothness of the policy function and demonstrating that the smoothed objective function satisfies the Polyak-Lojasiewicz (PL) condition.

The analysis begins with an inequality involving two parameterized policies, pθ1 and pθ2, which shows that the difference in their associated value functions can be bounded. This is derived from the Lipschitz continuity of the policy function, where it is established that the norm of the gradient of pθ is bounded, leading to a controlled difference between the policies.

Through a series of inequalities, the paper demonstrates that the difference in the expected values of the policies can be expressed in terms of the parameters θ1 and θ2. Specifically, the Lipschitz constant for the gradient of the objective function is derived, which combines the bounds on the value function and the policy's sensitivity to parameter changes.

The proof then transitions to showing that the smoothed objective function Jσ(θ) adheres to the PL condition. This is done by expanding the difference in value functions under the two policies and relating it to the differences in their respective action distributions and Q-values. The analysis highlights the role of the smoothed value functions and their gradients in establishing the necessary conditions for convergence in policy optimization.

Overall, the paper contributes to the understanding of robust reinforcement learning by providing theoretical foundations for the stability and convergence properties of policy gradient methods under perturbations.
