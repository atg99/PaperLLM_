This paper presents a proof concerning the convergence and global optimality of a robust policy gradient method in reinforcement learning. It begins by establishing that the difference between the expected return under a policy and the optimal return is bounded by a term involving the inner product of the policy gradient and the function ψµ(θ). The proof leverages standard results from stochastic differential inclusions, relying on several key assumptions about the properties of the algorithm.

The convergence of the sequence generated by the stochastic algorithm is established under certain conditions, including upper hemicontinuity and convex-compact values of the set-valued map, appropriate step-size sequences, and the characteristics of stationary points. The paper emphasizes that the robust policy gradient method can be interpreted as a descent method utilizing Clarke sub-gradients, which are shown to satisfy the required properties.

A crucial lemma demonstrates that for any parameter θ where the Clarke sub-gradient is zero, the expected return equals the optimal return, confirming that such points are global optima. The final verification of assumptions reveals that the set of stationary points is indeed a subset of global optimal points, consolidating the findings of the theorem.

Overall, the paper articulates a comprehensive framework for understanding the robustness of policy gradient methods in reinforcement learning, highlighting the conditions under which convergence and optimality can be assured.
