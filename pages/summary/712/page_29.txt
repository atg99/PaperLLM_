The paper presents a robust reinforcement learning approach utilizing policy gradient methods. It derives inequalities that compare the value functions of two policies, πθ1 and πθ2, through a series of mathematical manipulations. The key findings are based on bounding the differences between the expected value functions and the action-value functions under these policies.

The analysis starts with the formulation of the expected values and their relationship, using the definitions of Qπσ and LSE (log-sum-exp) functions. By applying recursive steps, the authors show that the difference in value functions can be bounded by a combination of terms that include the differences in action probabilities and expected rewards across states. 

The paper highlights that the maximum value functions can be approximated, leading to tighter bounds on the differences between policies. The results culminate in a final inequality that provides a comprehensive comparison between the two policies across all states, emphasizing the robustness of the proposed method in reinforcement learning scenarios.

Overall, the findings contribute to the understanding of policy performance in uncertain environments, offering insights into how different policies can be evaluated and compared in terms of their expected returns.
