The paper discusses a policy gradient method for robust reinforcement learning, focusing on the unbiased estimation of the gradient of the expected return function, denoted as ∇Jσ. The lemma presented establishes that if the action-value function Qt equals the function Qθtσ, then the gradient estimate gt is unbiased. This is formalized as E[gt|Ft] = ∇Jσ(θt), where Ft represents the σ-field generated by prior randomness.

The proof begins by noting that the time variable Ti follows a geometric distribution, allowing for the calculation of transition probabilities between states based on the policy πθ. The expectation of a certain function Bi is derived, leading to a representation involving the state distribution ρ and the action-value function Qt.

Further, the paper outlines the probability distribution of states at time zero, which is expressed in terms of a value function Vt. This leads to another expectation Di, incorporating the action gradients and the action-value function.

By combining the derived expressions for expectations Bi and Di, the paper concludes that the expected gradient gi under the σ-field Ft equals the gradient of the expected return, thereby proving the lemma. This establishes the foundation for using policy gradient methods in robust reinforcement learning contexts, ensuring that the estimates of gradients are statistically valid and can be effectively utilized in learning algorithms.
