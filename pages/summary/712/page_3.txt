The paper discusses a robust reinforcement learning approach using policy gradient methods in the context of Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) and introduces the concept of robust MDPs that account for uncertainty in state transitions. An MDP is defined by its state and action spaces, a transition kernel, a cost function, and a discount factor. The goal is to minimize the expected accumulated discounted cost by finding an optimal policy.

In robust MDPs, the transition kernel is not fixed but belongs to an uncertainty set, allowing for variations in state transitions. The paper specifically focuses on the (s, a)-rectangular uncertainty set and introduces the R-contamination uncertainty set, where transitions can be perturbed with a small probability. This model is particularly relevant for scenarios with random perturbations and adversarial attacks, making it suitable for robust learning applications.

The robust value function is defined as the worst-case expected accumulated cost when following a policy under the worst-case transition kernel. The paper derives the robust Bellman operator and establishes that the policy gradient method can be utilized to minimize the expected worst-case cost.

To address challenges related to differentiability in the robust value function due to the max operation over transition kernels, the authors propose using the Fréchet sub-differential. This approach enables the derivation of a robust policy gradient, facilitating the optimization process in robust reinforcement learning settings. The paper aims to provide a framework for developing policies that can withstand uncertainties in MDPs while maintaining effective learning and decision-making capabilities.
