The paper discusses a policy gradient method aimed at enhancing robustness in reinforcement learning. It specifically addresses the bias introduced when using an estimated value function Qt instead of the true value function Qθtσ in the context of Algorithm 6. The authors derive an upper bound on this bias through Lemma C.3, which states that if the condition ∥QTc−Qθtσ∥∞≤ϵest is met, then the expected gradient estimate E[gi_t|Ft] deviates from the true gradient ∇Jσ(θt) by a bound dependent on the estimation error ϵest and other parameters.

The proof begins by expressing the expected bias in terms of the difference between the estimated and true value functions. By utilizing properties of the policy gradient and bounding the differences between the estimated and true value functions, the authors establish that the bias of Bi_t is bounded by O(ϵest). Furthermore, the bias of Di_t is examined, leading to a more complex expression that incorporates the expected values of the policy and value functions.

Overall, the results indicate that careful management of estimation errors is crucial in maintaining the accuracy of the policy gradient estimates, which is essential for the effectiveness of robust reinforcement learning algorithms.
