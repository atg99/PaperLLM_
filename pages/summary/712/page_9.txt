This paper presents a robust reinforcement learning (RL) method utilizing policy gradient techniques. The authors compare their robust policy gradient (PG) algorithm with the Adversarial Robust Policy Learning (ARPL) method in the context of the Taxi and G(10,8) environments. The ARPL approach involves introducing random perturbations to the observed state during training, allowing the algorithm to update the policy based on these perturbed samples. 

In their experiments, the authors set a perturbation probability equal to R and generate multiple trajectories to estimate action-value functions and visitation distributions. The results, illustrated in figures, show that the robust PG algorithm consistently outperforms ARPL, achieving higher rewards under worst-case scenarios, indicating greater robustness to model uncertainties.

The paper emphasizes the scalability of their robust algorithms, which can be applied to problems with large state and action spaces, and discusses the potential for extending the analysis to other policy parameterizations. The study focuses on the R-contamination model for uncertainty sets, which can relate to other measures like total variation and Kullback-Leibler divergence. Future work may explore model-free approaches for different uncertainty sets.

The research is supported by the National Science Foundation, and references include various foundational works in reinforcement learning and related methodologies.
