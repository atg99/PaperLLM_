The paper discusses a policy gradient method designed for robust reinforcement learning. It introduces a mathematical framework for evaluating the robustness of policies in the context of reinforcement learning, characterized by the function lθ(s). The authors derive key inequalities and establish relations that lead to a performance bound for the policy gradient, showing that the difference between the achieved performance Jσ(θ) and the optimal performance J∗σ can be bounded in terms of the complexity of the policy and other parameters.

The proof of Theorem 5.5 focuses on the convergence rate of the proposed smoothed robust policy gradient algorithm. It utilizes a lemma from existing literature to demonstrate that the gradient mapping remains within a specified norm, allowing for the establishment of a convergence rate. The authors leverage the Polyak-Lojasiewicz (PL) condition to relate the performance gap to the gradient behavior across iterations of the algorithm. 

The results indicate that by appropriately choosing the step size and the number of iterations, one can ensure that the convergence to an optimal policy occurs within a controlled rate, as evidenced by the derived inequalities. The paper concludes by emphasizing the significance of these findings in enhancing the robustness of reinforcement learning frameworks.
