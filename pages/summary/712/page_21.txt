The paper discusses a policy gradient method aimed at enhancing robustness in reinforcement learning. The authors derive expressions for the value function differences between two policies, Vπθ and Vπθ∗, highlighting the bounds on these differences. Specifically, they present a formulation that links these value functions to the distribution of states and actions under different policies, employing the concept of advantage functions Qπθ(s, a).

Key equations illustrate how the behavior of the policy gradient can be affected by the distribution of states, denoted by dπθ(s), and the advantages of actions under various policies. The paper emphasizes the importance of minimizing the difference between the value functions of the current and optimal policies, Jρ(θ) and J∗, respectively.

Notably, the authors introduce the ratios of certain distributions, lθ(s) and l∗θ(s), which serve as critical components in bounding the performance gap between the policies. These distributions are derived from the expected rewards and the transition dynamics of the reinforcement learning environment.

The findings suggest that through careful parameterization and analysis of the policy gradient, it is possible to achieve more robust learning outcomes in reinforcement learning frameworks. The methodology outlined provides a structured approach to assess and improve policy performance, ensuring a more reliable reinforcement learning process.
