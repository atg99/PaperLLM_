The main argument is that the absolute difference between the two value functions can be expressed in terms of the distribution of states and the action probabilities of the policies. The paper shows that under certain assumptions, including bounded costs and Lipschitz continuity of the policies, the difference can be controlled by a constant multiplied by the distance between the policy parameters.

The analysis leads to a conclusion that both the action-value functions Qπθ(s, a) and the objective function Jρ(θ) are Lipschitz continuous with respect to the policy parameters. This provides a foundation for ensuring stability and robustness in reinforcement learning algorithms, as small changes in the policy parameters result in proportionally small changes in the value functions and objectives.

Overall, the findings support the robustness of the proposed policy gradient method, allowing for effective learning in environments where the policies may vary slightly without significantly impacting performance.
