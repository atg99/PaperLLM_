The paper presents a robust reinforcement learning approach centered around a policy gradient method, specifically addressing the challenges associated with non-differentiable objective functions and min-max problem structures. The main results are encapsulated in key theorems that establish the global optimality of the proposed algorithms.

The authors introduce a performance measure, denoted as ρ, and establish a relationship between the objective function Jρ(θ) and a stationary point of Jµ, indicating that the global optimality of Jρ(θ) can be achieved under specific conditions. Theorem 4.2 asserts that if certain conditions regarding the step size are met, then the algorithm converges to a global optimum almost surely.

The paper further explores a smoothed robust policy gradient method, which approximates the original non-differentiable objective function. This is achieved using the LogSumExp operator, which allows for a contraction property in the Bellman operator, leading to a unique fixed point. The smoothed objective function Jσ(θ) is defined, and it is shown that as σ approaches infinity, Jσ converges to J.

The authors derive the gradient of the smoothed objective function, providing a basis for the design of the smoothed robust policy gradient algorithm. Theorem 5.2 confirms that this smoothed objective satisfies the PL-condition, reinforcing the global optimality of the proposed algorithm under direct policy parameterization.

Overall, the paper contributes to the field of reinforcement learning by providing robust methods that ensure convergence to global optima, even in the presence of complex, non-differentiable objectives.
