This paper presents a policy gradient method designed for robust reinforcement learning, focusing on the convergence properties of the algorithm and its application in practical scenarios. The authors establish that if the gradient of the objective function is small, the policy parameter will be near the global optimum. By selecting a sufficiently large smoothing parameter, the difference between the smoothed objective and the true optimal value can be minimized, thus ensuring convergence to the global optimum.

The paper further analyzes the convergence rate of the proposed algorithm under specific smoothness assumptions about the policy parameterization. It is demonstrated that the convergence to an epsilon-global optimum occurs within a complexity of O(epsilon^-3) steps. This rate is slower compared to non-robust policy gradient methods due to the added robustness requirements and the smoothing technique.

Additionally, the authors address the practical limitations of having full knowledge of robust value functions and visitation distributions. They propose a robust actor-critic algorithm that utilizes a robust temporal difference learning method to estimate the robust value function, allowing for effective learning in a model-free setting. The convergence proof for this robust TD algorithm is provided, and the robust actor-critic algorithm is shown to be applicable with various differentiable value function approximations.

Overall, the paper contributes to the field of robust reinforcement learning by providing theoretical insights into convergence rates and practical algorithms that can be implemented without complete knowledge of the underlying transition kernels.
