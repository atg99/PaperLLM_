The paper discusses a robust reinforcement learning framework utilizing policy gradient methods. It begins by establishing a new cost function, c′(s, a), which incorporates a term related to the logarithm of the state space size. The robust action-value function corresponding to this cost function is shown to be non-negative, implying that both the smoothed robust value functions, denoted Qπσ and Vπσ, are also non-negative.

The paper further derives upper bounds for these value functions, indicating that they are constrained within a defined range. Specifically, it establishes that Qπσ(s, a) and Vπσ(s) are bounded by a constant Cσ, which is a function of the discount factor and the logarithmic term.

Moreover, the authors prove that the function B(s, θ) is Lipschitz continuous, providing a bound on the gradient of Vπθσ. This is crucial for ensuring stability during the optimization process. The paper also discusses the implications of Lipschitz continuity for the action-value functions, establishing a relationship between the differences in these functions and the parameters θ1 and θ2.

Lastly, the paper references previous works to relate total variation distance between different policies, linking it to the Lipschitz property of the value functions. The results provide a foundation for understanding the robustness of policy gradient methods in reinforcement learning settings, emphasizing the importance of boundedness and continuity in the optimization landscape.
