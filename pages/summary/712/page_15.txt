The paper discusses a policy gradient method aimed at achieving robustness in reinforcement learning. It focuses on deriving equations that capture the differences in value functions between two policies, denoted as πθ1 and πθ2. The derivations involve a series of mathematical expressions that account for state transitions, action distributions, and the expected rewards under different policies.

Key components include:

1. The use of a discount factor γ and a robustness parameter R, which influence the expected values and the overall convergence of the policies.
2. The equations illustrate how the difference in value functions can be expressed through a combination of state transition probabilities and action distributions, leading to a formulation that emphasizes the impact of both policies on the expected rewards.
3. The paper further simplifies the expressions by recognizing certain recursive relationships and the structure of the expected visitation distribution, ultimately providing a clearer understanding of how the policies interact over time.

The findings suggest that by leveraging these formulations, the proposed policy gradient method can enhance the robustness of reinforcement learning systems, enabling them to perform more reliably across varying scenarios. The emphasis on mathematical rigor underlines the method's theoretical foundation while aiming for practical applicability in reinforcement learning tasks.
