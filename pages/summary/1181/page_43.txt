This paper discusses two-step models for semi-supervised learning, particularly focusing on Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) as proposed by Kingma et al. in 2014. The first step involves training a VAE on both labeled and unlabeled data to derive meaningful latent representations, serving as an unsupervised preprocessing step. In the second step, the VAE is augmented with a label vector that incorporates one-hot-encoded true labels for the labeled data, treating these labels as additional latent variables for the unlabeled data. A classification network is then integrated to predict labels based on these latent representations.

The paper also contrasts inductive and transductive methods within semi-supervised learning. Inductive algorithms aim to create a classifier that generalizes across the entire input space, allowing a clear distinction between training and testing phases. In contrast, transductive algorithms focus solely on producing predictions for the specific set of unlabeled data given to the algorithm, without a separate testing phase. These methods typically define a graph representing the pairwise similarities among all data points, both labeled and unlabeled, and optimize an objective function to ensure that predicted labels for labeled data match true labels, while similar data points receive consistent predictions. This approach leads to the development of graph-based methods, which are closely related to inductive manifold-based techniques but differ in their operational scope. The discussion emphasizes that transductive methods are driven by specific optimization criteria rather than theoretical interpretations of manifolds.
