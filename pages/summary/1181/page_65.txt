The referenced works cover a wide range of topics in machine learning, particularly focusing on semi-supervised learning, feature selection, and neural network architectures. Key contributions include:

1. **Random Forests**: Oshiro et al. discuss the optimal number of trees in random forests, a crucial aspect for balancing model complexity and performance.
   
2. **Sentiment Analysis**: Pang and Lee introduce methods for sentiment analysis using subjectivity summarization, emphasizing the importance of capturing subjective information.

3. **Adversarial Training**: Park et al. present adversarial dropout techniques that enhance learning in supervised and semi-supervised contexts.

4. **Automatic Differentiation**: Paszke et al. discuss the implementation of automatic differentiation in PyTorch, which is essential for training deep learning models.

5. **Scikit-learn**: Pedregosa et al. provide an overview of Scikit-learn, a widely used Python library for machine learning, highlighting its versatility and ease of use.

6. **DeepWalk**: Perozzi et al. introduce DeepWalk, a method for online learning of social representations, demonstrating its application in network data.

7. **Ladder Networks**: Rasmus et al. and Rifai et al. explore ladder networks and contractive auto-encoders, which are significant for semi-supervised learning and feature extraction.

8. **Collective Classification**: Sen et al. discuss collective classification in network data, showcasing the complexity of classifying interconnected data points.

9. **Active Learning**: Settles provides a comprehensive overview of active learning strategies, which are essential for efficiently labeling data in machine learning tasks.

10. **Semi-supervised Feature Selection**: Sheikhpour et al. survey various semi-supervised feature selection methods, emphasizing their importance in improving model performance.

These contributions collectively advance the understanding and application of machine learning techniques, particularly in contexts where labeled data is scarce.
