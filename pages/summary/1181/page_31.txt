The paper discusses the role of unlabelled data in semi-supervised deep learning, particularly emphasizing how it can act as a form of regularization. The hierarchical structure of deep neural networks allows them to learn increasingly abstract representations, which can be enhanced by leveraging unlabelled data. This leads to the concept of perturbation-based semi-supervised neural networks.

One notable approach highlighted is the ladder network, introduced by Rasmus et al. in 2015. This architecture builds upon a standard feedforward network by integrating unlabelled data through a denoising autoencoder framework. In this setup, the feedforward network serves as the encoder, while a decoder is appended to reconstruct the input. The key innovation is the addition of a reconstruction cost to the overall loss function, which penalizes discrepancies between the original input and its reconstruction, applicable to both labelled and unlabelled data.

Ladder networks distinguish themselves from traditional denoising autoencoders by injecting noise at every layer rather than just the input layer. This design enables the network to maintain sensitivity to input perturbations while simultaneously enforcing a reconstruction cost that evaluates not only the final layer outputs but also intermediate hidden representations. Each layer in the decoder mirrors the structure of the encoder, allowing for comprehensive reconstruction and enhancing the model's overall predictive capabilities. 

In summary, ladder networks effectively utilize unlabelled data through a dual loss mechanism, promoting robustness and improving class prediction by learning useful latent representations.
