In this paper, the authors discuss the concept of mixup, a data augmentation technique that enhances the performance of machine learning models by interpolating between data points. Zhang et al. (2018) demonstrated significant improvements using mixup, particularly when the beta distribution hyper-parameter α is low, resulting in interpolated samples clustering near the original data points. The authors highlight that mixup can also be applied to unlabelled data by interpolating predicted labels, as shown in the work of Verma et al. (2019) and Berthelot et al. (2019), who integrated mixup into semi-supervised learning frameworks.

The paper further explores the relationship between mixup and graph-based methods, noting that while both approaches utilize combinations of data points for perturbations, mixup does not account for pairwise similarities between points. This presents an opportunity for future research to investigate the implications of this difference.

Additionally, the authors introduce the manifold assumption in the context of semi-supervised learning, which posits that data resides on lower-dimensional manifolds. This assumption suggests that minor perturbations should not significantly alter the classifier's outputs, emphasizing that data points on the same manifold share labels. The discussion includes two types of methods informed by this assumption: manifold regularization techniques that penalize prediction differences based on geodesic distances, and manifold approximation techniques. The exploration of these concepts underscores the potential benefits of understanding the underlying structure of input data in improving classification performance.
