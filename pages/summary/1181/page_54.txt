The paper discusses methods for constructing weight matrices in machine learning, specifically through modifications of the symmetric k-nearest neighbors approach. Liu and Chang introduce a method where nodes are connected if they are mutual neighbors, with the weights of connections summed if both nodes belong to each other's k-neighborhood. 

De Sousa et al. compare various weighting methods, including Gaussian weighting and locally normalized Gaussian weighting, finding that Gaussian weighting yields the best performance for transductive algorithms. 

The paper also presents a simultaneous graph construction and weighting approach, initially proposed by Yan and Wang. This method infers both the structure of the graph and the edge weights by reconstructing each node using contributions from all other nodes, rather than a fixed set of neighbors. The reconstruction is based on a coefficient vector that minimizes the reconstruction error, using an L1 norm to promote sparsity. 

The optimization problem seeks to find the coefficients that allow for accurate reconstruction of each data point, with the final graph constructed by adding edges based on the non-zero coefficients and setting edge weights to their magnitudes. Importantly, this approach can result in directed graphs.
