In structured output learning, the representation of output labels extends beyond simple binary or real-valued forms, often requiring histograms or probability distributions. This approach is exemplified in traffic density prediction over time, where Subramanya and Bilmes have utilized KL-divergence for propagating discrete probability distributions through graphs. Alternatively, Solomon et al. proposed the Wasserstein distance to evaluate the similarity between discrete distributions.

Graph construction is pivotal for graph-based methods, as it must accurately reflect local similarities to enable effective inference. Historically, research focused more on inference than on graph construction, but recent studies have explored various graph construction algorithms. The process involves creating edges between nodes (forming the adjacency matrix) and attaching weights (leading to the weight matrix), with similarity measures often guiding this connection.

The adjacency matrix is formed by determining the presence of edges between node pairs. Three primary methods for edge determination are discussed: epsilon-neighbourhood, k-nearest neighbours, and b-matching. The epsilon-neighbourhood method connects nodes within a specified distance, but its effectiveness is limited by the choice of epsilon and distance measure, making it less common in practice. The k-nearest neighbours method is widely used, connecting each node to its k closest neighbours based on a distance measure. However, it faces challenges due to its asymmetry, necessitating additional steps for undirected graph formation.
