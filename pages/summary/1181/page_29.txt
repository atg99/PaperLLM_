The paper discusses various methods for incorporating unlabelled data into machine learning models, particularly focusing on decision trees and perturbation-based techniques. It highlights the development of an objective function that ensures the posterior probability of labels conditioned on input remains stable in high-density regions. This is achieved by partitioning the input space into small, overlapping regions and calculating a cost term based on mutual information, building on the work of Szummer and Jaakkola.

Liu et al. propose enhancing decision tree node splitting by integrating prior density, using Gaussian kernel density estimators to penalize high-density areas during hyperplane selection. Their experiments with random forests demonstrate notable performance gains over traditional supervised methods. Levati et al. further generalize this approach by creating an impurity measure for unlabelled data, promoting feature consistency across splits.

The paper also examines pseudo-labelling as a margin maximization technique, particularly in the context of self-training with SVMs, where the decision boundary is moved away from unlabelled samples, leveraging the low-density assumption.

Perturbation-based methods are introduced, which capitalize on the smoothness assumption that models should be resilient to minor input variations. These methods can be implemented through noise application to input data or by perturbing the classifier itself, making them suitable for neural networks. The resurgence of interest in deep neural networks has led to the exploration of semi-supervised learning frameworks that extend traditional neural network architectures for improved performance in various applications.
