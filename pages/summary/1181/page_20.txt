The SemiBoost algorithm is a semi-supervised boosting technique that addresses the selection of data points for training base learners by leveraging the manifold assumption and graph-based methods. It assigns pseudo-labels to unlabelled data points and calculates their prediction confidence based on a predefined neighbourhood graph that reflects the similarity between data points. A subset of these pseudo-labelled points is then integrated into the labelled dataset for training the next base learner, with the selection probability being proportional to the prediction confidence.

SemiBoost operates within the standard boosting classification framework, where final predictions are derived from a linear combination of individual learners' predictions. However, its cost function differs significantly from other semi-supervised boosting approaches. The algorithm is guided by three key requirements for successful labelling: consistency among close unlabelled data points, alignment of unlabelled predictions with nearby labelled data, and accuracy of labelled predictions. These requirements are formalized in a constrained optimization problem that aims to minimize an objective function while ensuring that the predicted labels for labelled data points match their true labels.

The optimization objective resembles those found in graph-based methods, prioritizing classifiers that consistently label points on the same manifold. Unlike traditional graph-based methods, SemiBoost differentiates between labelled-unlabelled and unlabelled-unlabelled pairs.

In addition to SemiBoost, other semi-supervised boosting methods have been explored, such as RegBoost, which incorporates local label consistency and considers the local density of the marginal distribution. Efforts have also been made to adapt label consistency regularization to multiclass problems, further advancing the field of semi-supervised boosting.
