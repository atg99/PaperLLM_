The paper discusses wrapper methods in semi-supervised learning, highlighting their compatibility with various supervised base learners, which can remain unaware of the wrapper method's existence. Wrapper methods utilize pseudo-labelled samples treated as regular labelled data, but their effectiveness is contingent on the underlying base learners employed. Triguero et al. (2015) presented a comprehensive survey categorizing wrapper methods based on the number of classifiers, the diversity of classifier types, and the use of single-view or multi-view data.

The authors propose a simplified taxonomy of three main types of wrapper methods: self-training, co-training, and pseudo-labelled boosting. Self-training involves a single supervised classifier that iteratively retrains itself using its most confident predictions on unlabelled data. Initially, the classifier is trained solely on labelled data, then it predicts labels for unlabelled data, and the most confident predictions are incorporated into the training set for subsequent iterations. This method, first introduced by Yarowsky (1995) for word sense disambiguation, has seen various applications, including object detection and hyperspectral image classification, demonstrating its effectiveness in enhancing model performance.
