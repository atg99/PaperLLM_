The paper discusses the role of semi-supervised learning in improving classification tasks, particularly in scenarios where unlabelled data is abundant. It illustrates this with an example involving binary classification, where the presence of unlabelled data can provide insights that help classify documents related to physics, even when specific keywords are missing. For instance, the word "neutron" may not appear in a document about particle accelerators, but its co-occurrence with other related terms, such as "quark," can guide the classifier.

Figure 1 demonstrates this concept using a two-class artificial classification problem, showcasing how unlabelled data can help identify a more optimal decision boundary compared to a supervised learning approach that relies solely on a minimal labelled dataset. A critical assumption of semi-supervised learning is that the marginal data distribution p(x) must contain information about the posterior distribution p(y|x). If this condition is satisfied, unlabelled data can enhance the accuracy of predictions; otherwise, it cannot improve classification performance.
