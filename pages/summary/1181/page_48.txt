This paper discusses the computation of probabilities and label assignments in Markov random fields, particularly focusing on maximum-likelihood estimation and the challenges associated with marginal probability calculations. The normalization constant, while difficult to compute, is deemed irrelevant in maximum-likelihood contexts. The paper establishes that maximizing the probability of a configuration corresponds to finding the mode of the Markov random field, aligning with the min-cut objective.

In the inductive semi-supervised classification setting, predictions are independent, contrasting with transductive graph-based methods where predictions are interdependent. This dependency complicates the identification of the most probable label assignment, which does not necessarily minimize the expected error rate. Previous methods, such as those by Zhu and Ghahramani, attempted to calculate marginal probabilities through Markov chain Monte Carlo sampling but faced computational inefficiencies.

The paper introduces Gaussian random fields as a solution for efficiently computing label assignments. By relaxing binary labels to real values and employing a quadratic cost for predictions, a closed-form solution emerges, allowing for the computation of marginal probabilities that are also Gaussian. This facilitates the minimization of error rates in label predictions.

The graph Laplacian is defined as L=D−W, where D is the degree matrix. The paper highlights that the prediction function is harmonic, meaning the predicted label at unlabelled data points is the average of its neighbors' predictions. The solution is unique and constrained within the range [0,1], enabling straightforward label predictions through thresholding. However, the computation of marginals still requires inversion of a submatrix of the graph Laplacian, which poses challenges for large datasets.
