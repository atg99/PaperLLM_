The paper discusses graph-based inference methods in semi-supervised classification, specifically focusing on label propagation and its extensions. The objective function for label propagation incorporates a penalization term governed by a parameter λU, which addresses inconsistencies in label predictions among neighboring nodes in a graph. The method utilizes the normalized graph Laplacian, allowing for a closed-form solution and an iterative optimization process. The update rule for the label vector is defined, emphasizing the balance between calculated labels and base labels.

Further research has led to various adaptations of label propagation. Baluja et al. introduced a heuristic algorithm called adsorption for recommender systems, which employs a random walk strategy to predict labels. This algorithm allows for three actions at each step: continuing the walk, adopting a label from a neighboring node, or abandoning the prediction, with the decision influenced by hyperparameters. While successful in practical applications, its theoretical analysis is complex.

Additionally, the paper addresses the sensitivity of graph-based methods to class imbalance. Zhu et al. proposed threshold adjustments to align predicted label proportions with predefined ones, while Wang et al. developed an optimization scheme to mitigate noise and class imbalance sensitivity by modifying the objective function to include real-valued predictions alongside binary assignments. This approach allows for alternating optimization between the two types of predictions, with later work considering the problem from a graph max-cut perspective.
