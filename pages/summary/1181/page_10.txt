This paper presents a new taxonomy for semi-supervised classification algorithms, aiming to clarify the relationships among various methods and their connection to supervised learning. The taxonomy is organized into two primary categories: inductive and transductive methods. Inductive methods focus on creating a classification model that can predict labels for unseen data, similar to supervised learning, while transductive methods provide label predictions directly for given unlabelled data without yielding a general model.

At a deeper level, the taxonomy categorizes how these methods incorporate unlabelled data, leading to three distinct classes of inductive techniques. This structured approach enhances understanding of the landscape of semi-supervised learning, allowing for better insights into existing methods and the assumptions underlying them. The proposed framework is designed to be adaptable to future developments in the field, addressing limitations in existing categorizations that may not accommodate new approaches effectively.
