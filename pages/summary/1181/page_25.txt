This paper discusses the soft-margin Support Vector Machine (SVM) and its extension to semi-supervised SVMs (S3VMs). The soft-margin SVM allows certain data points to violate the margin, enabling a trade-off between the complexity of the decision boundary and prediction accuracy. The optimization problem seeks to minimize a weighted combination of the margin's width and the penalties for margin violations, controlled by a constant C.

In semi-supervised SVMs, the objective expands to maximizing the margin while also minimizing violations from unlabelled data points. The optimization problem for S3VMs incorporates additional terms to penalize unlabelled data, with a separate cost parameter C'. This approach was proposed by Vapnik, who emphasized a transductive perspective by optimizing label predictions alongside the other parameters.

However, a key challenge with S3VMs is that the optimization problem becomes non-convex and NP-hard, making practical training more complex. Consequently, research efforts have focused on developing efficient training methods for S3VMs.
