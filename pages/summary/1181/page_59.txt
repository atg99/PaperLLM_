The paper discusses advancements and challenges in semi-supervised learning, particularly focusing on graph-based methods and semi-supervised neural networks. Graph-based methods leverage the similarities among unlabelled data but face computational difficulties. Recent developments have aligned semi-supervised learning with supervised learning techniques, emphasizing neural networks through unsupervised preprocessing and regularization. A critical concern highlighted is the performance degradation that can occur when integrating unlabelled data, which has not been sufficiently addressed in literature. This issue, along with weak empirical evaluations against supervised methods, contributes to the limited practical use of semi-supervised learning.

However, semi-supervised neural networks, particularly those employing perturbation-based approaches, have shown promise by outperforming supervised models under certain conditions. Their ability to seamlessly integrate unsupervised loss terms into their cost functions enhances their flexibility and potential for complex regularization strategies. 

The paper also suggests that the application of automated machine learning (AutoML) techniques could improve the robustness of semi-supervised learning methods, although such applications have yet to be explored. Additionally, the development of standardized software tools for semi-supervised learning is deemed essential for its broader adoption. While popular toolkits exist for supervised learning, the semi-supervised domain lacks similar standardization, although some resources, like the KEEL package and implementations in PyTorch and TensorFlow, are noted. Overall, the authors anticipate a continued increase in the popularity of semi-supervised neural networks and emphasize the need for further research and development in this area.
