The paper discusses manifold regularization in the context of semi-supervised learning, emphasizing the assumption that labeled and unlabeled data points lie on the same manifold. This assumption suggests that if a labeled point is on a manifold, nearby unlabeled points are likely to share the same label. The authors introduce the concept of geodesics, or paths, connecting these points, which can be formalized using a similarity graph that connects close data points.

Belkin et al. developed a framework for regularizing inductive learners based on this manifold assumption. They proposed a general optimization problem for supervised learning that minimizes a loss function while incorporating a smoothing term. The smoothing term, controlled by a parameter γ, encourages the predictive function to be smooth across the manifold.

For semi-supervised learning, they added an unsupervised regularization term that penalizes label discrepancies between connected data points in the graph, promoting consistent label predictions for points on the same manifold. The unsupervised regularization term leads to the formulation of manifold regularization methods, characterized by a similarity graph represented by a symmetric weighted adjacency matrix. The regularization term is defined in terms of the graph Laplacian, which incorporates the relationships between the data points to enhance the learning process. The final optimization problem integrates this manifold regularization to improve label prediction accuracy.
