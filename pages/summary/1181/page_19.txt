The paper discusses the relationship between bagging and boosting methods in the context of semi-supervised learning. It highlights that while bagging methods train base learners independently, a truly semi-supervised bagging method would involve self-training for each base learner. Co-training is related but lacks the bootstrapping characteristic of bagging. In contrast, boosting methods inherently depend on the previous learners, allowing for natural extension into semi-supervised settings through pseudo-labelling.

The authors review semi-supervised boosting methods, which have gained attention over the last two decades, motivated by the success of supervised boosting techniques like AdaBoost, gradient boosting, and XGBoost. These methods can integrate pseudo-labelling approaches from self-training and co-training.

Two specific semi-supervised boosting methods are discussed: SSMBoost and ASSEMBLE. SSMBoost, developed by Grandvalet et al., adapts AdaBoost for semi-supervised learning without assigning pseudo-labels to unlabelled data, relying on base learners to utilize unlabelled data. It sets the stage for subsequent semi-supervised boosting algorithms. ASSEMBLE, on the other hand, does use pseudo-labeling after each iteration, facilitating the integration of unlabelled data into the learning process. It employs bootstrapping to determine which unlabelled points to use for the next base learner, effectively maximizing classification margins in function space.
