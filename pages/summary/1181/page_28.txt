This paper discusses advancements in Gaussian processes, specifically their application to semi-supervised learning in binary classification, as explored by Lawrence and Jordan in 2005. Gaussian processes are non-parametric models that predict the posterior probability of a function mapping input points to continuous outputs, which can be adapted for binary classification by mapping outputs to labels {-1, 1}. The model is trained by maximizing the likelihood of observed data points, resulting in a Gaussian distribution over the label vector.

Lawrence and Jordan's extension incorporates unlabelled data into the likelihood function, where the likelihood for an unlabelled point is low near the decision boundary and high away from it. This leads to the introduction of a null category in the label space, which the model is discouraged from assigning to unlabelled data, promoting decision boundaries that avoid high-density areas of unlabelled points. Interestingly, this semi-supervised approach can increase posterior variance with additional unlabelled data, highlighting increased uncertainty near the decision boundary.

The paper also explores density regularization as a method to encourage decision boundaries to pass through low-density areas. Grandvalet and Bengio formalized this concept using a maximum a posteriori framework, imposing a prior on model parameters to minimize class overlap, measured through Shannon's conditional entropy. They suggested using deterministic annealing to solve the resulting non-convex optimization problem. Additionally, Corduneanu and Jaakkola proposed incorporating an estimate of the input data distribution into the objective function, enhancing the classification model's performance.
