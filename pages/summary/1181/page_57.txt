The paper discusses various inference techniques for node classification in network data, highlighting the distinction between local and global classification methods. Local classification focuses on classifying each node based on its neighbors, often using iterative algorithms, while global classification optimizes a joint objective function. The iterative classification algorithm, referenced from Lu and Getoor (2003) and Neville and Jensen (2000), constructs a local supervised classifier for each node, iterating until predictions stabilize.

Yang et al. (2016) introduced a neural network-based method that predicts a node's label alongside its context through node embedding. This approach was further developed for inductive settings by framing the embedding as a function of a node's features rather than its context, utilizing random walks for context prediction. The paper also notes various adaptations of convolutional neural networks for network data.

The discussion extends to semi-supervised learning beyond classification, mentioning semi-supervised regression and clustering. Active learning is highlighted as another related area, where algorithms actively query users for labels of unlabelled data to enhance learning. The paper also touches on learning from positive and unlabelled data, a specific case of semi-supervised learning.

In semi-supervised regression, the paper notes the challenge of applying classification methods to regression problems due to the continuous nature of output values in regression, contrasting with the categorical label space in classification. Overall, the paper emphasizes the diverse methodologies and challenges within semi-supervised learning in the context of network data.
