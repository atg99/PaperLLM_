The paper discusses the integration of unlabelled data into supervised classification models, particularly through semi-supervised support vector machines (S3VMs). These models enhance traditional supervised SVMs by maximizing the margin on both labelled and unlabelled data. The authors categorize various semi-supervised methods based on the assumptions they rely on.

In Section 3.2, transductive methods are introduced, which differ from inductive methods by not constructing a classifier for the entire input space. Instead, they focus on the specific objects encountered during training, lacking distinct training and testing phases. Transductive learners operate without a model of the input space, relying on the propagation of information through direct connections between data points, leading to a graph-based approach. These methods are premised on the manifold assumption, where graphs represent lower-dimensional spaces of high-dimensional data.

Transductive graph-based methods involve three steps: constructing a graph representing data points as nodes, weighting the edges based on similarity, and inferring labels for unlabelled data. The paper elaborates on different techniques for these steps in Section 7.

Wrapper methods, discussed in Section 4, are established semi-supervised learning algorithms that iteratively train supervised learners using labelled data alongside pseudo-labelled data derived from previous iterations. This two-step process alternates between training the classifiers and inferring labels for unlabelled data based on the learners' confidence in their predictions.
