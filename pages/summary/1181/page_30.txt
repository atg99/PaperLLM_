The paper discusses the integration of unlabelled data into the optimization objective of neural networks, moving beyond traditional preprocessing methods. It begins with an overview of neural networks for readers less familiar with the topic, referencing Goodfellow et al. (2016) for more detailed insights.

Neural networks consist of nodes interconnected by weighted edges, organized into layers. In feedforward networks, nodes in each layer connect only to adjacent layers, and the output is derived by propagating the input through these connections. Each node's output, or activation, is determined by its activation function applied to the weighted sum of its inputs.

In supervised learning, the network weights are optimized to produce the desired output for given inputs. The model is represented as a function that maps input vectors to output vectors corresponding to possible classes. A loss function quantifies the cost of the output activations compared to true labels, leading to a complete cost function that is minimized during training.

The optimization of weights is performed using backpropagation, where the cost is propagated backwards through the network, updating weights to reduce the overall cost iteratively. This process typically requires multiple passes over the training data, known as epochs.

The paper also mentions the use of different notational styles in the literature, particularly the use of θ for network weights, which may be relevant for maintaining consistency with other articles discussed in the survey. Additionally, it highlights the potential to enhance the cost function by incorporating an unsupervised component, leveraging the efficiency of backpropagation across various loss functions.
