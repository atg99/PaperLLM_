The paper discusses the foundational assumptions of semi-supervised learning, emphasizing their relevance and application in real-world scenarios. It identifies three primary assumptions: the smoothness assumption, the low-density assumption, and the manifold assumption. 

The smoothness assumption posits that if two input points are close in the input space, their labels should be similar. This assumption is beneficial in semi-supervised learning as it allows label propagation through unlabelled data based on proximity. For instance, if a labelled point is close to an unlabelled point, and that unlabelled point is close to another unlabelled point, the label from the labelled point can be expected to apply to the second unlabelled point as well.

The low-density assumption suggests that decision boundaries should ideally reside in areas of low data density, avoiding high-density regions. This principle is crucial for ensuring that classifiers generalize well and do not overfit to densely populated areas of the input space.

These assumptions form the basis for many semi-supervised learning algorithms, guiding their design and implementation. The paper aims to provide a detailed exploration of these assumptions and their implications in various learning algorithms throughout the survey.
