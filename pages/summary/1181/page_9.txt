The paper discusses the significant influence of dataset selection and partitioning on the performance of semi-supervised learning algorithms. It emphasizes that different algorithms may perform variably depending on the amount and type of labeled and unlabeled data available. To accurately evaluate these algorithms, researchers should utilize diverse datasets.

A critical aspect of evaluation is the choice of a strong baseline. It is essential to determine whether the addition of unlabeled data genuinely enhances learner performance compared to existing supervised or semi-supervised methods. The paper references Oliver et al. (2018), highlighting the necessity of including well-tuned supervised baselines in evaluations.

The authors review studies that have assessed various semi-supervised learning methods. Chapelle et al. (2006b) compared eleven semi-supervised algorithms against supervised support vector machines and k-nearest neighbors, revealing no single algorithm consistently outperformed others across different datasets. Performance varied with the amount of unlabeled data and the specific datasets used. In contrast, Oliver et al. (2018) focused on semi-supervised neural networks, finding that most algorithms improved performance as more unlabeled data were added, except in cases of class mismatches between labeled and unlabeled data.

The paper concludes with a taxonomy of semi-supervised learning methods, noting the diversity in their underlying assumptions, usage of unlabeled data, and relation to supervised algorithms. It suggests that existing categorizations may not adequately capture the complexities and similarities among these methods. Future research could explore consistent performance improvements across various data types and investigate the foundational assumptions of semi-supervised neural networks for enhancing other learning methods.
