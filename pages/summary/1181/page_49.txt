The paper discusses various approaches to solving harmonic functions in the context of graph-based methods, particularly focusing on label propagation algorithms. Zhu and Ghahramani introduced an iterative label propagation algorithm that computes soft label assignments by propagating estimated labels across neighboring nodes based on edge weights. This process involves two main steps: propagating labels using a transition matrix and resetting the labels of known data points to their true values. The algorithm is shown to converge to a harmonic function solution and can be interpreted as a random walk stopping at labeled nodes.

Further developments include Wu et al.'s partially absorbing random walks, where the stopping condition is probabilistic, and Azran's approach where random walks start from unlabelled nodes, treating labeled nodes as absorbing states. 

The paper also addresses the limitations of the Gaussian random fields method, particularly its inability to manage label noise and the disproportionate influence of high-degree nodes in irregular graphs. Zhou et al. proposed the local and global consistency method to overcome these issues by penalizing the squared error between true and estimated labels without clamping true labels and regularizing predictions based on node degrees. This method aims to promote label consistency both locally and globally within the graph structure.
