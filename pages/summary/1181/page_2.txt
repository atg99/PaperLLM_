This paper provides an overview of semi-supervised learning, a branch of machine learning that integrates elements from both supervised and unsupervised learning. Supervised learning involves using labeled data to create classifiers or regressors, while unsupervised learning focuses on inferring structures from unlabeled data, such as clustering similar inputs. Semi-supervised learning aims to enhance performance in classification and clustering tasks by leveraging both labeled and unlabeled data.

The paper emphasizes the relevance of semi-supervised classification, particularly in scenarios where labeled data is scarce and difficult to obtain, such as in medical diagnosis or drug discovery. It notes that even when labeled data is available, incorporating unlabeled data can still improve classification performance. The authors reference previous surveys and foundational texts, highlighting significant advancements in the field since the last comprehensive survey by Zhu in 2008, including the rise of deep neural networks which have facilitated new semi-supervised learning approaches.

The paper aims to present a thorough overview of the current research landscape in semi-supervised learning, discussing early developments, recent advancements, key algorithms, and proposing a new taxonomy for semi-supervised classification methods based on underlying assumptions.
