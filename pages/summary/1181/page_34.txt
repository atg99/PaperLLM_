The paper discusses various methods for enhancing the performance of neural networks through the incorporation of perturbations and ensemble techniques. Pseudo-ensembles are introduced as a means to compare the activations of a perturbed network with those of an unperturbed model. Laine and Aila (2017) propose temporal ensembling, which evaluates the neural network's output at each training epoch against an exponential moving average of prior outputs. This method smooths the network's outputs over time, serving as an extension of the pseudo-ensembling concept, but differs in its approach by not reducing outputs to hard predictions.

The mean teacher model, developed by Tarvainen and Valpola (2017), addresses the challenge of integrating unlabelled data into the learning process by employing moving averages of network weights instead of activations. This method enhances robustness by introducing noise to the input data and calculating a loss function based on the comparison of activations from the averaged weights (teacher model) and the latest weights (student model).

Lastly, the paper touches on virtual adversarial training, emphasizing that most perturbation-based methods focus on isotropic noise without considering the directionality of perturbations, which can significantly affect a neural network's sensitivity to inputs. This highlights the need for further exploration into the directional aspects of perturbations for improved robustness.
