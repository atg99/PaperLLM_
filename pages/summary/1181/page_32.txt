The paper discusses the architecture and functioning of ladder networks in the context of semi-supervised learning. Ladder networks utilize a dual-pass mechanism where each data point is processed twice: once without noise to obtain a latent representation and once with noise for reconstruction. The semi-supervised cost function incorporates both labeled and unlabeled data, emphasizing the reconstruction of latent representations. The effectiveness of this approach is based on the assumption that a useful latent representation for reconstruction also aids in class prediction.

Rasmus et al. demonstrated that ladder networks achieve superior performance on partially labeled datasets, such as MNIST, and even improved results with solely labeled data. They also introduced a simplified variant, the /Gamma1-model, which focuses only on the last layer's reconstruction cost, eliminating the need for a full decoder while still enhancing performance compared to fully-supervised models.

Further empirical studies by Pezeshki et al. highlighted the importance of the first layer's reconstruction cost and noise introduction on overall network performance, contrasting it with the /Gamma1-model’s focus on the last layer.

Additionally, the paper explores the concept of pseudo-ensembles, where the neural network model itself is perturbed instead of the input data. Bachman et al. proposed a framework for this, allowing for the generation of child models from a parent model through perturbation, thus promoting robustness by measuring consistency between activations of the perturbed and original networks. The semi-supervised cost function in this framework combines supervised loss for labeled data and unsupervised consistency across perturbed networks for unlabeled data.
