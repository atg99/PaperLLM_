This paper presents a comprehensive survey on semi-supervised learning, a machine learning paradigm that leverages both labeled and unlabeled data for various learning tasks. Positioned between supervised and unsupervised learning, semi-supervised learning is particularly beneficial as it utilizes the vast amounts of unlabeled data alongside smaller labeled datasets. The authors highlight the recent trends in research, particularly the focus on neural network-based models and generative learning, while noting the growing volume and scope of literature in this field.

Despite the advancements, the authors identify a gap in the availability of recent surveys that systematically compile and organize this knowledge, which hinders the efforts of researchers and practitioners. To address this, the paper provides an updated overview of semi-supervised learning methods, emphasizing semi-supervised classification, the area where most research is concentrated.

The survey aims to equip both newcomers and seasoned researchers with a robust understanding of key approaches and algorithms developed over the last two decades. Additionally, the authors introduce a new taxonomy of semi-supervised classification algorithms, which clarifies various conceptual and methodological strategies for incorporating unlabeled data into training processes. They also discuss the interconnectedness of the fundamental assumptions underlying many semi-supervised learning algorithms and their relationship to the widely recognized semi-supervised clustering assumption.
