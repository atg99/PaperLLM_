The paper discusses various techniques for splitting single-view data sets into multiple views to enhance classifier performance. Wang et al. (2008b) proposed generating random projections of data, while Zhang and Zheng (2009) suggested using principal component analysis to create pseudo-views by selecting features with maximal variance. Yaslan and Cataltepe (2010) focused on iteratively selecting features based on their mutual information with labels without transforming the data.

Additionally, alternative methods for introducing diversity among classifiers in single-view settings are explored. Wang and Zhou (2007) and Zhou and Li (2005a) highlighted the importance of using different hyperparameters or algorithms to boost performance. Zhou and Li (2005b) introduced tri-training, a method involving three classifiers that share data points based on agreement, which can be applied broadly across various supervised learning algorithms.

The co-forest approach, proposed by Li and Zhou (2007), extends tri-training to random forests. It involves independent training of decision trees on labeled data and iterative pseudo-labeling based on joint predictions from other classifiers. This method incorporates a mechanism to reduce the influence of mislabelled data by weighting pseudo-labels according to prediction confidence. Deng and Zu Guo (2011) further refined this by removing suspicious pseudo-labels based on k-nearest neighbor comparisons.

The paper notes that existing research has limited co-forest implementations to six trees, despite evidence suggesting that performance improves with an increased number of trees in random forests. This implies that expanding the tree count in co-forest could significantly enhance performance relative to traditional random forests.

Finally, the co-regularization framework aims to reduce classifier disagreement and minimize ensemble error rates, as proposed by Sindhwani et al.
