The paper discusses various methods for semi-supervised learning, particularly in the contexts of regression and clustering. It highlights the adaptability of graph-based methods to regression, where real-valued predictions are integrated into the objective function through regularization. Notably, wrapper methods, including self-training and co-training, are identified as suitable for semi-supervised regression, with Zhou and Li's co-training algorithm serving as a key example. This algorithm employs k-nearest neighbor regressors that iteratively exchange pseudo-labelled data based on labelling confidence derived from performance metrics.

In the realm of semi-supervised clustering, the paper notes that the incorporation of supervised information can vary, including must-link and cannot-link constraints. Techniques for initializing clusters based on known assignments, such as those proposed by Basu et al., are discussed, emphasizing the importance of labeled data in seeding cluster centroids during k-means clustering.

The authors conclude by presenting a comprehensive overview of the semi-supervised learning field, tracing developments from the early 2000s to recent advancements. They introduce a new taxonomy for classifying semi-supervised methods based on learning objectives and the use of unlabelled data. The paper underscores the relatively limited research in semi-supervised regression and clustering compared to semi-supervised classification, encouraging further exploration in these areas.
