Miyato et al. (2018) introduced a regularization method that accounts for the perturbation direction in input data to enhance neural network robustness. They proposed a loss function that penalizes differences in network outputs for perturbed and unperturbed inputs, utilizing adversarial noise to maximize the output change. This method, termed virtual adversarial training, contrasts with traditional adversarial training by comparing outputs of perturbed inputs to those of the unperturbed inputs rather than true labels, allowing for unsupervised application. Their approach shares similarities with contractive autoencoders, which similarly penalize sensitivity to input perturbations.

Building on this, Park et al. (2018) combined virtual adversarial training with the /Pi1-model, introducing an adversarial dropout mask applied to network weights. By sampling a dropout mask and optimizing it to maximize output differences, they achieved slight performance improvements over previous methods.

Additionally, researchers have explored the concept of semi-supervised mixup, which allows for larger input perturbations that can affect network outputs more significantly when directed toward decision boundaries. Proposed by Zhang et al. (2018), the mixup method asserts that predictions for linear combinations of feature vectors should correspond to linear combinations of their labels. This involves generating augmented data points by interpolating pairs of existing samples and training the network on these combinations, thereby enhancing generalization capabilities.
