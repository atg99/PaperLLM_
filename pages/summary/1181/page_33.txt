The paper discusses various methods for improving neural network training through the use of perturbations and consistency losses. It introduces a cost function for pseudo-ensembles, which combines a supervised loss with a consistency loss that penalizes differences in activations between unperturbed and perturbed networks. The consistency loss is weighted by λk for each layer, and Bachman et al. suggest gradually increasing these weights to prioritize supervised objectives in early training.

One prevalent technique for inducing noise is dropout, which randomly deactivates weights in the network during training. Initially applied only to supervised loss, it has been shown to be effective with unlabelled data as well. The framework proposed is versatile and can be adapted to various supervised learning problems and algorithms beyond neural networks, although adaptations may be needed for the per-layer comparisons.

Additionally, the paper describes the /Pi1-model, where two perturbed models are directly compared instead of comparing them to an unperturbed model. This approach also utilizes dropout for perturbation and gradually increases the weight of the unsupervised term in the cost function.

Lastly, the concept of temporal ensembling is introduced, which extends the idea of comparing outputs from multiple perturbed models to enhance regularization. This method involves random transformations of input data and can improve the robustness of the model, although it may increase training time significantly. Overall, these methods highlight the importance of leveraging perturbations in enhancing neural network training.
