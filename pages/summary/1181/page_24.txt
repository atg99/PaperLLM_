The paper discusses the concept of pre-training methods in machine learning, highlighting their relationship to unsupervised feature extraction. Both approaches utilize unlabelled data to derive meaningful representations, but pre-training allows for adjustment of parameters during the supervised fine-tuning phase, unlike fixed parameters in unsupervised feature extraction.

The focus then shifts to intrinsically semi-supervised methods, which optimize an objective function incorporating both labelled and unlabelled samples without relying on intermediate steps or supervised base learners. These methods are often extensions of existing supervised techniques and are based on semi-supervised learning assumptions, such as the low-density and smoothness assumptions.

The paper elaborates on maximum-margin methods, which seek to maximize the distance between data points and the decision boundary, aligning with the low-density assumption. A prominent example of this is the support vector machine (SVM), which aims to find a decision boundary that maximizes the margin, defined as the distance from the boundary to the nearest data points. The discussion includes a brief introduction to supervised SVMs, suggesting further reading for a comprehensive understanding.
