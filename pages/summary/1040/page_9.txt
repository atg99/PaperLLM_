The paper discusses the evolving landscape of AI and ethics, particularly in the context of business practices. It highlights how AI's ability to leverage fine-grained user data allows for rapid reproduction and personalization of products, enabling smaller and less visible firms to compete in ways that are often unnoticed by the public and regulatory bodies. This shift complicates the ability of watchdog organizations to identify irresponsible practices, as ethical issues related to AI, such as biases and discrimination, can remain hidden from consumers.

The authors argue that the increasing integration of AI in business necessitates a reevaluation of traditional ethical frameworks in light of advancements in computer science. They advocate for a collaborative discourse between AI ethics and business ethics, emphasizing the need to address algorithmic biases and other ethical challenges through established principles in organizational ethics.

The conclusion calls for a deeper engagement with AI ethics as an academic discipline, stressing that it should not merely serve as a checklist for compliance but rather contribute to a broader, nuanced ethical dialogue. The authors propose that ethics should be viewed as a reflective tool that informs human discourse rather than a quantitative metric. This integration of ethical considerations is essential as AI continues to permeate various aspects of life, highlighting the need for a proactive approach to ethical challenges in technology.
