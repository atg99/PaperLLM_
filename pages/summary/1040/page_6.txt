The paper discusses the challenges and criticisms surrounding the ethical guidelines in AI, drawing parallels to business ethics. It highlights that while ethical principles may appear consistent, there are significant differences in their interpretation and implementation. Critics argue that these guidelines lack moral authority and are often vague, serving more as a means of self-regulation to avoid government oversight. This self-regulation has been likened to a flawed approach in business ethics, where it has been criticized for failing to effectively address ethical complexities within organizations.

The paper points out that the current AI ethics debate reflects similar skepticism about self-regulation, especially as global legislators, particularly in the European Union, push for more stringent regulations beyond industry pledges. The increasing lobbying efforts by Big Tech companies indicate a resistance to forthcoming legislation. Despite these challenges, the paper suggests that insights from business ethics, particularly in corporate social responsibility (CSR), could inform the establishment of AI ethics standards.

Moreover, the paper emphasizes the importance of integrating ethics into higher education for future AI practitioners. As AI technologies evolve, curricula must prepare students for the ethical implications of their work. While some institutions are starting to incorporate ethical reasoning into their programs, there remains a lack of consistency and standards in tech ethics education globally. This inconsistency is noted as a barrier to effectively teaching ethical decision-making in the context of AI development and deployment.
