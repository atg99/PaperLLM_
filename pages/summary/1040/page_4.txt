The paper discusses the challenges and opportunities in integrating stakeholder theory into AI ethics. It warns against "stakeholder colonialism," where corporations prioritize stakeholders with financial influence while neglecting marginalized groups. The authors highlight the need for a systematic approach to stakeholder engagement in technology firms, emphasizing that stakeholders include those involved in AI production and those affected by its use. The complexity of stakeholder interests complicates practical application, but the theory's adaptability can help address moral demands across various sectors.

A significant challenge in AI ethics is the opacity of algorithms and data collection practices. Many organizations make bold claims about their AI systems without disclosing training data or algorithmic code, leading to ethical concerns, particularly in law enforcement contexts. The paper advocates for standardized reporting on non-financial digital issues to improve transparency and accountability.

The authors identify several challenges faced by AI ethics, including ethics washing, lack of standardized curricula in AI education, and insufficient representation of stakeholder voices. They propose five "knowledge bridges" to enhance the institutionalization of organizational AI ethics, drawing parallels with business ethics practices. These bridges include stakeholder management, standardized reporting, and the integration of ethics into educational curricula, aiming to foster dialogue and accountability in AI development and deployment.
