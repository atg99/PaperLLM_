The article discusses the concept of ethics washing, highlighting its role in corporate strategies to circumvent external regulation, particularly in the context of AI. It draws parallels with greenwashing, a well-researched area in business ethics, where companies mislead stakeholders about their environmental practices. This comparison suggests that existing literature on greenwashing can inform the understanding of ethics washing in the realm of AI.

The paper identifies five key insights from business ethics literature that could facilitate knowledge transfer to AI ethics. These include stakeholder engagement, the types of information needed by stakeholders regarding algorithms, governance mechanisms to connect ethical codes with practice, educational content for future professionals, and deeper explorations of the causes and consequences of ethics washing.

Despite the potential for these insights, the paper acknowledges significant limitations, particularly the historical failure of business ethics to fully instill ethical practices in the corporate world, raising questions about the effectiveness of AI ethics in achieving similar goals. The article advocates for continued research to develop a comprehensive understanding of the intersection of AI and business ethics, while recognizing the structural differences between the two fields.

In conclusion, the paper emphasizes the necessity for ongoing exploration of ethical practices in AI, the need for effective governance, and the importance of preparing future professionals to navigate the ethical challenges posed by AI technologies.
