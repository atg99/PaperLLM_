The paper discusses the evolving field of AI ethics and its need for institutionalization to address ethical challenges in organizational settings. It highlights how AI ethics can benefit from the knowledge transfer and cross-fertilization with established fields of applied ethics, particularly business ethics. The authors argue for leveraging insights from business ethics to enhance AI ethics, drawing on a tradition of reciprocal enrichment among various ethical disciplines.

The paper references the work of scholars like Véliz, who has compared medical ethics with AI ethics, emphasizing the unique political and private nature of the digital context. The authors propose that AI ethics could gain from established concepts in business ethics, particularly in light of the numerous ethical challenges faced by organizations in recent decades. They suggest that learning from business ethics can help in the institutionalization of ethical practices within AI.

The authors introduce five key areas from business ethics to advance AI ethics, starting with stakeholder management. They note that the digital transformation driven by AI often leads to ambiguous societal impacts, and existing AI ethics boards have struggled to effectively address these concerns, sometimes being dismissed as mere public relations efforts. They propose that stakeholder management can offer a structured approach to engage with ethical issues in AI, advocating for dialogue and participation among all affected parties.

The paper critiques shareholder theory, which prioritizes profit maximization, and promotes stakeholder theory, which emphasizes the responsibility of corporations to manage relationships with all stakeholders. Despite its influence, stakeholder theory has its limitations, including challenges in defining stakeholders and balancing their conflicting interests. The authors argue that addressing these challenges is crucial for integrating ethical considerations into AI decision-making.
