It highlights the controversy surrounding AI systems that claim to predict criminality, arguing that such systems are inherently racially biased. The text also illustrates the evolution of Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) reporting, which has moved towards established standards that enhance transparency regarding corporate conduct. Organizations like the Global Reporting Initiative provide frameworks for standardized CSR reporting, which can inform AI ethics reporting.

The paper notes that while CSR has developed clear metrics for reporting, AI ethics faces challenges in providing reliable and comparable information about algorithmic systems. Proposed regulations in the EU, including obligations for high-risk AI systems, aim to enhance transparency but may impose significant costs on corporations. 

Furthermore, the paper discusses the role of soft-law guidelines in AI ethics, which reflect high-level principles like transparency and fairness but lack enforceability. Despite over 200 guidelines emerging in recent years, there is concern about their practical application. The authors call for a focus on developing better algorithms and establishing effective certification systems to strengthen AI ethics. Overall, the paper underscores the importance of formalizing AI ethics information disclosure for both practitioners and academics.
