The paper discusses the integration of ethics into computer science education, drawing parallels with the evolution of business ethics education following corporate scandals like Enron and the subsequent implementation of the Sarbanes-Oxley Act. This legislation mandated ethical standards for publicly traded companies, prompting business schools to require dedicated ethics courses to prepare students for ethical challenges in their careers.

The author argues that a similar institutionalization of AI ethics in curricula is necessary and possible. Currently, while the Accreditation Board for Engineering and Technology (ABET) in the U.S. requires students to understand ethical issues, the specifics of how ethics is taught remain at the discretion of individual institutions. The paper suggests leveraging existing literature in applied ethics to inform AI ethics education, incorporating diverse ethical perspectives, and utilizing modern educational technologies.

The role of regulatory bodies in establishing ethical education standards is emphasized, with the EU's GDPR serving as an example of increasing regulatory scrutiny in ethical matters. The author also refers to the U.S. National Science Foundation's funding conditions, proposing that similar requirements could be applied to AI curricula.

However, the paper raises concerns about the credibility of AI ethics, highlighting the phenomenon of "ethics washing," where organizations may superficially adopt ethical practices to evade regulation. This leads to questions about the genuine commitment to ethical inquiry in AI, as critics argue that ethical considerations have been trivialized. The author posits that true applied ethics can provide deeper insights and tools to prevent unethical behavior, emphasizing the need for a robust ethical framework in AI that goes beyond superficial compliance.

In conclusion, the paper calls for a serious commitment to ethical education in AI, warning against the risks of ethics washing and the need for organizations to genuinely engage with ethical principles to avoid reputational damage and ensure responsible AI development.
