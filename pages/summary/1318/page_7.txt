The paper discusses the complexities of function allocation between humans and machines, particularly in high-stakes situations such as aircraft control. It emphasizes that time criticality is influenced by the decision-making capabilities of both the human and the machine, as well as the specific context of the situation. A five-minute window allows for adequate human response, while a five-second window may not. The paper identifies three key factors influencing decision-making: time criticality, the skill level of the decision-maker, and the severity of potential consequences.

A significant focus is placed on the evolving relationship between humans and machines as the Level of Autonomy (LOA) for machines increases. As machines become more autonomous, the dynamics shift from simple function allocation to more complex interactions where machines may take on supervisory roles. The paper argues that current LOA frameworks are insufficient for addressing these future AI systems, suggesting that roles such as guiding, controlling, collaborating, fixing, and modulating should be considered for both humans and machines.

The challenges of achieving effective human-AI collaboration are outlined, with a list of ten hurdles that AI technology must overcome to work as an equal partner with humans. These challenges include the necessity for AI to understand human intentions and vice versa, as well as the need for AI systems to be interpretable and explainable. The paper concludes that substantial research remains to bridge the gap between current AI capabilities and the requirements for meaningful collaboration with humans.
