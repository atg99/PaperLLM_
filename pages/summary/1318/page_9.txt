The paper discusses the multifaceted nature of trust, particularly in the context of human-AI interactions. It identifies key elements influencing trust, such as a speaker's expertise, reliability, intentions, and personal attractiveness, as well as the opinions of a listener's associates. Trust is portrayed as a complex interplay of attitudes, beliefs, emotions, risks, and power dynamics.

From a behavioral psychology perspective, trust is seen as a social dilemma that leads to two potential outcomes: trust or distrust. Definitions of trust highlight the vulnerability that a trustor exposes themselves to when relying on a trustee. Mayer et al. describe trust as the willingness to be vulnerable based on expectations of the trustee's actions, while Lee and See focus on trust as the belief that an agent will help achieve an individual's goals amidst uncertainty.

The paper introduces a vulnerability model, expressing vulnerability as a function of capability, opportunity, and intent. This framework is applied to AI, where capability refers to the AI's skills, opportunity relates to its autonomy, and intent encompasses the AI's design and learning behaviors. The alignment of AI intent with human intent is crucial for fostering trust.

The relationship between humans and AI is illustrated through a vulnerability lens. Misalignment of intent can lead to mistrust, regardless of the AI's capabilities or autonomy. Conversely, when intent is aligned, the levels of automation and autonomy influence trust dynamics. If autonomy exceeds capability, it can result in over-reliance on the AI, leading to disappointment and distrust. Conversely, if capability exceeds autonomy, the AI may be underutilized but still perform effectively, maintaining trustworthiness.
