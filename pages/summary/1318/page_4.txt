The paper discusses the challenges associated with static allocation in function assignment between humans and machines, highlighting that this approach fails to account for the changing suitability of each over time due to environmental factors and workload variations. It critiques static allocation for treating humans as machines, which can lead to unengaging tasks and a lack of motivation. Furthermore, it points out that the design decisions regarding function allocation are typically made by designers without input from human operators.

In response to these issues, the concept of adaptive allocation has emerged, promoting a human-centered design approach. This adaptive allocation can adjust function assignments during a mission based on various strategies, including critical event logic, measurement-based logic, and modeling-based logic. These strategies aim to enhance human-machine interaction and optimize performance based on real-time assessments of human workload and capabilities.

The paper also contrasts earlier models of adaptive allocation, which relied on observational research, with newer developments in augmented cognition and cognitive-cyber symbiosis. Augmented cognition, particularly through EEG-based adaptive allocation, offers a means to monitor brain activity and derive indicators of workload and attention, allowing for real-time adjustments in function allocation. This research has evolved significantly since the early studies on EEG, leading to applications in the military and beyond.

However, the paper cautions that dynamic function allocation can also lead to distractions and reduced situational awareness for humans, potentially compromising safety and performance. It emphasizes the importance of overcoming these limitations through advancements in augmented cognition and other adaptive strategies, ultimately aiming to create a more effective human-AI collaboration.
