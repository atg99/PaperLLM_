The paper discusses the CoCyS concept, which introduces "e-cookies" as smart adaptive agents that facilitate the relationship between humans and machines. These agents standardize AI systems by providing a front-end that assesses human trustworthiness and communicates information effectively. E-cookies act as intermediaries, ensuring harmony and trust in interactions between humans and AI systems, such as in a chess game where they mediate between the player and the AI recommender.

E-cookies analyze various human data sources (e.g., EEG, heart rate) to infer human states and also assess the automation's task state. They adapt function allocation strategies based on trust indicators, reallocating functions between humans and machines when necessary, such as in cases of hacking or identity changes. This creates a control agent that enhances the adaptive allocation process.

The paper highlights the responsibility of function allocation, where humans can control the allocation agent or automate it based on predefined rules. The concept of Levels of Automation (LOA) is introduced, categorizing the maturity of automation and defining roles humans can play in human-robot interactions, such as supervisor, operator, teammate, mechanic, and bystander. Each role delineates the level of involvement and decision-making authority in relation to the machine's tasks.
