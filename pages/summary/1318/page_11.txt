Current indicators are criticized for being post-development, limiting their utility in guiding system design. The authors propose a functional analysis approach that breaks down missions into functions, assigning weights based on criticality and skill complexity. This allows for a measurable autonomy level expressed as a percentage of functions implemented relative to those required.

The paper emphasizes the complexity of AI and the necessity for new methodologies to address emerging questions about human-AI interaction. Key inquiries include the extent of human control over AI, the risks of over-reliance on AI, and the circumstances under which human intervention is warranted. The authors argue that determining function allocation is vital to understanding these dynamics. As AI integrates further into society, the relationship between human oversight and AI autonomy must be carefully designed to optimize performance and safety.

Overall, the paper underscores the importance of developing robust metrics and frameworks to navigate the evolving landscape of human-AI collaboration, ensuring that both efficiency and safety are prioritized in autonomous systems.
