The paper discusses the dynamics of human-AI relationships, emphasizing the importance of aligning automation with autonomy to foster trust in AI systems. It identifies four key components of this relationship: sense-making, decision-making, execution ability, and execution authority. The authors highlight that risks arise when these components are assigned entirely to either humans or AI, leading to potential distrust in the system.

A finer granularity of function allocation, termed function specificity, is introduced, suggesting that interactions between humans and AI should focus on specific functions. Trustworthiness can vary across these functions, complicating the overall perception of the system's reliability.

The paper presents various metrics for assessing the level of autonomy in AI systems. Neglect tolerance measures a system's performance under human neglect, while interaction efficiency evaluates how human interaction can enhance a system's performance. Neglect benevolence assesses the time required for an autonomous system to stabilize between tasks, likening it to task-switching.
