The paper discusses the role of data analytics in decision-making processes, emphasizing its importance in transforming raw data into actionable insights. The data mining cycle begins with defining an analysis objective, followed by data preparation, application of machine learning techniques, and model selection. The results enable decision-makers, whether human or AI, to understand their environment and anticipate outcomes.

However, the paper highlights that data analytics does not make decisions; it only provides information, which necessitates a careful management of risks associated with AI outputs. For example, in a vehicle, an AI might present data such as navigation and temperature, but ultimately, the driver must decide how to use that information. The paper points out that decision-making can either be autonomous, where AI acts based on its analysis, or collaborative, where AI makes recommendations for human approval.

Autonomy in AI requires capabilities for sensing, assessing, decision-making, and executing actions. An example provided is an autonomous loan agent that evaluates borrower information, determines loan amounts, and initiates transactions. The design of human-AI interactions is crucial to manage risks effectively, and function allocation—deciding which tasks are assigned to humans versus machines—is essential.

The paper outlines function allocation as a division of labor that raises three questions: how to allocate functions, who is responsible for the allocation, and who has the authority to make these decisions. Two allocation methodologies are identified: static and adaptive. Static allocation includes comparison, leftover, and economic allocation, each with specific criteria for determining whether a human or machine should perform a function. This framework aims to optimize the roles of humans and machines in various contexts, ensuring safety and efficiency in decision-making processes.
