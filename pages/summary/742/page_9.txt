The references provided encompass a range of studies and advancements in the field of reinforcement learning and robotics. Key works include Andrychowicz et al. (2018), which focuses on dexterous in-hand manipulation, and Kalashnikov et al. (2018), which presents scalable deep reinforcement learning for vision-based robotic manipulation. Faust et al. (2018) combine reinforcement learning with sampling-based planning for navigation tasks.

Notably, Silver et al. (2017) and Mnih et al. (2015) highlight significant breakthroughs in mastering complex games through deep reinforcement learning, demonstrating the potential of these methods in achieving human-level performance. The evolution of imitation learning is explored through works by Ross et al. (2011) and Ho and Ermon (2016), which reduce imitation learning to online learning frameworks and introduce generative adversarial approaches, respectively.

Further studies by Zhang et al. (2018) and Zhu et al. (2018) address deep imitation learning and the integration of reinforcement and imitation learning for diverse visuomotor skills. The challenges of sparse rewards in robotics are tackled by Večerić et al. (2017), while Fernández and Veloso (2006) and Azar et al. (2013) discuss policy reuse and regret bounds in reinforcement learning settings.

The references also include theoretical insights on policy advice and negative transfer (Zhan et al., 2016), as well as advancements in context-aware methods (Li et al., 2018). The foundational text by Sutton and Barto (1998) remains a cornerstone in the understanding of reinforcement learning principles. Lastly, recent studies by Fujimoto et al. (2018) and Bhatt et al. (2019) explore off-policy learning techniques and normalization methods, further contributing to the ongoing development of the field.
