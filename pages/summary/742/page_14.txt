The paper evaluates the performance of the AC-Teach method in reinforcement learning tasks using different sets of teachers. It presents findings on the impact of teacher quality and sufficiency on agent performance during both training and testing phases. The results indicate that when utilizing sufficient teacher sets, AC-Teach significantly outperforms other baselines in terms of convergence speed and asymptotic performance. This is particularly evident with multiple partial teachers, where the method excels despite the DQN baseline struggling to exceed teacher performance.

The experiments also assess scenarios with insufficient and contradictory teacher sets. The findings reveal that AC-Teach effectively learns from incomplete teacher guidance, even when some teachers provide conflicting advice. The agent demonstrates an ability to discern when to rely on teacher input and how to navigate parts of the task where no guidance is available. Overall, the paper emphasizes AC-Teach's robustness in leveraging various teacher configurations to enhance learning outcomes in reinforcement learning tasks.
