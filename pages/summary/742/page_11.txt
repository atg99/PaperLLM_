The appendix presents a problem formulation related to infinite-horizon Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) with deterministic and stochastic transition dynamics. 

In section A.1, the relationship between the value function and reaching time is explored within deterministic MDPs. The value function V for a given deterministic policy is defined as the sum of rewards received over time, with the reward structure indicating that reaching a goal state yields a reward of 1. Proposition 1 establishes that for two states, a policy has a higher value at one state if it can reach a goal in fewer time steps compared to the other state. This is demonstrated through a proof that leverages the monotonic nature of the function relating time to value.

Section A.2 extends the analysis to stochastic MDPs, where the transition dynamics are probabilistic. The value function for a stochastic policy is defined as the expected discounted sum of rewards over trajectories. Each trajectory either leads to a goal state or does not, categorizing them into goal-reaching and non-goal-reaching sets. The value function is expressed as an expectation that incorporates the probabilities of reaching a goal and the associated returns from those trajectories.

Overall, the appendix rigorously formulates the relationship between reaching times and value functions in both deterministic and stochastic settings, providing a foundation for understanding the dynamics of MDPs in relation to goal achievement.
