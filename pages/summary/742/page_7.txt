The paper presents a detailed analysis of an algorithm called AC-Teach, which utilizes sufficient partial teacher sets to enhance the performance of agents in robotic tasks, specifically focusing on Path Following, Pick and Place, and Hook Sweep tasks. The results indicate that AC-Teach demonstrates superior convergence speed and overall performance compared to various baseline methods, even when dealing with noisy partial solutions.

Figures illustrate the training and evaluation phases, showing that AC-Teach outperforms other approaches, including those utilizing full teachers, in terms of the average evaluation return during training. Notably, the agent trained with multiple partial teachers outperforms a hand-coded, nearly optimal teacher, highlighting the effectiveness of the proposed method.

The paper also describes the specific roles of various teachers in guiding the agent's actions, such as the hook-pick and hook-position teachers, which assist in grasping and positioning the hook for effective task execution. 

The performance of AC-Teach is contrasted against several baseline methods, including BDDPG without teachers, DDPG with teachers, and other variations incorporating different behavioral policies. These comparisons affirm the advantages of using AC-Teach in terms of both learning efficiency and task performance, establishing it as a robust approach in the domain of reinforcement learning for robotic applications.
