The paper introduces a framework for training an agent policy by efficiently utilizing a set of teacher policies, aiming for optimal performance with minimal interactions in the training environment. The authors formalize teacher attributes within the context of infinite-horizon Markov Decision Processes (MDPs), defining three types of teachers: partial, sufficient, and contradictory.

1. **Partial Teachers**: A teacher policy is considered partial if it can guide the agent from certain states to goal states more quickly than the optimal policy from those states. This indicates that the teacher offers useful advice in specific regions of the state space.

2. **Sufficient Teachers**: A teacher policy is deemed sufficient if it can reach a goal state from at least one starting state. A set of teacher policies is sufficient if a combination of them can achieve this as well. Conversely, insufficient teachers cannot help the agent reach goal states, highlighting the importance of learning from inadequate guidance.

3. **Contradictory Teachers**: A teacher policy is contradictory if following its advice results in longer paths to goal states compared to another teacher policy. This can hinder the agent's learning progress, necessitating the design of a behavioral policy that can effectively navigate such complexities.

The proposed method, AC-Teach, utilizes the actor-critic architecture, specifically incorporating Bayesian Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (DDPG), which enhances stability and performance in continuous action spaces. In this framework, the agent policy operates as the actor, while a critic network evaluates actions based on the state transitions stored in a replay buffer. The training involves alternating between experience collection and policy updates, leveraging a Bayesian approach to estimate the posterior value function for the critic, thereby improving the overall learning process. The paper concludes with a detailed methodology for implementing AC-Teach, emphasizing its adaptability to various off-policy actor-critic algorithms.
