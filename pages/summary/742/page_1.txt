The paper presents AC-Teach, a novel Bayesian Actor-Critic method designed for policy learning in reinforcement learning (RL) environments, specifically utilizing an ensemble of suboptimal teachers. The authors emphasize that effective exploration strategies are essential for improving sample efficiency, especially in long-horizon tasks with sparse rewards. AC-Teach distinguishes itself by guiding an RL agent's exploration through action suggestions from an ensemble of partial solutions, which may only address parts of the problem or even contradict one another.

The approach builds on the concept of learning from teachers, expanding it to a framework that can accommodate various teacher attributes, including partiality, insufficiency, and contradiction. By leveraging a probabilistic representation of expected outcomes from both teachers and the learner, AC-Teach directs exploration, mitigates dithering, and adapts to the learner's changing capabilities.

The authors evaluate a variant of AC-Teach with a Bayesian DDPG agent across three tasks: path following, robotic pick and place, and robotic cube sweeping. Results demonstrate significant improvements in sampling efficiency compared to several baselines, validating the effectiveness of using suboptimal teachers and highlighting the potential for faster convergence and enhanced performance in complex environments. The work underscores the value of heuristic solutions in guiding agent exploration and advancing policy learning in RL.
