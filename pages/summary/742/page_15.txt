This paper evaluates the performance of the AC-Teach algorithm in a Path Following task, focusing on its ability to handle various teacher sets, including those with random and adversarial teachers. The results show that AC-Teach significantly outperforms baseline methods in both convergence speed and overall performance, demonstrating its robustness against large and low-quality teacher sets. 

The evaluation involved multiple teacher configurations: sets E, F, and G, which included combinations of suboptimal partial teachers and additional random teachers, and set H, which featured a sufficient teacher alongside an adversarial one. AC-Teach excelled in these scenarios, effectively ignoring non-contributory random teachers and successfully adapting to the presence of the adversarial teacher.

Further analysis compared the policy choices made by AC-Teach and a DQN behavioral policy. Findings indicate that AC-Teach relied more on the learning agent over time, exhibited reduced policy switching due to its commitment mechanism, and ultimately favored the learner over the teachers by the end of training. These results suggest that AC-Teach is a more effective approach for leveraging teacher guidance in reinforcement learning contexts.
