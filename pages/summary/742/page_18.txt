This paper presents an approach to robot manipulation tasks, specifically focusing on a Hook Sweep problem where a robot arm must move a cube to a designated goal location. The scenario is designed such that the cube starts out of reach of the robot arm, necessitating the use of a hook to either sweep the cube closer or push it further away depending on randomized initial conditions. The robot's action and observation space are consistent with previous work by Plappert et al. The agent receives dense rewards defined as the negative L2 distance from the cube to the target location, with episodes lasting 100 steps without early termination.

The implementation features the BDDPG agent alongside BDDPG + DQN and DDPG + Critic baselines, building on existing frameworks in Stable Baselines. Training across multiple experiments involved alternating between task interactions and gradient updates to the policy, with specific hyperparameters for the ACT, DDPG, and DQN models detailed throughout the paper. Notably, commitment thresholds and decay rates were maintained consistently, while certain parameters were manually tuned to adapt to the challenges presented by the Mujoco tasks. The results are presented over five seeds, and observations and rewards were not normalized.
