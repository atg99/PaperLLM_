The paper presents a sensitivity analysis of an algorithm, as depicted in Fig. C.7, revealing that while many parameters maintain performance across a range of values, the dropout precision parameter significantly impacts the algorithm's effectiveness. Variations in sample sizes lead to notable drops in performance, highlighting the importance of this parameter in the regularization of the critic network and the commitment mechanism.

In the experimental setup, two tasks are described:

1. **Path Following**: An agent must navigate a 2D plane, visiting four corners of a square in a random order. The agent's observations include its current location, the goal point, and the number of goals left to visit. The agent's actions involve bounded 2D displacements, with a sparse reward system providing points for each corner visited, leading to a maximum possible return of four per episode.

2. **Pick and Place**: This task involves a robot manipulating a cube to place it at a designated target location. The cube's initial position and the robot's end effector are randomized at the start of each episode, while the target remains constant. The starting position of the cube is centered near specific coordinates with slight random offsets.

The structured experimental design allows for the examination of the algorithm's efficacy in diverse scenarios, revealing critical insights into parameter sensitivity and task performance.
