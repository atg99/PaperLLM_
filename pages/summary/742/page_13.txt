In this paper, the authors propose a novel approach for training reinforcement learning agents using a Bayesian critic and a behavioral target policy, referred to as AC-Teach. They utilize an ϵ-divergence loss for the critic to minimize extrapolation error, incorporating a behavioral target value for stable learning. The critic's loss function is designed to average over multiple Monte Carlo dropout samples of the critic's weights, allowing for robust performance.

The commitment probability estimation is introduced to evaluate the likelihood of a new policy yielding greater expected returns than a previously used one. This is achieved by modeling the action-values as independent Gaussian distributed random variables and fitting Gaussian distributions based on Monte Carlo samples. The authors derive the probability of one action-value being greater than another using the Gaussian cumulative distribution function.

To address the challenge of extrapolation error, the authors emphasize the importance of using the learner's critic to inform the behavioral policy, positing that this leads to stability similar to on-policy methods. They highlight the benefits of this approach in mitigating issues that arise from off-policy training.

Further experiments in path-following tasks demonstrate that the AC-Teach method achieves faster convergence and higher reward levels compared to baseline methods. The performance of the behavioral policies during training is also assessed, showcasing the quality of experience gathered for training the DDPG agent. Overall, the results indicate the effectiveness of the proposed method across various teacher setups.
