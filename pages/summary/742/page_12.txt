The paper presents the AC-Teach training loop, which incorporates an Actor-Critic framework with teacher policies to enhance learning in reinforcement learning scenarios. The algorithm operates over multiple epochs and episodes, where actions are taken based on a behavioral policy. Transitions are collected in a replay buffer for subsequent training of both the critic and actor components.

The value function is crucial in this context, as it decomposes into two components: the probability of reaching a goal from a given state and the efficiency of achieving that goal. In stochastic environments, the value of a state is influenced by the certainty of reaching goals and the speed of reaching them, whereas in deterministic scenarios, it primarily depends on speed.

The paper also defines several key concepts related to teacher policies in stochastic Markov Decision Processes (MDPs). Partial teachers are defined as those where an optimal policy yields a higher expected value in specific states. Sufficient teachers are those that have a non-zero value for some starting state, while teacher sets are sufficient if they can form a mixture policy that is also sufficient. Contradictory teachers are those whose advice leads to longer goal-reaching times or lower state values compared to another teacher policy.

Finally, the paper emphasizes the training of the Bayesian critic and agent policy, detailing how the samples collected by the behavioral policy are utilized in the training process, further enhancing the learning efficiency of the actor-critic model.
