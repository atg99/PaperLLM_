The paper discusses advancements in imitation and reinforcement learning (RL) from offline and online demonstrations, emphasizing the challenges and methodologies associated with training agents using multiple teacher policies. 

In the context of offline demonstrations, imitation learning traditionally relies on optimal expert samples. However, recent studies have shown that applying off-policy deep RL algorithms to suboptimal demonstrations can lead to instability, primarily due to function approximation and bootstrapping issues. To mitigate these problems, the paper introduces a behavioral policy capable of following any teacher from a diverse set, necessitating a strategy to handle the inherent instability of off-policy data.

The paper further explores online learning, where agents imitate available teacher policies during training. While some methods assume a single optimal teacher, others accommodate multiple teachers but often require selecting one teacher for the entire episode. This approach is inadequate when teachers provide partial solutions across different state spaces. The paper mentions existing methods that treat teachers as options but highlights their limitations, such as lack of support for continuous control.

The authors propose AC-Teach, a new DQN-based behavioral policy that can effectively utilize multiple teachers in various RL settings, including those requiring continuous control. They demonstrate that AC-Teach outperforms existing methods in experiments. 

The problem statement is framed within a discrete-time Markov Decision Process (MDP), detailing the components such as state space, action space, reward function, and transition dynamics. The goal is to learn a policy that maximizes the expected discounted sum of rewards, utilizing experience from both the behavioral and agent policies, which operate independently during training but only the agent policy is evaluated during testing.

The paper concludes by outlining the policy advice setting, where the behavioral policy can receive action suggestions from a set of teachers during training, thereby enhancing the learning process without relying on any single optimal teacher at test time.
