The paper presents the ECA module, designed to enhance convolutional neural network (CNN) architectures by improving channel attention through adaptive kernel size determination based on channel dimension. The authors propose a non-linear mapping between kernel size and channel dimension, allowing high-dimensional channels to utilize longer range interactions while low-dimensional channels use shorter ranges. The ECA module is integrated into existing CNN frameworks, replacing the Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) block, resulting in networks referred to as ECA-Net.

The effectiveness of the ECA module is evaluated through extensive experiments on large-scale datasets, including ImageNet for image classification and MS COCO for object detection and instance segmentation. The study examines the impact of kernel size on performance and compares ECA-Net with state-of-the-art methods. Implementation details outline the training protocols, data augmentation, and optimization strategies for various backbone models, including ResNet and MobileNetV2, ensuring consistency with established practices in the field. The results demonstrate the advantages of the ECA module across different tasks, indicating its potential for improving deep learning models in vision-related applications.
