This paper discusses the importance of avoiding dimensionality reduction in channel attention mechanisms, highlighting that a single fully connected (FC) layer performs better than two layers with dimensionality reduction. The proposed Efficient Channel Attention (ECA) module is developed without such reduction, allowing for more effective channel attention learning.

The paper introduces local cross-channel interaction in the context of channel attention. It describes how attention is computed using a parameter matrix W, with two variants: SE-Var2, which uses a diagonal matrix, and SE-Var3, which employs a full matrix to account for cross-channel interactions. While SE-Var3 demonstrates improved performance due to these interactions, it also increases model complexity.

To balance efficiency and effectiveness, the authors propose a block diagonal matrix approach (WG) that groups channels and learns attention locally. However, this method does not outperform SE-Var2, as it neglects dependencies among different groups. Instead, the authors introduce a band matrix (Wk) to capture local interactions more effectively and efficiently, leading to a lower parameter count compared to WG.

The ECA module, which employs a 1D convolution with shared parameters across channels, is shown to achieve similar performance to SE-Var3 while significantly reducing complexity. The coverage of local cross-channel interaction, determined by the kernel size of the 1D convolution, can be optimized for different CNN architectures, although manual tuning is resource-intensive. The findings indicate that the ECA module effectively captures local interactions while maintaining computational efficiency.
