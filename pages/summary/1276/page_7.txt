In this section, the paper compares the effectiveness of the proposed ECA module integrated into various deep convolutional neural networks (CNNs) against several state-of-the-art attention mechanisms. The evaluations are conducted on ResNet-50, ResNet-101, ResNet-152, and MobileNetV2, with a focus on both efficiency and effectiveness metrics, including model parameters, FLOPs, training/inference speed, and Top-1/Top-5 accuracy.

For ResNet-50, ECA-Net demonstrates a notable 2.28% improvement in Top-1 accuracy over the original ResNet-50, while maintaining similar model complexity compared to other attention methods like SENet and CBAM. ResNet-101 shows a 1.8% accuracy gain with comparable complexity, and ResNet-152 achieves a 1.3% improvement. ECA-Net also outperforms SENet by 0.5% in Top-1 accuracy with lower complexity.

Additionally, when tested on MobileNetV2, ECA-Net improves Top-1 accuracy by approximately 0.9% compared to the original MobileNetV2 and by 0.14% compared to SENet, while also being smaller and faster. 

The comparisons extend to state-of-the-art CNN models such as ResNet-200, Inception-v3, ResNeXt-101, and DenseNet, where ECA-Net101 outperforms ResNet-200 and shows competitiveness against ResNeXt-101 despite requiring fewer resources.

In the object detection context, ECA-Net is evaluated using Faster R-CNN and Mask R-CNN. Integration of the ECA module enhances detection performance significantly, surpassing SE block improvements by 0.3% and 0.7% in average precision (AP) for ResNet-50 and ResNet-101, respectively, and yielding a 1.8% to 1.9% improvement in AP for Mask R-CNN.

Overall, the results confirm the efficacy of the ECA module across various architectures, showcasing its potential to enhance performance while reducing computational costs.
