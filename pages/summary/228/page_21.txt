The paper discusses the impact of noise on training sensitivity and effectiveness across different hyper-parameter settings. It notes that while noise can increase the variability of training results based on the random seed, it has often yielded the best outcomes during limited hyper-parameter sweeps. Future research aims to better understand the interactions between learning rate, noise, EMA decay, and random seed in refining the training approach.

The effectiveness of this training strategy varies with batch size. Experiments indicated that larger batch sizes (1024-2048) frequently produced results slightly below those achieved with smaller sizes (256-768), which were utilized for various pre-trained weights. Further hyper-parameter tuning and adjustments to learning rate scaling may be needed to address this discrepancy.

The paper includes tables summarizing procedures and recommended value ranges for different classification tasks and architectures. It emphasizes the importance of strong augmentation and regularization for larger models. The training procedure C utilizes SGD with Nesterov's momentum and Adaptive Gradient Clipping (AGC), allowing for stable training with larger batches at higher learning rates. This method compensates for the absence of batch normalization in NFNets through enhanced augmentation and regularization.

Additionally, training Procedure D, which employs AdamP, has shown promising results, achieving a top-1 accuracy of 79.8 on ImageNet-1k. However, further experimentation is necessary, particularly with larger batch sizes. The paper suggests that there may be other effective training recipes that could outperform those discussed, highlighting the importance of tuning the recipe based on the target architecture. An AdamW recipe is also under development.
