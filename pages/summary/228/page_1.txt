The paper "ResNet strikes back: An improved training procedure in timm" by Ross Wightman, Hugo Touvron, and Hervé Jégou revisits the performance of the widely used ResNet-50 architecture by integrating recent advancements in training techniques, optimization, and data augmentation. Despite ResNet's established status as a benchmark in image classification, the authors argue that significant improvements in training practices since its introduction in 2015 warrant a reevaluation of its performance.

The authors conducted experiments using an enhanced training procedure and reported that with this new approach, a vanilla ResNet-50 achieved a top-1 accuracy of 80.4% on the ImageNet validation set at a resolution of 224x224, without relying on additional data or distillation techniques. They also provided competitive training configurations and pre-trained models within the timm open-source library, aiming to establish improved baselines for future research in the field.

The introduction emphasizes the challenges in measuring model performance improvements due to concurrent advancements in architecture (A) and training settings (T). The authors highlight the importance of separating the contributions of these two factors to accurately assess progress in image classification. Overall, the paper advocates for the adoption of updated training methodologies to enhance the effectiveness of classic architectures like ResNet in contemporary applications.
