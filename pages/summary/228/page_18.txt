This supplementary material elaborates on the augmentations and regularization techniques utilized in the timm library, which is essential for achieving top results in image classification tasks like ImageNet. It highlights the modifications made to various training components compared to their original implementations.

The timm library incorporates several data augmentation methods, including RandAugment, AutoAugment, AugMix, Random Erasing, and a combined approach for Mixup and CutMix. The primary augmentation strategy is Random Resized Crop with horizontal flipping.

RandAugment, a prominent variant of AutoAugment in timm, has two key hyper-parameters: M (distortion magnitude) and N (number of distortions applied per image). Initially, these parameters were intended to be human interpretable, but it was found that M did not consistently correlate with the strength of all augmentations. Specifically, the behavior of image enhancement operations like color and sharpness varied significantly with different values of M, leading to confusion.

To address these issues, the timm implementation introduces an 'increasing' mode, ensuring that all augmentation strengths rise with M. This adjustment makes the effect of increasing M more intuitive and introduces an additional hyper-parameter, MSTD, which applies Gaussian noise to the M value for each distortion. This enhancement prevents the occurrence of undesirably empty or heavily distorted images when using lower strength settings, thereby improving the overall effectiveness of the augmentation process.
