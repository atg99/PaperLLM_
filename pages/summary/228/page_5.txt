In this paper, we investigate the effectiveness of various data augmentation techniques and regularization methods in training neural networks. Our experiments reveal that setting all mixed concepts to a target of 1, rather than distributing them to sum to 1, aligns more closely with the principles of Mixup and CutMix, as it better reflects human recognition of mixed concepts.

We employed a combination of data augmentations, including Random Resized Crop, horizontal flip, and variants of RandAugment, Mixup, and CutMix, as previously utilized in the DeiT model. The timm library was referenced for additional details on these augmentations. 

We also examined different regularization techniques, such as weight decay, label smoothing, Repeated-Augmentation, and stochastic depth, noting that more regularization is beneficial for longer training schedules. For instance, label smoothing was only applied in the A1 training procedure, while RA and stochastic depth improved results at convergence but slowed early training. We highlighted the importance of adjusting hyper-parameters for different architectures, noting that with additional regularization, a ResNet-152 showed improved performance.

In terms of optimization, we found that while SGD is commonly used for convolutional networks, transformers and MLPs often utilize AdamW or LAMB optimizers. We observed that LAMB, combined with a larger batch size and binary cross entropy loss, consistently yielded better results than SGD. Consequently, we adopted LAMB with a cosine schedule as our default optimizer for training ResNet-50.

We compared our training procedures with existing methods in a series of experiments, focusing solely on the unmodified ResNet-50 architecture. Our findings indicate the significance of our proposed training approach, although we acknowledge that our comparison does not encompass all advanced training settings, such as distillation or pre-training methods.
