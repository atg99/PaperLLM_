The paper presents results from a study on various deep learning models trained using the A1 training procedure, focusing on their performance metrics such as peak memory usage, throughput, and accuracy. Table 4 summarizes the performance of different architectures, including ResNet, RegNet, SE-ResNet, EfficientNet, and Vision Transformers (ViT), under specific testing conditions using a single GPU. Metrics such as the number of parameters, floating point operations per second (FLOPs), throughput in images per second, peak memory usage in megabytes, and top-1 accuracy are provided for each model.

The results indicate that larger models tend to have higher accuracy but also consume more memory and computational resources. For instance, ResNet-152 achieves a top-1 accuracy of 82.0 with a peak memory usage of 1,355 MB, while EfficientNet-B4 shows a balance with a top-1 accuracy of 81.6 but a significantly higher memory usage of 5,058 MB.

In the section on transfer learning (Table 5), the authors evaluate different pre-training procedures across seven fine-grained datasets. They find that the A1 procedure yields the best downstream performance overall, while the default PyTorch pre-training and A2 procedure show similar results. However, A2 outperforms in specific cases like Imagenet-val and -v2. The A3 procedure, which employs a lower training resolution, resulted in considerably poorer performance, suggesting that resolution plays a critical role in transfer learning effectiveness. 

Overall, the findings emphasize the importance of model architecture and training procedures on both direct performance metrics and transfer learning capabilities across various datasets.
