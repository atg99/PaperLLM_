The paper addresses the need for improved training procedures for the vanilla ResNet-50 architecture, which has shown varying performance on the ImageNet-1k validation set, with reported accuracies ranging from 75.2% to 79.5%. The authors highlight the lack of a focused effort on optimizing the training process for this architecture, particularly in relation to hyper-parameter tuning and ingredient selection. 

To fill this gap, the paper proposes three distinct training procedures for ResNet-50 at an inference resolution of 224x224. These procedures incorporate different epoch counts (100, 300, and 600) along with tailored hyper-parameters. Notably, the authors introduce a departure from traditional cross-entropy loss, opting instead for a binary cross-entropy approach when utilizing Mixup and CutMix techniques, which treat the mixed concepts as present in synthesized images.

The study emphasizes the importance of evaluating the stability of accuracy across multiple runs with varying seeds and discusses overfitting by comparing performance on ImageNet validation and ImageNet-V2 datasets. The authors also advocate for the simultaneous optimization of both architecture and training procedures, demonstrating that identical training processes are insufficient for fair comparisons of different architectures.

Additionally, the paper includes ablations in Section 5 and supplemental materials detailing augmentation variants from the timm library and alternative training procedures for ResNet-50 that differ significantly from the proposed methods, which may yield improved performance on various architectures and tasks.
