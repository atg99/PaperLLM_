This paper discusses the evaluation of training procedures for ResNet-50, focusing on quantifying performance sensitivity to random factors and assessing overfitting through different test sets. The authors present their training procedure A1, which reportedly surpasses the state-of-the-art performance on ImageNet with a vanilla ResNet-50 architecture at a resolution of 224x224. Procedures A2 and A3 also yield high performance but require fewer resources.

The performance of various architectures trained using these procedures is compared, highlighting improvements over existing literature, particularly for older models similar to ResNet-50. Notably, A2 outperforms A1 for models like ViT-B, indicating that hyper-parameters may need adjustment for longer training schedules. For example, A2 achieves 81.8% top-1 accuracy with ResNet-152, which increases to 82.4% with enhanced regularization.

The paper emphasizes the significance of performance measurements influenced by random factors, such as weight initialization and the order of image processing. To analyze this variability, the authors conduct experiments using different random seeds, noting that the standard deviation of performance on ImageNet validation is approximately 0.1, with a higher variance observed on ImageNet-V2. The mean performance from their main weights (seed 0) is reported to overestimate average performance by about 0.13%.

Moreover, the authors stress the importance of controlling overfitting during their exploration process, ensuring that peak performance is accurately measured without excessive bias from validation accuracy. Overall, the findings contribute to understanding the robustness and generalizability of training procedures in deep learning architectures.
