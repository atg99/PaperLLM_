The paper presents an ablation study comparing Binary Cross Entropy (BCE) and Cross Entropy (CE) loss functions within the context of a specific training procedure (A2) for ResNet-50 models. The study evaluates the effects of varying learning rates (LR) and weight decay (WD) on model performance, with particular attention to the impact of repeated data augmentation.

Key findings include:

1. **Weight Decay Sensitivity**: The optimal weight decay values were identified in the range of 0.02 to 0.03. Further increases in weight decay risk divergence, indicating its sensitivity and interaction with other regularization methods.

2. **Loss Function Comparison**: Transitioning from BCE to CE resulted in a notable decrease in performance. BCE allows for a flexible approach to multi-class classification, particularly when using techniques like Mixup and Cutmix, while CE enforces a probability distribution that may not be beneficial in this context. The highest accuracy was achieved with BCE.

3. **Impact of Repeated Augmentation**: While repeated augmentation provided a modest performance boost, its effectiveness varied with different hyper-parameter settings. In certain scenarios, it was neutral or even detrimental, yet it proved advantageous in the most accurate configurations (A1 and A2).

Overall, the study highlights the significance of carefully selecting loss functions and regularization parameters to optimize model performance, particularly in complex augmentation scenarios.
