The paper presents a detailed analysis of training procedures and their effects on the performance of neural networks, particularly ResNet-50 and DeiT-S architectures, on ImageNet and various transfer-learning tasks. 

Figure 3 illustrates the evolution of top-1 accuracy on ImageNet validation set over 300 training epochs using the A2 procedure. It highlights a significant variance in accuracy during the early epochs, with a notable 10% performance difference observed at epoch 100. However, the accuracy variance decreases substantially towards the end of training, indicating convergence among the networks.

Table 5 compares performance across different pre-training recipes on several datasets, showing that the choice of training procedure can lead to different conclusions regarding model superiority. For instance, using A2 training, ResNet-50 outperforms DeiT-S on ImageNet validation, while DeiT-S performs better when trained with T2. However, the differences in performance are not statistically significant when focusing solely on A2.

The section on ablations discusses the impact of hyper-parameters and ingredients on training performance. It emphasizes the importance of the learning rate, noting that a higher value of 5 x 10^-3 yields the best results. The paper also mentions that certain modifications, especially concerning the optimizer, require adjustments across multiple parameters to maintain performance.

Overall, the findings underscore the complexity of model training and the implications of different procedures and parameters on neural network performance.
