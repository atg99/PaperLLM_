The paper presents a comparative analysis of various architectures in the context of ImageNet classification, specifically focusing on the performance of these models when trained using a newly optimized training procedure based on ResNet-50. The study emphasizes that deeper and larger models require tailored adaptations for optimal regularization. 

Table 3 summarizes the training costs and accuracies of different architectures, detailing factors such as training and test resolutions, training time in hours, number of GPUs used, peak memory per GPU, and accuracy percentages. The results indicate varying performance across architectures, with ResNet-152 achieving an accuracy of 82.0% and the EfficientNet-B4 reaching 82.9%. 

The paper also addresses the variability in model performance across different datasets, noting that high accuracy on one dataset does not guarantee similar performance on another. This is illustrated through a correlation analysis between ImageNet validation and ImageNet V2 datasets, indicating a limited relationship. Furthermore, the authors highlight the importance of systematic reporting on multiple datasets to mitigate measurement noise and advocate for a clear distinction between validation and test results.

Lastly, sensitivity analysis is conducted to explore performance variability across training epochs, providing insights into the stability of model performance over time. Overall, the findings underscore the necessity for careful hyperparameter tuning and rigorous evaluation methods in model training and assessment.
