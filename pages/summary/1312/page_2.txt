This paper discusses various augmentation techniques applied to audio input for Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) tasks, particularly using the Listen, Attend and Spell (LAS) network architecture. It presents two main figures showcasing different augmentation methods: Figure 1 illustrates individual augmentations such as time warp, frequency masking, and time masking, while Figure 2 depicts the effects of specific augmentation policies, including LibriSpeech basic (LB) and LibriSpeech double (LD). 

The paper details the parameters for these augmentations, including the number of frequency and time masks, with a focus on how these masks are applied. For instance, time masking is constrained to a maximum width relative to the number of time steps, ensuring effective augmentation without excessive distortion. The augmentation policies are summarized in Table 1, which lists parameters like mask width and the number of masks used.

The LAS network architecture is described, highlighting its end-to-end training capability and structure, including Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) and bi-directional Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) units. The text emphasizes the importance of learning rate schedules for enhancing ASR performance, specifically when combined with augmentation techniques. The paper introduces two learning rate schedules—Basic and Double—along with their specific parameters.

Overall, the study underscores the significance of augmentation and learning rate strategies in improving the performance of ASR systems, providing a comprehensive framework for implementing these methods effectively.
