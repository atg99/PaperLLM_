The paper presents a study on the effects of label smoothing and augmentation techniques on Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) performance using the Switchboard 300h dataset. Results indicate that label smoothing, when combined with augmentation, leads to improved Word Error Rates (WER) across various configurations, as shown in Table 4. Specifically, training with label smoothing consistently improved performance, particularly in the LAS-6-1280 model.

The work compares its findings with previous studies, highlighting significant advancements in WER with and without language models (LMs) across different methods in Table 5. The authors note that shallow fusion parameters do not transfer well between differently trained networks, emphasizing the need for specific tuning.

The discussion section reveals that while time warping is beneficial, it is the least influential among augmentations and should be reconsidered under resource constraints. The authors also observe that label smoothing can introduce instability in training, particularly when combined with augmentation, prompting the introduction of a label smoothing schedule during training phases.

The paper concludes that augmentation effectively mitigates overfitting issues, transforming them into underfitting challenges. Addressing underfitting through larger networks and extended training schedules yields significant performance improvements. The study highlights that SpecAugment is a powerful technique for enhancing ASR performance, achieving state-of-the-art results on both the LibriSpeech and Switchboard datasets.
