The paper discusses enhancements in the performance of a trained neural network for automatic speech recognition (ASR) through an extended training schedule, referred to as schedule L. This schedule involves specific parameters for training the largest model and incorporates label smoothing for specific time steps. 

In section 3.3, the authors introduce shallow fusion with a recurrent neural network (RNN) language model to further improve results. The next token in the decoding process is determined by combining the scores from the ASR model and the language model, along with a coverage penalty. Different configurations of RNNs are utilized for the LibriSpeech and Switchboard datasets, with hyperparameters optimized through grid search.

In the experiments section, the authors detail their methodology for training on LibriSpeech 960h and Switchboard 300h datasets, utilizing SpecAugment for data augmentation. The results indicate that augmentation significantly boosts performance, particularly when combined with larger network architectures and longer learning schedules. The LAS-6-1280 model achieves state-of-the-art results on the LibriSpeech dataset, even without a language model, though performance can be further improved with shallow fusion.

The paper presents detailed results in tables, comparing various networks and their performance with and without language models across different augmentation policies. The findings affirm the effectiveness of their approach, showcasing substantial improvements in word error rates (WER) compared to previous systems.

In summary, the study highlights the importance of training schedules, data augmentation, and language model integration in achieving superior ASR performance.
