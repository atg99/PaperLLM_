The paper discusses the implementation details of a Random Projection (RDP) technique for anomaly detection and clustering tasks. The RDP architecture comprises a fully connected layer with specific hidden units, followed by a leaky-ReLU layer. For anomaly detection, it utilizes 50 hidden units, is trained for 200 epochs using Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD), and employs a fixed learning rate of 0.1 with a batch size of 192 samples. The training is repeated 30 times to ensure statistical stability. Competing methods are adapted into ensemble formats for fair comparison.

For clustering, the RDP architecture is similar but employs 1,024 hidden units and is trained for 1,000 epochs. Clustering requires more semantic information, thus necessitating a more expressive representation compared to anomaly detection. The dimensions for representation and projection are kept equal to simplify parameter tuning, with 50 for anomaly detection and 1,024 for clustering. The paper notes that deeper networks were less effective compared to shallower ones, likely due to insufficient supervisory signals for learning deeper representations.

The runtime performance of RDP during testing is comparable to the most efficient competing methods, as detailed in the appendices. Additionally, the datasets used for these tasks, including one containing DDoS attack data, are summarized in provided tables. Overall, the paper emphasizes the effectiveness and efficiency of the RDP method in both anomaly detection and clustering contexts.
