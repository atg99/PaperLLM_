The paper discusses the limitations of traditional projection methods under specific data distribution assumptions, such as Gaussian distribution, which can lead to misleading proximities. The authors propose a Robust Distance Preserving (RDP) model that optimizes local proximity information through neural networks, resulting in a distance-preserving space that retains richer semantics compared to the projected space. 

The experimental section evaluates the RDP model's performance in unsupervised tasks, specifically anomaly detection and clustering. The RDP model is compared against five state-of-the-art methods, including iForest, autoencoder, REPEN, DAGMM, and RND, across 14 diverse datasets encompassing various domains such as network intrusion and credit card fraud detection. Performance metrics used are AUC-ROC and AUC-PR, with results averaged over 10 independent runs.

The findings, presented in tables, indicate that RDP consistently outperforms the competing methods in both AUC-ROC and AUC-PR across at least 12 datasets, showing statistically significant improvements at the 95% confidence level. The RDP model employs a novelty loss for anomaly detection and incorporates a boosting process to enhance anomaly modeling by iteratively filtering out likely anomalies. Further analysis in the paper examines the contributions of different components to the overall performance.
