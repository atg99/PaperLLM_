The ablation study presented in section 4.2.3 investigates the contributions of two loss functions, Lrdp and Lclu aux, to the performance of the RDP model, alongside the effects of various supervisory sources. The results, summarized in Table 5, indicate that the full RDP model, which utilizes both loss functions, outperforms its variants that exclude one of the losses. Notably, the model relying solely on Lrdp shows performance comparable to the full model, while using Lclu aux alone can lead to significant performance declines on datasets like R8, 20news, and Olivetti. This suggests that Lrdp is crucial for the RDP model's overall effectiveness. Additionally, the study finds that distances derived from non-linear random projection are particularly effective on certain datasets such as Olivetti and RCV1, while the supervisory sources yield similar performance on other datasets.

In the related work section, the paper discusses self-supervised learning as an increasingly popular method for representation learning, highlighting various techniques that utilize context information for learning high-level representations. Examples include methods in natural language processing and image processing, as well as video data analysis. The paper notes that while these methods are effective, they primarily focus on image and video data, which limits their applicability to traditional tabular data. The authors position their method as a solution to bridge this gap, emphasizing its focus on high-dimensional tabular data.

Furthermore, the paper outlines established unsupervised representation learning approaches for tabular data, such as random projection, PCA, manifold learning, and autoencoders. It points out the computational challenges associated with PCA and manifold learning in large-scale high-dimensional contexts, contrasting them with the efficiency of random projection and the limitations of autoencoders in preserving local structure. The proposed method aims to facilitate generic representation learning while allowing for the integration of task-dependent losses to enhance the richness of learned representations.
