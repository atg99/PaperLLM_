The paper presents RDP, a novel approach for anomaly detection that significantly outperforms existing methods in terms of AUC-ROC and AUC-PR metrics on multiple datasets. RDP integrates the strengths of two prior methods, REPEN and RND, by utilizing a pairwise distance-based ranking loss (Lrdp) and an auxiliary loss (Lad aux). The results show that RDP achieves over 10% improvement in AUC metrics compared to the best competing method across six datasets, including Donors, Ad, Bank, Celeba, Lung, and U2R.

The analysis indicates that RDP's performance benefits from both Lrdp and Lad aux, leveraging their complementary strengths. However, in some cases, such as the Backdoor and Census datasets, RDP's performance is slightly hindered when Lrdp is not as effective. An ablation study highlights the importance of the components within RDP, revealing that the full model consistently outperforms its variants. The use of distance information from non-linear random projections in RDP and the linear projections in SRP SS enhances the learning of class structures, leading to improved performance compared to the original space.

Overall, RDP demonstrates robust capabilities in anomaly detection, achieving notable results across diverse datasets and showcasing the effectiveness of combining different loss functions and projection techniques.
