In this paper, we present the Random Distance Prediction (RDP) model, which learns features in an unsupervised manner by predicting distances of data in a randomly projected space. The fundamental principle behind RDP is that random mapping can approximately preserve distances, allowing the representation learner to optimize for consistent proximity information while correcting inconsistencies. This results in representations that effectively maintain distance preservation.

Our approach is validated through extensive experiments in two unsupervised tasks: anomaly detection and clustering. The results demonstrate that the RDP-enabled models significantly outperform traditional methods across 19 real-world datasets. We also express our intention to extend the RDP model to accommodate various data types, thereby expanding its applicability.
