In this section, the performance evaluation of clustering methods is presented, focusing on a comparison between the proposed Random Distance Projection (RDP) and four established unsupervised representation learning techniques: HLLE, Sparse Random Projection (SRP), autoencoder (AE), and Coherence Pursuit (COP). The evaluation leverages K-means clustering applied to new representations generated by these methods, using Normalised Mutual Info (NMI) and F-score as performance metrics. The original feature space serves as a baseline for comparison.

The experiments involve five high-dimensional real-world datasets, which are transformed into tabular forms for analysis. The findings indicate that RDP outperforms the other methods on three datasets and secures second place on the remaining two, demonstrating its capability in capturing class structures more effectively than AE. Notably, RDP consistently surpasses SRP and COP as well. The original feature space performs best for the Sector dataset, attributed to its relevant features, while HLLE excels in complex manifold situations, although RDP shows more stable performance across all datasets.

The results are summarized in tables that detail NMI and F-score performances, highlighting RDP's effectiveness in clustering tasks compared to other methods. Additionally, RDP can also utilize advanced representation learning techniques for data transformation, with preliminary results indicating promising outcomes.
