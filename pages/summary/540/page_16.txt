The paper evaluates the performance of the RDP method in clustering tasks on five datasets, comparing its runtime with four competing methods. The results indicate that RDP outperforms or matches the performance of other methods across various datasets, with notable efficiency in terms of runtime.

A key focus is on RDP's applicability to raw text and image data. The authors propose a method to convert raw data into feature vectors using techniques like TF-IDF for text and pixel representation for images. They then compare RDP to advanced representation learning methods, specifically Doc2Vec, which learns document representations directly.

The findings reveal that RDP significantly outperforms Doc2Vec on the R8 dataset and performs comparably on 20news. This suggests that RDP's focus on local proximity information is more effective for clustering than the semantic representations generated by Doc2Vec. Furthermore, a combination of Doc2Vec and RDP (Doc2Vec+RDP) shows substantial improvements, particularly on the 20news dataset, indicating that RDP can optimize feature spaces effectively when provided with accurate supervision.

Overall, the study highlights RDP's potential advantages in clustering tasks, particularly when dealing with complex data types like raw text and images.
