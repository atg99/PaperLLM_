The paper discusses various datasets utilized for anomaly detection and clustering tasks, highlighting their characteristics and the corresponding percentage of anomalies present. Key datasets for anomaly detection include DDoS, Donors, Backdoor, Creditcard, and Lung, each having varying sizes and anomaly rates. The authors reference studies that transformed classification datasets into anomaly detection datasets by utilizing rare classes as anomalies, ensuring the generated anomalies are semantically relevant.

In the anomaly detection task, the performance of the proposed RDP model and its variants is evaluated using AUC-PR metrics. Results demonstrate that the RDP model, particularly when employing the Lrdploss, significantly outperforms its counterparts across most datasets. The ablation study reveals that both the Lrdploss and the task-dependent loss (Lad_aux) are crucial for effective performance, while the boosting process contributes but is less critical. Furthermore, it is noted that distances calculated in random mapping spaces yield better supervisory sources compared to the original space.

For clustering tasks, the NMI performance of the RDP model is assessed. The results indicate that the model retains strong performance when using the Lrdploss, aligning with prior observations. Overall, the findings underscore the importance of specific loss functions in enhancing the performance of anomaly detection and clustering models.
