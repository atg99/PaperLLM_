The paper presents an evaluation of the runtime performance of the RDP method in both anomaly detection and clustering tasks across various datasets. In the anomaly detection section, RDP is compared to five competing methods, demonstrating comparable speed to efficient methods like iForest and RND, while significantly outpacing deep learning methods such as REPEN and DAGMM on large and high-dimensional datasets. However, RDP shows slower performance on smaller datasets due to a constant base runtime associated with its boosting process.

In the clustering section, the testing runtime of RDP and four other methods is analyzed using K-means clustering on five datasets. RDP performs comparably to efficient methods like SRP and AE, while it is substantially faster than HLLE, which requires extensive computation for nearest neighbor searches. The results indicate that RDP maintains a desirable runtime consistency, making it suitable for high-dimensional and large-scale data applications.
