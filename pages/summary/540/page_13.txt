The paper presents a comprehensive evaluation of the RDP model and its variants in the context of anomaly detection and clustering tasks. The results are summarized in two tables: Table 8 showcases the AUC-PR performance of RDP and its variants across various datasets, revealing that RDP consistently outperforms its alternatives in most cases, with a notable performance drop observed when using Lrdp alone. This emphasizes the importance of incorporating Lrdp in RDP for effective representation learning. 

Table 9 focuses on the NMI performance of RDP in clustering tasks, indicating that RDP maintains stable performance across different representation dimensions, particularly excelling in datasets like Olivetti. A sensitivity analysis highlights that while RDP shows stability in both anomaly detection and clustering, it struggles with richer representations in higher dimensions due to insufficient supervision from random distances. The paper recommends a representation dimension of 50 for optimal anomaly detection performance across diverse datasets. 

Overall, the findings underscore RDP's reliability as a supervisory source compared to conventional methods, highlighting its effectiveness in learning expressive representations for both anomaly detection and clustering tasks.
