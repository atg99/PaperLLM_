This section discusses the application of linear and non-linear projection techniques in machine learning, particularly focusing on random projection and kernel methods.

In subsection 3.1, linear projection through random projection is introduced as an effective technique for maintaining distance preservation among data points. Given a dataset X in RD dimensional space, random projection employs a random matrix A to reduce dimensions to a lower K-dimensional space. The Johnson-Lindenstrauss lemma is cited, which ensures that the distances between points are approximately preserved when projected. Specific equations (5), (6), and (7) demonstrate how the distances and inner products are maintained under random projections, particularly emphasizing that as the projected dimension K increases, the preservation of inner products remains significant.

Subsection 3.2 shifts focus to non-linear projection methods, highlighting their relation to kernel functions. Kernel functions are essential for obtaining reliable distance or similarity measures, projecting non-linearly separable data into a linearly separable space. The relationship between non-linear random mappings and kernel methods is established through a randomized mapping function that approximates kernel evaluations. By utilizing Fourier transform-based mapping matrices, the inner products in the projected space can serve as unbiased estimators of the kernel evaluations.

In subsection 3.3, the model leverages random distances as supervisory signals to learn class structures. The optimization problem is formulated to minimize prediction errors based on the distance matrix derived from the random projections or kernel methods. The approach aims to effectively capture local proximity information in the data, indicating the model's capability to reveal underlying class structures.

Overall, the paper underscores the utility of both linear and non-linear projection methods in preserving essential data properties while facilitating dimensionality reduction and distance approximation.
