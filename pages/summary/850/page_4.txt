The paper presents an algorithm for MRI reconstruction using a federated learning (FL) framework, termed FL-MR. The algorithm is designed to address the challenges of training machine learning models across multiple institutions while preserving patient privacy. The input consists of datasets from K institutions, local and global epochs, a learning rate, and model parameters for local and global models.

The algorithm initializes model parameters and iteratively updates local models at each institution through several local epochs. Each institution computes a reconstruction loss and updates its model parameters based on its unique dataset, which may introduce biases due to variations in data characteristics across institutions. To mitigate these biases, the FL-MR framework aggregates local model updates at a central server, allowing the global model to learn from diverse data without direct access to it.

The paper highlights the limitations of traditional approaches that require combining datasets, which raises privacy concerns. Instead, the proposed FL-MR method facilitates collaborative training by averaging model updates, thereby enabling the global model to leverage multi-domain information effectively.

Furthermore, the paper introduces the concept of FL-MR with Cross-site Modeling (FL-MRCM) to address domain shifts that can degrade model performance. This approach draws inspiration from federated adversarial alignment techniques used in classification tasks, enabling effective training despite the constraints of federated learning.

Overall, the proposed framework allows for improved MRI reconstruction by combining insights from multiple institutions while adhering to privacy regulations.
