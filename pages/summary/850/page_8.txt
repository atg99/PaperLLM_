The paper discusses a federated learning (FL) framework designed to enhance magnetic resonance (MR) image reconstruction while preserving privacy across multiple institutions. In Scenario 2, the effectiveness of this FL-based method is evaluated by comparing its performance with models trained using data from a single institution. The proposed FL-MRCM method shows significant improvements in structural similarity index measure (SSIM) and peak signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR), particularly on smaller datasets, achieving an SSIM increase from 0.9001 to 0.9232 and a PSNR rise from 31.29 to 32.44 in T2-weighted sequences.

The study also includes an ablation analysis to assess the contribution of the proposed cross-site modeling approach. Results indicate that models utilizing cross-site modeling outperform those without it, as evidenced by improved error suppression in critical areas such as skull and lesion regions. The paper emphasizes the advantages of multi-institutional collaborations in MR image reconstruction tasks, demonstrating that the FL-based framework can effectively address domain shifts without requiring direct data sharing.

In conclusion, the research presents a robust FL approach that leverages collaborative data from various institutions to improve MR image reconstruction, showcasing better generalization and significant performance gains through cross-site modeling.
