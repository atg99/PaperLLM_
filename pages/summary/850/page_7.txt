The paper presents a comparison of various methods for reconstructing MRI images, specifically focusing on T1 and T2-weighted images across different datasets. The results are illustrated in Figure 5, which includes qualitative assessments of methodologies such as fastMRI, HPKS, IXI, and BraTS. The second row of each sub-figure displays the absolute differences between the reconstructed images and the ground truth.

The authors evaluate their proposed Federated Learning approach (FL-MR) against other strategies, including a model trained on data from a single institution and a fused model combining outputs from multiple institutions. The findings, summarized in Table 1, indicate that FL-MR demonstrates superior generalization and performance in preserving privacy while enhancing reconstruction quality. The FL-MRCM variant further improves results by addressing domain shifts.

Quantitative comparisons are provided in Table 2, showing metrics such as SSIM and PSNR across different strategies and data centers. The FL-MRCM method achieves the highest scores for both T1 and T2-weighted images, indicating its effectiveness in producing high-quality MRI reconstructions while maintaining privacy. The results suggest that FL-MR and its enhanced version, FL-MRCM, outperform traditional methods, underscoring the advantages of federated learning in medical imaging contexts.
