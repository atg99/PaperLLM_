The paper presents a quantitative comparison of various models trained with different strategies in Scenario 1, focusing on their performance in reconstructing images from T1-weighted and T2-weighted MRI scans. The evaluation is based on the structural similarity index measure (SSIM) and peak-signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR) across multiple datasets, including HPKS, IXI, and BraTS, which consist of T1 and T2-weighted images from different subjects. 

Table 1 summarizes the results, showcasing the average SSIM and PSNR values for each method, indicating that models such as FL-MRCMF and F, H, I yield the highest performance metrics. The dataset used for training and testing consists of a significant number of subjects, ensuring a robust evaluation of the models' generalizability. The findings suggest that the proposed framework outperforms traditional models trained on single datasets, highlighting its effectiveness in handling data from diverse sources. 

Additionally, a schematic in Figure 4 illustrates the different training strategies utilized in the experiments, emphasizing the distinction between source sites for training data and target sites for testing data. Overall, the results underscore the importance of diverse training strategies in improving the quality of reconstructed medical images.
