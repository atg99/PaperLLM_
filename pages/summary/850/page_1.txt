Despite their effectiveness, these deep learning approaches often require large datasets for training, which presents challenges related to data collection, high acquisition costs, and strict medical data privacy regulations.

To address these issues, the authors propose a federated learning (FL) framework that allows multiple institutions to collaborate and share their MR data without compromising patient privacy. However, the authors note that models trained under the FL paradigm may still suffer from suboptimal generalizability due to domain shifts caused by differences in data collected across institutions, which can include variations in sensors, disease types, and acquisition protocols.

To mitigate this challenge, the authors introduce a cross-site modeling technique for MR image reconstruction. This approach aligns the learned intermediate latent features from various source sites with the distribution of latent features at the target site, thereby improving model performance. The paper includes extensive experimental results that demonstrate the efficacy of the proposed framework, suggesting it is a promising method for utilizing multi-institutional data while preserving patient confidentiality and enhancing MR image reconstruction quality.

The authors provide their code for further research and implementation, emphasizing the potential of federated learning in advancing medical imaging technologies.
