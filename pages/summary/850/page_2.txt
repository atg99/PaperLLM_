This paper addresses the challenges of MRI image reconstruction from partial k-space data, highlighting the limitations of deep learning methods that typically require large, diverse datasets, which are difficult to collect due to privacy concerns and the labor-intensive nature of data gathering. To overcome these issues, the authors propose a Federated Learning (FL) framework that enables decentralized training across multiple institutions while preserving patient privacy. In this approach, institutions maintain their private data and only share model parameters with a central server, allowing for collaborative model development without compromising data security.

Despite its advantages, the FL setting may result in suboptimal model generalizability due to domain shifts caused by variations in data from different institutions, such as differences in imaging equipment and protocols. The paper introduces a solution called Federated Learning-based Magnetic Resonance Imaging Reconstruction (FL-MR) combined with Cross-site Modeling (FL-MRCM), which aligns the latent feature distributions between source and target domains to mitigate these domain shifts. This is achieved through a two-step optimization process: local networks are trained on private data, and then an adversarial domain identifier aligns the latent spaces of different datasets.

The contributions of the paper include the development of FL-MR for privacy-preserving MRI reconstruction, the FL-MRCM method to address domain shifts, and extensive experiments that provide insights into the application of FL in MR image reconstruction. The paper also reviews related work on MR image reconstruction, covering traditional methods and recent advancements in deep learning and federated learning.
