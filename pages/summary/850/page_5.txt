The paper addresses the main shift problem in federated learning (FL)-based MRI reconstruction, focusing on aligning latent space distributions between source and target domains. The authors propose a method that utilizes the encoder of reconstruction networks to project input data into latent spaces, where an adversarial domain identifier is introduced to facilitate the alignment process. The training of this identifier is adversarial, aimed at distinguishing between latent features from different sites while simultaneously training the encoder to confuse the identifier, thereby enhancing privacy by restricting access to raw data.

The loss functions for the adversarial domain identifier and the encoders are defined to promote this alignment, combining reconstruction and adversarial losses into a comprehensive training loss. The methodology employs a U-Net architecture for reconstruction, with specific hyperparameters set for effective training. 

In the experimental section, the authors outline two scenarios to evaluate the proposed framework: Scenario 1 assesses generalizability by training on data from various institutions while testing on unseen datasets, which reflects typical clinical settings where institutions may lack fully-sampled data. Scenario 2 involves collaborative training across all institutions to demonstrate the benefits of federated learning. The datasets used for experiments include T1-weighted images from the fastMRI dataset, comprising images from thousands of subjects.

Overall, the study presents a robust approach for improving MRI reconstruction through federated learning while ensuring compliance with data sharing regulations and enhancing model generalizability.
