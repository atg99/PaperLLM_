The paper discusses two main categories of target detection algorithms: two-stage and one-stage. Two-stage algorithms, such as R-CNN and its variants, first extract target information through a Region Proposal Network (RPN) before predicting target locations and categories. In contrast, one-stage algorithms like YOLO and SSD directly predict these features, leading to faster detection speeds, though often with lower accuracy.

The challenges in remote sensing target detection include the small size of targets, interference from environmental factors like shadows and lighting, and variations in target scales. Traditional methods such as template matching and image analysis have been used but are limited by their poor robustness and manual design requirements. 

Deep learning approaches have shown improved accuracy and robustness by eliminating manual feature design. Models like Faster R-CNN have been employed, but their effectiveness can be hindered by environmental factors. YOLO-V3, noted for its speed and accuracy, employs a feature pyramid network and predicts targets at multiple scales, improving the detection of small targets. Various enhancements to YOLO-V3 have been introduced to further optimize performance, demonstrating advancements in the field of remote sensing target detection.
