The paper presents experimental comparisons of a feature extraction network for remote sensing target detection, highlighting the impact of various configurations on performance metrics such as mean Average Precision (mAP) and frames per second (FPS). Notably, the introduction of dense connection modules based on Darknet53 in the fourth experiment led to a significant improvement in mAP from 77.10% to 84.38% and an increase in detection speed from 29.7 FPS to 32.3 FPS. The results indicate that the proposed network enhances both detection performance and speed.

Further analysis of detection layers revealed that increasing the detection scale improved mAP by up to 5.95% for larger targets, with even greater enhancements for small targets like aircraft, which saw accuracy increases of 8.72%. The 'Res 3' module was found to mitigate gradient fading and reduce parameters, contributing to faster detection speeds.

The ablation experiments confirmed that the proposed network improves feature extraction and detection accuracy for multi-scale targets, particularly small ones, while maintaining speed comparable to YOLO-V3, thus meeting real-time requirements. To further validate the effectiveness of the approach, the authors conducted visual comparisons of detection results against YOLO-V3 and Faster RCNN, demonstrating the superiority of their method.
