The paper presents a pseudocode for non-maximum suppression (NMS) utilized in an enhanced version of the YOLO-V3 model for remote sensing target detection. The algorithm processes original bounding boxes and their associated confidence scores to filter out redundant detections based on the Intersection Over Union (IOU) metric with a predefined threshold.

In the experimental section, the authors validate the performance of their improved YOLO-V3 against the original YOLO-V3, YOLO-V3 tiny, and other leading algorithms on two datasets: RSOD and USC-AOD. The experiments were conducted in a controlled environment using specific hardware and software configurations, with a total of 50,000 training steps. The learning rate was adjusted throughout the training process.

The loss function used for training the network is outlined, consisting of three components: coordinate prediction error, IOU error, and classification error. The coordinate prediction error is further detailed, with an emphasis on the weights assigned to the errors and the parameters used to define the bounding boxes.

Overall, the study aims to demonstrate the effectiveness of the modified YOLO-V3 in enhancing target detection capabilities in remote sensing applications.
