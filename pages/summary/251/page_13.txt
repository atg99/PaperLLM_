The paper discusses the challenges of training effective target detectors for remote sensing images using conventional datasets, highlighting the necessity for specialized remote sensing image databases. The authors selected the RSOD (Remote Sensing Object Detection) and UCS-AOD (University of California, Santa Barbara Aerial Object Detection) datasets for their experiments. The RSOD dataset includes aerial images of four target categories: aircraft, playgrounds, overpasses, and oil tanks, with targets classified by size based on their ground truth area. The analysis indicates that aircraft targets are predominantly small, while playgrounds and overpasses are larger, and oil tanks vary between small and medium sizes. 

The datasets encompass targets under various lighting conditions, heights, and shooting angles, with a dense distribution that complicates detection due to small pixel representation after processing through convolutional and down-sampling layers. Statistical data on the datasets reveal that the majority of targets are small or medium-sized. The paper also presents experimental results comparing the accuracy and real-time performance of the proposed algorithms with state-of-the-art models on the RSOD dataset, detailing metrics such as mean Average Precision (mAP) and speed, as well as performance differences based on target sizes.
