The paper discusses the application of the K-means algorithm to generate anchor boxes for object detection, inspired by previous models like Faster-RCNN, YOLO-V2, and YOLO-V3. The K-means algorithm aims to cluster anchor boxes in a way that maximizes the Intersection Over Union (IOU) with adjacent ground truth boxes, which is measured using the formula for distance defined as d(box, centroid) = 1 - IOU(box, centroid). The IOU itself is calculated as the ratio of the overlap area to the union area between the predicted box and the ground truth.

The pseudocode for the K-means process involves initializing cluster center points representing the dimensions of the anchor boxes, calculating the distance from each ground truth to these centers, and then updating the cluster centers based on the average dimensions of the assigned boxes. This iterative process continues until convergence is achieved.

The results showed that as the number of clusters increased, the average IOU became more stable. For the proposed model, 12 anchor boxes were selected, categorized into four scales based on their dimensions. The specific sizes of the anchor boxes are detailed, with three boxes designated for each scale. The findings highlight the effectiveness of K-means clustering in optimizing anchor box selection for improved object detection performance.
