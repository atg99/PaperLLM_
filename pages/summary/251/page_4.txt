The paper discusses the architecture of YOLO-V3, which utilizes Darknet53 as its feature extraction network. Darknet53 is characterized as a full convolutional network that consists of 53 convolutional layers, employing primarily 1x1 and 3x3 convolutional kernels. To mitigate information loss from pooling layers, it incorporates five residual modules, enhancing feature extraction and addressing gradient fading through the use of residual learning.

YOLO-V3 employs a feature pyramid network (FPN) approach, performing five down-sampling operations on input images. The output feature map is reduced to 1/32 of the original image size, and the last three down-sampled layers are utilized for target detection. The network predicts at three different scales: 13x13 for large targets, 26x26 for medium targets, and 52x52 for small targets. This multi-scale prediction allows for the extraction of both deep-level semantic information and shallow-level fine-grained information.

To facilitate feature fusion, YOLO-V3 performs up-sampling on the down-sampled feature maps, aligning the dimensions of the feature maps from different scales for concatenation. This design enables the integration of varying levels of feature information, enhancing the network's detection capabilities. The structural details of YOLO-V3 and its feature extraction network are visually represented in accompanying figures and tables.
