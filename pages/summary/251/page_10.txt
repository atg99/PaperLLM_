The paper discusses the process of detecting targets through bounding box predictions based on values generated by a neural network. The bounding box coordinates are calculated using predicted values and offsets relative to a grid cell. The equations provided illustrate how to compute the bounding box positions and dimensions based on the predicted values, utilizing a sigmoid function for normalization.

Following the bounding box predictions, the paper outlines the non-maximum suppression (NMS) algorithm, which is designed to merge overlapping bounding boxes that correspond to the same target. The NMS process involves three main steps: identifying the bounding box with the highest confidence score, comparing its intersection over union (IOU) with other boxes, and removing those that exceed a predefined IOU threshold. This procedure is repeated for the next highest confidence box until all boxes are processed, ensuring that only the most relevant bounding boxes are retained in the final detection results. The pseudocode for the NMS algorithm is also included to summarize its implementation.
