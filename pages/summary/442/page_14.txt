The Vision Transformer utilizes a self-attention mechanism to better capture long-range dependencies in image data, which is often a limitation in traditional convolutional neural networks (CNNs). The process begins with the sequentialization of volumetric images into non-overlapping patches, which are then transformed into embeddings through a linear layer. Positional encodings are added to retain spatial information, and the embedded patches are processed through multiple layers of Transformer blocks that include Multi-Head Self-Attention (MHSA) and Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) components.

The paper also categorizes the application of Transformers in U-Net architectures into two types: as a complement to CNN-based structures and as standalone Transformer architectures. The TransUNet model is highlighted as an early example of incorporating ViT into U-Net, aiming to enhance long-range modeling capabilities while preserving local features through CNNs. This model processes 3D medical images slice-by-slice and combines global context from Transformers with high-resolution local features from CNNs.

Additionally, the paper notes the limitations of standard Transformer models for segmentation tasks due to their 1D processing of tokenized information. As a response, TransBTS is introduced, which aims to effectively model both local and global information across spatial and depth dimensions, addressing the computational complexity and information loss associated with fixed-size tokenization in volumetric data.

Overall, the integration of Transformers into U-Net architectures represents a significant advancement in improving the performance of medical image segmentation by leveraging both local and global feature representations.
