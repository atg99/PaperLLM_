The paper discusses various architectures that integrate Transformers into image segmentation tasks, particularly in medical imaging. It begins by outlining the Vision Transformer (ViT) structure, which processes images by dividing them into fixed-size patches, linearly embedding them, and adding positional embeddings before feeding them into a Transformer encoder. While ViT includes a classification token for classification tasks, it is less applicable to segmentation, where the MLP head is often omitted.

The paper then introduces the TransUNet architecture, which combines Transformer layers in the encoder with CNN blocks to capture local representations and reduce computational demands. This hybrid approach improves performance in Brain Tumor Segmentation tasks compared to traditional CNN U-Net models.

Further, the GT U-Net structure is presented as a solution to prior segmentation methods' limitations in handling fuzzy boundaries while maintaining low computational complexity. By utilizing Group Transformers and a Fourier descriptor loss term, GT U-Net effectively minimizes misclassifications between background and foreground pixels, addressing critical challenges in medical imaging.

Lastly, the CoTr architecture is discussed, which enhances multi-scale functionality through a combination of CNN-based 3D residual blocks and Deformable Transformers to improve localization and convergence speed. This architecture aims to overcome the parameter overload issue found in TransUNet, where all image tokenization positions are treated equally.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of integrating Transformers in segmentation tasks to enhance the accuracy and efficiency of predictions in complex medical imaging scenarios.
