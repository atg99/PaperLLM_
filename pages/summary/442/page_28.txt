The evaluation metrics discussed in this section highlight the importance of various accuracy measures for assessing segmentation algorithms. Precision, recall, and the F1 score are fundamental metrics derived from the confusion matrix, providing insights into the model's performance in classifying pixels correctly. The accuracy metric offers a class average ratio of correctly classified pixels but may suffer from class imbalance issues, particularly in medical datasets. The Intersection over Union (IoU) and the Dice coefficient serve as measures of overlap between predicted and ground truth segmentation, with the Dice coefficient particularly relevant in binary segmentation contexts. The Hausdorff Distance quantifies segmentation error by measuring the maximum distance from points in the ground truth to points in the segmented contour.

In the experimental results section, the study focuses on skin lesion segmentation using the ISIC 2018 dataset, revealing challenges due to artifacts in dermoscopic images. The results indicate that while Dice scores are similar across CNN and Transformer-based methods, models with multi-scale feature representation, like U-Net++ and UCTransNet, outperform others in localizing abnormalities. Further analysis shows that Transformer models offer smoother segmentation results, particularly in cases of high background overlap, while CNN models tend to over-segment or under-segment lesions. UCTransNet is highlighted as achieving state-of-the-art results due to its effective combination of CNN hierarchical design and multi-scale feature fusion. The distinction in performance between MISSFormer and UCTransNet is noted, particularly regarding feature representation and down-sampling strategies.
