This paper provides a comprehensive overview of the U-Net architecture and its application in medical image analysis. It details the workflow involved in utilizing U-Nets for tasks such as classification, detection, and segmentation of biomedical signals and images. The process begins with image acquisition and preprocessing to standardize data for deep neural networks. A neural architecture search is then employed to identify the most efficient model for specific tasks, followed by post-processing steps to refine the network's output.

The paper emphasizes the significance of skip connections in the U-Net architecture, which facilitate the integration of deep, low-resolution semantic information with shallow, high-resolution local details. Initially introduced by Long et al., skip connections play a critical role in image segmentation by enabling the network to assign labels to each pixel, thereby enhancing localization capabilities. The paper also discusses various extensions and modifications of the classic U-Net design, underscoring its versatility and effectiveness in clinical applications like tumor growth monitoring and surgery planning.
