The paper discusses the BiO-Net architecture, which enhances the traditional U-Net model by incorporating both forward and backward skip connections. These connections facilitate the preservation of low-level visual features from the encoder while integrating high-level semantic information from the decoder. The architecture allows for recursive traversals, improving performance without increasing parameters, although it may extend training time.

In addition, the paper examines the attention U-Net, which introduces attention gates (AGs) in the skip connections. This mechanism focuses on relevant regions of the input image, particularly beneficial in biomedical imaging where organ shapes and sizes vary significantly across patients. By using attention gates, the model effectively highlights areas of interest while avoiding the computational burden of additional models for organ localization.

The attention mechanism is further elaborated through equations that calculate attention coefficients based on input feature maps and a gating signal derived from coarser scales. This integration enhances segmentation accuracy and eliminates the need for cascaded networks.

Moreover, the paper mentions adaptations like the attention U-Net++ and the RA-UNet, which build on the attention U-Net by incorporating nested structures and attention residual modules, respectively. These adaptations aim to tackle specific challenges in tasks such as liver tumor segmentation, improving focus on critical image areas while managing noise and redundancy. Overall, the proposed architectures leverage skip connections and attention mechanisms to enhance segmentation performance in complex imaging scenarios.
