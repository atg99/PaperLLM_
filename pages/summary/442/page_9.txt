Li et al. proposed the CR-Unet, integrating spatial recurrent neural networks (RNNs) into the U-Net architecture to enhance the segmentation of ovaries and follicles from ultrasound images. Given the spatial correlation among closely located follicles and between follicles and ovaries, the spatial RNNs aim to mitigate the loss of spatial information caused by max-pooling in traditional U-Net structures. The RNN modules process feature maps using four independent translations to capture local spatial information in multiple directions, followed by concatenation and a 1×1 convolution to produce enriched feature maps. This method is particularly effective in minimizing false positives and accurately identifying small follicles, although it introduces greater complexity and computational demands during training, which Li et al. addressed by incorporating deep supervision.

In a different approach, Lachinov et al. tackled the challenge of segmenting images with varying dimensions by introducing projective skip connections in a U-Net model. Their design allows for the encoder to retain a classic structure while modifying the decoder to address the dimensionality reduction necessary for specific medical protocols. By applying average pooling to the encoder feature maps, they ensure compatibility with decoder features, ultimately utilizing Global Average Pooling to generate the final segmentation results.

Azad et al. presented the BCDU-Net, enhancing skip connections through a bi-directional convolutional long-term-short-term-memory (LSTM) module. This approach combines high-resolution encoder maps and semantic-rich decoder maps using non-linear functions, improving segmentation accuracy by leveraging dependencies in both forward and backward processing paths.

Overall, these advancements in U-Net architectures emphasize the importance of spatial context, dimensionality management, and the integration of advanced memory mechanisms to improve medical image segmentation outcomes.
