The paper discusses various datasets utilized for medical image segmentation, specifically in the context of cancer diagnosis. It highlights three key datasets: SegPC, ISIC 2018, and Synapse.

1. **SegPC**: This dataset focuses on the segmentation of plasma cells in microscopy images of patients with Multiple Myeloma, a type of blood cancer. It contains 775 images, which are annotated for nucleus and cytoplasm. The dataset is divided into training (70%), validation (10%), and testing (20%) sets, with all images resized to 224x224 pixels.

2. **ISIC 2018**: This dataset consists of 2,594 dermoscopy images used for early melanoma detection. Given the rising melanoma cases, early detection is crucial for improving survival rates. The dataset is resized to 224x224 pixels and includes annotations for cancerous and non-cancerous lesions, with 1,815 images for training, 259 for validation, and 520 for testing.

3. **Synapse**: This multi-organ segmentation dataset includes 3,779 axial contrast-enhanced abdominal CT images. It is associated with the MICCAI 2015 Conference and provides annotations for eight organ classes. The images are also resized to 224x224 pixels for consistency in data preparation.

The paper further discusses the importance of selecting appropriate loss functions in segmentation networks, emphasizing Cross Entropy (CE) and Dice Sørensen Coefficient (DSC). CE is derived from Kullback-Leibler divergence, measuring dissimilarity between predicted and ground truth distributions. DSC evaluates the similarity between samples and was introduced as a loss function to enhance segmentation performance.

Overall, the study underscores the significance of these datasets and methodologies in improving automated diagnostic tools for cancer, while also detailing the complexities involved in the segmentation tasks.
