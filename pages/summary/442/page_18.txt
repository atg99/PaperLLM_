The paper discusses various transformer-based architectures for image segmentation, focusing on their ability to capture both local and global dependencies. 

The MedT architecture employs a dual-branch approach, where a global branch captures long-range dependencies at the original image resolution, while a local branch processes small patches to model local details. The outputs from both branches are combined using a 1x1 convolution layer to produce the final segmentation mask. MedT has shown superior performance on several medical image datasets, including Brain US and MoNuSeg, evaluated using Dice and IoU metrics.

The Swin-Unet architecture follows a U-shaped design, integrating an encoder, bottleneck, and decoder based on the Swin transformer blocks, with skip connections linking the encoder and decoder.

The MISSFormer architecture consists of a hierarchical encoder and decoder, utilizing enhanced transformer blocks and a context bridge to effectively model dependencies across different scales.

Lastly, the D-Former architecture includes dynamic position encoding blocks and local/global scope modules, structured to enhance segmentation capabilities through its D-Former blocks.

The paper highlights the inherent challenges transformers face, particularly their quadratic computational complexity, which limits their effectiveness in dense vision tasks like segmentation and detection due to difficulties in capturing inter-patch pixel dependencies.
