The paper presents a comparative analysis of energy consumption, data delivery rates, and scalability of centralized and distributed approaches in the context of machine learning and computational intelligence (ML/CI) algorithms. 

In terms of energy consumption, centralized approaches show 28.6% low, 57.1% average, and 14.3% high consumption, while distributed approaches have 21.1% low, 57.9% average, and 21.1% high consumption. Overall, the total energy consumption is distributed as 24.2% low, 57.6% average, and 18.2% high.

Regarding data delivery rates, centralized approaches exhibit 0.0% low, 37.5% average, and 62.5% high rates, whereas distributed approaches present 25.0% low, 50.0% average, and 25.0% high rates. The overall data delivery rate totals are 12.5% low, 43.8% average, and 43.8% high.

Scalability comparisons indicate that centralized approaches have 35.7% low, 28.6% medium, and 35.7% high scalability, while distributed approaches show 47.4% low, 31.6% medium, and 21.1% high scalability. The total scalability is 42.4% low, 30.3% medium, and 27.3% high.

The analysis highlights that most algorithms utilize a first-order radio/energy model, which is favored for its simplicity and its focus on energy spent during transmission and reception. Some algorithms also adopt the CC2420 radio model, which provides a more accurate representation of real-world deployments. However, these models may not be suitable for non-terrestrial wireless sensor network applications.
