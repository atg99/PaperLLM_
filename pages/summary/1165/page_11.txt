The paper discusses various algorithms aimed at enhancing energy efficiency and optimizing network performance in wireless sensor networks (WSNs). 

In the work by Kumar et al., a neural network (NN) based approach is introduced for energy-efficient clustering and routing. This algorithm employs a linear programming (LP) formulation to select nodes with higher residual energy for optimal routing while minimizing costs. The process consists of three phases: set-up, routing, and data transmission. The set-up phase involves electing cluster heads (CHs) based on a cost metric, while the subsequent phases focus on identifying the most energy-efficient route to the base station (BS). Comparative results indicate that this approach outperforms the PEACH algorithm by increasing the percentage of alive nodes.

The paper also covers reinforcement learning (RL) strategies in WSNs, where agents learn to optimize performance through experience. One notable algorithm, CLIQUE, utilizes Q-learning for role-free clustering, allowing nodes to independently decide on their roles as CHs based on routing costs. Simulation results show CLIQUE's superiority over traditional random cluster head assignment in terms of energy consumption, data delivery, and communication overhead.

Additionally, Ramli et al. propose a cooperative RL algorithm for dual-hop clustered networks, which enables cluster members to share historical channel information for better decision-making. This approach involves an exploration phase for updating channel weights and an exploitation phase for channel usage based on weight thresholds.

Finally, the paper introduces swarm intelligence (SI), which draws inspiration from the collective behavior of social insects and animals. SI algorithms, including Particle Swarm, Ant Colony, and Bee Colony Optimizations, are highlighted for their applicability in WSNs, leveraging the limited resources of sensor nodes to improve performance.
