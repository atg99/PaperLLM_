The paper discusses GABEEC, a genetic algorithm-based approach for energy-efficient clustering in wireless sensor networks (WSNs). GABEEC operates in two phases: a static set-up phase where clusters are created, and a dynamic steady-state phase where cluster heads (CHs) are selected based on residual energy. The algorithm employs a binary representation of the network to optimize energy use, resulting in improved network longevity compared to LEACH and other algorithms.

Additionally, the paper examines neural networks (NNs) as a mathematical model inspired by biological neuron networks, consisting of input, hidden, and output layers. NNs are utilized for sensor fusion, data mining, and clustering. Two NN-based clustering strategies are highlighted:

1. Cordina & Debono propose a Self Organizing Map (SOM) approach that minimizes energy consumption by optimizing cluster formation and includes mechanisms for CH election, rotation, and load balancing. This method shows enhanced network lifetime and data delivery compared to LEACH.

2. Kumar et al. introduce an NN-based clustering and routing algorithm that employs linear programming to select nodes with high residual energy, aiming to minimize routing costs and maximize network lifetime through a structured three-phase process: set-up, routing, and data transmission. 

Overall, both approaches demonstrate significant improvements in network efficiency and longevity over traditional methods.
