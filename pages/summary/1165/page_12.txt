Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) is an evolutionary computation technique inspired by social behavior patterns in nature, such as bird flocking and fish schooling. It operates using a population of random solutions, referred to as particles, which are evaluated for their fitness using a defined fitness function. Recent advancements have applied PSO to enhance Wireless Sensor Networks (WSNs) through energy-aware clustering strategies.

One significant approach, PSO-C, focuses on energy-efficient clustering for WSNs. This method involves a centralized, distance-based strategy that selects cluster heads (CHs) based on their energy levels and proximity to non-CH nodes. Nodes communicate their residual energy and positions to a base station (BS), which selects CHs with above-average energy for each round. Simulations indicate that PSO-C can increase data delivery to the BS by up to 101% compared to traditional methods like LEACH and LEACH-C, while also extending the network's operational lifetime.

Kuila and Jana propose another PSO-based framework that combines clustering and routing algorithms designed to optimize energy use. Their approach employs a unique particle encoding scheme that balances transmission distance with a multi-objective fitness function. The protocol includes a bootstrapping phase, route setup, and clustering phase to ensure efficient communication among nodes. Their results demonstrate significant improvements in network lifetime and data delivery compared to genetic algorithm-based approaches and other clustering strategies.

PSO-HC introduces a hierarchical clustering model aimed at maximizing network lifespan and scalability by minimizing active CHs and employing two-hop communication. This method consists of setup and steady phases, where nodes update their neighbor tables with Received Signal Strength Indicator (RSSI) values and energy information, facilitating efficient data transmission to the BS.

Overall, these PSO-based techniques effectively enhance the performance and energy efficiency of WSNs, leading to improved data delivery rates and extended network lifetimes.
