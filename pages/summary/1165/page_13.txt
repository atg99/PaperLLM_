The paper discusses various optimization algorithms for energy-efficient clustering and routing in wireless sensor networks (WSNs). 

The PSO-HC method utilizes a base station (BS) to identify eligible cluster heads (CCHs) based on energy levels and employs a Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) algorithm to select the best cluster heads. This approach uses Time Division Multiple Access (TDMA) for data transmission, allowing nodes to enter sleep mode after transmitting data, thus conserving energy. Results indicate that PSO-HC achieves lower average energy consumption and better link quality compared to other algorithms like LEACH and PSO-C.

MPSICA introduces a fast routing recovery scheme that maintains multiple disjoint paths from source nodes to super-nodes and the sink. This centralized approach enhances fault tolerance and energy efficiency by optimizing paths for energy consumption. The results show MPSICA has a lower energy depletion ratio and reduced packet delivery delays compared to EEHC and ICE protocols.

TPSO-CR presents a two-tier PSO protocol that combines clustering and routing optimization to improve energy usage and network reliability. The BS calculates average energy levels, selecting only nodes above this threshold as potential CCHs. TPSO-CR shows a lower number of non-clustered nodes and higher throughput while maintaining energy consumption levels comparable to some established protocols, making it effective in both homogeneous and heterogeneous scenarios.

Overall, these methods demonstrate significant advancements in energy efficiency and network performance in WSNs through intelligent clustering and routing strategies.
