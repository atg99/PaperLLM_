The paper discusses three energy-efficient clustering algorithms for wireless sensor networks: T-ANT, EBAB, and ACO-C, each inspired by ant behavior.

T-ANT employs a clustered data dissemination strategy that utilizes foraging and brood sorting behaviors to establish a uniform distribution of cluster heads (CHs). During the cluster setup phase, nodes that possess an ant become CHs and broadcast their IDs. The performance of T-ANT is compared to the Time-Controlled Clustering Algorithm (TCCA) and a multihop version of LEACH (m-LEACH). Results indicate that T-ANT consumes slightly less energy per round and significantly outperforms the others in maintaining a higher number of alive nodes.

EBAB, or the Energy Balanced Ant-Based Routing Protocol, focuses on balancing energy consumption by forming clusters of unequal sizes based on proximity to the base station (BS). Nodes calculate their distance from the BS and compete to become unique CHs when necessary. CHs communicate using an enhanced Ant Colony Optimization (ACO) method, which employs Forward Ants (FA) and backward ants to optimize routing. Comparisons with LEACH demonstrate that EBAB extends network lifetime and increases data reception at the BS by 1.7 times.

ACO-C, or Ant Colony Optimization for Clustering, is a centralized protocol where the BS selects potential CHs based on their residual energy. It uses the ACO algorithm to optimize clustering and communication. Each CH coordinates data transmission through a TDMA schedule and aggregates data for the BS. ACO-C is tested in two scenarios with 100 nodes and shows superior performance in terms of the number of alive nodes and data received at the BS compared to LEACH, LEACH-C, and PSO-C.

Overall, these algorithms showcase advancements in energy efficiency and network longevity in wireless sensor networks through innovative clustering strategies based on ant behavior.
