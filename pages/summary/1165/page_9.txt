The paper discusses various Genetic Algorithm (GA) approaches for clustering in Wireless Sensor Networks (WSN) aimed at extending network lifetime. Key contributions include:

1. **Wazed et al.** propose a GA model that optimizes the routing of relay nodes, allowing them to transmit data efficiently to either simple nodes, other relay nodes, or the base station (BS). This model is compared to two traditional routing models, Multi-Hop Data Transmission Model (MHDTM) and Minimum Transmission Energy Model (MTEM), demonstrating a significant increase in network lifetime.

2. **Hussain et al.** focus on using GA to form clusters dynamically, utilizing only active nodes to prevent early node failures. Their centralized approach, managed by the BS, emphasizes minimizing energy consumption and enhancing network longevity. The results show better performance in maintaining alive nodes compared to LEACH and other models.

3. **LEACH-GA** enhances the LEACH protocol by incorporating a preparation phase for selecting cluster heads (CHs) and candidate CHs. This centralized protocol optimizes energy consumption and increases the number of operational nodes per round, thereby improving network lifetime.

4. **GABEEC** introduces a binary representation for nodes, where CHs are dynamically selected based on residual energy after an initial static setup. This method also aims to maximize energy efficiency and network longevity.

Overall, the paper highlights the effectiveness of GA-based clustering methods in improving the performance and lifespan of WSNs compared to traditional algorithms.
