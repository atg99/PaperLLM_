The paper discusses the PSO-ECHS (Particle Swarm Optimization-based Energy Efficient Cluster Head Selection) algorithm proposed by Srinivasa Rao et al. This algorithm aims to enhance energy efficiency in wireless sensor networks (WSNs) through a two-phase process: cluster head (CH) election and cluster formation. The CH election phase utilizes a PSO approach that factors in the residual energy of nodes and their proximity to the base station (BS). Nodes initially communicate their location and energy levels to the BS, which applies the PSO algorithm to determine optimal CH positions. Unlike traditional methods where non-CH nodes join based solely on distance, PSO-ECHS employs a weight function (CH_Weight) that incorporates distance, remaining energy, and the degree of the CHs.

The algorithm’s effectiveness is evaluated across three scenarios regarding the BS's location: centered, top right corner, and outside the field. Experiments are conducted with varying node counts (300, 400, 500, and 700) and compared against LEACH, E-LEACH, LEACH-C, PSO-C, and Least Distance Clustering (LDC) algorithms. Results indicate that PSO-ECHS outperforms the other methods in energy consumption and maximizes network lifetime, achieving the highest packet reception by the BS across all scenarios.

Additionally, the paper introduces Ant Colony Optimization (ACO), a nature-inspired metaheuristic that addresses complex combinatorial optimization problems. ACO mimics the behavior of ants using pheromones to communicate and find optimal paths. This algorithm has been effectively applied in WSN clustering to enhance network performance.
