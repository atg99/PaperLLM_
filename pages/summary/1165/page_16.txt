The paper discusses two advanced algorithms for optimizing energy consumption in wireless sensor networks (WSNs): ACA-LEACH and MRP, along with an overview of Bee Colony Optimization (BCO) protocols.

The ACA-LEACH algorithm enhances traditional LEACH by integrating the Ant Colony Algorithm (ACA) into the inter-cluster routing process. It aims to minimize energy usage of cluster heads (CHs) and extend network lifetime. Key features include selecting CHs based on node residual energy and proximity, with a mechanism to choose the more energy-efficient CH when distances between CHs fall below a threshold. ACA-LEACH employs pheromone values to determine optimal paths between CHs and the sink, ensuring minimal energy consumption. Evaluation shows that ACA-LEACH outperforms traditional LEACH in terms of lower average energy consumption and a higher number of active nodes.

MRP, or Multipath Routing Protocol, utilizes Ant Colony Optimization for clustering in WSNs. It operates in three phases: cluster formation, multipath construction, and data transmission. CHs are selected based on their residual energy, neighbor count, and received signal strength indicator (RSSI). MRP uses three types of ants for path discovery: search ants (SANTs), backward ants (BANTs), and abnormal ants (AANTs), with a mechanism to re-elect CHs when energy levels drop significantly. Performance metrics indicate that MRP achieves superior average energy and lower energy consumption compared to TEEN and other multipath algorithms, thereby significantly enhancing network lifetime.

Additionally, the paper briefly introduces Bee Colony Optimization (BCO) protocols, which draw inspiration from the foraging behavior of honeybees. BCO is characterized by its ability to solve complex clustering problems efficiently, leveraging the self-organizing and proactive behaviors of bees during food sourcing and recruitment.

Overall, both ACA-LEACH and MRP demonstrate effective strategies for optimizing energy efficiency in WSNs, with BCO providing a complementary approach to tackling challenging clustering problems.
