The paper discusses various routing techniques in Wireless Sensor Networks (WSNs), focusing on group-based and hierarchical routing methods. Group-based algorithms, such as Group based Mobile Agent Routing (GMAR), utilize mobile agents for data aggregation within groups. Hierarchical routing involves clustering nodes to enhance energy efficiency and network longevity by creating a virtual hierarchy. Each cluster is managed by a Cluster Head (CH), which communicates with other CHs to optimize data transmission to the sink node, thereby minimizing energy consumption. The Low-Energy Adaptive Clustering Hierarchy (LEACH) is highlighted as a prominent clustering algorithm that employs probability and signal strength for CH selection, facilitating local data fusion and aggregation. However, LEACH's single-hop configuration limits its communication range. The paper also mentions other hierarchical algorithms like PEGASIS, TEEN, EEHC, PEACH, and HEED, which improve scalability, energy efficiency, and fault tolerance in large networks. Additionally, it categorizes WSN protocols based on path establishment, operational methods, and next hop selection strategies, providing a comprehensive overview of routing protocol classifications in WSNs.
