The paper discusses the application of a Bayesian network to analyze resilience in Brazil using the GeNIe modeler, which facilitates model building based on Bayes' inference theory. The network is structured in three levels: the top level outlines key national and local activities, the intermediate level encompasses three resilience pillars, and the bottom level represents the overall resilience output. Despite the deterministic nature of the indicators, the three resilience pillars exhibit a probability distribution of different states due to the conditional probability tables (CPTs) and the Bayesian inference approach utilized. The analysis indicates that Brazil's resilience is most likely at a "Moderate" state, with the probabilities distributed as follows: 16% High, 28% Good, 31% Moderate, 19% Low, and 7% Vulnerable. The Bayesian network also allows for backward analysis, enabling the determination of necessary indicator levels to achieve a predetermined resilience state, which is beneficial for system design and improvement.
