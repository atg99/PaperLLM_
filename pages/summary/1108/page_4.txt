The paper discusses various methodologies utilizing Bayesian Networks (BN) to analyze interdependencies among critical infrastructure systems and their resilience. The research aims to understand how these interdependencies affect the overall system fragility. Several studies are highlighted, including Cai et al. (2017), who developed a universal resilience metric for infrastructure systems using a BN approach to optimize designs against hazards like earthquakes and cyber-attacks. Hosseini and Barker (2016) introduced a resilience quantification methodology based on three parameters: absorptive, adaptive, and restorative capacities, demonstrated through a case study on an inland waterway port.

Further contributions include Hosseini et al. (2016a), who quantified supply chain resilience for sulfuric acid manufacturers, and Hossain et al. (2019b), who ranked port infrastructure assets while considering operational and disruption risks. Eldosouky et al. (2017) proposed an analytical resilience index to assess the impact of critical infrastructure components on failure probabilities, applied to hydropower dams and their interdependence with the power grid.

The paper also contrasts static approaches with dynamic modeling. Tabandeh et al. (2018) developed a probabilistic indicator-based formulation to estimate societal impacts, employing Dynamic Bayesian Networks (DBN) to integrate predictive models. Although BNs have been applied in various fields, a notable gap exists in dynamic resilience assessment through quantitative methods. The limitations of BN include its inability to capture dynamic behaviors, feedback loops, and the time-dependent nature of restoration processes. While some studies have attempted to use DBN for dynamic resilience, the transition models over time remain inadequately defined.
