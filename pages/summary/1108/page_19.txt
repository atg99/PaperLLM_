The paper discusses a time-dependent resilience analysis using Dynamic Bayesian Networks, emphasizing that resilience is a process rather than a static state. Traditional Bayesian Networks lack the capability to incorporate time in their assessments, prompting the need for a dynamic approach. The proposed dynamic resilience model is grounded in the four components of resilience defined by Bruneau and Reinhorn: 

1. Robustness (R1) - the system's ability to withstand stress while maintaining functionality.
2. Redundancy (R2) - the availability of alternative resources during recovery when primary resources fail.
3. Rapidity (R3) - the system's capacity to limit losses and prevent further disruptions, represented by the recovery phase's functionality curve slope.
4. Resourcefulness (R4) - the human element and the ability to mobilize necessary resources.

This model is presented as a more comprehensive tool for analyzing dynamic events compared to earlier static models. The analysis includes a sensitivity evaluation indicating the system's current state as vulnerable, with specific metrics and a reachable range for resilience values provided.
