The paper discusses the use of Bayesian Networks (BN) to evaluate resilience, specifically in the context of Brazil, through the lens of the Hyogo Framework for Action (HFA). It begins with the formulation of joint probability distributions (JPD) for a set of variables, represented mathematically, highlighting the relationships between these variables and their parent sets.

The HFA, established by the United Nations in response to rising natural and man-made disasters, serves as a global blueprint aimed at minimizing risks associated with such hazards. Originally introduced in 2005, the framework encourages countries to embed resilience measures within their national laws. Countries are evaluated on their progress through a detailed questionnaire submitted to the UN, which assigns scores based on a five-point scale reflecting their commitment to risk management.

The HFA focuses on significantly reducing disaster-related losses and is structured around three pillars of resilience: reduced vulnerability, robustness of institutions, and adaptive recovery capacity. Additionally, the UNISDR outlines five priorities to operationalize these strategic goals into actionable activities. The paper emphasizes the importance of these frameworks in enhancing community resilience against disasters.
