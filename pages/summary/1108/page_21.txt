The paper discusses the concept of resilience in engineering systems through the use of Dynamic Bayesian Networks (DBN). Each Bayesian Network (BN) generates performance points, which collectively form a resilience function that illustrates the system's performance changes from an initial stable state to a fully recovered state. This resilience function can be translated into a resilience index, with various methods available for quantification, such as measuring the area above the resilience curve to represent "loss of resilience" as proposed by Bruneau and Reinhorn and Cimellaro et al. 

The paper also establishes a mathematical foundation for dynamic resilience analysis using a probabilistic approach. The joint probability distribution is articulated in terms of static and dynamic variables, where static variables are represented as C, and dynamic variables as Z. This formulation includes hazard variables and resilience characteristics, such as redundancy and resourcefulness, and emphasizes the interdependencies among these variables over time. The equation presented captures the joint probability of these variables, providing a comprehensive framework for analyzing resilience in engineering systems.
