The paper discusses a Dynamic Bayesian Network (DBN) and its representation, illustrating the structure through figures. Figure 1(a) depicts a static snapshot of the DBN, while Figure 1(b) introduces the general DBN format, highlighting the temporal plate where variables are repeated in each time step and static nodes that appear only once. Figure 1(c) illustrates the unrolled DBN, showing how temporal arcs connect variables within the temporal plate across multiple time steps, with constant variables appearing singularly.

The transition model for the DBN is mathematically represented, detailing how the probability of a variable at time t depends on its parents in the temporal plate and contemporaneous variables. The joint distribution of a DBN sequence, considering additional anchor and terminal variables, is also outlined, emphasizing the relationships between these variables.

The paper further explores a resilience model based on definitions by Bruneau and Reinhorn, identifying three key indicators of resilience: reduced failure probability (vulnerability), reduced consequences from failure (robustness), and reduced time to recovery (recoverability). This framework aims to quantify resilience within the context of the DBN.
