This paper introduces a static framework for modeling systems of a static nature, specifically focusing on assessing system performance at a given moment. It utilizes Bayesian Networks (BN) to quantify resilience, demonstrated through a case study evaluating Brazil's resilience. Although BNs effectively represent physical-causal models, they require significant computational resources for learning and updates, which are essential for resilience modeling, particularly in monitoring potential disruptive events.

To address this, the paper presents a dynamic framework aimed at quantitatively assessing the resilience of dynamic systems, such as critical infrastructures and communities, using Dynamic Bayesian Networks (DBN) as an inference tool. DBNs can be developed from expert knowledge, databases, or a combination of both, making them valuable in disaster resilience contexts where such information is abundant. The DBN framework allows for transient analysis following disruptions, aiding in modeling the recovery process of affected systems. 

The resilience framework is mathematically formulated to integrate the probability distributions of all variable states, with a transportation network example illustrating its application. Results indicate the framework's capability to dynamically model complex systems, even in data-scarce scenarios. 

The paper is structured as follows: Section 2 reviews the fundamentals of BNs and DBNs. Section 3 presents the static resilience assessment framework using BN. Section 4 showcases an illustrative example of the static framework's applicability. Section 5 discusses the general framework for dynamic resilience modeling with DBN. Section 6 features a transportation network example to demonstrate the framework's effectiveness. Section 7 explores further considerations and applications, while Section 8 concludes with insights and proposed future work.
