The construction of a DBN follows similar principles to that of a BN, with the addition of temporal dynamics. Each time slice represents a snapshot of the system at a specific moment, and the relationships between the variables in different time slices are established through transition probabilities. The model can thus reflect how the states of variables evolve over time, accommodating the inherent complexities of dynamic systems.

In summary, Bayesian Networks (BN) provide a framework for modeling dependencies among variables in a static context, utilizing Bayes' Theorem for probability updates. Conditional Probability Tables (CPTs) quantify these dependencies, and sensitivity analysis helps identify key variables. Dynamic Bayesian Networks (DBN) extend this framework to incorporate time, enabling the analysis of systems that change over time through the use of multiple interconnected BNs.
