The paper discusses the concept of resilience in critical infrastructure systems, emphasizing three primary pillars: reduced vulnerability, robustness, and recoverability. Reduced vulnerability pertains to the lower likelihood of damage and failure in critical systems. Robustness refers to the extent of damage in terms of injuries, lives lost, and economic and social impacts. Recoverability indicates the time required to restore systems to their normal functionality following a disaster.

A resilient system is characterized by a low probability of failure, high robustness, and high recoverability. The paper outlines a framework for assessing resilience through indicators that experts select based on their relevance to the system. These indicators correspond to the three resilience pillars across different time spans: pre-disaster (probability of failure), during disaster (robustness), and post-disaster (recoverability).

Additionally, the paper introduces a network structure where indicators are connected to the resilience pillars, reflecting their contributions. This connectivity can be informed by past experiences or expert knowledge, with some indicators impacting multiple pillars. The overall resilience index is a composite measure derived from these factors, providing a comprehensive view of a system's resilience capabilities.
