Sensitivity analysis is a crucial technique for validating the probability parameters within a Bayesian network by examining how small changes in input probabilities impact output results. This process aids in identifying highly sensitive parameters, thereby guiding focused efforts to enhance the accuracy of the Bayesian network model.

In the presented example, an algorithm developed by Kjærulff and van der Gaag in 2000 is utilized to compute a comprehensive set of derivatives of the posterior probability distributions for target nodes in relation to the numerical parameters of the network. These derivatives highlight the significance of precision in the network's parameters, as a large derivative indicates that variations in a parameter will result in substantial changes in the posterior probabilities of the target nodes. Conversely, small derivatives suggest that even significant alterations in the parameter have minimal impact on the posteriors.

The analysis treats each state of the target node separately, revealing the most sensitive parameters for each specific state. Figures 7 to 9 illustrate the sensitivity analysis for the resilience target node across three states: High, Moderate, and Vulnerable. The analysis ranks the ten most influential parameters, with color-coding indicating the direction of change—red for negative and green for positive. Notably, parameter Q19 is identified as the most sensitive for the High state of resilience, while Q11 emerges as the most sensitive for the Moderate state. The order of sensitivity shifts for the Vulnerable state. It is important to note that these sensitivity outcomes are influenced by the conditional probabilities specified by the user.
