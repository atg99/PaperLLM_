The paper discusses the resilience of systems in the face of hazards, emphasizing the significance of damage levels and recovery processes. It outlines the four resilience components, labeled as R1 to R4, where R1 and R2 define potential damage from hazards and the system's initial functionality. Robust and redundant systems are noted to sustain less damage and maintain functionality post-disaster. The recovery process, influenced by R3 and R4, hinges on available resources and recovery capacity.

Additionally, the paper introduces Dynamic Bayesian Networks (DBN), which model system behavior over time with changing conditions. DBNs allow for connections between elements at different time steps, utilizing expert knowledge or historical data. The initial time step assesses system performance before hazard occurrence, while subsequent steps evaluate damage and recovery. The integration of resilience components into the DBN framework informs how the system's indicators evolve through the recovery period, illustrating the dynamic interplay between system characteristics and external hazards.
