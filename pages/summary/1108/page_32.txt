The paper discusses the modeling of 31 static and dynamic engineering systems using both quantitative and qualitative data, addressing uncertainties in inputs and variable relationships that result in probabilistic outputs. Two examples illustrate the framework's applicability: a static system assessing a country's resilience using indicators from the Hyogo Framework for Action and a dynamic transportation network.

In static analysis, resilience output is primarily determined by indicator states, where high uncertainty leads to increased standard deviation in resilience states. For dynamic analysis, resilience exhibits nonlinear behavior over time, influenced significantly by recovery variables and damage variables. A substantial drop in functionality post-disaster extends recovery time, underscoring the role of these variables in resilience assessment.

The paper advocates for a universal resilience framework to aid system designers in quantifying engineering resilience, promoting systematic understanding of system states during specific events. It emphasizes the framework's backward analysis feature, enabling decision-makers to identify variable inputs for achieving desired resilience states.

However, the proposed Bayesian Network (BN) and Dynamic Bayesian Network (DBN) approaches have limitations. The inclusion of subjectivity in model development is necessary due to the reliance on expert judgment, which can be mitigated by consulting multiple experts. Additionally, as system complexity increases, the challenge of accurately classifying and connecting variables grows, leading to increased subjectivity. The directed acyclic nature of BN restricts modeling interdependent variables, requiring potential workarounds that may affect system dynamics. DBN introduces further complexity in linking variables across different time states, complicating the identification of influences over time.
