The paper discusses the concept of a two-slice temporal Bayesian network (2TBN), which defines a transition model for probabilistic reasoning over time. The 2TBN comprises two slices, with the first slice lacking parameters and the second slice requiring conditional probability tables (CPTs). The model allows for the representation of unbounded sequences of time slices using a finite number of parameters due to its stationary nature, where the structure repeats after the second time slice. 

The probability distribution for a sequence of time slices can be obtained by unrolling the 2TBN, generalizing other temporal reasoning models like hidden Markov models (HMM) and Kalman filter models (KFM). The DBN's structure allows it to express state spaces in a factored form rather than relying solely on discrete random variables.

The paper also introduces the concept of temporal plates, which encompass variables that change over time, and contemporal nodes, which are constant across time steps and thus placed outside temporal plates to optimize memory and computational resources. Additionally, the author discusses anchor and terminal nodes, which connect only to the first and last time slices, respectively, and do not influence intermediate slices. Anchor nodes have children within the temporal plate, while terminal nodes have parents within it.
