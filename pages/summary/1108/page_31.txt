The introduction of special nodes, such as anchor and terminal nodes, enables the modification of the Dynamic Bayesian Network (DBN) model to accommodate additional variables, enhancing its applicability to different scenarios.

The paper emphasizes that the use of DBNs in modeling and analyzing engineering systems is underexplored, despite their potential to address the dynamic behaviors that arise from the interdependence of systems. By incorporating time as a parameter, DBNs can effectively analyze the resilience of transportation infrastructure and other engineering phenomena, facilitating improvements in intervention strategies and structural degradation modeling.

In conclusion, the proposed framework offers a probabilistic approach to resilience assessment that is both practical for decision-makers and adaptable to various engineering contexts. This dynamic resilience analysis contrasts with static methods by capturing the evolution of systems over time, thereby providing a more nuanced understanding of resilience in complex engineering environments.
