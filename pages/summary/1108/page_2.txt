This paper presents a novel probabilistic framework for evaluating the resilience of engineering systems using Bayesian and Dynamic Bayesian Networks. Resilience indicators are highlighted as effective tools for assessing the resilience of complex engineering systems, particularly in preliminary designs. The authors address the limitations of traditional Bayesian networks, which are typically used for static systems in equilibrium. Recognizing that most engineering systems are dynamic and experience fluctuations, especially following disturbances, the paper introduces Dynamic Bayesian Networks (DBN) to incorporate the time dimension. DBNs allow for the modeling of interactions among variables over different time steps, enabling the tracking of a system's performance evolution based on prior evidence. A mathematical framework based on DBN is developed to model the resilience of dynamic engineering systems. Two examples illustrate the framework's application: one assessing the resilience of Brazil and the other focusing on a transportation system. The study emphasizes the importance of considering temporal aspects in resilience analysis for critical infrastructure.
