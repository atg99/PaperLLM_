The paper discusses the concept of membership functions (MFs) within fuzzy theory, particularly focusing on how input values are transformed into degrees of membership ranging from 0 to 1 through a process called fuzzification. It highlights the use of triangular membership functions as examples, detailing their mathematical representations for specific input ranges. The study employs a set of continuous membership functions and presents a portion of the Conditional Probability Table (CPT) for a son node, derived from three father nodes, leading to a total of 27 combinations. 

The output of the model is defined in terms of five states: High, Good, Moderate, Low, and Vulnerable, which provide a nuanced understanding of resilience levels. The same methodology is applied to the resilience node, where the father nodes represent resilience pillars. Additionally, the paper emphasizes that each node in a Bayesian network (BN) is characterized by a probability distribution, collectively forming the joint probability distribution (JPD) of the BN. This foundational concept is critical for understanding the relationships and dynamics within the network.
