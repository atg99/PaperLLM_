The paper discusses various methodologies for determining the importance factors of indicators within a system, highlighting the work of Kammouh et al. (2019b), who created a matrix-based approach that assigns higher importance to variables with greater interdependencies. It contrasts this with methods that rely on subjective expert assessments, which, while simpler, may lead to inaccuracies. 

Indicators are categorized by their nature into "static" and "dynamic." Static indicators maintain consistent functionality over time and are unaffected by hazards, while dynamic indicators are influenced by such events, leading to changes in their functionality. Dynamic indicators are characterized by a set of variables, including pre-event serviceability, post-disaster functionality, recovery functionality, and restoration time.

The concept of a target value (TV) is introduced as a baseline to measure system resilience, allowing for the assessment of functionality deficiencies at any given time. 

The paper includes a table detailing the variables of a proposed transportation network model, noting their importance factors and nature. The indicators cover various dimensions, such as physical infrastructure, vehicles, and other facilities, with specific measures related to accessibility, road conditions, vehicle characteristics, and facility quality. Each indicator's measure is normalized against the target value, providing a quantitative basis for evaluating system resilience.
