The paper discusses various indicators related to the maintenance and functionality of infrastructure, particularly focusing on their effectiveness during emergencies. Key metrics include the number of maintenance activities during a specified period, the robustness of essential infrastructure measured by the percentage that remained operational during past emergencies, and traffic load capacity evaluated through excessive capacity in emergency lanes and tracks.

Urban form is assessed based on the number of city centers per 100,000 people, while network size is analyzed through connectivity and betweenness metrics of intersections. The presence of safety accessories in vehicles is noted as an important factor, alongside the safety features in the path environment. Serviceability indicators include the characteristics of traffic lines and travel time reliability.

The paper also distinguishes between static and dynamic indicators within a network structure context, utilizing software to visualize the connectivity of these elements. Static variables are represented with unconditional probability tables that remain constant, while dynamic variables are assigned both unconditional and conditional probability tables to reflect their functionality over time, factoring in external influences such as damage and recovery processes. 

Overall, the study aims to provide a comprehensive understanding of infrastructure resilience and user behavior in the context of emergency management.
