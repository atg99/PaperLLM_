The paper presents an analysis of the Conv-Orthogonal Convolutional Neural Networks (OCNNs) to understand how conv-orthogonal regularization enhances CNN performance. Key aspects analyzed include the spectrum of the doubly block-Toeplitz (DBT) matrix K, feature similarity among filters, the impact of the regularization weight λ, and the time and space complexity of OCNNs.

The spectrum analysis of the DBT kernel matrix K reveals that traditional models often experience rapid spectrum decay, leading to gradient vanishing issues. In contrast, OCNNs maintain a uniform spectrum close to 1, which preserves information flow across layers. The analysis of filter similarity shows that orthogonality minimizes correlations between channels, thereby reducing redundancy. Guided back-propagation patterns are utilized to visualize and quantify filter similarities, indicating that OCNNs enhance filter diversity compared to plain ResNet34.

The paper also examines the influence of the regularization weight λ on classification performance, finding that optimal results are achieved at λ=0.1 for the ResNet18 model on CIFAR100. Additionally, the analysis of time and space complexity indicates that while OCNNs introduce some training time overhead (3% for kernel orthogonal regularizers and 9% for conv-orthogonal regularizers), they do not increase the number of parameters or test time.

In conclusion, the OCNN approach effectively imposes filter orthogonality with minimal computational overhead, significantly improving feature diversity, training stability, robustness, and generalization across various tasks.
