The paper presents a comprehensive evaluation of a novel convolutional orthogonality regularizer (OCNN) applied to image inpainting and generation tasks. The results demonstrate that OCNN significantly outperforms state-of-the-art (SOTA) methods across various benchmark datasets.

In the inpainting task, quantitative comparisons in Table 6 reveal that OCNN surpasses existing techniques such as Deep Image Prior (DIP) and convolutional dictionary learning, achieving higher peak signal-to-noise ratios (PSNR) across multiple images, including Barbara, Lena, and Peppers. This improvement is attributed to better background quality and texture reconstruction.

For image generation using Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), Table 7 shows that OCNN achieves an Inception Score (IS) of 8.63 and a Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) of 11.75, outperforming the baseline model and demonstrating state-of-the-art performance. The experiments indicate that OCNN leads to faster convergence during training, as illustrated in Figure 7.

Furthermore, the robustness of OCNN under adversarial attacks is analyzed. The model exhibits enhanced resilience, requiring significantly more attack time and queries to achieve a comparable success rate against a baseline model (Table 8). Specifically, OCNN necessitates 7 times more attack time and 1.7 times more queries to reach a 90% attack success rate, while maintaining superior accuracy after attacks.

Overall, the findings illustrate that the convolutional orthogonality regularizer not only enhances image inpainting and generation performance but also improves model robustness against adversarial attacks.
