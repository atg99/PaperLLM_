The paper "Orthogonal Convolutional Neural Networks" by Jiayun Wang, Yubei Chen, Rudrasis Chakraborty, and Stella X. Yu addresses challenges in deep convolutional neural networks (CNNs) such as training instability and feature redundancy. The authors propose an innovative method to impose orthogonality on convolutional filters, utilizing a doubly block-Toeplitz matrix representation of the convolutional kernel. This approach is shown to be more effective than the common kernel orthogonality method, which, while necessary, does not guarantee orthogonal convolutions.

The proposed orthogonal convolutional neural network (OCNN) introduces orthogonality constraints during training without requiring additional parameters or significant computational overhead. This method leads to improved performance across various tasks, including image classification and inpainting, in supervised, semi-supervised, and unsupervised settings. By promoting de-correlation among filters, OCNN enhances feature expressiveness, training stability, robustness, and generalization.

The paper presents empirical results demonstrating that OCNN consistently outperforms standard convolutional architectures and kernel orthogonality methods across multiple metrics, such as classification accuracy on CIFAR100 and ImageNet, as well as image retrieval and inpainting quality. The findings indicate that OCNN effectively mitigates issues related to filter correlation and spectrum distribution in deeper layers, leading to improved model performance across different architectures and tasks. The authors have made their code publicly available for further research.
