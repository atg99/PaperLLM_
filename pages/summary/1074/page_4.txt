The paper discusses the conditions necessary for achieving row and column orthogonality in convolutional operations involving matrices, specifically focusing on a doubly block-Toeplitz matrix K. It differentiates between fat and tall matrices, indicating that uniform spectrum regularization is required in both cases, achieved through row orthogonal convolution for fat matrices and column orthogonal convolution for tall matrices. 

The authors introduce a row orthogonality condition that relates to the inner product of flattened filter vectors, Kih′w′,•, which must equal zero when filter patches do not overlap. They note that verification of this condition can be simplified by restricting checks to overlapping regions defined by the convolution parameters. The equivalent condition for row orthogonality is presented in the form of a self-convolution that should yield an identity matrix, indicating near row-orthogonal convolution.

For column orthogonality, the paper defines a tensor representation of input channels and details how to derive column vectors from the matrix K. The column orthogonality condition mirrors the row condition, requiring checks only within overlapping regions. A simplified equivalent condition is also provided for stride 1 convolutions, presented in terms of a transposed matrix convolution that yields another identity matrix.

The paper compares the derived orthogonality conditions to kernel orthogonality, clarifying that while kernel conditions are necessary, they do not suffice for ensuring the desired orthogonal convolutions except in specific cases. Finally, the authors propose a lemma that connects the row and column orthogonality conditions, indicating a unified approach to ensuring orthogonality in their framework.
