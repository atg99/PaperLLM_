The paper discusses several methodologies that enhance the integration of optimization problems within machine learning paradigms, specifically focusing on quadratic and linear programming. It highlights the significance of differentiable optimization layers, initiated by Amos and Kolter in 2017, which enable exact gradient calculations for backpropagation by differentiating the KKT conditions of quadratic programs. This advancement facilitates the incorporation of QPs into predictive models, as shown by Donti et al. in 2019, who effectively used QPs with stochastic constraints for real-world scheduling problems.

Elmachtoub and Grigas in 2020 introduced the smart predict-and-optimize framework, which emphasizes prediction coupled with optimization for linear objectives, laying groundwork for regret minimization in decision-making tasks. However, their work focused on surrogate loss functions rather than full optimization solutions.

Wilder et al. proposed an alternative method for linear programming that allows for smooth gradient calculations through a regularized objective function, demonstrating its application in problems like knapsack and bipartite matching. Mandi and Guns later presented a log-barrier approach for approximating gradients in linear programming, showing improved performance in learning tasks compared to previous methods.

Berthet et al. introduced stochastic perturbation techniques for differentiating LP outputs, allowing for smoother gradient calculations without explicit solving of LPs. Lastly, Ferber et al. extended these ideas to mixed-integer linear programming, successfully integrating more complex combinatorial optimization problems into deep learning frameworks by transforming them into LPs while preserving optimal solutions. 

Overall, the paper illustrates a trend towards combining machine learning with optimization techniques, enhancing the capabilities of predictive models in complex decision-making scenarios.
