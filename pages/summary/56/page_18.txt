The paper discusses the stability of recurrent neural networks (RNNs) and the challenges posed by vanishing and exploding gradients during training. It highlights that the overall Jacobian matrix's magnitude relies on the product of individual Jacobian matrices, which can grow exponentially if certain stability conditions are not met. Specifically, if any eigenvalue of the matrix Wr exceeds the stability threshold, it results in a gradient explosion, complicating the training process.

The stability analysis shows that if all eigenvalues of Wr are within the range of 0 to 1, the gradients will diminish, leading to effective training outcomes. Conversely, if the gradients explode, the system may either saturate at the limits of the warping function or remain in a quasi-linear region, both of which hinder proper parameter updates during training.

To address these issues, the paper introduces the Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) architecture, designed to mitigate the vanishing gradients problem. LSTMs incorporate nonlinear, data-dependent controls that help maintain stable gradients throughout training. The LSTM's design separates the state update into two parts, allowing for improved robustness and versatility compared to traditional RNNs. This modification helps ensure that the gradient of the objective function with respect to the state signal does not vanish, thus facilitating effective training and enabling the model to learn long-range dependencies.
