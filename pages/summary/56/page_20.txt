The paper discusses the derivation and modification of a standard Recurrent Neural Network (RNN) system, particularly focusing on how certain gate functions can be integrated to enhance stability and flexibility. The diagonal matrix W_s, containing positive fractions, allows for simplifications in the equations governing the system. By setting W_s to the identity matrix, the contributions from the previous state remain fractional, ensuring system stability.

The update term, represented as F_u, incorporates both the previous readout signal and the current input signal in equal proportions. However, this direct connection poses risks related to the vanishing and exploding gradient problems. To mitigate this, a control readout gate, g_cr, is introduced to modulate the readout signal, allowing only a fractional amount to influence the observable value at each step.

The paper further explores the addition of a control input gate, g_cx, which enhances flexibility by enabling a tailored contribution of the input signal. This results in a modified update term that accommodates both the readout and the input signal, allowing for a controlled mixture of contributions. 

The dynamic range of the cell's value signal is maintained through a saturating warping nonlinearity, G_d, which tempers the aggregate signal to produce a suitable update candidate signal. The final recurrence relationship reflects these modifications, emphasizing the role of the gating mechanisms in managing input and state contributions effectively.
