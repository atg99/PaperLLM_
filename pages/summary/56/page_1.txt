The paper titled "Fundamentals of Recurrent Neural Network (RNN) and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) Network" by Alex Sherstinsky, published in the Elsevier journal "Physica D: Nonlinear Phenomena," aims to provide a comprehensive understanding of RNNs and LSTMs, addressing gaps in existing literature. Despite the popularity of LSTM networks due to their effectiveness in various applications like language modeling and machine translation, many resources inadequately cover essential aspects, often omitting training formulas and justifications for techniques like RNN unrolling.

The tutorial begins by deriving the canonical RNN formulation from differential equations, offering a formal foundation. It then presents a precise statement that justifies the unrolling technique for RNNs. The paper highlights the training challenges associated with standard RNNs and proposes a transformation to the "Vanilla LSTM" network, supported by logical reasoning. The author emphasizes clarity through unconventional notation and detailed explanations of the LSTM's components.

Additionally, the paper identifies opportunities to enhance the LSTM architecture, proposing an augmented version that broadens its applicability. The target audience includes those familiar with RNNs and LSTMs seeking an alternative and thorough pedagogical approach, as well as machine learning practitioners looking to implement the new LSTM model for research and experimentation. Overall, the work strives to fill the void left by previous resources, providing a self-contained primer that integrates clear explanations, equations, and coherent schematics.
