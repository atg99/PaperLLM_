This section discusses the training difficulties associated with Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs), particularly focusing on the truncated unrolled RNN system. Proposition 1 establishes that specific equations define this system, which is inherently stable once truncated. However, practical training issues arise, primarily due to "vanishing gradients" and "exploding gradients." These problems become more significant when long-range dependencies exist within the data, necessitating a wider unrolled RNN model.

The training process typically employs Back Propagation Through Time (BPTT), a method adapted from standard Back Propagation for sequence data. BPTT involves unrolling the RNN into a directed acyclic graph to facilitate gradient computation. The algorithm utilizes available data pairs to optimize an objective function that depends on the readout signal at specific indices.

When applying BPTT, the total gradient of the objective function with respect to the readout signal is calculated, which also influences the state signal of the RNN. The overall objective function is often defined as the sum of individual contributions from the readout signal at each index. However, it is crucial to consider the dependencies between state signals across time steps, as they influence one another, complicating the gradient calculation.

In summary, while the truncated unrolled RNN system is stable, training remains challenging due to gradient issues and the complex dependencies of signals across time steps, which necessitate careful application of BPTT for effective optimization.
