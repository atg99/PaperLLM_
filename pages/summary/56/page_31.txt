The paper discusses the derivation of the partial derivatives of the objective function E with respect to various parameters of the Vanilla LSTM cell, as outlined in Equations 156 to 170. These equations illustrate how the gradients for different weight matrices and biases are computed at each time step n, utilizing the variables α corresponding to the respective gates (cu, cs, cr, du). 

The total partial derivative of the objective function with respect to the model parameters Θ at step n is presented in Equation 171, which consolidates all the individual derivatives. When the LSTM cell is unrolled for K steps to process a complete segment of training samples, the same parameters Θ are utilized across all steps. This leads to Equation 172, which sums the contributions from all unrolled steps to compute the total derivative of E with respect to Θ.

This total derivative is essential for optimization via Gradient Descent, as it allows for the computation of parameter updates based on the accumulated gradients from a batch of segments, typically ranging from 16 to 128. The paper notes that while regularization is crucial in the training of machine learning models, it is not the primary focus of the discussion.
