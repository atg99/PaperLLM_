The paper discusses the computation of control signals within a Vanilla Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) network. The control signals, represented as gcs[n], gcu[n], and gcr[n], are derived from available data signals at each step n. Specifically, gcs[n] utilizes the previous state signal s[n-1], along with data signals v[n-1] and x[n], while gcu[n] also incorporates these signals but focuses on the current update candidate u[n]. For gcr[n], which relates to the readout signal r[n], the signals s[n], v[n-1], and x[n] are employed, highlighting the importance of real-time data accessibility at the junction of the LSTM cell.

The input to each gate is constructed as a linear combination of the available data signals, facilitating the application of the backpropagation through time (BPTT) algorithm for training. The gates, governed by the functions gcs[n], gcu[n], and gcr[n], adaptively modulate data signals during inference, thereby enhancing the model's robustness against artificial boundaries present in input sequences due to unrolling.

The LSTM cell operates by balancing two main objectives: managing data flow and controlling the propagation of this data. The candidate data signals, constrained by control signals ranging from 0 to 1, determine the extent to which data propagates within the cell. This design ensures that only the relevant portions of the candidate data are passed forward, allowing for effective learning and generation of high-quality output sequences.

The section concludes with an overview of the Vanilla LSTM network mechanism, which emphasizes the collaborative nature of its internal operations and highlights the significance of both data and control components in achieving optimal performance.
