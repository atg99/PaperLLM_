The paper discusses the dynamics of Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks, particularly focusing on the challenges of vanishing gradients during training. It presents a mathematical framework to analyze how the gradients propagate through the network's layers over multiple time steps, specifically addressing the relationship between the partial derivatives of the state signals at various indices.

The key equation derived, Equation 180, indicates how the error gradient can be preserved across numerous time steps, allowing for effective parameter updates. It emphasizes that maintaining the condition where the partial derivatives are less than one at each step is crucial for preventing the gradients from diminishing to zero. The paper outlines three scenarios that could lead to this outcome: a trivial network state, a delicate balance of signals that is impractical, and a situation where the spectral radius of a specific matrix is less than unity, indicating that the error gradient will diminish over time due to the nature of the training data.

Furthermore, the analysis suggests several configurations to achieve the necessary conditions for effective gradient management, including constraining the norms of weight matrices and ensuring appropriate saturation of state signals. The overall conclusion emphasizes that the LSTM's ability to manage long-range dependencies hinges on the careful orchestration of its internal signals and parameters to maintain effective gradient flow.
