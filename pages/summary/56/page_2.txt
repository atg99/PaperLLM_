This paper aims to provide a comprehensive introduction to Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks, addressing a gap in existing literature. It is designed for students and practitioners who seek to understand not only how these networks function but also the theoretical foundations behind them.

The authors begin with RNNs, as LSTMs are a specific type of RNN. They derive the canonical RNN equations from differential equations, highlighting the significance of this approach in understanding neural networks and their applications in machine learning. The paper emphasizes that differential equations provide a clearer framework for modeling than difference equations, which are commonly used in RNNs.

The document outlines several key objectives for its content:

1. Intuitive explanations with clear notation.
2. Complete coverage of both inference and training equations.
3. General treatment of the Vanilla LSTM system, including cell state influences.
4. Illustrative diagrams to aid understanding.
5. Modular descriptions for easy integration into various architectures.
6. Use of vector notation for straightforward application in programming libraries.

The paper critiques existing resources for lacking one or more of these elements and aims to fill this void with a succinct and clear presentation. 

Subsequent sections of the paper are structured to build upon foundational concepts, starting with RNN systems, followed by the unrolling of RNNs, training mechanisms through Back Propagation Through Time, and the construction of the Vanilla LSTM cell. The document concludes with an exploration of the Augmented LSTM system and suggestions for future research. Overall, the work is intended to serve as a foundational guide for those looking to delve into RNN and LSTM networks.
