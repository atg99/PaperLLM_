The paper discusses several key remarks regarding the use of recurrent neural networks (RNNs) in modeling sequences and the implications of certain assumptions made in the context of their architecture and training.

Remark 1 highlights that the existing propositions and proofs do not explicitly account for advanced RNN architectures like Gated Recurrent Units (GRUs) and Attention Networks, which are increasingly relevant in contemporary applications.

Remark 2 points out that while segment-level state signal subsequences are independent, there is no requirement for independence among input signal subsequences from different segments. It is suggested that hidden layer weights can be trained to de-correlate dependencies in the input signal, thus maintaining the independence of the state signal subsequences.

Remark 3 emphasizes that no method for converting Infinite Impulse Response (IIR) systems to Finite Impulse Response (FIR) systems is universally optimal. The approximation of FIR to IIR systems must be evaluated against specific fidelity and performance measures. The effectiveness of an approximation technique is contingent upon its ability to adapt to the data distribution to meet the application's quality metrics. The technique of unrolling RNNs for a finite number of steps is discussed, noting that while more steps yield closer approximations to the desired output, they also increase computational demands. If the data can be treated as independent segments, unrolled RNNs can effectively reproduce outputs based on inputs.

Remark 4 reiterates that the unrolling technique is supported by the independence of output subsequences and specific initial state conditions. However, practical applications often reveal interdependencies, complicating the modeling process. The paper suggests that suitable initial conditions for subsequences can be determined through empirical testing and statistical analysis, allowing for a more effective partitioning of the output sequence.

Overall, the remarks underscore the importance of understanding the assumptions underlying RNN architectures and the potential implications of those assumptions on model performance, particularly in the context of independence and sequence dependencies.
