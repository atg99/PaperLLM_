In the Vanilla LSTM network, the cell state at the current step is derived from a weighted combination of the previous cell state and the aggregation of both historical and current update information. Similar to standard RNNs, the Vanilla LSTM can learn short-range dependencies in the training data when trained using Gradient Descent. If the model parameters lead to a condition where the norm of the gate signal for cell state updates, gcs[n], is less than 1, the error gradient can diminish and potentially vanish over time, despite the presence of non-zero contributions from the update candidate signal, gcu[n], and the gate signal for cell resets, gcr[n]. 

To ensure effective training with Backpropagation Through Time (BPTT), all functions within the system, including those defining the gate signals, must be differentiable. The logistic function is introduced as a suitable choice due to its continuous, differentiable, and monotonically increasing properties, effectively mapping any real number to a range between 0 and 1. This function is represented mathematically and can be applied element-wise when operating on vector inputs.
