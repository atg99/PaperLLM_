This paper presents a thorough exploration of Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks through a principled approach. It begins by relating RNNs to delay differential equations, establishing a canonical formulation that is applicable across various scientific fields. The authors detail the derivation of the standard RNN formulation via parameter selection and stability analysis. They explain the unrolling of RNNs in the context of approximating Infinite Impulse Response (IIR) systems with Finite Impulse Response (FIR) models, proving the conditions for effective sequence learning.

The paper addresses the challenges of training standard RNNs, particularly the vanishing and exploding gradient problems encountered during Gradient Descent. To overcome these limitations, the authors modify the canonical RNN to develop a more robust LSTM network, providing a comprehensive overview of its structure, including both the forward and backward passes. A significant contribution is the pedagogical approach to analyzing RNNs and LSTMs from a Signal Processing perspective, along with a clear derivation of the unrolling procedure and meaningful notation.

Two novel extensions to the Vanilla LSTM are introduced: convolutional non-causal input context windows and an external input gate, which enhance the LSTM's capabilities. The paper suggests future work involving the implementation of the Augmented LSTM in high-performance computing settings, particularly for applications in language representation, question answering, and customer support automation. It emphasizes the need to benchmark the Augmented LSTM against various models, including the Vanilla LSTM and Transformer, to evaluate performance improvements.

The authors express gratitude to collaborators and reviewers for their contributions to the development and refinement of the paper.
