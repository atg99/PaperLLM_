This paper presents Proposition 1, which asserts that a standard RNN system can effectively optimize its objective function L and infer outputs using a single, reusable RNN cell unrolled for a specified number of steps, Km. The proposition is based on the existence of a parameter set Θ that brings L close to its optimum for a sequence of N samples. It further assumes that the ground truth output sequence can be divided into independent segment-level output subsequences. 

The proof discusses how the objective function calculates training, validation, and testing errors while also tracking generalization on unseen data. When L is near optimal, the RNN's output is expected to closely match the ground truth output. By segmenting both the RNN output and the ground truth in the same manner, the independence of segment-level ensembles allows the objective function to be expressed as a combination of independent segment-level components.

Equations 50 and 53 indicate that if the RNN output approximates the ground truth at the ensemble level, it must do so at the segment level as well. The proof further establishes that the RNN's observable outputs, readout signals, and state signals for different segments remain independent. This independence imposes constraints on the initial conditions of the state signal subsequences, ensuring that the initial state of one segment does not depend on the samples from another segment. Overall, the proposition highlights the capability of a single RNN cell to manage independent segments effectively while optimizing performance across the training set.
