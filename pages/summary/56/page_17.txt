The paper discusses the calculation of total partial derivatives of an objective function in the context of a Recurrent Neural Network (RNN) using the chain rule. It presents two key equations (82 and 84) that signify the relationship between the derivatives of the objective function with respect to the readout and state signals at a given index, n. These derivatives form sequences that move backward through the indices, contrasting with the forward progression of the unrolled equations.

The initialization of these sequences is crucial, as indicated in Equation 85, where the state signal's error gradient must start from zero. The paper also derives expressions for the derivatives of model parameters (Equation 86), highlighting that all derivatives are interconnected and proportional to the state signal's error gradient, denoted as ⃗ψ[n].

The total derivative of the objective function with respect to the model parameters incorporates contributions from all steps in the RNN segment, as captured in Equation 90, which is essential for optimization through Gradient Descent.

A notable point is that when training the RNN using Backpropagation Through Time (BPTT), the error gradient flows in the reverse direction of the input sequence. The paper emphasizes how the gradient at a certain index can influence earlier indices, thus allowing the model to adjust its parameters based on information from distant samples. This relationship is critical for effectively capturing long-range dependencies within the RNN.
