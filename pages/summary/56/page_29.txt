This section focuses on deriving the equations essential for training a Vanilla LSTM network cell using Back Propagation Through Time (BPTT). It begins by introducing two gradient sequences, ⃗ χ[n] and ⃗ψ[n], which represent the total partial derivatives of the objective function E with respect to the observable value signal ⃗ v[n] and the state signal ⃗ s[n], respectively. The approach of anchoring the chain rule at the cell's border is motivated by modular design principles, aiming to simplify the backward pass calculations by expressing intra-cell derivatives in relation to ⃗ χ[n]. 

The backward-moving recursion of ⃗ χ[n] and ⃗ψ[n] means their values at index n depend on those at n+1, facilitating the computation of total partial derivatives for the model parameters across all steps from 0 to K-1. During inference, only the input signal ⃗ x[n] and the value signal ⃗ v[n] are externally observable, while the internal signals remain hidden, except for the state and value signals. 

The output signal ⃗ y[n] can be generated from the value signal through transformations like linear mapping and softmax, with the input signal potentially pre-processed for efficiency. The section concludes with the derivation of derivatives for warping functions, reinforcing the modular implementation of training equations for the Vanilla LSTM cell through the definition of ⃗ χ[n].
