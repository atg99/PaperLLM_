The paper introduces total partial derivatives of the objective function E concerning various variables in the Vanilla LSTM cell, specifically focusing on three intermediate variables and one border variable. The derivatives are defined as follows: ρ[n] for r[n], γ[n] for g[n], α[n] for a[n], and ψ[n] for s[n]. The border variable ψ[n] is highlighted as crucial since it serves as the main error gradient for parameter updates in the Vanilla LSTM network.

The backward pass equations leverage these derivatives using the chain rule, leading to a series of equations that describe how changes in the output, internal states, and various gate signals affect the objective function E. Key equations include those for χ[n], ρ[n], γcr[n], αcr[n], and ψ[n], which are interconnected and account for the contributions from the subsequent time step n+1.

The text also notes that the total partial derivatives with respect to model parameters at step n are linked to "accumulation derivatives" derived from earlier equations. To mitigate potential numerical issues during training, these accumulation derivatives are clipped to remain within the range of -1 to 1, ensuring stability in the learning process.
