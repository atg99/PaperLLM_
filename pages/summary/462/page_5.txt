This section outlines the framework of Unet, Segnet, and SegUnet models, as well as the high-resolution remote sensing aerial dataset used for applying the proposed approaches. It also details the performance metrics commonly used for evaluating state-of-the-art techniques in building extraction.

The Unet architecture is highlighted as a deep convolutional neural network (DCNN) designed for accurate image segmentation. Its key innovation is the substitution of pooling layers with up-sampling operators, which enhances output layer resolution by integrating high-resolution features from the contracting part with up-sampled outputs. This process is supported by continuous convolution layers that enable precise outcomes based on the available information. 

A notable aspect of the Unet model is its multiple feature channels in the up-sampling section, allowing for the dissemination of contextual information to higher resolution layers. The model consists of two main sections: the expansive part (right side) and the contracting part (left side), forming a symmetric U-shaped CNN. It operates without fully connected layers, utilizing only the right parts of convolution. To manage large images, an overlap-tile strategy is implemented for segmentation, while input image mirroring helps in predicting missing context at the image borders. The resolution is constrained by GPU memory, but the tiling strategy enables processing of extensive images.

The Unet model's framework begins with a contracting path that features two repeated convolution layers with a 3x3 window size, followed by a down-sampling layer with a 2x2 window. An activation function is employed during the convolution process, transforming the input based on the weight and bias vectors.
