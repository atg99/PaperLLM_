The challenges in this field are primarily due to the variability in building appearances caused by factors such as shadows, vehicles, and differing materials, which complicate the detection process. Traditional methods that combine genetic algorithms and support vector machines have been employed, but their effectiveness is limited because they rely heavily on low-level local features.

To overcome these limitations, there is a push towards utilizing high-level features that enhance image segmentation. Recent advancements in deep learning, particularly through convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and deep convolutional networks (DCNNs), have shown promise in improving image classification and semantic segmentation. These models are advantageous as they can learn from raw images without extensive preprocessing, efficiently integrating spatial and spectral features.

However, despite the progress, existing models face challenges that hinder their performance, such as difficulties in accurately detecting multiple objects and generating reliable predictions.
