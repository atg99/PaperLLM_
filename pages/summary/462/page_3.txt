The paper discusses three promising CNN approaches that leverage deep neural network features, which have demonstrated effectiveness in various tasks such as semantic segmentation, object detection, and visual identification. Notably, these frameworks have been applied in remote sensing for data merging, image classification, and detection of high-resolution images.

The introduction of an end-to-end trainable deep convolutional neural network (DCNN) has shown improvements in boundary detection and semantic segmentation. Previous studies, including the integration of conditional random fields (CRFs) with multi-scale CNNs, have addressed challenges in classifying dense urban scenes. However, enhancing the resolution of remote sensing images has not always led to conclusive classification results. This is attributed to the increased difficulty in distinguishing objects with similar spectral values, resulting in decreased inter-class differences and increased intra-class differences among features like cars, shadows, and buildings.

Recent advancements using Fully Convolutional Networks (FCN) have achieved reliable outcomes in semantic segmentation, enabling the identification of various object classes and their shapes. Nonetheless, challenges remain, particularly in accurately detecting objects with multiple borders or small sizes, as FCN can produce visually degraded results due to blurred object boundaries. Research has focused on enhancing CNN structures through methods like CRFs combined with dilated convolutions or by implementing skip-layer structures post up-sampling to recover high-frequency image details. These developments aim to improve the precision of pixel classification in building extraction and overall segmentation quality.
