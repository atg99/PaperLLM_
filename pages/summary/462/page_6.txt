The paper discusses the architecture and functioning of the Unet model, particularly focusing on the use of the rectified linear unit (ReLU) activation function. This activation function helps prevent the gradient vanishing issue during back-propagation, allowing neurons to operate efficiently while promoting sparsity in hidden layers and avoiding saturation. 

The model employs a down-sampling strategy where the number of feature channels is doubled at each stage, utilizing max-pooling layers to reduce image size, parameter count, and computational demands. This method retains significant information and ensures spatial uniformity in the features extracted through convolution. 

In the expansive path, up-sampling is followed by a convolution with a stride of 2×2 to halve the number of feature channels. Two convolution layers with a 3×3 kernel size, followed by ReLU activation and concatenation with corresponding cropped feature maps from the contracting path, are used. The process culminates in a convolution layer with a 1×1 window size and a sigmoid function to map the 32-component feature vector to the desired classes, specifically distinguishing between road and non-road areas.

The mathematical representations for the activation function and output mapping are presented, emphasizing the model's ability to convert input into probabilities effectively. The generic framework of the Unet model is visually represented in an accompanying figure.
