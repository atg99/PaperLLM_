The paper evaluates the performance of two neural network architectures, SegNet and U-NET, in binary and multi-class segmentation tasks, addressing dataset imbalance as discussed in the Materials and Methods section. The global accuracy for each test image was calculated and averaged, leading to the selection of the best experiments for class-level assessments.

In the binary segmentation results, both networks achieved high accuracy, with SegNet reaching 0.954 and U-NET 0.95 in their best experiments. However, SegNet consistently outperformed U-NET across most metrics, except for sensitivity and G-mean, where their performances were similar. The results indicate that both networks had difficulty recognizing the C3 class in multi-class segmentation, yet performed well with classes C1 and C2. The best accuracy for multi-class segmentation was 0.908 from U-NET.

The statistical results highlight SegNet's superior performance in binary segmentation, with notable true positive rates. The findings suggest that while both networks are effective, SegNet has a marginal advantage in binary classification tasks, as evidenced by the metrics detailed in the statistical tables.
