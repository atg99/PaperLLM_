The paper discusses the SegNet deep neural network (DNN), which features an encoder-decoder architecture with a depth of three. The encoder layers mirror the convolutional layers from the VGG16 network, while the decoder utilizes pooling indices from the encoder's max-pooling layers to create segmentation masks. By removing fully connected layers, the authors reduced the encoder's parameters significantly from 1.34e+8 to 1.47e+7.

For network training, the ADAM stochastic optimizer was chosen for its rapid convergence. Input images were resized to 256x256 pixels to optimize training time and memory usage. The dataset of 100 images was divided into training, validation, and testing sets in the ratios of 0.72, 0.10, and 0.18, respectively. To address class imbalance, median frequency balancing was used to calculate class weights for a weighted cross-entropy loss function.

The networks underwent nine training experiments with varying hyperparameters, including initial learning rates and mini-batch sizes, over 160 epochs. The average training time for each configuration was approximately 25 minutes. The paper also includes a table detailing the hyperparameters used in these experiments and visual representations of the DNN architectures for SegNet and U-Net.
