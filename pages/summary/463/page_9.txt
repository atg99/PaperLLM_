The paper by Saood and Hatem compares the performance of two deep learning networks, SegNet and U-NET, in detecting diseased areas in lung images of COVID-19 patients. A five-fold cross-validation was utilized due to the small dataset size, where images were randomized and divided into training, validation, and testing sets. Statistical results indicated low standard deviations for most scores, though the sensitivity of the C3 class showed variability. 

The Deep Dream method was employed to visualize features extracted by the SegNet network, highlighting discerning patterns between infected and non-infected classes. The findings demonstrated SegNet's effectiveness in distinguishing between healthy and infected tissues, while U-NET excelled in identifying infected areas through a multiple classification approach. The results suggest the potential for deep learning technologies to aid in the objective diagnosis of COVID-19 via CT imaging, marking a significant advancement in medical imaging analysis. 

The research utilized publicly available data, and both authors contributed equally to the project, which did not require external funding. The study emphasizes the importance of accurate identification and diagnosis in managing COVID-19.
