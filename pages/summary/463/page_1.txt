In the paper by Saood and Hatem, the authors address the urgent need for efficient diagnostic tools for COVID-19 patients through the segmentation of lung CT images. They compare two deep learning techniques, SegNet and U-Net, for semantically segmenting infected tissue in CT scans. SegNet is noted for its scene segmentation capabilities, while U-Net is recognized for medical segmentation applications.

The methodology involved training both networks with a dataset of 72 images, validating on 10 images, and testing on 18 images. The results indicated that SegNet outperformed U-Net in classifying infected versus non-infected lung tissues, achieving a mean accuracy of 0.95. Conversely, U-Net excelled as a multi-class segmentor with a mean accuracy of 0.91.

The authors conclude that effective segmentation of CT images is crucial for diagnosing COVID-19 and quantifying disease severity, which can facilitate prioritization in patient treatment. They highlight the potential of their proposed methods to automate and enhance the treatment process during the pandemic, thereby supporting healthcare systems overwhelmed by the volume of cases.
