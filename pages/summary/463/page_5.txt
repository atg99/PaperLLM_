In the paper by Saood and Hatem, the authors evaluate the performance of multi-class segmentors, specifically U-NET and SegNet, identifying them as the best models in their study. The training was conducted using MATLAB's Deep Learning Toolbox on a Windows 10 machine equipped with an INTEL core-i5 9400F processor and an NVIDIA 1050ti GPU, which significantly reduced training times by an average factor of 35. 

To assess the models' performance, five classification criteria were utilized: sensitivity, specificity, G-mean, Sorensen-Dice (F1 score), and F2 score. These metrics are defined mathematically, with sensitivity measuring true positive rates, specificity assessing true negative rates, and the Sorensen-Dice score providing a measure of overlap between predicted and actual values. The G-mean combines sensitivity and specificity, while the F2 score emphasizes sensitivity over precision. The results of the training accuracy and loss for both segmentors are visually presented, indicating their effectiveness in the segmentation task.
