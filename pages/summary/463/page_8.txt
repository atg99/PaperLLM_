The study by Saood and Hatem evaluates the performance of various segmentation networks, particularly SegNet, in analyzing medical imaging data. The findings indicate that SegNet demonstrates superior performance over Inf-Net and Semi-Inf-Net in terms of Dice, specificity, and sensitivity metrics, although U-NET outperforms them solely in sensitivity. The authors attribute SegNet's advantages to its focus on internal textures and its training on lung area images, which contrasts with Inf-Net's emphasis on edge information that may overlook critical texture details.

SegNet also marginally surpasses Semi-Inf-Net, which relies on pseudo labeling to augment training data, suggesting that the quality of pseudo labels may be a limiting factor. Additionally, SegNet outperforms COVID-SegNet in sensitivity and Dice metrics, although the latter achieves high accuracy in lung region segmentation but struggles with infection regions near the chest wall.

The paper notes that increasing mini-batch size negatively impacts network performance, supporting previous studies that advocate for smaller mini-batch sizes paired with low learning rates to enhance training outcomes. In multi-class segmentation, U-NET shows moderate results for Ground Glass Opacification and Consolidation, but its performance in pleural effusion segmentation is subpar, likely due to the limited representation of this class in the dataset. Overall, while the multiclass segmentors show slightly higher standard deviations, they maintain reliability in identifying non-infected tissue, underscoring the networks' solid performance in accuracy.
