This paper presents a novel adaptation of pretrained sequence-to-sequence models for the task of document ranking, contrasting with the prevalent classification-based methods that utilize encoder-only architectures like BERT. The authors propose a method where a sequence-to-sequence model, specifically T5, is trained to generate relevance labels as target words. The logits of these target words are interpreted as relevance probabilities for ranking documents.

Experimental results indicate that this sequence-to-sequence approach performs comparably to existing classification-based models on the MS MARCO passage ranking task and can exceed their performance with larger, more recent models. Additionally, on the TREC 2004 Robust Track test collection, the authors demonstrate a zero-shot transfer method that surpasses state-of-the-art models that require in-dataset cross-validation. Notably, their approach is particularly effective in data-poor scenarios, where it significantly outperforms encoder-only models.

The paper also explores the model's use of latent knowledge by varying the target words, further highlighting the advantages of their sequence-to-sequence adaptation for document reranking. Overall, this work contributes to the field by introducing a new methodology that leverages sequence-to-sequence models for improving document ranking performance.
