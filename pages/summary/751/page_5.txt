The paper presents a comparative analysis of the performance of T5 and BERT models when fine-tuned with varying amounts of training data. Results indicate that T5 consistently outperforms BERT, particularly in scenarios with limited training data. Specifically, with only 2k training samples, T5 achieves a mean score of 0.238, significantly higher than BERT's score of 0.127. Even with a larger dataset of 20k samples, T5 maintains a lead over BERT (0.261 vs 0.201). 

The authors highlight that while T5's superior performance might be attributed to its larger model size, it is evident that T5 demonstrates better data efficiency compared to BERT. In fact, with minimal training data, BERT performs worse than the BM25 baseline, whereas T5 showcases considerable effectiveness. 

The paper poses two key questions regarding the data efficiency of T5 over BERT and the fundamental differences between their approaches to classification. It argues that both models learn latent representations for relevance classification, but T5 benefits from leveraging both pretrained knowledge and the fine-tuning process more effectively than BERT. Unlike BERT, which relies on a single fully-connected layer for binary decision-making, T5 utilizes its architecture to incorporate additional latent knowledge related to semantics and language, enhancing its performance on the task at hand.
