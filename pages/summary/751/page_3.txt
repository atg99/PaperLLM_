The paper discusses the training and evaluation of T5 models for passage retrieval in a zero-shot transfer setting, utilizing the MS MARCO passage dataset as a held-out test set. The authors fine-tuned T5 models of varying sizes (base, large, and 3B) using a fixed learning rate and class-balanced batches, training for 100k iterations without convergence checks. The training process was guided by computational demands and time constraints, as recommended by previous studies.

The authors implemented a maximum input length of 512 tokens, using Google TPU v3s for training and inference, which took approximately 12, 48, and 160 hours for the respective model sizes. Greedy decoding was employed during inference, and a sliding window approach was used to segment documents into passages for relevance probability classification.

To evaluate their method, the authors compared it against several baseline models, including BM25, BM25+RM3 for query expansion, and BM25+BERT-large as a two-stage pipeline with BERT as the reranking model. The results showed that T5 models outperformed BERT-large in the MS MARCO passage retrieval task, with significant improvements noted in certain configurations. However, the T5-3B model appeared under-trained at the time of evaluation, suggesting potential for further effectiveness with additional computational resources.
