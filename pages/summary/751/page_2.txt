The paper presents a sequence-to-sequence model that demonstrates superior data efficiency compared to BERT, particularly in data-scarce situations. The authors argue that their approach leverages fine-tuned latent representations related to output target words, allowing for the utilization of the model's pre-trained knowledge in semantics and linguistic relations. They conduct probing experiments to validate their hypothesis by modifying target words to explore different facets of semantic relatedness.

The method employs a reranking strategy based on the T5 model, which utilizes a masked language modeling objective similar to BERT. The input for the model consists of a query and a document, with the task being to classify the document as relevant or not by predicting the words "true" or "false." At inference, the model applies softmax on the logits of these tokens to rank documents according to their relevance probabilities.

The experiments utilize the MS MARCO passage dataset, comprising 8.8 million passages and approximately 500,000 query-document pairs for training. The development and test sets include around 6,900 queries each, with relevance labels available only for the development set. The authors also use the Robust04 dataset, which contains 250 queries and relevance judgments across 528,000 documents. They aim to refine their models before making official submissions to evaluate their performance against BERT-based models.
