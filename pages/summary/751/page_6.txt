The paper presents an analysis of various target word manipulations on the performance of BERT and T5 models when trained on the MS MARCO passage dataset. It highlights how the amount of training data affects the models' ability to learn classification tasks, particularly in low-data scenarios. The authors argue that while BERT struggles with few training examples due to its reliance on pretraining, T5 can leverage its pretraining objective effectively to generate relevance labels.

To investigate this further, the authors conducted experiments by altering the target words used for predictions, focusing on their linguistic relatedness. They tested several configurations: 

1. **Baseline**: "true" for relevant and "false" for non-relevant documents.
2. **Reverse**: Swapping the meanings of the target words.
3. **Antonyms**: Using opposite adjectives unrelated to relevance.
4. **Related Words**: Using semantically related but non-contrasting terms.
5. **Unrelated Words**: Mapping to completely unrelated terms.
6. **Subwords**: Using subwords that remove semantic mapping.

The experiments involved varying the number of positive and negative instances, with results indicating that effectiveness decreases as linguistic relatedness is disrupted. The findings suggest that the choice of target words significantly influences model performance, particularly in low-data environments.
