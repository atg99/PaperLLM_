The paper presents results from experiments evaluating the performance of various models on the MS MARCO passage ranking task and the Robust04 dataset. The key findings are summarized in Tables 1 and 2, which detail the Mean Reciprocal Rank (MRR@10), Average Precision (AP), Precision at 20 (P@20), and Normalized Discounted Cumulative Gain (NDCG@20) for different models.

In Table 1, BM25 serves as the baseline with an MRR@10 of .184. Incorporating BERT-large significantly improves performance to .372, while T5 models further enhance results, with T5-large achieving .383 and T5-3B slightly lower at .382. The improvement illustrates the effectiveness of transformer-based models over traditional methods.

Table 2 compares the performance on Robust04, where T5 models are applied as rerankers on top of BM25 and BM25+RM3 retrieval results. T5-3B demonstrates the best performance across all metrics, achieving .384 for AP, .510 for P@20, and .601 for NDCG@20, outperforming both CEDR and Birch, which are state-of-the-art BERT-based models. The T5 models were trained solely on MS MARCO passage data, indicating a zero-shot transfer capability to Robust04.

The paper notes that larger models generally yield better performance, and although T5-large did not perform as well as expected compared to T5-base, this anomaly is attributed to the training methodology and the stability of effectiveness across model checkpoints. The authors suggest that while there are methods to improve model checkpoint selection, they may risk overfitting and compromise the zero-shot learning approach currently employed.

Overall, the findings indicate that T5 models, particularly T5-3B, achieve state-of-the-art results in passage ranking tasks, demonstrating the advantages of transformer architectures over traditional retrieval methods. Future work is suggested to further explore model differences and improve checkpoint selection strategies.
