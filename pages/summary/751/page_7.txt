The paper investigates the effectiveness of fine-tuning the T5 model on document ranking tasks using various training data conditions. It finds that while there is no clear pattern in performance across all datasets, the most significant difference is observed between baseline and reverse mappings. The hypothesis that T5 can learn arbitrary mappings with sufficient data is supported, particularly in low-data regimes where the baseline mapping shows the highest effectiveness.

In the experiments, the performance in the 2k and 20k conditions reveals that baseline mappings consistently outperform others, although the confidence intervals in the 2k condition indicate less statistical certainty. Notably, the T5 model struggles with subword manipulations, likely due to the removal of semantic associations and the introduction of unfamiliar token contexts. In contrast, other target word manipulations surpass the BM25 baseline, demonstrating the model's ability to leverage semantic knowledge.

The paper concludes that the generation-based approach of T5 outperforms classification-based methods, especially when training data is limited. It highlights the model's ability to utilize pretraining knowledge to generate coherent text, while the exact mechanisms remain a subject for further research.
