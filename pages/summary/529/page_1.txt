The paper introduces Federated Unsupervised Representation Learning (FURL), a novel approach aimed at utilizing large amounts of unlabeled data on distributed edge devices while ensuring data privacy. FURL addresses two primary challenges: the data distribution shift among clients, leading to inconsistent representation spaces, and the misalignment of representations due to the lack of unified information among clients.

To tackle these challenges, the authors propose the Federated Contrastive Averaging with dictionary and alignment (FedCA) algorithm. FedCA consists of two main components: a dictionary module that aggregates and shares representations among clients to maintain consistency, and an alignment module that aligns client representations to a base model trained on public data. The training process employs contrastive loss to refine local models.

The authors conducted extensive experiments under both IID and Non-IID settings, demonstrating that FedCA significantly outperforms existing baselines. The study emphasizes the potential of combining federated learning with unsupervised methods, particularly through contrastive learning, to effectively learn shared representations from decentralized and unlabeled data.
