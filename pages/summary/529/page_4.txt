The paper discusses the challenges of federated learning in a Non-IID setting, particularly focusing on the inconsistency of representation spaces across different clients. It compares two methods for generating representations from the CIFAR-10 dataset: a vanilla federated unsupervised approach (FedSimCLR) and a proposed method (FedCA) that incorporates a dictionary module.

The authors use T-SNE visualization to illustrate the representations of samples from classes 0 and 1. The results from FedSimCLR show that representations are scattered and difficult to distinguish, indicating that local models trained on limited data do not effectively capture the distribution of all classes. This inconsistency arises because clients can only train their local models on their own data, which varies significantly across clients.

To address this issue, the authors introduce a dictionary module that aggregates normalized projections from local models during each communication round. This shared dictionary serves as a set of negative samples for local contrastive learning, transforming the unsupervised learning problem into a multi-class classification task. The loss function is based on cross-entropy, allowing for better alignment of representations across clients.

Additionally, the authors implement a temporal ensembling technique to mitigate inconsistencies in the shared dictionary, maintaining a local ensemble dictionary that updates with projections from the trained local models. The results from the proposed FedCA method show improved clustering of representations for classes 0 and 1, confirming the effectiveness of the dictionary module.

The paper concludes that the alignment of representation spaces through the proposed methods significantly enhances the performance of federated learning in Non-IID scenarios, addressing the misalignment challenges posed by varying data distributions across clients.
