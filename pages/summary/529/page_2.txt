The paper identifies key issues in integrating these two domains, namely the inconsistency of representation spaces due to varying data distributions among clients and the potential misalignment of representations even when data are independent and identically distributed (IID).

To address these challenges, FedCA incorporates two main innovative modules: a dictionary module and an alignment module. The dictionary module, maintained by the server, aggregates and shares representations from clients to optimize local models. The alignment module trains a base model on a small public dataset, guiding local models to mimic this base model, thus ensuring aligned representations across clients.

The proposed algorithm operates in two stages during each round: clients train local representation models using contrastive learning and generate local dictionaries, followed by the server aggregating these models to form a global model and integrating local dictionaries into a global dictionary. Experimental results demonstrate that FedCA outperforms naive methods that merely combine federated learning with unsupervised techniques, marking it as a foundational advancement in addressing the FURL problem.

The paper also reviews existing literature on federated learning and unsupervised representation learning, highlighting the unique focus of FedCA on the consistency of representations rather than just parameters, setting it apart from previous works.
