The paper presents a comparison of various federated learning methods for unsupervised representation learning (FURL) using three datasets: CIFAR10, CIFAR100, and MiniImageNet. The methods evaluated include IID and Non-IID settings with baseline approaches such as FedAE, FedPR, and FedSimCLR, alongside the proposed FedCA method.

In the experiments, a centralized server coordinates five distributed client nodes. Each client trains on local data over five epochs, with the IID setting allowing random sampling of images and the Non-IID setting clustering samples by class. The models, including 5-layer CNN and ResNet-50, utilize contrastive learning, and results are measured using top-1 accuracy in a linear evaluation setup.

Results indicate that FedCA consistently outperforms baseline methods across all datasets and settings, particularly in Non-IID scenarios. For semi-supervised learning, the paper highlights that using the representation model from FURL significantly enhances performance on limited labeled data. In transfer learning, the features learned through FURL demonstrate strong transferability, with FedCA again showing superior results.

Key findings suggest that the design of FedCA modules effectively enhances representation learning in federated settings, particularly under the challenges of Non-IID data distributions.
