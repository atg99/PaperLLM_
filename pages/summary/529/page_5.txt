The paper discusses the challenges faced in federated learning, particularly in the context of unsupervised representation learning (FURL) using models like FedSimCLR and FedCA. It highlights the issue of misalignment in representations generated by local models trained on the same dataset, which can negatively impact model aggregation. To investigate this, the authors recorded the angles between normalized representations from local models trained on CIFAR-10 in an independent and identically distributed (IID) setting. The results showed that these angles often exceeded 20 degrees, indicating significant misalignment.

To address this issue, the authors proposed an alignment module that utilizes a small public dataset for training an alignment model. This model helps to regularize the outputs of local models, thereby reducing the angle between their representations. The implementation of this alignment module resulted in a reduction of the angle between the representations to within 10 degrees after ten training rounds.

The paper introduces the Federated Contrastive Averaging with Dictionary and Alignment (FedCA) algorithm, which combines contrastive loss with the alignment loss to improve the training of local models. The total loss function incorporates a scale factor to balance the contributions of both the contrastive and alignment losses.

The algorithm is detailed in a step-by-step manner, outlining the processes for both the server and client updates. The experimental setup includes comparisons with baseline methods such as AutoEncoder and Predicting Rotation, which are traditional approaches to unsupervised representation learning.

Overall, the findings suggest that the alignment module significantly enhances the alignment of representations in federated learning, ultimately improving the performance of the FedCA algorithm in learning effective representations from decentralized and unlabeled data.
