Phasic Policy Gradient (PPG) is introduced as an approach to improve sample efficiency in reinforcement learning, particularly in environments with high-dimensional input spaces, such as those found in the Procgen Benchmark. The paper highlights the effectiveness of PPG compared to a highly optimized Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) implementation, demonstrating that PPG significantly enhances sample efficiency across various environments.

The experiments conducted utilized the full distribution of levels in Procgen, and the results indicated that sharing representations between the policy and value function is crucial in these high-dimensional contexts. This contrasts with environments with lower dimensional input spaces, where such sharing may not be necessary. The findings suggest that PPG is particularly advantageous in vision-based tasks due to this representation sharing.

Additionally, the paper discusses the complexities associated with adjusting sample reuse in PPO, as it complicates the analysis of policy optimization effects. In contrast, PPG simplifies this aspect, allowing for clearer insights into the impact of sample reuse on performance.

Overall, the results emphasize PPG's potential to outperform PPO in specific environments, particularly those characterized by complex visual inputs, and provide a foundation for further exploration of its applications in reinforcement learning.
