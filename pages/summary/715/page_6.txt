The paper discusses Phasic Policy Gradient (PPG), comparing its implementation with a single-network variant and the Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) algorithm. The authors note that while PPG typically has a longer training time, the single-network version achieves similar efficiency to PPO. They emphasize the relationship between PPG and Iterative Relearning (ITER), highlighting that both alternate between standard reinforcement learning (RL) and a distillation phase, but with different goals: ITER focuses on improving generalization, while PPG aims to enhance sample efficiency.

The paper reviews related work, including Bejjani et al. who also used shared networks for value function training, and Bellemare et al. who explored value functions as auxiliary tasks. The research shows that value function representation learning is crucial for environments like Procgen, although it only considers the current policy's value function.

PPG employs a replay buffer to enhance sample efficiency during its auxiliary phase without utilizing off-policy data for policy updates. The paper acknowledges that while it adopts the clipped surrogate objective from PPO, PPG can integrate various actor-critic algorithms. Additionally, it notes that trust region methods like TRPO and ACKTR are compatible with the PPG framework, allowing for potential enhancements in policy updates.
