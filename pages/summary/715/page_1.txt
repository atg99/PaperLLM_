Following this, the auxiliary phase is dedicated to refining the value function. In this phase, the value function is optimized using the same data from the policy phase but allows for a more aggressive training approach, leveraging higher sample reuse. This separation of training phases allows the algorithm to avoid the interference that typically occurs when policy and value function objectives are optimized simultaneously.

The Phasic Policy Gradient framework demonstrates that by decoupling the training processes, it can achieve improved sample efficiency compared to traditional methods like PPO. This is particularly evident when evaluated on the Procgen Benchmark, where PPG shows significant enhancements in performance.

Overall, PPG offers a novel approach to reinforcement learning by addressing key challenges associated with shared networks in the actor-critic framework. It effectively balances the need for feature sharing with the necessity of distinct training phases, leading to better optimization of both policy and value functions.
