The paper discusses the Phasic Policy Gradient (PPG) method, highlighting its effectiveness in improving sample efficiency in reinforcement learning, particularly in the challenging Procgen Benchmark. The conclusions drawn from the results indicate that the optimal sample reuse levels differ significantly between policy and value function training. Traditional actor-critic frameworks struggle to train these objectives concurrently due to shared network architectures, leading to interference. PPG addresses this by decoupling the training processes, which allows for enhanced value function training without adversely affecting the policy.

While PPG introduces new hyperparameters leading to increased complexity, this is considered a minor drawback as the hyperparameters generalize well across multiple environments. The method not only improves sample efficiency but also establishes a framework for optimizing various auxiliary losses alongside reinforcement learning training. The authors suggest that further research could explore different auxiliary losses within the PPG framework, expanding its applicability and effectiveness.
