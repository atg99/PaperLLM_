The paper discusses the performance implications of varying levels of policy sample reuse and auxiliary phase frequency in Phasic Policy Gradient (PPG) methods. It highlights that in PPG, policy and value function training are decoupled, allowing for different sample reuse levels. The results indicate that training with a single policy epoch is typically optimal, suggesting that the benefits seen in Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) from multiple epochs are largely due to enhanced value function training. With well-tuned hyperparameters, a single policy epoch is nearly optimal.

Additionally, the paper examines the impact of additional auxiliary epochs on performance. It finds that while extra epochs generally improve performance, there is a diminishing return around six auxiliary epochs. The benefits of these epochs include improved feature sharing between policy and value networks, and a more accurate value function that reduces policy gradient variance. The significance of these benefits can vary by environment, with Procgen environments particularly benefiting from feature sharing.

Finally, the paper evaluates the frequency of auxiliary phases, controlled by the hyperparameter Nπ. Results show that excessive frequency of auxiliary phases can hinder policy optimization, suggesting that a balance must be struck to optimize performance. The paper concludes that further investigations may reveal more effective strategies for managing these phases.
