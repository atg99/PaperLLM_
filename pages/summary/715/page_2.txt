The paper introduces Phasic Policy Gradient (PPG), an enhancement over Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) that integrates periodic auxiliary training phases. The core idea is to distill features from the value function into the policy network during these auxiliary phases, thereby improving training efficiency for future policy updates.

PPG operates in two main phases: the policy phase and the auxiliary phase. In the policy phase, the method employs a disjoint network architecture for the policy and value functions, optimizing the policy using a clipped surrogate objective while also training the value function with a mean-squared error loss. The advantage function is estimated using Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE).

The auxiliary phase introduces a joint optimization objective that combines an arbitrary auxiliary loss and a behavioral cloning loss, allowing the policy network to leverage shared representations with the value function. The specific auxiliary loss used in this study is based on the value function, making it a key component to minimize policy distortion while enhancing feature learning.

Key hyperparameters such as the number of policy updates, sample reuse for both the policy and value function, and the number of epochs during the auxiliary phase are discussed, emphasizing their roles in optimizing the training process. The paper highlights that optimizing the auxiliary phase can lead to improved performance, suggesting a strategic approach to training reinforcement learning models by balancing policy updates with auxiliary learning.
