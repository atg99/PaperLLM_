The paper discusses advancements in medical imaging informatics through deep learning-based domain adaptation (DA). It categorizes various studies in medical imaging by DA methodology, tasks, modalities, anatomy, and learning scenarios. Key methodologies include direct transfer (DT), latent feature space transfer (LFST), and divergence approaches. 

The studies focus on tasks such as segmentation (S) and classification (C) across different imaging modalities like X-ray, MRI, and CT. For instance, in radiology, DA methods are applied to improve lung imaging from X-ray data across different demographics, utilizing both unsupervised and semi-supervised learning scenarios. 

Cross-modality adaptations are also highlighted, showcasing how techniques like reconstruction can translate data from CT to MRI for heart imaging. In pathology, studies explore the transfer of whole slide images (WSI) across varying stains and institutions, applying both unsupervised and supervised learning.

Additionally, the paper addresses ophthalmology, emphasizing the unidirectional adaptation of retinal fundus images from multiple datasets. Overall, the research illustrates the potential of deep learning and DA to enhance medical imaging accuracy and efficiency across multiple domains and scenarios.
