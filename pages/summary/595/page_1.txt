The paper discusses the advancements in medical imaging informatics through deep learning (DL) and domain adaptation (DA). It highlights the challenges faced in obtaining large labeled datasets for training DL models, particularly due to the tedious labeling process and privacy concerns. Multi-center datasets, while potentially increasing the amount of data, often suffer from heterogeneity and domain shifts, which can hinder the performance of pre-trained models in clinical settings.

The authors define transfer learning (TL) and DA, emphasizing that DA is a form of TL aimed at transferring knowledge from a labeled source domain to an unlabeled target domain. They categorize TL into inductive, transductive, and unsupervised types based on the availability of labeled data and the relationships between domains and tasks.

The survey reviews recent developments in DL-based DA methods for medical imaging, focusing on applications in pathology and radiology. It discusses various DA approaches, particularly domain transformation and latent feature-space transformation, and underscores the significance of unsupervised DA in image segmentation tasks. The paper concludes that DA presents a promising solution to the challenges of limited annotated training data, particularly through adversarial techniques, and identifies future opportunities in domain transferability, multi-modal DA, and leveraging synthetic data for enhanced applications.
