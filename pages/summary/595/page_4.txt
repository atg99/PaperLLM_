The paper discusses two primary families of domain adaptation (DA) approaches for medical imaging: Domain Transformation (DT-DA) and Latent Feature Space Transformation (LFST-DA). DT-DA focuses on translating images from one domain to another, typically using generative models like generative adversarial networks (GANs) to achieve pixel-level mapping. This method preserves semantic consistency and low-level appearance details, which enhances interpretability through visual inspection of synthesized images. 

The paper differentiates between unidirectional and bidirectional translation within DT-DA. Unidirectional translation involves mapping images from a source domain to a target domain, often using conditional GANs (cGANs) that incorporate additional information such as class labels. This approach has been employed to tackle dataset variations and cross-modality scenarios, such as translating cardiac images between CT and MRI.

Bidirectional translation, exemplified by CycleGAN, uses two GANs to ensure semantic consistency between original and reconstructed images. This method enhances image quality and expands training data for segmentation tasks. Variants of CycleGAN have been developed to address larger domain shifts and multi-domain translation.

The paper summarizes various applications of these DA methods in medical imaging, highlighting their effectiveness in improving adaptation performance and addressing challenges in image translation across different modalities.
