The paper discusses the application of deep learning (DL) in medical imaging, highlighting two major challenges: the scarcity of well-annotated images and the variability of multi-center datasets. Domain adaptation (DA) has been identified as a promising strategy to address these issues by reducing domain shifts and utilizing labeled data from related domains. Two notable DA techniques, FST-DA and DT-DA, have shown effectiveness in various medical imaging studies, particularly in unsupervised settings and organ segmentation tasks.

The authors emphasize the need for extensive benchmarking to better understand domain relationships across different imaging modalities and to evaluate adversarial versus non-adversarial methods. They also identify varying sample transferability and multi-modal domain issues as significant challenges. Potential solutions include exploring down-weighting or attention-based networks and alternative frameworks like MUNIT. Additionally, the authors suggest using synthetic data in less-explored areas such as 3D reconstruction and temporal disease analysis.

The work was supported by multiple grants and acknowledges the contributions of various organizations, while the authors clarify that the views expressed do not necessarily represent those of the NIH. The paper concludes with references to numerous studies that contribute to the ongoing dialogue in medical image analysis and the application of deep learning techniques.
