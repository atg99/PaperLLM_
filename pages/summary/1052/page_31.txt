The paper discusses the importance of model interpretability in the context of explainable artificial intelligence (XAI). It highlights various models, including SVM, ensembles, Bayesian models, decision trees, linear/logistic regression, kNN, and others, emphasizing the trade-off between model interpretability and performance. 

A significant concern raised is the need for a unified concept of explainability to facilitate the advancement of the field. The authors propose a definition of explainability as the capability of a model to clarify its functioning to its audience, building on existing literature. They argue for the establishment of a common framework that can guide the development of XAI techniques, thereby consolidating the diverse efforts within the field.

Metrics for measuring explainability are also discussed as critical for validating and comparing models. The authors stress that existing metrics, while useful, are inadequate and call for the development of more quantifiable and general XAI metrics. These metrics should assess the quality, usefulness, and satisfaction of explanations, as well as their impact on audience trust and model performance. The paper advocates for collective efforts from the community to create these metrics and enhance the evaluation of XAI techniques, aiming for a more robust understanding of explainability in machine learning.
