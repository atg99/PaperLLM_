This collection of references highlights various approaches and methodologies for interpreting and explaining black-box machine learning models. The papers cover a range of techniques, such as auditing indirect influences, utilizing influence functions, and applying sensitivity analysis to shed light on model predictions. 

Key concepts include the use of game theory for individual classification explanations, the propagation of Shapley values to elucidate model behavior, and the exploration of classifiers through randomization techniques. Additionally, works emphasize local rule-based explanations, iterative debugging methods, and high-precision model-agnostic explanations, showcasing the growing need for transparency in machine learning systems.

The studies collectively address the challenges of understanding complex model decisions, particularly in high-dimensional and sparse data contexts, and propose various frameworks for achieving greater interpretability in artificial intelligence applications.
