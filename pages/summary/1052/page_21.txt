The paper discusses the visualization of feature relevance techniques to enhance model interpretability, contrasting it with visualization methods for other model aspects, which are often model-specific. It emphasizes the need for post-hoc explainability in shallow machine learning (ML) models, particularly focusing on tree ensembles and Support Vector Machines (SVMs), which are popular yet complex.

Tree ensembles, such as Random Forests, improve accuracy by aggregating predictions from multiple decision trees but complicate interpretation. To address this, the paper reviews techniques for simplifying these models while preserving accuracy. For instance, one approach involves training a simpler model from a subset of data labeled by the ensemble, while another introduces the Simplified Tree Ensemble Learner (STEL). Additionally, a method using two models—one for interpretation and another for prediction—employs Expectation-Maximization and Kullback-Leibler divergence.

Feature relevance techniques are also explored, starting with Breiman's analysis of variable importance in Random Forests, which measures the impact of permutation on model accuracy. Further advancements highlight how variable importance can reveal relationships in complex systems and propose frameworks for improving decision-making, such as enhancing online ad rankings.

The paper notes that while simplification and feature relevance techniques are prevalent, most contributions date back to 2017, primarily focusing on bagging ensembles. There is limited recent research on the explainability of boosting and stacking classifiers, although the Stacking With Auxiliary Features (SWAF) approach aims to clarify how individual components of an ensemble contribute to predictions. Overall, the study underscores the importance of developing effective visualization and explanation methods to facilitate the understanding of complex ML models.
