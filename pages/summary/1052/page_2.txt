The introduction of the paper discusses the pivotal role of Artificial Intelligence (AI) in various sectors, highlighting its evolution from early interpretable systems to complex black-box models like Deep Neural Networks (DNNs). As AI systems increasingly operate with minimal human intervention, there is an urgent need for transparency in their decision-making processes, especially in critical areas such as medicine, law, and defense. The rise of opaque models poses risks of unjustifiable decisions, emphasizing the importance of explanations that support model outputs.

The paper argues that while there is a common perception of a trade-off between model performance and transparency, enhancing interpretability can actually improve the implementation of machine learning (ML) models. Interpretability ensures impartiality by detecting biases, facilitates robustness by identifying potential adversarial threats, and guarantees that meaningful variables influence model outputs, thereby establishing a truthful causal relationship.

To address these challenges, the concept of eXplainable AI (XAI) is proposed, which aims to develop ML techniques that produce explainable models without sacrificing performance. XAI also integrates insights from the Social Sciences to understand the psychology behind explanations, ultimately fostering trust and effective management of AI systems.
