This paper discusses Support Vector Regression (SVR) models and their capacity for interpretability in the context of various applications, particularly in drug discovery and neuroimaging. It highlights the integration of visual methods, such as heatmaps, to elucidate the relationships between input variables and output data. A notable contribution is the emphasis on the importance of the SVM margin, as argued in one study, which presents a statistic that accounts for this margin, enhancing the explanation of multivariate patterns observed in neuroimaging.

Furthermore, the paper explores the intersection of SVMs with Bayesian systems, where SVMs are interpreted as Maximum A Posteriori (MAP) solutions, allowing for better hyper-parameter tuning and class probability predictions. The challenges of interpretability in non-Conditional Positive Definite (CPD) kernels are also addressed, showcasing a geometrical interpretation that shifts focus from hyper-plane margin optimization to the minimization of distances between convex hulls in pseudo-Euclidean spaces.

The paper contrasts the post-hoc techniques used in SVMs with those applied to other models, noting a shift from broader model simplification methods to more localized explanations in SVMs. It remarks on the relative recency of these interpretability methods, with none dating beyond 2017, possibly due to the rise of deep learning models.

In the section on explainability in Deep Learning (DL), the paper reviews various post-hoc local explanations and feature relevance techniques for commonly used DL models, including multi-layer neural networks, Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN), and Recurrent Neural Networks (RNN). Despite the strong performance of multi-layer neural networks, their "black-box" nature has led to a demand for explainability techniques. Various methods, such as model simplification, feature relevance estimators, and local explanations, have been developed, though there are fewer simplification techniques for networks with multiple hidden layers. The DeepRED algorithm is highlighted as one of the few that extends rule extraction to multi-layer networks. Overall, the paper underscores the growing importance of explainability in both SVM and DL models to enhance their practical applications.
