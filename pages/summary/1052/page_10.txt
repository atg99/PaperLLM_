The paper discusses the critical role of end users in the success of machine learning (ML) models, emphasizing their need for the ability to interact and modify these models. It highlights the often-overlooked aspect of privacy awareness in the context of explainable artificial intelligence (XAI). The complexity of ML models can lead to potential privacy breaches if users cannot understand what data is represented internally. Conversely, if unauthorized third parties can explain the model's workings, it may compromise the confidentiality of the original data. The paper indicates that these privacy concerns will be addressed in more detail in subsequent sections.

The review further distinguishes between interpretable models, which are inherently understandable, and those requiring external XAI techniques for explanation. This distinction is framed as the difference between transparent models and post-hoc explainability. The paper outlines three levels of transparency: algorithmic transparency, decomposability, and simulatability. It also categorizes post-hoc techniques, including various forms of explanations such as text, visualizations, local explanations, and feature relevance.

The levels of transparency in ML models are essential for interpretability. Transparent models inherently convey some interpretability, while simulatability focuses on the human ability to understand the model's functioning. This complexity is significant, as simpler models tend to be more interpretable than their more complex counterparts. The paper aims to explore these concepts further, aligning with the broader classifications established in the literature.
