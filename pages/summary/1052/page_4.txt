This paper discusses the field of Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI), focusing on the challenges and methodologies related to explainability in machine learning models, particularly shallow and deep learning. The authors categorize XAI methods based on their suitability for different types of models, highlighting the need for improved metrics and research to enhance the understandability of deep learning systems.

A significant portion of the paper centers on the concept of Responsible Artificial Intelligence, which integrates explainability with principles of fairness, accountability, and privacy. The authors advocate for careful consideration of the implications of XAI techniques in contexts involving sensitive information, underscoring the necessity for compliance with evolving data privacy regulations.

The paper is structured to first clarify terminology surrounding explainability and interpretability in AI, distinguishing between these terms. It then reviews recent findings in XAI, including transparent models and post-hoc techniques, and discusses hybrid approaches that combine different methodologies. The authors analyze the benefits and challenges of these synergies, ultimately calling for a collective effort to address the societal impact of XAI, particularly in sectors increasingly reliant on machine learning technologies.

In conclusion, the paper emphasizes the importance of establishing a common understanding of explainability, while also engaging the research community in exploring the intersection of XAI with broader ethical considerations in AI deployment.
