The paper discusses various post-hoc explainability approaches for machine learning models, emphasizing the importance of feature relevance, local explanations, visualization, and text explanations. It highlights the relationship between model transparency and the audience interpreting the results, particularly in the context of linear and logistic regression.

Linear regression assumes a linear relationship between predictors and the predicted variable, while logistic regression is used for binary outcomes. Both models are considered transparent due to their algorithmic transparency, decomposability, and simulatability. However, they may still require post-hoc explainability techniques, especially when communicating results to non-expert audiences.

In social sciences, logistic regression has been widely utilized, leading to the development of various techniques for explaining model results. Key methods include overall model evaluation, statistical tests for individual predictors, goodness-of-fit statistics, and validation of predicted probabilities. These techniques provide a mathematical representation of the model's fit and behavior, aiding in the understanding of its outcomes. 

The paper underscores the necessity of tailoring explanations to the audience's expertise level, thereby bridging the gap between model complexity and interpretability.
