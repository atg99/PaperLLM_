The paper discusses the crucial role of explainability in artificial intelligence (AI) and machine learning (ML), highlighting it as a significant barrier to practical implementation. The authors emphasize that the current gap between the research community and business sectors, particularly in regulated industries such as banking, finance, security, and healthcare, hampers the adoption of advanced ML models. This reluctance stems from concerns about the risks these technologies pose to their assets.

Additionally, the paper points out a knowledge-related issue where the focus of research has shifted predominantly to performance metrics, overlooking the necessity for understanding the underlying mechanisms of AI models. This understanding is essential for further improvements and practical utility in various fields that handle large datasets.

The target audience for explainable AI (XAI) includes domain experts, regulatory entities, affected users, managers, and data scientists. Each group has specific motivations for seeking explainability, primarily centered around trust, compliance, and enhanced understanding of model decisions.

The exploration of XAI goals reveals a lack of consensus in the literature, as different studies propose varying objectives for what constitutes an explainable model. The authors aim to synthesize these goals to establish a classification framework for understanding the diverse purposes behind XAI efforts. This classification will assist in clarifying the motivations for developing explainable models in the context of AI and ML applications.
