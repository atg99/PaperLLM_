The paper discusses the evolving definition of Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI) as proposed by D. Gunning, which emphasizes the need for human users to understand, trust, and manage AI systems. However, this definition is deemed incomplete as it overlooks other critical aspects such as causality, transferability, informativeness, fairness, and confidence. The authors argue that a more comprehensive definition of explainability in AI must take into account the audience's perspective, leading to the suggestion that explainability involves the details and reasons a model provides to clarify its functioning for a specific audience.

The paper highlights the inherent ambiguities in defining explainability, noting that the effectiveness of an explanation is contingent upon the audience's understanding. It emphasizes the importance of cognitive psychology in evaluating explanations and acknowledges the challenge of objectively measuring the complexity of AI models and their interpretability. While model simplification can be quantified, other methods like visualization or natural language explanations do not lend themselves to clear metrics.

Additionally, the paper distinguishes between explainability and interpretability, with explainability focusing on post-hoc techniques that make non-interpretable models understandable. The authors propose a refined definition of explainable AI, emphasizing its role in producing clear explanations tailored to an audience. Overall, the paper calls for further exploration of metrics for assessing XAI approaches, recognizing this as a critical area for future research.
