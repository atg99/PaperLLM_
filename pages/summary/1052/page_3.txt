This paper presents an overview of the evolution and significance of Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI) from 2012 to 2019, highlighting the growing interest in interpretable AI models, particularly since 2017. The data, sourced from Scopus, indicates a clear upward trend in publications related to XAI, reflecting a broader recognition of the need for transparency and explainability in AI systems, which is essential in various application contexts.

The authors aim to establish a unified framework of categories and concepts for understanding XAI methods, while addressing the implications of explainability in contexts such as data privacy and model confidentiality. This discussion aligns with the principles of Responsible Artificial Intelligence, emphasizing the importance of model explainability in real-world applications.

Key contributions of the paper include:

1. A novel definition of explainability that emphasizes the audience's role in understanding machine learning (ML) models. The authors discuss various purposes for employing XAI techniques, ranging from fostering trust to enhancing privacy awareness.

2. An examination of different levels of transparency in ML models and various post-hoc explainability approaches for models that lack inherent transparency.

3. A comprehensive analysis of approximately 400 contributions in the XAI literature, organized into two taxonomies that differentiate between transparent and post-hoc explainability, covering both deep and non-deep learning models.

Overall, the paper underscores the critical importance of explainability in AI, advocating for a structured approach to understanding and implementing XAI techniques.
