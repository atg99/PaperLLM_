This alternative taxonomy highlights the multifaceted nature of explainability in deep learning (DL) and emphasizes the importance of understanding not just the outputs but also the internal workings of neural networks.

The Deep Nearest Neighbors (DkNN) approach provides human-interpretable explanations for deep neural network predictions by leveraging the concept of credibility, which reflects the confidence level across different layers of representation in the network. This method contrasts with traditional black-box models by associating them with more interpretable frameworks such as decision trees or rule-based systems, thereby enhancing understanding.

Moreover, hybrid models aim to enrich the interpretability of black-box systems by incorporating knowledge from transparent models. Techniques such as constraining neural networks with semantic knowledge bases or integrating ensembles of white- and black-box models exemplify this approach. This integration is particularly relevant in fields like reinforcement learning and recommendation systems, where symbolic and sub-symbolic methods are utilized to improve explainability.

The paper also discusses various post-hoc explainability techniques for DL, emphasizing that many methods can fit into multiple categories depending on their application. For instance, SHAP and LIME are versatile tools that can explain different aspects of deep learning models, illustrating the complexity and interconnectedness of explainability strategies. Overall, the exploration of hybrid models and alternative taxonomies reflects the growing focus on making deep learning more interpretable and accessible.
