The referenced works focus on various methods and techniques for interpreting and understanding machine learning models, particularly deep neural networks and decision trees. Key contributions include:

1. Sensitivity analysis and practical guidance on using convolutional neural networks (Zhang et al., 2015).
2. Simplification methods for decision trees (Quinlan, 1987).
3. Approaches for approximating complex models with single decision trees (Zhou and Hooker, 2016).
4. Techniques for interpreting support vector machines (Navia-Vázquez and Parrado-Hernández, 2006).
5. Methods for visualizing deep neural networks through feature-space partitioning (Thiagarajan et al., 2016).
6. Visualization techniques for understanding convolutional networks (Zeiler and Fergus, 2014; Mahendran and Vedaldi, 2015).
7. Fine-grained visual explanations for convolutional networks (Wagner et al., 2019) and learning to explain with complementary examples (Kanehira and Harada, 2019).
8. Visualization of predictor variable effects in black box models (Apley, 2016) and model prediction explanations through specific R packages (Staniak and Biecek, 2018).
9. Deconvolutional networks for enhanced understanding (Zeiler et al., 2010) and efforts to simplify network architectures (Springenberg et al., 2014).
10. Concept activation vectors for interpretability beyond feature attribution (Kim et al., 2017) and techniques for model compression (Polino et al., 2018).
11. Automatic rule extraction from long short-term memory networks (Murdoch and Szlam, 2017) and rule extraction methods from neural networks through sampling and genetic algorithms (Craven and Shavlik, 1994; Arbatli and Akin, 1997).

Overall, these studies contribute to the growing field of model interpretability, offering various strategies to make complex machine learning models more understandable and actionable for practitioners.
