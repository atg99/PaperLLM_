This paper explores the landscape of explainable artificial intelligence (XAI) within various machine learning (ML) models, categorizing techniques into transparent models, post-hoc explainability, and model-specific approaches. 

Transparent models include logistic regression, decision trees, and Bayesian models, which inherently provide interpretability. In contrast, post-hoc explainability techniques, such as LIME and G-REX, focus on explaining the behavior of complex, opaque models. This category encompasses methods like simplification, feature relevance explanation, and local explanations, which aim to elucidate model decisions.

The authors identify various feature relevance techniques, including influence functions and saliency maps, that help articulate how different inputs affect model outputs. Visual explanations, such as Shapley plots and variable importance charts, are also highlighted as essential tools for interpreting model behavior.

Model-specific techniques are discussed for ensembles, support vector machines, and neural networks, covering methods for simplification, feature importance, and architectural modifications. The paper emphasizes that recent literature continues to prioritize post-hoc explainability techniques, underscoring their critical role in the future of XAI.

Overall, the taxonomy presented in this paper offers a comprehensive overview of explainability techniques across different ML models, aiding in the understanding of how these models can be made more interpretable.
