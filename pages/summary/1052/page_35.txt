The paper discusses the importance of explaining the knowledge captured by AI models to ensure compliance with established theoretical principles, highlighting the role of Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI). It emphasizes a multidisciplinary approach in making AI models interpretable, considering the interests and needs of all stakeholders involved, from designers to end-users.

The paper outlines four key guidelines for implementing interpretable AI systems:

1. **Contextual Considerations**: Understanding the purpose of the AI model, the complexity of required explanations, and the performance of existing technologies is crucial. This ensures that the approach to interpretability aligns with domain-specific needs.

2. **Preference for Interpretable Techniques**: When developing AI systems, the choice of XAI methods should consider domain risks, available data, and the suitability of the machine learning model. Transparent models are generally preferred over complex ones that require post-hoc explanations, with black-box models being acceptable only when they better fit the problem's characteristics.

3. **Ethics and Impact Assessment**: If a black-box model is selected, it is essential to evaluate ethical, fairness, and safety impacts. This involves assessing explanatory strategies, ensuring they meet domain requirements, and creating an interpretability action plan that outlines responsibilities and timelines.

4. **Cognitive Considerations**: The final guideline focuses on human cognitive abilities, advocating for explanations that are accessible and understandable to the audience. This includes considering mental models and the appropriate vocabulary for effective communication of explanations.

Overall, the guidelines aim to enhance the transparency and accountability of AI systems, ensuring they are aligned with user needs and ethical standards.
