This collection of references highlights significant contributions to the field of explainable artificial intelligence, particularly focusing on the design, interpretation, and evaluation of models across various applications. 

Key concepts include the development of explainable BDI (Belief-Desire-Intention) agents, as discussed by Harbers et al., and the exploration of decision support models for clinical contexts by Aung et al. The challenges of transparency in AI systems are raised by Weller, while Freitas emphasizes the necessity for comprehensible classification models. 

The use of Bayesian decision tree ensembles in clinical applications is elaborated by Schetinin et al., and Martens et al. focus on user perspectives regarding classification model performance. Che et al. introduce interpretable deep models for predicting ICU outcomes, while Barakat and Diederich present eclectic rule-extraction techniques for support vector machines. 

The importance of natural language interfaces for autonomous robots is explored by Garcia et al., and Langley et al. discuss explainable agency in intelligent systems. Various methodologies for explaining nonlinear classification decisions through deep learning are examined by Montavon et al. and Kindermans et al. 

Ras et al. address user values and concerns regarding explanation methods, while Bach et al. propose techniques for controlling explanatory heatmap semantics. Katuwal and Chen focus on model interpretability in precision medicine, and Neerincx et al. investigate the role of cognitive explanations in human-agent teamwork.

Overall, these works collectively advance the discourse on explainability in machine learning, emphasizing its critical role in enhancing trust, usability, and effectiveness in intelligent systems.
