The paper discusses the importance of Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI) in the context of the growing use of AI technologies, particularly in Machine Learning. It identifies explainability as a critical challenge for deploying AI models effectively, especially with the rise of complex techniques such as deep learning that lack the transparency of earlier expert systems and rule-based models.

The authors provide a comprehensive overview of the existing literature on XAI, proposing a new definition of explainable Machine Learning that emphasizes the audience's perspective. They present a taxonomy of recent contributions to explainability, focusing on various Machine Learning models and developing a specific taxonomy for Deep Learning methods.

The paper highlights several challenges facing XAI, including the intersection of data fusion and explainability. The authors advocate for the concept of Responsible Artificial Intelligence, which aims to ensure fairness, accountability, and model explainability in the widespread implementation of AI in organizations. The goal is to create a detailed taxonomy that can guide newcomers and encourage professionals from various fields to adopt AI solutions without bias against their interpretability.

Overall, the paper serves as a resource for advancing research in XAI and promoting its benefits across different sectors.
