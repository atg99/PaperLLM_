The paper discusses the development of guidelines for the responsible use of artificial intelligence (AI), emphasizing the need for a comprehensive approach that incorporates various methodological principles. It highlights the importance of explainability in AI (XAI) while asserting that XAI alone is insufficient for achieving responsible AI. Essential principles such as privacy and fairness must also be integrated into practice.

The authors review significant AI principles published since 2016, categorizing organizations based on their nature, the content of their principles, target audiences, and alignment with international human rights. An example provided illustrates how some organizations outline principles that promote inclusion and equal opportunities, specifically emphasizing that AI outputs should not discriminate against any individual or group based on characteristics like race, gender, or disability. This leads to the concept of Fair AI, which prioritizes equitable treatment in AI system outcomes alongside performance optimization. 

The discussion sets the stage for exploring the implications of XAI and data fusion in fostering responsible AI practices, reinforcing the idea that a holistic approach is crucial in addressing the ethical challenges posed by AI technologies.
