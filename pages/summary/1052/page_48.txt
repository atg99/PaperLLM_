The referenced literature provides a comprehensive overview of the field of explainable artificial intelligence (XAI), highlighting key methodologies, frameworks, and theoretical foundations. Shane T. Mueller et al. present a meta-review that synthesizes significant ideas and publications in the domain, focusing on the role of explanation in human-AI systems. Guidotti et al. survey various methods for explaining black box models, while Montavon et al. discuss techniques for interpreting deep neural networks. Fernandez et al. explore evolutionary fuzzy systems within XAI, addressing their purpose and applications.

Gleicher introduces a framework for assessing comprehensibility in modeling, and Craven emphasizes the extraction of comprehensible models from neural networks. Michalski's work on inductive learning theory and methodology is foundational, providing insights into the learning process. Doran et al. and Doshi-Velez and Kim contribute to the conceptualization and scientific rigor of interpretable machine learning.

Vellido et al. focus on making machine learning models interpretable, while Holzinger et al. stress the importance of explainable AI in the medical domain. Interactive models, such as iBCM by Kim et al., empower users through intuitive interaction. Ribeiro et al. present a method for explaining classifier predictions, and Fox et al. discuss explainable planning.

Overall, these works collectively advance the understanding of XAI, addressing both theoretical perspectives and practical implementations necessary for fostering trust and comprehensibility in AI systems.
