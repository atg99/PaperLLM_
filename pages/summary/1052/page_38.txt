The section discusses the role of Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI) in identifying implicit correlations that may lead to discrimination in machine learning models. XAI techniques, such as SHAP, help model designers understand how input features affect model outputs, revealing hidden biases related to protected attributes like race or gender. The principle of Fair AI emphasizes the need for bias detection in datasets, as black-box models can inadvertently produce unfair outcomes by considering sensitive factors.

The text outlines several approaches to fairness, including individual fairness, group fairness, and counterfactual fairness, which aim to detect and mitigate bias. Various sources of bias are identified, such as skewed data, tainted data, limited features, sample size disparities, and the presence of proxy features that correlate with sensitive attributes.

To evaluate fairness in AI, particularly in supervised machine learning, three criteria are proposed: independence, where model predictions do not depend on sensitive features; separation, where predictions are independent of sensitive features given the target variable; and equalized odds, which ensures that true positive and false positive rates are consistent across different sensitive subgroups. These criteria help determine when AI systems operate without bias.
