The paper discusses the concepts of decomposability and algorithmic transparency in machine learning models, emphasizing their importance for human understanding and reasoning. Decomposability refers to the ability to explain each part of a model, including inputs, parameters, and calculations. It enhances intelligibility, allowing users to interpret and explain model behavior. However, not all models can achieve this, especially if inputs are complex or require additional tools for understanding.

Algorithmic transparency pertains to the user's ability to comprehend the processes by which a model generates outputs from inputs. Linear models exemplify transparency due to their understandable error surfaces, while deep architectures often lack this clarity due to opaque loss landscapes. For a model to be algorithmically transparent, it must be fully explorable through mathematical analysis.

The paper also introduces post-hoc explainability techniques designed for models that are not inherently interpretable. These techniques, including text and visual explanations, local explanations, and feature relevance explanations, aim to enhance interpretability by applying various methods suited to different data types. The classification of these techniques is organized by the author's intent, the methods used, and the type of data they apply to, facilitating researchers in selecting appropriate techniques for their needs.
