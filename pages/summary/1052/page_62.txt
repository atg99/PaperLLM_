The referenced papers explore various methodologies for extracting interpretable rules from artificial neural networks and enhancing their transparency. Thrun (1994) discusses rule extraction from networks with distributed representations, while Setiono and Leow (2000) introduce the FERNN algorithm for fast extraction. Taha and Ghosh (1999) focus on symbolic interpretation, and Tsukimoto (2000) examines rule extraction from trained networks. Zilke et al. (2016) present Deepred for rule extraction from deep networks, while Schmitz et al. (1999) propose ANN-DT for converting neural networks into decision trees. Sato and Tsukimoto (2001) further investigate decision tree induction for rule extraction.

Féraud and Clérot (2002) present a methodology for explaining neural network classifications. Recent works by Shrikumar et al. (2017) and Sundararajan et al. (2017) focus on feature attribution methods, while Adebayo et al. (2018) critique local explanation methods for their lack of sensitivity to parameter values. Papernot and McDaniel (2018) introduce deep k-nearest neighbors for interpretable deep learning. Li et al. (2015) emphasize visualization and understanding of neural models in NLP, and Tan et al. (2015) discuss improving interpretability through stimulated learning. Rieger et al. (2019) highlight the utility of interpretations in aligning networks with prior knowledge. Nguyen et al. (2016) explore synthesizing preferred inputs for neurons, while Li et al. (2016) investigate the convergence of learning across different networks. These studies collectively contribute to the understanding and interpretability of neural networks, addressing challenges in their opaque decision-making processes.
