A critical challenge for Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI) methods is to provide explanations that are comprehensible to non-technical stakeholders, including society, policymakers, and legal entities. This is essential for addressing ambiguities and fulfilling the anticipated right to explanation under the EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR).

Additionally, concerns regarding confidentiality in XAI have been underexplored. The proprietary knowledge embedded within machine learning (ML) models can be compromised even with limited access, highlighting the importance of developing XAI tools that maintain model confidentiality while providing explanations. 

XAI techniques can inadvertently assist adversarial attacks, where malicious actors manipulate input data to alter an AI model's output. For example, subtle changes to visual inputs can mislead systems in critical applications like autonomous vehicles. Therefore, efforts to enhance the robustness of models against such attacks are essential, utilizing resources like Cleverhans and other frameworks designed to detect and mitigate adversarial vulnerabilities.

Moreover, generative models such as Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) and Variational Autoencoders can be leveraged to enhance understanding of ML decision-making. These models can generate data representations to identify patterns and create counterfactuals, aiding users in comprehending model behavior and improving their trust in AI systems.

In summary, XAI must evolve to balance the need for transparency and robustness while safeguarding confidential information and addressing the implications of adversarial threats. This dual focus will be critical in fostering trust and accountability in AI applications across various sectors.
