The paper emphasizes the importance of respecting individuals' privacy rights, particularly in the context of data governance and the ethical use of data in artificial intelligence (AI) systems. The European Commission (EC) outlines three key aspects of privacy related to AI: 

1. **Privacy and Data Protection**: It is essential to ensure privacy throughout the AI system's lifecycle, safeguarding both user-provided information and data generated from user interactions. This is crucial to prevent potential misuse of data that could lead to discrimination or unfair treatment.

2. **Quality and Integrity of Data**: High-quality data sets are vital for the effective performance of AI systems, particularly those using machine learning (ML). However, data may contain biases and inaccuracies that need to be addressed before model training. Ensuring data integrity is also emphasized.

3. **Access to Data**: Protocols must be established to govern access to personal data, specifying who can access the data and under what conditions.

The paper also discusses the risks to privacy posed by deep learning (DL) methods, even without data fusion. For example, limited images can compromise privacy despite obfuscation efforts, and model parameters can be exposed through input queries. The authors advocate for the development of secure cryptographic protocols to protect privacy, particularly in the context of explainable AI (XAI).

As data fusion becomes relevant, the relationship between explainability and data fusion techniques is explored. Traditional data-level fusion methods lack direct connections to ML models, but advancements in DL have blurred these lines. The initial layers of DL architectures learn high-level features from raw data, effectively merging data in a task-oriented manner. 

The paper highlights the potential of XAI techniques to analyze feature correlations, enhancing understanding of how data sources are fused within DL models. This understanding can improve model usability and identify additional valuable data sources. However, in the era of Big Data, where data characteristics may be unpredictable, new methods for data fusion must be developed. The paper concludes by calling for further research into ensuring privacy and explainability in AI systems.
