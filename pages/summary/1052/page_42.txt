The paper discusses various levels of data fusion in machine learning, specifically focusing on Big Data fusion, Federated Learning, and Multiview Learning. 

In Big Data fusion, local models are trained on segmented data at Worker nodes through a Map task, while a Reduce node aggregates the outputs from these tasks. This approach distributes the complexity of model learning across multiple nodes, emphasizing the importance of the fusion strategy between Map and Reduce tasks to ensure quality outcomes.

Federated Learning differs as it involves local model training on data from remote client devices. Clients send encrypted model updates, such as gradients, to a central server without transmitting raw data, thus ensuring privacy. The central server aggregates these contributions to create a shared model, reducing processing latency and alleviating the server's computational load.

Multiview Learning focuses on creating different views of objects based on multiple data sources or feature subsets. It aims to optimize ML models from these views collectively to improve generalization, especially in scenarios with weak supervision that can lead to overfitting. Techniques for joint optimization include co-training and co-regularization.

The paper also highlights the importance of integrating privacy considerations throughout the lifecycle of AI systems, particularly when handling personal data from multiple sources, under the framework of Responsible AI.
