The paper discusses the development of explainable artificial intelligence (XAI) models designed to enhance the interpretability of their behaviors, leading to various classification methods for XAI taxonomies. It differentiates between several approaches used in deep learning (DL) and machine learning (ML), including linear proxy models, decision trees, automatic rule extraction, salience mapping, and more. The taxonomy presented in the paper is based on an alternative classification derived from previous literature, highlighting the complementarity and overlaps with existing frameworks.

Key insights from the taxonomy include the categorization of methods based on model processing and representation explanations, which allows for a distinction between the execution trace of models and their internal data structures. This differentiation is crucial for selecting appropriate XAI methods based on specific failure reasons, drawing parallels to debugging techniques in traditional programming.

The paper also identifies ongoing challenges in the field of XAI, emphasizing the need for further research to address these issues. It outlines potential research avenues that can be pursued to enhance the understanding and implementation of explainability in ML and data fusion models, aiming to improve transparency and interpretability in artificial intelligence systems.
