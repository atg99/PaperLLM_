The referenced articles focus on various approaches to interpreting machine learning models and enhancing their transparency. Seo et al. propose a dual attention mechanism in convolutional neural networks for predicting review ratings, facilitating interpretability. Larsen et al. discuss interpreting parameters in logistic regression models with random effects, while Gaonkar et al. explore support vector machine interpretations in neuroimaging analysis. Xu et al. introduce visual distillation to interpret deep classifiers, and Deng presents methods for interpreting tree ensembles.

Further contributions include Domingos's work on knowledge discovery through multiple models, and Tan et al.'s auditing of black-box models via model distillation. Berk et al. evaluate statistical methods for forecasting criminal behavior, and Hara and colleagues work on making tree ensembles interpretable. Henelius et al. investigate classifier interpretations via attribute interactions, while Bau et al. quantify interpretability in deep visual representations.

Núñez and team present rule extraction techniques from support vector machines, and Kearns et al. address fairness in machine learning with auditing methods. Akyol et al. analyze the implications of transparency in strategic machine learning, and Erhan et al. contribute to understanding representations in deep learning architectures. Collectively, these works underscore the importance of interpretability in machine learning, advocating for methods that enhance understanding and trust in automated systems.
