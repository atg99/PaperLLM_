This collection of references focuses on various methodologies and techniques aimed at enhancing the interpretability of machine learning models, particularly in the context of neural networks and other complex algorithms. The papers explore different approaches to understanding how these models make decisions, ranging from randomization techniques to visual inspection methods.

Olden and Jackson (2002) discuss a randomization approach to elucidate variable contributions in artificial neural networks. Krause et al. (2016) highlight the importance of visual interaction with predictions to better understand black-box models. Rosenbaum et al. (2011) provide insights into interpreting linear support vector machines using heat map molecule coloring.

Tan et al. (2014) present unsupervised feature construction and knowledge extraction from genomic data, while Krening et al. (2017) investigate the role of explanations in reinforcement learning through sentiment and advice. Ribeiro et al. (2016) offer a model-agnostic framework for interpretability across different machine learning models. 

Bach et al. (2015) introduce layer-wise relevance propagation for pixel-wise explanations in non-linear classifiers. Etchells and Lisboa (2006) propose orthogonal search-based rule extraction for trained neural networks, emphasizing practical efficiency. Zhang et al. (2017) examine plan explainability in robot task planning.

Additionally, Santoro et al. (2017) introduce a neural network module for relational reasoning. Peng et al. (2002) analyze the use of logistic regression in higher education research, while Büstün et al. (2007) focus on visualizing support vector regression models. Zhang et al. (2019) interpret convolutional neural networks using decision trees, and Wu et al. (2018) discuss tree regularization for enhancing interpretability in deep models.

Finally, Hinton and colleagues (2015, 2017) explore knowledge distillation techniques, demonstrating methods for simplifying neural networks into more interpretable structures like soft decision trees. Collectively, these studies contribute to the ongoing discourse on making machine learning models more transparent and understandable.
