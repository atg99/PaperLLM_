The section discusses the challenges in achieving explainable deep learning (XAI), highlighting the current lack of standardized terminology within the field, such as the inconsistent use of terms like feature importance and saliency maps. A significant trade-off exists between interpretability and accuracy, where simpler explanations may lack exhaustiveness. The intelligibility of explanations must cater to diverse audiences, from experts to laypersons, underscoring the need for objective metrics to evaluate explanations.

To enhance the quality of explanations, the paper suggests drawing from human psychology and cognitive sciences, emphasizing that effective explanations should be constrictive and selective, focusing on causal links rather than mere probabilities. Counterfactual explanations can also aid user understanding.

The integration of connectionist and symbolic paradigms is proposed as a means to improve explainability. While connectionist methods are precise, they lack transparency, whereas symbolic methods, despite being less efficient, offer greater clarity. The use of established reasoning rules and knowledge bases can facilitate qualitative data processing, making explanations more accessible.

The paper argues for a model that generates explanations without relying on user interpretation, advocating for a semantic representation of knowledge to produce understandable outputs. It also calls for rigorous evaluation methods inspired by social sciences to ensure consistency in assessing explanation quality.
