The paper discusses the trends in post-hoc explainability for various machine learning (ML) models, categorized into model-agnostic techniques and model-specific approaches. Model-agnostic techniques can be applied to any ML model, focusing on extracting information from its prediction process. These techniques include simplification methods, feature relevance estimation, and visualization strategies.

In the realm of simplification, local explanations are emphasized, with notable methods like Local Interpretable Model-Agnostic Explanations (LIME) that create locally linear models around opaque model predictions. Other rule extraction methods such as G-REX and approaches that learn rules in Conjunctive Normal Form (CNF) or Disjunctive Normal Form (DNF) are also highlighted. Additionally, the paper presents a novel perspective on model simplification as a model extraction process, emphasizing the importance of transparency in complex models.

The paper concludes with a second taxonomy specifically for deep learning models, addressing their unique characteristics and linking them to the broader classification criteria established earlier. This comprehensive analysis underscores the growing importance of explainability in ML, driven by the need for models that not only perform well but are also interpretable by humans.
