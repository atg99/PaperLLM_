The paper discusses various methods for enhancing the interpretability of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) through visualization techniques and feature relevance analysis. One approach involves using activation maps to identify critical image regions associated with specific object classes by projecting output layer weights onto convolutional feature maps. Subsequent research demonstrated that max-pooling layers could effectively replace convolutional layers with large strides without sacrificing accuracy, providing cleaner visualizations than Deconvnet through guided backpropagation.

To improve the interpretability of CNNs, some studies focused on designing specific loss functions for filters in higher-level convolutional layers, resulting in more exclusive activation patterns for different labels. Additionally, techniques like Layer-wise Relevance Propagation (LRP) have been employed to visualize pixel contributions to predictions, producing heatmaps that enhance understanding of model decisions. Grad-CAM, a method leveraging gradients from target concepts, generates coarse localization maps to highlight significant image regions relevant to predictions.

The paper also addresses methods for generating textual explanations of visual content. For instance, a combination of CNN feature extractors with RNN attention models has been used to automatically describe images, while a three-level attention model aids in fine-grained classification by proposing and filtering candidate image regions. In video captioning, a CNN paired with a bi-directional LSTM extracts features for textual description generation.

Furthermore, the authors explore frameworks for reconstructing images from CNN internal representations, revealing that certain layers maintain detailed visual information. They also introduced methods for maximizing class scores based on gradients and developed a Deep Generator Network to create representative images for specific output neurons. Lastly, network dissection was proposed as a quantitative approach to assess the interpretability of latent CNN representations by analyzing the activation of individual units in response to various images.
