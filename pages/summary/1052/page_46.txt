The paper addresses the topic of model explainability in machine learning (ML), beginning with a clarification of key concepts and the motivations for developing interpretable ML methods. It presents a systematic review of recent literature on explainability, categorized into two perspectives: ML models with inherent transparency and post-hoc explainable AI (XAI) techniques aimed at enhancing interpretability. This analysis results in a comprehensive taxonomy that classifies various approaches reported in the field.

The paper emphasizes the importance of explainability in deep learning models, offering an alternative taxonomy that aligns with specific domains where explainability is applicable. Furthermore, it extends the discussion to the concept of Responsible AI, which encompasses principles such as fairness, transparency, and privacy that must be adhered to when implementing AI models. The implications of XAI techniques for data fusion and fairness are explored, highlighting potential privacy risks associated with the fusion of protected data.

The authors envision a future where model interpretability is integrated with considerations of data privacy, model confidentiality, fairness, and accountability. They argue that a responsible implementation of AI requires a holistic approach to these principles. The paper concludes with acknowledgments of funding support from various organizations, underscoring the collaborative nature of the research.
