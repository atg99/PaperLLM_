The paper discusses the various goals associated with explainable machine learning (ML) models, emphasizing that while causality is not a primary focus in the literature, transferability, informativeness, confidence, fairness, accessibility, and interactivity are critical objectives. 

Transferability refers to the ability of models to be effectively applied across different contexts, supported by training-testing strategies and the clarity provided by explainability. Informativeness highlights the necessity of ML models to provide relevant information to users, ensuring that human decisions align with model outputs. 

Confidence relates to the reliability and stability of model interpretations, underscoring the importance of a model's robustness. Fairness addresses the ethical implications of ML, advocating for transparency in model decision-making to identify biases. Accessibility aims to make ML models comprehensible to a broader audience, facilitating user involvement in model development. Lastly, interactivity enhances user engagement, allowing for dynamic interactions with the model to foster understanding and trust.

Overall, the paper underscores that explainability in ML is not just a technical requirement but a multifaceted goal that addresses practical, ethical, and user-centric considerations.
