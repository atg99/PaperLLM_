The paper discusses the tradeoff between model interpretability and performance in machine learning (ML), emphasizing that enhancing interpretability may lead to a decline in decision quality. It explores the potential of explainable artificial intelligence (XAI) to find a balance between these two aspects, particularly in Subsection 5.1. The need for a clear consensus on the definition of explainability within AI is outlined in Subsection 2.2, noting that the reasons for pursuing explainability vary and are not consistently articulated in existing literature.

Subsections 4.3 and 4.4 focus on the explainability of deep learning models, analyzing recent advancements categorized by a specific bibliographic taxonomy. Subsection 5.3 continues this examination by discussing challenges related to the explainability of deep learning. The paper concludes with Subsections 5.4 to 5.8, which identify underexplored research areas connected to model explainability.

The discussion on interpretability versus performance highlights that complexity does not guarantee accuracy, particularly when data is well-structured and features are high-quality. More complex models can approximate intricate functions better when sufficient and varied data is available. However, when data diversity is lacking, increased complexity may hinder the model's effectiveness. The paper argues that while complexity typically reduces interpretability, advancements in explainability techniques could potentially reverse this trend. The approximation dilemma is also addressed, stressing that explanations must be sufficiently detailed yet accessible to meet the needs of their intended audience without oversimplifying the model's critical features.
