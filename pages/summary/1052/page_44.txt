This paper discusses the potential implications of explainable artificial intelligence (XAI) techniques on data privacy, particularly in the context of data fusion at the model level. It raises concerns that XAI could inadvertently compromise the confidentiality of private data by allowing sensitive information to be inferred from the explained relationships between protected and unprotected features. The fusion of outputs from multiple transparent models, such as tree ensembles, can lead to opacity, necessitating post-hoc explainability methods. 

The paper highlights a scenario where relationships among input features are revealed through post-hoc techniques, raising the question of whether unknown features could be inferred from known ones, leading to potential privacy breaches. An example involving multiview models demonstrates how shared data sources could expose sensitive information not directly shared. It also considers the implications of sharing model architecture in federated learning, questioning whether such sharing could facilitate adversarial attacks.

Furthermore, the paper discusses knowledge-level fusion, suggesting that XAI could enhance knowledge extraction from machine learning models. However, it warns that this capability might enable reverse engineering of explanations, potentially compromising differential privacy. 

The paper distinguishes between centralized and distributed data fusion, noting that while centralized approaches do not introduce additional privacy concerns, distributed fusion could pose risks, especially when sensitive information is inadvertently revealed through model updates. Federated learning is presented as a method to protect data privacy by training models locally, yet the use of post-hoc explainability techniques could still lead to privacy violations.

Overall, the intersection of data fusion, privacy, and model explainability remains underexplored, highlighting the need for further research to address these unresolved challenges.
