The referenced papers explore various methodologies for enhancing the interpretability and transparency of machine learning models, particularly focusing on black box models such as support vector machines and neural networks. 

Key contributions include:

1. Chen et al. discuss improving transparency and control in data-driven inferences, emphasizing the importance of understanding individual-level insights in big data contexts.
2. Goldstein et al. introduce individual conditional expectation plots as a means to visualize statistical learning, facilitating a deeper understanding of model behavior.
3. Casalicchio et al. present techniques for visualizing feature importance, helping to demystify the decision-making processes of complex models.
4. Tolomei et al. propose actionable feature tweaking for tree-based ensembles, allowing users to understand how changes in features affect predictions.
5. Auret and Aldrich utilize random forests to interpret nonlinear relationships in process variables, demonstrating the applicability of machine learning in industrial contexts.
6. Rajani and Mooney investigate stacking and ensembling methods for visual question answering, contributing to model explainability in computer vision.
7. Nunez et al. and Sollich et al. delve into rule-based learning systems and Bayesian methods for support vector machines, respectively, focusing on the interpretability of predictions and class probabilities.
8. Landecker et al. and Jakulin et al. analyze hierarchical networks and nomograms, respectively, to facilitate the interpretation of individual classifications and model outputs.

These works collectively advance the field of explainable artificial intelligence by providing tools and methodologies that allow users to gain insights into model predictions and the underlying data relationships.
