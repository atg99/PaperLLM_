The collection of references highlights various aspects of explainable artificial intelligence (AI) and its applications across different domains. Key contributions include foundational work on interpretable machine learning methods and their definitions, as discussed by Murdoch et al. This is complemented by studies on the importance of trust in automated systems, such as Haspiel et al.'s exploration of expectations in automated vehicles.

Several papers address the challenges of extracting knowledge from trained neural networks, indicating a growing interest in understanding the decision-making processes of AI systems, as seen in Tickle et al. and Samek et al. The application of causal inference methods in machine learning is also prominent, with Athey and Imbens discussing heterogeneous causal effects, while Goudet et al. and Louizos et al. focus on learning functional causal models through advanced neural network architectures.

The ethical implications of AI, particularly in risk assessment and healthcare, are critically examined by Barabas et al. and Caruana et al., emphasizing the need for intelligible models that can ensure fairness and transparency. Furthermore, the exploration of adversarial examples by Yuan et al. brings attention to the vulnerabilities in deep learning systems and the importance of developing robust defenses.

Overall, these references collectively highlight the necessity of transparency, interpretability, and ethical considerations in the development and deployment of AI systems, underscoring the ongoing efforts to bridge the gap between complex AI models and human understanding.
