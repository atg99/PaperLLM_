The paper discusses the concepts of explainability and understandability in the context of ethical AI and explainable AI (XAI). It defines explainability as a characteristic of a model that involves actions aimed at clarifying its internal functions. The terms used in this field are differentiated as follows:

1. **Understandability**: This refers to a model's ability to enable a human to grasp its function without needing to understand its internal structure or algorithms.

2. **Comprehensibility**: In relation to machine learning models, it indicates the model's capacity to present its learned knowledge in a way that is understandable to humans. This concept is tied to model complexity and is rooted in the idea that results from computer induction should resemble descriptions a human expert would provide.

3. **Interpretability**: This is the ability of a model to explain its decisions in understandable terms.

4. **Explainability**: This concept pertains to the interface between humans and decision-makers, ensuring that the explanations provided are both accurate and comprehensible.

5. **Transparency**: A model is transparent if it is inherently understandable. The paper categorizes transparent models into three types: simulatable, decomposable, and algorithmically transparent.

The paper emphasizes that understandability is central to XAI, as it encompasses both the model's and the human's ability to understand decisions. The audience's cognitive abilities and goals are crucial, making the notion of audience a cornerstone of XAI. While the paper notes the philosophical discussions surrounding explanation theories, it highlights the lack of consensus on interpretability and explainability in the literature, despite ongoing efforts to develop interpretable models and techniques.
