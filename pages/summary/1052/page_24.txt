The paper discusses advancements in model interpretability, particularly focusing on gradient boosting trees and convolutional neural networks (CNNs). It highlights methods for explaining complex models, such as hierarchical partitioning of feature spaces and knowledge distillation from ensembles into single models. 

In addressing multi-layer neural networks, the paper emphasizes the growing popularity of feature relevance methods due to the complexity of these models. Notable works include a method for decomposing network classification decisions into contributions from input elements, known as deep Taylor decomposition, and DeepLIFT, which computes importance scores by comparing neuron activations to reference levels. 

The paper also critiques existing feature relevance techniques, pointing out that many violate essential axioms like sensitivity and implementation invariance. It introduces integrated gradients as a new method that adheres to these axioms and presents two new explanation methods, PatternNet and PatternAttribution, which are deemed more theoretically sound.

For CNNs, recognized as state-of-the-art in computer vision tasks, the paper outlines two primary approaches to understanding their function: mapping output back to the input space to identify discriminative parts and interpreting how intermediate layers process information. Key contributions include the use of DeconvNet for visualizing activations and the occlusion sensitivity method for generating saliency maps. 

Subsequent efforts to enhance input mapping quality led to architectural modifications, such as incorporating a global average pooling layer, which improved the interpretability of CNNs. Overall, the paper underscores the importance of developing robust methods for explaining complex neural network models while addressing theoretical soundness in existing approaches.
