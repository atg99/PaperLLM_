The paper discusses the concept of sufficiency, which is achieved when the target variable is independent of sensitive features given the model output, ensuring that the Positive Predictive Value remains consistent across different subgroups. This principle, known as Predictive Rate Parity, emphasizes the importance of minimizing bias in machine learning (ML) models, although not all fairness criteria can be simultaneously fulfilled.

To address bias, the paper outlines two main actions: evaluation and mitigation. Evaluation involves measuring the bias in the model using various metrics tailored to specific criteria, such as statistical parity difference for independence or equal opportunity difference for separation. Mitigation encompasses techniques to correct bias related to sensitive features, classified into three categories: 

1. **Pre-processing**: Techniques applied before training the ML model, like Reweighing, which adjusts feature weights to eliminate discrimination.
   
2. **In-processing**: Techniques applied during training, such as Adversarial Debiasing, which optimizes predictive ability while minimizing sensitivity to protected features.

3. **Post-processing**: Techniques applied after training, like Equalized Odds, which adjust classification thresholds to balance true positive and false positive rates across sensitive groups.

The paper also highlights the interrelationship between fairness and explainability in AI. It notes that many AI principles related to explainable AI (XAI) explicitly address fairness, indicating that organizations often consider both aspects in responsible AI practices. XAI techniques can aid in bias detection, providing visual frameworks to analyze model bias and generating explanations for model decisions using methods like SHAP. This integration facilitates the identification of hidden correlations that could lead to discrimination.

Additionally, the paper mentions a fair-by-design approach that combines fairness and interpretability by developing ML models that generate human-readable explanations while maintaining reduced bias. This approach leverages weak supervision to identify relevant data prototypes for model training, enhancing both fairness and transparency in AI systems.
