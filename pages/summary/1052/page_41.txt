The paper discusses the importance of redress mechanisms to address unforeseen adverse impacts in AI systems, emphasizing the need for accountability and trust, particularly for vulnerable groups. Explainable AI (XAI) is highlighted for its role in enhancing auditability and fairness, thereby minimizing negative impacts.

The paper also explores the concept of data fusion, which merges various information sources to improve machine learning (ML) model performance across different applications, such as industrial prognosis and the Internet of Things. However, the integration of data fusion techniques raises concerns about data privacy, making it a critical area for research in Responsible AI.

The discussion on data fusion is categorized into three levels: data level, model level, and knowledge level. Data-level fusion involves combining raw data from multiple sources to create a coherent data flow. Model-level fusion aggregates models trained on different data subsets, while knowledge-level fusion merges knowledge representations to enhance understanding and manageability of the data.

Additionally, the paper notes that data fusion can be centralized, where data is processed at a single location, or distributed, where local nodes independently process and merge their data. Emerging data fusion approaches are also mentioned, indicating ongoing developments in the field that impact data privacy. The paper underscores the need for further exploration of the intersection between XAI and data fusion, given its relevance to responsible AI practices.
