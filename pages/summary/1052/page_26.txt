The paper discusses various techniques for enhancing the interpretability of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) in the context of semantic segmentation. It highlights the distinction between local and global explanations, emphasizing that many methods rely on local features while others aim to establish broader insights through prototypes. Evidence from previous studies indicates that local explanations are heavily influenced by low-level features due to the strong priors established by deep architectures.

A notable framework presented combines multiple interpretability methods, such as feature visualization and attribution, to provide a comprehensive understanding of network decision-making processes. This framework organizes visual interpretability into distinct blocks that relate to different network layers and components, allowing for a more nuanced exploration of how the network differentiates between labels.

Additionally, the paper introduces the LIME framework, which simplifies the interpretability process by perturbing input images and analyzing the resultant changes in predictions. By breaking down images into manageable superpixels, LIME facilitates a clearer understanding of the model's decision-making pathways. Overall, the paper underscores the importance of combining various interpretability techniques to enhance the understanding of complex CNN models.
