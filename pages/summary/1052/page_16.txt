K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN) is a transparent model used for classification and regression problems. It predicts the class of a test sample by voting on the classes of its K nearest neighbors, based on a distance measure. In regression, this voting is replaced by averaging target values of the nearest neighbors. KNN's reliance on distance and similarity makes it interpretable, resembling human decision-making based on past experiences. This model allows users to inspect classification reasons and observe how predictions change with different values of K. However, high values of K and complex features can hinder interpretability.

Rule-based learning generates rules to characterize data, ranging from simple if-then rules to complex combinations. Fuzzy rule-based systems enhance interpretability by using linguistic terms and performing better in uncertain contexts. These models are transparent and often help explain complex models through rule generation. A challenge in rule generation is balancing coverage and specificity; while more rules can improve performance, they may reduce interpretability. Fuzzy rules can mitigate this by covering more range with simpler interpretations. Overall, rule-based learners are effective for interpretability and can communicate model behavior to non-expert users while maintaining prediction capabilities.
