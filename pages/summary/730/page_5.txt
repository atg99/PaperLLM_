The paper presents a detailed analysis of the impact of various factors on the performance of reinforcement learning algorithms, particularly focusing on Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) and network architectures. Key findings indicate that trust-region optimization is essential for maintaining sample efficiency and that the PPO clipping threshold significantly influences performance, with an initial recommendation of 0.25 while also exploring values of 0.1 and 0.5.

The study emphasizes the advantages of using separate policy and value networks, which consistently yield better results across environments. It suggests that the optimal architecture size varies according to environmental complexity, with wider value networks being beneficial without adverse effects on performance. Additionally, two hidden layers are found to be effective for both policy and value networks, with tanh activation functions outperforming relu.

A noteworthy observation is the critical role of initial policy settings, particularly the importance of centering the action distribution around zero with a small standard deviation, which can lead to substantial performance improvements. The paper also compares methods for transforming actions into a bounded domain, with the tanh function showing superior results over clipping.

Overall, the research provides valuable insights into the design choices for neural networks in reinforcement learning, advocating for specific configurations that enhance training efficiency and effectiveness across various environments.
