The paper presents an analysis of various performance factors in reinforcement learning, particularly focusing on the Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE) and V-trace methods. The authors found that using GAE with a discount factor of 0.9 consistently outperformed V-trace across all tasks, although tuning this value per environment could yield modest improvements. They also discovered that PPO-style value loss clipping negatively impacts performance, and the Huber loss consistently performed worse than Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss, regardless of the chosen thresholds.

In their training setup analysis, the authors explored data collection and minibatch handling strategies, emphasizing the importance of multiple passes over experience data for better sample efficiency. They proposed a modification to the original PPO approach, recommending the recomputation of advantages at the beginning of each pass to enhance data diversity in minibatches while acknowledging the trade-off of using slightly stale advantages.

The findings indicate that increasing the number of parallel environments can lead to faster training times but may decrease performance due to shorter experience chunks. Increasing batch size did not adversely affect sample complexity, suggesting it should be maximized for quicker iterations. However, the number of transitions gathered per iteration significantly impacted performance, and careful tuning of this parameter is advised.

In the investigation of timestep handling, the authors highlighted the sensitivity of performance to the discount factor, with a value of 0.99 performing well across environments. They noted that frame skipping improved performance in some cases, while the handling of episode terminations due to timestep limits did not appear to affect outcomes.

Overall, the paper recommends using GAE with a discount factor of 0.9, avoiding Huber loss and PPO-style clipping, performing multiple passes over experience data, shuffling transitions before minibatching, and recomputing advantages at each data pass. It also suggests utilizing many parallel environments and increasing batch size for efficient training while tuning the number of transitions per iteration for optimal performance.
