The paper investigates key hyperparameters and optimizers in reinforcement learning (RL), focusing on their effects across various environments. It emphasizes the importance of the discount factor, recommending a starting value of 0.99 and suggesting frame skipping when applicable. 

In terms of optimizers, the study evaluates Adam and RMSprop, noting that performance differences between them are minimal. The learning rate is identified as a significant factor, with Adam's default rate of 0.0003 performing well across tasks. Momentum enhances Adam's performance, while its effect on RMSprop is less pronounced. The paper suggests using Adam with a momentum of 0.9 and a tuned learning rate, with linear decay potentially offering slight performance improvements.

The analysis of regularization methods reveals that while various policy regularizers were tested, significant benefits were only observed in the HalfCheetah environment, particularly from the entropy constraint. The findings suggest that regularization may be less critical in the contexts studied, possibly due to the inherent mechanisms of the PPO policy loss and effective policy initialization.

Overall, the paper contributes to the understanding of RL by highlighting the importance of hyperparameter tuning, optimizer choice, and the limited role of regularization in certain scenarios. It also references related work that addresses reproducibility and implementation impacts on RL performance.
