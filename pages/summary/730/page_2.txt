This paper investigates the impact of various design choices on the performance of on-policy deep actor-critic reinforcement learning algorithms, particularly in the context of continuous control tasks. A surprising finding is that the policy initialization scheme significantly affects performance, with the authors noting that initializing the network to have an action distribution with zero mean and low standard deviation enhances training speed. 

The paper is structured as follows: Section 2 outlines the experimental setup and performance metrics, Section 3 presents and analyzes the experimental results, Section 4 discusses related work, and Section 5 concludes the findings. The appendices provide detailed descriptions of design choices, default hyperparameters, and raw experimental results.

The study focuses on on-policy deep actor-critic reinforcement learning, defining it as a policy iteration process that generates experiences using the current policy and then improves that policy based on the experiences. The authors benchmark their approach using five continuous control environments from OpenAI Gym, including Hopper-v1 and Humanoid-v12.

To create a highly configurable algorithm, the authors researched existing literature and codebases, compiling a comprehensive list of commonly used choices, such as loss functions and initialization methods. They implemented a unified on-policy deep actor-critic agent based on the SEED RL code base, ensuring that all choices are available as configuration options for ease of experimentation.

Investigating the effect of choices is complex due to the need for optimal hyperparameter configurations. Random sampling of choices often leads to poor performance due to sub-optimal settings, which can hinder learning. Additionally, variations in the Mujoco physics simulator can influence the behavior of environments, which the authors acknowledge as a potential factor in their experiments.
