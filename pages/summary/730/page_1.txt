The paper "What Matters for On-Policy Deep Actor-Critic Methods? A Large-Scale Study" addresses the complexities involved in the implementation of reinforcement learning (RL) algorithms, particularly on-policy deep actor-critic methods. Despite the conceptual simplicity of these algorithms, their state-of-the-art performance hinges on numerous low- and high-level design decisions, which are often overlooked in the literature. This gap complicates the attribution of progress in RL and hinders further advancements.

The authors implement over 50 design choices within a unified on-policy deep actor-critic framework and conduct an extensive empirical study involving the training of over 250,000 agents across five continuous control environments of varying complexity. The study aims to investigate the impact of these design choices on agent performance.

Key contributions of the paper include the implementation of a comprehensive set of design choices, the execution of a large-scale experimental study, and the provision of practical insights and recommendations for optimizing the training of on-policy deep actor-critic RL agents. By systematically analyzing the results, the authors highlight the importance of lower-level implementation details and their influence on the effectiveness of high-level algorithmic strategies. The findings aim to foster clearer understanding and facilitate progress in the field of reinforcement learning.
