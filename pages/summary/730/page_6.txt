This paper explores various factors affecting the performance of reinforcement learning algorithms, particularly focusing on the initialization of neural networks, normalization techniques, and advantage estimation methods. 

Key findings include the observation that the decreased magnitude of initial actions significantly influences performance, while other factors, such as last layer initialization scale and network initialization schemes, have lesser importance. The paper recommends initializing the last policy layer with weights that are 100 times smaller, using softplus for transforming network outputs into action standard deviations, and tuning a negative offset to reduce the initial action standard deviation. The use of tanh as the activation function and for transforming samples to bounded action space is also advised, along with creating a wide value MLP while potentially narrowing the policy width.

The study on normalization techniques reveals that input normalization is essential for optimal performance across most environments, while value function normalization has a strong impact, particularly on specific tasks like HalfCheetah and Humanoid. However, it can hinder performance on Walker2d. Per-minibatch advantage normalization and clipping of normalized observations show minimal effects, while gradient clipping offers slight performance improvements.

In terms of advantage estimation, GAE and V-trace outperform N-step returns, suggesting that combining value estimates from multiple timesteps is beneficial. The paper also discusses the effects of PPO-style pessimistic clipping and the use of Huber loss instead of MSE for value learning.

Overall, the findings highlight the importance of careful initialization, normalization, and advantage estimation techniques in enhancing the performance of reinforcement learning algorithms.
