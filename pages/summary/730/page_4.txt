The paper presents a comprehensive examination of various aspects of on-policy deep actor-critic methods through a series of experiments grouped into eight thematic areas: Policy Losses, Network Architecture, Normalization and Clipping, Advantage Estimation, Training Setup, Timesteps Handling, Optimizers, and Regularization. Each section includes detailed experimental designs and results, with raw data and plotting scripts available online for further analysis.

In the Policy Losses section, the authors explore different policy loss functions, including vanilla policy gradient, V-trace, PPO, AWR, V-MPO, and a limiting case termed Repeat Positive Advantages (RPA). The objective is to assess the significance of the policy loss function in the context of the experiments, rather than to declare a definitive superior loss function. The findings indicate that PPO consistently outperforms the other loss functions in most environments, particularly excelling in challenging tasks such as Humanoid and Ant. Conversely, PG and V-trace show inferior performance likely due to their limitations in handling off-policy data across iterations.

The results highlight the importance of selecting appropriate hyperparameters for each loss function, as this can influence performance outcomes. However, even with optimal hyperparameter configurations, PPO remains a leading choice. The empirical analysis suggests that while some losses may be sensitive to hyperparameter choices, PPO's robustness across various settings makes it a favorable option for training agents in continuous control tasks.
