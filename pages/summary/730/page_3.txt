The paper addresses the intricate relationships between various hyperparameter choices in machine learning experiments, particularly their potential interactions, which can lead to misleading results if not considered together. To systematically explore these interactions, the authors propose a structured experimental design that includes grouping related choices, such as those affecting neural network architecture and the Adam learning rate.

Key experiments involve training multiple models by randomly sampling choices within these groups while maintaining a competitive base configuration. This approach ensures a diverse set of models that incorporate different combinations of interacting choices, allowing for robust performance assessments.

Two main analyses are performed for each choice: the conditional 95th percentile of performance distributions based on specific choice values, and the distribution of these values among the top-performing configurations. The first analysis provides a reliable estimate of expected performance, while the second identifies which choices contribute significantly to high performance.

Performance is measured through a comprehensive training process, evaluating models based on their average undiscounted episode return across multiple seeds to mitigate the effects of randomness. The results are further validated through extensive sampling across hundreds of configurations, with confidence intervals calculated for the reported metrics.

Overall, the methodology emphasizes the importance of considering interactions among hyperparameters in order to derive meaningful insights from experimental results in reinforcement learning settings.
