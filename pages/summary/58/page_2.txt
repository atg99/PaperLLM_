This paper discusses a sequence-to-sequence architecture for speech applications, focusing on the use of Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) and Transformers. The architecture includes both encoder and decoder components that process source and target sequences. The encoder, termed "EncBody," typically employs a bi-directional Long Short-Term Memory (BLSTM) network to transform the source sequence into an intermediate representation. In Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR), this encoded sequence can be utilized for frame-wise predictions through Connectionist Temporal Classification (CTC).

The decoder, referred to as "DecBody," is generally unidirectional and generates target frames using the encoded input and a prefix of the target sequence. Attention mechanisms are integrated to enhance the decoder's ability to focus on relevant parts of the source sequence when generating outputs.

The paper also introduces the Transformer model, which replaces recurrent connections with a self-attention mechanism. It details the multi-head attention (MHA) approach, which allows the model to process multiple attention heads in parallel. The Transformer encoder uses self-attention to refine the input sequence, while the decoder employs both self-attention and encoder-decoder attention to produce the target sequence.

Overall, the paper highlights the advancements in sequence modeling through RNNs and Transformers, emphasizing their roles in improving the performance of speech applications.
