The paper presents a comparative study of Transformer and RNN models in text-to-speech (TTS) and automatic speech recognition (ASR) tasks across various corpora. The baseline RNN model used is Tacotron 2, while the Transformer configurations were optimized for performance. Experiments were conducted using two datasets: M-AILABS and LJSpeech, with results indicating that both models achieve similar L1 loss convergence, but Transformers show better performance with larger minibatch sizes. 

Key findings include the effectiveness of using larger minibatches for accelerating training in TTS, though this can lead to overfitting in validation loss for Transformers. Attention maps should be monitored for convergence rather than solely relying on loss metrics. Furthermore, the decoding speed of Transformers was slower compared to RNNs, but using FastSpeech improved this significantly. 

The study concludes that while Transformer-based TTS can match RNN performance, there are trade-offs regarding training speed and quality. Future work will focus on enhancing these aspects and integrating ASR techniques into TTS systems. The authors emphasize the importance of reproducible recipes, pretrained models, and training tips to further advance research in speech applications.
