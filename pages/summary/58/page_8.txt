The references listed provide a comprehensive overview of significant contributions to the fields of automatic speech recognition (ASR), speech synthesis, and related datasets. Notable works include the Aurora working group's evaluation of large vocabulary continuous speech recognition systems, the development of various speech corpora, such as the spontaneous speech corpus of Japanese and the LibriSpeech corpus derived from public domain audiobooks. The CHiME challenges illustrate advancements in speech separation and recognition, while the SWITCHBOARD corpus has been pivotal for research and development in telephone speech.

Further, the TED-LIUM corpus has been expanded for automatic speech recognition tasks, and various methodologies for improving end-to-end speech recognition systems have been explored, including the integration of language models and the application of transformer architectures. The references also highlight datasets like the LJ Speech dataset and the M-AILABS speech dataset, which are essential for training and evaluating speech models. Collectively, these works underscore the evolution and ongoing challenges in speech technology, emphasizing the importance of robust datasets and innovative techniques in advancing the state of the art in ASR and speech synthesis.
