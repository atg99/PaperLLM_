During inference, the model generates outputs in an autoregressive manner, utilizing previously predicted features as input for the subsequent predictions.

The TTS architecture consists of an encoder that transforms a sequence of character IDs into encoded features, incorporating positional encoding to capture temporal information. The decoder then processes these encoded features alongside the target log-mel filterbank features, utilizing a Prenet to align the hidden representations with the encoder's feature space, facilitating effective attention mechanisms.

The training process optimizes for two primary loss functions: L1 loss for the reconstruction of target features and binary cross-entropy loss focused on predicting the end of sequence token. This dual-loss approach aims to enhance the overall performance of the TTS system.

In summary, the paper outlines the architectures and methodologies for automatic speech recognition (ASR), speech translation (ST), and text-to-speech (TTS) systems, emphasizing the differences in their respective frameworks, particularly in how they handle input sequences, attention mechanisms, and training processes. Each extension builds on a similar sequence-to-sequence (S2S) framework, adapting to the unique requirements of the tasks at hand.
