This paper addresses the challenges of class imbalance in the calculation of Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) by applying a constant weight to positive samples. A guided attention loss is introduced to enhance the learning of diagonal attention in specific layers of the encoder-decoder model, as it is known that attention matrices are diagonal in certain heads. The training process combines multiple loss values without introducing hyperparameters for balance.

In the Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) experiments, the authors utilized 15 diverse datasets covering various languages and recording conditions. Data preparation followed the Kaldi "s5x" recipe, with optimizations in feature extraction and data augmentation methods such as speed perturbation and SpecAugment applied to several corpora.

The ASR system employed a Transformer architecture, adjusting parameters for different datasets, and compared it to RNN architectures. Training configurations for the Transformer were optimized based on previous literature, with performance assessed over a range of epochs without using development sets for early stopping. Results indicated that the Transformer consistently outperformed RNN in 13 out of 15 datasets, achieving competitive character and word error rates compared to traditional DNN/HMM systems like Kaldi, even under challenging conditions.

The findings suggest that increasing the minibatch size can mitigate underfitting in Transformer models, and that the accumulating gradient strategy can help emulate larger minibatch sizes when GPU resources are limited. While dropout was found essential for Transformer models to prevent overfitting, it did not benefit RNN models. However, decoding speed remains a concern for the Transformer, which is slower than the Kaldi system due to the complexity of self-attention mechanisms. The paper concludes with plans for further exploration into multilingual ASR performance, building on the success observed with the Transformer architecture.
