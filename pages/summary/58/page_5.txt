This paper presents a comprehensive analysis of automatic speech recognition (ASR) and speech translation (ST) systems across various datasets and languages. It includes detailed descriptions of ASR datasets, their characteristics, and the corresponding results in terms of character error rates (CER) and word error rates (WER). The datasets encompass a range of languages including English, Japanese, Mandarin Chinese, and Spanish, with some datasets enhanced by additional external data.

Key findings indicate that the Transformer model outperforms the recurrent neural network (RNN) model in ASR tasks across nine languages, achieving over a 10% relative improvement in most cases, particularly noted in the VoxForge Italian dataset. The results demonstrate that the Transformer model, even without additional language model fusion, consistently surpasses RNN performance in multilingual end-to-end ASR.

In the speech translation experiments, the authors implemented a baseline RNN model and compared it to a Transformer model. The Transformer achieved a BLEU score of 17.2, surpassing the RNN baseline of 16.5, highlighting its effectiveness in translation tasks as well. The study also addresses issues of underfitting in the Transformer model and suggests using pretrained encoders to enhance performance.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the superiority of the Transformer architecture in both ASR and ST applications, providing robust results across multiple languages and datasets.
