This paper investigates the performance of ARIMA, LSTM, and BiLSTM models in predicting financial time series data. The dataset, collected from Yahoo Finance, includes daily, weekly, and monthly stock data from various indices and companies, covering a period from January 1985 to August 2018. The “Adjusted Close” variable was utilized as the sole feature for model training and testing, with a split of 70% for training and 30% for testing.

The assessment of model performance is based on loss values and Root-Mean-Square-Error (RMSE), which measures the accuracy of predictions. The goal of the models is to minimize loss and improve RMSE, with a percentage reduction in RMSE calculated to evaluate improvements.

The paper distinguishes between different types of neural networks: feed-forward Artificial Neural Networks (ANN), which do not utilize past input data for feedback; Recurrent Neural Networks (RNN), which incorporate feedback to retain memory of past inputs; and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks, designed to capture long-term dependencies through mechanisms like input, forget, and output gates. The BiLSTM networks enhance LSTM by processing inputs in both forward and backward directions, which may further improve the learning of long-term dependencies.

Overall, the study highlights the effectiveness of LSTM and BiLSTM architectures in financial time series prediction compared to traditional ARIMA models, emphasizing the importance of memory in capturing complex patterns in the data.
