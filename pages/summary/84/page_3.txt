This paper discusses various types of Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) and focuses specifically on those that produce a single output, which is the probability of the next element in a sequence given previous inputs. The sequence probability is expressed as a product of conditional probabilities that depend on the preceding inputs. However, RNNs face significant challenges, particularly the "vanishing gradients" problem, where gradients diminish as they propagate through layers, hindering the model's ability to learn long-term dependencies. Conversely, the "exploding gradients" problem can occur when gradients accumulate excessively, making training difficult. 

To address these challenges, the paper introduces Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) models, which extend RNN capabilities by incorporating a memory mechanism that allows them to learn and retain long-term dependencies effectively. LSTMs utilize three types of gates: the forget gate, which decides what information to discard; the input gate, which determines the extent of new information to add; and the output gate, which controls the contribution of memory to the output. Each gate employs specific mathematical functions, such as sigmoid and tanh, to facilitate decision-making and information processing.

Additionally, the paper presents Deep Bidirectional LSTMs (BiLSTM), an advancement of LSTM models that processes input data in both forward and backward directions, enhancing the model's ability to capture context and dependencies from both past and future sequences. This dual processing capability aims to improve performance in tasks requiring a comprehensive understanding of sequential data.
