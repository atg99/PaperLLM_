The paper presents a comparative analysis of forecasting performance among three models: ARIMA, LSTM, and BiLSTM, as shown in Table II, which lists the Root Mean Square Errors (RMSEs) for various stock indices. The results indicate that BiLSTM consistently outperforms both LSTM and ARIMA across all datasets, demonstrating significant reductions in RMSE. For instance, the BiLSTM model for the N225 monthly stock achieved a reduction of 96.98% compared to ARIMA.

The discussion section elaborates on the superior performance of BiLSTM models, attributing it to their ability to process input data in both directions, thus capturing underlying context more effectively. This capability is particularly beneficial for sequential data, such as financial time series, despite the uncertainty of its advantages in numerical data compared to textual data.

Figures 2(a)-(c) depict the forecasts for IBM stock using the three models, highlighting the overlap between predicted and actual values. Additionally, the paper explores the loss values during training for LSTM and BiLSTM when evaluated over batch steps at a single epoch. The loss for LSTM decreases steadily, while the BiLSTM exhibits an initial increase before gradually declining, suggesting that BiLSTM may require more training data to stabilize compared to LSTM.

Overall, the findings underscore the effectiveness of BiLSTM models in time series forecasting, prompting further investigation into the operational dynamics of these recurrent neural network variations.
