This paper presents an analysis of the performance and training behavior of ARIMA, unidirectional LSTM (LSTM), and bidirectional LSTM (BiLSTM) models using IBM stock data. The key focus is on whether training data in both directions (left-to-right and right-to-left) enhances time series forecasting accuracy.

The findings indicate that the BiLSTM model outperforms the unidirectional LSTM, achieving an average accuracy improvement of 37.78%. The descriptive statistics reveal that the LSTM model stabilizes faster than the BiLSTM, with loss values for LSTM reaching equilibrium quicker, suggesting that the unidirectional approach requires fewer data batches. In contrast, the BiLSTM shows greater variability in loss values, necessitating more batches to optimize its parameters.

The analysis of loss values during training rounds highlights that LSTM stabilizes after the first round, while BiLSTM continues to learn and adjust in the second round, indicating its ability to capture additional features in the data.

Moreover, the study notes that LSTM divides data into larger batches, while BiLSTM requires smaller batches due to its dual-directional training process, which limits the amount of data processed at once.

In conclusion, the research advocates for the use of BiLSTM over LSTM for time series forecasting tasks, suggesting potential for further exploration in multivariate and seasonal forecasting scenarios.
