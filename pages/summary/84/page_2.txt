This paper presents an experiment comparing the performance and behavior of two RNN-based architectures, LSTM and BiLSTM, focusing on three main research questions: the impact of bidirectional learning on prediction accuracy, the differences in how these architectures process input data, and their speed in reaching equilibrium. Key contributions of the study include an investigation into the effectiveness of additional training layers in financial time series prediction, a performance analysis that reveals BiLSTM models outperform LSTMs by 37.78% reduction in error rates, and a behavioral analysis showing that BiLSTMs utilize smaller training batches but take longer to reach equilibrium than LSTMs.

The structure of the paper is organized into several sections: related works are reviewed in Section II, while Section III provides essential background and mathematical formulations. Section IV describes the experimental setup, data collection, and preparation. The algorithms developed are outlined in Section V, and results are reported in Section VI. Section VII discusses performance under controlled factors, and the paper concludes with future research directions in Section VIII.

The paper also contrasts traditional time series forecasting methods, such as ARIMA, with machine learning approaches, highlighting the limitations of regression-based models in capturing nonlinear relationships and achieving long-term predictions. Previous studies comparing LSTM and BiLSTM performance are referenced, setting the stage for this research, which specifically examines the benefits of bidirectional training in financial data analysis.
