Capsule networks demonstrate potential for learning hierarchical relationships between layers through parameter-free routing processes, enhancing generalization capabilities. However, challenges exist in their application to natural language processing (NLP), including the selection of iteration numbers for routing processes, which currently lack reliability in determining convergence across different examples. While recent studies have shown promise in NLP tasks, issues such as the computational intensity required for training capsule networks due to a high number of capsules and large output spaces persist.

This paper addresses these challenges with several contributions. First, it formulates routing processes as a proxy problem aimed at minimizing a total negative agreement score to assess performance at the instance level. Second, it introduces an adaptive optimizer that adjusts the number of iterations for each example, improving convergence reliability. Third, it presents capsule compression and partial routing to enhance scalability for datasets with large output spaces. The proposed framework outperforms established baselines in multi-label text classification and question answering, showcasing superior generalization in low-resource scenarios.

The NLP-Capsule framework integrates a convolutional layer to extract features from document embeddings, utilizing filters of varying widths to generate diverse feature maps. These are then transformed into primary capsules through a group-convolution operation, which allows for richer information representation compared to traditional scalar approaches. Overall, the proposed methods aim to overcome existing limitations and improve the effectiveness of capsule networks in NLP applications.
