The paper discusses the NLP-Capsule framework, which utilizes capsules to enhance the representation of features in natural language processing tasks. It begins with the transformation of feature maps into capsules through a convolutional layer using 1x1 filters, where each capsule is represented as a vector indicating its significance for the task. The length of each capsule vector, constrained between 0 and 1 by a squashing function, reflects its utility.

A key challenge identified is the excessive number of primary capsules generated relative to the input document size, leading to increased computational demands during the routing processes. To address this, the authors propose capsule compression, which condenses primary capsules into fewer, more relevant capsules by merging similar ones and eliminating outliers. This is achieved through a weighted sum of primary capsules, with weights learned through supervision.

The aggregation layer is introduced to efficiently route these condensed capsules to subsequent layers. While traditional pooling methods risk losing critical information, the proposed routing processes iteratively connect condensed capsules to high-level capsules, enabling the learning of hierarchical relationships. The routing algorithm is designed to transform condensed capsules into candidate high-level capsules and represent them through a weighted sum of these candidates, with coupling coefficients updated in a clustering-like manner.

The paper critiques existing routing algorithms for their inconsistent performance at the instance level, highlighting the risk of unreliability. To improve this, the authors frame the routing process as a minimization problem aimed at enhancing the agreement score between capsules, thereby refining the routing outcomes for individual instances.
