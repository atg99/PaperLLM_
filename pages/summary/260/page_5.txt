The experiments were conducted on two NLP tasks: multi-label text classification and question answering, focusing on scalability and generalizability.

In the multi-label text classification task, we tested our approach on two datasets: RCV1 and EUR-Lex, which differ significantly in label scale and training examples per label. RCV1 has 103 labels with an average of 729.67 training examples per label, while EUR-Lex has 3,956 labels with only 15.59 training examples per label. Our method outperformed strong baselines on EUR-Lex and showed competitive results on RCV1, highlighting superior generalization capabilities in low-resource settings.

We compared our approach against various non-deep learning and deep learning baselines, using standard evaluation metrics like Precision@k and NDCG@k. The implementation utilized GloVe word embeddings and a convolutional layer to extract features, transforming them into capsules. To manage the extensive output space during inference, we employed a CNN baseline to generate a constrained set of candidate labels.

The results indicated that our capsule-based approach not only achieves competitive performance with fewer parameters but also maintains a reasonable computational speed. The analysis of generalization showed that even when training examples per label were halved, our method continued to perform effectively. This suggests that our approach is well-suited for applications where training data is scarce, making it a valuable contribution to the field of NLP.
