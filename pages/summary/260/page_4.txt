The paper explores the optimization of Neural Architecture Search (NAS) functions by introducing Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) as a non-parametric alternative to traditional approaches, which often assume parametric distributions for agreeable pairs. The authors formulate the NAS function using KDE to maximize the agreement score between pairs of capsules. The function is defined to minimize the weighted distance between capsules, employing an Epanechnikov kernel.

The optimization process involves updating capsule representations through Mean Shift to minimize the NAS function, while also introducing an adaptive optimizer to adjust the number of iterations based on negative agreement scores, addressing convergence issues at the instance level. The authors also implement a leaky-softmax function to mitigate noise in capsule outputs.

The final representation layer, which contains the last capsules, is computationally intensive as it scales with the output space. To improve scalability, the authors propose a partial routing algorithm that constrains the output space to the relevant labels for individual examples, thus avoiding unnecessary computations for each training instance. The NAS function is adapted to reflect this approach, focusing on both positive and randomly selected negative outputs.
