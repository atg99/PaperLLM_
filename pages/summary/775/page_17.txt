The paper presents findings related to the performance of a cancer ROI detection model, focusing on examples of regions of interest (ROIs) and discrepancies between model predictions and consensus grades. 

In part a, the authors illustrate cancer ROIs marked in cyan, with bounding boxes in red, highlighting how the model uses broader context for classification. Part b discusses the alignment of prior CT scans with current scans based on detected cancer bounding boxes, noting that when prior detections are unavailable, the lung center serves for approximate alignment. This approach allows for the comparison of features derived from a larger context without requiring precise voxel-to-voxel alignment, as the model processes these features after several max-pooling layers.

Part c showcases a case where the model successfully downgraded a cancer-negative case with scarring from a consensus Lung-RADS grade of 4B to LUMAS 1/2. In part d, a cancer-positive case with a nodule was correctly upgraded from Lung-RADS grades 3 and 4A to LUMAS 4B/X, demonstrating the model's capability to accurately assess and classify lung nodules.

Overall, the examples illustrate the model's effectiveness in detecting and grading lung cancer, as well as its ability to reconcile differences in expert consensus.
