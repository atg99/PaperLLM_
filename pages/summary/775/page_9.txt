The study utilized data from the National Lung Screening Trial (NLST), which included 42,290 CT cases from 14,851 patients, of which 638 developed biopsy-confirmed lung cancer within a year of low-dose CT (LDCT) screening. The dataset was divided into training (70%), tuning (15%), and test sets (15%), with cancer rates slightly higher than the general NLST population. Informed consent was obtained from all participants, and data were collected from multiple institutions using varying slice spacings and scanner vendors, with scans filtered to a maximum of 2.5 mm slice thickness for consistency.

The model developed aimed to predict whether a patient received a cancer diagnosis within the same study year based on the entire CT volume. It involved a two-stage approach, utilizing bounding boxes drawn around malignancies by radiologists. The model included components for lung segmentation, cancer ROI detection, full-volume cancer prediction, and cancer risk prediction, all trained using the TensorFlow platform. Specifically, lung segmentation was performed using a Mask-RCNN approach, while the cancer ROI detection was handled by a modified 3D RetinaNet, trained on annotated bounding boxes from the NLST dataset. 

The full-volume model employed a 3D Inception architecture, fine-tuned from ImageNet, to predict cancer within one year, and was trained with focal loss to address the scarcity of positive examples. The final cancer risk prediction model combined features from both the cancer ROI detection and full-volume models, allowing for input of either a single CT scan or both current and prior scans. The alignment of volumes was based on lung bounding box centers, facilitating the integration of spatial features from both scans.

Overall, the study presents a comprehensive model for lung cancer prediction using advanced machine learning techniques, demonstrating the potential for automated screening processes in clinical practice.
