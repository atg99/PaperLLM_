This paper presents a comprehensive study on developing a deep learning model for assessing malignancy risk in lung cancer screening CT scans. The approach involves three main components: a full-volume model trained on pathology-confirmed cancer data, a cancer region-of-interest (ROI) detection model, and a cancer risk prediction model that integrates outputs from the first two models along with prior patient scan data.

The study utilized a dataset from the National Lung Screening Trial (NLST), comprising 42,290 CT cases from 14,851 patients, of whom 578 were diagnosed with biopsy-confirmed cancer within a year. The dataset was divided into training, tuning, and test sets, ensuring a rigorous evaluation process. The model demonstrated a high area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) of 94.4% on the test dataset, with further analysis indicating superior sensitivity and specificity compared to average radiologist performance.

In a two-part retrospective study involving six experienced radiologists, the model was assessed against individual CT volumes and in conjunction with prior scans. Results showed that the model consistently outperformed the radiologists in terms of sensitivity and specificity for various malignancy score thresholds. This suggests that the deep learning model could enhance lung cancer screening efficacy by providing more accurate malignancy risk assessments.

Overall, the study highlights the potential of advanced deep learning techniques in improving lung cancer detection and risk stratification, emphasizing the need for further validation and integration into clinical practice.
