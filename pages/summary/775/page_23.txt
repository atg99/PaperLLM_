This reporting summary outlines the data availability and study design for a research manuscript. All manuscripts are required to include a data availability statement, detailing publicly accessible datasets, associated raw data figures, and any restrictions on data availability. The study utilized three publicly accessible datasets: LUNA, LIDC, and NLST, while another dataset from Northwestern was used under license and is not publicly available. Access to this dataset may be granted subject to ethical approvals.

In terms of study design, the sample size was determined based on the National Lung Cancer Screening Trial (NLST) dataset, which was split into training (70%), tuning (15%), and testing (15%) subsets. The total sample included 29,541 cases for training, with 401 cancer positive cases, ensuring representation from all trial sites and stages of cancer. The independent dataset included all available cases from lung cancer screening, while reader studies employed positive enrichment and a larger negative sample to ensure statistically significant results.

Data exclusions were made only when necessary for analysis, such as removing studies deemed ungradable or cases lacking sufficient follow-up for ground truth assessment. The model's high performance was replicated on an independent dataset with different scan parameters. Randomization was applied to the NLST dataset for splitting, and blinding ensured that researchers did not access the test set images until model choices were finalized, preserving the integrity of the results.
