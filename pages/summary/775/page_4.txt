The paper presents a novel methodology for comparing the sensitivity and specificity of a model against average readers in lung cancer screening. Instead of using the LUMAS framework, the authors set model sensitivity to align with the average reader's performance, allowing for a comparison of specificity. The analysis is further validated using data from the NLST screening arm, with a reported overall AUC of 94.4% based on 6,716 cases, including 86 cancer-positive instances.

The authors utilized an established algorithm to estimate Lung-RADS performance from NLST nodule annotations. Due to the incomplete nature of these annotations concerning Lung-RADS guidelines, a tailored approach was adopted for model comparison. The results indicate that the model's performance is comparable to average readers across various Lung-RADS categories, with specific AUC values reported.

The results, illustrated in Extended Data Fig. 3 and summarized in the paper, demonstrate that the model exhibits strong sensitivity and specificity relative to individual readers and the average reader. Statistical analyses, including permutation tests, support the findings and highlight the model's effectiveness in localizing cancerous lesions accurately. Overall, the study suggests that the proposed methodology enhances the evaluation of lung cancer screening models by considering both current and prior CT volumes.
