The extended data results presented in this paper focus on a reader study assessing lung cancer screening performance using current and prior CT volumes. The analysis involved 15,000 patients from the NLST dataset, with adjustments made to account for the sampling selection. Specifically, the examples from screening groups with no nodules but some or no abnormalities were upweighted to reflect their downsampling.

Key findings include the model's performance in comparison to the average radiologist across various Lung-RADS categories, evaluated through an AUC curve and summarized in tables. The performance metrics are illustrated with a blue line representing the model and crosses denoting the average radiologist's performance, including 95% confidence intervals. A detailed magnification of the data further outlines the individual performance of six radiologists.

Sensitivity and specificity comparisons between the model and radiologists were conducted on 308 patient volumes, with P values calculated via a two-sided permutation test involving 10,000 random resamplings. Additionally, a hit rate localization analysis was performed to determine the accuracy of the model in identifying cancerous lesions. Overall, these results contribute to understanding the efficacy of the model in lung cancer screening relative to radiologist performance.
