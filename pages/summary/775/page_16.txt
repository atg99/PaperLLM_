The paper presents findings from an independent dataset evaluating the performance of a detection model through two key metrics: the Area Under the Receiver Operating Characteristic (AUC) curve and the Intersection Over Union (IOU) for localization. In the analysis of 1,739 cases, the AUC curve was generated using a two-sided permutation test with 10,000 random resamplings, indicating the model's effectiveness. The IOU metric measures the accuracy of detections by comparing the volume of overlap between detected malignancies and ground truth labels. The results highlight a significant difference in IOU performance between three-dimensional and two-dimensional assessments, with 3D IOU decreasing more rapidly. For instance, a 1-mm nodule with a correctly positioned 2-mm bounding box results in an IOU of 0.125 in 3D, compared to 0.25 in 2D. This illustrates the challenges in achieving accurate localization in three-dimensional spaces.
