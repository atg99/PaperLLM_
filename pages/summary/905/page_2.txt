This paper presents three novel data augmentation techniques for text, specifically aimed at enhancing hate speech detection across three distinct databases. The key findings are:

1. The proposed techniques improve the generalization capabilities of models, resulting in an overall Macro-F1 score enhancement of approximately 5.7% across various network architectures, including fully connected neural networks, recurrent neural networks, and convolutional neural networks. Notably, the recall for the minority class (hate speech) increases by up to 30%.

2. An optimized deep learning architecture utilizing Global Vector (GloVe) embeddings, followed by stacked convolutional layers and gated recurrent unit layers, outperforms existing models by 0.5% in Macro-F1 score and 1.3% in hate speech recall.

3. The study employs three online hate speech databases, namely HON, RSN-1, and RSN-2, to validate the effectiveness of the proposed methods. The implementation of the data augmentation techniques is made available on GitHub.

The paper also discusses the challenges in defining hate speech due to its subjective nature and the differences in legal interpretations across countries. Previous studies have often treated hate speech detection as a binary classification problem, overlooking nuances such as implicit versus explicit hate speech. The authors argue that deep learning approaches, particularly those leveraging distributional word embeddings, have shown greater effectiveness compared to traditional machine learning methods, though issues of class imbalance and recall rates for hate speech remain inadequately addressed.

In conclusion, the paper emphasizes the need for tailored neural architectures and data augmentation techniques to improve hate speech detection in text, contributing to the ongoing research in this area.
