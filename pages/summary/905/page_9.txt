This paper addresses the challenge of online hate speech detection in short text formats, highlighting the scarcity of research focused on class imbalance and data augmentation techniques. The authors employed a deep learning approach, utilizing pre-trained neural embeddings such as Word2Vec and GloVe, which enhanced the model's ability to understand word context beyond task-specific databases. This strategy led to a 5.7% increase in macro-F1 score and a significant 30% improvement in hate speech class recall on a major online hate speech database.

The paper also discusses the limitations of the generated samples from two augmentation methods: ThreshAug and GenAug. The ThreshAug model successfully identified synonyms and alternative spellings, while GenAug produced samples that often lacked syntactical and grammatical accuracy. Additionally, the authors noted that the subjective nature of hate speech and offensive language leads to high annotator disagreement, with only 19% consensus on hate speech labels.

For future work, the authors suggest automating word replacement methods and exploring generative adversarial networks for data augmentation. They also recommend investigating the application of their techniques to longer texts and other Natural Language Processing tasks. The paper emphasizes the potential of using sub-word level embeddings and Bayesian neural networks to better address annotator variability.
