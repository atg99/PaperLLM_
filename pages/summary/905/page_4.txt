The paper discusses three data augmentation techniques aimed at enhancing the performance of models in natural language processing, particularly in the context of hate speech detection. 

1. **ThreshAug**: This technique utilizes cosine similarity and part-of-speech (POS) tagging to generate perturbed copies of original text. It substitutes words in a sequence based on their vector representations, ensuring that replacements exceed a specified cosine similarity threshold and match the POS of the original word. This method emphasizes capturing contextual patterns rather than focusing solely on individual words, aligning the embedding model used for substitutions with the deep text classifier.

2. **PosAug**: This second approach addresses the variability in sentence lengths, which often leads to zero-padding in training batches. By shifting and warping word tokens within the padded sequences, this method creates meaningful variations of the original samples while preserving their semantics and class.

3. **GenAug**: Leveraging recurrent neural networks (RNNs) for natural language generation, GenAug trains a text model on class-conditional subsets to generate new samples. It predicts the next word in a sequence based on previous words, utilizing an LSTM architecture to produce a feature matrix from the input embeddings. This method is noted for its efficiency compared to substitution-based techniques once the model is trained.

The experiments conducted in the study focus on tweet classification, employing a 50-25-25 train-validation-test split and reporting results using Macro-F1 scores to ensure balanced class emphasis. Regular down-sampling is crucial to prevent bias toward the majority class during training, ensuring a fair representation of all classes.

Overall, the paper presents these augmentation strategies as effective means to enhance model training and performance in the context of text classification tasks.
