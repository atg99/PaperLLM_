The paper discusses the challenges and methodologies associated with hate speech detection, particularly focusing on data augmentation techniques for neural networks. It highlights the limitations of existing text-based data augmentation methods, such as those that utilize external thesauruses, especially in the context of hate speech where colloquial spellings and out-of-vocabulary words are prevalent. The authors propose a substitution method that leverages neural word embeddings, like Word2Vec, and part-of-speech tagging to maintain the semantics of the original text while replacing words based on similarity criteria.

The study employs three Twitter databases (HON, RSN-1, and RSN-2) that categorize tweets into multiple classes, including hate speech and offensive language, with a significant class imbalance. The HON database contains approximately 25,000 labeled tweets, while RSN-1 and RSN-2 focus on differentiating between racist and sexist comments. The paper outlines the process of annotating these databases and emphasizes the need for class-conditional data augmentation techniques that generate new samples without requiring manual labeling.

The proposed methods aim to change the input to neural networks while preserving the meaning of original sentences. The authors argue that using pre-trained neural word embeddings can enhance the relevancy of word substitutions, which is essential for improving hate speech detection models. Overall, the paper contributes to the field by offering new approaches to data augmentation tailored for natural language processing tasks involving hate speech detection.
