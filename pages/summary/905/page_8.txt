The paper discusses the effectiveness of data augmentation techniques tailored for short text in improving the performance of hate speech detection models, specifically the GloVe+CNN+LSTM model applied to the HON database. A confusion matrix illustrates the model's predictive performance across three classes: Hate (H), Offensive (O), and Neutral (N).

Three proposed augmentation methods—PosAug, ThreshAug, and a generative approach (GenAug)—were evaluated. PosAug showed the highest performance on the HON dataset, while ThreshAug provided complementary benefits when combined with PosAug. The alignment of pre-trained neural embeddings was crucial for optimal performance, as it allowed the models to better capture hate speech patterns by augmenting minority class samples.

The authors tested these methods on two additional Twitter datasets with significant class imbalances, revealing that the framework struggled with extreme imbalance in hate speech sub-categories. A parameter sensitivity analysis found that a moderate level of augmentation yielded the best results, highlighting a trade-off between quantity and quality of samples generated.

The paper includes examples of data generated through ThreshAug, demonstrating that the grammatical structure remains intact while producing diverse hate speech expressions. However, GenAug's performance was inferior due to the nonsensical nature of some generated samples. The generative method's effectiveness is limited by the quality of the initial dataset and requires substantial sample sizes for training.

Challenges in the synonym replacement method include its computational inefficiency, which hampers extensive parameter optimization. The authors suggest that future improvements could involve training a unified generative model across all classes to address class imbalance issues more effectively. Overall, the study emphasizes the importance of data augmentation in enhancing model robustness against class imbalances in hate speech detection tasks.
