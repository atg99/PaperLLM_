This paper presents an overview of a computational process for analyzing tweets using a neural architecture that combines embedding with CNN and GRU/LSTM RNN models. The GloVe method is identified as the most effective embedding choice based on F1 scores across various architectures.

In the data augmentation experiments, the study compares different techniques, revealing that the PosAug method significantly enhances baseline models, achieving improvements of approximately 5% in Macro-F1 and 26-28% in hate recall scores. The GenAug method performed slightly worse, while the ThreshAug method provided minor improvements in Macro-F1 and hate recall for GloVe and FastText embeddings but yielded poorer results with Word2Vec.

The alignment of embeddings is crucial for maximizing augmentation effectiveness. The results indicate that using aligned embeddings for augmentation leads to superior performance, particularly with FastText and GloVe.

The research also investigates the sensitivity of the ThreshAug method to threshold values, determining that a threshold of 0.70 yields optimal F1 scores and hate recall.

To validate the applicability of the augmentation technique, experiments were conducted on two additional databases, RSN-1 and RSN-2, which exhibit higher class imbalances. The results demonstrate that the GloVe+CNN+GRU method with various augmentation techniques outperforms baseline models, affirming the method's effectiveness across different datasets.
