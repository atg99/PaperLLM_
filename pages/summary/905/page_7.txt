The paper presents a comparative study of various data augmentation methods and their effectiveness in hate speech detection using deep learning architectures. The analysis focuses on different neural embedding types and models, including Word2Vec, FastText, Google, and GloVe, combined with CNN and RNN layers.

In the performance comparison, several data augmentation techniques—No augmentation, GenAug, PosAug, and ThreshAug—were evaluated across different models. Results indicated that ThreshAug, which utilized a threshold parameter of 0.7, generally provided superior recall rates for hate speech detection, particularly when combined with GloVe embeddings.

Further analysis revealed that the choice of embedding significantly impacted performance, with GloVe embeddings yielding the best results due to their ability to integrate counting and distributional approaches. The study highlighted the importance of using pre-trained embeddings on larger corpora, as they facilitated better learning of alternate spellings used to bypass filters.

The investigation also demonstrated that data augmentation methods, particularly PosAug and ThreshAug, notably improved recall rates for hate speech detection, emphasizing their critical role in enhancing classification performance on limited datasets. The paper concluded that while embeddings play a role, effective data augmentation techniques are vital for achieving better results in hate speech detection tasks.

Finally, the authors pointed out the necessity of addressing class imbalance in datasets and suggested that future work should include confusion matrices to provide clearer insights into model performance across different classes.
