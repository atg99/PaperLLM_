In this section, the authors detail the implementation of deep learning models to enhance predictive performance on the hate speech database (HON). They conduct a comprehensive comparative study using various word embedding methods, including Word2Vec, Google News Word2Vec, GloVe, and FastText, all implemented via the Gensim library. The authors utilize different deep model architectures, specifically fully connected neural networks (FCNN), convolutional neural networks (CNN), and recurrent neural networks (RNN), to optimize sentence context learning.

The EMB+CNN+Dense model employs a CNN layer with 32 filters and a max pooling layer, which is followed by an FCNN layer with ReLU activation. The EMB+CNN+LSTM and EMB+CNN+GRU models incorporate LSTM and GRU layers, respectively, before additional FCNN layers.

Baseline results from previous studies on the HON database are summarized, noting a Micro-F1 score of 94% for a CNN+GRU approach. The authors then present their findings on the performance of various architectures, highlighting improvements achieved through data augmentation techniques, particularly PosAug and ThreshAug.

Results show that the application of PosAug significantly enhances Macro-F1 and hate class recall scores, with the best-performing model achieving a 5.7% increase in Macro-F1 compared to the DeepBase implementation. The EMB+CNN+LSTM and EMB+CNN+GRU architectures demonstrate superior performance in capturing sequential word patterns across embedding choices, indicating their effectiveness in this context. The authors conclude that their approach surpasses existing methods and showcases the benefits of data augmentation in deep learning models for sentiment analysis in hate speech detection.
