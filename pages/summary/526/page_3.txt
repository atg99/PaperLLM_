The paper discusses Spatiotemporal Deep InfoMax (ST-DIM), a framework designed to maximize mutual information across both spatial and temporal dimensions. It introduces two main mutual information objectives: local-local infomax, which focuses on maximizing mutual information between local features at consecutive time points, and global-local infomax, which aims to maximize the mutual information between the full observation at one time and small patches of the observation at the subsequent time. 

The approach utilizes convolutional layers to extract feature maps, with a specific focus on ensuring that the hidden activations have a limited receptive field corresponding to a fraction of the full observations. To address the challenges posed by loose mutual information bounds in high-dimensional data, ST-DIM employs multiple smaller mutual information objectives, which have been found to be more effective in estimating lower bounds. 

The mutual information estimation is performed using InfoNCE, a variant of noise-contrastive estimation that distinguishes between positive examples (samples from the joint distribution) and negative examples (samples from the product of marginals). The InfoNCE objective aims to maximize a score function that assigns high values to positive pairs and low values to negative pairs, thereby enhancing the learning of representations. Overall, ST-DIM seeks to improve representation learning by leveraging the dynamics of both spatial and temporal information in data.
