The paper presents a novel representation learning technique, ST-DIM, which demonstrates superior performance in capturing small objects compared to other methods, particularly generative models like VAE and pixel prediction approaches. This advantage arises because generative models tend to focus on modeling every pixel, which allows them to overlook the few pixels that constitute small objects. ST-DIM also outperforms Global-T-DIM due to its ability to penalize the loss of critical local features, unlike Global-T-DIM, which relies on a global representation.

Additionally, the paper discusses the limitations of representation learning approaches that utilize mutual information or contrastive losses. These methods can struggle to capture all salient features when easy-to-learn features dominate the learning process. An example is provided from the game Boxing, where a representation that encodes the displayed time can lead to near-perfect predictions while neglecting other important features. The analysis indicates that while several methods perform well on the clock variable, only ST-DIM successfully encodes additional relevant variables like player scores and positions.

The findings highlight a qualitative difference between contrastive and generative methods: contrastive methods favor high-entropy features, while generative methods excel in capturing low-entropy features associated with larger objects. This complementary nature suggests that hybrid models could be a promising area for future research.

The paper concludes by introducing a new benchmark based on the Atari 2600 games for evaluating state representation learning, emphasizing the importance of capturing multiple generative factors. The authors express hope that their work will stimulate further research in the field of state representation learning.
