In this paper, various methods for evaluating representations in reinforcement learning environments are compared. The methods include a randomly-initialized CNN encoder (RANDOM-CNN), a variational autoencoder (VAE), a next-step pixel prediction model (PIXEL-PRED), contrastive predictive coding (CPC), and a supervised model that learns from labeled data. All methods utilize a CNN architecture adapted for 160x210 Atari frame sizes, maintaining a representation size of 256 for fairness. A blind majority classifier (MAJ-CLF) is also included for comparison.

In the probing phase, a 256-way linear classifier is trained using representations while ensuring high entropy in state variables and preventing duplicates in the training and test sets. Each probe is trained on 35,000 frames, with validation and testing on smaller subsets. Early stopping and a learning rate scheduler based on validation loss plateaus are employed.

Results indicate varying performance across games, with the SUPERVISED model consistently achieving the highest F1 scores. The mean F1 scores across methods suggest that supervised learning significantly enhances representation quality compared to unsupervised methods. The findings highlight the effectiveness of leveraging labeled data for improving representation learning in reinforcement learning tasks.
