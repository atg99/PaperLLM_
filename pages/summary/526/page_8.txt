The paper presents an analysis of various methods for probing F1 scores across different game categories, utilizing data collected by random agents. Table 3 details the F1 scores for multiple methods, revealing that the ST-DIM method significantly outperforms others in mean F1 scores across all categories. The results indicate that contrastive methods, specifically ST-DIM and CPC, generally outperform generative methods like VAE and PIXEL-PRED in these probing tasks.

An interesting observation is that RandomCNN serves as a strong prior, likely due to the inductive biases inherent in CNN architectures. Despite the strong performance of contrastive methods, there remains a notable gap between ST-DIM and fully supervised approaches, suggesting potential for further advancements in unsupervised representation learning.

The discussion includes ablation studies comparing ST-DIM with two variants: Global-T-DIM and JSD-ST-DIM. Results indicate that the InfoNCE loss outperforms the JSD loss, and that spatiotemporal contrastive learning is critical for accurately capturing latent factors across categories. Notably, ST-DIM excels at small object localization and demonstrates robustness against easily exploitable features, attributed to its mutual information maximization of patch representations.
