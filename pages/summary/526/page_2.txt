The paper introduces a novel method called Spatiotemporal DeepInfomax (ST-DIM), designed to enhance state representation learning by maximizing mutual information across spatial and temporal dimensions. This approach is evaluated on a benchmark of 22 Atari 2600 games utilizing the Arcade Learning Environment (ALE), which allows for the extraction of key state variables such as player and object locations, as well as other game elements. 

The authors aim to systematically assess the performance of various unsupervised representation learning techniques in capturing the underlying factors of variation in these games. They employ linear probing to measure how well learned representations can predict latent generative factors, thereby establishing a metric for performance evaluation.

Key contributions of the paper include:
1. The introduction of ST-DIM, a self-supervised technique that leverages the spatial-temporal characteristics of visual observations in reinforcement learning contexts.
2. The development of a benchmark for state representation learning based on 22 Atari 2600 games.
3. Comprehensive evaluations of existing representation learning methods against the proposed ST-DIM on the new benchmark.

The ST-DIM method assumes an agent observes high-dimensional data across episodes, aiming to abstractly represent these observations by focusing on high-level semantics while ignoring low-level details. This approach is inspired by neuroscience findings that suggest the brain optimizes predictive information at an abstract level to prevent sensory overload, thus informing the design of the mutual information maximization strategy used in this representation learning framework.
