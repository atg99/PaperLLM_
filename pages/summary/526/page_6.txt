This paper discusses advancements in state representation learning, particularly in the context of robotics and reinforcement learning. It highlights the work of various researchers who have explored different approaches to improve the learning of state representations. Notably, Cuccu et al. and Eslami et al. demonstrate the decoupling of visual processing and policy learning in pixel-based environments. Other methods, such as Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) and self-supervised objectives, are also employed to create effective mappings from observations to state representations.

A key contribution of this work is the ST-DIM framework, which extends previous methods like TDC and TCN by incorporating local spatial structures and aiming to capture all temporally evolving factors in an environment, including those that are not under the agent's control. This contrasts with EMI, which focuses solely on controllable factors. ST-DIM maximizes mutual information between global and local representations across time steps, utilizing an InfoNCE objective, differing from the JSD approach of EMI.

The paper also addresses the challenge of evaluating representation learning, emphasizing the need for domain-specific benchmarks. It compares existing evaluation frameworks in vision and NLP with a proposed GLUE-like tool for reinforcement learning, which aims to assess how well agents perceive objects within their environments.

The experimental setup involves training an encoder and evaluating its performance through linear probing tasks. The data for these tasks is collected using two modes: random actions and a PPO agent trained over a significant number of timesteps, ensuring diversity in the data collection through multiple workers and introducing stochasticity in the PPO agent's actions.
