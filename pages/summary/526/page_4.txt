Agent localization pertains to the position of the player within the game environment, while small object localization involves the positions of smaller entities or items relevant to gameplay. The other localization category encompasses additional spatial information about non-player elements. The score/clock/lives/display category tracks the game’s progress and player status, and the environment state includes broader context about the game world that may affect gameplay.

The paper discusses a bilinear model for representation learning in dynamic environments, specifically in video games. This model, inspired by the work of van den Oord et al., employs an InfoNCE objective to encourage the encoder to produce semantically meaningful representations. The study introduces two loss functions: the global-local objective (LGL) and the local-local objective (LLL), which focus on learning from consecutive and non-consecutive observations, respectively.

The authors argue that evaluating the usefulness of representations should extend beyond classification performance, advocating for measures that assess utility across various tasks in dynamic environments. They highlight the potential of video games as a testing ground for these representation learning algorithms due to their spatiotemporal nature, which more accurately reflects real-world scenarios. 

To facilitate this evaluation, the authors developed a framework to annotate the RAM state of Atari games, allowing for the extraction of essential state information. This framework includes a gym wrapper that integrates seamlessly with existing code, providing state labels for generated frames. The paper categorizes state variables into six groups, enhancing the understanding of how different aspects of the game can inform representation learning models.
