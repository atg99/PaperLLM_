The paper presents a benchmark detailing the number of ground truth labels available for various games categorized into localization and score-related metrics. The categories include agent localization, small object localization, other localization, score, clock, lives, and miscellaneous variables. Each game is listed with corresponding counts for these categories, illustrating the diversity and complexity of state variables across different games.

The authors emphasize that agent localization refers to the coordinates of controllable sprites, while small object localization pertains to smaller entities like balls or missiles. Other localization includes larger objects or enemies, and score-related metrics track game performance indicators. Miscellaneous variables capture game-specific state information.

The paper also addresses the challenges in evaluating representation learning methods, highlighting the concept of disentanglement as a measure of representation utility. The focus here is on explicitness, which assesses how well generative factors can be retrieved through linear transformations of learned representations. The evaluation methodology includes training linear classifiers for each state variable and reporting the mean F1 score.

In the related work section, the authors review recent advancements in unsupervised representation learning that utilize mutual information objectives to extract latent representations. They reference various approaches that leverage mutual information to enhance representation learning, underscoring the ongoing developments in this field.
