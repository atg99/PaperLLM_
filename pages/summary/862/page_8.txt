This paper presents a new global self-attention (GSA) module designed to enhance deep learning models by incorporating both content and spatial location information of pixels. The GSA module features parallel branches for content and positional attention, which are combined at the output, allowing for efficient global attention while also addressing the importance of pixel interactions.

The experiments conducted demonstrate the effectiveness of the GSA module across various configurations. Table 4 shows that the full GSA module achieves the highest accuracy (Top-1: 78.5, Top-5: 93.9) compared to variants with reduced attention components. Notably, the content-only attention variant suffers a significant accuracy drop, highlighting the necessity for positional attention.

Further analysis in Table 5 reveals that integrating GSA modules into the later residual groups of ResNet-50 consistently improves performance, while replacements in the first residual group yield a slight decline. This indicates that the GSA mechanism is beneficial throughout the network, particularly in modeling long-range dependencies, except in the initial layers where low-level features are prioritized.

Performance improvements are also evident in experiments on the CIFAR-100 dataset, where GSA networks surpass traditional CNNs in accuracy while maintaining lower computational costs and faster runtimes. The GSA networks exhibit notable accuracy gains of up to 2.7% over their convolution-based counterparts.

In conclusion, the proposed GSA module effectively enhances deep network architectures by enabling comprehensive pixel interaction modeling, leading to improved performance on benchmark datasets like CIFAR-100 and ImageNet. The results suggest that GSA networks can serve as efficient alternatives to standard convolutional architectures, showcasing their potential in advancing deep learning methodologies.
