The paper introduces a novel global self-attention module, termed the GSA module, aimed at enhancing image recognition capabilities while addressing the computational and memory limitations associated with traditional self-attention mechanisms. Existing methods often apply self-attention only to low-resolution feature maps or confine attention to small local regions due to the quadratic complexity of attention, which is impractical for high-resolution images containing thousands of pixels.

The GSA module comprises two parallel layers: a content attention layer that focuses on pixel content and a positional attention layer that considers pixel spatial locations. The output is a summation of the outputs from both layers, enabling the GSA module to model long-range pixel interactions effectively.

The authors propose deep networks built on the GSA module, replacing conventional convolutions with global attention to better capture pixel interactions. Experimental results demonstrate that GSA networks surpass convolution-based counterparts on CIFAR-100 and ImageNet datasets while requiring fewer parameters and computations. Additionally, GSA networks outperform several existing attention-based models on ImageNet, showcasing their effectiveness in image recognition tasks.
