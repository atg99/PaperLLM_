This paper presents a novel Global Spatial Attention (GSA) module designed to enhance the attention mechanism in spatially-structured data, particularly images. The GSA module employs a combination of content attention and positional attention layers, processed in parallel. The keys, queries, and values are generated using 1x1 convolutions, facilitating the computation of attention maps that consider both pixel content and spatial relationships.

The content attention layer operates independently of pixel positions, making it equivariant to pixel shuffling and less effective for spatially-structured tasks. To address this limitation, the positional attention layer is introduced, which utilizes learned relative position embeddings to compute attention for each pixel based on its content and the spatial positions of its neighbors. This layer is structured into column-only and row-only components, enabling localized attention across pixel neighborhoods while maintaining computational efficiency.

The authors argue that removing softmax normalization from the query processing enhances the expressive power of the attention mechanism. Experimental results indicate that this modification significantly improves performance on the ImageNet validation dataset, with a 1% increase in top-1 accuracy compared to models using softmax normalization.

Overall, the proposed GSA module effectively combines content and positional attention, allowing for improved representation of spatially-structured data while minimizing computational complexity.
