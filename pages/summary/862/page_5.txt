The paper presents a novel Global Spatial Attention (GSA) network that replaces traditional spatial convolution layers with GSA modules to enhance the modeling of pixel interactions. The GSA network differentiates itself from existing attention-based networks, as outlined in Table 1, by employing a global attention mechanism that facilitates long-range pixel interactions across the entire network, unlike previous methods that either insert attention modules as auxiliary blocks or focus on local regions.

The GSA module utilizes a direct global attention operation for content attention while employing an axial attention mechanism for positional attention. This design choice is justified based on the inherent limitations of axial content attention, which can lead to undesirable interactions influenced by intermediate pixels. Experimental results indicate that the GSA module's direct global content attention outperforms axial mechanisms, supporting the effectiveness of the proposed approach.

Additionally, the paper highlights the significance of using relative positional attention over direct global positional attention to maintain translation equivariance, as the key at each pixel varies based on its relative position to the query pixel. This distinction underscores the thoughtful design of the GSA network, aiming to improve performance in tasks requiring comprehensive spatial understanding.
