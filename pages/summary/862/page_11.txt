This paper presents a comparison between structure-based CNNs, specifically ResNet architectures, and GSA networks, highlighting the latter's superior performance in terms of accuracy, parameter efficiency, computations, and runtime. The analysis, illustrated in Figure 3 and Table 6, shows that GSA networks consistently outperform ResNet models across different datasets, including ImageNet and CIFAR-100, while utilizing fewer parameters and less computational resources.

For instance, ResNet-38 achieves a top-1 accuracy of 75.9% on ImageNet with 19.6 million parameters, whereas the GSA variant improves this by 1.9%, achieving 77.8% accuracy with only 14.2 million parameters. Similar trends are observed for ResNet-50 and ResNet-101, where GSA networks lead with higher accuracies and fewer parameters despite slightly increased runtimes.

The paper also details the KQV layer, which processes input tensors to derive keys, queries, and values through point-wise convolution layers. This process is optimized by splitting these components into slices for multiple attention heads, streamlining computations via efficient matrix operations. The content attention mechanism further enhances performance by using matrix multiplication to compute attention scores, followed by a softmax function to normalize these scores across spatial dimensions.

Overall, the findings suggest that GSA networks are a promising alternative to traditional CNN architectures, offering improved performance with reduced resource demands, which could have significant implications for future neural network design and implementation.
