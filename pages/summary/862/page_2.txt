This paper introduces a novel global self-attention (GSA) module that effectively integrates both content and spatial position information of pixels, addressing limitations in existing attention mechanisms that primarily focus on content. Previous works have improved global attention efficiency but often neglect spatial arrangements, making them less suitable for image understanding tasks. The GSA module consists of two parallel layers: a content attention layer, which processes all pixels simultaneously based on their content, and a positional attention layer, which computes attention based on both pixel content and relative spatial positions. This design allows the GSA module to maintain linear computational and memory complexity concerning the number of pixels.

The GSA module can serve as the backbone of deep networks, leading to the development of global self-attention networks that replace traditional convolutions with GSA modules to model long-range pixel interactions. Unlike prior models, the GSA module employs a non-axial global content attention mechanism, allowing it to consider the entire image at once. Experimental results demonstrate that GSA-ResNet, which incorporates this module, significantly outperforms the original convolution-based ResNet and other recent attention-based networks on the ImageNet dataset.

The major contributions of the paper include the introduction of the GSA module, which efficiently attends to pixel interactions based on both content and spatial positions, and the establishment of standalone global attention-based networks that utilize GSA modules. The proposed networks outperform traditional CNNs and existing attention-based models on benchmark datasets, thus highlighting their effectiveness in image understanding tasks.
