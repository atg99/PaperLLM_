This paper presents a proposed Global Spatial Attention (GSA) module and evaluates its performance against existing attention mechanisms, specifically axial attention. The results, summarized in Table 2, indicate that the GSA module achieves a Top-1 accuracy of 78.5% and a Top-5 accuracy of 93.9%, with comparable parameters and FLOPs to axial attention systems. Notably, the GSA module demonstrates enhanced performance despite using a similar number of parameters (18.1M) and slightly fewer FLOPs (7.2G) than the axial attention variant.

Further comparisons in Table 3 reveal that GSA networks outperform several recent attention-based architectures, including modified versions of ResNet-50 and ResNet-101, while maintaining similar or fewer computational costs. The GSA networks leverage global attention across the entire network, leading to superior results compared to local attention-based models and those that incorporate attention modules as auxiliary components.

Additionally, the paper discusses the importance of individual components within the GSA module through ablation studies, highlighting the contributions of content and positional attention layers. The findings suggest that the axial mechanism does not effectively utilize content-only interactions, which supports the superiority of the GSA approach. Overall, the proposed GSA module showcases promising advancements in attention mechanisms, emphasizing its efficacy and efficiency in neural network architectures.
