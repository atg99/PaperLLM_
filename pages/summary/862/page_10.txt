The paper under review presents an analysis of Global Self-Attention (GSA) networks and their performance compared to traditional Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) on the CIFAR-100 dataset. The experiments show that GSA networks significantly outperform ResNet-based CNNs while utilizing fewer parameters, computations, and less runtime. The models were trained for 10,000 steps using ImageNet pretrained weights, with specific hyperparameters including a momentum of 0.9, weight decay of 10^-4, and a mini-batch size of 128. The learning rate was set at 5x10^-3, reducing by a factor of 10 after every 3,000 steps, with input images sized at 224x224.

Additionally, the paper includes a detailed mathematical implementation of the GSA module, utilizing Einstein notation to enhance clarity and conciseness. This notation facilitates the representation of linear algebra operations, with support from popular frameworks like TensorFlow and PyTorch.

The findings underscore the effectiveness of GSA in image recognition tasks, contributing to the ongoing exploration of self-attention mechanisms in deep learning models.
