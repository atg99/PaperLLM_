This conference paper presents a comparative analysis between structure-based CNNs, specifically ResNet architectures, and a novel Global Spatial Attention (GSA) network. The authors argue that GSA networks outperform traditional CNNs in terms of parameters, computational efficiency, and runtime, highlighting the effectiveness of the axial mechanism in achieving efficient positional attention. 

The GSA-ResNet-50 model is introduced, which integrates GSA modules by replacing the standard 3x3 convolution layers of ResNet-50. The model employs a multi-head attention mechanism and utilizes shared relative position embeddings across heads within each module. The training process is conducted on the ImageNet dataset, utilizing standard data augmentation techniques and label smoothing regularization.

Results demonstrate that GSA networks significantly surpass CNNs in performance, suggesting the potential for further optimization of hardware accelerators for attention-based models. The paper also includes a comparison with axial attention to emphasize the advantages of the GSA module's global content attention mechanism. Overall, the findings advocate for the transition towards attention-based architectures in future deep learning applications.
