The paper presents a novel watermarking scheme for deep neural networks called Free Fine-tuning. The scheme involves the integration of a proprietary model, PTYNet, into a target model to embed watermarks effectively. The process begins with selecting a PTYNet that matches the input dimensions of the target model. The outputs from both models are combined, with the final output being influenced by a hyperparameter that adjusts the impact of PTYNet. This arrangement allows for embedding multiple watermarks, enhancing the learning process of PTYNet.

To ensure robustness, the authors explore semantic-based patterns as trigger mechanisms, focusing on the background's role in object recognition. They identify three strategies for generating backgrounds that serve as triggers: fixed backgrounds, search-based backgrounds, and generation-based backgrounds. The fixed background approach is straightforward but can be easily compromised. The search-based method improves security by selecting backgrounds from a curated dataset, although it remains vulnerable to collection attacks. The generation-based method offers the most promise, as it creates backgrounds tailored to each input, reducing the risk of adversarial inference.

The authors conduct extensive experiments to evaluate the watermarking scheme's effectiveness, functionality preservation, and robustness against various attacks, including model fine-tuning and input preprocessing. Results indicate that the proposed method is superior to existing baselines in terms of efficiency and effectiveness in protecting commercial deep neural networks.

Overall, the study contributes a significant advancement in watermarking techniques for deep learning models, emphasizing the importance of robustness and adaptability in the face of potential adversarial threats.
