The paper discusses the challenges in developing practical watermarking schemes for deep neural networks (DNNs), particularly the issues arising from fine-tuning target models during watermark embedding. Fine-tuning can lead to performance degradation and is computationally intensive, making it unsuitable for real-world applications. The authors propose key requirements for effective watermarking: the ability to handle real-world datasets, preserve model functionality, facilitate deployment across various DNN architectures, and resist watermark removal attacks.

To address these challenges, the authors introduce a novel proprietary model, termed PTYNet, designed specifically for watermark embedding. This model operates independently of the target model, eliminating the need for fine-tuning. The PTYNet is trained on a custom poisoned dataset to learn sample-label pairs that are resistant to erasure. Furthermore, the authors leverage the concept of image backgrounds as semantic-based triggers in the watermarking process, enhancing the robustness of the scheme against removal attacks.

The methodology involves injecting PTYNet into the target model without compromising its fidelity. PTYNet is designed to remain inactive when processing benign inputs but activates during ownership verification, ensuring it provides the correct labels for verification samples. The architecture of PTYNet can be a simple DNN, such as ResNet18, chosen for its competitive performance and smaller size compared to most target models. The poisoned dataset used for training PTYNet includes generated backgrounds as triggers and diverse backgrounds from other sources, ensuring high confidence in ownership verification and resilience against input preprocessing. This approach aims to bridge the gap between laboratory settings and practical applications in watermarking schemes.
