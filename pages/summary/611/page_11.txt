The paper discusses a watermarking scheme for deep neural networks, specifically focusing on its application in speaker recognition using the VGGVox model on the VoxCeleb1 dataset. The proposed method, PTYNet, aims to protect intellectual property while maintaining the functionality of the original model. 

In the experiments, the performance of the original model and the model after injecting PTYNet was compared. Results indicated that the fidelity of the model remained unchanged at 84.2%, while ownership verification effectiveness improved significantly from 0% to over 92% after PTYNet injection. This suggests that PTYNet can effectively verify ownership without degrading the model's ability to predict benign samples.

An ablation study highlighted the importance of a parameter, α, which influences the effectiveness of ownership verification. When α was set to 1.0, the accuracy for ownership verification reached nearly 100%, whereas it dropped below 40% at 0.75. 

The discussion section emphasizes the competitive performance of the method on challenging datasets like ImageNet, while also acknowledging some limitations. The fixed and generation-based strategies for selecting background trigger patterns were found to be less effective than search-based strategies, as they did not meet class-consistent requirements. Furthermore, the method showed sensitivity to removal attacks through input preprocessing techniques such as rotation and scaling. The authors suggest that employing data augmentation during PTYNet training could improve robustness against these attacks, indicating a need for more effective defense mechanisms in IP protection.
