This paper discusses advancements in watermarking techniques for deep neural networks (DNNs), focusing on a new method called PTYNet. The authors compare their approach to prior data-poisoning watermarking schemes, demonstrating significant efficiency improvements. Through experiments on popular datasets CIFAR10 and CIFAR100, they show that PTYNet requires substantially less time for watermark embedding across multiple DNN models, completing the process in under 103 seconds compared to over 50,000 seconds for existing methods.

The paper also evaluates PTYNet's performance on state-of-the-art (SOTA) vision backbone models, specifically the multi-path vision transformer (MPViT). Results indicate that PTYNet maintains high fidelity and effectiveness, with minimal impact on model performance—averaging a fidelity drop of less than 0.1% and nearly 100% effectiveness. Furthermore, PTYNet is tested on commercial DNN models from platforms like Amazon Rekognition and Google Cloud Vision API, showcasing its ability to remain inactive on benign samples while effectively verifying predictions on watermark samples.

Overall, the findings highlight PTYNet's efficiency and compatibility with modern DNN architectures, marking a significant step forward in the protection of intellectual property in machine learning.
