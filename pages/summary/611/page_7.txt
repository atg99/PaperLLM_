The paper discusses a watermarking scheme for deep neural networks (DNNs) that is designed to be robust against various attacks, such as model fine-tuning, pruning, and input preprocessing. The effectiveness of the scheme is evaluated through experiments on datasets CIFAR10 and CIFAR100, as presented in Table 3. The results show that the proposed method maintains high prediction accuracy and watermark verification effectiveness, even after transfer learning.

The robustness of the scheme is further assessed against model fine-tuning, where it achieves over 94% accuracy on popular DNN models, except for Inception, which shows a lower accuracy of nearly 60%. The study highlights that the search-based strategy for selecting backgrounds outperforms fixed and generation-based strategies in defending against fine-tuning attacks due to its class-consistency and visual quality.

Additionally, the paper evaluates the method's resilience against model pruning, demonstrating an accuracy above 74% at a pruning rate of 0.4 and over 93% at a lower pruning rate. The search-based strategy again shows superior performance, achieving an average accuracy of over 98.6%.

Robustness against input preprocessing is assessed through common transformations like Gaussian blur, image scaling, and rotation. The proposed method maintains an accuracy of 74.5% with a Gaussian blur kernel size of 19 and over 86.7% accuracy for rotation angles under 20 degrees. Furthermore, the method exhibits resilience against state-of-the-art watermark removal attacks using adversarial relighting perturbations, showing an average performance decline of less than 7.2%, significantly better than existing schemes.

Overall, the paper presents a comprehensive evaluation of the watermarking scheme's robustness, demonstrating its potential for practical applications in protecting DNN ownership.
