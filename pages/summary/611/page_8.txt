The paper presents experimental results evaluating the robustness and effectiveness of a new watermarking method for deep neural networks (DNNs) against various attacks. Key findings are summarized from several tables detailing performance metrics across different models such as AlexNet, VGG16, ResNet18, SqueezeNet, DenseNet, and Inception.

In Table 4, the method's performance is assessed against state-of-the-art (SOTA) watermark removal attacks involving adversarial relighting perturbations. The original accuracy in watermark verification is very high across models, with a decline in accuracy after relighting perturbations ranging from 4.21% to 14.10%. 

Table 5 evaluates the impact of input rotation and scaling on model performance, showing a significant drop in accuracy, particularly with scaling, indicating a sensitivity that could be mitigated through data augmentation strategies.

Table 6 compares the proposed watermarking approach with a baseline pattern-based method, revealing a substantial decrease in fidelity for the baseline (over 41% decline) while the proposed method maintains nearly zero degradation. This highlights the baseline's inadequacy in preserving functionality, despite achieving competitive effectiveness.

Further, the second baseline introduces a passport layer for ownership verification, which also demonstrates a decline in fidelity, especially in white-box settings, with a decline rate of 11.3% compared to the proposed method's mere 0.18%.

Robustness evaluations indicate that the proposed method significantly outperforms both baselines against various watermark removal attacks, particularly in scenarios of model pruning and fine-tuning, although it shows some vulnerability to image scaling due to the amplification of trigger patterns.

The paper concludes that the proposed search-based strategy for background selection is robust against model alterations but sensitive to input preprocessing, suggesting future work could focus on enhancing resilience through data augmentation.
