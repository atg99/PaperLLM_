The paper presents a comprehensive evaluation of a proposed method for watermarking deep neural networks (DNNs) using two prominent datasets: CIFAR100 and ImageNet. The experimental setup includes various DNN models, such as VGG, AlexNet, ResNet, and Inception, with a focus on assessing the method's effectiveness in ownership verification while maintaining model fidelity.

In the experimental setting, the researchers used ResNet18 as the backbone for their PTYNet watermarking method. They compared their approach against two baselines: a pattern-based watermarking technique and a model modification technique involving a passport layer for watermark verification. The experiments were conducted on a robust server environment, ensuring high computational capability.

Results indicate that the proposed method effectively preserves model functionality, with CIFAR100 showing minimal degradation in classification accuracy (from 74.3% to 73.1%) after watermark injection. The method achieved over 82.3% accuracy in ownership verification across various models, demonstrating its robustness and practicality.

On the ImageNet dataset, the watermarking method maintained high fidelity with average classification accuracies around 99.5% to 100%, depending on the background selection strategy used. The search-based strategy proved to be the most effective for ownership verification, while fixed and generation-based strategies displayed lower performance due to potential quality issues.

Overall, the findings confirm that the proposed watermarking method for DNNs does not significantly compromise performance, achieving only a 1.6% average degradation while ensuring effective ownership verification. This underscores the method's potential applicability in real-world scenarios.
