The paper discusses the function approximation error in the context of reinforcement learning, specifically within the Clipped Double Q-Learning algorithm. It posits that the function approximation errors from two critics can be treated as correlated Gaussian random variables. The analysis reveals that both critics exhibit similar mean errors due to delayed policy updates and shared experience replay buffers, leading to the conclusion that the expected function approximation error can be quantified.

The correlation between the critics is significant, as it affects the underestimation of action value estimates. If the variances of the critics' errors exceed a certain threshold, underestimation occurs. The paper highlights that while delayed updates aim to mitigate error growth, the variances of value estimates remain influenced by future rewards and estimation errors, resulting in increasing discrepancies as the agent interacts with the environment.

Empirical results from experiments using OpenAI Gym tasks demonstrate a clear underestimation bias in the value estimates produced by the critics. Initially, the critics overestimate the true Q-values, but as the agent explores more, the variances grow, leading to persistent underestimation. This issue is exacerbated in tasks with larger state and action spaces.

To address the underestimation bias, the authors propose a novel modification termed the Triplet Critic Update, which aims to significantly reduce this bias by improving the target Q-value update process. The findings underscore the importance of accounting for underestimation in reinforcement learning frameworks to enhance the agent's performance in various environments.
