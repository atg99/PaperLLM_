This paper discusses TCD3, a refined version of the TD3 algorithm, and its performance in various OpenAI Gym continuous control tasks. The results highlight that TCD3 consistently achieves more accurate Q-value estimations across all environments compared to TD3. The empirical analysis reveals two main observations: TCD3 provides precise Q-value estimations with minimal error, although a slight underestimation persists, which is still less than that observed in TD3. This underestimation is attributed to increased variance in reward signals, particularly evident in the Ant environment.

The learning curves presented in the paper demonstrate that TCD3 either surpasses or matches TD3 in learning speed and final performance, as shown in Figure 2. The study indicates that estimation errors hinder agents from maximizing rewards and achieving stable returns. By employing a minimum of two approximate critics, TCD3 effectively eliminates overestimation bias and significantly reduces underestimation, leading to higher and smoother evaluation returns.

Furthermore, the paper argues that the underestimation caused by the critics can assign lower values to beneficial state-action pairs, which slows convergence and results in suboptimal action selections. The findings particularly emphasize that environments like BipedalWalker and Humanoid benefit from reduced estimation bias, enabling faster convergence to optimal returns.

In conclusion, the paper identifies the challenge of accumulated overestimation bias in deep reinforcement learning and contrasts it with the underestimation bias in deterministic actor-critic settings. It demonstrates that varying reward signals contribute to underestimation bias and introduces a novel deep Q-learning variant that significantly mitigates this issue, allowing agents to achieve higher evaluation returns in fewer time steps.
