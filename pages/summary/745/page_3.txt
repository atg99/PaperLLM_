The paper discusses reinforcement learning (RL) formalized through a Markov Decision Process (MDP), characterized by a state space, action space, transition dynamics, and a reward function. The goal is to maximize the cumulative reward, defined as a discounted sum of future rewards. The optimal policy, which maximizes expected return, can be approximated using deep neural networks in continuous control domains. 

In the actor-critic framework, the policy (actor) is updated using the deterministic policy gradient algorithm, while the action-value function (critic) evaluates the quality of state-action pairs. The paper presents the Q-learning approach where the action-value function is recursively estimated using the Bellman equation, employing a target network to stabilize updates.

A significant focus of the paper is on the underestimation bias present in deterministic actor-critic methods. While previous studies have highlighted overestimation issues in discrete action settings, this paper argues that underestimation can occur in continuous control environments with variable reinforcement signals. By analyzing the target update rule for Q-values in the TD3 algorithm, the authors demonstrate that the policy updates based on one of two critics can lead to an underestimation bias. They propose a modified target Q-value update rule aimed at mitigating this bias while maintaining function approximation accuracy, thus enhancing the learning process in these methods.
