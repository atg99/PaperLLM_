This paper addresses the issue of estimation bias in deep reinforcement learning, particularly within deterministic actor-critic methods. The authors identify that while these methods attempt to mitigate overestimation bias, they inadvertently introduce underestimation bias when the reinforcement signals are high in variance. This underestimation can lead to suboptimal policies as it assigns lower values to potentially beneficial state-action pairs.

To tackle this problem, the authors propose a novel, parameter-free variant of deep Q-learning that integrates concepts from Clipped Double Q-learning and Maxmin Q-learning. Their approach employs a unique Q-value update rule that uses a nested combination of maximum and minimum operators to constrain the value estimates, thereby minimizing the underestimation bias.

The effectiveness of their method is demonstrated through experiments on several continuous control tasks using the OpenAI Gym framework, where it consistently outperforms existing state-of-the-art techniques. The paper highlights the significance of addressing both overestimation and underestimation biases to enhance the performance of deep reinforcement learning agents in complex environments.
