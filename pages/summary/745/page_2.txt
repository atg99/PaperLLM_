The paper addresses the underestimation bias prevalent in deterministic actor-critic methods within reinforcement learning. To enhance performance, it introduces a novel algorithm called Triplet Critic Delayed Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (TCD3), which integrates principles from Clipped Double Q-learning and Maxmin Q-learning. TCD3 employs three value estimators to create approximate upper and lower bounds on value estimates, thereby mitigating underestimation bias while also controlling overestimation bias and variance.

The authors build upon the Twin Delayed Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (TD3) algorithm, noting that while TD3 effectively reduces accumulated errors, it still suffers from underestimation bias in Q-value estimates. Previous approaches to address this bias, such as Weighted Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (WD3), involved modifying the update rules for critics but did not eliminate the bias entirely. The paper highlights that large-scale variation in reinforcement signals can exacerbate estimation bias and demonstrates through empirical results that, even in the absence of overestimation and variance, underestimation can hinder performance and convergence speed.

The contributions of the paper include demonstrating the significant impact of reinforcement signal variability on estimation bias, introducing a hyperparameter-free Double Q-learning variant that effectively bounds value estimates, and showcasing through extensive experiments that TCD3 improves convergence speed and overall performance across 12 continuous control tasks in OpenAI Gym. The results indicate that the proposed method offers a substantial advancement in addressing the biases associated with Q-value estimation in reinforcement learning.
