The paper discusses the challenges of modeling real-world data distributions using traditional PAC-learning frameworks, which are distribution-free. It highlights kernel methods as a well-established approach within this framework, known for their convex optimization tasks and reliable theoretical guarantees regarding convergence rates and generalization error bounds. 

Recent research has focused on the convergence of over-parameterized neural networks within the PAC-learning context, particularly through the Neural Tangent Kernel (NTK) technique. This technique links the training of these networks to kernel learning over randomly initialized weights, suggesting that neural networks can achieve comparable generalization to kernel methods. However, empirical evidence shows that neural networks often outperform kernel methods in practical tasks, raising a fundamental question: Can neural networks learn concept classes efficiently and with better generalization than kernel methods in a distribution-free manner?

The authors assert a positive answer for neural networks with ReLU activations, demonstrating that a three-layer residual network can learn a concept class that includes smaller three-layer ResNets and smooth activations without any distributional assumptions. This learning can be efficiently executed using stochastic gradient descent (SGD), leading to small generalization errors with a polynomial number of training examples. The paper also establishes a provable separation in generalization error between neural networks and kernel methods. Specifically, for a given error threshold, neural networks can achieve efficient generalization over any distribution with a certain number of training samples, while kernel methods struggle with simpler distributions. This finding emphasizes the potential of neural networks to surpass kernel methods in terms of generalization capabilities in a distribution-free learning context.
