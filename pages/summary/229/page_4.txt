The paper discusses the complexities of learning functions using different neural network architectures, particularly focusing on three-layer ResNet models. It contrasts the sample complexity of prior works, which require a large number of samples to learn polynomial functions accurately, with the proposed approach that leverages an inductive bias through over-parameterization. The authors argue that while traditional methods may over-fit with limited training examples, the ResNet architecture avoids this issue by learning in a hierarchical manner.

The key insight is that the learning process can be structured so that one layer learns a function F, while a subsequent layer learns G(F) with the aid of the previously learned F. This simultaneous training is critical, as it prevents errors from propagating through layers, which could hinder the second layer's ability to correct mistakes made by the first.

The paper also highlights limitations of kernel methods in learning complex functions and emphasizes the need for robust training strategies that can adapt and improve accuracy over time. A follow-up work further refines this concept by introducing "backward feature correction," allowing for enhanced training outcomes as the layers learn together, ultimately leading to lower generalization errors.

In summary, the paper presents a compelling argument for the effectiveness of three-layer ResNet architectures in learning complex functions with reduced sample complexity, while also addressing the challenges and limitations of alternative learning methods.
