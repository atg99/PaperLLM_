In the paper "What Can ResNet Learn Efficiently, Going Beyond Kernels?" by Zeyuan Allen-Zhu and Yuanzhi Li, the authors investigate the superior learning capabilities of neural networks, specifically ResNet, in comparison to traditional kernel methods, particularly in the context of achieving high test accuracy on the CIFAR-10 dataset. They seek to provide theoretical justifications for why neural networks can outperform kernel methods, especially in scenarios without distributional assumptions.

The authors demonstrate that neural networks, particularly three-layer residual networks with smooth activations, can efficiently learn a significant class of functions without relying on specific input distributions. They establish that for certain simple functions, neural networks can achieve much lower test errors than kernel methods, including neural tangent kernels, even when trained with the same number of examples.

The core argument revolves around the concept of hierarchical learning, where multi-layer neural networks can leverage different layers to reduce sample complexity, contrasting with the “one-shot” learning approach of kernel methods. This hierarchical learning theory is further supported by a process called “backward feature correction” in the training of deep networks.

Additionally, the paper highlights a computational complexity advantage of ResNet over other learning methods, such as linear regression with arbitrary feature mappings. The study underscores the practical success of neural networks while addressing the theoretical gaps in understanding their optimization and generalization capabilities. Overall, the findings suggest that neural networks can learn more efficiently than kernel methods in various settings, providing insights into their effectiveness in real-world applications.
