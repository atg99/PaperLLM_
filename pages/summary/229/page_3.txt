This paper presents a significant advancement in understanding the generalization capabilities of neural networks, particularly focusing on three-layer ResNet architectures with ReLU activations. The authors establish that certain kernel methods cannot achieve a generalization error better than a specified threshold for a particular class of functions. This work is notable for providing a provable separation between the performance of neural networks and kernel methods in a distribution-free context.

The research centers on a specific learner network, a single-skip three-layer ResNet, which is defined mathematically. The target functions the authors aim to learn are structured as a combination of a simpler base signal and a more complex composite signal, represented as H(x) = F(x) + βG(F(x)). Here, F and G are functions that can be modeled as two-layer networks with smooth activations.

The goal is to efficiently learn this concept class with minimal samples while performing a regression task. The core finding, encapsulated in Theorem 1, suggests that with high probability, stochastic gradient descent can effectively learn the network output, achieving a specific population risk, given a certain number of samples dependent on the complexities of the functions involved.

A key contribution of the paper is the demonstration that the sample and time complexity for learning these functions is based solely on the complexities of the individual components, F and G, without any dependency on the composite function G(F). This contrasts with previous works, which often included such dependencies. The authors provide an example to illustrate this point, highlighting the implications for learning efficiency.

Overall, the paper lays a foundation for understanding the advantages of neural networks over traditional kernel methods and provides insights into the computational benefits of using ResNet architectures in learning complex function classes.
