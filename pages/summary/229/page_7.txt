The paper discusses learning a concept class defined by target functions that include two smooth functions, F and G, and a positive value β. The target function H(x) is expressed as the sum of F(x) and a scaled version of G(F(x)). The authors assume that both F and G are generated by two-layer neural networks with smooth activation functions. They aim to learn an unknown distribution of data points with a population risk that is close to the best possible within this concept class. 

The learning process utilizes three-layer ResNet architectures with ReLU activations. The paper simplifies notation by incorporating bias into weight matrices and re-parameterizing certain components to facilitate proof and empirical performance. The goal is to train the weights associated with these networks while keeping specific parameters fixed. 

The authors establish that their learning approach can achieve a population risk close to the optimal risk defined by the best target function, under the assumption of certain bounded complexities for the functions involved. They also provide a framework for understanding the Lipschitz continuity of the functions and the relationships between various parameters. The paper emphasizes the importance of proper scaling of inputs when using ReLU networks to ensure effective learning of non-homogeneous functions.
