This paper focuses on characterizing the mean convergence of the actor-critic method, particularly regarding various policy evaluation methods. It explores the sample complexity of the actor-critic framework, distinguishing between Classic, Gradient, and Accelerated Actor-Critic algorithms. A significant advantage of this characterization is its flexibility in using critic-only methods to estimate the state-action function, leading to faster convergence rates and fewer assumptions. 

The authors emphasize that recent studies indicate tighter sample complexity bounds for critic-only methods, challenging the adequacy of existing convergence bounds. They also note that if the critic converges as described in Proposition 1, it is possible to relax the i.i.d. assumption for the critic.

The paper then derives the rate of convergence in expectation for a generic actor-critic algorithm, highlighting that this rate is influenced by the critic update method employed. The results are specialized for two established policy evaluation methods and a new accelerated variant. 

Key findings include the Lipschitz continuity of the policy gradient, which is essential for establishing the convergence properties of the objective sequence. The authors present lemmas that support their claims and provide a framework for analyzing the actor parameter sequence, ultimately underscoring the importance of the chosen critic method in determining convergence rates.
