This paper discusses the convergence properties of actor-critic algorithms in the context of machine learning, specifically examining the convergence rates of Gradient Temporal Difference (GTD) methods and introducing an Accelerated GTD (A-GTD) method. The authors analyze convergence under both independent and Markovian sample settings, establishing that the actor-critic framework behaves similarly to gradient ascent algorithms with biased gradient directions. The bias is influenced by the step-size and the number of critic updates per actor update.

The paper highlights that actor-critic methods can converge to stationary points of the value function, paralleling stochastic gradient ascent in non-convex scenarios. Unique to this analysis is the relationship between convergence rates and the bias of the search direction, which is dictated by the chosen critic scheme. The authors derive explicit convergence rates for various critic methods, including temporal difference (TD) and GTD, noting that A-GTD outperforms both TD and GTD in terms of convergence speed.

For continuous state spaces, the paper presents a trade-off between smoothness assumptions and convergence rates, showing that TD achieves a conservative rate, GTD benefits from additional smoothness, and A-GTD realizes the fastest convergence rate under specific conditions. In finite state action spaces, actor-critic methods maintain a consistent convergence rate.

The authors position their results against existing literature, emphasizing their approach's advantages in simplifying the analysis by avoiding complex concentration probabilities and ergodicity assumptions. Overall, the study contributes to the understanding of sample complexities in actor-critic algorithms, providing a framework for future research on convergence behaviors in reinforcement learning.
