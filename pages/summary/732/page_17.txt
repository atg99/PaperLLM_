This paper discusses the application of Stochastic Compositional Gradient Descent (SCGD) for updating the critic in reinforcement learning, particularly in the context of approximating the Bellman operator using neural networks. It begins by establishing that neural networks with a single hidden layer can approximate continuous functions, which is foundational for the method presented.

The authors define the function F in terms of expectations and demonstrate how it can be expressed as a nested composition of functions f and g. The update mechanism derived from SCGD is linked to the Bellman evaluation error, leading to Gradient Temporal Difference (GTD) updates. 

To ensure the convergence of the proposed algorithm, certain assumptions are outlined: 
1. The outer function f must be continuously differentiable, while the inner function g must be continuous. The feasible set for the critic parameters should be closed and convex, and there should be at least one optimal solution.
2. The sample first-order information must be unbiased.
3. The function g should exhibit Cg-Lipschitz continuity, with bounded second moments for the samples.
4. The gradient of function f must be Lipschitz continuous.

The paper provides specific formulations for the functions involved and conditions necessary for the convergence rate to be established, referencing previous work in the field for further insights into the convergence requirements. Overall, the study presents a detailed approach to enhancing the efficiency of critic updates in reinforcement learning through advanced gradient descent techniques.
