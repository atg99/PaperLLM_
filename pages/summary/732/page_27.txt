The paper discusses the performance of various reinforcement learning algorithms, particularly focusing on the Bellman optimality operator and its implications for convergence rates and the quality of stationary points. A critical observation is the disconnect between convergence in reward and convergence in gradient norm, illustrated through experiments on the pendulum problem. 

Results show that while the advantage actor-critic with AGTD converges the fastest in terms of gradient norm, its cumulative reward does not outperform GTD by the final episode. Initially, TD(0) shows rapid convergence in both gradient norm and cumulative reward, but ultimately performs worse than GTD, highlighting a trade-off between convergence speed and the quality of the stationary point.

The paper outlines several future research directions, including establishing bounds for non-i.i.d. samples with Markovian noise, generalizing results for a broader class of critic convergence rates, exploring feature representation for TD(0) updates, and characterizing variance behavior for improved training efficiency.

Figures included demonstrate the performance of the algorithms, with Fig. 4(a) showing cumulative rewards and Fig. 4(b) presenting average gradient norms, emphasizing the advantages of using the advantage actor-critic approach in this context. Overall, the work contributes to understanding the dynamics of reinforcement learning algorithms in relation to convergence behavior.
