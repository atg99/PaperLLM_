The paper discusses the convergence rates of actor-critic algorithms in the context of machine learning, specifically focusing on the actor parameter sequence as defined by a particular algorithm. It establishes that with specific step sizes for the actor and critic parameters, a bound on the convergence rate can be determined. 

The proof utilizes existing results, notably Theorem 3 from Wang et al. (2017), which characterizes the convergence rate for the critic parameter. By applying Jensen's inequality and taking the square root, the authors derive that the convergence rate is O(k^-3) for the critic parameter. 

Furthermore, the paper suggests that with additional smoothness assumptions, faster convergence can be achieved using accelerated variants of Generalized Temporal Difference (GTD) methods. The authors introduce the Accelerated Actor-Critic method, which modifies the critic updates in the original algorithm. They outline conditions under which accelerated convergence rates are valid, emphasizing the necessity for Lipschitz gradients and bounded sample gradients.

In summary, the findings indicate that the choice of step sizes and the incorporation of acceleration techniques can significantly influence the convergence properties of actor-critic algorithms in reinforcement learning contexts.
