The paper discusses the convergence rates of actor-critic algorithms with TD(0) critic updates, particularly focusing on the conditions under which these rates can be improved. The authors derive specific results regarding the choice of parameters, highlighting that the convergence behavior is sensitive to the values of certain parameters, specifically /u1D70E and /u1D706TD. They establish that as /u1D706TD increases, the critical value of /u1D70E also increases, facilitating faster convergence by allowing larger step sizes for the critic.

The proof of the theorem relies on ensuring that certain terms dominate one another, which influences the validity of Proposition 1. The paper emphasizes that the feature space representation significantly impacts the performance of the actor-critic approach, especially under TD(0) updates. The results are quantitatively assessed, revealing that the conditions for strong convexity of the projected Bellman error are crucial for achieving optimal convergence rates, although these conditions are not always met in practice.

In the subsequent section, the authors analyze the actor-critic framework under the assumption of finite state and action spaces, which enhances the convergence rates. They introduce a key quantity related to the minimal eigenvalue of the covariance of the feature map weighted by the policy, which plays a central role in their analysis. The results indicate that the convergence bounds are improved in finite spaces, thus reducing the sample complexity required for policy parameters near stationary points of the value function.
