The paper discusses the convergence properties of policy evaluation methods in reinforcement learning, particularly focusing on the bias of critic estimators in actor-critic frameworks. It establishes that the expected error of the critic parameter is bounded by O(k−b) for certain decay rates of bias, where b is in the interval (0, 1]. This finding is formalized in Proposition 1, which suggests that many policy evaluation schemes share this intrinsic property, allowing for the substitution of traditional Monte Carlo-based estimators with online estimates derived from methods like temporal difference learning.

The authors highlight that the first term in their gradient estimate becomes negligible with an increasing number of critic estimation steps, which supports the validity of their proposed actor-critic method. Specifically, they suggest running a critic estimator for TC(k) steps, leading to updates in the actor's parameters based on trajectories of increasing length H(k). This approach helps in managing the bias of the gradient estimate.

The paper further explores the relationship between critic evaluations and the horizon length, asserting that both can be adjusted with k to reduce bias in the gradient estimates. The authors define a trajectory and characterize the bias in the policy gradient estimate using the entire trajectory length, providing mathematical formulations to illustrate their findings. Overall, the work emphasizes the importance of managing critic parameters and trajectory lengths to improve the efficiency and accuracy of policy evaluation in reinforcement learning.
