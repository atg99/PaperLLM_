The paper examines the performance of various reinforcement learning algorithms, specifically focusing on the convergence behavior of the Advantage Generalized Temporal Difference (A-GTD), Generalized Temporal Difference (GTD), and Temporal Difference (TD(0)) methods. The findings indicate that A-GTD converges the fastest regarding both gradient norm and cumulative reward but ultimately arrives at a suboptimal stationary point compared to TD and GTD. Conversely, while TD converges more quickly in gradient norm, it does so at the expense of slower cumulative reward convergence. The study identifies rewards above -66 as "solved trajectories," indicating effective navigation to the target region, while trajectories not reaching this region result in lower accumulated rewards. The results underscore the relationship between training methodologies and the quality of learned policies, suggesting that, similar to supervised learning, factors such as optimization method and sample size significantly influence reinforcement learning outcomes. The paper also includes visualizations of the learned policies for the navigation problem, demonstrating that TD and GTD yield effective navigation strategies, whereas A-GTD primarily focuses on obstacle avoidance.
