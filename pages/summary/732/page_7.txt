The paper discusses several key assumptions and properties related to policy search algorithms in the context of reinforcement learning. 

Firstly, it establishes that the absolute value of the reward \( R \) is uniformly bounded by \( UR \) for any state-action pair \( (s,a) \). This boundedness is a standard requirement in policy search algorithms, as noted in previous literature. The Q function is shown to be absolutely upper bounded by \( \frac{UR}{1 - \gamma} \), indicating that the value function \( V_{\theta}(s) \) and the objective \( J(\theta) = V_{\theta}(s_0) \) also adhere to similar bounds.

The paper further asserts that the policy \( \pi_{\theta} \) is differentiable with respect to \( \theta \), and the score function \( \nabla \log \pi_{\theta}(a|s) \) exhibits L-Lipschitz continuity with a bounded norm. The conditions outlined are satisfied by canonical policy parameterizations such as Boltzmann and Gaussian policies. For instance, the Gaussian policy is represented as \( \pi_{\theta}( \cdot | s) = N(\phi(s)^{\top} \theta, \sigma^2) \), where the score function takes a specific form that complies with the stated conditions provided the feature vectors and parameters are bounded.

The paper acknowledges that the value function is typically nonconvex with respect to the parameter \( \theta \), making the pursuit of a globally optimal solution challenging without additional structured properties. Consequently, the authors aim to design actor-critic algorithms to find stationary points of the value function \( J(\theta) \). They also explore the sample complexity of these algorithms, highlighting a notable gap in the literature despite their foundational role in modern AI architectures.

Key inequalities related to the norms of score functions and bounds on the Q and V functions are provided, reinforcing the theoretical framework for the proposed algorithms. The paper concludes with a practical observation regarding the bounded nature of the action space, necessitating the use of a truncated Gaussian policy.
