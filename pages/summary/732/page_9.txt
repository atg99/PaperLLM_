The paper discusses the evaluation of the Q function in the context of policy search, specifically using a linear parametrization approach. The Q function is represented as a linear combination of a feature map, which is referred to as the critic. This choice is motivated by the established convergence results of linear critic-only methods, contrasting with the potential divergence of nonlinear methods.

A bounded estimate of the Q function is ensured by assuming the feature representation is normalized. The paper outlines assumptions about the boundedness of the true Q function and its estimate, which is critical for deriving the properties of the gradient. It introduces a stochastic gradient estimate for policy updates, emphasizing that unbiased estimates of the Q function lead to accurate policy gradient estimates. 

However, it notes that typical critic-only methods do not yield unbiased Q function estimates, though their bias decreases with the number of estimation steps. The paper derives a decomposition of the policy search direction that accounts for this bias, providing a framework for understanding how the Q function's estimation can influence the actor updates in reinforcement learning. The results underscore the importance of accurate Q function estimation in optimizing policy performance.
