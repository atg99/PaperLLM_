The paper discusses the convergence properties of actor-critic algorithms in reinforcement learning, specifically focusing on the sample complexity associated with achieving a desired level of accuracy in value function gradients. The authors establish a theoretical framework to analyze the convergence rate of the actor-critic method, independent of the chosen critic scheme.

Key results include a characterization of the necessary number of actor updates to achieve a value function gradient below a specified threshold. The paper specifies that under certain conditions regarding the actor step-size and critic updates, the sample complexity can be expressed in terms of triangular numbers, linking the actor-critic framework to stochastic gradient methods for non-convex optimization.

The findings indicate that under specific conditions, the actor-critic method with temporal difference (TD) updates can achieve a convergence rate of O(ε^(-2)), while using generalized TD (GTD) updates may yield a rate of O(ε^(-3)) under milder assumptions. The authors emphasize that their analysis does not impose restrictions on the size of the state-action space, thus broadening the applicability of their results.

Furthermore, the paper contrasts their approach with recent works that focus on lock-in probabilities, which measure stability rather than convergence in expectation. The authors prioritize global convergence to stationarity and propose future work to explore lock-in probabilities. The results are formally derived in subsequent sections, reinforcing the theoretical foundation of actor-critic algorithms in reinforcement learning.
