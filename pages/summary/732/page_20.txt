The paper discusses the sample complexity of actor-critic algorithms in the context of reinforcement learning, particularly under various conditions and update methods. It introduces an additional smoothness assumption, which allows for a reduction in sample complexity, as demonstrated in Corollary 2. This corollary establishes an O(K^(-5/2)) sample complexity for the actor-critic approach when accelerated Gradient Temporal Difference (GTD) steps are employed for the critic's updates, marking it as the most efficient method analyzed in the study for continuous state spaces. However, this fast convergence rate comes with stringent smoothness requirements.

The paper then transitions to analyze the classic actor-critic framework, focusing on the convergence rates when the critic is updated using the TD(0) method, originally proposed by Konda and Borkar in 1999. The analysis is split into two sections based on the nature of the action space: continuous and finite. The author references the use of linear function approximation as described by Sutton in 1988, establishing a basis for the derivation of convergence rates in these settings. The key equations and assumptions are detailed to support the findings regarding the sample complexity and convergence properties of the actor-critic algorithms.
