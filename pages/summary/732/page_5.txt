This paper explores the convergence properties and sample complexities of actor-critic algorithms in reinforcement learning, particularly focusing on the effects of different policy evaluation methods such as TD(0), GTD, and A-GTD. The authors highlight the challenges associated with establishing convergence rates, especially under the constraints of rapidly mixing Markov transition densities and the potential difficulties of independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) sampling.

While prior research has improved sample complexities for actor-critic methods, it often does not consider alternative policy evaluation schemes. This work aims to provide a unified perspective by examining biased stochastic gradient iterations, allowing for a broader analysis of various critic updates and their interactions with problem characteristics such as smoothness and parameterization.

The authors report empirical findings from simulations on a navigation problem and the pendulum problem. Their results indicate that while A-GTD converges faster than both GTD and TD in terms of gradient norm, it may reach a suboptimal stationary point compared to the other methods. This suggests a complex relationship between optimization strategies and generalization in reinforcement learning.

The paper is structured into several sections: Section 2 outlines the reinforcement learning problem and key assumptions, Section 3 derives a generic actor-critic algorithm, Section 4 presents the convergence rate derivation for the generic actor-critic, and Sections 5 and 6 provide specific analyses for the different evaluation methods. Overall, the work contributes to a deeper understanding of the interplay between convergence rates and sampling strategies in actor-critic frameworks.
