The paper discusses the mathematical foundations of optimization in the context of machine learning, specifically focusing on the convergence of iterative updates to a policy parameterized by θ. By employing the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, the authors derive important relationships involving the gradient of the objective function J and the parameter updates. They leverage the Lipschitz continuity of the gradient to establish bounds on the differences between gradients at different iterations.

Key results include the formulation of the actor update and the expectation taken with respect to the filtration Fk, leading to inequalities that indicate how the updates improve the objective function. The authors define Uk as the difference between the optimal value J(θ*) and the current value J(θk), demonstrating that Uk remains non-negative throughout the iterations.

The proof concludes by reinforcing that the updates yield a "nearly" ascent direction, indicating that the algorithm makes progress towards optimizing J. The authors also present inequalities that relate the expected value of J at the next iteration to its current value, thus establishing a framework for convergence analysis.

Overall, the paper provides a rigorous mathematical treatment of policy optimization, utilizing properties of gradients and expectations to demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed iterative methods.
