The paper discusses a mathematical framework for analyzing the convergence properties of certain machine learning algorithms. It begins by manipulating an expression involving a variable \( k \) and a parameter \( N \), deriving bounds for a quantity \( U_k \) based on assumptions made earlier in the text. 

The authors establish that under certain conditions, specifically that \( J(\theta^*) \) is bounded by \( UR/(1-\gamma) \), it follows that \( U_k \) is also bounded, leading to the conclusion that \( U_k \leq C_3 \) for all \( k \). This is crucial as it allows for the simplification of the expressions derived later in the proof.

The proof further breaks down into two cases based on the value of \( b \): one where \( b \in (0, 1) \) and the other potentially involving \( b = 1 \). The analysis proceeds by substituting specific integration rules into the expressions, leading to inequalities that relate the gradient norms \( \|\nabla J(\theta_k)\|^2 \) to the bounded quantities \( U_k \).

The results culminate in a series of inequalities that provide insights into the behavior of the algorithm's convergence, particularly in relation to the step size and the properties of the function being minimized. The authors aim to demonstrate that under certain conditions, the convergence rates can be effectively controlled and analyzed, which is a significant contribution to the understanding of algorithmic performance in machine learning contexts.
