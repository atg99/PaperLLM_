The paper discusses the convergence rates of actor-critic methods in reinforcement learning, specifically under the framework established by prior work. It references the finite state action space assumption and utilizes results from Bhandari et al. (2018) to derive conditions for step-size attenuation in temporal difference learning updates. 

Corollary 4 presents the actor parameter sequence defined by a specific algorithm, detailing the conditions under which the actor and critic step-sizes decrease. The corollary states that if the number of critic updates per actor update follows a specific relationship, a certain convergence rate can be achieved.

The proof leverages the TD(0) convergence results, applying Jensen's inequality to establish the validity of the proposed convergence rate. The findings suggest that the convergence rate parameter for the critic is b=1/2, allowing for the application of a theorem to derive the stated convergence rate in Corollary 4.

In the numerical results section, the authors compare the convergence rates of actor-critic methods against critic-only methods through experiments on a two-dimensional navigation problem and the inverted pendulum. The evaluation metrics focus on maximizing long-term average rewards and include cumulative reward measurements and a proxy for the gradient norm, which assesses the difference between consecutive normalized actor parameters. This approach ensures a meaningful comparison of performance while controlling for action noise and fixed trajectory conditions.
