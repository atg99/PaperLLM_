In this section of the paper, the authors derive the actor-critic method from an optimization perspective, emphasizing its role in performing stochastic gradient ascent with biased ascent directions. The bias's magnitude is influenced by the number of critic evaluations within the algorithm's inner loop. The foundational concept is the policy gradient method, which is a direct policy search approach based on stochastic gradient ascent.

The authors reference the Policy Gradient Theorem to express the gradient of the objective function with respect to policy parameters. They discuss the concept of the occupancy measure, which represents the probability of a given state occurring under a specific policy, and introduce the ergodic distribution associated with a Markov Decision Process (MDP) for a fixed policy.

A critical aspect of the discussion is how to obtain unbiased samples of the gradient required for stochastic gradient ascent. The authors propose estimating the gradient by evaluating the score function and the Q function at the end of a rollout, which follows a geometric distribution. If the Q function evaluation is unbiased, the resulting stochastic estimate of the gradient will also be unbiased.

The authors highlight the inefficiency of this approach, as it does not utilize the state-action transitions leading up to the final tuple. Consequently, they present a more comprehensive expression for the gradient that incorporates the entire trajectory of state-action pairs, which is essential for improving sample efficiency in the learning process.
