In this paper, the authors investigate the statistical behavior of actor-critic algorithms within the context of reinforcement learning, specifically focusing on scenarios involving continuous state and action spaces defined by a Markov Decision Process. They highlight that while the asymptotic stability of these algorithms is well-established, their sample complexity remains less understood. 

The paper clarifies the functioning of actor-critic algorithms, which merge the principles of approximate dynamic programming and policy search. The authors note that in finite spaces, sample complexity can be quantified based on the gap to the optimal value function, but this approach becomes complicated in continuous spaces due to the need for policy parameterization and the non-convexity of the value function.

The actor step involves conducting stochastic gradient updates on a parameterized policy using the Policy Gradient Theorem, while the critic step estimates the action-value function using temporal difference methods. The authors point out that combining these techniques with nonlinear function approximations can lead to instability, thus motivating the use of linear function approximations for practical applications.

The paper also discusses the asymptotic stability of linear TD algorithms and the emergence of finite-time characterizations for TD methods that leverage stochastic fixed-point iterations and gradient-based approaches. Overall, the authors contribute to the understanding of sample complexity in actor-critic algorithms and provide a comprehensive analysis of their statistical properties in reinforcement learning settings.
