This paper discusses advancements in actor-critic methods within reinforcement learning, particularly focusing on Neural TD updates introduced by Cai et al. (2019), which achieve global optimality under specific conditions related to deep neural network (DNN) architecture and initialization. A key challenge highlighted is the identification of suitable initializations or the design of DNNs that meet these criteria.

The authors address the sample complexity of actor-critic methods, emphasizing the role of value function gradient norms when using non-linear function approximators, which are known to potentially diverge. To mitigate this issue, the paper restricts its analysis to linear function approximation methods, such as radial basis function (RBF) networks and fixed autoencoders, which are well-established and behave predictably.

Moreover, the paper notes ongoing efforts to refine the analysis, including relaxing assumptions on sampling distributions to allow for Markovian dependence and improving the critic objective for practical variance reduction. It also presents convergence rates for different critic methods, indicating how these methods perform under various conditions and assumptions in both continuous and finite state-action spaces.

The findings contribute to the understanding of actor-critic methods and propose a framework for ensuring stability and convergence in reinforcement learning applications.
