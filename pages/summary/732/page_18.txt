The paper discusses the theoretical foundations of actor-critic methods in reinforcement learning, particularly focusing on the convergence properties of Gradient Temporal Difference (GTD) updates for the critic. It begins by establishing certain assumptions necessary for the analysis, notably that the projected Bellman error is strongly convex with respect to the critic parameter. Assumption 3 highlights the need for state-action pairs used for updating the critic to be independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.), a standard condition in the field. The authors reference previous works to clarify the implications of these assumptions, including the importance of i.i.d. sampling for unbiasedness in gradient estimators.

The paper integrates results from Wang et al. to demonstrate that the Hessian of the function F is positive definite under the i.i.d. assumption. This leads to the establishment of convergence rates for the actor-critic framework using GTD updates. The authors summarize their findings through Algorithm 2, termed Gradient Actor-Critic, which connects GTD and Stochastic Coordinate Gradient Descent (SCGD). The results emphasize the significance of strong convexity and the structure of the Hessian for ensuring convergence in the learning process. Overall, the work contributes to the understanding of sample complexity and convergence in policy evaluation and learning in continuous spaces.
