This paper presents an actor-critic algorithm that updates the actor and critic independently, differing from traditional methods that update both in tandem. The proposed approach allows the critic to learn the state-action value function from scratch with each actor update, which enhances the algorithm's flexibility and convergence properties. The authors establish that by allowing both the horizon for the actor update and the number of critic evaluations to grow linearly with each iteration, the bias in the estimates can be reduced effectively.

The algorithm is summarized in Algorithm 1, which emphasizes that it can recover classical forms by adjusting parameters appropriately. The authors note that existing convergence proofs typically address asymptotic behavior and rely on the critic learning faster than the actor, while their method demonstrates convergence to a stationary point by utilizing the entire trajectory instead of relying on single state-action pairs.

The paper also discusses two main forms of critic updates: temporal difference (TD) and gradient temporal difference (GTD) estimators. It highlights a modification of GTD that uses an extrapolation technique to reduce bias and improve error dependency, distinguishing it from other methods like accelerated stochastic approximation. The authors provide specific update equations for both TD and GTD-based estimators, demonstrating their approach's effectiveness in reducing bias and enhancing learning stability.
