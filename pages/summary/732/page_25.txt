The paper discusses the implementation and evaluation of various reinforcement learning algorithms, focusing on their convergence rates rather than optimal navigation strategies. It presents two primary problems: a navigation task and the pendulum balancing problem, both formulated under continuous state-action spaces. 

For the navigation problem, the agent incurs a higher cost when driving off-track, emphasizing the significance of convergence rates. The algorithm parameters are specified, with a critic update strategy that utilizes trajectory data. The paper details the specific steps and constants used for actor and critic updates, highlighting the uniform random distribution of initial states and the target state.

In the pendulum problem, the goal is to balance the pendulum from any starting position. The feature representation leverages a radial basis kernel, and a linear policy is employed. Again, the focus is on convergence rates, with specific constants set for the actor and critic updates.

The analysis reveals the convergence rates for different methods: GTD, A-GTD, and TD(0), which are O(ε^(-3)), O(ε^(-5/2)), and O(ε^(-2/ε)), respectively. The results are illustrated through performance metrics for the navigation problem, indicating the effectiveness of the algorithms in learning policies.
