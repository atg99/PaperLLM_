The paper presents a theorem that establishes the bias between the true policy gradient and its finite horizon estimate, under specific assumptions. The theorem demonstrates that the finite sum of the function values can be expressed in terms of the gradient of the true objective function. Using Fubini’s Theorem, the authors show that the summation and integrals can be interchanged, allowing them to bound the function values effectively. This leads to the conclusion that the bias can be controlled by a finite constant.

The paper further discusses the limitations of the actor-critic algorithm, noting that while the true Q function is ideal, only a biased version is available in practice. The proposed algorithm is characterized as an actor-critic variant of the finite horizon gradient estimate, with the actor's parameter update explicitly defined. The bias of the stochastic gradient estimate is also quantified, revealing the relationship between the estimates and the true gradient.

The results highlight the importance of bounding the estimate's bias and provide a framework for updating the actor's parameters in reinforcement learning contexts. The findings contribute to a deeper understanding of the actor-critic methods and their behavior concerning policy gradient estimates.
