The paper discusses the Reinforcement Learning (RL) problem, focusing on an agent navigating a continuous state space S and action set A, both of which are compact subsets of Euclidean space. The agent's objective is to maximize long-term rewards, encapsulated mathematically as a Markov decision process (MDP). The MDP is characterized by a tuple (S, A, ℙ, R, γ), where ℙ represents the transition probabilities, R is the reward function, and γ is the discount factor.

At each time step, the agent selects an action according to a stochastic policy and receives a deterministic reward while transitioning to a new state based on the current state and action. The value function V represents the long-term average reward, while the action-value function Q provides a more detailed view based on specific actions taken.

The paper investigates actor-critic methods, a hybrid approach that combines policy search and approximate dynamic programming, to solve the optimization problem of finding the optimal policy that maximizes the value function. It introduces the policy gradient method as a canonical technique for policy search, which simplifies the search for optimal policies by parameterizing the policy with a vector in Euclidean space.

The authors establish assumptions regarding the regularity of the MDP and the parameterized policy, ensuring that the reward function and the policy exhibit certain properties that facilitate the optimization process. These assumptions align with previous work, specifically Zhang et al. (2020), and aim to bound the state-action feature representation. Overall, the paper lays the groundwork for further exploration of actor-critic methods and their effectiveness in solving RL problems.
