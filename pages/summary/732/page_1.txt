The paper discusses the sample complexity of actor-critic methods in reinforcement learning, which integrates both dynamic programming and policy search techniques. Actor-critic algorithms alternate between estimating the value function and updating the policy, but their updates often involve correlated noise and biased gradients. Previous research primarily examined the asymptotic behavior of these algorithms in relation to dynamical systems.

This study introduces a new variant of the actor-critic algorithm that utilizes Monte Carlo rollouts during policy updates, allowing for controllable bias based on the number of evaluations of the critic. This innovation enables the authors to derive the convergence rate of actor-critic methods when employing policy gradient techniques, independent of the policy evaluation method used. The findings indicate that the sample complexity can be comparable to stochastic gradient methods for non-convex problems, although it may be slower due to estimation errors from the critic, identified as a key complexity bottleneck.

The results are applicable to continuous state and action spaces with linear function approximations for the value function. The authors further explore specific cases where the critic is estimated using Temporal Difference, Gradient Temporal Difference, and Accelerated Gradient Temporal Difference methods. The learning rates derived from these analyses are validated through experiments on a navigation problem with obstacles and the pendulum problem, highlighting the relationship between optimization and generalization in reinforcement learning.
