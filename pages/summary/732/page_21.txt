The paper discusses the Classic Actor-Critic method, particularly focusing on the convergence analysis of TD(0) in continuous state and action spaces using linear function approximation. It builds on prior work by Dalal et al. to establish convergence rates for the critic under specific assumptions, notably that the samples exhibit uniformly bounded second moments. 

A key assumption outlined is the existence of a constant related to the filtration defined for the TD(0) critic updates. The paper establishes that the projected Bellman error is strongly convex and that the matrix A is positive definite. It introduces a corollary regarding the actor parameter sequence, specifying conditions for the actor and critic step-sizes that influence convergence rates.

The proof leverages previous convergence results, demonstrating that under certain conditions, the convergence of the actor parameter sequence is assured as the iterations increase. The analysis highlights the importance of the feature representation of the state space and the choice of step-sizes in determining the convergence rate for TD(0). 

Overall, the findings underline the significance of careful step-size selection and the boundedness of second moments in ensuring effective convergence in actor-critic algorithms within continuous spaces.
