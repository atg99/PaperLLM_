The paper discusses advancements in policy gradient methods within the context of reinforcement learning, particularly focusing on optimizing the agent's navigation through free space while avoiding obstacles. The authors introduce variations of the actor-critic approach by substituting the Q function with advantage and value functions, which simplifies the learning process by reducing the dimensionality of the function approximator to the state space.

The problem is framed as a point agent navigating from an initial state to a target destination in a two-dimensional space, defined by a free space region. The state representation utilizes a Gaussian radial basis kernel, with actions sampled from a multivariate Gaussian distribution to determine the agent's movement direction. The state transition is modeled to ensure the agent remains in the free space.

To incentivize the agent's behavior, a reward function is designed that penalizes actions leading outside the designated free space and rewards proximity to the target. This function is guided by previous work suggesting that the reward should remain above zero to encourage exploration and effective navigation. The simulation allows for the agent to maneuver through obstacles, akin to a car navigating a racetrack, emphasizing the importance of structured reward design in achieving the navigation goal.
