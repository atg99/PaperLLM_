The paper discusses the global sample complexity of actor-critic algorithms, aiming to establish convergence rates without presupposing local extrema. It highlights the challenges in achieving sharper convergence characterizations due to the requirement of local Hessian parameterizations. 

In the section on gradient rates for actor-critic methods, the authors apply Gradient Temporal Difference (GTD) techniques, linking them to compositional stochastic programming. The focus is on finding fixed points of the Bellman evaluation operator and minimizing the Bellman error, which measures the distance from these fixed points. The authors suggest a parameterization of the Q function that restricts the feasible set, making it more manageable to search for solutions, especially in high-dimensional spaces.

Under certain conditions, the paper shows that projections can be defined over function classes, allowing for effective approximations of the Bellman operator. The use of Radial-Basis-Function networks is noted for their capability to achieve these approximations. The authors aim to provide a foundation for future work that may explore local convergence rates based on the findings presented.
