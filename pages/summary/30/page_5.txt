The paper discusses a mesh prediction model that incorporates a mesh refinement branch trained to minimize a weighted sum of losses, including Chamfer loss, normal consistency loss, and edge loss. The model is benchmarked on the ShapeNet dataset, which consists of 3D shapes represented as CAD models and is widely used for evaluating 3D shape prediction methods. The dataset allows for training with pairs of RGB images and corresponding 3D meshes.

For evaluation, the authors use metrics such as Chamfer distance, normal consistency, and F1 score, sampling points from predicted and ground-truth meshes to assess performance. The implementation details include a ResNet-50 backbone and a voxel branch that predicts a voxel grid from image features. The mesh refinement consists of three stages with graph convolution layers, and the model is trained using the Adam optimizer across multiple GPUs.

The results from ShapeNet demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed method, which outperforms existing state-of-the-art approaches in terms of the evaluated metrics. The paper also compares the model with various baselines, including voxel-only approaches and other mesh prediction methods, highlighting the advantages of their mesh refinement strategy. The authors suggest that their approach is capable of predicting connected meshes of genus zero, unlike some prior methods.
