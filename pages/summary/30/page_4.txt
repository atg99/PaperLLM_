The paper discusses a method for predicting voxel occupancies aligned to an image plane, utilizing a transformed voxel grid based on the camera's intrinsic matrix. This transformation results in frustum-shaped voxels in world space, allowing for pixelwise correspondence between the image and predictions, which is complicated by the perspective of the camera.

The process involves two main branches: the voxel branch and the mesh refinement branch. The voxel branch generates a 3D grid of occupancy probabilities, which are then converted into a triangle mesh through an operation called cubify. This operation replaces occupied voxels with cuboid triangle meshes, merging shared vertices and edges to create a watertight mesh.

The mesh refinement branch enhances the initial cubified mesh by refining vertex positions through a series of stages that include vertex alignment, graph convolution, and vertex refinement. Vertex alignment extracts features from the image for each mesh vertex, while graph convolution propagates information along mesh edges. Vertex refinement updates the geometry of the mesh while maintaining its topology.

For training, the voxel branch minimizes the binary cross-entropy between predicted and true voxel occupancies. The mesh refinement branch employs pointcloud losses, approximating losses over shapes by sampling points from the mesh surface. The chamfer distance and normal distance are used to penalize mismatches in positions and normals, supplemented by an edge loss to maintain mesh quality.

Overall, the paper presents a comprehensive framework for 3D shape prediction from voxel occupancy to refined mesh representation, addressing the complexities of maintaining accuracy and quality in mesh generation.
