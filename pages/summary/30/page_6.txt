The paper presents a comparative analysis of different methods for shape prediction from single images, focusing on the performance of their proposed model against existing ones. The results are summarized in two tables: one detailing the performance on the full ShapeNet test set and another on a subset with visible holes. Key metrics such as Chamfer distance, normal consistency, and the number of vertices and faces in the predicted meshes are reported.

The authors highlight the importance of shape regularizers in improving mesh quality, noting that models trained without these regularizers may score well on standard metrics but produce degenerate meshes unsuitable for practical applications. They introduce two model versions: Best, optimized for metrics, and Pretty, which balances shape quality and quantitative performance.

The paper also discusses the limitations of prior work, specifically pointing out that methods like Pixel2Mesh+ struggle to accurately model objects with holes due to their reliance on deforming an initial sphere. In contrast, the authors' approach can handle arbitrary topologies, demonstrating a significant performance improvement over previous methods.

An ablation study is conducted to ensure fair comparisons by using consistent backbone networks and training protocols. The evaluation includes both the entire test set and a specific subset containing objects with holes, with the authors providing detailed annotations for the test models. Overall, the findings validate the design of their mesh predictor, emphasizing the need for careful consideration of shape regularization in achieving high-quality mesh outputs.
