The paper presents a novel generalization of transformer networks applicable to arbitrary graphs, addressing the limitations of the original transformer architecture designed for Natural Language Processing (NLP). The authors argue that the standard transformer, which operates on fully connected graphs, fails to leverage the inherent graph connectivity, leading to suboptimal performance when graph topology is crucial and not encoded in node features.

The proposed graph transformer introduces four key innovations: 

1. The attention mechanism is adapted to consider the neighborhood connectivity of each node.
2. Positional encoding is derived from Laplacian eigenvectors, offering a natural extension of the sinusoidal encodings used in NLP.
3. Layer normalization is replaced with batch normalization to enhance training speed and generalization.
4. The architecture accommodates edge feature representation, which is vital for applications such as chemistry and link prediction in knowledge graphs.

Numerical experiments validate the proposed architecture's performance on graph benchmarks. This work bridges the gap between traditional transformers, limited to line graphs, and graph neural networks (GNNs) that accommodate arbitrary graphs. The authors believe their architecture serves as a versatile tool for future applications integrating transformers and graph structures.
