The paper presents a novel approach to Graph Transformers, incorporating Laplacian eigenvectors as positional encoding (LapPE) to enhance the model's ability to capture structural information in graphs. The architecture is designed to process both node and edge features, enabling the explicit modification of pairwise attention scores based on edge attributes. The Graph-BERT framework is discussed, which utilizes a subgraph batching scheme to facilitate training on linkless subgraphs while retaining structural information through a combination of positional encodings.

The paper also reviews existing literature on related models, such as Graph Transformer Networks (GTN) and Heterogeneous Graph Transformer (HGT), which focus on heterogeneous graphs and incorporate various forms of positional encoding to capture the dynamics of information flow. Additionally, it mentions a generative model for temporal graphs and another approach using coordinate embeddings for homogeneous graphs, highlighting the limitations of these methods.

The authors aim to improve upon these existing models by developing a Graph Transformer that effectively utilizes edge features and positional encodings, providing a comprehensive analysis and experimental comparison in subsequent sections.
