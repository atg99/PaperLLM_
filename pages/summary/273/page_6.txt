This paper presents an innovative approach to applying transformer networks to arbitrary graphs, introducing a corresponding architecture and demonstrating its effectiveness through extensive experiments. The study evaluates the performance of the GraphTransformer (GT) model on various datasets, specifically ZINC, CLUSTER, and PATTERN, comparing its results against traditional Graph Neural Network (GNN) baselines such as GCN, GAT, and GatedGCN.

Key findings reveal that the use of Laplacian eigenvectors as node positional encodings significantly enhances model performance across all datasets. Additionally, the implementation of batch normalization instead of layer normalization around the transformer feed-forward layers contributes positively to the model's overall efficacy. The experiments show that the GT model consistently outperforms GNN baselines, indicating its potential as a strong baseline for future graph applications and improvements.

The paper also discusses different positional encoding (PE) schemes, highlighting the advantages of LapPE over Weisfeiler Lehman based PE (WL-PE), particularly in terms of generalization capabilities. The results suggest that while WL-PE can lead to overfitting in specific scenarios, LapPE captures structural and positional information more effectively.

Overall, this work lays a foundation for future research directions, including efficient training on large graphs and extending the model's applicability to heterogeneous domains. The authors express their commitment to advancing graph representation learning by leveraging recent innovations in graph inductive biases.
