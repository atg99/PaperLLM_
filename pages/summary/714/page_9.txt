The paper "Is the Policy Gradient a Gradient?" presented at AAMAS 2020 explores the theoretical foundations of policy gradient methods in reinforcement learning. It questions whether policy gradients truly satisfy the properties of a gradient, particularly in the context of function approximation and the convergence of learning algorithms. 

The authors argue that while policy gradients are often treated as gradients in optimizing policies, discrepancies arise due to approximation errors in function representation. They delve into the implications of these discrepancies for the convergence and stability of reinforcement learning algorithms. Through a rigorous analysis, the paper highlights the conditions under which policy gradients can be considered legitimate gradients and discusses the potential pitfalls when these conditions are not met.

Key findings suggest that understanding the true nature of policy gradients can lead to improved algorithm design and performance in reinforcement learning tasks. The paper concludes by emphasizing the need for further research into the theoretical underpinnings of policy gradient methods to ensure their effective application in practice.
