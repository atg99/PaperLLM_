The paper by Chris Nota and Philip S. Thomas investigates the theoretical foundations of policy gradient methods in reinforcement learning (RL). The authors highlight a significant gap in understanding regarding the relationship between these methods and the policy gradient theorem, which describes how to compute the gradient of the expected discounted return concerning an agent's policy parameters. 

The central argument of the paper is that many modern policy gradient algorithms deviate from the original theorem by omitting the discount factor from the state distribution, leading to a lack of clarity about what these methods are actually optimizing. The authors prove that the update direction used by most state-of-the-art algorithms does not correspond to the gradient of any function, which raises concerns about their convergence properties. They present a counterexample demonstrating that some algorithms may converge to a globally pessimal fixed point, irrespective of whether the discounting is considered.

The paper reviews popular policy gradient algorithms included in the stable-baselines repository, noting that nearly all of them utilize the "incorrect" policy gradient. The authors emphasize the prevalence of misunderstandings and errors in the literature, including misleading claims made by authors of highly-cited papers. The goal of this work is to clarify these misconceptions and improve the theoretical understanding of RL algorithms, ultimately enhancing the precision of future research in the field.
