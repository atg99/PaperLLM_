In this paper, Nota and Thomas analyze the differentiability of the objective function J? in the context of reinforcement learning algorithms. They derive second order partial derivatives of J? and demonstrate that, although the sigmoid function used is continuously twice differentiable, J? itself is not, leading to a contradiction. The authors then review literature from various reinforcement learning algorithms, specifically focusing on misleading claims related to the gradient ∇J?(θ).

They critique the ACER algorithm for presenting the policy gradient estimator in a way that may mislead readers into thinking it is unbiased regarding some objective, while it is actually biased relative to the choice of advantage function. The authors highlight the ambiguity in the language used, which does not convey the necessary precision for a clear understanding.

In the section on Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), they note that while the authors do not mislead readers, they sidestep addressing issues with ∇J?(θ) directly. For the TD3 algorithm, the authors point out that the definition of the state distribution pπ is ambiguous and misused, making it unclear how it relates to the definitions of J and Q.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of clarity and precision in discussing policy gradients and related concepts in reinforcement learning, as misleading claims can arise from ambiguous presentations of the algorithms.
