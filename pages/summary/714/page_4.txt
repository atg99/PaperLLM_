In this paper by Chris Nota and Philip S. Thomas, the authors explore the implications of using the policy gradient method ∇J?(θ) in reinforcement learning, particularly in relation to discounted and undiscounted objectives. They provide examples illustrating how the choice of discount factor γ can lead to suboptimal policy decisions. The first example demonstrates that while the fixed point of ∇J?(θ) may yield optimal results for discounted rewards, it can be detrimental for undiscounted returns. The second example shows that in certain scenarios, the policy derived from ∇J?(θ) can lead to consistently poor choices, emphasizing the challenges posed by myopic decision-making and the limitations of policy sharing across states.

To substantiate their arguments, the authors conducted a comprehensive review of policy gradient algorithms included in the stable-baselines library. This library is noted for its popularity and relevance in the field of deep reinforcement learning. Their analysis focused on whether these algorithms utilize ∇J?(θ) as a biased estimator, whether this bias is acknowledged in the associated literature, and if any misleading claims are present regarding the use of ∇J?(θ).

The results of their review revealed that all eight algorithms examined relied on ∇J?(θ) instead of unbiased estimators, and only one of the papers acknowledged the bias inherent in this estimator. The authors aim to clarify the ambiguities in the literature surrounding policy gradient methods, underscoring the need for greater awareness of the limitations and implications of using biased estimators in reinforcement learning research.
