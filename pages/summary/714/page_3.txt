The paper "Is the Policy Gradient a Gradient?" from AAMAS 2020 examines the characteristics of the policy gradient ∇J?(θ) in reinforcement learning, particularly its differentiation properties and implications for Markov Decision Processes (MDPs). The authors highlight a fundamental issue: the expression for ∇J?(θ) does not account for how updates to the policy parameters θ affect the state distribution, leading to an asymmetry in its second derivative ∇2J?(θ).

The main theorem presented asserts that for a set of MDPs with bounded rewards and certain conditions, a continuously differentiable objective function J? cannot exist if ∇2J?(θ) is continuous. This conclusion is supported by a counterexample illustrating that the second derivative becomes asymmetric under specific conditions (e.g., when the discount factor γ < 1).

The paper further explores the implications of these findings, questioning whether ∇J?(θ) converges to a reasonable policy. Through examples, the authors demonstrate that despite some expected behaviors under certain objectives, there are cases where following ∇J?(θ) leads to suboptimal or even irrational policy choices—suggesting that ∇J?(θ) may not be a reliable direction in some scenarios.

The authors also review the literature on policy gradient methods, arguing that the lack of theoretical clarity regarding ∇J?(θ) has contributed to various misconceptions and errors in the field. The paper ultimately calls for a reevaluation of the theoretical foundations of policy gradient methods in reinforcement learning.
