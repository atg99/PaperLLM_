The paper discusses the policy gradient theorem in the context of reinforcement learning (RL) and focuses on the implications of using a specific form of the policy gradient, denoted as ∇J?(θ). The authors, Chris Nota and Philip S. Thomas, argue that this expression is not the gradient of any objective function, which challenges the validity of many existing policy gradient algorithms.

A policy, π, defines the probability of selecting actions in given states, while a parameterized policy, πθ, is influenced by a parameter vector, θ. The paper introduces key concepts such as the value function, action-value function, and advantage function, which are essential in assessing the performance of the policy. The objectives of an RL agent are framed as maximizing either a discounted or an undiscounted reward function.

The authors explore the formulation of the policy gradient theorem as established by Sutton et al., which differentiates between the average reward objective and the discounted objective. They highlight that the discounted policy gradient, ∇Jγ(θ), provides a true gradient for optimization, while ∇J?(θ) diverges from this due to its omission of certain terms, leading to ambiguity regarding its status as a gradient.

Through a series of theoretical proofs, including the application of the Clairaut-Schwarz theorem on mixed partial derivatives, the authors demonstrate that ∇J?(θ) cannot be the gradient of any function. They provide a counterexample showing that if the second derivatives of J?(θ) are continuous but asymmetric, it indicates the non-existence of a corresponding objective function.

In conclusion, the paper critically examines the foundations of policy gradient methods and offers a rigorous argument against the use of ∇J?(θ) as a legitimate gradient, ultimately questioning the efficacy of many current RL algorithms that rely on this formulation.
