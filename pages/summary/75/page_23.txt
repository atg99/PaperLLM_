The paper by T. Lees et al. examines the formation of hydrological concepts within Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks, highlighting the potential of deep learning methods in understanding and predicting hydrological processes. The authors discuss the integration of LSTM networks with hydrological modeling, emphasizing their ability to capture complex temporal dependencies. The study references various works that contribute to the field, including the interpretation of model predictions and the transfer of hydrologic data across different regions, thus enhancing predictive capabilities. Key concepts such as soil moisture drought, the impact of anthropogenic factors on hydrology, and the importance of interpretability in AI models are addressed. The paper ultimately advocates for a synergy between data-driven approaches and traditional hydrological knowledge to improve decision-making in water resource management.
