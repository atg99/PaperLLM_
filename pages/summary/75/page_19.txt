The paper by T. Lees et al. investigates the formation of hydrological concepts within long short-term memory (LSTM) networks, focusing on their ability to capture non-linear patterns in hydrological data. The study initially utilized a linear probe for simplicity but later shifted to a non-linear model, specifically a two-layer neural network, to better extract complex relationships from the LSTM cell states. The optimal hidden layer sizes were identified through a grid search, and the rectified linear unit (ReLU) activation function was employed to mitigate vanishing gradient issues.

Results indicated that the non-linear probe demonstrated a similar correlation to observed time series data as the linear probe, but it was more effective in modeling catchment-specific offsets, particularly noted in Catchment 15021. This improvement suggests that non-linear combinations of LSTM cell states can retain critical information about catchment dynamics that linear models may overlook. The authors propose further research into various probe architectures to comprehensively understand the capture of catchment offset information by the LSTM state vector.
