The paper by T. Lees et al. investigates the ability of long short-term memory (LSTM) networks to capture hydrological concepts, specifically focusing on soil moisture and snow processes. The study compares soil moisture data from different sources, including the ERA5-Land data and ESA CCI Soil Moisture, noting that while results are consistent, absolute correlation scores are lower. Control experiments indicate that the LSTM state vector retains specific information about each catchment and timestep, suggesting the signals extracted are not random.

The authors highlight challenges in measuring catchment-scale soil moisture accurately, as remote sensing typically captures only surface-level moisture, and in situ observations are difficult to obtain. The LSTM was employed to learn effective mappings from inputs to outputs, allowing for the interpretation of intermediate state vectors.

Regarding snow processes, the authors found that training a linear probe on catchments with significant snowfall led to effective modeling of snow dynamics. Conversely, training on all catchments resulted in zero weights, demonstrating the importance of selecting appropriate training data.

Overall, the study represents a preliminary step towards understanding LSTM dynamics in hydrology. Future research may utilize feature attribution methods to further analyze informative cell-state values and explore states unrelated to the primary processes studied. While the probe captures soil moisture dynamics well, it struggles with absolute predictions, indicating a need for refinement in modeling approaches.
