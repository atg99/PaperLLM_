The paper by T. Lees et al. investigates the formation of hydrological concepts within long short-term memory (LSTM) networks. It presents experiments comparing the performance of LSTM probes on unshuffled data versus data that has been shuffled in space or shifted in time. The results show that when data is shuffled in space, there is a significant decline in the correlation scores of catchments, indicating a loss of performance compared to the original unshuffled experiments. Similarly, shifting data in time also leads to a decrease in correlation scores. These findings highlight the importance of data organization in training LSTM networks for hydrological modeling.
