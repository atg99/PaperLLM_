In the study by T. Lees et al., the authors investigate the capabilities of long short-term memory (LSTM) networks in learning hydrological concepts through an analysis of catchment storages. The research focuses on the LSTM's ability to model soil moisture and snow water processes without direct training on these variables. Instead, the LSTM is trained solely on discharge predictions using meteorological inputs such as temperature, precipitation, and potential evapotranspiration, alongside static catchment attributes.

The findings demonstrate that the LSTM successfully encodes physically realistic mappings of catchment storages, indicating its robustness across various setups, including different random seeds and probe designs. The model's learned representations are predictive of a broad range of hydrological processes, despite the absence of direct soil moisture and snow data in the training set. This suggests that the LSTM autonomously identifies relevant processes from raw inputs to optimize discharge predictions.

Overall, the study provides compelling evidence that LSTMs can learn complex hydrological dynamics, supporting the notion that these models can effectively capture essential aspects of the hydrological system through their internal state representations.
