The paper by T. Lees et al. investigates the application of long short-term memory (LSTM) networks in hydrology, specifically focusing on the information captured by LSTM state vectors in predicting soil moisture and snow depth. The authors utilize a linear probe to assess the robustness of the relationships between the LSTM state vector and the target variables, demonstrating that the correlations are catchment- and time-specific, which reduces the likelihood of spurious correlations.

Two main advantages of the linear probe are highlighted. Firstly, its inflexibility helps prevent overfitting, ensuring that the correlations are meaningful. Secondly, the probe weights provide interpretable insights into the model's learned concepts. However, the authors acknowledge limitations, such as the probe's inability to capture non-linear relationships that may exist in different flow regimes, suggesting the potential use of more complex models at the cost of interpretability.

The results indicate that the LSTM effectively learns a physically realistic mapping from meteorological inputs to discharge outputs, aligning with established hydrological concepts like soil stores and snowpacks. This finding supports the notion that LSTMs can generalize to out-of-sample conditions due to their grounding in physically plausible mappings.

The paper concludes by emphasizing the potential of the linear probe method for extracting information from LSTM state vectors in hydrological applications. The authors also discuss control experiments designed to ensure the specificity of the detected signals to particular catchments and time periods, reinforcing the validity of their findings. Overall, the study contributes to the understanding of LSTM networks in Earth system sciences, advocating for further exploration of their capabilities.
