The paper by T. Lees et al. explores the use of long short-term memory (LSTM) networks for hydrological concept formation, focusing on the extraction of information related to soil moisture and snow processes. The authors present a linear probe method to analyze the internal states of LSTM networks. They visualize informative weights assigned to cell-state dimensions, particularly noting Cell State 48, which correlates with winter snow signals.

The study emphasizes the training of a single linear probe for all catchments, allowing for a unified understanding of hydrological relationships across different regions. While this approach effectively captures the correlation and dynamics of soil moisture levels, the authors acknowledge that a linear model may not fully capture the complexity of the underlying processes. They suggest that incorporating catchment-specific biases or using non-linear probes could enhance the modeling of soil saturation conditions.

The paper also discusses the distribution of information across multiple cell states within the LSTM, highlighting how dropout techniques during training can lead to the network learning similar processes in various parts of the architecture. Overall, the authors argue that while linear probes provide valuable insights, they may be limited in their capacity to model the intricate dynamics of hydrological systems.
