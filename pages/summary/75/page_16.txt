The paper by T. Lees et al. explores hydrological concept formation within long short-term memory (LSTM) networks, particularly focusing on the comparison of two soil moisture datasets: ESA CCI Soil Moisture and ERA5-Land Soil Moisture. The authors present a histogram (Fig. B3) that illustrates the correlation distribution between these two soil moisture products, revealing a median Pearson correlation of 0.50 across various catchments. This indicates a moderate similarity in the catchment-averaged soil moisture values.

The spatial resolution differences between the datasets are highlighted, with ERA5-Land having approximately six times the number of pixels as ESA CCI, leading to a more detailed representation of soil moisture patterns (Fig. B4). Despite these differences, both datasets exhibit similar spatial patterns in soil saturation across regions such as the Scottish Highlands and eastern England.

In addressing the challenge of predicting catchment-specific offsets, the authors critique the limitations of a simple linear model, which defaults to a single bias term per catchment. To overcome this, they propose incorporating one-hot encoded information for each catchment as additional input features. This approach allows the model to learn catchment-specific biases by augmenting the input state dimensions with binary encodings, thus enabling the linear model to capture the mean values specific to each catchment effectively.

Overall, the paper contributes to the understanding of how LSTM networks can be utilized for hydrological modeling, particularly in relating and interpreting different soil moisture datasets.
