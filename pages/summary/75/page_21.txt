The paper by T. Lees et al. explores the hydrological concept formation within long short-term memory (LSTM) networks. It illustrates the structure of LSTM cells, emphasizing the role of cell states and gates in processing input data over time. The authors also provide resources for data and code availability, including CAMELS-GB data and the neuralhydrology package, which aids in rainfall-runoff modeling. 

The research involved contributions from multiple authors, with TL conducting experiments and producing analyses, while SJD and SR supervised from hydrological and machine learning perspectives. The study highlights the collaborative effort in conceptualizing research questions and designing experiments, particularly focusing on LSTM interpretability. 

Financial support for the research came from the Natural Environment Research Council, and the authors acknowledge various teams and projects that facilitated their work. The peer-review process was handled independently, ensuring impartiality. 

Overall, the paper contributes to the understanding of machine learning applications in hydrology, particularly through the lens of LSTM networks, and underscores the importance of interpretability in model predictions.
