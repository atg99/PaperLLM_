The paper by T. Lees et al. investigates the performance of long short-term memory (LSTM) networks in capturing hydrological concepts, specifically in relation to soil moisture predictions. In Appendix B, the authors present findings from probing the ESA Climate Change Initiative (CCI) Soil Moisture product. They provide a histogram of catchment correlation scores, showing a median score of 0.64 for probe-simulated soil moisture against the smoothed ESA CCI Soil Moisture.

The analysis utilized a blended soil moisture product derived from both active and passive satellite sensors, covering the period from 1978 to 2015 at a resolution of 0.25 degrees. The authors acknowledged the noisy nature of daily estimates, opting for a 7-day moving average to smooth the data. Despite the lower spatial and temporal resolution of the ESA CCI product compared to ERA5-Land, it offers an independent assessment of soil moisture.

The results indicated that while the LSTM probes effectively captured the temporal dynamics of soil moisture signals, they struggled with catchment-specific variability, particularly in matching the peaks and troughs of the ESA CCI Soil Moisture. Ultimately, the authors concluded that the LSTM probes performed better with ERA5-Land data due to its higher spatial resolution, leading to more precise catchment-averaged soil moisture estimates.
