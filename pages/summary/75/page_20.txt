The paper discusses the use of long short-term memory (LSTM) networks in hydrological modeling, specifically focusing on how these networks can improve predictions of discharge by capturing time-varying embeddings. The authors present time series data comparing non-linear probe predictions with target variables for two catchments and four soil moisture levels. They highlight that non-linear probes exhibit reduced offset errors compared to linear probes, which struggle to account for catchment-specific biases due to their inherent limitations.

The LSTM is characterized as a recurrent neural network that processes input data at each timestep, yielding embeddings that reflect the latent system dynamics pertinent to discharge prediction. This approach parallels state-space models, with the key distinction lying in the task-specific transformations applied to the input data. The paper references prior works for a more thorough understanding of the LSTM's framework and functionality.
