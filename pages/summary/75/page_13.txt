The paper by T. Lees et al. investigates the formation of hydrological concepts within Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks. It presents two key experiments involving spatial shuffling and temporal shifting of input data to assess the information retention capabilities of LSTM networks in understanding hydrological variables.

In the spatial shuffling experiment, the state vector from one gauge (ID 39056) is used to predict the time series of another gauge (ID 36003). The results indicate a strong spatial correlation in soil moisture measurements, with original correlation scores for various soil moisture levels (swvl1 to swvl4) being high (0.84 to 0.90). However, when spatially shuffled, these scores decline significantly, demonstrating the importance of specific catchment data.

The temporal shifting experiment involves moving input data by 180 days, which disrupts the temporal relationship with target variables. This also leads to a marked decrease in correlation scores, suggesting that the information captured by the LSTM is closely tied to both the specific catchment and the time context. The drop in performance is more pronounced with temporal shifts than with spatial ones, likely due to the high spatial correlation in the data.

Overall, the findings affirm that the correlations observed in the original experiments are unlikely to be coincidental, as the LSTM's state vector effectively reflects the underlying hydrological variables.
