The paper by T. Lees et al. examines the use of long short-term memory (LSTM) networks for hydrological concept formation, focusing on the extraction of known physical processes from the learned representations of the LSTM. The authors employ a linear probe analysis to assess whether the LSTM state vector contains predictive information regarding latent hydrological variables, such as soil moisture and snow water equivalent.

The methodology involves training a linear model to extract these variables as a linear combination of cell-state values, which simplifies interpretation and visualization of the results. The researchers fit a single probe across all catchments, hypothesizing that a common set of weights can generalize across different basins. To ensure the validity of their findings, control experiments are conducted by shuffling target variables in space and shifting them in time, confirming that the information in the cell-state vector is indeed location- and time-specific.

The linear probe is parameterized using a penalized linear regression model with elastic-net regularization, combining the advantages of both lasso and ridge regression. This approach allows for effective handling of correlated features in the LSTM state vector, promoting a more informative regression model.

Overall, the study advances the understanding of how LSTM networks can be used to diagnose and extract known hydrological processes, thereby contributing to the field of hydrological modeling.
