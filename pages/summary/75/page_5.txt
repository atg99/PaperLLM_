The paper by T. Lees et al. explores the capacity of Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks to form hydrological concepts by utilizing the ERA5-Land dataset. The authors focus on how LSTMs can learn to represent hydrological states such as soil moisture and snow depth, comparing the LSTM outputs to established soil moisture products derived from reanalysis data.

Key aspects of the methodology include the setting of parameters for the LSTM to optimize training performance and the selection of ERA5-Land data, which provides a global land surface reanalysis with improved soil moisture and snow depth observations. The data is characterized by a 9 km grid spacing and hourly frequency, facilitating the calculation of catchment-averaged soil moisture.

The study highlights the importance of using reanalysis data, as it allows for a longer time series and global applicability, although it acknowledges the uncertainties associated with these estimates. The authors emphasize that the focus is on whether LSTM models can accurately reflect changes in intermediate water storage, rather than achieving the most accurate soil moisture values.

Four separate probes were fitted to capture soil moisture data from different depths, while the performance of the LSTM was also assessed against alternative products like ESA CCI Soil Moisture. The study notes potential limitations of these datasets, including the lack of direct observations for deeper soil layers and the complexities introduced by satellite-derived estimates.

Overall, the authors conclude that ERA5-Land data improve the representation of hydrological variables, particularly in complex terrains, which contributes to a better understanding of the water and energy cycles. The findings aim to demonstrate the LSTM's ability to learn and represent hydrological processes effectively.
