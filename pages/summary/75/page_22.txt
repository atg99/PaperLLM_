The paper by T. Lees et al. explores the concept formation in hydrology through Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks. It emphasizes the potential of LSTM networks in enhancing hydrological predictions, particularly in rainfall-runoff modeling. The authors benchmark various LSTM-based models against traditional lumped conceptual models in Great Britain, demonstrating LSTM's effectiveness in capturing complex hydrological behaviors. 

The study draws on extensive literature, referencing works that outline advancements in machine learning applications in hydrology, including the integration of diverse datasets and the importance of interpretability in model design. The authors advocate for a rigorous approach to machine learning in hydrology, emphasizing the need for transparency and understanding in model outputs to improve scientific discovery and practical applications in water resource management. 

Overall, the paper contributes to the ongoing discourse on utilizing advanced machine learning techniques in hydrological modeling while addressing the associated challenges of model interpretability and data integration.
