In the study by T. Lees et al., the authors investigate the application of long short-term memory (LSTM) networks in hydrological modeling by training a linear probe using augmented input data. This data combines the LSTM state vector with an encoding of the gauging station number, which allows for the generation of predicted outputs. The findings demonstrate that incorporating these augmented input variables significantly reduces biases in the probe outputs and effectively addresses catchment offsets. 

The linear probe's ability to learn a catchment-specific intercept term enables it to adjust outputs to align with the mean saturation conditions of different catchments. Performance improvements are particularly notable in terms of root mean square error (RMSE) across various soil moisture levels, while correlation metrics show only minor enhancements. This discrepancy arises because the modeled dynamics remain similar across catchments, leading to high correlation values. However, the model's capacity to accurately predict mean catchment saturation levels results in a marked reduction in absolute squared error.

Overall, the study highlights the importance of incorporating catchment-specific information into hydrological models to enhance prediction accuracy and reduce biases.
