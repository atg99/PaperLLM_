The paper by T. Lees et al. explores the use of long short-term memory (LSTM) networks for hydrological concept formation, particularly focusing on soil moisture and snow depth dynamics. The study employs linear probes to analyze the correlation between inferred soil moisture from LSTM outputs and target data from ERA5-Land across four soil water volume levels, ranging from 0-289 cm in depth. The results show high correlation scores for the upper three soil layers, with median scores of 0.85, 0.90, and 0.89, while the fourth layer has a lower median correlation of 0.77. This suggests that the LSTM effectively models the dynamics of soil water content across all layers, although it struggles with catchment-specific offsets.

The authors note that the linear probe captures soil moisture dynamics well, but it is limited in modeling catchment-specific offsets, particularly in catchments with differing saturation levels compared to the global mean. The analysis also reveals that the probe identifies soil moisture anomalies rather than absolute values, highlighting the need for alternative modeling approaches to account for these biases.

In addition, the study examines snow processes using a snow depth probe, which shows that the LSTM can accurately represent snow water storage dynamics. The probe output correlates well with ERA5-Land snow depth data, with a median Pearson correlation coefficient of 0.84, indicating a strong relationship between the simulated and observed snow depth variability.

Overall, the findings demonstrate the potential of LSTM networks in hydrological modeling, particularly in capturing the temporal dynamics of soil moisture and snow processes, while also identifying areas for improvement in modeling catchment-specific behaviors.
