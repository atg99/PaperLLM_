The paper presents a detailed analysis of the A V AE-SS model and its performance in adversarial robustness, particularly in digit and color classification tasks on the color MNIST dataset. The graphical model illustrates the target distribution of A V AE-SS and its variational approximation, emphasizing the role of fixed decoder factors.

Table 1 summarizes the adversarial test accuracy, mean squared error (MSE), and Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) scores across various models, including V AE, A V AE, and SE methods, under different adversarial attack radii (ε). The results indicate that A V AE not only enhances nominal accuracy but also maintains high adversarial robustness, even under strong attacks. Notably, A V AE operates effectively without being specifically trained for particular attack types or radii, outperforming SE in terms of computational efficiency, as it requires significantly less time per iteration.

The evaluation protocol involves training an encoder-decoder pair, followed by freezing the encoder to train a linear classifier based on the learned representations. The adversarial accuracy is measured by the percentage of examples where the classifier withstands adversarial perturbations generated via projected gradient descent.

The findings reveal that while A V AE performs well at small ε values, its advantage diminishes at larger radii. Additionally, A V AE-SS demonstrates improved robustness when fine-tuning a pretrained V AE model, although it does not match the adversarial accuracy of other methods. Experiments further illustrate the impact of varying the coupling strength parameter ρ and different architectures on training dynamics and adversarial accuracy.

Overall, the paper highlights A V AE's effectiveness in achieving a balance between accuracy and robustness in adversarial settings, making it a practical choice for large networks.
