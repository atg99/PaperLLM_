The paper discusses the challenges and solutions in learning a Variational Autoencoder (VAE), particularly focusing on the asymmetry of joint distributions and the necessity of regularization. The authors propose constraining the encoder to align more closely with the desired properties of the exact posterior, as detailed in propositions 2.1 and 2.2. They highlight that the exact decoder model remains independent of the coupling strength, suggesting that under ideal conditions, the extended model may be redundant.

In practice, the authors emphasize the importance of maintaining properties of the exact posterior while learning from data. They argue that consistency between the encoder and decoder is vital for robustness, particularly in the context of adversarial examples. The paper introduces a framework that extends the Smooth Encoder (SE) method to enhance robustness without relying on computationally expensive adversarial attacks. This is achieved through data augmentation using the learned generative model, ensuring that samples generated by the decoder are consistently encoded.

The experimental section aims to validate the effectiveness of the proposed A VAE objective by assessing its impact on encoder and decoder performance, particularly in terms of adversarial robustness and reconstruction quality, measured by Frechet Inception Distance (FID) and mean square error (MSE). The authors compare their model against standard VAE and SE models, as well as hybrid approaches, on datasets like colorMNIST and CelebA, highlighting various classification tasks.

Overall, the study suggests that training an encoder to retain the properties of an exact posterior is crucial for achieving adversarial robustness.
