The paper discusses the limitations of traditional Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) in achieving true autoencoding, highlighting the inconsistency between the encoder and decoder. It identifies that even with global minimization of the VAE objective, the model fails to ensure that samples generated by the decoder can be accurately encoded back into their latent representations. This inconsistency arises from the use of finite data and local optimization, leading to a gap between the true posterior and the variational approximation.

To address this issue, the authors propose a novel approach called Autoencoding VAE (A VAE), which introduces an additional constraint on the approximating distribution. This new specification aims to ensure that the decoder's generated samples can be consistently encoded by the encoder. The paper also presents a new lower bound for the true marginal likelihood, enabling data augmentation and self-supervised density estimation in a theoretically grounded manner.

Key contributions of the work include:
1. Demonstrating that commonly used VAE models do not autoencode, necessitating an additional constraint for accurate representation mapping.
2. Introducing the A VAE approach, which improves upon the traditional VAE framework by enforcing autoencoding properties.
3. Showing that the learned representations exhibit adversarial robustness, validated through classification tasks on colorMNIST and CelebA datasets, suggesting that high performance can be achieved without adversarial training.

The paper reinforces the importance of ensuring consistent encoding and decoding processes within VAEs to improve their performance and robustness in various applications.
