The paper discusses the challenges and properties of approximating the transition kernel in variational autoencoders (VAEs). It highlights that the exact conditional distribution ¯P(X0|X=x;✓,⇢) is generally unavailable, necessitating the use of an approximate transition kernel ¯Q(X0|X;·) derived from the variational distribution. The paper investigates how well this approximation holds for various coupling strength parameters ⇢, particularly when ⇢ approaches 1.

Key propositions are presented: Proposition 2.2 establishes that the marginal distribution ¯P(X0,X;✓,⇢) is symmetric and independent of ⇢, while Proposition 2.3 asserts that if the encoder and decoder satisfy a consistency condition, then ¯Q(X0|X;·) will match ¯P(X0,X;✓,⇢) for all values of ⇢ within the specified range. However, the authors emphasize that even if the VAE objective is minimized, it does not guarantee that ¯Q(X0|X;·) is p(X;✓)-invariant. The empirical distribution’s discrete support limits control over the encoder outside this support, suggesting that additional terms in the objective may be necessary for effective conditional generation.

The paper also illustrates the potential issues with a nominally learned VAE through examples, including a case study on colorMNIST, which reveals inconsistencies between the encoder and decoder. To address these issues, the authors propose the Autoencoding Variational Autoencoder (A VAE) model, which incorporates an auxiliary observation (denoted as ˜X) and aims for consistency between the encoder and decoder. The proposed model allows the encoder and decoder to remain consistent with both training data and auxiliary observations, ensuring that the log likelihood remains unchanged.

The paper concludes with a proposition that validates the A VAE's consistency, asserting that the transition kernel defined within this framework is invariant to the latent distribution p(Z). This framework aims to enhance the performance of VAEs, particularly in generating coherent representations and conditional outputs.
