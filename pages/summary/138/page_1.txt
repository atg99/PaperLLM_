The paper titled "The Autoencoding Variational Autoencoder" by A. Taylan Cemgil et al. presents an investigation into the behavior of Variational AutoEncoders (VAEs) regarding their ability to encode typical samples generated from their decoder. The authors find that VAEs do not consistently amortize inference for these typical samples, which raises questions about the reliability of the learned representations.

To address this issue, the authors propose a self-consistency approach that redefines the variational approximation distribution in an extended VAE model. This model incorporates a Markov chain that alternates between the encoder and decoder. The proposed method can be applied to train a VAE from scratch or as a post-processing step for an already trained VAE without needing original training data.

Experimental results on benchmark datasets such as ColorMnist and CelebA demonstrate that encoders trained with the self-consistency method yield robust representations that are less sensitive to input perturbations from adversarial attacks. The study highlights the importance of the encoder in representation learning, particularly for achieving properties like adversarial robustness, which are vital for effective machine learning applications.

Overall, the paper emphasizes that while VAEs are capable of learning generative models, significant attention must be paid to the encoder's role in developing useful and reliable representations.
