The paper presents results on adversarial accuracy for digit classification tasks using the colorMNIST dataset, comparing two architectures: MLP and ConvNet. The analysis focuses on the relationship between adversarial accuracy and various factors, including coupling strength and evaluation attack radius. 

In the first set of results, adversarial accuracy is shown as a function of coupling strength for different attack radii. The second set provides a comparative analysis of algorithms at a fixed attack radius, highlighting the performance of various models. The results indicate that models trained with SE (Stochastic Ensemble) methods exhibit limited robustness beyond the training radius, whereas the A V AE (Adversarial Variational Autoencoder) model demonstrates more graceful degradation in performance.

Table 2 summarizes the adversarial test accuracy for different classification tasks on the CelebA dataset. Notably, the A V AE model shows robust performance and outperforms SE5 and SE20, which require more computational resources. The A V AE-SS variant also displays significant adversarial accuracy across tasks, occasionally surpassing SE5. Additionally, the SE-A V AE model demonstrates improvements over both SE and A V AE in several downstream tasks.

However, the increased robustness of A V AE models comes at a cost, as indicated by higher FID and MSE scores, suggesting a decline in decoder quality and reconstruction accuracy. This tradeoff may stem from a more constrained encoder compared to the V AE approach. Overall, the findings emphasize the advantages of the A V AE framework in enhancing adversarial robustness while acknowledging the associated challenges.
