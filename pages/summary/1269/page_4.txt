The paper introduces SUNRISE, a framework designed to enhance the diversity among reinforcement learning agents through two primary strategies: initializing model parameters randomly and using different samples for each agent's training. Each agent's training incorporates binary masks drawn from a Bernoulli distribution, which are applied to the objective functions during updates. This method extends previous work on ensembles in discrete tasks to both continuous and discrete tasks using Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) and Rainbow DQN.

In addition to promoting diversity, SUNRISE employs Upper Confidence Bound (UCB) exploration to facilitate efficient exploration by leveraging the ensemble's ability to express uncertainty regarding unseen samples. The exploration strategy selects actions that maximize a combination of the empirical mean and standard deviation of the Q-functions, encouraging exploration of less-visited state-action pairs. A proposed approximation scheme addresses challenges in continuous action spaces by generating candidate action sets from ensemble policies.

The experimental section evaluates several hypotheses regarding SUNRISE’s effectiveness in improving off-policy RL algorithms like SAC and Rainbow DQN across various tasks and assesses the significance of weighted Bellman backups, the utility of UCB exploration in sparse reward scenarios, the comparative performance of SUNRISE against a single agent, and the impact of ensemble size on performance.

Experiments were conducted on continuous control tasks using simulated robots from OpenAI Gym and DeepMind Control Suite, comparing SUNRISE with state-of-the-art model-based RL methods. Overall, the framework aims to enhance learning efficiency and performance through diversity and exploration strategies.
