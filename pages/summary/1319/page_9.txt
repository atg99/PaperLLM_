The paper discusses various applications of deep learning (DL) techniques in biological data analysis, particularly in cancer diagnosis, gene expression, and protein structure prediction. Key methods highlighted include stacked denoising autoencoders (DA) for cancer classification, generative adversarial networks (GAN) for gene expression pattern identification, and deep belief networks (DBN) for splicing junction prediction at the DNA level. The authors also emphasize the integration of active learning with DBN to select relevant gene features from expression profiles. Furthermore, bimodal DBNs are utilized for predicting cellular responses in translational research.

The paper presents a comprehensive table summarizing numerous datasets and DL architectures employed across various biological tasks, such as autism disorder identification, Alzheimer's diagnosis, brain pathology segmentation, and gene expression prediction. Notable architectures mentioned include convolutional neural networks (CNN), recurrent neural networks (RNN), and deep neural networks (DNN), showcasing their versatility in handling different types of biological data. The findings underscore the potential of DL in enhancing the understanding of biological processes and improving diagnostic accuracy.
