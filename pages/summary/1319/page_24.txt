This paper investigates the performance of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) across various GPU platforms compared to CPUs, focusing on training speedups. The analysis uses DeepMark benchmark scripts to measure the time taken for one training iteration per batch, which includes both forward and backpropagation operations. Results indicate that training on GPUs significantly accelerates the process compared to CPUs, with varying performance across different GPU models and deep learning frameworks.

The study evaluates four CNN models—AlexNet, GoogLeNet, Overfeat, and VGG—across four frameworks: Caffe, TensorFlow, Theano, and Torch. Key findings reveal that the Tesla P100 GPU offers the best speedup, followed by Tesla M40 and K80. Among the frameworks, TensorFlow shows the shortest training time on CPUs, while Caffe generally provides the best GPU speedup. However, TensorFlow and Torch demonstrate faster training overall, with Torch slightly outperforming TensorFlow in most cases. Theano consistently performs the least effectively across the evaluated scenarios.

In summary, the research highlights the significant advantages of utilizing GPUs for CNN training, while also emphasizing the importance of selecting the appropriate hardware and software combinations to optimize performance.
