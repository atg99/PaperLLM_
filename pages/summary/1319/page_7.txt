The table summarizes key points of various deep convolutional neural network (CNN) architectures, highlighting their design parameters and notable features. 

1. **LeNet (1998)**: The first CNN with 2 convolutional and 3 fully connected layers, designed for computational efficiency with sparse layer connections. It has 0.06 million parameters.

2. **AlexNet (2012)**: Comprising 8 layers (5 convolutional and 3 fully connected), this architecture is deeper than LeNet and introduces aliasing artifacts due to large filter sizes, totaling 60 million parameters.

3. **VGG-16 (2014)**: Features 13 convolutional layers and 2 fully connected layers, allowing for a deeper architecture compared to AlexNet. It is computationally intensive with 138 million parameters.

4. **Inception-v1 (2014)**: Known as GoogleNet, it has 22 layers and 5 million parameters. It utilizes sparse connections to minimize redundancy and improve accuracy while reducing computational costs.

5. **Inception-v3 (2015)**: With 48 layers and 23 million parameters, this architecture enhances accuracy and reduces complexity compared to Inception-v1 by using smaller filters and addressing representational bottlenecks.

6. **ResNet-50 (2015)**: A 50-layer network with 25.5 million parameters that accelerates training, mitigates the vanishing gradient issue, and achieves high classification accuracy through residual learning.

7. **Xception (2016)**: Comprising 36 convolutional layers, this architecture features linear residual connections and demonstrates improved classification performance on specific datasets, with 22.8 million parameters.

8. **Inception-v4 (2016)**: This architecture includes a feature extractor with multiple convolutional blocks and has 43 million parameters. It excels in deep feature representation but has a slower learning speed.

9. **Inception-ResNet-v2 (2016)**: With 164 layers and 56 million parameters, it combines inception and residual connections, enhancing training speed and feature representation.

10. **ResNeXt-50 (2016)**: Similar to ResNet-50 but with increased cardinality (32 parallel towers), allowing for grouped convolutions. It has 25 million parameters and maintains a homogeneous structure.

11. **DenseNet-121 (2016)**: Features 4 dense blocks with maximum connectivity among layers, promoting data flow and minimizing redundant feature learning, totaling 8 million parameters.

Overall, these architectures illustrate the evolution of CNNs in terms of depth, efficiency, and performance in image classification tasks.
