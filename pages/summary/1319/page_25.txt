The paper discusses the ongoing efforts to decode the brain's natural pattern recognition mechanisms and apply these insights to artificial intelligence (AI) systems. Despite advancements in understanding the brain's information processing and technological improvements in computing, there remain significant challenges in deep learning (DL) methods, particularly when applied to biological data.

Key issues identified include the requirement for large datasets for effective DL application, which limits their utility in cases of smaller datasets. The complexity of causal relationships within data can hinder DL methods, regardless of dataset size. Additionally, the lack of interpretability of DL models, often referred to as "black boxes," poses a challenge due to insufficient understanding of the underlying theories.

The paper emphasizes the need for further research to address these shortcomings, suggesting that future work should focus on improving the applicability of DL methods to smaller datasets and enhancing the interpretability of models. Overall, while significant progress has been made in achieving human-level computation in specific scenarios, the paper calls for continued exploration of the limitations and potential improvements in DL approaches.
