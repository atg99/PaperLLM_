The paper discusses the development of machine learning (ML) tools designed to analyze biological big data, emphasizing the need for rational, reliable, and robust methods. ML techniques are categorized into supervised and unsupervised learning. Supervised learning involves classifying data using known labels, while unsupervised learning identifies patterns from unlabeled data. Reinforcement learning is also mentioned but is not the focus of this work.

Various supervised methods are highlighted, including Artificial Neural Networks (ANN), Bayesian Statistics, Support Vector Machines, and Decision Trees, among others. Unsupervised methods such as Autoencoders, k-Means clustering, and Hierarchical Clustering are also discussed, noting their successful application to biological data.

The paper categorizes biological data into several broad types, such as sequence data from Omics technologies, gene expression data, and medical imaging data. It emphasizes the role of deep learning (DL) in mining these diverse data types, showcasing the ecosystem of modern data analytics that integrates advanced ML methods for biological applications. The focus is on how these methodologies contribute to various applications, including disease prediction, drug design, and anomaly detection.
