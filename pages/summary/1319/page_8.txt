This paper discusses the application of various deep learning (DL) techniques to three data types: images, signals, and sequences. 

In the context of images, convolutional neural networks (CNNs) have been extensively employed for tasks such as detecting mitosis in breast histology images, segmenting brain tumors from MRI scans, and diagnosing Alzheimer's disease. Additionally, deep belief networks (DBNs) and restricted Boltzmann machines (RBMs) have been utilized for similar diagnostic purposes. Generative adversarial networks (GANs) have gained traction for image synthesis and augmentation, with applications including survival prediction in chest CT scans and generating hyperspectral images from histological data.

For signals, various DL architectures have been applied to process electroencephalography (EEG) signals, focusing on tasks like emotion detection and seizure prediction. Techniques such as stacked denoising autoencoders (DA) and DBNs have been used to classify emotional states and decode motor imagery. GANs have also been implemented for generating and augmenting EEG and ECG signals, enhancing data quality and stability.

The paper includes a trend analysis of DL publications from 2015 to 2019 across these data types, indicating a balanced growth in research efforts related to images and signals, while also showing a notable focus on sequences. Overall, the findings highlight the diverse applications of DL in medical imaging and signal processing, underscoring its significance in advancing diagnostic and predictive capabilities in healthcare.
