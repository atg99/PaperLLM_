This paper provides a comprehensive overview of deep learning (DL) architectures and their applications in mining biological data. It outlines the hierarchical learning process in DL where data representations are learned at increasing abstraction levels. The paper identifies several popular DL models, including Deep Neural Networks (DNN), Deep Boltzmann Machines (DBM), Deep Belief Networks (DBN), Deep Autoencoders (DA), Generative Adversarial Networks (GAN), Recurrent Neural Networks (RNN), and Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN). Each model's architecture, advantages, and disadvantages are summarized.

The DNN emulates the brain's visual processing but faces challenges with slow training. Restricted Boltzmann Machines (RBM) serve as nonlinear feature detectors but are computationally intensive. The DBM enhances RBMs by allowing feedback mechanisms for inference, though it struggles with parameter estimation. The DBN combines RBMs with both directed and undirected layers, offering a hybrid approach but requiring significant computational resources. The DA focuses on dimensionality reduction through unsupervised learning but also encounters issues like vanishing error and a need for pretraining.

The paper concludes with a discussion of open issues and future perspectives in the field, emphasizing the evolving landscape of DL tools and their performance in biological data analysis.
