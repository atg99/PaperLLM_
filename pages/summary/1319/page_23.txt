The paper discusses the integration of 3DL4j with neural network models initially configured and trained using Keras, which supports various backends such as TensorFlow, Theano, Caffe, and CNTK. It emphasizes the importance of hardware-based scalability in deep learning (DL) tools, noting the dominance of GPUs and CPUs in current computing devices, while anticipating a growing role for ASICs and FPGAs due to their enhanced computing capacity and energy efficiency.

The performance of DL tools is a crucial aspect, particularly during the time-consuming and computationally intensive training phase. The paper highlights the challenges scientists face in selecting the most suitable tool for their applications, given the varying target focuses of different DL frameworks. To aid in this selection, the paper references benchmarking efforts that evaluate the training times of popular DL tools across various architectures, including Fully Connected Networks (FCN), Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN), Recurrent Neural Networks (RNN), and Deep Autoencoders (DA).

Benchmarking experiments were conducted using multiple setups, primarily with Intel Xeon CPUs and different GPU configurations. The results indicate that Theano and Torch generally outperform TensorFlow in terms of both gradient and forward runtime across various configurations. For RNNs, the benchmarks show that MCT excels across all platforms, with TensorFlow slightly outperforming Torch on CPUs. The paper includes detailed hardware configurations for the experimental setups, underscoring the diversity of resources used in evaluating the performance of DL tools.
