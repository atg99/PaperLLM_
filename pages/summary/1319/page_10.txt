The paper discusses various applications of machine learning models, particularly Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN), Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks, and Generative Adversarial Networks (GAN), in the field of biological data analysis. Notable applications include predicting RNA-binding protein interaction sites, gene transcription factor activity profiles, DNA sequence accessibility, and miRNA precursor targets. Additionally, GANs have been utilized for generating realistic single-cell RNA-seq data and predicting disease genes from RNA-seq data.

The paper also highlights the importance of open-access biological data sources for reproducing scientific results. It emphasizes how data sharing initiatives have enabled researchers to verify findings through open data repositories. A comprehensive list of these repositories is provided, categorized into sequences, images, and signals data.

In the section on biological image research, several leading open-access databases are described. The Cell Centered Database offers high-resolution 3-D reconstructions, while the Cell Image Library contains a vast array of datasets across various organisms and cellular processes. The Euro Bioimaging database aims to foster collaboration in bioimaging, providing access to advanced imaging techniques and data. Other notable resources include the Cancer Imaging Archive for clinical imaging data and the Autism Brain Imaging Data Exchange for neurological disorder research. Each database serves distinct purposes, enabling advancements in bioinformatics and medical research.
