The paper discusses the significant role of deep learning (DL) in mining biological data, particularly in light of recent advancements in data acquisition technologies. With the capacity to gather vast and complex multimodal data categorized into images, signals, and sequences, the challenge of pattern recognition in biological datasets has become increasingly prominent. The authors conducted a meta-analysis to explore how various deep learning architectures have been applied across different biological domains.

The introduction highlights the evolution of life sciences driven by the need for understanding human behavior, diagnosing diseases, and discovering cures. It notes the emergence of advanced tools for data collection, leading to what is termed a 'Data Deluge.' The paper characterizes biological big data as hierarchical, heterogeneous, dynamic, and complex, posing challenges for data scientists in pattern identification and analysis.

The authors also examine open access data sources related to the three data types and provide a comparative analysis of popular open-source DL tools from qualitative, quantitative, and benchmarking perspectives. Additionally, the paper outlines open research challenges in the application of DL for biological data mining and suggests future research directions.
