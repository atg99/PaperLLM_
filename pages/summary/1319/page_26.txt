This paper discusses the challenges and advancements in applying deep learning (DL) techniques to biological data analysis. It highlights issues such as misclassification, overclassification, and the difficulties in utilizing open access data due to a lack of standardized reporting and data provenance. The authors note that distributed cloud computing's potential for DL remains largely untapped, with concerns about data privacy and the need for real-time data processing.

To address these challenges, the paper suggests improving the theoretical foundations of DL methods to enhance their ability to generalize across different datasets and assess individual models' learning efficiency. The authors advocate for novel data visualization tools and emphasize the need for standardization in data annotation and curation.

The paper also points out the underutilization of deep reinforcement learning (RL) in biological pattern recognition, proposing that such methods could help identify novel biomarkers. Furthermore, it stresses the necessity for powerful computational infrastructures and specialized benchmarking for biological data.

The conclusion underscores the complexity and multimodal nature of biological data, emphasizing the need for sophisticated machine learning tools to analyze it effectively. The authors present a comprehensive survey of existing DL techniques and open-source tools, addressing their performance and popularity while outlining future research directions and open issues in the field.
