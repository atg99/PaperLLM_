This paper explores the propagation mechanisms of Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) in networks with varying structural properties, specifically focusing on homophily, heterophily, and randomness. Traditional GCNs typically use 1-hop neighbors for information propagation, but the authors question whether different types of networks require distinct propagation mechanisms due to their unique structural characteristics.

The paper highlights that while existing methods have attempted to address the challenges posed by heterophily and other structural properties, there remains a gap in understanding the underlying propagation mechanisms. The authors conducted experiments that indicate that for networks characterized by complete homophily, complete heterophily, and randomness, the optimal neighborhoods for information propagation differ: 1-hop neighbors are best for homophily, 2-hop for heterophily, and k-nearest neighbors (kNN) for random networks.

Given the complexity of real-world networks, which may exhibit a mix of these properties, the authors propose a universal GCN model named U-GCN. This model aims to leverage the strengths of information from 1-hop, 2-hop, and kNN neighbors through an adaptive fusion process. U-GCN incorporates a multi-type convolution mechanism and a node-level attention mechanism to extract relevant embeddings from each type of neighbor. By aggregating these embeddings based on their importance, U-GCN enhances the extraction of correlated information for tasks like node classification.

The effectiveness of U-GCN is demonstrated through extensive experiments on benchmark datasets, showing its superiority over existing state-of-the-art models. The findings suggest that a more nuanced approach to information propagation in GCNs can lead to improved performance across diverse network structures.
