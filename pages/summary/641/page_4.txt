This study investigates the effectiveness of Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) in different network structures characterized by homophily and heterophily. A binary vector is generated with specific attributes, and the remaining attributes are derived from a binomial distribution. The parameters are set such that the h-dimensional attributes are more strongly associated with the m-th class, while other attributes are irrelevant.

The results demonstrate that in networks with strong homophily, high accuracy can be achieved using 1-hop neighbors, but accuracy declines with increased inter-class edges due to the limitations of the homophily assumption in GCNs. Conversely, in networks with strong heterophily, accuracy improves significantly when using 2-hop neighbors compared to 1-hop neighbors, suggesting that the homophily ratio among 2-hop neighbors increases with more inter-class edges.

The study highlights that the current GCN propagation mechanism is not universally effective across all network types, indicating the need for tailored approaches based on network structural properties. Experiments on Newman networks are conducted to explore optimal propagation mechanisms for node classification. A proposed strategy involves learning network embeddings from different types of neighbors (1-hop, 2-hop, and kNN) and aggregating them to enhance classification performance.

The findings are illustrated through attention values that reflect the importance of various neighbor types over training iterations, revealing that networks with complete homophily benefit from 1-hop neighbors for direct information propagation, while other network types may require different neighbor strategies for optimal performance.
