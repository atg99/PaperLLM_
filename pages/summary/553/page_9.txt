The paper discusses the challenges of redundancy in neural network architectures as the number of allowed nodes in each computational block increases. It highlights an empirical observation that redundancy becomes more pronounced with higher node counts. To address this, the authors introduce an algorithm for efficiently checking for duplicate architectures, leveraging the properties of Directed Acyclic Graphs. This algorithm compares connectivity matrices derived from genome bit-strings, using row and column swapping to identify duplicates.

The authors argue that traditional metrics like inference time and the number of parameters are inadequate for measuring computational complexity. Instead, they propose using the number of floating-point operations (FLOPs) as a more reliable indicator. The paper includes a comparison of various architecture building blocks, illustrating the relationship between active nodes, connections, parameters, and FLOPs.

Overall, the proposed methods aim to streamline the evaluation of neural architectures, reducing computational burdens and improving the efficiency of architecture search processes.
