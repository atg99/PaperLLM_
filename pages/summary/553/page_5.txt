The paper discusses NSGA-Net, a method for automating neural architecture search (NAS) specifically for image classification tasks. It emphasizes the exploitation phase, which utilizes a Bayesian Network (BN) to model the relationships among different phases of a neural network. This approach allows for the sampling of network architectures based on patterns observed in previously successful designs. The exploitation process is inspired by Bayesian Optimization, focusing on correlations between optimization variables across the network's phases.

The experiments assess NSGA-Net's performance using two primary objectives: classification error and computational complexity, measured through floating-point operations (FLOPs). The study concludes that FLOPs provide a more reliable estimate of network complexity compared to other metrics such as the number of parameters or active connections. The hypervolume (HV) metric is employed to evaluate the effectiveness of the multi-objective search methods, with higher HV indicating better solutions along the Pareto frontier.

The experimental setup involves training on the CIFAR-10 dataset, with specific hyper-parameters for NSGA-Net, including three phases and six nodes per phase. The architecture search process generates 1,200 different network architectures through a combination of exploration and exploitation stages. Training is conducted using stochastic gradient descent with a cosine annealing learning rate schedule, ensuring a structured approach to optimizing the network architectures identified by NSGA-Net.
