The paper discusses the implementation and validation of a neural architecture search (NAS) method called NSGA-Net, which is evaluated on the CIFAR-10 dataset. The authors describe their training procedure, extending the number of epochs to 600 with a batch size of 96, and incorporating techniques such as cutout for data pre-processing and scheduled path dropout for regularization. An auxiliary head classifier is added to enhance training, with its loss contributing to the overall loss during back-propagation.

To ensure a fair comparison with other NAS methods, the authors include NASNet-A, AmoebaNet-A, and DARTS cells in their training protocol. They present the search progression of NSGA-Net through graphical representations, indicating a gradual improvement in the network architectures over generations, as evidenced by metrics such as normalized hypervolume and offspring survival rate. The results demonstrate that NSGA-Net achieves a classification error of 3.85% on the CIFAR-10 test set, with significantly fewer parameters compared to state-of-the-art architectures, while also requiring less computational resources than other NAS methods.

The paper concludes that NSGA-Net performs competitively with both human-designed and other search-generated architectures, though it is less efficient compared to gradient-based methods like DARTS. Comparisons with existing multi-objective NAS methods reveal that NSGA-Net offers a favorable trade-off between accuracy and computational efficiency, as detailed in the provided tables and figures.
