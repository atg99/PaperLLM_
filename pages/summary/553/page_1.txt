The paper presents NSGA-Net, an evolutionary approach for neural architecture search (NAS) that aims to optimize multiple conflicting objectives. The goals of NSGA-Net are to develop a procedure that effectively balances exploration and exploitation of potential neural network architectures, finds a diverse set of trade-off architectures, and operates efficiently in a single run. The method employs a population-based search algorithm that includes three main steps: initializing a population based on prior knowledge of hand-crafted architectures, exploring the architecture space through crossover and mutation, and exploiting historical knowledge using a Bayesian Network.

Experimental results indicate that NSGA-Net can minimize both error metrics and computational complexity, measured by FLOPs, achieving competitive neural architectures with significantly reduced computational resources compared to state-of-the-art methods. Specifically, NSGA-Net performs comparably to existing NAS techniques on the CIFAR-10 dataset while demonstrating a more efficient use of resources.

Key features of NSGA-Net include its focus on multi-objective optimization, a flexible architecture search space that allows for varied network structures, non-dominated sorting for maintaining diverse solutions, efficient recombination through the use of both crossover and mutation, and Bayesian learning to leverage historical solutions.

Overall, the findings suggest that NSGA-Net has the potential to enhance the application of evolutionary computation methods in deep learning tasks, particularly in scenarios demanding high performance with resource constraints.
