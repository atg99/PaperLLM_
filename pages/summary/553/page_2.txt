The paper presents NSGA-Net, a novel approach for Neural Architecture Search (NAS) that optimizes for multiple objectives, specifically error and computational complexity, defined by the number of floating-point operations (FLOPs) during a forward pass. The study demonstrates that NSGA-Net outperforms hand-crafted methods and is competitive with leading single-objective NAS approaches. It effectively utilizes a population of networks through recombination and search history exploitation, leading to efficient exploration of the search space and reduced computational time.

The related work section highlights the evolution of NAS methods, broadly categorized into evolutionary algorithms (EA) and reinforcement learning (RL) approaches. EA methods treat network structuring as a combinatorial optimization problem, employing mutation and crossover strategies to evolve solutions. On the other hand, RL methods view network construction as a decision-making process, training agents to select network components in an optimal sequence.

Key existing methods are reviewed, including Q-learning and its extensions like MetaQNN and BlockQNN, which focus on layer connections and computational blocks. The paper contrasts NSGA-Net with these RL methods, emphasizing its multi-objective selection criteria and ability to search across various computational blocks, unlike previous methods that typically focus on repeating a single block.

In the context of EA, the paper references the NEAT algorithm and its successor, CoDeepNEAT, which aimed to extend neuroevolution to deep networks. It also mentions AmoebaNet, which is noted for its large-scale application of evolutionary algorithms and its competitive results on the CIFAR-10 dataset.

Overall, NSGA-Net integrates aspects of previous methods while introducing enhancements such as binary encoding for connections, additional residual connection bits, phase-wise crossover, and a multi-objective selection scheme, positioning it as a significant advancement in the field of NAS.
