In this paper, the authors present NSGA-Net, a genetic algorithm-based architecture search method aimed at optimizing deep neural networks (DNNs) for image classification tasks. The method focuses on balancing multiple objectives, such as predictive performance and computational complexity, which are crucial for real-world applications where hardware constraints exist. The authors argue that, rather than seeking a single optimal solution, a set of solutions representing trade-offs among various performance metrics is more beneficial for practitioners.

The encoding scheme of NSGA-Net is a key component, where DNN architectures are viewed as phenotypes and their representations as genotypes. The authors utilize an operation encoding method that captures the computational blocks and spatial resolution changes within the network architecture. This encoding allows for the implementation of genetic operations like crossover and mutation in the genotype space, while facilitating the construction of diverse architectures without repeating computational blocks.

The paper also discusses the limitations of previous approaches to neural architecture search (NAS), particularly in their reliance on evolutionary algorithms (EA) and reinforcement learning (RL). The authors highlight the need for a more principled selection scheme in evolutionary methods and the potential of meta-modeling approaches to improve architecture search efficiency.

Overall, NSGA-Net aims to provide a comprehensive framework for generating a diverse set of DNN architectures that effectively navigate the trade-off frontier between performance and complexity, thereby enhancing the design of neural networks for practical applications.
