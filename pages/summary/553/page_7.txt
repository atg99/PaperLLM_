The paper presents NSGA-Net, a neural architecture search (NAS) method that employs an evolutionary algorithm to design convolutional cell architectures within the NASNet micro search space. Figure 7 illustrates the normal and reduction convolutional cell architectures generated by NSGA-Net, highlighting the flow of inputs from previous cells and the output generated through a concatenation operation across branches.

Table 2 compares NSGA-Net's performance on the CIFAR-10 image classification task against various baseline architectures, including those designed by human experts and other NAS methods. The table categorizes architectures based on their design approach, indicating parameters, test error rates, search costs, and training methods. Notably, NSGA-Net demonstrates competitive test error rates with significantly fewer parameters than many human-designed and reinforcement learning-based models, with a notable advantage of generating multiple architectures from the trade-off frontier at no additional cost.

Additionally, the paper evaluates the transferability of architectures discovered by NSGA-Net using the CIFAR-100 dataset. Results indicate that the architecture retains competitive performance, achieving results comparable to both human-designed and reinforcement learning-generated models while using considerably fewer parameters. This suggests that the architectures found by NSGA-Net not only excel in CIFAR-10 but also generalize well to other datasets.
