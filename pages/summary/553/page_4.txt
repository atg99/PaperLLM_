This paper presents a novel approach to neural architecture search (NAS) through a genetic algorithm called NSGA-Net. The method utilizes a binary encoding scheme to represent network architectures as directed acyclic graphs, allowing for a compact yet flexible representation of various architectures like VGG, ResNet, and DenseNet. The encoding captures different operations within computational blocks, enabling efficient exploration of the search space governed by the number of phases and nodes.

The search process is iterative and includes two main stages: exploration and exploitation. In the exploration phase, diverse architectures are generated using genetic operations such as crossover and mutation. The crossover operation preserves common elements between parent architectures while allowing for the exchange of unique features, thus creating offspring that maintain a similar complexity to their parents. Meanwhile, the mutation operation introduces diversity by flipping bits in the encoding, potentially leading to new architectures.

The exploitation phase focuses on reinforcing the best architectures discovered during exploration. To enhance computational efficiency, the authors propose an algorithm to identify and eliminate duplicate architectures, ensuring that the search process remains tractable.

Overall, NSGA-Net effectively combines genetic algorithms with neural architecture search, offering a structured method to evolve and optimize neural networks while managing the complexity inherent in architecture evaluation.
