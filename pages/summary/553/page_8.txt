The paper introduces NSGA-Net, a multi-objective evolutionary approach for neural architecture search (NAS). It highlights the effectiveness of NSGA-Net in optimizing neural network architectures across multiple competing objectives, outperforming traditional methods such as random search and single-objective NAS approaches.

Key findings include:

1. **Trade-off Frontier Comparisons**: NSGA-Net produces superior network architectures compared to random search, as shown in Figure 8a. The inclusion of crossover in the evolutionary algorithm significantly enhances the trade-off frontier, as depicted in Figure 8b.

2. **Bayesian Network Utilization**: The paper demonstrates that offspring creation using a Bayesian network leads to better network architectures than uniform sampling, with results indicating that architectures sampled from the Bayesian network dominate those from uniform sampling (Figure 8c).

3. **Complexity Control**: NSGA-Net provides finer control over network complexity compared to methods that rely on repeated computational blocks. The search process adapts by reducing complexity primarily at higher image resolutions, allowing for a diverse set of optimized architectures.

4. **Performance Evaluation**: Experimental results show that NSGA-Net achieves lower test errors and better performance on the CIFAR-100 dataset compared to various classifiers, including those derived from manual and reinforcement learning methods.

5. **Conclusion**: NSGA-Net effectively balances prediction performance and computational complexity, yielding architectures that surpass handcrafted designs and compete well against state-of-the-art single-objective NAS methods.

The paper acknowledges support from the National Science Foundation and discusses redundancy in the search space due to the directed acyclic nature of the encoding used in NSGA-Net.
