The paper discusses the development of two innovative approaches for generating double random forest models, specifically oblique double random forest ensembles and rotation-based double random forest ensembles. The oblique models utilize a multi-class support vector machine (MPSVM) to create splitting hyperplanes at non-leaf nodes, incorporating geometric structures for improved generalization performance. To address sample size issues as decision trees grow, the authors employ Tikhonov regularization along with axis-parallel and null space regularization.

In the rotation-based approach, the authors apply transformations like principal component analysis (PCA) and linear discriminant analysis (LDA) on randomly selected feature subspaces at each non-leaf node. This method enhances the diversity of decision tree ensembles, contributing to better generalization. A significant distinction from standard random forests is that the proposed models use bootstrap aggregation at every non-terminal node for selecting optimal splits, resulting in larger decision trees.

Statistical analyses demonstrate the superiority of the proposed double random forest models over standard baseline classifiers. The authors suggest future work to extend these models to regression and time series forecasting, as well as to benchmark their performance against standard random forests and XGBoost on a unified platform.
