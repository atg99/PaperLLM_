This section presents an analysis of existing baseline models and the proposed oblique and rotation double random forest in terms of diversity among individual decision tree classifiers and their classification accuracy. The analysis utilizes kappa-error diversity diagrams for visualization, which plot the individual accuracy and diversity of the base learners in a two-dimensional format. Each point in the scatter plot corresponds to a pair of classifiers, where the x-coordinate represents the diversity (Kappa coefficient) and the y-coordinate reflects the average error of the classifiers.

The Kappa coefficient quantifies the level of agreement between two classifiers while accounting for chance agreement. It is calculated using the observed agreement (pr(a)) and the hypothetical probability of chance agreement (pr(e)). A Kappa value of 1 indicates complete agreement, while a value of 0 signifies independence between the classifiers. The analysis evaluates all possible pairs of classifiers to assess their diversity and accuracy, where smaller Kappa values indicate greater diversity and lower correlation, while lower average error signifies stronger classifiers. The ideal scenario is represented by classifiers located in the bottom left corner of the diagram, indicating high diversity and low error.
