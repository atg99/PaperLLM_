The paper introduces two novel approaches to enhance the performance of Random Forest models: oblique double random forests and rotation double random forests. The authors emphasize that the strength of ensemble models, such as Random Forest, lies in the diversity among unstable learners. 

The rotation double random forest approach involves transforming the feature space at each node with different random feature subspaces. This transformation is achieved using techniques like principal component analysis and linear discriminant analysis, leading to improved diversity among base learners and better generalization performance.

In contrast, the oblique double random forest approach addresses the limitations of univariate decision trees, which often result in axis-parallel splits that do not effectively capture the data's geometric structure. By employing multivariate decision trees and utilizing multisurface proximal support vector machines to generate optimal planes at non-leaf nodes, this method enhances both the depth of decision trees and their performance. The paper also discusses the integration of various regularization techniques to mitigate small sample size issues.

The proposed models are evaluated on 121 benchmark UCI datasets and real-world fisheries datasets, with results indicating that both the oblique and rotation double random forest models outperform standard ensemble models. The findings are supported by statistical analysis and experimental results, showcasing the effectiveness of the proposed enhancements in ensemble learning through the use of bagging and improved decision tree structures.
