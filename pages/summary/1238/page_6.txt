Additionally, the paper discusses the Random Forest algorithm, which is an ensemble learning method utilizing decision trees as base learners. Random Forest employs bagging and random subspace methods to introduce diversity among trees. Each tree selects optimal splits from randomly chosen subsets of features at non-leaf nodes, using impurity criteria such as information gain or Gini impurity for decision-making.

The training phase of the Random Forest algorithm involves generating bootstrap samples from the training dataset and constructing decision trees based on these samples. The process includes selecting a subset of features and determining the best split at each node until a stopping criterion is achieved. During the classification phase, test data points are labeled based on majority voting from the predictions of the ensemble of decision trees.
