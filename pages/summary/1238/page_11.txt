The paper discusses the development and evaluation of enhanced ensemble models, specifically DRaF-PCA and DRaF-LDA, which build on the standard RaF and DRaF models. These new models incorporate PCA and LDA transformations at non-leaf nodes, in addition to the existing techniques of random subspace and bagging. This integration is aimed at increasing the diversity of the base learners, resulting in superior performance compared to their predecessors. Notably, DRaF-PCA and DRaF-LDA utilize the bagging concept at each non-leaf node, allowing for deeper tree structures and improved classification results.

In the experimental analysis section, the authors detail their methodology for evaluating these models against several baseline classifiers, including various existing models such as MPRaF and RaF variants, using UCI and real-world fisheries datasets. The analysis is based on a comprehensive set of 121 datasets with varying sample sizes and feature dimensions, ensuring a robust evaluation framework. Each ensemble model comprises 50 base learners, and the CART classifier serves as the foundation for these models.

The statistical analysis reveals that the proposed models, DRaF-LDA, DRaF-PCA, and MPDRaF-P, consistently outperform existing classifiers in terms of average accuracy and ranking. The Friedman test, complemented by the Nemenyi post hoc test, is employed to assess the significance of the performance differences among the classifiers. The results indicate that the proposed models not only achieve better accuracy but also rank higher, implying better generalization capabilities. The paper concludes by emphasizing the effectiveness of the proposed models in enhancing classification performance through increased diversity among base learners.
