The paper presents a novel approach called Double Random Forest, which utilizes two methods for rotating the feature subspace: Principal Component Analysis (PCA) and Linear Discriminant Analysis (LDA). The proposed algorithms, DRaF-PCA and DRaF-LDA, enhance the performance of random forests by applying transformations to bootstrapped samples at each non-leaf node.

In DRaF-PCA, the algorithm calculates the total scatter matrix from the bootstrapped samples at each node. The training phase involves generating decision trees with randomly chosen feature subsets and applying PCA to transform the data before searching for optimal splits. The classification phase rotates test samples using the same transformation matrix derived during training, and predictions are made through majority voting from the ensemble of trees.

In contrast, DRaF-LDA focuses on calculating within-class and between-class scatter matrices instead of the total scatter matrix. This method computes generalized eigenvectors to facilitate the feature transformation, thereby enhancing class separation.

The proposed models, MPDRaF-T, MPDRaF-P, and MPDRaF-N, differ from existing baseline models by implementing bagging techniques at each non-leaf node, leading to larger trees with more unique training instances. These models capture the geometric properties of data more effectively than traditional random forest variants, thereby improving their predictive capabilities.
