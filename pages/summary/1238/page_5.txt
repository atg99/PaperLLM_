The paper discusses methods for measuring the similarity between discrete and continuous probability distributions, particularly focusing on the separability of two normal classes represented by C1 and C2. Each class follows a multivariate Gaussian distribution characterized by their respective means and covariance matrices. The authors utilize Bhattacharyya distance as a metric to assess class separability, allowing for the transformation of a multiclass classification problem into a binary classification problem.

The proposed approach is outlined in Algorithm 1, which involves the following steps: 
1. For each pair of classes, the function F is calculated based on their means and covariances.
2. The classes with the maximum Bhattacharyya distance are identified and assigned to two hyperclasses, Cp and Cn.
3. Remaining classes are grouped based on their distances to these hyperclasses.

Additionally, the paper introduces the Multisurface Proximal Support Vector Machine (MPSVM), which is a binary classification algorithm that generates two hyperplanes to separate the positive and negative classes. MPSVM minimizes the sum of squared distances between positive class samples and their respective hyperplane, while also considering the distances from negative class samples. The optimization problems for MPSVM are presented, highlighting the dual focus on maintaining proximity to both classes while ensuring effective separation.

This framework emphasizes the importance of probabilistic metrics in enhancing class separability and proposes a structured methodology for addressing multiclass classification challenges through binary decomposition.
