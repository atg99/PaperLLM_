The section discusses the Win-Tie-Loss sign test for evaluating the performance of classification models. Under the null hypothesis, classifiers are considered significantly different if one wins more than a specified number of datasets. The wins follow a binomial distribution, allowing for a z-test to assess significance, with a p-value threshold of less than 0.05. Ties are evenly distributed or ignored if odd.

The impact of the "mtry" parameter, which determines the number of candidate features evaluated at each node, is examined next. A smaller mtry increases randomization but can hinder performance by failing to capture data geometry. Results in Figure 2 demonstrate that low mtry values lead to poorer performance, while stability in performance is achieved at higher mtry settings, particularly around the value of round(pn).

The "minleaf" parameter, representing the maximum number of samples in an impure node, is also analyzed. Higher minleaf values generally create smaller trees, leading to higher bias and lower variance. Evaluations across 120 datasets reveal significant performance differences based on minleaf values, with smaller minleaf parameters typically yielding better results. This aligns with findings that larger ensemble trees enhance performance. Table 7 ranks model performance across different minleaf settings, highlighting that most models perform better with a minleaf of 1.
