The kappa error diagrams, detailed in supplementary figures, showcase the performance of classification models trained on training data against testing samples, with axes adjusted for clarity in some instances.

The analysis reveals distinct characteristics among random forest models. Notably, MPRaF-N is identified as the most diverse classifier, while DRaF demonstrates the highest accuracy. The DRaF-LDA ensemble classifiers emerge as the best performers in terms of overall generalization on the datasets. The findings suggest that models achieving a balanced combination of diversity and error yield superior performance.

In the computational complexity analysis, the focus is on the complexities at individual nodes within decision trees. For axis-parallel splits, the optimal threshold is determined with a complexity of O(nm log m). In contrast, MPSVM-based oblique decision trees exhibit a higher complexity of O(n^3). When feature transformations like PCA and LDA are employed, additional computational time is required, with complexities of O(mn^min(m,n) + n^3) for PCA and O(mn^2) for LDA. The MPSVM method is noted to be faster than standard ensemble models, particularly near the root nodes. However, the training times for DRaF-PCA and DRaF-LDA are longer than their RaF counterparts due to the bootstrapping process leading to deeper decision trees.
