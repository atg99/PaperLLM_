The paper discusses the implementation of a Genetic Algorithm (GA) for feature selection in a dataset, emphasizing the importance of mutation for maintaining diversity within the population. The GA employs a uniform crossover method to combine features from selected individuals, while a low mutation probability of 0.05 is introduced to enhance search ability and avoid premature convergence.

The termination criteria for the GA are set at 10 generations, after which it selects the individual with the highest predictive feature set. The experimentation resulted in the identification of 70 unique features from the training dataset and 66 from the testing dataset, culminating in a comprehensive feature set of 99 unique features.

The analysis reveals that the majority of selected features are from service and flag categories, with protocol type also being significant. This indicates that the GA effectively identifies features that are crucial for predicting attack types.

The paper then outlines the construction of an LSTM-RNN model, detailing the selection of hyper-parameters such as batch size, number of epochs, learning rate, dropout, and activation function. These parameters are vital for the model's training process, impacting how well it learns from the data. Overall, the study highlights the effectiveness of GA in feature selection and sets the groundwork for utilizing LSTM-RNN for classification tasks.
