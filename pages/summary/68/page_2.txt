Anomaly-based NIDS utilize heuristic methods to identify deviations from normal traffic, which can lead to high false-positive rates but are valued for their potential to detect novel attacks. Various machine learning techniques, including support vector machines, decision trees, and deep learning approaches such as deep neural networks, recurrent neural networks, and long short-term memory networks, have been employed to enhance NIDS performance.

Feature selection is critical in developing effective NIDS, as relevant features can significantly impact classifier accuracy. Different approaches to feature selection, including filter, wrapper, and embedded methods, offer varying advantages and challenges. This study introduces a new NIDS that integrates genetic algorithms (GA) for optimal feature selection with LSTM-RNN for classification, utilizing the NSL-KDD dataset. 

The research establishes an optimal set of hyper-parameters for the LSTM-RNN model, leading to improved classification accuracy compared to using the full feature set. The results indicate that the LSTM-RNN combined with GA effectively detects various attack classes, reducing misclassification rates. Performance metrics such as accuracy, precision, recall, and confusion matrix demonstrate the superiority of the proposed model over traditional methods like SVM and random forests, highlighting its efficacy in both binary and multi-class classification tasks.
