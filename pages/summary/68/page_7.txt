The paper discusses the distribution of normal and attack records in the NSL-KDD dataset, highlighting a total of 125,973 instances in the training set and 22,544 in the testing set, with detailed counts of various attack types. It reviews existing research on intrusion detection systems (IDS), particularly focusing on recurrent neural networks (RNN) and long short-term memory (LSTM) networks. Studies indicate that RNN-based models, such as RNN-IDS, outperform traditional machine learning algorithms like J48, SVM, RF, and ANN in both binary and multi-class classifications. The paper also references successful implementations of CNN-LSTM and LSTM-RNN models on KDD datasets, emphasizing the importance of hyper-parameter optimization and feature selection.

The authors introduce their approach, combining LSTM-RNN with genetic algorithms (GA) for feature selection, to enhance detection rates. They conducted binary classification (normal vs. anomaly) and multi-class classification (including DoS, probe, U2R, and R2L), measuring performance through metrics like accuracy, precision, recall, and F-score. The LSTM-RNN models were tested with varying hidden layer neuron counts, and the implementation utilized Python libraries such as scikit-learn, TensorFlow, and Keras. The study aims to validate the effectiveness of LSTM-RNN against traditional classifiers, using SVM and RF as benchmarks for comparison.
