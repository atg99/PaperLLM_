The paper discusses the application of Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks and Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) in network intrusion detection systems (NIDS), achieving high accuracy rates of 93.88% for multi-class classification and 99.91% for binary classification. The methodology incorporates a genetic algorithm (GA) to enhance the LSTM-RNN model. 

The paper is structured as follows: Section 2 provides background on deep learning, LSTM, RNN, and genetic algorithms, along with an overview of the NSL-KDD dataset utilized for the research. Section 3 reviews existing literature on LSTM and RNN applications in NIDS. Section 4 details the methodology employed for the LSTM-RNN with GA. Section 5 presents experimental results and performance comparisons among various classifiers. Section 6 discusses the experimental findings, and Section 7 concludes with future work directions.

The background section elaborates on deep learning as a multi-layer neural network approach, detailing RNN architecture, which mimics biological neural connections. RNNs consist of input, output, and hidden units, with at least three hidden layers facilitating signal processing through weight adjustments. The RNN model's structure allows for a one-way information flow and error comparison between hidden layers, enhancing learning efficiency.
