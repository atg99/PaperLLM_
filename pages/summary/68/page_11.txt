The paper discusses the development of an LSTM-RNN model for classification tasks, detailing the selection of hyper-parameters and optimizers for both binary and multi-class classifications. Key hyper-parameters include batch size, which defines the number of training records processed in one pass, epochs that represent full training passes, learning rate for weight updates, dropout for regularization through the omission of certain neurons during training, and the activation function used to process inputs within the network.

For optimization, the paper identifies stochastic gradient descent (SGD) as the preferred choice for multi-class classification due to its lower computational cost, while the Adam optimizer is chosen for binary classification for its simplicity and efficiency. 

The model's performance is evaluated using testing datasets to obtain accuracy rates and loss values, with metrics such as precision, recall, F-score, true positive rate, false positive rate, and confusion matrix calculated from predicted and expected classes. The evaluation compares the performance of the LSTM-RNN model against support vector machines (SVM) and random forests (RF).

The experimental setup employs TensorFlow and Keras for the LSTM-RNN implementation and scikit-learn for SVM and RF. Two classification types are performed: binary classification for normal and anomaly detection, and multi-class classification for various attack detection types. The optimal feature set was determined using a genetic algorithm. To mitigate overfitting, dropout techniques were applied, with varying rates based on the number of hidden layers in the network. Each experiment was repeated multiple times to ensure reliability of the results.
