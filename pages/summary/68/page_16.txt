In the binary classification experiment, the study utilized both 122 features and an optimized set of 99 features. Initially, with 122 features, the model achieved an accuracy of 96.51% on the KDDTest + dataset and 99.88% on the KDDTrain + dataset. The true positive rate (TPR) and false positive rate (FPR) were 0.944 and 0.007, respectively. After implementing the genetic algorithm, the accuracy improved to 99.91% for KDDTest + and 99.99% for KDDTrain +, with a TPR of 0.999 and an FPR of 0.003. This demonstrates over a 3% increase in accuracy, even while employing a smaller LSTM-RNN network, which also reduced running time. The Random Forest (RF) classifier yielded the highest testing accuracy, attributed to its effective construction of decision trees when features clearly define classes.

In multi-class classification, the model initially achieved an accuracy of 82.68% on KDDTest + with 122 features and 99.87% on KDDTrain + using 60 neurons. After applying the genetic algorithm and refining to 99 features, the accuracy rose to 93.88% for KDDTest + and 99.0% for KDDTrain +, with a TPR of 0.896 and FPR of 0.005. This represented a more than 10% improvement over using all features, while also decreasing training and testing times.

The confusion matrices for both binary and multi-class classifications illustrated the reduction in misclassifications and highlighted the effectiveness of the genetic algorithm in enhancing model performance.
