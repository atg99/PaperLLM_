In this section, the authors present their experimental setup and results for the proposed model, DDP-GCN, in traffic speed forecasting. The experiments utilized datasets Urban1 and Urban2, with parameters set for M, M0, T0, and T, where both T0 and T represent a one-hour span of 12 samples. The implementation was conducted using TensorFlow 1.15 on a Linux cluster equipped with Intel Xeon CPU and NVIDIA TITAN V GPU.

The model was compared against several baseline models, including Historical Average (HA), Vector Auto-Regression (VAR), Linear Support Vector Regression (LSVR), Auto-Regressive Integrated Moving Average (ARIMA), Fully Connected LSTM (FC-LSTM), Diffusion Convolutional Recurrent Neural Networks (DCRNN), and Spatio-Temporal Graph Convolutional Networks (STGCN). 

Performance metrics such as Mean Absolute Error (MAE), Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE), and Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE) were used to evaluate the models, with results summarized in a table showing the accuracy of predictions at 30, 45, and 60 minutes. The authors reported that DDP-GCN(Stacked) outperformed all models in most cases, with a notable 7.52% average improvement for 60-minute forecasts compared to the previous state-of-the-art.

Despite the simplicity of HA, which leverages historical data from previous weeks, it achieved reasonable accuracy, attributed to the strong weekly periodicity of the dataset. However, DDP-GCN consistently outperformed HA, suggesting potential for further improvements by incorporating weekly data. The authors also highlighted the strength of non-Euclidean spatial relationships in enhancing forecasting accuracy. Overall, DDP-GCN(Stacked) demonstrated robust performance, particularly when extending the forecasting horizon, unlike other graph convolution methods.
