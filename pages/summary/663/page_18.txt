The study demonstrates that the DDP-GCN(Stacked) outperforms other models in tracking ground truth curves, highlighting the significance of non-Euclidean information for effectively capturing abrupt changes in complex networks. An ablation test was conducted to assess the contribution of various spatial graph elements, revealing that removing the Positional Relationship resulted in the most significant performance decline, while removing the Distance had the least impact. Additionally, replacing graph elements with a distance-only element reaffirmed the importance of including Direction and Positional Relationship for enhanced forecasting accuracy. 

Further, the ablation test on partition filters indicated that utilizing standard distance measures instead of modified ones also affected model performance. The results suggest that a well-rounded integration of spatial relationships, particularly directional and positional elements, is crucial for improving the model's predictive capabilities in complex spatial contexts.
