This paper discusses the development of a deep learning model, DDP-GCN, aimed at enhancing traffic forecasting in crowded urban environments by leveraging three identified spatial dependencies. The authors acknowledge a limitation in their research, noting that the specific contributions of these spatial dependencies to performance improvements remain unclear. They propose future investigations into multiple traffic datasets to better understand how directional and positional relationships influence traffic speed forecasting.

The research is supported by grants from the National Research Foundation of Korea, the Electronics and Telecommunications Research Institute, and the IITP. The paper references a variety of related works that explore spatio-temporal forecasting methods and deep learning applications in intelligent transportation systems, highlighting the relevance of their study in the broader field of traffic prediction.
