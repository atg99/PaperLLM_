This paper introduces a novel approach for traffic forecasting using a model called DDP-GCN, which stands for Directed Dynamic Partition Graph Convolutional Network. The proposed model enhances traditional forecasting methods by incorporating non-Euclidean spatial relationships and directionality of road links through the use of multi-graphs.

Key contributions of the paper include:

1. **Identification of Spatial Relationships**: The authors identify essential spatial dependencies in traffic forecasting, specifically focusing on directional and positional relationships. They propose encoding these relationships using multiple graphs and introduce partition filters that subdivide spatial graph elements into components with similar characteristics.

2. **Development of DDP-GCN**: The DDP-GCN model is designed to effectively exploit spatial dependencies for traffic forecasting, making it particularly suited for long-term predictions in complex urban networks, which present significant forecasting challenges.

The paper is organized into several sections. Section 2 examines the representation of spatial dependencies in traffic forecasting and describes the role of graph convolutional networks (GCNs) in this context. Section 3 formulates the research problem and defines key concepts. Section 4 details the DDP-GCN model. Section 5 presents the prediction performance and results from ablation tests conducted on two large-scale urban datasets. Finally, Section 6 concludes the paper.

Overall, this work highlights the limitations of traditional forecasting methods, such as ARIMA and Kalman filters, which struggle with the non-linear complexities of traffic data. By leveraging deep learning techniques, particularly GCNs, the authors demonstrate a significant advancement in modeling traffic patterns effectively, showcasing the potential of DDP-GCN to outperform existing state-of-the-art methods in challenging urban environments.
