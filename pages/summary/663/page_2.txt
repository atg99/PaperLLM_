Recent advancements in deep learning for traffic prediction have shifted towards integrating historical speed data with spatial information from road networks. Traditional methods often use image-based formats, such as unfolded road networks or grid-based representations, but these fail to capture the intricate spatial relationships inherent in traffic systems, like driving directions and proximity on paths. Consequently, researchers are increasingly adopting graph convolutional networks (GCNs) to better model these complexities.

While many existing GCN applications rely solely on Euclidean distances, some studies have begun incorporating non-Euclidean dependencies. For instance, early applications in traffic forecasting defined graphs using various non-Euclidean factors such as demand between stations and trip durations. Further modifications have included relationships like inflow/outflow and reachability, enhancing the modeling of traffic dynamics. In bike demand forecasting, multi-graph convolution has utilized factors beyond distance, such as transportation connectivity and functional similarities.

To effectively apply multi-graph convolution to traffic forecasting, it is crucial to identify significant non-Euclidean dependencies. An illustrative example highlights the importance of directional characteristics: while directly neighboring links may appear most relevant based on distance, they may not share similar speed patterns due to differing driving directions. Links that align directionally or lead to the same area may exhibit more consistent speed patterns, particularly during peak commuting times.

To address these insights, the authors propose a new traffic prediction network termed DDP-GCN, which incorporates distance, direction, and positional relationships as key spatial dependencies. This approach leverages multi-graphs to effectively represent the non-Euclidean characteristics of complex road networks, thereby enhancing the accuracy of traffic predictions.
