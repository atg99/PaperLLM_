The paper introduces DDP-GCN, a novel multi-graph convolutional network designed to enhance spatiotemporal traffic forecasting. The authors highlight that traditional methods primarily focused on distance-based spatial dependencies, neglecting other critical factors such as direction and positional relationships within the road network. To address this, the paper proposes a framework that incorporates these three spatial relationships—distance, direction, and positional relationship—into the forecasting model.

The DDP-GCN leverages these building blocks to improve the accuracy of traffic speed predictions, particularly in complex urban environments. The model is evaluated using two large-scale real-world datasets, demonstrating significant improvements in long-term forecasting, especially during peak commute hours. However, the authors note that the enhancements may be limited for short-term predictions.

The study emphasizes the importance of integrating diverse spatial dependencies in traffic forecasting systems to better support applications like autonomous vehicle operations and transportation management. Overall, DDP-GCN presents a promising approach for improving traffic speed forecasting in Intelligent Transportation Systems.
