This section discusses the experimental setup and results of a study on traffic forecasting using graph convolutional networks. The authors present three types of spatial blocks: single, parallel, and stacked, represented as small gray boxes in Figure 9, which denote graph convolution elements. Layer normalization is indicated in the diagram, and the methodology includes a temporal block with a kernel size of 3 and a stride of 1.

The experiments utilize two large-scale datasets from Seoul, South Korea: Urban1 (Gangnam) and Urban2 (Mapo), which are known for their intricate connectivity patterns and heavy traffic. Data was collected from over 70,000 taxis over a month in April 2018, with trajectory samples recorded every five minutes. The processed data reflects average traffic speeds on bidirectional links with complex traffic signals.

Distinct from previous studies that primarily focused on freeway networks and simplified point links, this research emphasizes the challenges posed by urban traffic conditions. The authors highlight the availability of their codes and processed datasets for further research, contributing to the understanding of traffic forecasting in urban environments.
