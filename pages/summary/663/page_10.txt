The thresholded Gaussian kernel is identified as a prevalent method for assigning edge weights between vertices when no inherent weights are present.

The paper introduces three key priors related to link relationships in graph convolutional networks (GCNs). Prior 2 emphasizes that the direction of links can influence their relationships, suggesting that links aligned in the same direction may exhibit stronger connections than those in opposite directions. The proposed directional measure, W⊥, facilitates the learning of directional patterns by GCNs, enhancing their predictive capabilities if such patterns exist in the dataset.

Prior 3 highlights the importance of positional relationships between links, positing that the proximity of destinations can inform link interactions. The authors extend link vectors to include start points and directions, defining WP as unweighted adjacency matrices that capture various intersection points of these extended links. Four types of positional relationships are identified, indicating different potential interactions between links based on their orientations and connections.

Overall, the paper underscores the significance of incorporating direction and positional information into GCNs to improve understanding and prediction of link interactions in complex networks.
