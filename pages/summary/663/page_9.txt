The paper presents a study on the creation of weighted adjacency matrices for directed networks, specifically focusing on traffic speed forecasting. It introduces three types of weighted adjacency matrices for the same link pair, denoted as W(i;j). The first matrix represents the shortest path distance between links (WD(i;j)), the second captures the difference in link directions (W*(i;j)), and the third describes the positional relationship between links (WP(i;j)). 

The authors emphasize the importance of accurately modeling the complex spatial relationships within road networks. They build on previous research that focused on undirected networks and introduce multi-graphs in the context of directed networks. This approach is aimed at enhancing the modeling capabilities for traffic forecasting.

The paper outlines three priors that guide the definition of spatial graph elements, with only the first being previously established. Prior 1 highlights the principle that proximity influences the strength of relationships between entities. The distance measure used in Graph 1 (WD) is defined based on the shortest inter-link distance, calculated through the Dijkstra algorithm for unconnected links, or as an average of link lengths for directly connected links.

The weighted adjacency matrices are designed to incorporate these various spatial relationships, allowing for a more nuanced representation of the road network, which is crucial for effective traffic speed forecasting.
