The paper discusses three primary methods for representing road networks as inputs for deep neural networks (DNNs), as illustrated in Figure 2. Each representation has unique characteristics and implications for performance.

The first method, depicted in Figure 2(b), organizes road network data into a simple vector format. This approach has been commonly used in earlier DNN studies, where data is stacked based on human intuition rather than established guidelines. For example, in a circular road network, links were arranged in a clockwise order. While this method is straightforward and flexible, it lacks a systematic approach for data representation.

The second method, shown in Figure 2(c), utilizes a two-dimensional grid structure, often referred to as grid-based map segmentation. This representation mimics image data, allowing the application of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), which have proven effective for image processing tasks. However, the 2D-grid representation can be inefficient, as it may not capture complex spatial relationships as effectively as other methods.

Overall, the choice of data representation significantly influences the performance of DNNs in traffic forecasting tasks, highlighting the importance of selecting an appropriate method for optimal results.
