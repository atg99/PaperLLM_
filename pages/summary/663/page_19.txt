In this paper, we introduce a novel traffic speed forecasting network that leverages three spatial dependencies: distance, direction, and positional relationships. Our model employs multi-graph convolution, using spatial graph elements as foundational components, and incorporates partition filters to segment each spatial graph element into multiple similar characteristic components. 

The performance of our proposed DDP-GCN(Stacked) model was evaluated against popular baseline models, demonstrating significant improvements, particularly in long-term forecasting for complex urban networks. Our findings indicate that DDP-GCN(Stacked) consistently outperformed state-of-the-art GCN methods, even with the integration of K-hops, although performance did not enhance with larger K values.

While our model accounts for temporal dependencies through consecutive time sequences, it does not fully utilize daily or weekly periodicity. This limitation is acknowledged, as such periodicities can improve forecasting power, yet modifying graph convolutional networks to effectively incorporate them poses challenges. The performance of a simpler model, like Historical Average, which inherently leverages weekly periodicity, proved competitive in our complex urban settings.

In conclusion, our research emphasizes the importance of spatial dependencies in traffic forecasting and suggests that future work should explore effective methods to integrate periodicity into graph convolutional frameworks.
