In this section, the authors focus on two primary components: the creation of partition filters and the construction of spatial and temporal blocks for analyzing complex relationships in data. 

The creation of partition filters involves generating filters based on angles, with the X-axis representing angles and the Y-axis showing the corresponding values from the histogram of directional links. The authors note that while their work identifies four specific angles (0, 90, 180, 270 degrees) that align with histogram peaks, the design allows for flexibility in choosing any number of directions based on histogram analysis. This contrasts with previous work where filter weights were dynamically generated for each input.

Next, the authors describe the building of spatial blocks using graph elements WD, W*, and WP, alongside their partitioned versions. They emphasize the use of a hybrid approach by employing fWD*WPg instead of WP alone. The spatial blocks consist of different configurations: Single, which involves simple graph convolution based on Euclidean distance; Parallel, which incorporates multiple graph convolutions considering distance, direction, and positional relationships; and Stacked, which also employs multiple graph convolutions but with a different structural connection among the graph elements. The Parallel structure is noted to be similar to multi-graph convolution as defined in earlier works.

In building temporal blocks, the authors conducted various experiments with different methodologies such as graph convolution and self-attention. However, they found that simple convolution yielded the best performance with the least training time. This observation led them to adapt their approach based on empirical results, highlighting the effectiveness of simplicity in their temporal block design.
