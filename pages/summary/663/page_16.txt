The paper presents a performance comparison of various forecasting algorithms on two urban datasets (Urban1 and Urban2) across different time horizons (30, 45, and 60 minutes). The metrics used for evaluation include Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE), Mean Absolute Error (MAE), and Root Mean Square Error (RMSE). 

In the Urban1 dataset, the DDP-GCN(Stacked) model demonstrates the best performance across all time horizons, achieving a 9.75% average improvement and a 16.18% improvement for the 60-minute forecasting horizon compared to other algorithms. However, for shorter forecasting horizons (15 minutes), it performs worse than DCRNN and STGCN, suggesting that short-term forecasting relies more on temporal information, while longer-term forecasting benefits from spatial information.

For the Urban2 dataset, similar trends are observed, with DDP-GCN(Stacked) again exhibiting superior performance. The analysis also highlights the relationship between RMSE and average traffic speed, indicating that high traffic speeds in the early morning lead to larger RMSE values due to scaling effects. 

Overall, the findings indicate that while DDP-GCN models offer significant enhancements for longer forecasting horizons, their effectiveness may vary depending on the time horizon considered.
