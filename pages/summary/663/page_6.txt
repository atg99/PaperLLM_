The paper discusses the differences between 2D convolution and graph convolution, particularly in the context of traffic forecasting using Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs). In 2D convolution, the filter shape and size are straightforward due to the structured nature of grid data, allowing for easy identification of neighboring nodes. In contrast, graph convolution deals with unstructured data, such as traffic networks, where neighborhood sizes can vary. This is managed by using an N x N matrix as a spatial filter, where non-zero weights represent neighboring nodes connected to a specific node.

The effectiveness of GCNs in traffic forecasting heavily relies on the appropriate weighting of edges in the graph, which reflects the network's geometry. Previous studies have modeled road networks as undirected binary graphs or weighted graphs, with edge weights based on physical distance or travel time. Recent research has expanded this by incorporating additional factors like flow relationships and functional similarities. The paper also introduces a multi-graph convolution approach to better capture semantic correlations among distant roads and proposes new non-Euclidean graph elements alongside a partition filter for modifying these elements. This exploration aims to enhance the design of multi-graph convolutions in traffic flow prediction.
