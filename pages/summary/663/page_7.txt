In this section, the key concepts for modeling road traffic are defined, and the problem is formulated. The paper introduces the concept of a link, which is a segment of road without internal merging or diverging sections. A link vector is defined as the difference between the endpoint and the start point of the link, represented mathematically as \( R_i = R_{end} - R_{start} \). The link direction is derived from the link vector and is expressed in terms of angles.

Additionally, a traffic network graph is defined as a weighted directed graph \( G = \{ V, E, W \} \), where \( V \) represents the set of road links, \( E \) signifies the edges indicating connectivity, and \( W \) is the weighted adjacency matrix illustrating spatial inter-dependencies among road links. It is noted that the adjacency matrix may include connections that do not adhere to the standard zero-weight property for unconnected links.

The problem is formulated as a traffic forecasting challenge, where a graph signal \( X \) represents traffic speed on the graph \( G \). The goal is to learn a function \( g(:) \) that maps a series of historical graph signals to future signals over a defined time interval. Specifically, the relationship is expressed as mapping past observations to future predictions within the context of the graph structure.
