The paper discusses the role of positional relationships in urban planning, particularly in the context of Seoul's complex urban network. It emphasizes the significance of understanding how different business districts and commuter towns interact through various transport routes. The authors introduce a concept of "WP" (possibly referring to waypoint or working point) that can effectively capture these intricate positional relationships, which have not been extensively studied before. They acknowledge the potential limitations of this approach when applied to different traffic environments, despite its relevance to their dataset.

The paper also proposes the use of partition filters to enhance the learning capabilities of deep learning models in recognizing grid structures prevalent in urban road networks. These filters explicitly define four directional inputs within the spatial graph, facilitating the model's understanding of the grid concept. The authors emphasize that the partition filters are designed to maintain the overall sum of elements while enabling the neural network to learn from the structured data effectively. The filters are aligned with recent advancements in edge-conditioned filters for graph-based convolutional networks, indicating a novel approach to spatial analysis in urban environments.
