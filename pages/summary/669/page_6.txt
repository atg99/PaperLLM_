The paper discusses the architecture and training methods of the FR-CNN model for speech separation tasks. It introduces macro-level unfolding schemes, where blue boxes represent components from previous figures and pink boxes indicate 1x1 convolutions with shared weights. The integration of model inputs with outputs from each block is proposed through feature map concatenation or summation, termed concatenation connection (CC) or summation connection (SC). 

The unfolding process is differentiated for single-input-single-output blocks like A-FRCNN and multi-input-multi-output blocks like S-FRCNN, with downsampling and upsampling applied to manage different scales of feature maps. The training employs the backpropagation algorithm, focusing on maximizing the scale-invariant signal-to-noise ratio (SI-SNR), which also serves as a performance metric for speech separation.

For experimental settings, the paper utilizes three datasets: Libri2Mix, WSJ0-2Mix, and WHAM!, each with varying configurations of speaker combinations, noise levels, and sampling rates. Implementation details include the use of 1-D convolutional layers for both encoder and decoder, with specific kernel sizes and channel configurations outlined. The unfolding methods are adapted to facilitate the connections depicted in the figures, ensuring effective data flow through the model.
