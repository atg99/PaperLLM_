The paper discusses the structure and updating schemes of a fully recurrent convolutional neural network (FRCNN), emphasizing its potential for improving speech separation performance. The FRCNN model, depicted in Figure 1a, features bottom-up, top-down, and lateral connections that facilitate multi-scale information fusion across stages, allowing for a more comprehensive integration of sensory information compared to existing multi-scale fusion (MSF) methods.

The authors highlight that the static representation of the sensory system in Figure 1a does not capture the dynamic nature of neural responses, which vary in timing and speed across different neurons and sensory pathways. This variability challenges the faithful replication of biological systems in artificial neural networks (ANNs). However, the authors argue that inspiration from biological models can lead to advancements in ANNs when combined with task-specific techniques.

The paper distinguishes the proposed asynchronous FRCNN (A-FRCNN) from the synchronous FRCNN (S-FRCNN), which was previously studied for visual recognition. The A-FRCNN architecture, illustrated in Figure 1c, processes information in a sequential bottom-up manner before utilizing parallel fusion and skip connections, allowing for a more thorough processing of information compared to the rapid back-and-forth transmission in the S-FRCNN.

The authors assert that the depth of the A-FRCNN model enhances its performance in deep learning tasks, particularly in speech separation, and they plan to provide empirical evidence of its advantages over the S-FRCNN in their experiments.
