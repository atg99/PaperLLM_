The paper presents a comparative analysis of various models for speech separation, focusing on their performance metrics such as SI-SNRi and SDRi across different datasets including Libri2Mix, WHAM!, and WSJ0-2Mix. Notably, models like DualPathRNN, Gated DualPathRNN, and Sepformer achieved state-of-the-art results but exhibited significantly higher computational costs. For example, DualPathRNN required 142 hours for training on Libri2Mix using 8 GPUs, contrasting with A-FRCNN-16’s training time of 39 hours. 

The results in Table 3 highlight that while some models, such as SuDoRM-RF at various scales, achieved competitive performance, they also varied in computational efficiency. A-FRCNN-16 demonstrated a favorable balance between separation accuracy and computational demands, performing well even with limited resources. Further analysis indicated that modifying the information fusion method from concatenation to summation reduced computational load without substantially impacting performance. Overall, A-FRCNN-16 stands out for its effective trade-off between accuracy and efficiency compared to other advanced models.
