The paper discusses enhancements to the architecture of an asynchronous fusion recurrent convolutional neural network (A-FRCNN) aimed at improving efficiency and performance. 

1. **Control Blocks Overview**: The initial block, Control 1, involves complex connections for processing high-resolution input through down-sampling and up-sampling. However, this complexity leads to inefficiencies in parameters and computation.

2. **Refinement of Connections**: Control 2 simplifies the model by removing bottom-up skip-connections, which are deemed redundant. This adjustment helps streamline the architecture while maintaining essential connections.

3. **Sequential Fusion Method**: The authors propose a sequential fusion approach for information from adjacent stages before non-adjacent ones, addressing the need for increased firing delay. This method preserves the connectivity of stages even after removing certain connections, leading to the development of the A-FRCNN.

4. **Biological Plausibility**: The sequential fusion method aligns more closely with biological processes, as connections between adjacent stages are shorter and signal transmission is not instantaneous.

5. **Multi-scale Information Fusion**: The paper describes the use of C feature maps at each stage, where feature maps from multiple inputs are concatenated and reduced through a convolutional layer. This process is crucial for multi-scale information fusion within the blocks.

6. **Macro-level Unfolding**: The unfolding scheme consists of multiple blocks with tied weights, where blocks are repeated to process information over time. A direct connection method, involving a 1x1 convolution between blocks, is introduced to enhance multi-scale information fusion.

Overall, the proposed modifications to the A-FRCNN architecture aim to improve computational efficiency while maintaining a biologically inspired approach to information processing.
