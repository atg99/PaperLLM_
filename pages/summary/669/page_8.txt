This paper presents a comparison of various speech separation models on the Libri2Mix test set, focusing on the performance of the proposed A-FRCNN model against existing architectures like U-Net, S-FRCNN, and others. The results are summarized in two tables, highlighting key metrics such as SI-SNRi, SDRi, parameters, and computation time.

Table 1 indicates that A-FRCNN outperforms other models, achieving SI-SNRi and SDRi scores of 15.2 and 15.7, respectively, with fewer parameters and less computation time compared to S-FRCNN and its variants. Notably, the S-FRCNN (no-skip) version performed worse than the standard S-FRCNN, emphasizing the importance of the skip connections in these models.

In Table 2, A-FRCNN also surpasses U-Net and its variants, which struggled to achieve competitive results. The U-Net-Delay variant showed improvement with delayed global fusion but still lagged behind A-FRCNN. Adjusting the number of channels in the U-Net blocks resulted in larger models, but these modifications did not translate into better performance, particularly for U-Net-Delay (large), which overfitted.

The paper further compares A-FRCNN to popular models in both time-frequency and time-domain approaches. Despite some models like DualPathRNN performing well on certain datasets, A-FRCNN demonstrated superior separation accuracy on Libri2Mix, particularly with increased unfolding times in the macro-level, where A-FRCNN-16 outperformed DualPathRNN.

In conclusion, A-FRCNN is shown to be an effective architecture for speech separation, achieving high performance metrics while maintaining computational efficiency. The paper highlights the model's clarity in separated speech, supported by audio examples, and provides a comprehensive evaluation against both traditional and cutting-edge models in the field.
