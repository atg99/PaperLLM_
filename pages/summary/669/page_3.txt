The paper discusses advancements in speech separation methods, primarily focusing on approaches that model different audio sources in the temporal-frequency (TF) domain. Traditional techniques, such as DPCL and uPIT, use the short-time Fourier transform (STFT) to analyze mixed sounds and reconstruct clean signals through inverse STFT. Recent innovations include time-domain methods that utilize non-STFT encoders, exemplified by DualPathRNN, which processes overlapping audio chunks through recurrent neural networks (RNNs). While effective, these methods often incur high computational costs.

Subsequent models like Sepformer and Gated DualPathRNN have improved separation performance while maintaining complexity. To address the lengthy training times associated with RNNs, Conv-TasNet employed Temporal Convolutional Networks, resulting in faster processing but limited multi-scale feature (MSF) capability. Newer multi-branch models, such as SuDoRF-RF and FurcaNeXt, further enhance efficiency and performance by integrating various time resolutions.

The paper also examines the role of lateral and top-down recurrent connections in artificial neural networks (ANNs). Historical developments include the introduction of recurrent connections in multi-layer perceptrons and convolutional neural networks (CNNs). A 2016 framework proposed a hierarchical model with both connection types but did not validate the superiority of synchronous over asynchronous updating schemes. The authors introduce a novel asynchronous scheme for their proposed FRCNN, demonstrating improved results and parameter efficiency in speech separation tasks.

The overall goal of the discussed algorithms is to accurately extract individual speech signals from a mixture, mathematically represented to estimate the waveforms of individual speakers from the combined input.
