The paper discusses the integration of Semantic Web technologies within the context of Autonomous Agents and Multi-Agent Systems (MAS), specifically focusing on two models: LPaaS (Logic Programming as a Service) and LVLP (Labelled Variable Logic Programming). These models address the need for intelligent environments and situated distributed cognition by enabling logic-based services to be embedded in the environment, thereby allowing agents to access these services as needed.

From a logical perspective, LPaaS utilizes logic programming, particularly Prolog, to distribute intelligence within complex distributed systems, ensuring that reasoning capabilities are available where and when they are most required. The technology is implemented as a Java web service, although it is currently not deployed on a ready-to-use webpage, with only the source code available via a GitLab repository.

An analysis of a word cloud generated from a survey of 271 papers reveals key themes within the literature. The most prominent words can be categorized into three groups: actions and goals related to agents, logic and models concerning reasoning, and programming languages. This suggests that logic-based technologies are primarily used in MAS to facilitate agent actions and planning through structured knowledge representation. Additionally, other frequently mentioned terms highlight the importance of semantics, constraints, and the interaction between agents, their environments, and temporal events, which are crucial for formalizing the properties of MAS.
