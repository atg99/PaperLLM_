The paper discusses the exclusion of certain technologies from a survey on Autonomous Agents and Multi-Agent Systems (MAS) based on three primary motivations: general purpose, no logic, and technology not found. 

1. **General Purpose**: Technologies that can be applied broadly but are not specifically designed for MAS, such as various Prolog engines (e.g., SICStus, SWI-Prolog), were excluded because they do not intentionally target any specific MAS abstractions.

2. **No Logic**: Contributions that do not adhere to a recognizable logical framework from existing literature are also excluded. An example provided is JaCaMo, which is built on Jason but does not conform to a clearly identifiable logic.

3. **Technology Not Found**: Technologies that lack actual software resources or evidence of existence are excluded from the survey.

Each of these criteria serves as a sufficient condition for exclusion, meaning that if any one of them applies, the technology is removed from consideration. The paper emphasizes the importance of these criteria in ensuring the relevance and applicability of the technologies included in the survey.
