The paper discusses the current state of logic-based technologies in the context of Multi-Agent Systems (MAS) and identifies key areas for further research and development. It highlights the need for improved coordination and reasoning within agent societies, with a focus on argumentation and defeasible reasoning. While some technologies, such as ASPIC+, show promise, many are not yet mature enough for distributed MAS environments.

The paper notes that the integration of logic into MAS environments is limited, with few technologies effectively addressing the intelligence of these environments or representing distributed knowledge. Despite this, the authors argue that this area warrants further exploration due to its potential.

The analysis reveals that logic programming and BDI logic are the most commonly used frameworks in MAS technologies, owing to their adaptability and relevance to rational agents. Additionally, defeasible, deontic, and description logics are utilized to handle uncertainty and normative issues, while fuzzy logic holds potential for uncertainty management, albeit without practical applications in the surveyed research.

The paper also examines the real-world applicability of various logic-based MAS technologies. Among the 47 surveyed, only IndiGolog, JACK, JIAC, and Agent Factory (AFPL) have demonstrated substantial real-world use in areas like robotics, workflow management, and decision support systems. However, only JACK and JIAC remain actively maintained.

The authors conclude that there is a larger pool of mature technologies that could potentially be applied in real-world contexts than those currently in use. They suggest that a broader assessment of technologies, considering maintenance, documentation, and functionality, could reveal more candidates ready for real-world application.
