The paper discusses various logical frameworks and technologies related to autonomous agents and multi-agent systems (MAS). It begins with AGENT-0, which utilizes a specific modal logic framework to model agents as intentional systems. AGENT-0 employs a propositional temporal logic that associates facts with points in time and includes modal operators for knowledge, belief, desire, and abilities. However, it is noted that AGENT-0 lacks formal logic-based semantics, leading to an intuitive presentation of its temporal components.

The paper then introduces Concurrent MetateM, a framework that extends the original MetateM by incorporating concurrent computation into the modeling of MAS. This system allows multiple agents to execute concurrently and communicate via message passing. The MetateM interpreter is based on temporal logic, guiding agent behavior through rules that relate past antecedents to future consequents, as established by earlier research.

Finally, the paper references AgentSpeak(L) and Jason, which are also significant in the context of agent programming. While the AGENT-0 and Concurrent MetateM technologies are still accessible, they face some limitations in modern execution environments, with only older implementations being available for use. The paper emphasizes the foundational role that these logical frameworks play in the development and understanding of autonomous agents and MAS.
