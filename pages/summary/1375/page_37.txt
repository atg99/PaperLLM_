The paper discusses the technological landscape of Autonomous Agents and Multi-Agent Systems (MAS), focusing on interaction models and argumentation. It highlights that logic-based techniques are pivotal for verifying reactive systems, offering a robust foundation for interactions, communication, and integrity constraints. In contrast, the field of argumentation lacks a unified set of abstractions, with existing solutions typically tailored to specific application domains. The paper emphasizes the recognized potential of logical approaches in norm-based systems and conflict resolution, particularly regarding their explainability in complex MAS.

Regarding environment modeling, the paper notes that knowledge representation, especially through description logics, is a primary focus, as it effectively encapsulates stateful information and supports distributed cognition in knowledge-intensive systems. Predicate logic and action languages are primarily used for agent behaviors, while modal and epistemic logics are favored in model-checking and verification due to their ability to express agent knowledge and actions over time.

The BDI architecture is mentioned as a successful logic-based framework, although it has not been applied to collective agent behavior due to challenges in interpreting desires and intentions within broader contexts. Additionally, the paper observes that many MAS simulation tools lack a formal logic-based framework, relying instead on logic for operational semantics or temporal specifications.

A notable gap identified is the absence of MAS technologies based on fuzzy or probabilistic logic, despite significant interest in Bayesian and Markovian approaches within the community. The paper concludes with a technological analysis of selected frameworks, indicating the need for further exploration in response to specific research questions related to their freshness and applicability.
