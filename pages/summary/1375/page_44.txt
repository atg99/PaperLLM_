The paper discusses the role of logic-based technologies in the context of Multi-Agent Systems (MAS). It distinguishes between technologies that are directly linked to logical approaches and those that are not, leading to the inclusion of Jason while excluding CArtAgO and Moise due to their limited connection. Additionally, technologies such as JASDL, n-Jason, VerICS, and SOCS are excluded due to the lack of accessible downloadable versions. The paper notes that while some theoretical models, like Cupid, are significant, they are not included for similar reasons of unavailability.

The central question posed is about the current role of logic-based technologies in MAS. To explore this, the authors identify five specific research questions and systematically review the literature to provide detailed answers. The fundamental conclusion is that logic-based technologies play a crucial role in addressing the cognitive abilities required for intelligent agent-based systems. However, they often lack the maturity needed for real-world applications.

Research question Q1 identifies 47 assessed logic-based technologies, with 21 still operational, but only 12 having received recent updates. The assessment indicates that few technologies meet basic standards. Q2 and Q3 explore the impact and coverage of logic-based technologies on various aspects of MAS, revealing that they encompass nearly all MAS abstractions with a diverse distribution. Notably, 50% focus on agent programming, utilizing logic for behavior, goals, and knowledge representation through beliefs, desires, and intentions. Among societal abstractions, TuCSoN is highlighted as the most developed technology.
