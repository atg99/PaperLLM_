The paper discusses the availability and functionality of various automated planning systems, specifically focusing on the SHOP (Simple Hierarchical Ordered Planner) and its variants, SHOP2 and JSHOP2. These systems utilize ordered task decomposition within the framework of Hierarchical Task Network (HTN) planning, adhering to the Planning Domain Definition Language (PDDL) standards. This compliance allows for the expression of domain-specific predicates and actions, facilitating a clear definition of the planning environment.

SHOP operates by producing plans where tasks are executed in a specified order, which simplifies reasoning by reducing uncertainty. The system supports the definition of axioms, symbolic and numeric conditions, and external function calls, enhancing its planning capabilities. Although SHOP is not inherently an agent-related technology, it has been applied in agent-oriented contexts.

From a logical perspective, SHOP employs predicate logic, where tasks and states are defined through logical atoms. The system utilizes an inference mechanism based on Horn clauses, allowing transitions between states through operators whose preconditions are logically derived from the current state. The knowledge base is integral to SHOP, containing domain-specific knowledge that aids in improving planning outcomes.

The paper also notes that while many SHOP-related technologies remain unmaintained, a reboot known as SHOP3 is actively developed on GitHub and distributed under the Mozilla Public License. The authors successfully installed SHOP3 in their Common Lisp environment but were unable to evaluate its effectiveness due to a lack of examples.
