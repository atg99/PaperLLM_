This paper addresses the readiness of various technologies in the context of intelligent systems and artificial intelligence, particularly focusing on logic-based technologies. The motivation stems from the increasing complexities posed by modern intelligent systems, which are intricately linked to the Internet of Things (IoT) and Cyber-Physical Systems (CPS). 

Key characteristics of contemporary intelligent systems highlighted in the paper include their distributed nature, which necessitates attributes such as robustness, efficiency, interoperability, portability, standardization, situatedness, and real-time support. Additionally, there is a need for a synthesis of symbolic and sub-symbolic AI, aiming to enhance explainability, observability, interpretability, responsibility, and trustworthiness—central to the concept of Explainable AI (XAI).

The paper further examines the applicability of existing agent solutions within the IoT and CPS domains, noting that many are limited to specific devices. For instance, the Agent Factory Micro Edition (AFME) allows for the execution of deliberative agents on mobile devices, utilizing TCP/IP and Zigbee protocols. However, certain technologies are specifically designed for IoT and CPS, such as the LPaaS architecture, which promotes distributed intelligence and addresses cloud and edge architecture requirements while maximizing availability and interoperability.

Moreover, the TuCSoN and ReSpecT model is highlighted for its situated coordination capabilities, effectively managing coordination issues in multi-agent systems (MAS). It also outlines how environmental resources can serve as sources of perceptions or targets for actions. 

The paper concludes by identifying technologies that, while not explicitly aimed at IoT and CPS, exhibit qualities of portability and interoperability, such as Jason, which supports communication with non-Jason agents, and JACK, which has extensions for open systems. Additionally, the Teleo-Reactive approach is discussed for its effectiveness in developing IoT systems through goal-oriented, perceptive, and action-based specifications, enhancing user engagement in the development process.
