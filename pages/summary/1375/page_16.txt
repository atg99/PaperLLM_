The paper discusses two technologies relevant to multi-agent systems (MAS): Go! and Event-B with the Rodin Platform. 

Go! is a multi-paradigm programming language featuring both declarative and imperative subsets. It is strongly typed and supports multi-threading, allowing threads to communicate through message passing or synchronized access to shared data. Unlike Prolog, Go! avoids certain readability-limiting features and includes higher-level constructs, making it suitable for defining functional relationships and representing ontologies. Despite being available on GitHub under the GNU General Public License, Go! is not actively maintained, and there is insufficient documentation for proper compilation and usage.

Event-B is a formal method designed for the step-wise development of reactive distributed systems, addressing the situated nature of MAS. It allows for rigorous specification and verification of agent interactions through formal reasoning. The methodology is based on first-order logic and set theory, using contexts for static model components and machines for dynamic parts. The Rodin tool facilitates the construction and verification of Event-B models and is built on a custom variant of the Eclipse IDE. Although the source is publicly available, the lack of examples and benchmarks hinders further evaluation.

In summary, both technologies offer distinct methodologies for developing and verifying multi-agent systems, with Go! focusing on programming constructs and Event-B emphasizing formal specifications and reasoning. However, both face challenges in terms of maintenance, documentation, and availability of resources for practical evaluation.
