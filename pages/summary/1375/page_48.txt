This paper presents a systematic literature review (SLR) focused on logic-based technologies for multi-agent systems (MAS). The review aims to explore three main research questions: the availability of logic-based technologies for MAS, the role of logic within these technologies, and the current state and maturity of these technologies. The methodology employed ensures reproducibility and rigor, with clear inclusion and exclusion criteria for selecting relevant works that define or utilize logic-based MAS technologies.

The analysis reveals a categorization of selected technologies into three main MAS abstractions: agents, societies, and the environment. This categorization uncovers an uneven distribution of logic-based technologies across these abstractions, indicating potential research opportunities, particularly within the less explored environmental abstraction. A technical assessment of each technology is conducted, highlighting that, despite significant efforts by the MAS community, many technologies remain immature and not fully suitable for contemporary AI challenges, with few notable exceptions.

The review identifies promising research areas and those requiring technological advancements, establishing itself as a reliable resource for understanding the current state of logic-based MAS technologies and guiding future research directions.
