The paper discusses various programming languages and technologies for developing autonomous agents and multi-agent systems, focusing on their unique features and functionalities. 

Mozart is highlighted as a concise language that abstracts search details, making it suitable for agent programming. It is actively maintained and available on GitHub, supporting major operating systems. The authors successfully installed and tested the Mozart compiler with Oz code snippets.

GOAL (Goal-Oriented Agent Language) is another significant language inspired by the UNITY concurrent language. Unlike UNITY, GOAL emphasizes high-level concepts such as beliefs and goals rather than simple variable assignments. It allows agents to make decisions based on their beliefs and goals, promoting a higher level of declarative programming. GOAL incorporates features like intra-agent communication and decision-making, utilizing technologies such as Prolog and OWL/SPARQL, and is integrated with the Eclipse IDE, although its code base remains private.

JACK is presented as a cross-platform environment developed by the AOS Group, aimed at creating commercial-grade multi-agent systems inspired by BDI architecture. It provides tools for building, running, and integrating intelligent agents in industrial applications.

Overall, the paper outlines the capabilities and characteristics of these programming languages, emphasizing their potential for developing effective autonomous agents and multi-agent systems.
