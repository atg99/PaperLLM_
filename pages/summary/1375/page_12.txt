This paper discusses various technologies related to multi-agent systems (MAS), focusing on CaseLP, DyLOG, DCaseLP, and the Mozart Programming System.

CaseLP, introduced in 1999, is a logic programming-based environment designed for prototyping in MAS, particularly to aid in the planning of goods transportation in collaboration with the Italian Railways and Elsag. DCaseLP, a distributed version of CaseLP, incorporates JADE, tuProlog, and Jess to facilitate agent integration within a distributed environment. DyLOG, initially known as "DYnamics in LOGic," is a high-level logic programming language aimed at modeling rational agents through a modal theory that represents actions and beliefs.

From a technological standpoint, CaseLP, DCaseLP, and DyLOG extend Prolog and are implemented in various programming environments. DCaseLP consists of Java archives enabling the integration of agents specified in declarative languages. However, it lacks maintenance and documentation regarding dependencies, complicating its usability. DyLOG is implemented in SICStus Prolog.

The paper also covers the Mozart Programming System, which, while not explicitly agent-oriented, serves as a robust development platform for intelligent distributed applications. It utilizes the Oz language, which supports multiple programming paradigms, including declarative, object-oriented, and constraint programming. The Oz language is particularly noted for its ability to handle concurrent programming through logical constraint-based inference, making it applicable to search problems.

Overall, the discussed technologies highlight the intersection of logic programming and multi-agent systems, showcasing their potential applications and challenges in development and integration.
