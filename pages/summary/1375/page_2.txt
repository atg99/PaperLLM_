The introduction of the paper discusses the growing importance of artificial intelligence (AI) across various sectors, highlighting a shift in focus from theoretical discussions to practical applications and benefits. It notes significant investments in AI by the European Commission, the American government, and China, indicating a global recognition of AI as a key driver in the next industrial revolution. 

The paper emphasizes the necessity for academia to enhance efforts in intelligent system engineering, particularly in integrating diverse AI techniques. It argues that the successful application of AI is often limited to well-defined scenarios, and the challenge lies in maintaining conceptual integrity amidst the integration of heterogeneous approaches.

Agents and multi-agent systems (MAS) are presented as effective frameworks for addressing these integration challenges. The paper points out that the agent abstraction has been widely adopted in AI education and provides a solid foundation for designing intelligent systems. Furthermore, it highlights ongoing research in agent-oriented computing and software engineering as essential for developing robust methodologies for intelligent system engineering.

The paper also identifies a dichotomy within AI: while sub-symbolic techniques, such as deep learning, are gaining prominence, there is a rising public concern regarding the transparency and explainability of intelligent systems. It suggests that achieving explainable AI (XAI) may require a renewed focus on symbolic techniques, which could enhance the interpretability and accountability of AI systems, particularly in critical human contexts. This dual focus reflects the need to balance the effectiveness of AI applications with the necessity for human understanding and oversight.
