The paper discusses the classification of technologies within the context of autonomous agents and multi-agent systems, particularly focusing on the Semantic Web. It highlights various frameworks and technologies that facilitate interaction and interoperability among agents in this domain.

One key area discussed is the "Agents in the Semantic Web," which includes technologies such as Onto2JaCaMo, AgentOWL, and EMERALD. These frameworks leverage semantic web technologies to enhance agent communication and reasoning capabilities. For instance, AgentOWL supports RDF/OWL ontology models within the JADE multi-agent system, enabling agents to model knowledge using OWL and communicate via OWL and SPARQL.

The paper also addresses the technological challenges faced by AgentOWL, noting compatibility issues with various Java Virtual Machine (JVM) versions, which hinder its functionality. In contrast, EMERALD is presented as a robust framework that allows agents to engage in interoperable reasoning, utilizing third-party reasoning services without requiring uniformity in rule paradigms among agents.

Additionally, the paper mentions the situated ReSpecT technology, emphasizing its focus on situated interaction and coordination, and introduces LPaaS (Logic Programming as a Service), which facilitates the distribution of logical knowledge in the environment, allowing artefacts to participate in the cognitive processes of agents.

Overall, the paper provides insights into the interplay between agents and semantic web technologies, underscoring the importance of interoperability and situatedness in multi-agent systems.
