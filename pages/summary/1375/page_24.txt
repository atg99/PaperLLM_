The paper discusses TuCSoN, a middleware technology that facilitates the interaction of Java and Prolog agents through logic tuple-centres. Developed as a coordination infrastructure, the TuCSoN model is based on the tuple centre concept by Omicini and Denti, allowing agents to communicate via logic tuples that can be observed through logic templates. The tuple centres are programmable using the ReSpecT language, which is designed to enhance social intelligence in multi-agent systems (MAS). TuCSoN supports multiple spatially distributed ReSpecT tuple centres, enabling local deployment in distributed environments, and governs social behavior through local coordination laws.

The paper highlights that TuCSoN has evolved over many years, with various contributors aiming for different objectives, resulting in multiple variants of TuCSoN and ReSpecT. The TuSoW project is identified as a significant initiative to modernize TuCSoN by incorporating contemporary technologies.

From a logical perspective, TuCSoN's tuple centres utilize Prolog, allowing for the distribution of intelligence within the system, particularly through cognitive agents. The coordination media in TuCSoN and ReSpecT are logic-based, utilizing Prolog-like rules for defining reactions and resources.

Technologically, both TuCSoN and ReSpecT are available as a Java project that requires JVM 8 or higher and is distributed under the GNU Lesser Public License. The paper confirms successful testing of these technologies against provided examples.

Additionally, the paper introduces 2CL, a methodology and toolkit for designing commitment-based interaction protocols among agents. These protocols consist of actions with shared meanings shaped by context, influencing agents' behavior. 2CL emphasizes behavior-oriented commitment protocols that encompass both constitutive and regulative rules, enhancing the understanding of agent interactions in MAS.
