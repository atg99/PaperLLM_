This paper defines agents as computational abstractions that encapsulate control alongside a task or goal, while multi-agent systems (MAS) are collections of these agents that interact within a computational framework. Logic, rooted in Aristotle's foundational principles, is described as the study of reasoning and formal expression. The focus of the paper is on logic-based technologies for MAS, emphasizing the importance of these technologies in engineering intelligent systems.

The authors argue that for agent-based technologies to be effective, they must be scientifically identifiable and technically accessible. This includes having a clear scientific publication detailing the technology, which must also be available through a public repository or website at the time of the survey. The paper specifies that logic-based MAS technologies must involve a well-defined logic model and demonstrate technological realization.

The discussion highlights that technological aspects are crucial, as they represent the application of scientific knowledge for practical purposes. The paper particularly focuses on technologies that make logic and agent-orientation explicit to users, such as Jason, which is built on the Belief-Desire-Intention architecture, and Golog, a Prolog-based programming tool for MAS. The criteria for including technologies in the survey are that they must have a sound scientific basis, be executable, be accessible for testing, and not be found solely on web archive services, indicating a lack of active maintenance.
