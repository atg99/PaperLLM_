This section discusses technologies and methodologies for modeling and verifying multi-agent systems (MAS). It specifically highlights two model checkers: SPINdle and MCK.

SPINdle is a Java-based project available on SourceForge, designed to represent various elements within MAS through derivation rules, facts, queries, and integrity constraints. Although it has not been maintained since 2017, both source code and binaries are accessible under the GNU General Public License. SPINdle has been tested successfully with .dlf files included in the pre-compiled binaries.

MCK, developed in 2004, is another model checker that focuses on the logic of knowledge in MAS. It automates formal analysis of MAS by examining how agents' information states evolve over time. MCK can verify specifications expressed in temporal-epistemic logics, utilizing techniques based on Ordered Binary Decision Diagrams (OBDD) and SAT solvers. The input for MCK includes a system design and a formal question regarding time, probability, and knowledge. It accommodates various semantics for agent knowledge, depending on their observation capabilities. Additionally, it supports both linear and branching time temporal expressiveness. MCK is available solely as a web service, with no provided source or binary code, and has been successfully tested against its web service examples.

MCMAS, introduced in 2006, is another model checker that utilizes various agent-based logics to specify MAS. It employs symbolic representation of state spaces and algorithms for computing epistemic operators, which capture private and group knowledge along with Alternating-time Temporal Logic (ATL). MCMAS also relies on OBDDs for its computations.

Overall, these technologies contribute significantly to the formal verification and analysis of multi-agent systems, ensuring reliability and correctness in their operations.
