The paper discusses various technologies within the sub-category of "Organisational reasoning & coordination" in the context of Autonomous Agents and Multi-Agent Systems (MAS). The highlighted systems include ALIAS, 2CL, TuCSoN, ReSpecT, and AORTA, each contributing to agent coordination and reasoning processes. 

In agent societies, argumentation is essential for reaching agreements and adapting to changes, especially in managing disputes and conflicts. The paper underscores the complexity of reasoning tasks based on Dung's abstract argumentation frameworks, necessitating efficient algorithms and solvers. The International Competition on Computational Models of Argumentation (ICCMA) serves as a platform for empirical comparisons of these solvers, which predominantly utilize logic-based programming.

ALIAS, introduced in 1999, employs abductive logic programming to facilitate reasoning in a multi-agent environment through a distributed protocol that coordinates agents' abductive reasoning. It maintains a dynamic global knowledge base, though its technological implementation is currently unmaintained.

TuCSoN and ReSpecT, developed in 1999 and 2001 respectively, also contribute to this discourse by providing frameworks for agent interaction and communication. The paper emphasizes the need for verification of MAS from a social perspective, leading to the inclusion of systems like MCK, SALMA, SCIFF, MCMAS, and Trace Expressions in the "MAS reliability & verification" sub-category. 

Overall, the paper highlights the importance of argumentation, coordination, and verification in the effective functioning of multi-agent systems.
