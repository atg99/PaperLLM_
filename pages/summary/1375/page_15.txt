The paper discusses two agent programming languages: DALI and eXAT, focusing on their functionalities, logical frameworks, and technological aspects.

From a Multi-Agent Systems (MAS) perspective, DALI is designed to create executable logical specifications for agents, using reactive and proactive rules. Reactive rules respond to environmental events, while proactive rules allow agents to act independently. The DALI inference engine employs an extended resolution form, and its semantics, referred to as "evolutionary semantics," reinterprets DALI rules as standard Horn Clause rules.

Technologically, DALI relies on the SICStus Prolog system and is available on GitHub under the Apache License. However, the authors faced challenges in verifying its functionality due to insufficient guidance on example usage.

Conversely, eXAT, based on the Erlang programming language, integrates high-level abstractions and libraries for agent programming. It combines logical and functional paradigms, allowing for the expression of agent programs through Erlang functions. eXAT features a rule production system called ERESYE, enabling concurrent rule engines that contribute to an agent's mental state. The platform supports coordination among Erlang processes, enhancing its functionality.

eXAT is available on GitHub as an unmaintained project under the GNU General Public License, but the authors encountered compilation issues due to a missing dependency.

In summary, both DALI and eXAT are platforms for agent programming with distinct logical frameworks and technological implementations, although both face maintenance and usability challenges.
