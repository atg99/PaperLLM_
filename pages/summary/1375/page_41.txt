The paper discusses various technologies used in autonomous agents and multi-agent systems, focusing on their licensing, target runtime platforms, and operational status. It highlights the significance of licenses, specifically the Mozilla Public License and the Apache License, and notes the potential implications for users when technologies lack licenses. 

The analysis includes an examination of the target runtime platforms for each technology, with a particular emphasis on the Java Virtual Machine (JVM), which is the most commonly targeted platform. The paper introduces a notation system, “JVM N+,” to indicate the compatibility of technologies with different JVM versions. Other platforms mentioned include native operating systems, Common Lisp, Python, Erlang, and specific Prolog implementations.

The methodology for testing the technologies involves launching them on their respective platforms and checking for errors or crashes. If a technology runs without issues, further assessments are made by running benchmarks or examples provided in their documentation. A question mark in the results indicates either a lack of information or an inability to execute the technology.

From the aggregated data, it is revealed that only 12 out of 47 technologies have been actively maintained since 2019, representing 25.53% of the total. This observation raises concerns about the longevity and reliability of the technologies assessed.
