The paper discusses the structure and dynamics of Multi-Agent Systems (MAS), emphasizing the interplay between agents, society, and the environment as foundational categories for understanding MAS. This perspective, advocated by Omicini and supported by various models, infrastructures, and methodologies, organizes the presentation of technologies in MAS.

The section outlines a schema for categorizing technologies into three main areas: Agent, Society, and Environment. Each category includes specific sub-categories with a chronological presentation of relevant technologies, accompanied by descriptions, primary references, and URLs to project home pages. The technologies are analyzed from both MAS and logical perspectives to address key research questions. 

In the "Agent" category, the focus is on logic-based agent programming languages that facilitate societal interactions and environmental engagements, though they primarily concentrate on the agents themselves. For instance, Jason is highlighted as a BDI logic technology, primarily recognized for its Belief-Desire-Intention (BDI) paradigm, despite its capability to handle logic programming through a Prolog interpreter.

Overall, the paper provides a comprehensive framework for studying MAS technologies, integrating multiple perspectives to enhance understanding and research in the field.
