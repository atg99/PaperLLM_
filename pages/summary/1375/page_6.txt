The paper discusses the methodology for conducting a systematic literature review (SLR) on logic-based technologies for multi-agent systems (MAS). It outlines the process of extensive searching through various scientific literature sources, including Google Scholar, IEEE Xplore, ScienceDirect, SpringerLink, DBLP, and the ACM Digital Library. Keyword-based queries are employed to identify relevant papers, focusing on multiple aspects of MAS logic, such as implementation, verification, programming, reasoning, and their societal and environmental impacts.

The identified papers are categorized into three classes: primary papers that introduce and describe relevant technologies, auxiliary papers that mention these technologies in their contributions, and secondary papers that survey the technologies. Secondary papers are particularly valuable, as their bibliographies are recursively searched for additional relevant works. The paper emphasizes that while the secondary works serve a similar purpose, they lack systematic review methodologies, which this paper aims to provide.

The authors also highlight the unique contribution of their work, which includes a detailed technological assessment of the identified logic-based technologies for MAS. They aim to present an updated and comprehensive understanding of these technologies, differentiating their approach from previous works that either lacked completeness or did not focus on technology analysis. Overall, the paper aspires to represent the current state of usable logic-based technologies in the engineering of intelligent systems.
