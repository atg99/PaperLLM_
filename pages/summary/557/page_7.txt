This paper presents the performance evaluation of various Auto-DeepLab model variants on the Cityscapes and PASCAL VOC 2012 datasets, focusing on their effectiveness in semantic segmentation tasks. The results are summarized in tables that highlight metrics such as Multi-Adds, parameters, and mean Intersection over Union (mIOU) percentages.

In the Cityscapes validation set results (Table 2), the Auto-DeepLab models exhibit substantial improvements in mIOU as the filter multiplier (F) increases, with Auto-DeepLab-L achieving an mIOU of 80.33%. The models were trained from scratch using a polynomial learning rate schedule with a significant crop size and batch normalization adjustments. The training iterations impact performance, with an increase from 500K to 1.5M iterations resulting in an improvement of 2.8%.

Table 4 compares test set results, revealing that Auto-DeepLab-L, without pretraining, surpasses other models like FRRN-B and GridNet by notable margins. When using coarse annotations, Auto-DeepLab-L achieves an mIOU of 82.1%, matching DeepLabv3+ while requiring significantly fewer Multi-Adds. The lightweight variant, Auto-DeepLab-S, also performs competitively with just 10.15M parameters.

For the PASCAL VOC 2012 dataset, results indicate that Auto-DeepLab-L significantly outperforms previous models, with multi-scale inference yielding additional performance gains. The paper emphasizes the effectiveness of the Scheduled Drop Path method, which further enhances performance.

Overall, the findings demonstrate that the Auto-DeepLab models are highly efficient for semantic segmentation, achieving high accuracy while maintaining a lightweight architecture.
