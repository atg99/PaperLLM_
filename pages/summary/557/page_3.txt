This paper discusses advancements in neural architecture search (NAS) specifically aimed at optimizing network backbones for semantic segmentation tasks. The authors demonstrate that their approach achieves state-of-the-art results on the Cityscapes dataset without relying on ImageNet pretraining, outperforming existing frameworks such as FRRN and GridNet.

The paper highlights the evolution of NAS methods, contrasting traditional approaches that require extensive computational resources with newer strategies like progressive search and architectural sharing, which significantly reduce search costs. The authors build upon differentiable NAS formulations, proposing a two-level hierarchical architecture search space that includes both a cell level and a network level.

At the cell level, the authors adopt a directed acyclic graph structure, where each cell consists of multiple blocks with defined input selections, output types, and combination methods. They outline a set of possible layer types, including various convolutional and pooling operations, and specify that element-wise addition is the sole combination operator.

For the network level, the authors introduce a novel search space that enables joint exploration of both the architectural cells and the overarching network structure. This approach contrasts with previous works that fixed the network design after determining cell structures. The proposed method aims to optimize the entire architecture holistically, enhancing performance on large-scale segmentation datasets such as Cityscapes, PASCAL VOC 2012, and ADE20K.

In summary, the paper presents a comprehensive framework for NAS that efficiently searches across both cell and network levels, leading to improved architectures for semantic segmentation tasks without the need for extensive pretraining.
