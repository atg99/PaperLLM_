The paper presents the Auto-DeepLab architecture, which was optimized for semantic image segmentation using a Hierarchical Neural Architecture Search on the Cityscapes dataset. The architecture consists of 12 layers and 5 blocks per cell, resulting in a vast search space of approximately 10^19 unique paths. Key components include atrous and depthwise-separable convolutions, with the Atrous Spatial Pyramid Pooling module simplified to three branches during the search.

The architecture search was conducted using random image crops from half-resolution Cityscapes images over 40 epochs, utilizing a batch size of 2 due to GPU memory constraints. The optimization process involved the SGD optimizer for network weights and the Adam optimizer for architecture parameters, with a careful adjustment of learning rates and weight decay. The results showed a steady improvement in validation accuracy throughout the process, confirming the effectiveness of the search strategy.

The best-found architecture emphasizes maintaining higher resolutions in the early stages and downsampling in the first three-quarters of the layers, followed by upsampling in the final quarter. The combination of atrous and depthwise-separable convolutions indicates a learned importance of contextual information for segmentation tasks. The architecture was evaluated across multiple datasets, including Cityscapes, PASCAL VOC 2012, and ADE20K, demonstrating its robustness in semantic segmentation applications.
