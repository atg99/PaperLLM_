In "Auto-DeepLab: Hierarchical Neural Architecture Search for Semantic Image Segmentation," the authors explore the application of Neural Architecture Search (NAS) to the task of semantic image segmentation. While previous approaches primarily focused on optimizing the repeatable cell structures while manually designing the outer network structure, this paper proposes a hierarchical search that includes both the cell-level and network-level architectures. This approach addresses the complexities inherent in dense image prediction tasks.

The authors introduce a network-level search space that encompasses various popular designs and present a gradient-based architecture search method that is computationally efficient, requiring only three P100 GPU days on Cityscapes images. The effectiveness of Auto-DeepLab, the architecture specifically designed for semantic segmentation, is demonstrated through its performance on challenging datasets such as Cityscapes, PASCAL VOC 2012, and ADE20K, achieving state-of-the-art results without relying on ImageNet pretraining.

The paper emphasizes the significance of advancing NAS beyond image classification, highlighting the unique challenges and requirements of semantic segmentation. The proposed hierarchical architecture search not only enhances performance but also showcases the potential of automated architecture design in complex computer vision tasks.
