The paper discusses the advancements in high voltage Schottky barrier diodes (SBDs) made from bulk GaN substrates, highlighting significant milestones achieved since 2010. Initially developed by Sumitomo Electric Industries, these diodes exhibited a breakdown voltage (BV) exceeding 1 kV, with further optimizations leading to an impressive electron mobility of 930 cm²/Vs and an ultra-low specific on-resistance (R on,sp) of 0.71 mΩcm². The implementation of a field plate termination allowed for a BV of 1100 V with only a 5 µm thick undoped GaN layer, resulting in a figure of merit (BFOM) of 1.7 GW/cm².

The study also presents large-area SBDs with a Schottky electrode area of 1.1 × 1.1 mm², achieving a forward current of 6 A at 1.46 V and a R on,sp of 0.84 mΩcm² while maintaining a high BV of 600 V, indicating their potential for commercial scalability. Comparative analysis with silicon fast recovery diodes (FRDs) and silicon carbide (SiC) SBDs demonstrated that GaN SBDs exhibited superior performance in terms of reverse recovery time, charge, and losses.

Further improvements in bulk GaN substrate quality and MOCVD growth optimizations yielded SBDs capable of forward currents of 50 A at 2.05 V, sustaining a BV of 790 V. These 3 × 3 mm² SBDs featured mesa termination with a field plate to enhance BV, alongside an electron mobility of 1200 cm²/Vs and a low differential ON-resistance of 25-29 mΩ. The results indicate promising enhancements in the electrical characteristics and reliability of GaN SBDs for future applications.
