The paper discusses the impact of scaling the gate length from 1 µm to 0.25 µm on reducing resistive losses in power semiconductor devices, particularly in GaN devices. It highlights that for high breakdown voltage devices, the resistive contribution from the gate-drain access region becomes significant, and the on-resistance (R_on) scales with the breakdown voltage. The use of GaN devices with an ideal breakdown field of 3.3 MV/cm offers substantial improvements in on-resistance compared to previous reports of 1 MV/cm, emphasizing the importance of optimizing the breakdown field to minimize resistive losses.

The paper also addresses switching losses, which can limit the efficiency of switching mode power converters. It introduces the gate charge (Q_G) as a parameter to quantify switching losses, utilizing a simple circuit model that incorporates parasitic capacitances. This model aids in understanding the relationship between gate voltage and gate current, providing insights into the efficiency of the device. Overall, the paper underlines the critical factors influencing resistive and switching losses in GaN HEMTs, contributing to advancements in power semiconductor technologies.
