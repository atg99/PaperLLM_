The section discusses the converse piezoelectric effect in polar materials like GaN. When mechanical stress is applied, these materials generate a potential, known as the direct piezoelectric effect. Conversely, applying an external voltage can cause expansion or contraction, termed the converse piezoelectric effect. This phenomenon is not merely theoretical; experimental measurements using interferometric techniques on GaN crystals demonstrate it. For instance, a 2.5 µm thick single crystal GaN film shows a displacement of 0.05 nm at an electric field of 64000 V/cm, escalating to 1.56 nm at 2 MV/cm. In high power operation of HEMT devices, the electric field can approach or exceed these values, raising concerns about potential damage and reliability due to displacement levels. While localized electric fields alone may not induce displacement, the resulting piezoelectric stress can reach hundreds of MPa.
