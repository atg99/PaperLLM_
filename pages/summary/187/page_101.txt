The paper discusses advancements in GaN-on-Si p-i-n diodes, focusing on strategies to mitigate current crowding and improve performance. A device termination scheme utilizing ion implantation and an anode field plate significantly reduced leakage current. Traditional quasi-vertical structures resulted in non-uniform current distribution, prompting Zou et al. to develop a fully-vertical p-i-n diode by removing the Si substrate beneath the GaN layers, achieving an on-resistance (Ron,sp) of 3.3 mΩcm2 and a breakdown voltage (BV) of 350 V, with stable operation up to 175 °C. Zhang et al. improved this design, reporting a Ron,sp of 1 mΩcm2 and a BV of 500 V, alongside a low reverse recovery time of 50 ns and thermal stability up to 300 °C. Mase et al. introduced a novel method using a conductive n-type Si substrate and a strained superlattice to manage edge dislocations, yielding a Ron,sp of 7.4 mΩcm2 and a BV of 288 V. A detailed analysis by Zhang et al. highlighted the importance of the thickness and doping of the bottom n-GaN layer in reducing current crowding. High voltage diodes by Khadar et al. achieved a BV of 820 V and an ultra-low Ron,sp of 0.33 mΩcm2, setting a record for figure of merit (BFOM). The paper concludes with Zhang et al.'s selective Si removal method, achieving a Ron,sp of 0.35 mΩcm2 and a BV of 720 V, further enhancing the efficiency of GaN-on-Si diodes.
