The paper discusses the development of 1.2-kV GaN vertical power FinFETs, achieving a significant enhancement in switching performance. It highlights the structure of the GaN vertical FinFET and presents simulation results showing how electron density varies with different gate biases. At zero gate voltage, the device remains in the OFF state with low electron density, while increasing gate voltage leads to a peak in electron density at the Al2O3/GaN interface, indicating improved channel conductivity.

The paper also addresses the challenge of fin width, noting that for effective depletion, the fin must be narrower than 500 nm. However, this narrow width can compromise the current handling capabilities of the device. Initial findings from MIT show promising results with a threshold voltage of 1 V, specific on-resistance of 0.36 mΩcm², and a breakdown voltage of 800 V for a fin width of 450 nm.

This study contributes to the ongoing research in GaN-based power devices, particularly in enhancing performance metrics while addressing the limitations associated with device architecture.
