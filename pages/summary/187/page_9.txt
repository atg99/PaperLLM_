The paper discusses the advancements in GaN transistors designed for breakdown voltages exceeding 1 kV, highlighting their competitive potential against SiC devices in various applications including industrial, automotive, and photovoltaic sectors. It outlines that current GaN devices typically utilize a lateral layout, which poses challenges in optimizing performance due to factors such as gate-drain spacing affecting breakdown voltage and the impact of surface charges on the 2-dimensional electron gas (2DEG) density. 

The structure of a GaN-based High Electron Mobility Transistor (HEMT) is presented, emphasizing the formation of the 2DEG at the heterojunction between AlGaN and GaN layers. The challenges of growing GaN on a Si substrate, including thermal expansion and lattice mismatches that can lead to defects, are discussed. The paper notes that optimization of the buffer layer is essential to mitigate defect propagation towards the 2DEG.

To address the limitations of lateral structures, particularly when targeting high breakdown voltages and power densities, the paper suggests exploring vertical device structures, which may offer improved performance similar to advancements made in Si and SiC technologies. The paper sets the foundation for a detailed examination of device design, fabrication processes, and performance enhancements in subsequent sections.
