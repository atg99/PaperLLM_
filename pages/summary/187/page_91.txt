The paper discusses advancements in GaN trench MOSFET technology, highlighting the importance of surface treatment for achieving smooth vertical sidewalls, predominantly m-plane, which enhances channel mobility. Toyoda Gosei Corporation reported significant improvements in breakdown voltage (BV) and specific on-resistance (Ron,sp) for GaN trench MOSFETs, achieving a BV over 1.6 kV and Ron,sp of 12.1 mΩcm² in 2014, with further enhancements in 2015 where Ron,sp was reduced to 1.8 mΩcm² and BV reached 1.2 kV. These improvements were attributed to optimized doping and thickness of the p-GaN channel and i-GaN layers, minimizing scattering effects. In 2016, large area trench MOSFETs were developed with over 10 A on-state current and a high BV exceeding 1.2 kV, demonstrating that dislocation densities in bulk GaN substrates do not limit device performance. A hexagonal cell array design was utilized to increase current density and reduce Ron,sp. Recent developments included a vertical trench MOSFET with a current distribution layer (CDL) beneath the p-GaN, achieving a 1.17 times higher forward current density and 100 A for large area devices. Additionally, HRL Laboratories introduced a method to mitigate plasma etch damage by selectively regrowing n-GaN on p-GaN, resulting in a trench MOSFET with Ron,sp of 8.5 mΩcm² and a BV of 600 V. The paper concludes with an analysis of device parameters influenced by gate dielectric thickness, body layer doping, and cleaning processes.
