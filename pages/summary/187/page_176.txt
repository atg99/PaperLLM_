The paper discusses the effects of various types of irradiation on material degradation, particularly focusing on proton, neutron, electron, and gamma ray irradiation. 

Proton irradiation primarily causes displacement damage, which is recoverable through high-temperature annealing. Neutron irradiation results in an increase in threshold voltage and a decrease in material mobility, attributed to the rise in pre-existing acceptor states and consequent scattering. Neutrons are generally less damaging compared to protons regarding ON-resistance and breakdown voltage, causing extended damage rather than point defects.

Electron irradiation leads to a negative shift in threshold voltage at lower fluences, transitioning to a positive shift at higher fluences, primarily due to the generation of traps with varying charge states. A consistent decrease in channel mobility is also noted, with identified defects including nitrogen vacancies and traps with various activation energies.

Gamma ray irradiation effects are less thoroughly documented but include increased ON-resistance and negative shifts in threshold voltage. The findings underscore the differing mechanisms of damage and recovery associated with each type of irradiation.
