The paper discusses the degradation mechanisms in GaN power devices, particularly focusing on the impact of additional dielectrics such as silicon nitride used for passivation and field-plate isolation. The presence of weak spots correlating with electric field peaks is noted, along with threshold voltage shifts linked to defect generation that could impair dynamic performance. The degradation of dielectrics leads to increased gate leakage and noise, potentially causing catastrophic failures below the device's breakdown voltage. It is highlighted that the Weibull distribution's shape parameter β is less than 1, indicating that reliability issues stem from extrinsic factors, specifically the silicon nitride degradation. Numerical simulations reveal that during off-state conditions, the electric field peak occurs in the silicon nitride near the gate overhang, reaching approximately 6 MV/cm, which aligns with the theoretical breakdown field. In contrast, the electric field in the AlGaN barrier remains below 3 MV/cm, suggesting that degradation in this layer is unlikely. The paper proposes verifying these hypotheses through electroluminescence measurements and cross-sectional transmission electron microscopy analysis, showing that damage initiates at the gate edge and extends into the channel region.
