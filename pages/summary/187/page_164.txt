The paper discusses the electrical characteristics and breakdown behavior of various semiconductor structures, specifically focusing on C:GaN and AlN/Si stacks. It highlights that structures analyzed demonstrated breakdown voltages exceeding 800 V, with the C:GaN layer causing positive charge trapping due to hole accumulation at the GaN/AlGaN interface. This phenomenon results in the storage of positive charge within the buffer layer.

For the AlN/Si stacks, the leakage current is attributed to the series connection of AlN/n+-Si and n+-Si/p-Si junctions, influenced by an inversion layer at the interface. Numerical simulations confirm that the p-doping level of the Si substrate and the electric field at the interface are critical factors, as they govern the tunneling injection from the Si substrate into the AlN layer.

Additionally, the paper notes that the breakdown of (Al)GaN-on-Si is primarily due to high defect densities rather than avalanche effects, particularly in vertical devices.
