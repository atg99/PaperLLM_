The paper provides a detailed exploration of gallium nitride (GaN) and its applications in semiconductor technology. It begins with an introduction to GaN, followed by an examination of its properties and physical parameters. The discussion includes the role of polarization charges in GaN and the analysis of band diagrams and charge density in AlGaN/GaN heterostructures.

The paper then focuses on lateral GaN transistors, outlining their technology and operation. Various device architectures are discussed, along with approaches for achieving normally-off operation, such as cascode configurations, recessed gate MISHEMTs, fluorine-treated HEMTs, P-GaN gates, and tri-gate designs. The commercial perspective on these technologies is also considered.

Breakdown mechanisms in lateral GaN transistors are explored, along with strategies for improving breakdown voltage, including field plate structures, buffer optimization through super-lattice buffers, and local substrate removal. Future perspectives on AlGaN channel HEMTs, multi-channel devices, super junctions, and N-polar GaN HEMTs are also addressed.

The paper concludes with a section on vertical GaN device structures, explaining the advantages of this approach. Overall, the manuscript presents a comprehensive overview of GaN technology, highlighting both current advancements and future research directions.
