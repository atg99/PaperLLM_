The paper discusses various methods for characterizing traps in GaN High Electron Mobility Transistors (HEMTs), focusing on techniques such as stretched exponential fitting and on-the-fly characterization. The stretched exponential fitting method involves least-squares fitting of experimental data with stretched exponential functions, each representing different charge emission or capture processes. The equation provided illustrates how the fitted current is derived from these functions, with the number of transitions typically ranging from one to three. The Arrhenius plot is then constructed from the time constants at varying temperatures to analyze each process.

An alternative method mentioned is the analysis of the derivative of the device current with respect to the logarithm of time. This approach identifies peaks in the spectra, which correspond to characteristic time constants of traps, aiding in the determination of emission energies.

The paper also highlights the limitations of trap characterization methods that rely on the device being in the on-state, as this is necessary to observe current variations linked to charge emission processes. It discusses a specific trap level associated with carbon-doping that may emit charges during off-state conditions, making standard characterization challenging. The proposed on-the-fly characterization method allows the device to be periodically turned on while biased to promote charge emission, thus enabling the observation of current evolution and better characterization of such traps.
