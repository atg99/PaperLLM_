The section discusses the degradation of p-GaN gate stacks, which are utilized for normally-off operation in devices. Under typical operating conditions, significant voltage drops and electric fields in the thin p-GaN layer can lead to reliability concerns, particularly when a Schottky metal/p-GaN contact is present. Even below catastrophic failure levels, time-dependent degradation can occur, resulting in noise and sudden jumps in gate current. These jumps correspond to new electroluminescence spots at the gate edge, indicating defect generation and percolation processes similar to dielectric degradation.

Initial studies suggest that traps are formed due to impact ionization of electrons from the channel into the p-GaN region, driven by high electric fields. However, further validation through spectral electroluminescence measurements is necessary, as current observations show no evidence of band-to-band recombination, only bremsstrahlung and yellow luminescence.

Research by Tallarico et al. highlights that during stress, a significant voltage drop occurs in the depleted p-GaN region, contributing to the percolation process. The degradation mechanism shows an exponential relationship with stress voltage, exhibiting Weibull distribution characteristics. The degradation is concentrated at the gate edge, supported by electroluminescence measurements and numerical simulations indicating maximum electric field values at the gate edges.

Stoffels et al. proposed that under constant voltage stress, the p-GaN/AlGaN/GaN stack behaves like a positively biased pin diode, leading to current-dependent degradation and the formation of percolation paths. The characteristics of these paths are influenced by processing conditions.
