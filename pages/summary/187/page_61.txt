The paper discusses the fabrication and electrical characterization of AlGaN/GaN metal-insulator-semiconductor high-electron-mobility transistors (MISHEMTs) utilizing local substrate removal (LSR) techniques. The authors, E. Dogmus et al., detail the device processing steps, including the formation of ohmic contacts on the AlGaN barrier through rapid thermal annealing and the implementation of a metal-insulator-semiconductor gate structure with a Ni/Au metal stack atop an in-situ SiN cap layer. Following the front-side processing, the silicon substrate is locally etched to expose the AlN nucleation layer. 

The study compares devices fabricated with and without LSR, ensuring consistency in processing and epitaxial growth. Results indicate a slight decrease in maximum current density post-LSR, attributed to self-heating effects, highlighting the need for improved heat dissipation strategies. Conversely, a significant enhancement in blocking voltage is observed when the substrate is replaced with a wider bandgap material, demonstrating the potential advantages of the LSR technique in high-voltage applications. The findings emphasize the trade-offs between current density and voltage performance in the development of GaN-on-silicon MISHEMT technology.
