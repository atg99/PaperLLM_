The paper discusses the CID-DLTS/DLOS technique, which is designed to dynamically control either the gate or drain voltages of a device to maintain a constant drain current while observing thermally and optically stimulated trap emissions. In the gate-controlled method, a sufficiently high drain-source voltage (VDS) is applied to keep the device in saturation. The gate voltage (VGS) is then pulsed to induce trap filling, followed by a reduction near pinch-off conditions. This transient VGS response is sensitive to trap levels beneath the gate contact that influence the threshold voltage (VTH) of the device. Conversely, in the drain-controlled method, the device operates in its linear region with low VDS and a high enough VGS to minimize forward transconductance. The trap filling occurs at pinch-off conditions with a high VDS, and changes in device resistance (ΔRD) are recorded as a function of ΔVDS/IDS. This method provides insights into trap levels affecting device performance.
