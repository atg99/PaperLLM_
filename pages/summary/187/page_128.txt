The paper discusses various mechanisms contributing to threshold voltage (V T) instabilities in devices with p-GaN gates, particularly under positive gate bias (PGB) stress. Key phenomena include electron injection from the gate leading to capture by dielectric traps, hole-induced defect generation in the gate insulator, and Zener electron trapping in GaN near gate edges. The paper notes that PGB stress typically results in positive V T shifts due to channel electron injection into traps, while negative shifts occur from electron removal from dielectric traps. The stability of V T is crucial for normally-off operation of these devices. The author emphasizes the role of trap-related instabilities, particularly how electron injection into defects in the AlGaN barrier can cause positive V T shifts, while hole trapping at the p-GaN/AlGaN interface leads to negative shifts. The effects are visually represented in accompanying figures. The manuscript is a peer-reviewed version, with the final online record expected to differ post-editing.
