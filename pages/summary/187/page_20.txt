The paper discusses the phenomenon of spontaneous polarization in wurtzite structured materials, specifically Gallium Nitride (GaN), Indium Nitride (InN), and Aluminum Nitride (AlN). It highlights that the arrangement of sublattices can cause deviations from the ideal wurtzite position, which influences the direction of spontaneous polarization. The positive direction of this polarization is defined from Gallium (Ga) to the nearest nitrogen atom along the c-axis. 

The paper provides quantitative expressions for spontaneous polarization at interfaces between binary and ternary semiconductors, specifically for AlxGa1-xN/GaN, InxGa1-xN/GaN, and InxAl1-xN/GaN. These expressions indicate how polarization values vary with the composition of the materials. A figure illustrating the crystal structure of GaN and the associated polarization is also included to support the discussion. 

The findings underscore the importance of material composition in determining the properties of semiconductor interfaces and are relevant for applications in optoelectronic devices.
