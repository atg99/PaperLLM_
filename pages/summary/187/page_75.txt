This paper discusses the development of GaN p-n junction diodes, highlighting their evolution from initial reports in 2000 on sapphire substrates to significant advancements in 2011 with the introduction of high-voltage p-i-n diodes. The authors note the contributions of various startups and research institutions, including Avogy Inc. and Cornell University. Key advancements include the implementation of mesa termination with SiO2 passivation and field plate structures to enhance breakdown voltage (BV) from approximately 450 V to 1100 V, achieving a Baliga’s figure of merit (BFOM) exceeding 3.0 GW/cm2.

Further progress led to the first demonstration of GaN p-i-n diodes with BV over 3 kV in 2013. This was achieved through a low-damage field plate process that utilized a bilayer spin-on-glass/sputtered SiO2 dielectric, which protected the p-GaN anode contact area. The resulting low on-resistance (R on,sp) of 0.9 mΩcm2 combined with the high BV resulted in a record BFOM of 10 GW/cm2. The paper emphasizes the importance of these developments in enhancing the performance and reliability of GaN-based devices.
