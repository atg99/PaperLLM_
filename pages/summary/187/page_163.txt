The paper discusses the percolation process in lateral power devices, particularly focusing on buffer decomposition experiments to understand the impact of different layers on vertical breakdown voltage and device reliability. When the device is in the off-state, a voltage drop occurs across layers such as the AlN nucleation layer, AlGaN buffer, and carbon-doped GaN buffer. To evaluate the reliability and charge trapping effects of each layer, separate structures are tested after the growth of each layer on a silicon substrate.

The analysis reveals that AlN layers grown directly on silicon exhibit a breakdown field of 3.25 MV/cm, significantly lower than the 12 MV/cm observed in high-quality crystalline AlN. This reduced value is attributed to vertical leakage paths due to defects like V-pits and threading dislocations. The breakdown voltage decreases with temperature, correlating with increased leakage current, which suggests that localized defects can lead to premature breakdown.

Moreover, negative charge trapping in AlN/Si structures is linked to electron injection from the silicon substrate to deep traps in AlN. The introduction of an AlGaN layer above the AlN reduces defect density and promotes more consistent leakage currents, while adding a C:GaN layer further enhances breakdown voltage. The findings emphasize the importance of layer composition and defect management in improving the performance and reliability of lateral power devices.
