The section discusses the limitations of lateral GaN HEMTs, which are currently the most common GaN-based power devices. While these devices can handle breakdown voltages up to 900 V and output currents up to 150 A, their lateral structure presents challenges for high-voltage applications, such as in electric vehicles and renewable energy systems. The primary issues include reliability concerns and difficulties in scaling breakdown voltages beyond 700 V, primarily due to the lateral electron flow that is sensitive to surface traps. This sensitivity can lead to current collapse and performance degradation, particularly at higher breakdown voltages.

The lateral transport also results in an inhomogeneous electric field distribution, which can exacerbate electron trapping and cause premature breakdown, negatively impacting both forward and reverse performance. Additionally, the requirement for larger device sizes to achieve higher breakdown voltages increases costs. Furthermore, GaN HEMTs are typically normally-on devices, which can pose safety risks and complicate gate driver designs that are better suited for normally-off devices. Various methods have been explored to achieve normally-off operation, such as cascode configurations and fluorine ion implantation.
