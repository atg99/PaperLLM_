The paper discusses advancements in GaN super-junction devices and N-polar GaN HEMTs. Initially proposed in 2008, the development of super-junction devices faced challenges in matching the concentrations of two-dimensional electron gases (2DEG) and two-dimensional hole gases (2DHG). However, recent research has reignited interest, leading to a better understanding of their working principles and potential, suggesting rapid advancements in this field could emerge.

In the context of N-polar GaN HEMTs, the paper highlights the shift from traditional Ga-polar (0001) devices to N-polar (000-1) devices, which offer several advantages. These include a strong back-barrier from the AlGaN layer minimizing short-channel effects, low-resistivity ohmic contacts due to the 2DEG being above the barrier, and improved scalability with reduced gate-channel distances. Recent studies indicate that N-polar devices, previously focused on mm-wave applications, may also be suitable for power switching, achieving breakdown voltages over 2000 V with specific device architectures.
