Proton irradiation can significantly impact GaN-based devices by introducing new defects or increasing the concentration of existing ones due to interactions between the energetic protons and the crystal lattice. This defect concentration leads to decreased mobility as carriers scatter off defects, resulting in reduced carrier density from charge trapping and electrostatic effects. Consequently, device performance is affected, manifesting as increased on-resistance, a shift in threshold voltage, and decreased saturation current and peak transconductance.

Interestingly, some studies report a decrease in dynamic on-resistance following proton irradiation, attributed to increased off-state leakage indicating higher conductivity in unintentionally-doped GaN layers, which enhances deionization rates. The extent of damage is influenced by proton energy, with lower energy protons causing more significant damage due to their greater non-ionizing energy loss in key device regions, while higher energy protons tend to cause damage deeper within the structure.

Typical fluence thresholds for performance variation are noted at 1.8 MeV (10^12-10^13 p+/cm²) and at 40 MeV (5×10^9 p+/cm²), although the lower threshold at higher energy is unexpected and may be linked to differences in device structures and quality across studies. Additionally, proton irradiation specifically generates deep donor and acceptor levels, along with de-hydrogenation and reconfiguration of ON-H complexes.
