The paper discusses the robustness of transistors against electrostatic discharge (ESD) and electrical overstress (EOS) events, focusing on the effects of device structure and testing conditions. In forward conduction mode, high local power dissipation is identified as a critical factor. Various failure modes are noted, including melting of gate metal and formation of cracks in normally-ON devices, as well as damage at the drain side due to high electric fields during short events. 

If gate reliability is not a primary concern, devices can be tested without gate metal. The migration of contact metal leading to drain-source shorts is emphasized, particularly in devices without mesa isolation, where localized melting occurs at high power dissipation. The paper highlights that GaN-based devices exhibit cumulative effects under ESD testing, which typically employs a single pulse, and that testing configurations may not accurately reflect real-world conditions where gate voltage varies. 

The findings indicate that catastrophic failure occurs at consistent voltages during ESD events, regardless of the gate voltage, suggesting that failure is related to reaching the breakdown electric field of the material. This insight stresses the importance of analyzing ESD robustness across a range of gate voltages in three-terminal devices to better understand their performance in practical applications.
