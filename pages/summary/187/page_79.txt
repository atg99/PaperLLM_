The paper discusses advancements in the fabrication and performance of high-voltage vertical GaN p-n diodes. It identifies a high concentration of impurity atoms at the growth interface, attributed to a defective etching process. A solution proposed involves low power etching combined with UV-ozone and acid treatment of the etched surface before regrowth of p-GaN. Additionally, a novel edge termination scheme utilizing hydrogen plasma to passivate p-GaN around the anode region has been introduced, resulting in significant improvements in breakdown voltage.

Figures included illustrate the heterostructure for epitaxial liftoff devices and the cross-section of GaN-on-GaN p-n diodes with nitrogen implanted edge termination. Notable contributions by groups from the University of Notre Dame and Sandia National Laboratory highlight the successful application of nitrogen implantation for edge termination in p-i-n diodes, achieving an ultra-low on-resistance and a breakdown voltage of 1.68 kV, corresponding to a high Baliga's figure of merit. The paper also mentions the exploration of bevel edge termination as an alternative approach.
