The paper discusses the advancements in fin power FETs with a focus on their performance characteristics and challenges. The devices operate through electron accumulation rather than inversion, achieving a high electron mobility of 150 cm2/Vs in the accumulation layer. Zhang et al. optimized the fin width, resulting in a breakdown voltage (BV) of 1200 V and a specific on-resistance (Ron,sp) of 0.2 mΩcm2. They also demonstrated large area devices with a current capability of 10 A and a BV of 800 V. 

Switching characteristics of these fin power FETs were compared to commercial Si and SiC power transistors, showing superior performance with lower capacitances and gate charges, along with high-frequency switching capabilities. However, the devices are prone to catastrophic failure potentially due to the lack of p-GaN layers to manage electric field peaks. Additionally, the complexity of controlling the fin width during fabrication poses challenges compared to other vertical devices.

The threshold voltage is noted to be around 1V, necessitating a well-designed gate driver for safe operation. Recent proposals for fin-FET and nanowire structures incorporating a p-type layer in a vertical stack aim to achieve robust normally-off operation, particularly under gate-stress conditions. The findings indicate that integrating a p-type layer is a promising strategy for improving device reliability.
