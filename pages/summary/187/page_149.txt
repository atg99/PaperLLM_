The paper discusses the degradation processes of electronic devices, highlighting that while degradation accelerates at higher temperatures, complex interactions suggest a different dynamic. Specifically, the study identifies that the accumulation of positive charges at the p-GaN/AlGaN interface enhances degradation by promoting electron injection from the channel into the p-GaN. Higher temperatures facilitate the release of holes, which can improve reliability. The phenomenon of hole trapping is evidenced by a decrease in gate current during stress, attributed to electrostatic repulsion between injected and trapped holes. Additionally, the paper notes that at elevated temperatures, carrier acceleration and impact ionization are diminished due to increased scattering, contributing to a positive temperature coefficient. The findings are illustrated in a figure that correlates electroluminescence intensity with gate current.
