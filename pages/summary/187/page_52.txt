The paper discusses the operational characteristics of normally-off HEMTs with a p-GaN gate, focusing on the effects of the p-GaN layer above the AlGaN barrier and GaN channel, which forms a p-i-n diode. In the on-state, this diode can activate, leading to increased gate current, which is influenced by the metal/p-GaN interface properties. The study highlights that most single-metal contacts to p-GaN exhibit a Schottky-like behavior due to the wide band gap and high electron affinity of GaN, making true ohmic contacts challenging. The work functions of common metals like Pt, Pd, and Ni are insufficient to achieve ohmic behavior. Consequently, the conductivity at the metal/p-GaN interface is contingent upon the materials' characteristics formed during deposition and annealing. The paper also notes that the choice of metal and doping levels can result in varying leakage currents, with recent advancements indicating that TIN contacts can significantly reduce gate leakage. Additionally, the paper introduces tri-gate transistors, which incorporate fins in the gate region and are covered by a 3D gate electrode, providing advantages over conventional planar designs.
