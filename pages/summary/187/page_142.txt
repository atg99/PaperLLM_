The paper discusses degradation processes in Gallium Nitride (GaN) devices, highlighting the diverse types and structures of these devices and their various applications. It reviews significant degradation and failure modes, categorized by operating conditions relevant to both lateral and vertical devices. 

The initial focus is on ON-state and OFF-state bias conditions, essential for power devices, followed by an examination of the transition between these states, which can lead to additional degradation during the semi-ON state. The paper also addresses reliability in realistic environments, including the impact of electrostatic discharge (ESD), electrical overstress (EOS), and radiation hardness.

In the ON-state section, the paper emphasizes the challenges posed by high gate overdrive, which is necessary to enhance channel carrier density and maximize current levels. However, this condition results in high voltage drops and electric fields across the gate stack, potentially leading to significant power dissipation and self-heating, which can adversely affect long-term performance. The role of dielectrics in extrinsic degradation is also discussed, noting the risks associated with exceeding the breakdown value of the gate dielectric.
