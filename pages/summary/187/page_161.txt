The paper discusses the degradation mechanisms of AlGaN/GaN high electron mobility transistors (HEMTs) under reverse gate bias, highlighting the effects of environmental factors such as water and hydroxyl groups. The study presents scanning electron microscopy (SEM) images that illustrate the structural changes in the HEMT over time, including the formation of stringers and pits. It emphasizes that the degradation occurs due to anodic oxidation of the AlGaN layer, which is facilitated by an electrochemical reaction involving the gate metal and the passivation layer. The main consequences of this degradation are a reduction in drain current and dynamic performance, while the increase in gate leakage is not attributed to the observed pits. The findings underscore the importance of understanding these degradation processes for improving the reliability of HEMTs.
