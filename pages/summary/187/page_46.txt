The paper discusses the factors affecting the threshold voltage (V TH) in AlGaN/GaN high electron mobility transistors (HEMTs). It highlights that increasing the thickness of AlGaN barriers and enhancing polarization differences between AlGaN and GaN results in a more negative V TH, which increases the two-dimensional electron gas (2DEG) density at zero bias. The authors note various parameters that can be adjusted to tune V TH, such as the Schottky barrier height and the carrier density linked to the AlGaN layer's aluminum content and thickness.

Several topologies for achieving normally-off GaN HEMTs are presented, including a cascode configuration, fluorine implantation under the gate, gate recessed metal-insulator-semiconductor HEMTs, and p-GaN gated HEMTs. 

The cascode configuration, which combines a normally-on GaN HEMT with a low-voltage Si MOSFET, is detailed as a reliable switching circuit. In this setup, the Si MOSFET controls the switching of the GaN HEMT. When a positive gate voltage is applied to the MOSFET, the GaN HEMT remains off, allowing for safe operation. Conversely, when the MOSFET is off, current cannot flow through the HEMT, ensuring high reliability when high drain voltages are applied.
