The paper discusses various trapping mechanisms affecting the performance of GaN transistors, particularly focusing on two-dimensional electron gas (2DEG) and buffer traps. It highlights how barrier traps in the gate-drain access region can lead to a positive shift in threshold voltage or increased drain access resistance, influenced by their location. The ionization of donor-like barrier traps during drain voltage sweeps can cause an increase in output conductance, known as the "kink effect." 

In normally-off p-GaN high electron mobility transistors (HEMTs), hole trapping occurs at the p-GaN/AlGaN interface under positive gate bias. The paper identifies buffer traps as a significant source of trapping effects in modern GaN transistors, especially following advancements in surface passivation. Various mechanisms for buffer trap effects are discussed, including electron leakage from source and gate contacts forming source-drain and gate-drain currents, and substrate leakage current contributing to trapping under off-state conditions. 

Under semi-on and power-state bias conditions, energetic 2DEG electrons can penetrate the buffer and become trapped. Additional mechanisms include the capture of valence-band electrons through Zener processes and trap impact ionization leading to the discharge of traps at the barrier/buffer interface, which also contributes to the kink effect. The findings emphasize the complexity of trapping mechanisms in GaN transistors and their impact on device performance.
