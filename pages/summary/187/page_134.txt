The paper discusses the influence of device bias conditions on trap occupancy and the resulting detection of Deep Capture Transitions (DCTs) during emission processes. It highlights that variations in bias points can lead to different trap behaviors. For trap characterization, several methods are proposed, including drain current Deep Level Transient Spectroscopy (ID-DLTS), constant drain-current DLTS/DLOS, and pulsed drain-current methodologies. 

ID-DLTS involves applying a constant drain-source voltage while pulsing the gate between filling and sensing conditions to monitor current transients across a temperature range. This process allows for the identification of trap levels through detected peaks in DLTS spectra. The activation energy of these traps can be determined using an Arrhenius plot. Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of bias conditions in understanding trap dynamics within semiconductor devices.
