The introduction highlights the emergence of gallium nitride (GaN) as a superior material for power semiconductor devices over the past decade. GaN diodes and transistors outperform silicon counterparts, making them suitable for next-generation power converters due to their flexibility and energy efficiency. The tutorial aims to review the physical properties, operating principles, fabrication parameters, and reliability issues of GaN-based power transistors.

GaN devices enable the production of high-efficiency power converters, achieving efficiencies near or above 99%. These converters can operate at switching frequencies exceeding 1 MHz, with potential frequencies of 40-75 MHz through advanced design and integration techniques. High-frequency operation reduces the size and weight of necessary components, leading to more compact converter designs. Innovations include monolithically integrated all-GaN circuits, which allow for smart control and rapid switching capabilities, benefiting applications in portable electronics, automotive, and avionics.

As a wide-bandgap semiconductor with a 3.4 eV energy gap, GaN supports operation at high temperatures, improving power density and allowing the use of smaller heat sinks. Numerous studies have demonstrated the high-temperature stability of GaN high electron mobility transistors (HEMTs), with operational temperatures reaching above 400-500 °C.
