The paper discusses the sensitivity of devices to traps located in access regions, highlighting that the detection of peaks in temperature or optical spectra indicates the presence of a trap level, from which activation energy can be derived using an Arrhenius plot. It emphasizes the benefits of multiple DCTs (Deep Capture Transients) measurements, which reduce the number of required evaluations by assessing DCTs at several temperature levels. The technique is noted for its simplicity and applicability to actual transistors, though it requires voltage pulse generators or load-line drain biasing for switching between filling and sensing conditions. The results from multiple DCTs can vary significantly based on device bias conditions and the analysis method used for Arrhenius plot extraction. Further details on bias conditions and DCT analysis are promised in subsequent sections. The paper includes a figure illustrating the device switching system and acknowledges the source of the figure as a prior publication.
