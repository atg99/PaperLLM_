The paper discusses the limitations of Deep Level Transient Spectroscopy (DLTS) in characterizing impurities with high activation energies, which can result in low emission rates and large time constants that may be masked by slow drifts. To address these issues, a novel technique called Deep-Level Optical Spectroscopy (DLOS) has been introduced. DLOS utilizes photostimulated capacitance transients after electrical, thermal, or optical excitation to extract information about deep levels, including ionization energies, electron-phonon interactions, and temperature dependencies.

In the context of Gallium Nitride (GaN) materials, both DLTS and DLOS are used for characterization. DLTS is effective for levels with activation energies below approximately 1 eV, while DLOS is applied to deeper levels, allowing for a comprehensive investigation of trap levels across the entire GaN band-gap.

The paper also highlights that while DLTS and DLOS are typically used on large area diodes for better capacitance measurements, their application to three-terminal devices is limited due to lower capacitance values. The electric field distribution in transistors under normal bias conditions differs from that in large area diodes, potentially limiting the insight these techniques provide into the dynamic operation of actual devices. The study emphasizes the importance of drain current transients (DCTs) in monitoring the effects of trapped charge emission on device performance, as the emission of trapped electrons can increase the two-dimensional electron gas (2DEG) concentration and, consequently, the drain current.
