The paper discusses the characteristics and implications of avalanche operation in GaN devices, which exhibit lower dislocation densities compared to GaN-on-Si, allowing them to withstand higher electric fields and reach avalanche conditions. The impact ionization coefficients for GaN have been estimated by several studies, highlighting variations in coefficients based on electric field strength.

Avalanche operation is portrayed as a beneficial feature for power diodes, enabling them to endure surge events. However, the paper cautions that non-optimized structures may suffer reliability issues during avalanche operation due to high electric fields and energetic carriers, which can lead to lattice damage and device failure.

The involvement of carbon in nitrogen substitutional positions as a defect species during avalanche operation is hypothesized. These defects can lead to increased series resistance, leakage currents, altered turn-on voltage, and changes in avalanche breakdown voltage, complicating the analysis due to variable vertical electric fields. The paper emphasizes the need for careful optimization of device structures to mitigate these potential issues while leveraging the advantages of avalanche operation.
