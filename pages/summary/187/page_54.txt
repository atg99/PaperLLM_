The paper discusses advancements in normally-off GaN devices, particularly focusing on the challenges and solutions related to threshold voltage (VTH) and on-resistance (RON). Initially, the use of small fin widths in tri-gate devices resulted in negative VTH and degraded RON. Recent developments demonstrated a tri-gate device with 20 nm-wide fins and a large work-function gate metal stack that achieved a VTH of 0.64 V and a competitive RON of 7.4 Ω·mm. However, achieving this performance requires high-resolution lithographic processes, which are currently limited in power device foundries.

To address these challenges, the paper suggests combining the tri-gate architecture with conventional methods such as recessed gate and p-GaN cap to achieve higher VTH values while reducing lithographic demands. Notable demonstrations include the integration of tri-gate architecture with AlGaN barrier recess, yielding a VTH of 1.4 V and RON of 7.3 Ω·mm. Additionally, combining tri-gate architecture with pGaN caps enhances VTH and mitigates trade-offs related to sheet resistance.

From a commercial perspective, the paper notes that existing GaN normally-off devices primarily utilize cascode or pGaN technologies. Cascode devices provide high reliability and robustness, gaining automotive certifications, but may complicate packaging due to the introduction of low-voltage Si devices. In contrast, pGaN devices offer a simpler fabrication process but may face limitations in gate swing. The potential of gate recess is highlighted as a method to improve performance while navigating the trade-offs associated with pGaN technology.
