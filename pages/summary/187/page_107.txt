This paper discusses the impact of charge-trapping processes in Gallium Nitride (GaN) transistors, particularly in the context of power RF amplifiers and power switching converters. It emphasizes the significance of deep-level traps in GaN and their effects on performance metrics such as output power and power-added efficiency. The paper outlines the various factors influencing trapping processes, including the spatial position of traps, their parameters, the type of carriers involved, and the associated charging and discharging pathways.

Key detrimental effects of charge trapping are identified, including current collapse, dynamic on-state resistance increase, threshold voltage instabilities, and the kink effect. These phenomena illustrate both the challenges posed by traps and the potential for probing them. The paper further details common trapping mechanisms by correlating trap locations with their charging and discharging paths, and it assesses the implications of these effects for practical applications.

Additionally, the paper reviews various measurement methods developed to quantitatively characterize traps in GaN structures and transistors, providing a comprehensive understanding of this complex topic. The structure of the paper is organized to first present the properties of common traps, followed by an exploration of trapping mechanisms, their effects, and finally, measurement techniques.
