The paper discusses the kink effect in RF amplifiers caused by various mechanisms, including impact ionization, trapping of holes via trap-assisted tunneling, and field-enhanced ionization of traps in the AlGaN barrier. These effects lead to transconductance compression and increased output conductance.

The section on trap characterization techniques outlines various methods used to investigate trapping phenomena. It begins with pulsed IV (PIV) characterization, which, while not providing quantitative activation energies, effectively reveals the presence of traps. Deep-Level Transient Spectroscopy and Deep-Level Optical Spectroscopy are highlighted for their ability to characterize trap levels across the GaN bandgap, though they require specific test structures that complicate direct application to transistors.

The paper further elaborates on monitoring device drain current to study emission processes related to traps. It emphasizes that traps emitting charge during the off-state can complicate characterization, but on-the-fly methods can address this issue. Additionally, capacitance-voltage (C-V) and conductance-voltage (G-V) measurements are mentioned for characterizing interface states, alongside a brief description of photoluminescence (PL) techniques. Overall, the paper provides a comprehensive overview of techniques for understanding trapping phenomena in semiconductor devices.
