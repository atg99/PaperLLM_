The paper discusses the challenges and advancements in growing high-quality GaN films, particularly focusing on substrate selection. Key substrates include sapphire, SiC, and Si, each with distinct advantages and disadvantages. Sapphire substrates are low cost and have high resistivity but suffer from low thermal conductivity, leading to self-heating issues unsuitable for power applications. SiC, while having excellent properties, is too expensive for large-volume use. Emerging GaN substrates, which are lattice-matched, show promise for vertical GaN architectures.

To achieve crack-free GaN films and reduce defect density, particularly on Si substrates, it is crucial to manage tensile stress during growth and cooling. The use of an AlN nucleation layer is highlighted as an effective strategy, preventing melt-back etching of Ga into Si and introducing a compressive strain that offsets the tensile stress from cooling. The paper includes a table comparing the properties of various substrates, detailing lattice constants, thermal conductivity, and lattice mismatch percentages. The findings underscore the importance of substrate choice in optimizing GaN film quality for electronic applications.
