The paper discusses the challenges associated with the growth of p-GaN using metal-organic chemical vapor deposition (MOCVD), including the Mg memory effect and hydrogen passivation of Mg dopants. To address these issues, high breakdown voltage (BV) GaN p-i-n diodes have been developed using molecular beam epitaxy (MBE) grown p-GaN. The study also presents an alternative approach for p-GaN regrowth via MOCVD, which may lead to impurity incorporation at the growth interface and complications related to non-planar growth, such as increased leakage currents.

Figures included in the study illustrate the SIMS profile of the regrown p-n junction, emphasizing the elevated levels of silicon (Si) and oxygen (O) atoms at the regrowth interface, as well as a schematic of GaN-on-GaN p-n diodes with hydrogen plasma-based edge termination.

Furthermore, research from Arizona State University highlights the advantages of growing a thick buffer layer (approximately 1 µm) on bulk GaN substrates before further growth. The regrowth of p-GaN layers on etched GaN surfaces is explored, noting that this process can lead to a slight rise in edge dislocations and a high concentration of Si and O impurities. The study underscores the complexity of ion implantation methods for junction termination extension structures in GaN, which are not yet fully developed compared to silicon and silicon carbide vertical power devices.
