The study focuses on the leakage current accumulation in Gallium Nitride (GaN) Schottky Barrier Diodes (SBDs) and the advancements made to mitigate initial failures attributed to this issue. Researchers identified that leakage paths formed by polygonal pits due to carbon impurity accumulation during growth affected performance at low voltages. They improved impurity levels in m-plane GaN, achieving values close to c-plane by employing a quartz-free flow channel. In 2019, they reported a vertical GaN SBD with a breakdown voltage (BV) of 2.4 kV for a 30 µm drift layer, observing a reduction in effective donor concentration correlated with increased drift layer thickness.

Recent work involved compensating for unintended n-type doping by introducing magnesium dopants during growth, leading to SBDs with over three times higher BV than non-compensated versions, although this resulted in higher resistivity affecting on-state current and specific on-resistance. Arizona State University explored double drift layer (DDL) configurations to balance specific on-resistance and BV, utilizing a top unintentionally-doped layer to suppress peak electric fields and enhance BV.

Several termination methods were introduced to further reduce leakage current. One method involved planar nitridation using N2 plasma, which lowered the Fermi level at the GaN surface and reduced thermionic field emission, resulting in a significant decrease in leakage current and an increase in BV from 335 V to 995 V. This termination scheme also demonstrated excellent switching performance. Another method included fluorine implantation, which acted as fixed negative ions to spread the electric field and prevent localized peaking, further enhancing device performance.
