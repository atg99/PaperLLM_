The paper discusses the critical role of buffer layers in GaN-based high electron mobility transistors (HEMTs), emphasizing their impact on device performance. It highlights that inadequate carrier confinement can lead to undesirable soft pinch-off characteristics and increased sub-threshold leakage, primarily due to high defect or impurity densities in the buffer. To mitigate these issues, a high-quality AlN nucleation layer (NL) and an appropriate buffer configuration are essential.

One effective strategy is the use of step-graded AlGaN buffers, which consist of several micrometers thick layers with varying aluminum content. This configuration helps alleviate thermal and lattice mismatch problems and improves two-dimensional electron gas (2DEG) confinement under high electric fields, thus reducing the risk of punch-through effects. Additionally, the introduction of intentional compensating centers, such as iron or carbon doping, can enhance the resistivity of buffer layers, with carbon being preferred for GaN applications. The findings underscore the importance of optimizing buffer layers for reliable and efficient HEMT devices.
