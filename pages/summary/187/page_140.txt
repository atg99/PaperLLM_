The paper discusses the characterization of interface traps at metal-insulator-semiconductor interfaces using capacitance-voltage (C-V) and conductance-voltage (G-V) measurements. It highlights that C-V measurements are effective for evaluating surface state density and energy distribution. The combination of C-V and G-V measurements allows for the calculation of interface state density (NSS) using the Nicollian-Goetzberger theory, though this method requires extensive data acquisition. An alternative single-frequency approximation method is presented for estimating NSS, which has been successfully applied to GaN-based devices. The paper notes that G-V measurements assume negligible energy losses and leakage currents in dielectrics, which can be problematic in AlGaN/GaN high electron mobility transistors (HEMTs) due to high leakage currents at the Schottky gate. High-frequency C-V measurements are typically used to address oxide leakage issues, but standard room temperature measurements may not be suitable for wide bandgap materials like GaN and AlGaN due to the long time constants of deep interface traps, necessitating elevated temperature measurements for accurate characterization. The discussion emphasizes the potential limitations of the conductance method for characterizing these interfaces.
