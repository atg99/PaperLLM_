The paper discusses the impact of stress conditions on power devices, highlighting the differences in degradation modes between DC and RF stress, with RF conditions resulting in more significant degradation. This finding emphasizes the necessity of examining degradation under real switching patterns, which accounts for the complex interactions between various mechanisms across different bias states.

In the OFF-state, power devices face high electric fields, particularly in critical regions, which can damage materials beyond the semiconductor itself, such as insulators. The paper also notes that the high electric field may interact with the external environment, leading to field-assisted chemical reactions and potential breakdown of GaN.

Additionally, the degradation of dielectrics is more pronounced under ON-state stress due to higher voltage drops across the gate insulator. However, in OFF-state stress, the voltage drop in the depleted semiconductor reduces the electric field across the gate stack, indicating that findings related to the reliability of engineered bilayer insulators remain relevant under these conditions.
