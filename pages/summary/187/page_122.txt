The paper discusses the role of buffer traps in managing unintentional n-type conductivity in nitrides, which is crucial for enhancing the performance of transistors, particularly in achieving abrupt channel pinch-off, reducing source-drain leakage current, and increasing off-state breakdown voltage. The optimization of buffers involves a trade-off between trapping effects and breakdown voltage, often achieved by adjusting impurity flow during growth at the barrier/buffer interface.

Additionally, the paper highlights the impact of buffer electron traps on threshold voltage and drain access resistance, noting that these traps can either increase or decrease resistance depending on their location. It also addresses the influence of gate-dielectric traps in insulated-gate structures like AlGaN/GaN MIS-HEMTs, where both bulk oxide traps and interface traps can serve as electron trapping sites. The mechanisms of charging and discharging for these traps vary with gate voltage, influencing device performance.

A unique mechanism involving hole capture into interface traps in C-doped buffers is also presented, where holes originate from C-related acceptors rather than from high-field electron-hole generation processes. The findings underscore the complexity of trapping phenomena and their significant implications for the design and optimization of semiconductor devices.
