Super Junction (SJ) devices have significantly advanced silicon power devices, achieving performance that surpasses traditional one-dimensional material limitations. The potential for SJ technology to be applied to other semiconductor materials is promising, particularly with wide bandgap (WBG) semiconductors, which could greatly enhance power device performance. However, creating conventional vertical SJ devices in Gallium Nitride (GaN) presents notable challenges, primarily due to the inefficiencies associated with Mg-based p-doping in GaN. The low Mg activation ratio leads to inadequate doping concentrations and complicates charge-matching. Additionally, issues with implantation doping and high-quality p-GaN regrowth further impede the development of vertical SJ structures. Consequently, the realization of vertical GaN SJ devices remains elusive until advancements in p-GaN doping techniques are made.

In contrast, alternative approaches for SJ device implementation involve AlGaN/GaN lateral architectures. These designs can generate a two-dimensional electron and hole gas (2DEG and 2DHG) with equal concentrations, facilitated by matching polarization charges. Such devices, termed polarization super junctions (PSJs), exhibit performance characteristics akin to conventional doping-based SJs, leading to enhanced off-effect transistors.
