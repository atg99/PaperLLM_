The section discusses trapping mechanisms in transistors, emphasizing their impact on electrical behavior due to their slow charge and discharge times compared to device capacitances. Traps, whether from intrinsic defects, impurities, or defect-impurity complexes, can affect device performance when they change charge states through the capture or emission of mobile carriers. The mechanisms can be categorized by trap location, charging/discharging paths, and the type of mobile carriers involved.

Traps can be found in semiconductor or dielectric layers and at interfaces, with GaN transistors particularly affected at the device surface, passivation regions, barriers, buffers, and gate dielectrics. Charging paths include leakage currents from terminals, two-dimensional electron gas (2DEG) at interfaces, and high-field mechanisms such as trap impact ionization. The activation of these paths depends on the device bias.

The text notes two key points: first, traps in n-channel field-effect transistors can decrease channel conductivity and drain current through negative charge trapping or positive charge depletion, while increasing conductivity and current can occur through the opposite processes.
