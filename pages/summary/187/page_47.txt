The paper discusses the implementation of a cascode circuit utilizing a normally-on HEMT in series with a normally-off Si-MOSFET. This configuration leverages the positive threshold voltage of the MOSFET, the low on-resistance of the two-dimensional electron gas (2DEG), and the high breakdown field of the GaN HEMT during off-state conditions. However, the presence of the silicon device limits high-temperature operation and introduces packaging complexity, increasing size and parasitic inductances, which may negatively affect the circuit's switching performance.

Additionally, the paper describes an alternative approach involving a recessed gate MISHEMT. This method entails etching the AlGaN barrier layer beneath the gate area and depositing a gate dielectric insulating layer. The complete etching of the AlGaN barrier in the gate region facilitates high threshold voltages, while the thick gate dielectric allows for a significant maximum forward gate bias exceeding +10 V.
