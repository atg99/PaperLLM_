The SEMI-ON-state refers to the condition of a power switch during the transition between ON and OFF states, where internal and parasitic capacitances prevent instantaneous voltage changes. This condition results in a partially formed conductive channel, allowing the presence of a relatively high drain voltage even when the gate voltage nears the threshold. Consequently, electrons in the channel can be accelerated by a strong electric field, leading to the generation of hot carriers, which can adversely affect the device's reliability.

The stress induced by SEMI-ON-state can lead to several detrimental effects on transistors, including deterioration of gate diode isolation, shifts in threshold voltage, increased ON-resistance, decreased transconductance peak, alterations in peak electric field, knee walkout, and amplified drain-lag transient amplitude. These issues are attributed to an increase in defect concentration near the channel, resulting from energy exchanges between hot electrons and the crystal lattice, as evidenced by various deep level characterization techniques.

Recent studies have focused on how hot electrons impact the dynamic ON-resistance of AlGaN/GaN high-electron mobility transistors (HEMTs) under SEMI-ON stress. It was found that additional dynamic resistance in this state is linked to hot-electron trapping at the passivation/AlGaN interface. Specific testing circuits have been developed to evaluate the degradation of GaN HEMTs due to hot electrons. A notable approach employs switching setups that monitor SEMI-ON stress effects on-wafer by adjusting drain capacitance to control the energy released during switching. Results indicate that hot-electron trapping occurs rapidly, within nanoseconds, providing insights into the stress effects on devices.
