The paper discusses the development and advantages of tri-gate architecture for GaN MOSHEMTs, first introduced for Si ultra-scale MOSFETs in 2002 to mitigate short channel effects. The architecture was further adopted for power devices, with the first tri-gate GaN demonstration occurring in 2008. This design offers several benefits, including enhanced current stability, lower subthreshold slope, and reduced off-state leakage.

A notable feature of the tri-gate structure is its capacity to modulate the device threshold voltage (VTH) through adjustments in the fin width (wfin). This modulation is linked to partial AlGaN strain relaxation and sidewall depletion effects due to the side gate electrode. The dependence of VTH on wfin facilitates achieving normally-off operation through careful lithographic design, bypassing the need for complex etching processes used in other methods.

The paper emphasizes the significance of these advancements in the context of high-performance power GaN devices, highlighting their potential for improved device functionality and manufacturing simplicity.
