In 2015, a 75-layer design was introduced to enhance electric field distribution, utilizing a low doping concentration of 10^15/cm^3 in the drift layer of the p-n junction to achieve a near-flat electric field profile. This modification effectively reduced the electric field, resulting in a specific on-resistance (Ron,sp) of 1.7 mΩcm² while maintaining a high breakdown voltage (BV) of 4.7 kV. By 2018, a new p-i-n diode featuring a guard-ring termination was developed, yielding a 200 V increase in BV to reach 5 kV and reducing leakage current.

Avogy Inc. reported the avalanche capability in p-i-n diodes with BVs of 2.6 kV and 3.7 kV, employing a proprietary edge termination based on ion implantation. This approach improved electric field redistribution and achieved breakdown voltages approaching 85% of theoretical values for parallel-plane junctions. Furthermore, substrate orientation was found to significantly influence reverse leakage current and device reliability, with slight miscut angles beneficial for eliminating surface defects and ensuring smooth morphology, critical for low leakage currents. The devices underwent rigorous reliability testing, confirming their robustness.
