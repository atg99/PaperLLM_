The paper discusses the calculation of conduction band discontinuity at the AlGaN/GaN interface, using the equation ΔEC = 0.7[EG(x) - EG(0)], where EG(x) is defined as x * 6.13 eV + (1 - x) * 3.42 eV - x(1 - x) * 1 eV. It notes that for mole fractions (x) around 0.2-0.3, the sheet charge density is approximately 10^13 cm^-3. As the thickness of the AlGaN layer increases, the electron density in the two-dimensional electron gas (2DEG) also increases due to the conduction band dropping further below the Fermi level at the interface. 

The paper highlights that AlGaN/GaN high electron mobility transistors (HEMTs) are intrinsically normally-on, meaning that a channel with high electron density forms at zero gate bias. One method to alter the threshold voltage while maintaining the same aluminum content in the barrier is to reduce the thickness of the AlGaN barrier. However, if the barrier is thinned excessively, the electron density may drop to zero, eliminating the 2DEG, as experimentally observed. The author indicates that while this method can facilitate normally-off operation in HEMTs, it may also lead to increased leakage currents, necessitating countermeasures to ensure device performance. The paper concludes by indicating that various approaches for achieving normally-off operation will be critically compared in subsequent sections.
