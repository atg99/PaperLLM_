The section discusses lateral GaN device architectures, specifically focusing on GaN-on-Si HEMT structures. These structures are composed of multiple epitaxial layers, including AlGaN and GaN, which create a two-dimensional electron gas (2DEG) at their interface. This 2DEG formation is attributed to spontaneous and piezoelectric effects, resulting in high electron mobility exceeding 2000 cm2/Vs and a significant carrier density, leading to superior electrical performance.

A key challenge in GaN-based heteroepitaxy is the lattice mismatch and differing thermal expansion coefficients between the GaN layers and the substrate, which can result in high dislocation densities and potential device degradation due to leakage currents and residual stress. Common substrates include Si, sapphire, SiC, and bulk GaN, with Si being favored for power applications due to its cost-effectiveness and availability in larger diameters. The dislocation density in these epitaxial layers typically ranges from 10^3 to 10^10 cm^-2.
