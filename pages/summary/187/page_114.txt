The paper discusses the energy states in Gallium Nitride (GaN) related to impurity-induced defects. It highlights that silicon, used as a shallow donor in moderately doped GaN, has activation energies between E_C - 0.011 eV and E_C - 0.028 eV, with deeper levels found at E_C - 0.37 eV, E_C - 0.4 eV, and E_C - 0.59 eV. Magnesium is introduced for p-type conductivity, with trap states near the valence band edge, typically located between 0.15 eV and 0.2 eV above it. Several energy levels, including E_C - 3.2 eV to E_C - 3.28 eV, are linked to magnesium, while levels at E_C - 0.355 eV and E_C - 0.597 eV are attributed to Mg-related defects.

Hydrogen's interaction with gallium vacancies forms deep levels between E_C - 2.62 eV and E_C - 2.47 eV, which may also relate to V_Ga defects. Additional levels at E_C - 0.49 eV and E_C - 0.578 eV are tentatively assigned to carbon- and hydrogen-related defects. The paper also notes oxygen's high electronegativity leading to bonding with impurities, with levels at E_C - 1.118 eV, E_C - 0.642 eV, and E_C - 0.599 eV linked to V_Ga-O complexes or dislocations. The findings emphasize the complex interactions of impurities in determining the electronic properties of GaN.
