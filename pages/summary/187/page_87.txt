The paper discusses the advancements in trench MIS barrier Schottky rectifiers, specifically focusing on the TMBS diode, which significantly enhances performance by reducing leakage current by a factor of 104 and increasing breakdown voltage (BV) from 400 V to 700 V. The schematic cross-section and TCAD simulations illustrate how the implementation of a field ring at the base of the trench reduces electric field peaks and how narrowing the TMBS pillar from 3 µm to 2 µm contributes to leakage current reduction. 

Further, it highlights the successful demonstration of high-performance vertical Schottky barrier diodes (SBDs) on bulk GaN and sapphire substrates. Improvements in the material quality of GaN epitaxial layers, alongside various leakage current mitigation strategies and edge termination techniques, have led to current collapse-free operation with zero reverse recovery characteristics. This positions GaN SBDs as optimal candidates for low-loss rectification applications. 

The paper also introduces GaN current aperture vertical electron transistors (CAVETs), indicating ongoing research in this area.
