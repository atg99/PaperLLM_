The paper discusses the challenges associated with achieving a high electron density (ns) in heterostructures and devices, particularly its negative impact on mobility (μ) due to increased electron-to-electron scattering. This results in limitations in reducing the heterostructure's sheet resistance (Rsh) and complicates channel control, leading to negative threshold voltages (VTH) and degraded voltage blocking capabilities. 

A proposed solution is the multi-channel heterostructure, which stacks multiple barrier and channel layers to create several two-dimensional electron gases (2DEGs). This design helps distribute the high ns across parallel channels, improving conductivity while mitigating the trade-off between ns and μ. Additionally, integrating a tri-gate architecture enhances control over the channels and manages high off-state electric fields effectively.

While initially developed for RF applications, there is increasing interest in multi-channel devices for power electronics, which require features such as normally-off operation and high voltage blocking capabilities. The first multi-channel power high electron mobility transistor (HEMT) was introduced in 2018, showcasing the concept, although performance improvements were limited at that time.

The paper also includes benchmarks comparing multi-channel devices against state-of-the-art power devices, highlighting advancements in on-resistance (RON) and breakdown voltage (VBR) capabilities. Overall, multi-channel structures represent a promising avenue for improving the performance of power electronic devices.
