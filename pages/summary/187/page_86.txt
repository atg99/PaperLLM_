The paper discusses advancements in the development of Junction Barrier Schottky (JBS) diodes, particularly focusing on Gallium Nitride (GaN) technology. Traditional methods for creating JBS structures in silicon and silicon carbide involve alternate undoped and p-type regions through ion implantation. However, due to the challenges of doping GaN via ion implantation, alternative techniques have been explored.

One notable approach is the trench JBS diode developed at Cornell University, which involves selectively etching p-GaN layers from a p-i-n diode and forming Schottky contacts. This design leverages the reduced surface field effect, enhancing performance as studied through TCAD simulations. Mitsubishi Electric Corporation's research demonstrated a merged p-i-n Schottky diode that achieved a breakdown voltage (BV) of 2 kV, although it faced high leakage currents that require further optimization.

Ion implantation techniques have also been applied to GaN JBS structures. Koehler et al. reported success in creating p-doped regions via magnesium ion implantation, leading to improved leakage current and breakdown voltage compared to standard Schottky barrier diodes (SBDs). Zhang et al. further advanced this by creating vertical GaN JBS diodes with both magnesium and silicon implantation, achieving significant reductions in reverse leakage current and BVs between 500 and 600 V.

Lastly, the paper introduces Trench Metal Barrier Schottky (TMBS) diodes, which utilize a trench metal insulator semiconductor structure to better control reverse leakage currents. While this structure enhances breakdown voltage by depleting the semiconductor region, it also presents challenges such as higher on-resistance and potential breakdown risks due to trench depth. Strategies like employing argon-implanted field rings are suggested to mitigate these issues.
