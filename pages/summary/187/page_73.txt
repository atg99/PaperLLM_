The paper discusses the challenges and opportunities in the use of bulk GaN substrates for high-voltage vertical power devices, particularly those operating around 5000 V. While these substrates offer significant advantages due to their low dislocation densities, which are crucial for applications like laser diodes and high-brightness LEDs, their high cost (approximately 50 $/cm2) and limited availability (mostly in small 2-inch wafers) hinder widespread commercialization. The bulk GaN market is highly concentrated, with three Japanese companies controlling about 85% of it. 

To address the cost and size limitations, hetero-epitaxy on cheaper foreign substrates, such as Si, SiC, and sapphire, is proposed. Although these substrates can support larger wafer sizes (up to 12 inches), they present challenges due to lattice and coefficient of thermal expansion (CTE) mismatches with GaN, leading to increased defect densities and stress-related issues during growth. The paper emphasizes the need for advancements in reducing dislocation densities and developing stress-relaxation buffer layers to facilitate the growth of thick GaN layers on these alternative substrates. 

A comparison of material properties shows that bulk GaN has a significantly lower defect density compared to GaN grown on foreign substrates, reinforcing the material's superior qualities. The paper highlights the importance of overcoming current limitations to fully leverage GaN's benefits for power device applications.
