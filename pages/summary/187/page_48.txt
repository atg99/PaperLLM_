The paper discusses the significance of dielectric selection in recessed gate GaN MISHEMT devices, emphasizing its impact on channel mobility and threshold voltage stability. It highlights the importance of dielectric quality, surface roughness, and interface charge density control, noting that these factors can contribute to device degradation. The paper also explores the use of fluorine ions implanted in the AlGaN layer to achieve normally-off behavior, which alters surface potential and depletes the 2DEG. However, concerns regarding the stability of threshold voltage after high-temperature annealing and the relationship between fluorine and current collapse are raised, particularly for power switching applications. The paper references various studies to support these claims and provides insights into fluorine-treated MIS/MOS-HEMTs.
