The paper discusses advancements in GaN technology and its implications for performance in electronic devices. Ongoing research aims to enhance the etched interface of GaN and develop high-quality gate dielectric layers, which would also benefit tri-gate technology facing similar challenges with sidewall interface quality and gate dielectric issues. The integration of GaN logic, including DCLF and CMOS, necessitates higher-resolution lithographic techniques to reduce minimum fin width, potentially making tri-gate technology more viable.

The paper also addresses breakdown mechanisms in MOSFET converters, noting that while they can tolerate brief overvoltage exposure, lateral GaN HFETs are susceptible to catastrophic dielectric breakdown without the p-n junction for voltage blocking. Key sources of leakage current and breakdown voltage in AlGaN/GaN HEMT power transistors include the punch-through effect, leakage through the passivation layer, and vertical breakdown due to inadequate doping compensation of the buffer.

To mitigate these breakdown risks, the paper suggests using high-quality dielectrics for surface passivation, proper doping compensation in buffer layers (with materials like carbon or iron), and employing back barriers made from graded AlGaN or AlN materials to enhance buffer resistivity and prevent carrier injection.
