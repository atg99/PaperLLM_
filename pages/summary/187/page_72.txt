The paper discusses the challenges and advancements in GaN HEMTs and vertical GaN power devices. It highlights that while GaN HEMTs can achieve a threshold voltage of only 1-2 V, which is not optimal for fail-safe operations, they lack avalanche capability, necessitating overdesign for higher breakdown voltage (BV) ratings, consequently increasing size and cost. 

In contrast, vertical GaN power devices, where current flows parallel to the growth direction of the epitaxial layers, offer significant advantages. These devices can achieve high ON-state currents and BVs by increasing the thickness of the voltage blocking layer without affecting device size. They are less influenced by surface traps, which enhances breakdown voltage and reliability. Vertical MOSFETs can achieve a positive threshold voltage of 5-15 V, making them suitable for high-power applications. Additionally, the presence of avalanche breakdown in these devices improves reliability and reduces the need for overdesign.

The paper also emphasizes the importance of substrate choice for high-quality GaN epitaxial layers, advocating for homoepitaxy on bulk GaN substrates produced mainly by hydride vapor-phase epitaxy (HVPE). This method results in low dislocation density and matched lattice properties, facilitating the growth of thick GaN layers.
