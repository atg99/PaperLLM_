The paper discusses the phenomenon of time-dependent dielectric breakdown (TDDB) in insulators, which can lead to performance degradation over time despite adherence to design rules. It highlights that defects present in the oxide material can be exacerbated when the device is subjected to stress, such as high electric fields or current flow. The initial random distribution of these defects can become concentrated due to the influence of pre-existing defects, increasing the likelihood of further defect creation. As these defects connect to form a conductive pathway, a significant current may flow through the oxide, risking loss of isolation and potential catastrophic failure if power dissipation exceeds safe levels. The findings emphasize the critical role of defect management in ensuring the reliability of insulating materials in microelectronics.
