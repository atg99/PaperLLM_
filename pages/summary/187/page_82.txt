The paper discusses advancements in the quality of epitaxial GaN layers and termination methods for Schottky Barrier Diodes (SBDs) from 2015 to 2020. It highlights work by Cao et al. from HRL laboratories, who developed an SBD featuring a graded AlGaN cap layer atop the voltage blocking drift layer (i-GaN). This innovation significantly reduced reverse leakage current by three orders of magnitude and effectively shortened the depletion width due to the polarization field in the graded AlGaN, leading to a low turn-on voltage of 0.67 V.

The AlGaN capped SBDs, with a Schottky contact area of 0.8 x 0.8 mm², achieved a breakdown voltage (BV) exceeding 700 V, more than doubling the performance of control SBDs without the AlGaN cap. The study also explored the effects of carbon (C) incorporation during metal-organic chemical vapor deposition (MOCVD) growth. By adjusting growth pressure and the V/III ratio, C concentrations ranging from less than 3 x 10^15 to 3 x 10^19 cm^-3 were achieved. The findings indicated that lower C incorporation improved both forward and reverse characteristics, resulting in SBDs with a turn-on voltage of 0.77 V and a high BV of 800 V for devices with a large Schottky contact area.

The research underscores the importance of material quality and impurity management in enhancing the performance of GaN-based SBDs.
