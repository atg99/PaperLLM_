The paper discusses advancements in GaN p-i-n diodes and Schottky Barrier Diodes (SBDs), focusing on key design parameters such as bevel angle, layer thickness, and doping. It highlights the innovative approach of using a thin i-InGaN layer to facilitate the epitaxial lift-off from bulk GaN substrates, which are typically 350-400 µm thick. This method allows for the bulk substrate to be reused multiple times for growing new epitaxial layers, demonstrating no degradation in performance, with p-i-n diodes exhibiting an R_on,sp of 0.2-0.5 mΩcm² and a breakdown voltage (BV) of 1300 V.

The paper further contrasts p-i-n diodes with SBDs, noting that SBDs are unipolar devices with lower turn-on voltage and no reverse recovery charge, but typically have a higher R_on due to the lack of p-type conductivity modulation. Initial reports of vertical GaN SBDs on sapphire substrates showed a BV of 550 V, while later developments on bulk GaN substrates incorporated Mg+-ion implanted p-guard rings, achieving an R_on,sp of 3.01 mΩcm².

Overall, the study provides significant insights into the material properties and fabrication techniques for improving the reliability and performance of GaN-based devices.
