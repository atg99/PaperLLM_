The paper discusses the challenges and mechanisms of doping wide bandgap semiconductors, specifically Gallium Nitride (GaN). Controlled doping typically involves introducing foreign atoms to induce mobile carriers, with the energy difference between the dopant level and the related band determining the binding energy. In GaN, shallow donors like silicon are effective, while magnesium serves as the conventional acceptor but creates a deeper level, complicating the achievement of high hole densities. GaN inherently exhibits residual n-type conductivity due to native defects and impurities, which can hinder the creation of highly insulating layers necessary for high-voltage devices.

A significant advantage of GaN is its polarization characteristics. The wurtzite crystal structure of III-N semiconductors, particularly when grown along the (0001) orientation, leads to spontaneous and piezoelectric polarization effects. The study by Bernardini et al. highlights that the electronegativity difference between nitrogen and gallium results in a spontaneous polarization along the (0001) axis. The unique arrangement of cations and anions in the wurtzite structure facilitates this polarization, which contributes to the ability to achieve high carrier densities even without intrinsic doping.
