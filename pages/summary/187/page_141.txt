The paper discusses the use of semiconductor capacitors and methodologies to analyze interface state density in dielectric/GaN metal-insulator-semiconductor (MIS) devices. It highlights that the wide bandgap of GaN minimizes hole generation at room temperature, enabling measurements in deep depletion. A photoassisted high-frequency capacitance-voltage characterization method is proposed to measure total interface state density across the bandgap using an above bandgap light source.

Additionally, the paper elaborates on photoluminescence (PL) spectroscopy as a technique for examining optical properties, measuring band-gap energy, and identifying impurity or defect energy levels. During PL measurements, a light source with energy above the material's energy gap is used, allowing detection of luminescence at various wavelengths corresponding to allowed radiative transitions. The paper notes significant findings from PL on GaN, particularly the yellow-band (YL) phenomenon. PL is praised for being fast, contactless, nondestructive, and capable of high spatial resolution, facilitating the evaluation of material properties from samples of varying sizes without complex preparation.
