The paper discusses improvements in GaN-HEMT devices, highlighting how variations in the grounding of the floating buffer reduce the electric field intensity. This reduction is attributed to a larger retraction of the two-dimensional electron gas (2DEG), resulting in a significant increase in time-to-failure by over three orders of magnitude. Additionally, the authors propose alternative solutions to mitigate peak electric field stress, such as modifying the length of the gate head at the drain side or incorporating an extra SiN layer beneath the gate head. The findings underscore the importance of device design in enhancing reliability and performance in electronic applications.
