The paper discusses the identification of a critical mechanism related to the degradation of GaN devices, first proposed in 2006. Subsequent research has demonstrated that a specific critical gate or drain voltage is necessary for degradation to occur, which correlates with the accumulation of strain within the device structure leading to defect formation. Tests reveal that exceeding this critical voltage results in increased charge trapping in the AlGaN barrier, while trapping in the buffer remains unaffected. The study also indicates that the effect of hot electrons on this process is negligible, as varying stress currents do not influence the critical voltage. Additionally, transmission electron microscopy (TEM) measurements show that cracks develop only on the drain side of the gate when the critical voltage is surpassed, with no observed impact from temperature or hot electrons on degradation.
