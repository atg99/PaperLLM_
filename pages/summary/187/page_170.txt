The paper discusses the impact of electrostatic discharges (ESD) and electrical overstress (EOS) on the reliability of devices, highlighting the risks posed by unexpected voltage or current pulses during operation, fabrication, or handling. These conditions can lead to significant degradation in device components, particularly ohmic contacts at the source and drain, which can experience a substantial increase in specific resistance under high current stress. Backside infrared camera measurements have revealed the formation of dark spots on grounded contacts, indicating electromigration and current filamentation as contributing factors to degradation. Notably, this degradation occurs without any observable changes in material parameters, charge density, or electron mobility. The findings emphasize the importance of considering both high power dissipation and the direction of current flow or electric field in assessing device reliability.
