The paper discusses advancements in vertical trench gate MOSFETs utilizing an n-p-i-n heterostructure. In these devices, the channel is created by the n-p-i sidewall of the trench, where a positive gate bias induces an inversion sheet charge of electrons in the adjacent p-GaN layer. However, dry etching introduces defects that degrade the field effect mobility of these electrons. To address this, Prof. Mishra’s group at UCSB developed a method involving the regrowth of a thin undoped GaN layer in the gate trench using MOCVD, followed by in-situ deposition of Al2O3 dielectric. This approach resulted in a 60% reduction in on-resistance compared to traditional MOSFETs, achieving normally-off operation with a threshold voltage of 2 V and a breakdown voltage (BV) of 195 V. Later devices on bulk GaN substrates achieved a BV of 990 V due to improved gate trench etching. Ji et al. reported large-area OGFETs with an output current nearing 0.5 A and a BV of 320 V. Further optimizations led to OGFETs with a record channel mobility of 185 cm2/Vs and a low on-resistance of 2.2 mΩcm2. A novel double field plate design enabled a high BV of 1.4 kV for single devices and 0.9 kV for larger devices. Variations of the OGFET have been explored by other researchers, including Li et al., who reported a trench MOSFET with an MBE regrown channel, achieving a record BV of 600 V while reducing the thermal budget.
