The paper discusses the performance characteristics of Al-rich AlGaN-based high electron mobility transistors (HEMTs), highlighting their limited electron mobility of 145 cm²/V.s and low leakage current without the use of field plates, indicating the absence of tunneling mechanisms. Despite a high defect density, these transistors achieve a blocking voltage exceeding 4000 V and an off-state leakage current below 0.1 µA/mm. The low gate-drain distance of 5 µm results in a breakdown field of 3.5 MV/cm, surpassing that of SiC and GaN devices. Additionally, the transistors exhibit stable behavior up to 200°C with consistent threshold voltage and minimal leakage increase.

A significant challenge faced in advancing Al-rich AlGaN transistors is optimizing ohmic contacts, as high resistance in source and drain electrodes hampers current density. Efforts to reduce contact resistance include tuning the heterostructure and employing regrown ohmic contacts using a SiO2 mask. The paper references ongoing strategies to address the issue, confirming improvements at the transistor level. The transfer and output characteristics of AlN/AlGaN/AlN HEMTs with regrown contacts are illustrated, emphasizing the importance of low resistance contacts in enhancing performance in wide bandgap HEMTs.
