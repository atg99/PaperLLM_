The paper discusses techniques for evaluating the I-V characteristics of semiconductor devices through the application of synchronous and short voltage pulses. These pulses, ranging from 100 ns to 10 µs with a duty cycle of 0.1% to 1%, allow for the assessment of device behavior without traps reaching equilibrium. This method reveals the I-V characteristics based on trap filling at specific quiescent bias points (QBPs). 

Key QBPs include: 
1. V_GS=0 V, V_DS=0 V as a reference for fresh device conditions.
2. A QBP with V_GS below the threshold voltage and V_DS=0 V to evaluate gate-lag effects.
3. A QBP in off-state conditions with large V_DS to assess drain-lag effects.

The comparison of pulsed I-V measurements at different QBPs facilitates the identification and quantification of trapping phenomena. The study also notes that shifts in threshold voltage may indicate trapping in specific layers, while decreases in transconductance may suggest increased access region resistance due to traps.

Additionally, the paper addresses deep-level transient spectroscopy (DLTS) and its role in trap investigation within semiconductor devices. By utilizing capacitance transients following nonequilibrium conditions, DLTS provides insights into impurity levels and activation energies related to traps, allowing for faster characterization compared to traditional methods. Overall, the findings offer a qualitative evaluation of device performance impacted by trapping phenomena.
