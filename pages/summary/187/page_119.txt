The section discusses two types of traps in GaN HEMTs: surface traps in the gate-drain access region and barrier traps under the gate. 

Surface traps can capture electrons injected from the gate contact when the device is in an off-state, specifically when the gate voltage is below threshold and a positive drain-source voltage is applied. Electrons can leak through passivation layers or move via hopping mechanisms to these traps. Once the device switches to an on-state, these trapped electrons are slowly emitted, which can significantly limit RF output power and power-added efficiency. To mitigate this, optimization of passivation and the introduction of field plates have been employed to reduce surface defect density and the electric field in the gate-drain area. Additionally, surface traps can also capture channel hot electrons, especially during semi-on or power-state operations, which requires a high gate-source voltage and drain-source voltage.

Barrier traps, located beneath the gate, behave similarly by capturing gate-injected electrons during off-state conditions and releasing them when the device transitions to on-state conditions. This process affects the device's performance, particularly under power-state conditions where hot electrons are present.

Overall, both types of traps contribute to increased drain access resistance and reduced transconductance peak, impacting the efficiency and performance of GaN HEMTs.
