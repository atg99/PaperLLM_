The paper discusses the characteristics of AlGaN/GaN heterostructures, which are fundamental to GaN-based high electron mobility transistors (HEMTs). It highlights that the GaN and AlGaN layers are typically undoped to reduce electron scattering, resulting in a weak n-type conductivity in undoped GaN, with electron densities influenced by epitaxial quality. The paper emphasizes the need to optimize background electron density in HEMTs by controlling growth processes and residual impurities.

Key features of the charge distribution, electric field profile, and band diagram of the AlGaN/GaN heterostructure are presented. The total charge at the AlGaN surface is a combination of surface donor charges and polarization charges, leading to a positive charge difference at the heterojunction due to the higher polarization of AlGaN compared to GaN. This creates a triangular potential well on the GaN side, where a two-dimensional electron gas (2DEG) is formed. The position of the 2DEG can be determined through the solution of the Schrödinger equation in this potential well.
