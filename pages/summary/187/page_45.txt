The paper discusses the challenges and advancements in the development of normally-off AlGaN/GaN High Electron Mobility Transistors (HEMTs). Typically, lateral heterostructure devices are normally-on, meaning they conduct current without applied gate voltage, which poses safety risks due to the potential for uncontrolled current flow during gate driver failures. This characteristic complicates circuit designs by necessitating a negative-voltage supply.

To address these issues, significant research has focused on creating normally-off devices. The threshold voltage (V_TH) of a conventional AlGaN/GaN HEMT is influenced by various parameters, including the gate metal and heterojunction properties. The paper provides an equation detailing how V_TH is affected by factors such as the Schottky barrier height, conduction band discontinuity, polarization charge, and Al content in the barrier layer. This equation highlights the complexity of achieving normally-off operation in these transistors.
