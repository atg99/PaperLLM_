The paper discusses the performance characteristics of GaN-based transistors, particularly focusing on AlGaN/GaN HEMTs. It emphasizes two critical parameters: on-resistance (Ron) and breakdown voltage (Vbr). Both parameters are interrelated, as carriers are generated by polarization, and Vbr is influenced by the distance between the gate and drain (LGD). 

In a first-order approximation, Ron is determined by the sum of the channel resistance (Rchannel) and the drain-side access region resistance (Rdrain), with the equation provided to illustrate this relationship. The contribution from source and contact resistance is excluded for simplicity. The paper also notes that the gate-drain access region supports the off-state voltage, and Vbr is related to LGD and the critical electric field (Ecrit).

The discussion includes a graphical representation showing how Ron (multiplied by gate width, Wg) varies with target Vbr for devices with different gate lengths (0.25 µm and 1 µm). The results indicate that for devices with low breakdown voltage and short gate-drain distances, Ron is significantly influenced by gate length. The analysis highlights the trade-offs in designing high-voltage devices, where the gate-drain spacing is typically larger than the gate length.
