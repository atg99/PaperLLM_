The paper discusses advancements in transistor technology, specifically focusing on characteristics of transistors with regrown non-alloyed contacts. These transistors exhibit a significant increase in current density, exceeding 100 mA/mm, compared to similar devices with partially etched barrier and annealed contacts. This improvement is attributed to a notable reduction in contact resistances. Preliminary findings indicate that contact resistivity is expected to improve over time, and innovative techniques like etch and regrowth processes are anticipated to enable higher aluminum composition in the devices.

Additionally, the paper presents a detailed examination of multi-channel devices, illustrated through three-dimensional schematics and cross-sectional images. The design features multiple parallel channels controlled by a tri-gate electrode, enhancing the device's performance. The study highlights that despite recent advancements, the performance of AlGaN/GaN devices remains below the theoretical limits for GaN materials. A key strategy for improving device performance is increasing carrier concentration, which correlates with reduced on-resistance. Overall, the research emphasizes the potential of advanced fabrication techniques and multi-channel designs for enhancing power conversion efficiency in semiconductor devices.
