The paper discusses the role of field plates (FP) in extending the depletion region from the gate to the drain electrode in high-voltage GaN devices, thereby reducing electric field peaks at the gate edge. Various designs of field plates, including single FP, multiple FPs, and slant field plates, are explored for their effectiveness and challenges in planar processes. Key to these designs is the precise control of dielectric thickness, which influences the field plate threshold voltage. For high-voltage GaN devices, combinations of FP structures with increasing dielectric thickness are utilized to achieve a smoother electric field profile. The tri-gate architecture is highlighted as a promising design for lower voltage sections of FPs, allowing for a slanted shape in the fin width that optimizes electric field distribution and enhances breakdown voltage. Additionally, the ability to finely tune the field plate threshold voltage is crucial for improving the blocking performance of AlGaN/GaN Schottky barrier diodes.
