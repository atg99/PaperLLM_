The paper discusses the significance of defects in Gallium Nitride (GaN) as a semiconductor, emphasizing how the periodicity of its crystalline structure influences its properties. Defects arise from either the incorporation of foreign atoms (impurity-related defects) or non-ideal arrangements of host atoms (native defects), both of which disrupt the periodic potential of the lattice. This disruption introduces allowed energy states within the forbidden bandgap, which can act as carrier traps or recombination centers, adversely affecting the performance and reliability of GaN-based devices.

To better understand these defects, a comprehensive database was created, compiling data from nearly one hundred scientific publications. This database includes over 480 records of traps identified in GaN through various experimental techniques, facilitating a correlation of energy positions, signatures, and trapping behaviors of commonly found defects. The paper presents findings on specific native defects, such as nitrogen interstitials, nitrogen antisites, and nitrogen vacancies, detailing their physical origins and the range of energy levels associated with them. This study aims to enhance the understanding of defects in GaN, which is crucial for improving the performance of GaN-based electronic devices.
