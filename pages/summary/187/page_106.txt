The paper discusses the challenges and limitations associated with the use of p-GaN in trench power MOSFETs, particularly focusing on the effects of high magnesium doping concentrations. It highlights that annealing processes activate only a small fraction of dopant atoms, leading to low hole concentrations and mobility. This low mobility results in electron scattering in the p-GaN channel, degrading channel performance and increasing ON-state losses in p-i-n diodes. 

Additionally, the commercialization of bulk GaN is impeded by high costs, small substrate sizes, and significant initial investments required for dedicated fabrication facilities. The paper emphasizes the need for advancements in substrate size and cost to facilitate broader adoption of GaN devices. It suggests that GaN-on-Si technology could provide a cost-effective alternative, but improvements in GaN quality and layer thickness are necessary to support the development of high-voltage devices. The availability of CTE matched substrates in larger diameters may further enhance the progress of vertical GaN power devices.
