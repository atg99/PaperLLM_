The paper discusses the nature of defects in GaN crystals, particularly focusing on extended defects and impurity-related defects. It suggests that extended defects may behave as non-interacting point defects and do not form in highly defective regions. Impurities in the lattice structure can arise from the introduction of foreign atomic species, either intentionally as dopants like Si and Mg or unintentionally from contaminants such as residual oxygen and hydrogen. These impurities can influence the electrical properties of GaN, with specific attention given to the deep levels they introduce. The paper indicates the need for a deeper understanding of these defects to improve GaN material quality and performance.
