The section discusses the bias conditions for Deep Capture Transients (DCTs) and their impact on trap localization within semiconductor devices. It highlights that DCTs can be recorded in either the linear or saturation regions, with significant differences in results depending on the chosen condition. Sensing in the linear region with a high gate-source voltage (VGS) can reveal traps in the access regions, while sensing in saturation near pinch-off minimizes access region effects, making it sensitive to traps beneath the gate.

The analysis also notes that factors such as self-heating and electric field-enhanced emission can distort DCT results. Self-heating raises local temperatures, accelerating carrier emission and leading to an incorrect evaluation of the de-trapping process. Similarly, Poole-Frenkel mechanisms can introduce errors. To obtain accurate trap activation energy estimations, the authors recommend using low drain-source voltages (VDS) to mitigate these effects and suggest employing pulsed-drain current transient (P-DCT) methodologies, which maintain the device at zero bias during recovery.

Additionally, the section outlines the process for extracting time constants at various temperatures to construct an Arrhenius plot for determining trap activation energy. A multi-exponential fitting approach is proposed, involving the least-square fitting of experimental data with multiple exponential functions to accurately characterize the DCT response.
