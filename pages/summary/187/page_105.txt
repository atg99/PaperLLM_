The paper discusses the potential of GaN-based vertical power devices for next-generation power-efficient converters, highlighting the challenges associated with material quality, device fabrication, and performance. The growth of bulk GaN crystals is particularly difficult due to the high temperatures and pressures required, making alternative methods like hydride vapor phase epitaxy necessary. Despite these efforts, defect densities in GaN substrates remain a concern for commercial applications.

The paper emphasizes the need for GaN devices to meet the standards established by the Joint Electron Device Engineering Council (JEDEC), which have evolved to address the unique characteristics of GaN compared to traditional silicon (Si) and silicon carbide (SiC) devices. The complexity of doping in GaN, particularly through ion implantation, presents additional challenges, as it requires specialized high-temperature and high-pressure conditions. Although selective area growth has been explored, it has faced limitations due to high concentrations of silicon at the regrowth interface.

Innovative approaches, including Ar ion implantation and plasma treatments, have been tested for GaN vertical devices, but their scalability and long-term reliability remain uncertain. Additionally, there are significant concerns regarding the low activation efficiency of p-GaN produced via metal-organic chemical vapor deposition (MOCVD), attributed to the formation of stable Mg-H complexes during growth. Overall, the paper underscores the necessity for further research and development to overcome these obstacles and realize the full potential of GaN-based power devices.
