The paper discusses advancements in GaN CAVET (Conductively Active Vertical Electron Transistor) technology, highlighting various improvements in device performance and structure. Chowdhury et al. (2012) reported a GaN CAVET with MBE regrown AlGaN/GaN layers, achieving a low on-resistance of 2.2 mΩcm² and a breakdown voltage (BV) of 200-260 V at a gate-source voltage (VGS) of -15 V. Further enhancements were made by incorporating a buried conductive p-GaN layer, which reduced the on-resistance to 0.4 mΩcm². Avogy's 2014 modified CAVET design included a p-GaN gate layer for normally-off operation, yielding a forward current of 2.3 A and a threshold voltage of 0.5 V, with a high BV of 1.5 kV at VGS of -5 V. Panasonic also developed a similar device that achieved a low on-resistance of 1 mΩcm² and a BV of 1.7 kV through innovative channel designs.

Ji et al. explored a gate trench structure without a p-GaN gate, achieving a threshold voltage of 20 V and a BV of 225 V, later improving the BV to 880 V by enhancing gate trench etching and using a gate dielectric. However, this change resulted in a significant shift in VGS to -21 V. 

The paper identifies critical challenges for the commercialization of GaN CAVETs, particularly issues related to the regrowth process, which leads to high leakage currents, and the sensitivity of on-state performance to the doping and dimensions of the aperture region, complicating fabrication.
