The paper discusses the advancements and potential of gallium nitride (GaN) as a material for power devices over the past decade. GaN is highlighted for its superior properties compared to other semiconductors, including the widest energy gap, largest critical field, and highest saturation velocity, making it ideal for high-speed and high-voltage applications. The authors emphasize the role of spontaneous and piezoelectric polarization in forming a two-dimensional electron gas in AlGaN/GaN heterostructures, which enhances mobility and channel density without the need for doping, thus reducing resistive losses. Additionally, GaN transistors exhibit low switching losses due to minimal parasitic capacitances, further contributing to their efficiency in power device applications. The paper presents a comprehensive analysis of the physics, reliability, and future perspectives of GaN-based power devices.
