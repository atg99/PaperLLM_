The paper discusses the mechanisms of electron trapping in AlGaN/GaN HEMTs, particularly under conditions of large drain-gate voltage. It highlights the roles of various electron sources: gate electrons can be trapped in surface, barrier, and buffer traps, while source electrons are limited to buffer traps. Channel hot electrons can also be trapped in all three types of traps. The trapping can influence key parameters such as threshold voltage (V T) and drain access resistance, with significant effects noted in the gate-drain access region.

The study emphasizes the importance of surface passivation and the introduction of field plates, which have reduced the impact of surface and barrier traps on RF current collapse. This reduction is attributed to a decreased electric field at the drain-end edge of the gate, leaving buffer electron trapping as the primary concern, especially in technologies utilizing compensating impurities like iron to enhance breakdown voltage.

The findings are supported by experimental data illustrated in Figure 74, which shows the dispersion of pulsed output characteristics from a Fe-doped AlGaN/GaN HEMT, indicating the effects of buffer traps on device performance.
