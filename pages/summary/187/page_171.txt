The paper discusses the degradation processes in AlGaN/GaN high electron mobility transistors (HEMTs), particularly focusing on the impact of electrical overstress. It highlights the appearance of dark spots on contacts during electrostatic discharge (ESD) events, indicating potential failure points. The authors emphasize that contact-related issues can often be mitigated through improved device processing, but other factors, including substrate materials, also play a critical role in device performance and failure. 

Testing lateral Schottky diodes reveals that failure voltage becomes independent of diode length after a certain threshold, suggesting that breakdown is no longer a lateral issue. To further explore the role of vertical electric fields in degradation, the authors propose fabricating specific buffer isolation structures with and without Si substrate removal. Results indicate that structures retaining the Si substrate fail at lower voltages, confirming that the vertical electric field contributes significantly to device failure. 

This work underscores the importance of considering substrate materials and electric field effects in the design and processing of semiconductor devices to enhance reliability and performance.
