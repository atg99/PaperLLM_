The paper discusses the effects of iron and carbon doping in GaN-based transistors on the intrinsic n-type conductivity of GaN layers. Iron is noted to introduce various energy levels, specifically from E C - 0.94 eV to E C - 0.34 eV, with shallower states at E C - 0.34 eV and E C - 0.44 eV linked to different charge states of iron. Recent findings identify a trap at E C - 0.58 eV associated with Fe Ga sites.

Similarly, carbon doping is employed to compensate for n-type conductivity. Carbon atoms can occupy substitutional nitrogen sites (C N), which act as a deep acceptor with a transition level at E V + 0.9 eV, contributing to semi-insulating layers without generating significant free hole density. C N can also lead to the formation of shallow acceptor-like levels between E C - 3.31 eV and E C - 3.15 eV. In contrast, carbon occupying interstitial positions (C I) introduces deep donor levels in the energy gap, typically from E C - 1.35 eV to E C - 1.2 eV. Shallower levels, such as E C - 0.14 eV and E C - 0.4 eV, have also been associated with carbon- or hydrogen-related defects, with a possible link to C Ga sites.
