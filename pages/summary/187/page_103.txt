The paper discusses advancements in GaN-on-Si vertical power MOSFET technology, highlighting significant developments by Liu et al. in 2017, which achieved a normally-off operation with a threshold voltage of 6.3 V and an ON/OFF ratio exceeding 10^8. The device demonstrated a low specific on-resistance (Ron,sp) of 6.8 mΩcm² and a breakdown voltage (BV) of 645 V, comparable to bulk GaN MOSFETs. The subsequent work by Khadar et al. introduced fully vertical MOSFETs with a robust fabrication process that included selective substrate removal, achieving an improved Ron,sp of 5 mΩcm² and a BV of 520 V. This design exhibited significantly higher current density and lower Ron,sp due to the elimination of current crowding.

The impact of gate trench alignment on output current density was also analyzed, revealing that alignment along the m-plane resulted in three times higher output current compared to the a-plane. The use of a metal mask during gate trench etching enhanced electron mobility to 41 cm²/Vs.

Additionally, the paper emphasizes the advantages of GaN-on-Si technology for integrating multiple devices on a single chip, leading to smaller footprints and reduced parasitic capacitance and resistance. Liu et al. further demonstrated the first vertical monolithically integrated device in 2018, combining a Schottky barrier diode with a power MOSFET to enhance efficiency in various power converter applications, particularly in configurations requiring freewheeling diodes for managing reverse current flow.
