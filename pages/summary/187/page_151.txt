The paper discusses the reliability failures in vertical p-n-n diodes and vertical junction field-effect transistors, emphasizing the influence of substrate quality, miscut angle, and surface morphology post-epitaxial growth on performance. Experimental studies indicate that forward bias stress leads to increased series resistance, elevated turn-on voltage, and higher leakage currents, alongside a decrease in optical power due to band-to-band recombination. The relationship between series resistance and optical power exhibits a square root dependence on time, consistent with diffusion-related degradation. This degradation model suggests that carrier flow during stress can disrupt residual Mg-H bonds, allowing hydrogen interstitials to diffuse towards the n-type material, passivating Mg atoms near the p-n interface and reducing hole concentration. This results in higher turn-on voltage and diminished radiative recombination, aligning with the principles outlined in Fick's second law of diffusion.
