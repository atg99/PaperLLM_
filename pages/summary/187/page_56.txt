The paper discusses strategies to enhance the breakdown voltage of AlGaN/GaN transistors, particularly in the context of their application on silicon substrates. It highlights the importance of optimizing the thickness of the GaN channel to achieve a high electron density in the two-dimensional electron gas (2DEG), effective electron confinement, and reduced trapping effects, especially when doping compensation is employed.

The research indicates that the breakdown voltage (VBR) of AlGaN/GaN transistors increases linearly with smaller gate-drain distances (below 15 µm) but saturates at larger distances due to substrate conduction influenced by vertical electric fields. The quality of the crystal is also critical, as high defect densities in GaN/Si epilayers can adversely affect breakdown voltage.

To mitigate issues related to electric field distribution, which can lead to early breakdown, the paper suggests the implementation of field plate structures. These structures are designed to manage the inhomogeneous electric fields present in the off-state, particularly at the gate edge, thereby improving the voltage-blocking capabilities of the devices. The paper emphasizes ongoing research efforts aimed at overcoming the inherent breakdown mechanisms in GaN-on-Si high electron mobility transistors (HEMTs).
