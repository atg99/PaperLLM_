The paper discusses the increase of dynamic R ON in GaN transistors used in power switching converters, which leads to higher power losses compared to their DC values. This phenomenon is attributed to various electron trapping processes that occur during both the off-state and hard switching phases, where high drain-source voltages and currents can coexist. The increase in negative trapped charge reduces channel conductivity, contributing to the dynamic R ON rise.

Additionally, specific trapping mechanisms related to power transistors, such as electron trapping in buffer traps induced by substrate leakage current, are identified. These effects become significant at high drain voltages typical of GaN transistors. The presence of carbon doping in the buffer region, which is used to enhance blocking voltage, introduces further trapping mechanisms linked to the C N acceptor state. This can lead to an increase in negatively-ionized acceptors and, consequently, dynamic increases in both channel conductivity and R ON.

The paper also highlights that R ON transients under off-state bias stress and recovery may be thermally activated with an activation energy of 0.9 eV, suggesting underlying processes of electron capture or hole redistribution. Recent findings indicate that dynamic R ON can show a non-monotonic relationship with off-state drain voltage, recovering to lower values after reaching a peak. This behavior is explained using a "leaky dielectric" buffer model, where band-to-band tunneling may generate holes at high off-state biases.
