The paper discusses the response of a measurement instrument to transient emissions from traps in semiconductors, specifically focusing on the thermal activation of emission rates. The response peak is observed at a temperature where the trap emission rate aligns with the instrument's detection window. The emission rate is described by an equation that incorporates several parameters: the minority-carrier capture cross section, mean thermal velocity of minority carriers, effective density of states in the minority-carrier band, degeneracy of the trap level, and the energy separation between the trap level and the minority-carrier band. Additionally, a diagram illustrates the processes involved in majority-carrier pulse sequences that generate capacitance transients for majority-carrier traps, highlighting capture and emission processes along with trap occupation dynamics. The paper references D. V. Lang's work on deep-level transient spectroscopy as a method for characterizing semiconductor traps.
