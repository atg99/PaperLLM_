The paper discusses the characteristics and origins of various defect-related energy levels in gallium nitride (GaN). It highlights the presence of gallium vacancies (V Ga) and their associated trap levels, which are located in the energy range from E C – 0.19 eV to E C – 2.85 eV. The presence of similar Arrhenius signatures in both extended and point defects indicates that gallium vacancies retain their emission properties despite the influence of nearby ionized traps. 

Gallium interstitials are less frequently studied, with reported trap levels at E C – 0.91 eV and E C – 0.8 eV. The paper also notes that deep levels associated with gallium vacancies are often linked to complexes formed with oxygen or hydrogen, and various specific energy levels have been identified as related to these vacancies. 

Additionally, the paper outlines trap states connected to extended defects, which cover a wide range of the upper GaN bandgap, specifically from E C – 1.118 eV to E C – 0.24 eV. The findings suggest a correlation between nitrogen vacancies and nitrogen antisite defects, indicating they may interact closely with other defects. Overall, the investigation emphasizes the complexity of defect interactions in GaN and their impact on material properties.
