The paper discusses the advantages of GaN vertical devices, which exhibit a defect density of 10^4-10^6/cm², making them suitable for rapid commercialization. Avogy p-i-n diodes outperform silicon fast diodes in power converter applications, such as hard-switched boost circuits, showing minimal ringing and no reverse recovery loss. These diodes can handle large currents, with 16 mm² devices achieving 400 A in pulsed operation and 100 A at 4.5 V in DC operation, alongside an impressive breakdown voltage (BV) of 700 V.

The paper includes figures illustrating a vertical GaN p-n diode and surface morphology on devices grown on different GaN substrates, indicating that devices on lower miscut angle substrates demonstrate reduced reverse leakage currents. Cornell University has shown that high-quality GaN layers grown via metal organic chemical vapor deposition (MOCVD) yield a Shockley-Read-Hall (SRH) lifetime of 12 ns. Their p-i-n diodes have an ultra-low on-resistance of 0.12 mΩcm² and a high BV of 1.4 kV, resulting in a figure of merit (BFOM) of 16.5 GW/cm². Additionally, GaN p-i-n diodes with bevel termination and a long field plate have achieved a BV improvement to approximately 4 kV compared to non-terminated designs.
