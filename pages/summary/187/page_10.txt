This paper provides an in-depth examination of GaN-based power devices, focusing on their physics, performance, and reliability. It highlights the advantages of vertical GaN transistors, which allow for high current and power densities due to their unique structure. The modulation of electron density in the channel is achieved through a metal-oxide-semiconductor (MOS) stack, with a p-type body utilized to adjust the threshold voltage.

The breakdown voltage of vertical GaN transistors is primarily influenced by the thickness of the lightly-doped drift region, differing from lateral transistors where size and area are significant factors. Despite their advancements, GaN devices face challenges related to performance and reliability. The on-resistance of GaN transistors is affected by intrinsic polarization charges and surface state charges, necessitating an understanding of trapping phenomena that can lead to dynamic performance issues, such as the dynamic-Ron problem and threshold instability (PBTI or NBTI).

Additionally, GaN devices operate under extreme conditions, including electric fields exceeding 3 MV/cm and channel temperatures above 300 °C, which can trigger breakdown phenomena and device failure. High-frequency operations further complicate degradation processes linked to hard switching events. Therefore, the paper emphasizes the importance of understanding these degradation mechanisms to enhance the robustness of GaN power devices.

Sections 3-5 of the paper delve into the properties and physical parameters of GaN, providing insights into its superior performance compared to other semiconductors, including key figures of merit for high-speed applications.
