The Johnson figure of merit (FOM) is defined as the product of maximum voltage and maximum transit frequency for a given drain-source spacing. It demonstrates that devices with higher breakdown voltages tend to be slower than those with lower voltage ratings. In a comparison of various semiconductors, GaN shows a Johnson FOM slightly higher than SiC and comparable to β-Ga2O3, while significantly outperforming Si and GaAs. AlN and diamond exhibit even higher FOM values, but their advantage over GaN is marginal. 

The Johnson FOM is useful for evaluating materials for high-speed devices, whereas the Baliga FOM is proposed for power electronics applications. In the context of a unilateral p+/n- junction, the electric field profile is triangular, with the critical electric field determining the width of the space charge region. The breakdown voltage can be calculated based on the electric field, relative permittivity of GaN, and donor density at the n-side of the diode.
