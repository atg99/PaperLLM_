The paper discusses the properties of Gallium Nitride (GaN) as a semiconductor material, highlighting its advantages over other materials like Silicon (Si), Gallium Arsenide (GaAs), Silicon Carbide (SiC), Aluminum Nitride (AlN), diamond, and Gallium Oxide (Ga2O3). GaN exhibits the largest energy gap, critical field, and saturation velocity among these materials, making it particularly suitable for high-temperature and high-voltage power devices.

Figures in the paper illustrate the atomic structure of GaN and the spontaneous polarization dipole direction, while a table compares key parameters of GaN with other semiconductors. The relationship between breakdown field and energy gap is explored, showing a power-law dependence that underscores the benefits of wide bandgap semiconductors for high breakdown voltage applications.

The paper also mentions the importance of figures of merit for evaluating semiconductor materials, specifically the Johnson figure of merit for high-speed devices and the Baliga figure of merit for high-power devices, emphasizing that a single property does not encapsulate the overall performance of a material.
