The paper discusses the presence of polarization charges in Gallium Nitride (GaN) layers, highlighting how internal polarization charges cancel each other, leaving a dipole formed by charges at the N and Ga faces. The spontaneous polarization charge in GaN ranges from 1.8 to 2.1 x 10^13 e-/cm², which can generate a high electric field if not screened. The paper examines the role of a screening dipole, suggesting it may originate from atmospheric ions or from donor states at the GaN surface. The presence of donor states leads to a pinned Fermi level at the surface, forming a screening charge that compensates the polarization dipole. For sufficiently thick GaN layers, the bands flatten, indicating negligible electric fields except near the surface, where the polarization charge is balanced by ionized surface defects and the charge density in depleted n-type GaN. The findings contribute to the understanding of charge dynamics in GaN layers, relevant for electronic applications.
