The paper provides a comprehensive overview of GaN technology, focusing on its properties, device structures, and reliability aspects. It begins by discussing AlGaN/GaN heterostructures, including band diagrams and calculations for sheet electron charge and threshold voltage. The characteristics of lateral GaN devices are examined, particularly methods for achieving normally-off operation and optimizing buffer structures. 

The paper then shifts to vertical GaN devices, highlighting their advantages and comparing different substrate options, such as GaN-on-GaN and GaN-on-Si. Various vertical device architectures are analyzed based on performance and structural parameters. 

A detailed analysis of device stability follows, concentrating on the impact of surface, barrier, and buffer traps on device performance. The discussion includes an overview of dominant defects and deep levels in GaN. Finally, the paper outlines key degradation processes that affect the longevity of GaN-based transistors, emphasizing mechanisms related to off-state stress, semi-on state, and on-state degradation, particularly regarding gate reliability in p-GaN and insulated-gate devices.

Overall, the paper adopts a pedagogical approach to elucidate the advantages and intricacies of GaN technology, aiding readers in understanding its performance, design, and reliability.
