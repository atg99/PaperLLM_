The paper discusses the advantages of Gallium Nitride (GaN) devices over Silicon (Si) Metal-Oxide-Semiconductor Field Effect Transistors (MOSFETs). GaN devices can operate at temperatures up to 900 °C, significantly higher than the 125-150 °C limit of Si MOSFETs. They also feature a high breakdown field of 3.3 MV/cm, which is eleven times greater than that of Si, allowing for thinner layers and reduced resistive losses in switching mode power supplies. 

Additionally, GaN transistors, particularly high electron mobility transistors (HEMTs) or heterojunction field effect transistors (HFETs), benefit from high channel mobility (up to 2000 cm2/Vs) through the formation of a 2-dimensional electron gas (2DEG) at the AlGaN/GaN interface. This high mobility contributes to lower resistivity and improved performance.

The paper outlines the commercial availability of GaN devices across three voltage ranges: low/mid-voltage (maximum V_DS < 200 V) for various applications including power converters and robotics; high voltage (up to 650 V) for telecommunication and industrial uses; and ultra-high voltage devices (over 1 kV), although currently, the highest commercially available GaN transistors are rated at 900 V. These advancements highlight the growing feasibility and application potential of GaN technology in modern electronics.
