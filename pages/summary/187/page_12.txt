The paper discusses the structure and properties of Gallium Nitride (GaN) and its alloys, which are significant for optoelectronics and electronics. Figure 1 illustrates the structure of a lateral GaN high-electron mobility transistor (HEMT) and provides cross-sectional images of the GaN HEMT on silicon. GaN possesses a direct bandgap of 3.4 eV, making it suitable for manufacturing ultraviolet optoelectronic devices and enabling the tuning of the energy gap across a range from 0.7 eV in InN to 6.2 eV in AlN, facilitating the production of LEDs across ultraviolet, visible, and infrared spectra. The crystal structure of III-N semiconductors is typically wurtzite, characterized by a hexagonal shape that lacks an inversion plane perpendicular to the c-axis. This structural feature is crucial as it influences the polarity of polarization charges on the surfaces, which is essential for device performance.
