The paper discusses the development of GaN-based CAVET structures for high voltage applications, originating from proposals by Yaacov et al. in 2004. CAVETs utilize a double-diffused MOS structure with an AlGaN/GaN heterostructure, current blocking layers, and an n-type doped GaN layer. The architecture allows the source terminals to make ohmic contact with the 2DEG, while the gate creates a Schottky contact with the AlGaN. Current flow is restricted to a small area beneath the gate due to Mg-ion implantation used for the current blocking layer. This design enables switching of the 2DEG via gate bias, demonstrating transistor behavior.

One significant advantage of the CAVET design is that the high field region is located under the gate, potentially accommodating large breakdown voltages, unlike lateral HEMTs where surface-related breakdown can occur. Traditionally, CAVETs operate as normally-ON devices due to the modulation of the AlGaN/GaN channel by the gate. However, Chowdhury et al. in 2008 achieved the first enhancement-mode CAVET with a threshold voltage of 0.6 V through CF4 treatment in the gate region prior to gate metallization. The paper highlights advancements in GaN CAVET technology, including improvements that enable normally-off behavior and higher breakdown voltages.
