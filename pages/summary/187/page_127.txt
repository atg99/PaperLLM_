The paper discusses threshold-voltage (V T) instabilities in isolated-gate AlGaN/GaN MIS-HEMTs, which are designed to suppress gate current, a crucial requirement for power transistors. The study differentiates between unrecessed, partially-recessed, and fully-recessed MIS-HEMTs, emphasizing the role of the gate dielectric and its interface with the semiconductor as potential trapping sites that can affect device performance. 

The analysis of V T instabilities is conducted using negative gate bias (NGB) and positive gate bias (PGB) stress voltages to isolate V T effects from drain access resistance. For normally-on devices, V T stability under NGB is critical, while for normally-off devices, PGB stability is of major concern. NGB measurements are also relevant for normally-off devices as they help prevent false turn-on.

The paper notes that V T shifts can occur in both negative and positive directions, influenced by factors such as applied bias, temperature, and stress time. Negative V T shifts are attributed to electron emission from traps, a decrease in negatively ionized carbon-related acceptors, and hole capture into interface traps. Conversely, positive V T shifts are discussed, although the specific mechanisms for these shifts are not detailed in the provided text. 

Overall, the findings highlight the importance of understanding V T instabilities in the development of reliable GaN-based transistors.
