The paper discusses the degradation mechanisms in GaN-HEMTs (Gallium Nitride High Electron Mobility Transistors) under off-state stress, emphasizing the identification of issues through various analytical methods including TEM cross-section analysis, numerical simulations, and electroluminescence measurements. It highlights the critical role of electric field peaks at the edge of the gate head in causing device failure. 

The authors propose that altering the vertical conductivity in the epilayer can lead to improved device performance by affecting the grounding of the floating buffer and promoting a greater retraction of the two-dimensional electron gas (2DEG). This modification results in significantly lower electric fields in the silicon nitride layer for the second generation of devices, leading to an enhancement in time-to-failure by over three orders of magnitude. 

The findings underline the importance of device geometry and passivation in mitigating field-related failures, offering insights for future GaN-HEMT design improvements.
