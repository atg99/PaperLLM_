The paper discusses the breakdown mechanisms in thin oxide layers, emphasizing the field-assisted defect creation process. It highlights that operating devices at higher gate overdrive levels accelerates degradation, evidenced by a Weibull distribution parameter (β) greater than 1, indicating intrinsic degradation causes. Before catastrophic failure, devices exhibit increased gate leakage currents due to the formation and disruption of metastable conduction paths. While these changes affect gate leakage significantly, their impact on threshold voltage and drain current remains minor. The paper also speculates about hole trapping in the oxide, potentially arising from impact ionization under high electric fields. Overall, the findings underscore the reliability challenges associated with thin oxide layers in electronic devices.
