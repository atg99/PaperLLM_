The paper "Online Meta-Learning" by Chelsea Finn et al. addresses the challenge of continual learning by integrating two research paradigms: meta-learning and online learning. Meta-learning focuses on learning a prior over model parameters for quick adaptation to new tasks, but typically requires tasks to be presented in a batch. Conversely, online learning deals with tasks revealed sequentially but often lacks task-specific adaptation.

The authors propose an online meta-learning framework that combines these approaches to enhance learning efficiency in a dynamic environment where tasks are encountered one at a time. They introduce the Follow the Meta Leader (FTML) algorithm, which extends the Model-Agnostic Meta-Learning (MAML) algorithm to this online setting. Theoretical contributions include an O(log T) regret guarantee under a higher order smoothness assumption, which is an improvement over traditional online learning frameworks.

Experimental results demonstrate that FTML significantly outperforms standard online learning methods across various large-scale problems. The paper emphasizes the importance of leveraging past experiences to improve adaptation to new tasks, showcasing the limitations of both meta-learning and online learning when applied in isolation.
