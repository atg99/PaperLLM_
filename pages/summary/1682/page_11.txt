The references listed cover various aspects of online meta-learning and related fields in machine learning and computer vision. Key contributions include:

1. Szegedy et al. (2016) discuss improvements to the Inception architecture, enhancing performance in computer vision tasks.
2. Thrun (1998) and Thrun & Pratt (1998) introduce lifelong learning algorithms, emphasizing the importance of learning from past experiences to improve future learning.
3. Todorov et al. (2012) present Mujoco, a physics engine that aids in model-based control, facilitating research in robotics.
4. Wang et al. (2016) explore the concept of learning to reinforcement learn, focusing on enhancing reinforcement learning strategies.
5. Wang et al. (2017) propose methods to fine-tune models by increasing their capacity, contributing to improved performance in computer vision.
6. Xiang et al. (2014) introduce a benchmark for 3D object detection, addressing the limitations of existing datasets.
7. Yang et al. (2016) investigate optimal dynamic regret in online learning, providing insights into handling true and noisy gradients.
8. Zenke et al. (2017) present a method for continual learning through synaptic intelligence, enabling models to retain knowledge over time.
9. Zhao & Schmidhuber (1996) discuss incremental self-improvement in multi-agent reinforcement learning, highlighting adaptability in dynamic environments.
10. Zinkevich (2003) addresses online convex programming and gradient ascent, laying foundational concepts for online learning algorithms.

Together, these works contribute to the understanding and advancement of online learning, meta-learning, and their applications in various domains.
