The paper discusses advancements in fruit detection technologies, highlighting the effectiveness of LiDAR sensors and RGB-D cameras. Gené-Mola et al. (2018) found that certain fruits, such as Fuji apples, exhibit higher reflectance compared to leaves and trunks, achieving an 85% detection success rate with LiDAR. RGB-D cameras, which provide both 3D and color data, enable the detection and localization of fruit through techniques like stereo triangulation and structured light or Time-of-Flight (ToF) methods. These systems also measure backscattered light, which correlates to reflectance, suggesting potential for enhanced fruit detection.

Despite the advances in sensor technology, traditional handcrafted features have dominated fruit detection processing. However, the introduction of deep neural networks, particularly Faster R-CNN, has significantly improved object recognition capabilities in this field. Although other networks may offer computational efficiency, Faster R-CNN is preferred for its superior performance with smaller objects, despite its requirement for large labeled datasets, which remains a challenge in fruit detection research.

The section concludes with a brief mention of the Kinect v2 sensor, which operates on the ToF principle, providing a foundation for understanding the theoretical aspects of the technology used in fruit detection.
