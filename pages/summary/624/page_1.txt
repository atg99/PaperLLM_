The paper discusses the significance of fruit detection and localization for improving agronomic management in fruit crops, which is vital for applications such as yield prediction and automated harvesting. It highlights the potential of RGB-D cameras, which provide both color and depth information, in enhancing fruit detection capabilities. The study introduces the KFuji RGB-DS database, comprising 967 multi-modal images with 12,839 Fuji apples, enabling an exploration of the advantages of integrating RGB-D data with radiometric information from a Kinect v2 sensor.

The authors note that the radiometric capability, specifically the backscatter signal intensity, has not been fully utilized in fruit detection. By correcting the signal intensity for range to mitigate signal attenuation, they achieved a reflectance-proportional image. A registration process was implemented to align RGB, depth, and intensity images, and the Faster R-CNN model was modified to accommodate five-channel input images. The results demonstrated an improvement of 4.46% in the F1-score, reaching 0.898 and an average precision of 94.8% when all channels were utilized.

The findings suggest that the radiometric capabilities of time-of-flight sensors provide valuable information for enhancing fruit detection, which is crucial for the future of agricultural robotics and efficient fruit harvesting. The paper emphasizes the need for innovative technologies to address the challenges of increasing fruit production amid rising costs and labor shortages in horticulture.
