The paper presents a comparative analysis of fruit detection using different imaging modalities. Among single modality images, color images yielded the highest performance with an F1-score of 0.829 and an average precision (AP) of 88.7%. Range-corrected intensity images followed with an F1-score of 0.806 and an AP of 85.9%, marking a notable performance for a modality not previously utilized for fruit detection. The depth channel was the least effective, only detecting highly exposed apples.

Combining multiple modalities significantly improved results, achieving an F1-score of 0.866 and an AP of 91.2% with all channels. The integration of the saturation (S) and depth (D) channels notably enhanced precision from 0.808 to 0.869, indicating a reduction in false positives. The S channel proved particularly beneficial in detecting fruits in shadowed areas, where the RGB image struggled.

The best overall performance was observed with multi-modal images incorporating RGB high-resolution, S, and D channels, resulting in an F1-score of 0.898 and an AP of 94.8%. The computational efficiency of the neural network remained unaffected by the number of channels, as the initial layer's operations were minimal compared to the entire network.

While it is challenging to compare methodologies across different datasets, the results align closely with previous studies, which reported F1-scores between 0.838 and 0.929. A key advantage of using RGB-D sensors is the straightforward inference of 3D locations for detected fruits.

The primary limitation noted is the reliance on low illumination conditions, although advancements in sensor technology, such as LiDAR, may address this issue in the future. The paper anticipates that future RGB-D sensors could operate effectively in daylight, enhancing their applicability in various lighting conditions.
