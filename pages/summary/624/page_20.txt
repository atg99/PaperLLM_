The collection of references presented in this document illustrates advancements in precision agriculture, particularly focusing on fruit detection and yield estimation through various imaging technologies and machine learning techniques. Linker (2017) emphasizes a method for estimating the number of green mature apples in nighttime orchard images, which leverages light distribution for accurate yield prediction. Liu et al. (2016) introduce the Single Shot Multibox Detector (SSD), a significant development in real-time object detection that can be applied in agricultural contexts.

Maldonado and Barbosa (2016) discuss automatic counting of green fruits in orange trees, contributing to efficiency in fruit harvesting. Other studies, such as those by Nguyen et al. (2016) and Okamoto and Lee (2009), highlight the use of RGB-D cameras and hyperspectral imaging for detecting specific apple varieties, showcasing the diversity of imaging techniques employed in agriculture.

Rahnemoonfar and Sheppard (2017) present a deep learning approach for fruit counting, emphasizing the role of artificial intelligence in enhancing agricultural practices. The references also include significant methodologies like Faster R-CNN and Yolo9000, which are pivotal for real-time object detection tasks, further underscoring the integration of advanced technology in agricultural monitoring.

Overall, the cited works reflect a growing trend towards automation and precision in agriculture, utilizing various imaging and machine learning techniques to improve fruit detection, monitoring, and yield estimation processes.
