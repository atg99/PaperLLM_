This paper discusses the process of yield estimation through the preparation of data for a convolutional neural network. The primary goal is to ensure that images are registered to avoid counting apples in overlapping areas multiple times, although this specific aim is not the focus of the work.

The data preparation involved several key steps: range correction of the backscattered signal, 2D projection of a 3D point cloud, and registration between the range-corrected intensity images and raw RGB images. A flowchart (Fig. 2) illustrates these steps, showing that each frame from the sensor produces a 3D point cloud with RGB and backscattered intensity data alongside a raw RGB image.

Initially, the intensity signal undergoes range correction. The 3D point cloud is then projected onto a plane parallel to the sensor, producing range-corrected intensity and depth images. Due to the differing vertical field-of-view of the depth sensor and the RGB camera, the top and bottom parts of the projected image, which lack RGB information, are cropped, resulting in a change of aspect ratio from the original dimensions.

To align the RGB image with a higher resolution than the infrared image, the projected images are resized to 1600 x 1080 pixels using bicubic interpolation. This resizing allows for proper registration between the RGB raw image and the projected images, culminating in a 5-channel multi-modal image where each pixel contains information from color (RGB), range-corrected intensity, and depth data. The paper distinguishes between the RGB image obtained after projection (RGB p) and the RGB image obtained post-registration (RGB hr). Finally, to ensure uniformity in mean and variance across channels, adjustments are made to the range-corrected intensity and depth images.
