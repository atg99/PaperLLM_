This paper introduces a new methodology for fruit detection utilizing RGB-D sensors, leveraging their radiometric capabilities. The approach involves creating multi-modal images using data from Microsoft's Kinect v2, which includes a range correction of the backscattered intensity signal to address signal attenuation. The study presents the KFuji RGB-DS dataset, the first public dataset for fruit detection that incorporates RGB, depth, and range-corrected intensity channels. 

The effectiveness of fusing these modalities was assessed using the Faster R-CNN object detection network, resulting in a 4.46% improvement in F1-score, increasing from 0.829 with RGB alone to 0.866 with the combined RGB-D-S modalities. This advancement is significant, as it provides comparable results to existing fruit detection methods while also enabling the inference of the 3D position of detected fruits. 

The research further analyzed the optimal anchor scales for the region proposal network, determining that anchor scales of 4 and an aspect ratio of 1:1 are best suited for the spherical, small fruits in the KFuji dataset. A noted limitation is the reduced performance of depth sensors in direct sunlight. Future work will focus on 3D fruit localization using depth data and the collection of diverse fruit variety data at various growth stages.
