The proposed AugGPT framework leverages ChatGPT for data augmentation, where it generates new samples that maintain semantic consistency with existing labeled instances. This process is followed by training a BERT-based sentence classifier on both the few-shot and generated samples to evaluate classification performance.

While recent advancements in prompt-tuning and meta-learning have emerged, they face significant challenges. Prompt engineering is often labor-intensive and requires expertise, while meta-learning can be unstable and sensitive to hyper-parameters. These complexities hinder accessibility for average practitioners. The AugGPT method addresses these limitations by effectively generating comprehensive training samples, thus enhancing the FSL landscape in NLP.

The paper also discusses the evolution of pre-trained language models (PLMs), particularly large models like GPT-3 and PaLM, which have set new performance benchmarks across various tasks. These models, characterized by their vast number of parameters, are designed to learn context-dependent and domain-specific feature representations. Unlike smaller models that require continuous fine-tuning, very large models can deliver strong performance without it, further streamlining the deployment of NLP applications. Overall, the findings underscore the potential of AugGPT and large PLMs in advancing few-shot learning methodologies.
