The paper discusses the potential harms of using AI models like GPT-4, particularly in relation to representation and bias. It highlights concerns about stereotypes and demeaning content, especially in sensitive contexts such as government decision-making and resource allocation. The authors note that while GPT-4's usage policies prohibit its application in high-risk areas, disparities in performance across different demographics can still lead to unequal quality of service.

The paper also addresses strategies to mitigate bias, such as training the model to refuse certain prompts that may elicit stereotypical or harmful content. However, these refusals can sometimes exacerbate bias or create a misleading sense of reliability, particularly if the model's refusal behavior varies across demographic groups.

An example is provided to illustrate how the model's responses can reflect biases regarding marriage norms, specifically favoring heterosexual representations. The authors caution that as AI systems become more integrated into knowledge discovery and learning, they risk reinforcing existing ideologies and limiting opportunities for critical reflection and improvement unless proactive governance measures are implemented. Overall, the paper emphasizes the need for careful evaluation and governance of AI systems to ensure equitable and fair outcomes.
