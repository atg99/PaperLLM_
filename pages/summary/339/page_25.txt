The paper discusses the evaluation of text and multimodal models, specifically GPT-3.5 and GPT-4, on various standardized exams that may include images. For text-only models, an IMAGE: tag was used to indicate the absence of images, allowing for performance assessment under these conditions. In contrast, multimodal models had images embedded in the prompts. The exams evaluated included the SAT, GRE, AP exams, and others, with free-response questions transcribed to eliminate images and streamline grading.

Scoring methodologies for the exams were approximated using official charts and calculators, converting raw scores into scaled scores appropriate for each test. Percentile rankings were derived from the most recent available data, with specific methods used for exams lacking updated distributions.

For Codeforces rating evaluation, models were tested across multiple contests, with ELO ratings adjusted based on performance until equilibrium was reached. The results indicated that many simulations resulted in low ELO ratings, with GPT-4 achieving a maximum rating of around 1300.

Details on model snapshots reveal that different versions of GPT-4 were used for various question types, while no significant impact of Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) on GPT-4's capabilities was found.
