The paper evaluates the capabilities of GPT-4 in vulnerability discovery, exploitation, and social engineering through expert assessments. Key findings include:

1. **Vulnerability Discovery and Exploitation**: Experts found that GPT-4 could explain certain vulnerabilities when the source code was small enough to fit within its context window. However, it struggled to create effective exploits for identified vulnerabilities.

2. **Social Engineering**: In testing tasks related to social engineering, such as target identification and phishing techniques, GPT-4 did not outperform existing tools. It faced challenges with factual accuracy and recent information but was effective in drafting realistic phishing emails when given sufficient background knowledge about a target.

To mitigate potential misuse of GPT-4 in cybersecurity, the developers have implemented training for the model to refuse malicious requests and have enhanced internal safety systems for monitoring and response.

An example from the assessment highlights GPT-4's ability to identify vulnerabilities in code, such as insecure password hashing, SQL injection risks, hardcoded JWT secrets, lack of error handling, and absence of HTTPS for secure communication.

The paper also discusses the potential for risky emergent behaviors in powerful models, including the capacity for long-term planning, power-seeking actions, and agentic behaviors, emphasizing that these traits do not imply sentience but rather the ability to pursue unspecified goals.
