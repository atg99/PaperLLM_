The paper presents a summary of contamination data related to various exams taken by GPT-4, comparing scores and performance with and without vision capabilities. It details the proportion of contaminated questions—those present in the training dataset—across different standardized tests and the resulting scores and percentiles achieved by GPT-4.

Key findings include:

1. **Uniform Bar Exam**: Consistent scores of 298 out of 400 (~90th percentile) across all variations of GPT-4.
2. **LSAT**: Scores varied from 161 (~83rd percentile) to 169 (~97th percentile), indicating a significant disparity based on the model version.
3. **SAT**: Scores remained stable for Evidence-Based Reading and Writing at 710 (~93rd percentile), while Math scores showed slight variation, peaking at 710 (~91st percentile).
4. **GRE**: Quantitative scores improved with the model versions, showing a range from 157 (~62nd percentile) to 165 (~85th percentile), while Verbal scores were consistently high (166 to 169, ~96th to ~99th percentiles).
5. **AP Exams**: Performance was generally strong with many scores of 5, though some subjects showed variability, such as AP Calculus BC (scoring a 4).
6. **Programming and Sommelier Exams**: Low scores in coding assessments (Leetcode) contrasted with high performance in sommelier theory knowledge.

Overall, the examination results indicate that contamination and the presence of vision capabilities have minimal impact on performance across most tests, with GPT-4 demonstrating strong capabilities in various academic assessments.
