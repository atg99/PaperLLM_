The introduction of the paper discusses the growing significance of large language models (LLMs) in various aspects of daily life, highlighting their potential societal impacts. It specifically analyzes GPT-4, the latest model in the GPT series, which has undergone extensive evaluation and iterative improvement since its training completion in August 2022. The authors emphasize a balanced deployment approach that aims to minimize risks while promoting beneficial applications and learning from real-world use.

Training of GPT models occurs in two stages: initial training on a vast corpus of internet text to predict the next word, followed by fine-tuning through reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) to enhance output quality. This process has enabled capabilities such as few-shot learning and proficiency in diverse natural language tasks.

The paper outlines safety challenges associated with GPT-4, focusing on two model versions: an early variant optimized for instruction following (GPT-4-early) and a later version designed for increased helpfulness and safety (GPT-4-launch). While both versions share risks typical of smaller models, GPT-4 also poses unique challenges due to its advanced capabilities. Notable risks include the potential generation of harmful content, the perpetuation of societal biases, and the creation of vulnerable code.

To assess these risks, the authors consulted over 50 experts, aiming to deepen their understanding of GPT-4's deployment risks. The document serves as a call for further work in safety measurement and mitigation, emphasizing the importance of proactive governance and safety research in the context of deploying advanced AI technologies.
