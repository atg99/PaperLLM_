The paper discusses the process of fine-tuning GPT-4 through supervised learning and reinforcement learning to enhance its behavior and safety. Initially, demonstration data is used for supervised fine-tuning (SFT), while ranking data helps train a reward model (RM) that predicts user preferences for model outputs. This RM is employed as a reward signal during reinforcement learning, specifically using the Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) algorithm. 

To improve the model's responses, specific instructions are given to contractors to encourage appropriate refusals to certain prompts, especially in sensitive areas like medical and legal advice. Despite these efforts, the model remains somewhat brittle, occasionally displaying overly cautious behavior or refusing benign requests.

To refine the model's behavior further, rule-based reward models (RBRMs) are introduced. These RBRMs utilize a GPT-4 classifier to assess model outputs against human-written rubrics, categorizing responses into various classifications, such as desired refusals or unwanted evasions. This classification helps guide the model's responses to harmful or benign requests.

The training dataset is enhanced through various means, including prompts from human reviewers, red teamers, and synthetic prompts. By combining RBRM signals with the RM, the authors address conflicting data and improve the model's ability to handle edge cases by generating boundary prompts that are similar to disallowed content but do not request it.

Overall, the methods implemented have significantly improved GPT-4's safety compared to earlier versions, reducing its tendency to engage with harmful content by 82% relative to GPT-3.5. Furthermore, the model aligns with safety policies regarding sensitive requests 29% more frequently. The paper concludes that while the measures taken have enhanced the model's safety, challenges such as "jailbreaks" remain an ongoing concern.
