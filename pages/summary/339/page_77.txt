The cited works collectively explore the implications of artificial intelligence (AI) on various socio-economic aspects, including income distribution, productivity, and employment dynamics. Krueger et al. discuss hidden incentives related to distributional shifts caused by automation. DeCanio examines whether robots serve as complements or substitutes for human labor, while Korinek and Stiglitz delve into AI's effects on income distribution and unemployment. 

Choi et al. investigate the integration of AI in legal education, whereas Raymond et al. analyze how augmented intelligence influences productivity and work practices. Research by van Inwegen et al. shows that algorithmic assistance in resume writing can enhance hiring rates. Ziegler et al. assess the productivity improvements associated with neural code completion, and Noy and Zhang provide experimental evidence on generative AI's productivity effects.

Peng et al. focus on AI's impact on developer productivity, specifically through tools like GitHub Copilot. Acemoglu and Restrepo discuss the relationship between demographics and automation, while the Partnership on AI reports on AI's influence on job quality. Additional resources include foundational documents like the OpenAI Charter and literature on AI development models.

Overall, these studies highlight the multifaceted interactions between AI technologies and economic factors, stressing both potential benefits and challenges posed by their integration into the workforce and productivity frameworks.
