The paper discusses the potential for language models, specifically GPT-4, to generate harmful content that violates policies and poses risks to individuals and society. It emphasizes that the assessment of harm is context-dependent, but certain content areas are inherently harmful. Examples of such harmful content include hate speech, self-harm encouragement, graphic material, harassment, and instructions for illegal activities. The authors highlight efforts made to mitigate these risks through model refusals.

Furthermore, the paper addresses the issue of bias in language models, noting that both GPT-4-early and GPT-4-launch can perpetuate stereotypes and reinforce social biases. The evaluation of these models revealed a tendency to reproduce harmful associations with marginalized groups. It also discusses the complexities of defining "harmful" or "toxic" content, as mislabeling can lead to negative consequences for users, particularly in cases of bias in classifiers.

The authors caution against oversimplifying the classification of content, noting that interpretations of "unwanted" content can vary based on user expectations and provider policies. They emphasize the need for careful consideration of the implications of content filtering and the justifications behind it. Overall, the paper illustrates the multifaceted challenges associated with harmful content generation and the biases embedded in language models.
