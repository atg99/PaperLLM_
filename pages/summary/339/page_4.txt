The paper discusses the evaluation of GPT-4's performance on the HumanEval benchmark and various standardized exams, highlighting the relationship between model size and problem-solving capabilities. It establishes that for a subset of problems, predictions regarding pass rates can be made based on prior performance of smaller models. The authors report that their predictions for the third easiest difficulty bucket were highly accurate, although GPT-4 underperformed expectations in the easiest bucket. 

The paper also touches on the challenges of predicting certain capabilities, particularly in the context of the Inverse Scaling Prize, where model performance can decrease with scale. However, GPT-4 defies this trend in specific tasks, such as Hindsight Neglect, indicating its unique capabilities.

Furthermore, the authors emphasize the importance of predicting future capabilities for safety reasons and express a commitment to refining their methodologies for future evaluations. They tested GPT-4 using a variety of exams without specific training, assessing its performance through a combination of multiple-choice and free-response questions. The results are reported in terms of percentile rankings, providing a benchmark for understanding GPT-4's capabilities relative to human performance. Overall, the paper seeks to establish a framework for evaluating model performance and improving predictive accuracy in future developments.
