The section discusses the dual-use potential of large language models (LLMs), particularly GPT-4, in the context of conventional and unconventional weapons proliferation. The study involved stress testing and red teaming to determine if these models could assist proliferators in developing or acquiring nuclear, radiological, biological, and chemical weapons. While access to GPT-4 alone is not sufficient for proliferation, it can significantly reduce the time required to gather difficult-to-find information, making it more accessible to individuals and non-state actors lacking formal scientific training.

The findings indicate that GPT-4 can provide insights into proliferation pathways, historical cases of successful proliferation, security measures for dual-use materials, and even the basic components needed for creating radiological dispersal devices. The model demonstrated the ability to re-engineer publicly available biochemical compounds and identify mutations affecting pathogenicity, although it could not be compelled to create new substances.

Red teamers noted that the model's capability to critique acquisition strategies could be advantageous for threat actors, offering information on facility rentals and companies potentially violating export restrictions. The integration of GPT-4 with other tools could further enhance its utility for malicious purposes. The analysis underscores the importance of understanding the risks associated with advanced AI technologies in relation to weapons proliferation.
