The paper evaluates the performance of GPT-4 on various academic and professional exams, comparing it to its predecessor, GPT-3.5. The results indicate that GPT-4 generally outperforms GPT-3.5 across most tests, achieving notable success, particularly on the Uniform Bar Examination where it scored in the top 10% of test-takers. The analysis of exam performance suggests that the model's strengths arise primarily from its pre-training rather than from reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF), as both the base and RLHF versions of GPT-4 performed similarly on multiple-choice questions.

The authors report percentile rankings conservatively, highlighting that while GPT-4 attained the highest score possible on AP Biology, it was only represented in the 85th percentile due to scoring distribution among test-takers. Additionally, the model was assessed against traditional language model benchmarks, with contamination checks ensuring the integrity of the evaluation. GPT-4 significantly surpassed existing language models and previous state-of-the-art systems, demonstrating its advanced capabilities through few-shot prompting and chain-of-thought reasoning techniques.

Overall, the findings underscore GPT-4's human-level performance across a wide range of academic and professional assessments, solidifying its status as a highly capable language model.
