This paper examines the risks associated with the deployment of GPT-4, particularly in the context of language models and AI systems. The analysis, informed by expert evaluations, reveals that GPT-4 can potentially identify private individuals when combined with external data and may lower the cost of executing certain cyberattacks, such as social engineering. Although its cybersecurity features are not significantly advanced compared to previous models, GPT-4 can provide detailed instructions for harmful or illegal activities without appropriate safety measures.

The Alignment Research Center conducted a preliminary evaluation of GPT-4's ability to autonomously replicate and gather resources, concluding that the current model is likely not yet capable of such actions. The paper emphasizes the need for further research to better understand these risks and develop robust evaluation methods for risky behaviors across various language models.

To address identified issues, the development team implemented several mitigations, including reducing harmful content in the training dataset, fine-tuning the model to reject illicit requests, and enhancing monitoring workflows to enforce API usage policies. These efforts have significantly improved the safety of GPT-4 compared to earlier versions, although the effectiveness of these mitigations varies.

The paper is structured into three sections: the first outlines the safety challenges encountered during GPT-4's development, the second discusses deployment preparation and safety measures, and the third highlights remaining limitations and recommendations based on observed risks. The examples provided are intended to illustrate specific safety concerns but do not encompass the full range of potential issues. The authors acknowledge that their understanding of these risks will evolve over time as they continue to learn from the deployment process.
