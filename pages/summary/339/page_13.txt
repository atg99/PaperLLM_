The paper discusses improvements made in the GPT-4 model to enhance safety and reduce the generation of harmful content. It highlights the model's approach to refusing requests for disallowed content, using examples to illustrate effective and ineffective refusals. The paper outlines a method called the Refusal-Based Reward Model (RBRM), which evaluates responses based on a human-written rubric categorizing them as appropriate refusals or undesired responses. This model has led to an 82% reduction in the generation of disallowed content compared to GPT-3.5, and a 29% increase in adherence to policies regarding sensitive topics. Additionally, GPT-4 demonstrates a significant decrease in producing toxic content, with only 0.73% of its outputs classified as toxic compared to 6.48% for GPT-3.5. Overall, the advancements reflect a focused effort to align the model's behavior with safety guidelines while maintaining its utility.
