The paper discusses the calibration and safety measures implemented in the GPT-4 model, particularly focusing on its performance before and after post-training. Calibration plots illustrate that the pre-trained model exhibits better calibration (ECE: 0.007) compared to the post-trained model (ECE: 0.074), indicating a deterioration in its ability to accurately reflect confidence in its predictions after training.

A critical aspect of the model's safety involves expert red teaming, where over 50 specialists from various fields assessed the model's behavior in high-risk scenarios. Their insights led to improvements in the model's capacity to refuse requests related to synthesizing dangerous substances, highlighting the importance of expert feedback in refining AI behavior.

The paper outlines a model-assisted safety pipeline that includes reinforcement learning with human feedback (RLHF) to enhance the model's alignment with user intent. Despite these efforts, the model still faces challenges with unsafe inputs, sometimes generating undesirable content or being overly cautious with safe requests. To address this, the authors employ an additional set of safety-focused RLHF prompts and rule-based reward models (RBRMs) that function as classifiers, providing extra signals during training to guide the model towards safer responses.
