The paper discusses the process of adversarial testing referred to as "red teaming," which is defined as an organized effort to identify flaws and vulnerabilities in systems, particularly focusing on AI models like GPT-4. The internal red teaming for GPT-4 was conducted on March 10, 2023, with prior testing on similar versions informing the analysis. Red teaming aims to reduce harmful outputs and incorporates external expertise for domain-specific testing.

The approach taken is iterative, starting from an initial hypothesis about high-risk areas, followed by testing and refining the model as new mitigation strategies are integrated. Experts from various fields, including fairness, cybersecurity, and healthcare, were engaged to provide insights into potential deployment risks of GPT-4. This selection process reflects a bias towards individuals with significant educational and professional backgrounds, mainly from English-speaking Western countries, which may influence the interpretation of risks and model behavior.

The experts had access to early versions of GPT-4 and identified initial risks that informed further safety research. While some risks were mitigated through technical and policy measures, many remain, indicating an ongoing learning process about AI-related risks. The paper emphasizes that this qualitative red teaming exercise, while valuable, is not exhaustive in evaluating all possible risks associated with the model.

Additionally, the authors mention utilizing stress and boundary testing methods to uncover edge cases and potential failure modes. There is an effort to clarify terminology around red teaming to avoid confusion and ensure better contextual understanding of the methods employed.
