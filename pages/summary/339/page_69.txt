The paper emphasizes the importance of safe usage and deployment of language models by focusing on real-world contexts, user demographics, and specific use cases. It advocates for thorough evaluations and mitigations to address potential harms and vulnerabilities. The authors stress the need for safety assessments to cover emergent risks associated with advanced capabilities that may arise in future models, suggesting the development of targeted evaluation methods to identify unforeseen risks.

Additionally, the paper highlights the necessity of accounting for capability jumps that may occur through techniques like fine-tuning and chain-of-thought prompting, advocating for a precautionary principle in internal safety testing. The increasing capabilities and adoption of AI models necessitate further research into economic impacts, public participation in decision-making, evaluations of risky emergent behaviors, and enhancing model interpretability and explainability.

The authors call for a greater emphasis on AI literacy, economic and social resilience, and anticipatory governance to ensure responsible societal adoption of language models. They urge OpenAI, other labs, and academia to invest in effective evaluation tools and technical improvements in model safety, noting that progress has been made and further investment is likely to yield additional benefits. The paper concludes by encouraging readers to explore related work on the impacts of language models in various sectors.
