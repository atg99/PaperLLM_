The paper examines the impact of Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) on the capabilities of the GPT-4 model. The evaluation involved comparing the performance of the base GPT-4 model and the RLHF-enhanced version across multiple-choice questions from various standardized exams. The results indicate that the average score for the base model is 73.7%, while the RLHF model slightly improves to 74.0%. This suggests that the RLHF process does not significantly enhance the model's overall capabilities.

Specific exam results show a mix of improvements and declines across different subjects. For instance, the RLHF model outperformed the base model on the LSAT and GRE quantitative sections, but showed declines in some SAT and AP exam scores. The analysis highlights the complexity of comparing free-response questions due to differences in instruction-following abilities.

Additionally, the paper addresses concerns about potential contamination in professional and academic exams by measuring overlaps between evaluation datasets and pre-training data. This analysis is crucial for ensuring the integrity of the model's assessment capabilities. Overall, the findings present a nuanced view of the RLHF process, indicating minimal overall capability change while noting specific areas of performance variation.
