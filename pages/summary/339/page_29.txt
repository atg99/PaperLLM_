The paper discusses the methodology for evaluating the contamination of training data in language models, specifically focusing on substring matching to identify contaminated examples. The authors randomly select three substrings of 50 characters from evaluation examples to check if they match any substrings from the training data. This process can lead to false negatives and false positives due to the limited information used from the evaluation examples, as only partial data is considered. 

Despite these limitations, the impact of contamination on results is minimal, as shown in the provided tables. The paper also addresses contamination in academic benchmarks, using a similar methodology to measure cross-contamination. 

In the context of improving GPT-4’s mathematical reasoning, the authors incorporated data from the MATH and GSM-8K training sets. They ensured that the test set for GSM-8K was not included in the training set and recommend interpreting the performance results as a blend of true few-shot transfer and specific tuning.

For the multilingual MMLU evaluation, the authors translated questions and answers using an external translation model to avoid potential biases from GPT-4's own translations. They noted that translation imperfections could affect performance, with some translations preserving proper nouns in English. The evaluation is structured using a three-shot prompt format to accommodate varying token lengths across languages, and the correct answer is classified based on the highest probability from the model's output. 

Overall, the paper highlights the challenges and methodologies in assessing data contamination and performance in language models, particularly in mathematical reasoning and multilingual contexts.
