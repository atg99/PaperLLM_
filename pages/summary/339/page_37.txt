The InstructGPT paper discusses enhancing large language models to better follow human instructions through the incorporation of human feedback. The authors argue that simply increasing model size does not improve adherence to user intent, as larger models can produce outputs that are inaccurate or inappropriate.

To improve model performance, they employ a three-step training process:

1. **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)**: The authors gather demonstration data consisting of prompts and desired responses crafted by labelers. This data is used to fine-tune the GPT-3 model through supervised learning.

2. **Reward Model (RM) Training**: They collect comparison data where labelers rank outputs from various model samples. This information helps train a reward model that evaluates the quality of outputs.

3. **Reinforcement Learning with Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)**: The model's policy is optimized against the reward model using reinforcement learning. For each prompt, the model generates an output, which is then scored by the reward model. The resulting reward informs updates to the policy via the PPO algorithm.

This iterative process aims to align the InstructGPT model more closely with human preferences, resulting in improved truthfulness and reduced toxicity in outputs, while maintaining performance on standard NLP tasks. The authors suggest that this approach of fine-tuning with human feedback shows promise for better aligning language models with user intent.
