At the nanoscale, advancements in GPU systems and multi-core architectures are facilitating deep learning applications. Meanwhile, exascale data centers, such as those exemplified by Google's DeepMind acquisition, are enabling vast storage and processing capabilities for video and imaging content.

A key inquiry raised is whether the interconnected nature of large cloud platforms resembles brain-like functionality. Historical references, such as Peter Russell's 1995 proposition, suggest that the evolving global network of humans could lead to a new stage in human development. Recent studies indicate that the Internet of Things, supported by powerful cloud clusters running machine learning, may indeed act as a worldwide brain-like system.

The paper draws parallels between the Internet and brain anatomy, highlighting similarities in specialization, modular architecture, plasticity, and learning processes. It posits that the aggregation of information on the Internet can lead to oscillatory behaviors akin to brain synchronization, fostering new communication pathways and rapid learning. This leads to the concept of Cloudets, which emphasizes automated self-configuration and resource optimization in cloud platforms through machine learning.

Additionally, the paper contrasts data correlation with information diversity across various scientific disciplines. Traditional statistical methods for measuring similarity, such as Pearson’s correlation and Kendall’s tau, are noted alongside the emerging need for information diversity metrics in fields like machine learning and artificial intelligence. Cognitive diversity is introduced as a concept for assessing dissimilarity in scoring systems, with applications in visual cognition, information retrieval, target tracking, and feature selection. Overall, the paper underscores the significance of these developments in enhancing the capabilities of intelligent cloud computing and machine learning systems.
