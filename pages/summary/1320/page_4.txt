This paper discusses Cognitive Informatics (CI), a concept introduced by Wang, which explores the interplay between natural intelligence and brain function through both theoretical and computational lenses. CI employs denotational mathematics to model the brain's mechanisms, contributing to the development of cognitive computers. 

The paper emphasizes the concept of Abstract Intelligence (αI), which defines intelligence as the ability to transform information into behaviors at various levels, including neurological and cognitive. αI serves as a foundational theory for understanding the brain and its processes, positing that intelligence is a complex mechanism that operates through a hierarchical framework. 

Wang's work identifies 52 cognitive processes within the Layered Reference Model of the Brain (LRMB), spanning sensation to intelligence, which aids in understanding cognitive behaviors. The distinction between cognitive computers and traditional data processors is highlighted; while the latter rely on Boolean algebra, cognitive computers utilize advanced mathematical models for knowledge processing. The paper details various applications of cognitive computing, including cognitive robots and machine learning systems, indicating a significant potential for advancement in these fields.
