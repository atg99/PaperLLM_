The paper discusses the limitations of traditional first-order logic in achieving meaningful semantic interpretation of language, emphasizing the need for a comprehensive ontology that reflects human knowledge and relationships among objects. A prototype ontology has been developed to support applications in cognitive computing, which is proposed as the future direction for advancements in artificial intelligence and cognitive science.

In addressing the impact of big data, the paper highlights the growing importance of cognitive computing in managing the vast amounts of data generated by service information systems, particularly in healthcare. It emphasizes the necessity of human-like interpretation, specifically through the concept of granulation, which allows for varying levels of data abstraction. This is crucial for effectively analyzing medical big data, which can be overwhelming in scope.

The paper also explores the concept of perpetual learning, a framework for machine learning that enables systems to continuously improve their performance over time in dynamic environments. This approach is essential for intelligent agents to adapt to unforeseen circumstances and evolving situations. The authors outline the dimensions of a perpetual learning agent, which include learning stimuli, tasks, experiences, and performance metrics, emphasizing the system's ability to adapt and enhance its capabilities autonomously.
