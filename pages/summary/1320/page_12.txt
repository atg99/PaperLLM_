The collection of works by Wang Y. spans various aspects of cognitive informatics and computational intelligence, contributing significantly to the understanding of knowledge representation, cognitive computing, and the intersection of mathematics with these fields. Wang's research includes the development of models such as the OAR Model of Neural Informatics, which addresses internal knowledge representation in the brain. He explores denotational mathematics as a foundational framework for cognitive computing and machine reasoning, emphasizing its relevance in understanding cognitive processes.

Wang's investigations into cognitive robots and their authentication mechanisms highlight the practical applications of cognitive theories in technology. His works also delve into the semantics of cognitive computing, proposing formal frameworks that bridge cognitive linguistics and machine learning. Notably, his research on neuroinformatics models emphasizes the mapping of cognitive functions to neurophysiological structures, enhancing the understanding of human memory.

Overall, Wang's contributions advocate for a unified theory that integrates natural, artificial, and computational intelligence, proposing a paradigm shift in how intelligence is conceptualized and applied across various domains. His emphasis on contemporary mathematics as a methodology for science and technology underscores the importance of mathematical frameworks in advancing cognitive and computational sciences.
