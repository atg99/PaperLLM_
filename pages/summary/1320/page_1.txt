The paper discusses the concept of abstract intelligence (aI), which arises from foundational studies in denotational mathematics and mathematical engineering. It presents a framework that models both natural and computational intelligence within the realms of cognitive informatics (CI) and cognitive computing (CC). The authors assert that aI has catalyzed significant advancements in cognitive systems, including cognitive computers, robots, neural networks, and learning mechanisms. The insights shared in this paper are derived from a plenary panel at the IEEE ICCI*CC'16 conference, featuring contributions from leading experts in CI and CC. The discussion emphasizes the importance of mathematical models in advancing understanding and applications of cognitive systems. Key terms highlighted include abstract intelligence, cognitive engineering, and brain-inspired systems, underscoring the intersection of mathematics and cognitive technologies.
