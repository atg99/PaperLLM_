This paper discusses agent systems designed for perpetual learning, which enables agents to improve their problem-solving abilities over time. Learning is stimulated by inconsistencies or anomalies in an agent's knowledge, which highlight deficiencies in their ability to perform tasks. The author references Henri Poincaré's assertion that contradictions drive scientific inquiry, suggesting that inconsistencies reveal gaps in knowledge that prompt learning episodes.

Upon identifying an inconsistency, agents engage in learning episodes where specific heuristics are applied to resolve the contradiction. This process allows agents to refine and augment their knowledge, ultimately enhancing their performance in future tasks. The paper outlines key characteristics of STEP learning agents, including that learning is triggered by inadequacies in task performance, occurs in discrete episodes, and requires stimulus-specific algorithms tailored to the identified deficiencies.

The conclusion synthesizes insights from a plenary panel on cognitive informatics, emphasizing the foundational theories in cognitive computing and intelligence science. It highlights advancements in areas such as cognitive robots, deep learning, and cognitive engineering, showcasing the dynamic interplay between theoretical developments and practical applications in the field.
