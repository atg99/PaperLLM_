The paper discusses various perspectives on cognitive computing, big data cognition, and machine learning, highlighting significant advancements and ongoing research in these fields. It emphasizes the impact of the AlphaGo deep learning system, which defeated human experts in Go, showcasing the potential of big data in cognitive computing.

The concept of big data, initially defined by the three Vs—Volume, Velocity, and Variety—has evolved into a more comprehensive model of five Vs, including Veracity and Value. However, the author suggests an alternative characterization that includes Volume, Velocity, Distribution, Randomness, and Regularity. This aligns with a formal definition of big data encompassing diverse aspects such as complexity, storage, and processing costs.

The paper also examines basic research on self-driving technology, particularly focusing on drivers' behavior through real-time data collected by driving recorders. The study employs meta-cognition to analyze driver characteristics and behaviors on highways, ultimately contributing to the development of next-generation self-driving systems based on human driving patterns.

Finally, the author argues for the importance of cognitive computing in understanding and formalizing human intelligence, advocating for a balanced approach that prioritizes comprehension of cognitive processes over mere exploitation of existing methods. The paper raises critical questions about the limitations of statistical methods in natural language understanding, emphasizing the need for deeper insights into the meanings of texts.
