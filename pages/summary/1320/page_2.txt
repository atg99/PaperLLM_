The paper presents an overview of Cognitive Informatics (CI) and Cognitive Computing (CC), highlighting their interdisciplinary nature and foundational theories. CI investigates the brain's internal information processing mechanisms and their applications in cognitive computing and engineering. CC represents a new paradigm in intelligent computing that utilizes cognitive methodologies to mimic brain functions. The paper notes the significance of the IEEE ICCI*CC’16 conference, which focused on topics like cognitive computing, big data cognition, and machine learning.

The theoretical framework discussed encompasses brain and intelligence sciences, organized into a hierarchical structure that includes abstract intelligence, cognitive informatics, brain informatics, and neuroinformatics. This framework posits that understanding the brain requires a multidisciplinary approach, integrating insights from various fields such as neuroscience, cognitive science, and artificial intelligence. Neuroinformatics serves as the foundational level, exploring basic forms and mechanisms of natural intelligence at neurological and physiological levels.

The paper summarizes the position statements from a plenary panel at the conference, emphasizing that the views expressed may not represent a consensus among all participants. Key methodologies, such as mathematical engineering, are identified as essential for analyzing and modeling the complexities of intelligence systems.
