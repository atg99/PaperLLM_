The paper discusses the transdisciplinary field of 3NI, which focuses on the neurological models and neural representations of genetic information through DNA and acquired information via cognitive neurology and neurocomputation. It highlights key concepts in Neural Informatics (NI), including neural models, signaling theory, neural circuits, and the representation of memory and knowledge.

Brain Informatics (BI) is introduced as the second level of brain studies, building upon NI to explore the physiological mechanisms of brain information processing. BI employs computational and imaginative technologies to understand the complexities of the human brain, emphasizing the need for contemporary denotational mathematics to tackle its intricate nature.

The paper critiques traditional approaches in cognitive psychology and brain science, which often rely on empirical relationships without a robust logical framework. This limitation has spurred interest in higher-level cognitive and abstract intelligence theories to provide a more comprehensive understanding of brain function.

Cognitive Informatics (CI) is identified as the third level in the hierarchical framework of brain/intelligence science, integrating insights from both NI and BI. The theoretical framework presented in the paper aims to advance the understanding of brain and intelligence sciences through a structured approach.
