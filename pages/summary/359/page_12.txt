The paper presents a comparative analysis of traditional machine learning models and a fine-tuned XLNet model on two datasets, CVSS v3 and CVSS v2, focusing on various performance metrics including accuracy, precision, recall, and F1 score.

For the CVSS v3 dataset, XLNet outperforms traditional models across multiple metrics. It achieves an accuracy of 0.9625, precision of 0.8932, recall of 0.8685, and an F1 score of 0.8800. In contrast, other models such as Decision Tree and Logistic Regression show lower accuracy and performance in precision, recall, and F1 scores.

Similarly, in the CVSS v2 dataset, XLNet continues to lead with an accuracy of 0.9711 and a precision of 0.9216, while traditional models like Decision Tree and Logistic Regression also perform well but do not match XLNet's overall effectiveness. 

The findings indicate that fine-tuned XLNet is superior in handling vulnerability scoring, suggesting its potential for improved performance in predictive modeling tasks within cybersecurity applications.
