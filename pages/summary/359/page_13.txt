This paper presents a comprehensive analysis of various models' performance on the CVSS v3.1 and CVSS v2 datasets. The results highlight the effectiveness of the proposed method, which outperforms related approaches in multiple metrics, including accuracy, precision, recall, and F1 score.

In the CVSS v3.1 dataset, the proposed method achieved an accuracy of 0.9625, significantly higher than the models by Shahid M. R. and Costa J. C. The precision and recall metrics also showed strong performance, with values of 0.8931 and 0.8685 respectively. The F1 score reached 0.8800, indicating a balanced performance across precision and recall.

For the CVSS v2 dataset, the proposed method similarly excelled, recording an accuracy of 0.971, a precision of 0.922, a recall of 0.897, and an F1 score of 0.909. These results further establish the superiority of the proposed method compared to various traditional machine learning algorithms and pre-trained models, including CNNs and BiLSTMs.

The analysis, supported by visual data representation, reinforces the conclusion that the XLNet model utilized in this study surpasses other models in assessment metrics, demonstrating its effectiveness in vulnerability assessment tasks. Overall, the findings suggest that the proposed methodology achieves superior performance levels in comparison to previous studies in the field.
