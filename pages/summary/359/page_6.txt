The paper discusses advancements in vulnerability assessment methodologies, particularly focusing on the shortcomings of the Common Vulnerability Scoring System (CVSS) and proposing an improved approach using the XLNet model. It highlights the limitations of existing studies, which often rely on a single CVSS version and do not consider the interrelationships between different vulnerability metrics. Researchers have attempted to enhance vulnerability prioritization through various methods, such as the Exploit Prediction Scoring System (EPSS) and deep learning algorithms. However, these approaches often overlook the contextual information that can enrich predictions.

The study introduces a novel vulnerability metric prediction method utilizing the XLNet model, which effectively captures contextual characteristics from vulnerability descriptions. Key contributions of the research include: 

1. The introduction of transfer learning within vulnerability assessment, leveraging the maturity of pre-trained models to improve performance in this area.
2. The use of the XLNet model to incorporate contextual information, enhancing the accuracy of classification outcomes compared to traditional machine learning techniques.
3. The development of datasets for both CVSS v2 and v3.1, enabling concurrent investigation and providing actionable metric value suggestions to assessors, thereby streamlining the vulnerability severity assessment process.

Overall, the paper aims to enhance the efficiency and effectiveness of vulnerability assessments in cybersecurity.
