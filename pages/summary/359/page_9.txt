The paper discusses the use of the fine-tuned XLNet model for predicting vulnerability metrics based on token embeddings. The token embedding of layer l from the fine-tuned XLNet is denoted as e[l], which is derived from a sequence of tokens t. The vulnerability metrics are predicted using the softmax function, converting prediction results into probabilities between 0 and 1.

The research utilizes data from the US National Security Vulnerability Database, covering vulnerabilities from 1999 to May 2022. Two datasets were created: one for CVSS version 2.0 with 174,838 vulnerabilities and another for CVSS version 3.1 with 101,519 vulnerabilities. The datasets were split into training (80%) and testing (20%) sets, with most vulnerability descriptions being concise, averaging around 43 to 40 words per sample.

Hyperparameter selection was performed using grid search, finding that a learning rate of 5 × 10^-5 and 3 epochs yielded the best convergence for the loss function. The effectiveness of the proposed method was assessed through comparative studies against other pre-trained models and traditional machine learning algorithms, using metrics such as accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score to evaluate performance. Each metric's best-performing model is highlighted in the results.
