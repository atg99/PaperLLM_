The section on hyperparameter selection in this paper describes the use of the grid search method to identify optimal hyperparameters for a model. It highlights the importance of the learning rate, noting that a high learning rate leads to non-convergence, while a low learning rate results in slow convergence and reduced learning efficiency. The experiments indicated the best convergence of the loss function occurred with three epochs and a learning rate of 5 x 10^-5.

The paper also presents a comparative study to assess the effectiveness of the proposed XLNet model against other pre-trained models and traditional machine learning algorithms. It details three types of experiments: comparing XLNet to other pre-trained models (BERT, ROBERTA, DISTILBERT), comparing XLNet to traditional machine learning models (decision tree, nearest neighbor, multilayer perceptron, plain Bayesian, logistic regression), and comparing results with similar studies. Performance metrics used in the evaluation include accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score, with the best-performing models highlighted in bold. Tables are provided to illustrate the effects of these models on different datasets (v3.1 and v2).
