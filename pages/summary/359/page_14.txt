This paper presents a comprehensive analysis of the XLNet model for predicting vulnerability metrics, specifically focusing on its performance against various machine learning algorithms. The results indicate that the fine-tuned XLNet model outperforms traditional models in key evaluation metrics such as accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score, as illustrated in the bar charts (Figures 6 and 7). The study emphasizes the model's ability to leverage extensive domain knowledge from a large-scale corpus, which mitigates data limitations in downstream tasks.

Despite its strengths, the findings reveal that the XLNet model does not significantly surpass logistic regression in prediction impact and exhibits limitations in interpretability. Future research directions include exploring the integration of pre-trained and traditional models to enhance outcomes and improve model interpretability to provide more convincing outputs.

In conclusion, the proposed method for rapid vulnerability assessment using the XLNet pre-trained model addresses the challenges of time-consuming manual assessments, demonstrating its potential utility in cybersecurity contexts.
