The paper discusses the effectiveness of a fine-tuned XLNet model for predicting vulnerability metrics, specifically the Common Vulnerability Scoring System (CVSS). The study highlights that the XLNet model, leveraging pre-trained knowledge from a large-scale corpus, significantly enhanced prediction capabilities compared to traditional machine learning methods. However, it notes that the XLNet model's performance is not markedly superior to logistic regression and suffers from weak interpretability. Future research aims to combine the strengths of pre-trained and traditional models to improve outcomes and increase model interpretability.

In the conclusions, the authors emphasize the urgency of quickly assessing vulnerability severity due to the annual disclosure of thousands of vulnerabilities. They present a method that fine-tunes the XLNet model on a self-constructed cybersecurity corpus to extract features from vulnerability descriptions, transforming the prediction task into a multi-label classification problem. The results demonstrate that the XLNet model achieves state-of-the-art performance in predicting CVSS metric values based on a dataset of 276,357 vulnerabilities. The study underscores the potential of advanced models in enhancing vulnerability assessment processes while acknowledging the need for improved interpretability in future work.
