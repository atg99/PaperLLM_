The paper "Learning to Transfer Examples for Partial Domain Adaptation" by Zhangjie Cao et al. addresses the challenges of partial domain adaptation (PDA), which involves transferring knowledge from a large labeled source domain to a smaller unlabeled target domain where the target labels are only a subset of the source labels. The authors highlight that the primary challenges in PDA are to effectively transfer relevant examples from the source domain while ignoring irrelevant ones to prevent negative transfer.

To tackle these challenges, the authors propose a unified approach called the Example Transfer Network (ETN). ETN learns domain-invariant representations that facilitate knowledge transfer and incorporates a progressive weighting scheme to assess the transferability of source examples. This approach aims to enhance positive transfer by focusing on shared classes between the source and target domains while mitigating the impact of outlier classes that could lead to negative transfer.

The authors provide thorough evaluations using several benchmark datasets, demonstrating that ETN consistently achieves state-of-the-art performance across various PDA tasks. The findings underscore the potential of ETN in improving domain adaptation processes, particularly in scenarios with limited target domain labels.
