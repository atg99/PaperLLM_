The paper discusses the challenges and advancements in domain adaptation, particularly focusing on Partial Domain Adaptation (PDA). Traditional unsupervised domain adaptation methods assume that source and target domains share identical class labels, which limits their applicability in real-world scenarios where relevant datasets with matching label spaces are often unavailable. PDA addresses this issue by allowing the source label space to be larger than the target label space, enabling knowledge transfer from a comprehensive dataset to a more limited one.

Despite the potential of PDA, existing methods face significant challenges, including negative transfer, where irrelevant source classes negatively impact the target domain's learning process. The paper introduces the Example Transfer Network (ETN), which aims to mitigate negative transfer by automatically evaluating the transferability of source examples based on their similarity to the target domain. This is achieved through a transferability quantifier that enhances the quality of example weighting, allowing for better filtering of irrelevant examples.

ETN also improves upon prior methods by confining the source classifier and domain-adversarial network to a shared label space, promoting positive transfer of relevant examples while mitigating the effects of outliers. The effectiveness of ETN is validated through extensive experiments, demonstrating state-of-the-art performance on various benchmark datasets, including Office-31, Office-Home, ImageNet-1K, and Caltech-256.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the necessity for more sophisticated algorithms in partial domain adaptation to ensure effective knowledge transfer while minimizing the risk of negative transfer.
