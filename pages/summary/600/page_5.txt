The paper introduces a transferability weighting framework aimed at quantifying the transferability of source examples in a domain adaptation context. The methodology involves training an auxiliary domain discriminator, denoted as ˜Gd, which distinguishes between representations of source and target domains. This discriminator uses a loss function similar to a previous equation while excluding the transferability weights. The key assumption is that source examples belonging to shared classes with the target domain are more likely to be classified as target examples than those from outlier classes.

To enhance the transferability quantification, the authors propose integrating discriminative information into the auxiliary domain discriminator by introducing an auxiliary label predictor, ˜Gy. This predictor employs a leaky-softmax activation function to classify source examples while ensuring target examples receive lower probabilities. The output of the discriminator, ˜Gd, is influenced by the predictions from the label predictor, ensuring that it effectively differentiates between shared and outlier classes.

The training of both the auxiliary label predictor and the domain discriminator is accomplished through multitask loss functions, allowing for improved accuracy in quantifying the transferability of each source example. The final transferability measure for each source example is defined as w(xs_i) = 1 - ˜Gd(Gf(xs_i)), where lower outputs from ˜Gd indicate a higher likelihood of being relevant to the target domain. The weights are then normalized within each mini-batch to ensure consistent scaling. This approach aims to produce more accurate and discriminative weights for evaluating the transferability of source data, ultimately enhancing the effectiveness of partial domain adaptation strategies.
