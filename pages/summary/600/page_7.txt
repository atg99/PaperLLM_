The paper presents a comparative analysis of classification accuracy for various methods of Partial Domain Adaptation (PDA) using the ResNet-50 architecture across multiple datasets, namely Office-Home, Office-31, and ImageNet-Caltech. 

In Table 1, results on the Office-Home dataset show that the proposed method, ETN, achieves an average accuracy of 70.45%, outperforming several other methods such as DANN and IWAN. Notably, ETN demonstrates significant robustness against outlier classes, especially in the larger-scale ImageNet-Caltech dataset, as highlighted in Table 2, where it reaches an average accuracy of 96.73%.

Table 3 reveals performance using the VGG architecture on the Office-31 dataset, with ETN again showing strong results. The analysis of ETN’s variants in Table 4 indicates that the inclusion of a classifier and auxiliary components significantly enhances performance by reducing the influence of outlier classes and improving the filtering of irrelevant examples.

The findings suggest that ETN's dual advantages of learning discriminative weights and effectively filtering outlier classes contribute to its superior performance in partial domain adaptation tasks. Additionally, the paper includes feature visualization through t-SNE embeddings, which further illustrates the effectiveness of ETN compared to other methods like DANN and SAN.

Overall, the results substantiate ETN's effectiveness in improving classification accuracy in challenging domain adaptation scenarios, emphasizing its potential for practical applications in machine learning and computer vision.
