This paper introduces the Example Transfer Network (ETN), a novel approach to partial domain adaptation that enhances the transferability of source examples while mitigating the negative impact of outlier classes. The study evaluates the feature representations learned by ETN in comparison to existing methods such as DANN, IWAN, and SAN, revealing that ETN produces clearer clusters and better discriminative performance in the target domain.

Key findings include the observation that as the number of target classes decreases below 23, DANN's performance declines sharply due to increased negative transfer, while SAN shows a more stable degradation. IWAN only outperforms DANN under significant label space non-overlap. ETN demonstrates superior stability and performance across varying numbers of target classes, even outperforming DANN in scenarios with complete label space overlap.

In terms of convergence, ETN consistently achieves the lowest test error compared to the other methods, indicating more efficient and stable training. The weight visualization analysis reveals that ETN assigns greater importance to shared classes and significantly reduces the weights of outlier classes, which contributes to its robust performance.

The paper concludes by emphasizing the effectiveness of ETN in partial domain adaptation tasks, underpinned by its ability to integrate discriminative information into the transferability quantification process. The research is supported by various funding programs in China.
