The paper introduces the Example Transfer Network (ETN) model, which addresses the minimax optimization problem in partial domain adaptation. The ETN model integrates transferability weighting and quantification to enhance performance by transferring relevant examples while minimizing the impact of outlier examples. The model's parameters are derived through a saddle-point solution approach, focusing on optimizing the source classifier and domain discriminator.

The experimental section evaluates the ETN model against state-of-the-art (partial) domain adaptation methods using several datasets, including Office-31 and Office-Home. The results indicate that ETN consistently outperforms other methods, demonstrating its effectiveness in managing negative transfer issues. Key observations include the relative performance of various models, with ETN exhibiting superior capability in differentiating between relevant and outlier classes.

The methodology employs PyTorch for implementation, utilizing a learning rate adjustment strategy and hyper-parameter optimization. The findings suggest that ETN's approach to example transfer significantly enhances classification accuracy across diverse tasks and datasets, further establishing its robustness compared to both traditional and contemporary methods in domain adaptation.
