This paper by Türkoğlu and Hanbay investigates the performance of deep feature extraction versus transfer learning for detecting plant diseases and pests using various deep neural network architectures. The study reveals that models such as ResNet50, despite having fewer layers and connections than InceptionResNetV2, achieved higher performance metrics. 

The authors calculated execution times for both deep feature extraction and transfer learning, demonstrating that deep feature extraction significantly reduced training time compared to transfer learning. For instance, training time for deep feature extraction with AlexNet was nearly ten times lower than that for fine-tuned AlexNet, while ResNet50's deep feature extraction was about five times lower than its fine-tuned version. Testing times were consistent across methods, ranging from 10 to 30 seconds, due to limited test data.

The study utilized nine deep learning architectures, extracting features from fully connected layers and evaluating their performance with SVM, ELM, and KNN classifiers. The findings indicated that deep learning models outperformed traditional methods, with the best accuracy of 97.86% achieved by the ResNet50 model using the SVM classifier. Moreover, deep feature extraction demonstrated lower computational complexity compared to transfer learning models.

Future research will focus on using regional images and enhanced CNN models to further improve classification performance. There is also a goal to develop a mobile application for automatic plant disease diagnosis to aid users with minimal plant knowledge.
