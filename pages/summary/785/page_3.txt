The paper by TÜRKOĞLU and HANBA focuses on the detection of plant diseases using deep convolutional neural networks (CNNs). It highlights the limitations of traditional classification methods based on color, shape, and texture, which often require segmented images and yield low performance when used in isolation. The authors point out that these methods are not practical for real-life applications due to difficulties in obtaining suitable datasets.

In contrast, the paper emphasizes the advantages of deep CNNs, which can perform feature extraction without requiring segmentation, making them more applicable in real-world scenarios. The authors reference various studies that have successfully utilized CNN architectures, such as LeNet, AlexNet, and GoogleNet, for plant disease classification.

The proposed system leverages a dataset of real plant disease and pest images from Turkey. It employs multiple deep learning architectures, including AlexNet, VGG16, VGG19, GoogleNet, ResNet50, ResNet101, Inception V3, InceptionResNetV2, and SqueezeNet, for deep feature extraction. The extracted features are then classified using support vector machine (SVM), extreme learning machine (ELM), and k-nearest neighbors (KNN). The authors also utilize transfer learning by modifying the last layers of the pretrained CNNs to enhance classification performance.

The main contributions of the study include a comprehensive examination of nine popular deep learning networks for classifying plant diseases and pests, alongside a thorough discussion of the results obtained through transfer learning and deep feature extraction methods. Overall, the paper illustrates the potential of deep learning techniques in advancing plant disease detection.
