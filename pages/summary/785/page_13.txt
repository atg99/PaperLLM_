The study by TÜRKOĞLU and HANBA evaluated the performance of various classifiers for plant disease detection using both traditional methods and pretrained convolutional neural networks (CNNs). Traditional methods included Local Binary Patterns (LBP), Histogram of Oriented Gradients (HOG), color features, and Gray Level Co-occurrence Matrix (GLCM), with accuracy scores for classifiers such as SVM, ELM, and KNN reported. The best traditional method accuracy was 70.9% with SVM using LBP features.

In contrast, pretrained CNN models were utilized for deep feature extraction, where AlexNet, VGG16, VGG19, GoogleNet, ResNet50, ResNet101, Inception V3, InceptionResNetV2, and SqueezeNet were examined. The highest classification accuracy from these models was achieved using features extracted from the fc6 layer of AlexNet (95.5% with SVM), followed closely by VGG16 (95.0% with SVM) and VGG19 (94.74% with ELM). The results indicated that SVM and ELM outperformed KNN across all models.

Further analysis revealed that ResNet50 achieved the highest accuracy of 97.86% with SVM. Other models like ResNet101, Inception V3, and SqueezeNet also performed well, particularly with SVM and ELM. Overall, VGG16 produced the highest accuracy among all deep models, while ResNet101 followed closely. The performance of KNN was consistently lower than that of SVM and ELM across all models, highlighting the effectiveness of deep learning approaches in plant disease detection.
