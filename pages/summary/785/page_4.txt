2.2. Traditional classifiers
In addition to deep learning networks, traditional classifiers such as Support Vector Machine (SVM), Extreme Learning Machine (ELM), and K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN) are employed in the proposed architecture for classification. These classifiers are well-established and have been widely used in various applications, providing a comparative baseline against the deep learning methods.

3. Proposed architectures
The proposed architecture integrates deep learning for feature extraction and traditional classifiers for final classification, thus leveraging the strengths of both approaches. The architecture is designed to optimize performance while maintaining a lower computational complexity than transfer-learning-based models.

4. Experimental results
The experimental results demonstrate that the proposed classification architecture achieves higher accuracy in identifying real plant diseases and pests compared to existing transfer-learning approaches. The dataset used comprises 1965 images, categorized into eight clusters, showcasing the method's applicability in real-world scenarios.

5. Discussion and contributions
The findings indicate that the proposed method not only enhances classification accuracy but also reduces computational demands, making it suitable for practical applications in agriculture and plant health monitoring. The study contributes to the field by presenting an effective approach that combines pretrained deep learning networks with traditional classifiers, paving the way for future research in plant disease identification.
