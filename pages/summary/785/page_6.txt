In the paper by TÜRKOĞLU and HANBA, the authors discuss the implementation of Extreme Learning Machines (ELM) for the classification of plant diseases. The mathematical model of ELM is presented, emphasizing the relationship between input-output pairs and the hidden layer's output. The output can be represented in matrix form, with the output weight derived from the hidden layer's output matrix using the Moore–Penrose generalized inverse.

The dataset used for testing the proposed method consists of images of common plant diseases in the Malatya, Bingöl, and Elazığ regions of Turkey. These images, captured using a Nikon 7200d camera, have a resolution of 4000 x 6000 pixels and feature eight different diseases and pests, totaling 1965 images. Details of the dataset, including the number of training and testing samples for each disease and pest, are provided.

The authors propose a method that incorporates deep feature extraction from various fully connected layers and utilizes transfer learning based on pretrained deep learning architectures. The overall approach is illustrated in accompanying figures, with further details on transfer learning and feature extraction discussed in subsequent sections.
