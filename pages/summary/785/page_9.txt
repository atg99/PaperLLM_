In this study, TÜRKOĞLU and HANBA evaluated the performance of nine deep neural network architectures for plant disease detection using MATLAB's deep learning toolbox. The experiments were conducted on a server with a dual-core Intel Xeon E5 processor and an NVIDIA Quadro M4000 GPU. The study employed various classification methods, including Support Vector Machine (SVM), Extreme Learning Machine (ELM), and k-Nearest Neighbors (KNN), to assess the classification accuracy of deep features extracted from a dataset of 1965 images representing eight plant diseases. A 10-fold cross-validation was utilized to calculate performance metrics such as accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, and F1-score.

The results highlighted deep feature extraction from pretrained models AlexNet, VGG16, and VGG19, specifically from their fully connected layers (fc6, fc7, and fc8). Accuracy scores indicated that the fc6 layer generally provided the best features across all models, with AlexNet achieving the highest accuracy of 95.5% using SVM. VGG16 reached a maximum accuracy of 95%, while VGG19 attained 94.74% with ELM. Additionally, AlexNet demonstrated the shortest execution time compared to VGG16 and VGG19. The study concluded with detailed performance measures for sensitivity, specificity, and F1-score, further supporting the findings related to the effectiveness of the deep learning approaches in plant disease detection.
