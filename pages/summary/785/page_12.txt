The paper presents a comparative analysis of various deep learning models for plant disease detection, focusing on their accuracy and execution times. The results indicate that the VGG16 model achieved the highest accuracy at 96.92%, while the InceptionResNetV2 model had the lowest accuracy at 89.23%. The ResNet101 model followed closely with a 96.41% accuracy, and both GoogleNet and ResNet50 models achieved 95.38%. Execution times varied significantly, with AlexNet being the fastest at 5 minutes and 48 seconds, and InceptionResNetV2 taking the longest at 1 hour and 51 minutes. Notably, the ResNet50 model was almost twice as fast as VGG19 and quicker than ResNet101.

The paper also provides performance measures for sensitivity, specificity, and F1-score across the models. The VGG16 model exhibited a sensitivity of 96.64% and specificity of 99.54%, while the F1-score was highest for VGG16 at 96.38%. 

Additionally, traditional methods such as LBP, HOG, GLCM, and color features were applied for feature extraction and evaluated using classifiers like SVM, ELM, and KNN. The SVM classifier yielded the best results with an accuracy of 70.9% for the LBP method, while the HOG, color features, and GLCM methods achieved lower accuracies of 56.8%, 45.3%, and 45.2%, respectively. 

Overall, the study highlights the effectiveness of deep learning approaches over traditional methods for plant disease detection, emphasizing the potential for improved accuracy and efficiency in agricultural applications.
