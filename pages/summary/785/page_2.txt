The paper discusses the critical role of timely and accurate diagnosis of plant diseases in preventing agricultural productivity losses. It emphasizes the application of machine learning, particularly deep learning, in image processing for precision agriculture. The authors evaluated nine deep neural network architectures for plant disease detection, employing transfer learning and deep feature extraction techniques. Pretrained models were utilized for feature extraction, which were then classified using support vector machine (SVM), extreme learning machine (ELM), and K-nearest neighbor (KNN) methods, using real disease and pest images from Turkey.

Performance metrics including accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, and F1-score were calculated. Results indicated that deep feature extraction combined with SVM and ELM classification outperformed transfer learning approaches. Notably, features from the fc6 layers of AlexNet, VGG16, and VGG19 achieved superior accuracy compared to other layers. The study underscores the importance of automated plant disease diagnosis as a means to benefit users lacking expertise in agriculture, thereby enhancing disease management in crops.
