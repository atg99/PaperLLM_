The paper presents performance measures for different classifiers based on deep feature extraction from pretrained convolutional neural networks (CNNs) including AlexNet, VGG16, and VGG19. The classifiers evaluated are Support Vector Machines (SVM), Extreme Learning Machines (ELM), and K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN), with performance metrics such as sensitivity, specificity, and F1-score reported in percentages.

From the results, SVM consistently outperformed ELM and KNN across all models, with AlexNet showing a sensitivity of 94.34% and an F1-score of 94.58%. VGG models exhibited slightly lower performance, especially in sensitivity compared to AlexNet. The study also explored additional deep learning models including GoogleNet, ResNet50, ResNet101, Inception V3, InceptionResNetV2, and SqueezeNet. 

Among these models, ResNet50 and SVM achieved the highest accuracy of 97.86%. The execution times varied significantly, with SqueezeNet being the fastest at 1 minute and 3 seconds, while InceptionResNetV2 took the longest at 28 minutes and 13 seconds. The findings indicate that SVM is the most effective classifier for deep feature extraction while highlighting the trade-offs between accuracy and execution time across different models.
