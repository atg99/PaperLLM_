In this paper, TÜRKOĞLU and HANBA discuss various deep learning architectures and their efficiencies in training and classification tasks. Notably, the authors highlight the significant acceleration achieved in training through the use of residual connections in certain architectures. SqueezeNet is particularly emphasized for its ability to reach AlexNet-level accuracy on ImageNet with a remarkably reduced parameter count, making it a more efficient model.

The architectures include AlexNet, VGG16, VGG19, SqueezeNet, GoogleNet, Inceptionv3, InceptionResNetv2, ResNet50, and ResNet101, illustrating the trade-offs between complexity and performance.

The paper also covers traditional classifiers used to construct classification models based on deep features extracted from pretrained models. The classifiers discussed include:

1. **K-nearest neighbor (KNN)**: A supervised learning method that classifies new data by assigning it to the nearest cluster in a pre-established sample set using distance metrics such as Euclidean, Minkowski, or Manhattan distance. Its simplicity and ease of application are notable advantages.

2. **Support vector machine (SVM)**: Based on statistical learning theory, SVM seeks to find the hyperplane that maximizes the margin between classes. It can handle both linearly separable and non-separable data, making it versatile for various applications including image and voice recognition.

3. **Extreme learning machine (ELM)**: This algorithm focuses on single hidden-layer feedforward networks, utilizing the least squares method to compute output weights while hidden layer weights are randomly assigned. 

Overall, the paper provides a comprehensive overview of deep learning architectures and traditional classifiers, emphasizing their characteristics and applications in feature extraction and classification tasks.
