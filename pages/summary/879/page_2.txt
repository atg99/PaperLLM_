The paper presents a novel image style transfer framework called StyTr2, which leverages transformer-based architectures to improve the representation and generation of stylized images. The authors highlight two main advantages of transformers in computer vision: their ability to learn global information through self-attention mechanisms and their capacity to model relationships within input structures, which enhances content representation and preserves fine details.

StyTr2 addresses the shortcomings of CNN-based style transfer methods, particularly the biased representation issue. It introduces two transformer-based encoders designed to capture domain-specific information, followed by a transformer decoder that generates output sequences of image patches. The paper emphasizes the need for a content-aware positional encoding scheme (CAPE) that adapts to different image resolutions while maintaining scale invariance, which is crucial for generating high-quality stylized images.

The contributions of the work include the development of the StyTr2 framework, the innovative CAPE, and comprehensive experimental results demonstrating its superiority over baseline methods in preserving content structures and style patterns.

The paper also reviews existing work on image style transfer, noting that traditional methods often struggle with long-range dependencies and detail retention. It discusses the rise of vision transformers and their applications in various image-related tasks, contrasting them with CNNs. Finally, the authors explain the different types of positional encoding used in transformers and propose their content-aware approach as particularly suited for image generation tasks.
