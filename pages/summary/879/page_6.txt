The paper presents a comparative analysis of various style transfer methods, focusing on the effectiveness of their content and style preservation. Key techniques discussed include StyleFormer, IEST, AdaAttN, and others, with Table 2 providing quantitative evaluations of content and style loss. The findings indicate that the proposed method achieves the lowest content losses, suggesting superior preservation of content structures while also maintaining desirable style patterns. In contrast, IEST exhibits higher style loss, indicating a lesser degree of style preservation.

The paper also addresses the issue of content leak, a common problem in stylization where CNN-based feature representations fail to adequately capture image details. This can lead to noticeable artifacts after repeated stylizations. While previous approaches, such as the reversible network proposed by An et al., aim to mitigate this issue, the authors argue that strict reversibility may not be ideal for generative tasks. The paper concludes that their method effectively balances the preservation of both content and style, outperforming many existing techniques in qualitative and quantitative assessments.
