The paper discusses advancements in style transfer techniques, particularly focusing on the content leak issue prevalent in CNN-based methods. The authors introduce StyTr2, which utilizes a transformer-based architecture to better capture long-range dependencies, significantly mitigating content leakage compared to traditional methods. 

Through visual comparisons, as illustrated in Figure 5, the results demonstrate that while CNN-based methods exhibit deteriorated content structures after multiple rounds of stylization, StyTr2 maintains clarity and detail. Although ArtFlow preserves content structure, its stylization quality is deemed subpar due to flaws and inappropriate style patterns.

The paper also introduces the Concept-Aware Positional Encoding (CAPE), which is compared to sinusoidal positional encoding (PE). The authors highlight that CAPE takes semantic information into account, leading to more consistent stylization results, particularly with images containing repetitive patterns. The robustness of CAPE is emphasized through its ability to handle various input resolutions without significant artifacts, as shown in Figures 6 and 7.

Furthermore, user studies involving 100 participants were conducted to assess the effectiveness of StyTr2 against other methods like AdaAttN, ArtFlow, MCC, AAMS, and AdaIN. Participants were asked to evaluate the quality of stylized images, reinforcing the advantages of the proposed approach in terms of maintaining content integrity while achieving high-quality style transfer. 

Overall, the findings suggest that StyTr2 and CAPE represent significant improvements in the field of style transfer, addressing common issues faced by existing methods.
