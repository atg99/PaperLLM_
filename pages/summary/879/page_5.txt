The paper presents StyTr2, a novel image stylization method that utilizes a pure transformer-based architecture to address the shortcomings of traditional CNN-based approaches. The study highlights the importance of style perceptual loss, defined by the mean and variance of extracted features, and incorporates identity loss to enhance content and style representation. The optimization function combines various loss terms to minimize discrepancies between generated and input images.

The experiments utilize the MS-COCO dataset for content and WikiArt for style, with images cropped to a resolution of 256x256 during training. The model is trained using the Adam optimizer over 160,000 iterations and compared against several state-of-the-art methods, including AdaIN, Avater, and StyleFormer. Results indicate that while existing methods struggle with issues like style pattern insufficiency and artifacts, StyTr2 effectively captures long-range dependencies, leading to superior visual quality and detail preservation.

Timing comparisons reveal the inference speed of various methods, with StyTr2 showing competitive performance. Qualitative evaluations demonstrate that StyTr2 outperforms other techniques in maintaining style consistency and visual fidelity. Overall, this research contributes to the field by offering a transformer-based solution that mitigates the limitations of convolutional networks in image stylization.
