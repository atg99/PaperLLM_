The paper presents a novel framework called StyTr2 for image style transfer using transformer models. The approach involves breaking down content and style images into smaller patches, akin to tokens in natural language processing, and subsequently projecting these patches into a sequential feature embedding. The framework consists of a content transformer encoder and a style transformer encoder, which process the respective sequences, followed by a multi-layer transformer decoder that combines the two to produce stylized output images. 

A key innovation in this method is the introduction of Content-Aware Positional Encoding (CAPE), which enhances the traditional positional encoding used in transformers. CAPE accounts for both the spatial relationships of image patches and their semantic content, addressing the limitations of standard sinusoidal positional encoding in visual tasks, particularly when image sizes vary. This allows for a more nuanced understanding of patch relationships, ensuring similar content patches yield comparable stylization results.

The overall architecture is designed to effectively capture long-range dependencies in image features, thereby improving the quality of style transfer. The paper emphasizes the importance of integrating semantic information in visual tasks and proposes a scalable solution that adapts to varying image sizes, making it suitable for a range of style transfer applications.
