The paper discusses the architecture of U-Net and its variations, specifically focusing on the Vanilla U-Net, TransMorph, and Large Kernel U-Net (LKU-Net). The Vanilla U-Net employs sequential 3x3 convolutional layers in both its encoder and decoder. In contrast, TransMorph integrates transformer-based blocks into the encoder, utilizing layer normalization, multi-head self-attention, and a multi-layer perceptron.

LKU-Net introduces a Large Kernel (LK) block that enhances the effective receptive field (ERF) of the network. While a standard 5-layer U-Net theoretically has a sufficient receptive field, practical applications show that the ERF is often smaller than expected. The LK block comprises four parallel sub-layers: a kxkxk convolution, a 3x3x3 layer, a 1x1x1 layer, and an identity shortcut. This design allows the network to efficiently process both distant and fine spatial information.

A significant challenge in enlarging convolutional kernel sizes is the exponential increase in parameters, which can lead to issues such as overfitting. The LK block mitigates this problem by incorporating identity shortcuts and 1x1x1 convolutions, aiding in the training process. The paper provides numerical results from ablation studies to support these claims.
