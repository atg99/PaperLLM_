The paper discusses the development of LKU-Net, an advanced architecture for medical image registration that combines the traditional U-Net with Lucas-Kanade (LK) blocks. The integration of LK blocks into the U-Net aims to enhance performance in comparison to TransMorph, which employs transformer blocks. LKU-Net takes a stacked image pair as input and outputs estimated deformations, with architecture-specific hyperparameters such as the number and size of kernels defined for the convolutional blocks. The number of kernels is strategically adjusted through the network layers, culminating in a specific configuration for 3D and 2D displacements.

Additionally, the authors introduce a diﬀeomorphic variant called LKU-Net-diﬀ, which outputs a stationary velocity field instead of direct displacements. This variant employs scaling and squaring layers to produce diﬀeomorphisms, enhancing the registration process.

For training, the model utilizes an unsupervised loss function that combines a normalized cross-correlation data term with a diﬀusion regularization term, balanced by a hyperparameter. The performance of LKU-Net was evaluated using two datasets, including the OASIS dataset, which contains MRI scans for inter-subject brain registration. The results were reported using metrics such as the Dice Score, demonstrating the effectiveness of LKU-Net in medical image registration tasks. The paper highlights the potential of LKU-Net and its diﬀeomorphic variant in improving registration accuracy in medical imaging.
