The paper compares the performance of U-Net and various Transformer-based models in the context of medical image registration, specifically using the IXI dataset. It presents a table summarizing the Dice scores, parameters, and computational complexity (Mult-Adds) of several models, including traditional methods like SyN and LDDMM, as well as modern approaches like VoxelMorph, CycleMorph, and TransMorph.

Key findings from the performance comparison indicate that TransMorph models generally achieved the highest Dice scores, with TransMorph-diﬀ reaching 0.599. U-Net and its variants (U-Net, U-Net-diff, LKU-Net) also showed competitive performance, with LKU-Net achieving a Dice score of 0.757, signifying its effectiveness in generating realistic warped images. Visual comparisons highlight that LKU-Net produces smoother deformations compared to others, indicating its potential advantages in practical applications.

The results suggest that while U-Net remains a robust option, Transformer-based models, particularly TransMorph, may offer superior performance in medical image registration tasks, prompting a discussion on whether U-Net is becoming outdated in this domain.
