The study highlights the challenges associated with traditional iterative optimization methods, such as slow inference speeds and the need for manual tuning. In contrast, deep learning-based methods, particularly those employing U-Net architectures, have demonstrated both speed and accuracy advantages.

The paper examines the efficacy of U-Net compared to modern transformer architectures like TransMorph for medical image registration. By analyzing the average voxel displacement fields from the IXI brain dataset, the authors argue that the displacements are relatively small, suggesting that the complexity of transformer models may not be warranted for this task. Consequently, the authors introduce the large kernel U-Net (LKU-Net), which enhances the receptive field of the U-Net architecture to better capture spatial information without the need for extensive long-range dependency modeling.

Overall, the findings underscore the potential of LKU-Net as a viable alternative to transformer-based methods, providing a balance between performance and computational efficiency in medical image registration.
