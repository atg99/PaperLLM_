The paper discusses the use of the IXI dataset, which consists of nearly 600 3D MRI scans from healthy subjects, for brain registration tasks. Specifically, 576 T1-weighted MRI images were utilized, divided into training, validation, and testing sets. The atlas used for registration was generated from these images. The study employed a 5-layer U-Net architecture, modified to use 3x3x3 kernels, and a 2D U-Net version with 3x3 kernels. The Adam optimizer was used for training, maintaining a fixed learning rate.

The results of ablation and parameter studies are presented, comparing the performance of the LKU-Net against the vanilla U-Net using Dice metrics. The findings indicate that varying kernel sizes in the U-Net affects performance, with larger kernels initially improving results before causing a decline. The LKU-Net consistently outperformed the vanilla U-Net across various configurations. Additionally, the use of identity shortcuts and 1x1 layers was found to enhance registration performance, with the best results achieved when both were combined. Increasing the model size also led to improved performance.

The paper emphasizes a fair comparison with TransMorph by using identical data processing and evaluation protocols. Results are detailed in accompanying tables, showcasing the effectiveness of the proposed methods in atlas-to-subject registration on the IXI dataset.
