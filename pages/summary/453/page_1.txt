The paper investigates the relevance of U-Net in the context of medical image registration, particularly in comparison to modern transformer-based approaches that have gained popularity due to their long-range modeling capabilities. The authors argue that the receptive field of a 5-layer convolutional U-Net is sufficient for capturing accurate deformations without the need for long-range dependencies. 

To support this, they propose a large kernel U-Net (LKU-Net), which enhances the effective receptive field by incorporating a parallel convolutional block into the standard U-Net architecture. The evaluation of this model is conducted using the public 3D IXI brain dataset for atlas-based registration, demonstrating that the performance of the vanilla U-Net is already competitive with state-of-the-art transformer models like TransMorph. Notably, LKU-Net outperforms TransMorph while utilizing only 1.12% of its parameters and 10.8% of its multiply-add operations.

Further testing on the MICCAI Learn2Reg 2021 challenge dataset for inter-subject registration reinforces the findings, with LKU-Net achieving top rankings on the public leaderboard. The results indicate that with modest modifications, U-Net can surpass transformer-based architectures in both inter-subject and atlas-based 3D medical image registration tasks. The authors conclude that U-Net remains a viable option in this domain, challenging the notion that it is outdated. The code for LKU-Net is made available for further research and application.
