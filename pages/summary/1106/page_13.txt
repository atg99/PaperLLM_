The paper presents findings on the effects of interventions in learning discrete Directed Acyclic Graphs (DAGs), specifically examining how varying the number of interventions per node and the number of nodes under intervention influences the True Positive Rate (TPR). The study utilizes four types of networks: scale-free, small-world, polytree, and bipartite, with observational data set at n=500 and p=50.

Figure 3 illustrates the impact of interventions across different network structures. The results indicate that TPR varies with the number of interventions per node (m), showcasing distinct patterns for each network type. The scale-free network demonstrates a solid green line indicating higher TPR, while the small-world, polytree, and bipartite networks show varying TPR levels with dashed lines.

Figure 4 further explores the effect of the number of nodes under intervention (k), maintaining m=10 interventions for k randomly selected nodes. Results are averaged over 20 permutations of intervention order. The TPR trends across network types are consistent, emphasizing the utility of interventions in enhancing causal inference.

The paper concludes that the improvements in TPR cannot be attributed to sample size increases, as n remains constant. Instead, the findings highlight how the sparsebn method effectively reduces reversed edges that are observationally equivalent, thereby demonstrating the value of experimental interventions in refining the estimation of true causal graphs.
