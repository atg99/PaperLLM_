The paper discusses the sparsebn package, designed to effectively learn large-scale Bayesian networks from high-dimensional experimental data. Unlike existing packages, sparsebn is specifically tailored to handle complex datasets and is capable of scaling to larger data while also accommodating simpler, low-dimensional data scenarios. It ensures compatibility with other methods to enhance usability.

The learning process involves a score-based approach that utilizes regularized maximum likelihood estimation, incorporating experimental data. Key to this process is the block coordinate descent algorithm, which approximates the optimization problem. The paper outlines the formulation of the problem, which seeks to minimize the negative log-likelihood combined with regularization terms. Various regularizers, such as the lasso, group norm, and nonconvex penalties like SCAD and MCP, are considered.

For continuous data, the Gaussian likelihood is employed alongside lasso or MCP regularization, while a multi-logit model combined with group lasso is used for discrete data. The paper also discusses how to handle data generated under experimental interventions, allowing for a factorization of the negative log-likelihood based on known marginal distributions when interventions occur. This structured approach allows for effective learning and inference in Bayesian networks from complex data.
