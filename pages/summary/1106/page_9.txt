This paper discusses advancements in causal Bayesian networks, particularly focusing on score-based structure learning. It highlights the challenges of finding global minimizers in nonconvex problems and notes that score-based structure learning is NP-hard. The authors propose a local minimization approach using block coordinate descent, which simplifies the optimization by addressing one edge at a time. This method builds on previous strategies and introduces a block-cyclic strategy that outperforms traditional greedy updates.

The algorithm operates through an iterative process where pairs of edges are minimized, with checks for cycles to avoid invalid graphs. It emphasizes the need to predefine a regularization parameter, λ, and suggests generating a solution path for various λ values instead of a single directed acyclic graph (DAG) estimate. As λ decreases, the estimates become denser, prompting the algorithm to terminate early if the number of edges exceeds a user-defined threshold, which is set at 10 edges for continuous data and 3 edges for discrete data due to computational complexities. The methodology is implemented in the software sparsebn, demonstrating its versatility across different data types and likelihood models.
