The paper discusses the development of the R package sparsebn, designed for structure learning and parameter estimation of large-scale Bayesian networks (BNs) from high-dimensional data. Despite significant advancements in undirected graphical models, progress in directed graphical models, particularly in high-dimensional settings, has lagged due to complexities such as nonconvexity, nonsmoothness, and nonidentifiability. These challenges hinder the creation of efficient algorithms capable of processing datasets with thousands of variables.

To address this issue, sparsebn leverages recent advancements in structure learning and statistical optimization, offering a user-friendly tool specifically tailored for high-dimensional data with experimental interventions. Unlike other existing R packages, sparsebn is built to handle the intricacies of BNs and includes functionalities for learning undirected graphs and fitting structural equation models. The package is open-source and accessible via the Comprehensive R Archive Network (CRAN).

An illustrative example in the paper demonstrates the use of sparsebn to estimate the structure of the pathfinder network, showcasing its efficiency with a quick execution time for generating a solution path. Overall, sparsebn aims to bridge the gap between theoretical advancements and practical applications in Bayesian network learning.
