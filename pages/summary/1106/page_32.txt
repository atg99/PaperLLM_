The paper discusses the development of the sparsebn package, which enables the learning of large-scale Bayesian networks in high-dimensional data settings that involve numerous variables and experimental interventions. This addresses a gap in existing software that typically focuses on traditional problems without interventions. The authors provide access to the code necessary to replicate their findings on GitHub, and the sparsebn package is available on CRAN.

A notable challenge in this area is the nonconvexity of the minimization problems encountered. The authors propose future enhancements, including the integration of stochastic optimization methods such as stochastic gradient descent to improve the global search capabilities and reduce computational complexity. They also express interest in implementing divide-and-conquer strategies for extremely large graphs, potentially involving up to 100,000 nodes, and exploring parallel and distributed algorithm implementations.

Additionally, the package can be utilized alongside various post-learning functions for multi-stage causal network learning. This allows for further inference of causal relations in specific subgraphs by applying additional causal assumptions or utilizing new experimental data.
