In the study, the authors applied the package to gene expression data from late-onset Alzheimer's disease patients and healthy individuals, demonstrating its capability to handle substantial datasets with thousands of variables. 

The analysis involved three datasets sourced from different brain regions, focusing on 5,000 gene expression probes. The algorithm successfully estimated solution paths for each dataset, with computation times around four minutes for a maximum of 5,000 edges. The results indicated varying numbers of edges across different datasets, showcasing the flexibility of the method in accommodating real-world data complexities. 

The edge.threshold parameter played a crucial role in guiding the algorithm's termination, highlighting its adaptability based on prior knowledge of the graph's sparsity. The paper concludes that sparsebn represents a significant advancement in the field of Bayesian network structure learning, emphasizing its speed and modern optimization techniques.
