The paper presents an overview of a proposed method called InST, which utilizes Stable Diffusion Models (SDMs) as its generative backbone, integrating an attention-based textual inversion module. This module processes the CLIP image embedding of an artistic image and generates a learned corresponding text embedding. This embedding is then encoded into the SDMs' caption conditioning, allowing for the generation of new images conditioned on the encoded latent or random noise.

The paper discusses existing methods in the realm of image synthesis and style transfer, noting that while many focus on color and brushstroke transfer, they often overlook other artistic factors like object shape and decorative elements. It highlights various text-guided synthesis methods, including CLIPDraw and VQGAN-CLIP, which utilize textual descriptions to generate artistic images. However, these methods typically lack fine-grained control over content and style.

Furthermore, the paper addresses the inversion of diffusion models, which seeks to obtain a noise map and conditioning vector for generated images. It critiques existing methods for their tendency to produce images that diverge significantly from the original content when noise is naively added and denoised. The authors reference various techniques aimed at improving quality in artistic image generation, including textual inversion methods that optimize the embedding of specific visual concepts.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the challenges in generating images with complex artistic characteristics and proposes its method as a solution to enhance control and fidelity in the synthesis of artistic images.
