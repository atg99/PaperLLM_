The study by Laghrissi et al. emphasizes the trade-off between the number of features used in machine learning models and the processing time required for training. The findings indicate that utilizing a larger set of features significantly increases the training duration, with models using all features taking over 15 times longer than those using just two features. This suggests that optimizing the number of features can enhance efficiency and conserve resources during training.

In the discussion, the authors compare their results with previous studies, acknowledging that while such comparisons are primarily for reference, they highlight the variability in performance of intrusion detection systems across different environments. The LSTM-PCA model developed by the authors, which utilizes only two features, demonstrated superior accuracy and sensitivity rates compared to other models. This advantage is attributed to the reduced complexity and processing time, making it more suitable for real-time applications and large-scale, high-dimensional domains. The authors also note that models employing all features, like the one referenced in [22], face challenges in real-time environments due to longer training and testing times.
