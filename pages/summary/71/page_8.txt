The paper by Laghrissi et al. discusses data preprocessing techniques for binary and multiclass classification tasks in the context of detecting attacks. In binary classification, the aim is to categorize records as either Normal or Attack. To address the issue of unbalanced data, the authors implemented random sampling, selecting 100,000 records for the Normal category and 100,148 for the Attack category.

In contrast, multiclass classification involves classifying instances into three or more categories. The authors grouped the attack records into three main categories: Normal, Denial of Service (DoS), and other specific attack types. The paper provides a detailed breakdown of instances for various attack types, noting significant counts for DoS attacks, such as SMURF and NEPTUNE, as well as lesser instances for user-to-root (U2R) and remote-to-local (R2L) attacks.

The paper also includes a comparison of attack records before and after sampling, showing a significant reduction in the number of records to facilitate effective classification. These preprocessing steps are crucial for developing robust classification models in the context of cybersecurity.
