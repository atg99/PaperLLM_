The paper by Laghrissi et al. presents a method for intrusion detection using Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) models, which effectively learn features from training data to differentiate between normal network traffic and attacks. The authors employed the Keras library and TensorFlow on Google Colab, utilizing Principal Component Analysis (PCA) and Mutual Information for dimensionality reduction. Their findings indicate that models based on PCA, particularly with two components, yielded the highest classification accuracy at 99.44% for binary and 99.39% for multiclass classification. This approach demonstrated superior accuracy and sensitivity compared to other methods, while also being resource-efficient due to the reduced number of features used.

The study, however, is limited to a single type of LSTM, with plans for future research to explore various LSTM variants and additional neural network algorithms, as well as different feature selection techniques.
