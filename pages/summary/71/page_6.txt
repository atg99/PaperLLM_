In their 2021 paper, Laghrissi et al. discuss the importance of dimensionality reduction and feature selection in machine learning to improve model training speed and interpretability. They focus on two techniques: Principal Component Analysis (PCA) and Mutual Information (MI).

PCA is highlighted as a method for handling large and complex datasets by creating uncorrelated variables that maximize variance. The process involves standardizing the data, extracting eigenvectors and eigenvalues from the covariance matrix, and selecting a subset of these eigenvectors to form a projection matrix. This allows the transformation of the original dataset into a lower-dimensional feature subspace, thereby reducing computational costs and estimation errors.

On the other hand, MI measures the statistical dependence between two variables, indicating how much information one variable provides about another. A high MI score reflects a significant reduction in uncertainty, while a score of zero signifies independence. The authors reference the definition of mutual information as established by Cover and Thomas, emphasizing its role in feature selection by assigning scores that quantify the relationship between features.

Through the application of PCA and MI, the authors aim to enhance the effectiveness of machine learning algorithms by streamlining the data used for training.
