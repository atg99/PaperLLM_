This section of the paper details the methodology employed in the study, focusing on the KDD99 dataset, data preprocessing, and model implementation. The KDD99 dataset, despite being over 19 years old, remains a key resource in academic research, containing approximately five million connection records characterized by 53 features and categorized into four types of attacks: Denial of Service (DoS), User to Root (U2R), Remote to Local (R2L), and Probing attacks. 

DoS attacks aim to incapacitate a machine or network by overwhelming it with requests. U2R attacks exploit vulnerabilities to gain unauthorized root access, while R2L attacks leverage weaknesses to illegally access systems and data. Probing attacks involve scanning for vulnerabilities as a precursor to more serious attacks.

To enhance data quality, preprocessing and filtering were performed, resulting in the KDD99 dataset being imported as a Panda Data Frame for statistical analysis. The analysis revealed a significant imbalance between the number of attack records and normal records, which could adversely affect the model's classification performance. To address this imbalance, the authors proposed the use of various sampling techniques, which will be elaborated upon in subsequent sections.
