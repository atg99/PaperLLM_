The paper by Laghrissi et al. explores the significance of dimensionality reduction in improving the performance of classifiers in intrusion detection systems (IDS). The authors highlight that unimportant and noisy data can lead to irrelevant features, which negatively impacts classifier performance. They implement three models based on Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM): LSTM, LSTM-PCA (using Principal Component Analysis), and LSTM-MI (using Mutual Information). After preprocessing the KDD99 dataset, these models were tested for both binary and multiclass classification. Results indicated that the LSTM model utilizing PCA achieved the best performance.

The paper also reviews related works in the field of IDS. It references various studies that have employed different algorithms and techniques, noting the transition from traditional manual feature extraction to the utilization of deep learning methods. Notable contributions include Anand's use of an improved genetic K-means++ algorithm, Ma's application of a Deep Neural Network for attack classification, and Nikolov's implementation of LSTM on a different dataset. Other mentioned works include Jayaprakash's anomaly detection mechanism and Kang's effective IDS for in-vehicle networks. Overall, this research underscores the importance of applying advanced techniques to enhance IDS effectiveness.
