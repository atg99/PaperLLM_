The paper discusses advancements in noisy student training for automatic speech recognition (ASR), particularly focusing on the LibriSpeech 100-860 and LibriSpeech-LibriLight datasets. It highlights the effectiveness of gradational filtration, which maintains comparable word error rates (WERs) across generations while increasing the dataset size. The authors found that a slower evolution of the model leads to better performance, with models trained in a six-generation pipeline outperforming those in a three-generation setup at similar dataset sizes.

Key experiments evaluated the impact of time mask size on training, showing that stronger augmentations are beneficial as the dataset grows. Control experiments on batch-wise mixing and balancing indicated that while balancing reduces the dataset size, it can diminish the model's performance.

In the LibriSpeech-LibriLight task, the authors found that combining filtering and balancing did not yield significant benefits compared to using the entire dataset, as the baseline performance was already strong. The results suggest that the quality of filtered transcripts did not significantly surpass that of unfiltered transcripts, limiting the advantages of gradational filtration.

The conclusion emphasizes the successful adaptation of noisy student training for ASR through the integration of various techniques like SpecAugment and language model fusion. The gradational methods were particularly effective for the lower-supervised LibriSpeech 100-860 task, while their impact was less pronounced for the higher-supervised LibriSpeech-LibriLight task. The work underscores the potential of mixing and matching different components to enhance performance in ASR tasks.
