The paper discusses the performance of various models trained on the LibriSpeech dataset, particularly focusing on the generation-4 model, which is the best performing model in their experiments. The authors compare their results with baseline models and other literature, presenting Word Error Rates (WERs) in tables for both the 100-hour and 960-hour training sets. 

In the LibriSpeech 100-hour experiments, the baseline model combined with SpecAugment achieved a WER of 5.3% on the development set, which improved to 3.9% after applying Neural Sequence Training (NST) with language model (LM) fusion. The use of a 3-layer LSTM language model contributed to significant performance improvements.

The authors employed a semi-supervised approach using the LibriSpeech training set and an unlabeled subset from LibriLight to create approximately one million utterances. They describe their training methodology, which involved five generations of models, with each generation showing improvements in WER.

Additionally, the paper details the gradual filtration process for the semi-supervised dataset, indicating that the filtering criteria were relaxed over generations based on the normalized filtering scores. The results indicate stable performance across generations, with WERs ranking the quality of transcripts effectively, although some issues arose in generation 4.

Overall, the findings suggest that the combination of NST and LM fusion significantly enhances the performance of the models, validating the effectiveness of their approach in reducing WER in speech recognition tasks.
