The paper "Improved Noisy Student Training for Automatic Speech Recognition" by Daniel S. Park et al. presents an enhancement of the noisy student training (NST) method, originally successful in image classification, for automatic speech recognition (ASR). The authors adapt NST by integrating (adaptive) SpecAugment as the data augmentation technique, which directly modifies the spectrogram of audio inputs. They also implement strategies for filtering, balancing, and augmenting data during the self-training iterations to improve model performance.

Key findings include achieving significant reductions in word error rates (WERs) on the LibriSpeech test sets, with results of 4.2%/8.6% on clean/noisy datasets using only a 100-hour clean subset for supervision and the remainder as unlabeled data. Additionally, using the unlab-60k subset of LibriLight, they attained WERs of 1.7%/3.4%, outperforming previous benchmarks on the same datasets.

The paper introduces several measures to enhance NST for ASR, including:
1. Using SpecAugment for effective augmentation.
2. Employing shallow fusion with a language model to generate improved transcripts.
3. Implementing a normalized filtering score to evaluate the quality of teacher-generated transcripts.
4. Utilizing sub-modular sampling to balance the dataset for the student model.

The authors also propose gradational self-training techniques, where the filtering criteria and augmentation strength are progressively adjusted based on model performance, enabling the effective growth of the semi-supervised dataset.

In conclusion, the work demonstrates that improvements in data augmentation and self-training processes can lead to state-of-the-art results in ASR, showcasing the potential of adapting image-based methods to speech recognition tasks.
