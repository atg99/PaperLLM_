The paper discusses the performance of the DAEN method in retrieving abundance estimates from hyperspectral data, highlighting its stability and accuracy, especially in challenging scenarios characterized by low signal-to-noise ratio (SNR), the presence of outliers, and a high number of endmembers and pixels. The results from simulations demonstrate that DAEN outperforms other methods, as evidenced by the endmember signatures obtained from a dataset with 3364 pixels, 4 endmembers, and 10 outliers at an SNR of 20 dB.

The effectiveness of DAEN was further validated through experiments with real hyperspectral images, specifically the Mangrove dataset obtained from the USGS Earth Resources Observation and Science Center. The data underwent atmospheric correction to convert radiance to reflectance, facilitating the identification of pure signatures of mangrove species. The parameters used in the experiments were consistent with those in the simulated scenarios, ensuring a reliable evaluation of the method's capabilities. Overall, the DAEN approach demonstrates significant advantages in accurately identifying endmembers and producing stable abundance estimates in various conditions.
