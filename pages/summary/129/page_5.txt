The proposed DAEN algorithm consists of two main steps: the first utilizes a Stacked Autoencoder (SAE) for initialization, where initial endmembers and abundances are computed through the Vertex Component Analysis (VCA) method. The algorithm iteratively updates the endmember and mixture matrices until convergence, followed by estimating abundances using the Fully Constrained Least Squares (FCLS) method. The second step employs a Variational Autoencoder (VAE) for unmixing, where the endmember and abundance matrices are further refined through iterative updates.

Experiments conducted with synthetic data, generated from the USGS spectral library, evaluated the effectiveness of DAEN under various conditions, including different pixel counts, endmember numbers, and outlier presence. Performance metrics such as Spectral Angle Distance (SAD), Relative Error (RE), and Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) were used to assess the accuracy of the unmixing results. 

The findings suggest that DAEN is particularly effective with specific parameter settings, showing insensitivity to variations within a range, and consistently outperforms existing unmixing methods in terms of endmember quality as indicated by lower SAD values. Overall, DAEN demonstrates significant promise as a robust approach for hyperspectral unmixing, combining the strengths of SAE and VAE methodologies.
