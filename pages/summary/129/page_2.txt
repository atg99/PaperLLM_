In this paper, the authors present a novel deep autoencoder network (DAEN) for hyperspectral unmixing, specifically designed to handle outliers in hyperspectral data. The discussion begins by highlighting the limitations of traditional handcrafted methods for unmixing, which are divided into pure pixel-based and nonpure pixel-based approaches. Pure pixel-based methods often fail in low spatial resolution scenarios, while nonpure pixel-based methods leverage geometrical and statistical data structures to improve unmixing performance.

As technology has advanced, learning-based approaches have emerged, such as joint Bayesian unmixing and artificial neural networks, which offer improved performance by incorporating prior knowledge of endmember signatures. However, these methods still face challenges, particularly regarding the presence of outliers and the enforcement of sum-to-one constraints on abundance fractions.

The proposed DAEN consists of two main steps: first, it utilizes stacked autoencoders (SAEs) to learn spectral signatures and generate effective initializations for unmixing; second, it employs a variational autoencoder (VAE) for the actual unmixing process, which ensures nonnegativity and the sum-to-one constraint through a reparameterization technique.

The advantages of DAEN over traditional nonnegative matrix factorization (NMF)-based methods include its ability to effectively manage outliers, maintain nonnegativity and sum-to-one constraints, and achieve simultaneous estimation of endmember signatures and abundance fractions. The paper compares DAEN’s performance with other methods using synthetic and real hyperspectral datasets, demonstrating its effectiveness.

The organization of the paper includes a detailed description of the DAEN approach, quantitative evaluations using synthetic datasets, experiments with real hyperspectral data, and concluding remarks that outline potential future research directions.
