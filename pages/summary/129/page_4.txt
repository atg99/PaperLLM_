This paper presents a novel approach for unmixing hyperspectral data using a combination of stacked autoencoders (SAEs) and variational autoencoders (VAEs). The focus is on reconstructing the endmember matrix and obtaining abundance fractions through a linear mixing model. The method begins with the initialization of the weight matrix and the adaptive learning rate is set to ensure positivity. The reconstruction process continues until convergence is achieved, as indicated by the difference between successive autoencoder outputs.

The authors introduce an objective function that combines a reconstruction error term, a volume regularizer for the mixing matrix, and a VAE-based regularizer for the abundance matrix. The volume regularizer is designed to ensure that all pixels are enclosed within a simplex formed by the endmembers. The VAE regularizer enforces the nonnegativity and sum-to-one constraints on the abundance fractions.

To optimize the nonconvex objective function, the authors propose an iterative gradient descent method to update the mixing matrix and abundance fractions. The gradients of the objective function are derived and utilized to compute updates for both matrices using tailored learning rates and methods such as Adadelta.

The paper concludes with a pseudocode representation of the proposed Deep Autoencoder Network (DAEN), detailing the initialization and training process. The method's efficacy lies in its structured approach to handling the challenges associated with hyperspectral unmixing, leveraging the strengths of autoencoding techniques for improved accuracy in abundance estimation.
