The paper discusses the performance of a proposed Deep Autoencoder Network (DAEN) in estimating endmember signatures and abundance maps from hyperspectral data, particularly the Samson and Cuprite datasets. The analysis indicates that the DAEN achieved superior results, characterized by the best mean Spectral Angle Distance (SAD) and Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) when compared to other methods. 

Figures illustrate the estimated endmember signatures and abundance maps for the Samson dataset, showing a strong correlation with ground truth references. The results highlight the effectiveness of DAEN in accurately capturing the spectral characteristics of different vegetation types, such as Avicennia, Bruguiera, and Excoecaria, as well as the palm species Phoenix.

The Cuprite dataset, collected with the Airborne Visible Infra-Red Imaging Spectrometer, was also analyzed, emphasizing the importance of preprocessing to remove bands with low signal-to-noise ratios. Overall, the findings demonstrate the DAEN's capability in hyperspectral image analysis, providing promising results for mineral and vegetation mapping applications.
