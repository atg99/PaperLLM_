In this paper, a novel hyperspectral unmixing method is proposed that utilizes a Dual Autoencoder Network (DAEN) comprising two main components: a set of Stacked Autoencoders (SAEs) for initialization and a Variational Autoencoder (VAE) for nonnegative matrix factorization. This approach aims to extract endmember signatures and abundance fractions simultaneously. The experimental results, derived from both synthetic and real datasets, demonstrate that the DAEN effectively addresses challenges posed by significant outliers, which are common in real-world scenarios. Traditional unmixing algorithms often misinterpret these outliers as endmembers due to their distinctiveness. The effectiveness of the DAEN in this context has been validated through comprehensive testing.

The paper notes that the proposed method is based on a linear model, and future research will explore its applicability to nonlinear unmixing scenarios to improve the characterization of endmembers in hyperspectral datasets. 

The appendix details the objective function related to the DAEN, specifically the Kullback–Leibler divergence and its components, providing a mathematical framework for the optimization of the model. The analysis includes derivations involving variances and expectations of the latent variables, leading to a formulation that incorporates the matrices of parameters. This rigorous mathematical foundation supports the overall approach and paves the way for further investigation into enhancing hyperspectral unmixing techniques.
