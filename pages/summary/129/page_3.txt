The paper presents a Deep Autoencoder Network (DAEN) designed for hyperspectral unmixing, which aims to estimate the mixing matrix W and the abundance matrix H from observed data Y. The unmixing process utilizes a Non-negative Matrix Factorization (NMF)-based optimization approach, minimizing the difference between the observed data and the product of W and H, while incorporating regularization terms for both matrices.

The DAEN consists of two main components: Stacked Autoencoders (SAEs) and a Variational Autoencoder (VAE). The SAEs provide initial estimates for W and H, which are then refined by the VAE. The endmember matrix W is derived from the final weight matrix of the VAE's decoder, and the abundances H are extracted from the VAE's hidden layers.

For initialization, the SAEs use a pure pixel-based method to select candidate pixels, leveraging Vertex Component Analysis (VCA) to generate a set of potential endmembers. These candidates are clustered based on spectral angle distance to form training sets for the SAEs. The training focuses on minimizing reconstruction error while enforcing non-negativity constraints.

The paper also details the training mechanism of the SAEs, which involves updating weights and biases using a gradient descent approach. The parameters for the activation function and learning process are specified, ensuring the model effectively captures the necessary features for accurate unmixing.

Overall, the proposed DAEN framework aims to improve the accuracy of hyperspectral unmixing by leveraging deep learning techniques alongside traditional methods for initialization and optimization.
