The paper presents a model called EmoBERTa, designed for emotion recognition in conversations using two datasets: MELD and IEMOCAP. The loss function used is a combination of cross-entropy loss and L2 regularization, expressed as L(w), where y(i) is the one-hot label vector, ^y(i) is the softmax output vector, and w represents the model weights. The final model selection is based on the highest weighted F1 score on a validation split.

Experiments were conducted using MELD, a multimodal and multi-party dataset derived from the TV series Friends, consisting of seven emotions, and IEMOCAP, a multimodal and dyadic dataset featuring six emotions. Both datasets exhibit highly imbalanced class distributions, necessitating the use of a weighted F1 score for evaluation.

The results demonstrate that EmoBERTa outperforms several baseline models across both datasets, achieving maximum weighted F1 scores of 65.61% for MELD and 68.57% for IEMOCAP. Notably, these performances surpass the best reported state-of-the-art results without any modifications to the original RoBERTa architecture. The paper also highlights the impact of including speaker names, as removing them resulted in a slight drop in performance.

Overall, the findings indicate that EmoBERTa is an effective model for emotion recognition in dialogue systems, leveraging both past and future utterances to enhance performance.
