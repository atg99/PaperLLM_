The referenced works collectively contribute to advancements in natural language processing (NLP) and emotion recognition in conversational contexts. 

Mikolov et al. (2013) introduced distributed representations for words and phrases, emphasizing their compositionality, which laid groundwork for understanding semantic relationships in language. Pennington et al. (2014) developed the GloVe model for global word representations, enhancing the ability to capture word meanings based on their context.

Poria et al. (2019) presented MELD, a dataset designed for emotion recognition in conversations, facilitating research in multimodal and multi-party dialogue systems. Sennrich et al. (2016) addressed the challenge of translating rare words through subword units, improving the robustness of neural machine translation systems.

Shen et al. (2020) proposed DialogXL, an all-in-one XLNet model for emotion recognition in multi-party conversations, illustrating the integration of advanced architectures in understanding emotional dynamics. Sheng et al. (2020) introduced a heterogeneous graph inference network that processes conversational data in a global-to-local manner to enhance emotion recognition accuracy.

The foundational work by Vaswani et al. (2017) on the Transformer architecture revolutionized NLP by demonstrating that attention mechanisms could replace traditional recurrent architectures, leading to significant improvements in various tasks. Wang et al. (2020) explored sequence tagging for contextualized emotion recognition, emphasizing the importance of context in understanding emotional content.

Wolf et al. (2020) reviewed state-of-the-art NLP techniques, showcasing the capabilities of Transformers across various applications. Yang et al. (2019) contributed to language understanding through XLNet, which generalized autoregressive pretraining, enhancing model performance on language tasks.

Lastly, Zhong et al. (2019a, 2019b) proposed a knowledge-enriched transformer model for emotion detection in textual conversations, demonstrating the effectiveness of integrating external knowledge into emotion recognition frameworks.

Together, these studies illustrate the evolution and intersection of word representation, emotion detection, and the application of advanced neural architectures in improving conversational understanding in NLP.
