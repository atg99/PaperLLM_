The paper discusses the effectiveness of EmoBERTa in emotion recognition from dialogue, particularly using the IEMOCAP and MELD datasets. It highlights that encoding speaker information significantly enhances the model's performance. The findings reveal that, in the IEMOCAP dataset, using only past utterances yields better results compared to incorporating both past and future utterances. This is attributed to the greater number of utterances available in IEMOCAP, allowing for a more extensive sequence of past dialogues.

A qualitative analysis of classification results indicates that EmoBERTa learns the dynamics between interlocutors effectively, as evidenced by its attention to speaker tokens in correctly classified samples. The analysis shows that in successful predictions, the model's attention focuses on the current speaker, while in incorrect predictions, attention shifts to irrelevant tokens, resulting in misclassification.

The conclusion emphasizes that EmoBERTa outperforms other models in emotion recognition tasks by effectively attending to input tokens and speaker names, thus facilitating a clearer understanding of the model's decision-making process. The research underscores the importance of contextual dynamics in dialogue for accurate emotion classification.
