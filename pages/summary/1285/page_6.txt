The paper presents a method for training a Sinkhorn AutoEncoder (SAE) using the Sinkhorn algorithm to compute the Sinkhorn distance as a differentiable surrogate for optimal transport (OT) cost between empirical distributions. The algorithm, detailed in Algorithm 1, takes as input samples from two distributions and computes a matrix that approximates the optimal coupling through repeated iterations. The method utilizes an unbiased Sinkhorn loss, which omits entropy terms during evaluations to enhance differentiability.

The convergence of the Sinkhorn algorithm is guaranteed, with the rate being linear in the number of iterations. The output of the algorithm is a doubly stochastic matrix that approximates the OT-cost, with the approximation quality improving as the entropy regularization parameter decreases. The differentiability of Sinkhorn operations allows for the backpropagation of gradients, facilitating the training of the encoder and decoder weights.

In addition, the paper introduces a closed-form expression for the 2-Wasserstein distance between two Gaussian distributions, which is leveraged for matching and backpropagation in the context of the SAE, even when the aggregated posterior may not be Gaussian. The method for estimating the mean and covariance from minibatches is outlined, referred to as Wasserstein Gaussian AutoEncoder (W2GAE).

Overall, the SAE framework is flexible, capable of working with various prior distributions, and is designed to ensure numerical stability during training. The computational efficiency of the Sinkhorn algorithm, combined with its differentiability, makes it suitable for learning representations in latent spaces defined by desired geometric properties.
