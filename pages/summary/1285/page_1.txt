The paper introduces Sinkhorn AutoEncoders (SAE), a novel approach to generative autoencoding models that leverages optimal transport theory. The authors demonstrate that minimizing the p-Wasserstein distance between the generator and the true data distribution is equivalent to a min-min optimization problem involving the encoder's aggregated posterior and the prior in latent space, alongside a reconstruction error. The trade-off hyperparameter in this framework reflects the generator's capacity, specifically its Lipschitz constant.

The authors establish that optimizing the encoder using universal approximators, such as deterministic neural networks, can yield results close to the optimum. This framework is posited as a favorable balance among existing generative autoencoding objectives, applicable across various metric spaces and priors.

The SAE approximates and minimizes the p-Wasserstein distance in latent space through backpropagation via the Sinkhorn algorithm. It operates directly on samples, modeling the aggregated posterior as an implicit distribution without needing a reparameterization trick for gradient estimation. This capability allows SAE to adapt to different metric spaces and priors with minimal modifications.

The paper further validates the flexibility of SAE by applying it to latent spaces with diverse geometries and priors, comparing its performance against other methods on benchmark datasets. Overall, SAE represents a significant advancement in the domain of generative modeling by integrating optimal transport principles into the autoencoding framework.
