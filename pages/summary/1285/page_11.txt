This paper presents a collection of key references relevant to the fields of generative models, optimal transport, and machine learning methodologies. It highlights significant advancements such as the Sliced-Wasserstein Autoencoder, which offers a straightforward generative model framework, and the Hungarian method for solving assignment problems, which is foundational in optimization.

Several studies focus on the reparameterization of the Birkhoff polytope for variational inference and the differential properties of Sinkhorn approximations, emphasizing the role of Wasserstein distances in learning. The references also include works on visualization techniques like t-SNE, adversarial autoencoders, and implicit generative models, showcasing the diversity in learning approaches.

The importance of representation learning is underscored through methods like solving jigsaw puzzles and learning to count, which contribute to unsupervised learning paradigms. The collection further discusses computational optimal transport, distribution matching in variational inference, and the development of Wasserstein Auto-Encoders, which integrate latent dimensionality considerations.

The references span foundational texts, such as Villani's work on optimal transport, to recent advancements in empirical measures convergence in Wasserstein distance, reflecting ongoing research trends and methodologies in the field. Overall, this compilation serves as a vital resource for understanding contemporary developments in generative modeling and optimal transport within machine learning.
