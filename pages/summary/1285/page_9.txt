The paper presents a generative model based on Optimal Transport principles, emphasizing the use of empirical Wasserstein distances and deterministic networks to create a flexible likelihood-free framework for latent variable modeling. The study evaluates the performance of different models, specifically focusing on the Wasserstein Autoencoder (WAE) with a Gaussian prior and the Softmax Autoencoder (SAE) with a uniform prior on the hypersphere, as illustrated in Figure 2.

The authors explore the application of Dirichlet priors on the MNIST dataset, demonstrating the adaptability of the SAE model. By employing a Dirichlet prior that samples from the probability simplex, they constrain the encoder with a softmax layer, which facilitates clustering of digit representations. The results indicate that a 10-dimensional Dirichlet(1/2) prior leads to an embedding similar to a uniform sphere, while a more skewed Dirichlet(1/5) prior organizes the latent space such that each digit aligns with a vertex of the simplex, albeit with some digits sharing vertices to accommodate different styles.

Further experimentation with a 16-dimensional Dirichlet(1/5) prior results in more distinct clusters, as shown in the t-SNE visualizations. The aggregated posterior is compared to the prior, highlighting the representation of each digit at the vertices and the presence of multiple styles for certain digits, such as the digit 7. The central samples in the figures represent interpolations along edges connecting these vertices, while samples from the vertices exhibit sharper features compared to other prior samples, indicating a mismatch between the prior and the aggregated posterior in regions of lower probability mass.

In conclusion, the authors emphasize the effectiveness of their proposed model, showcasing its flexibility and capability to learn from data, including the potential to adapt Dirichlet hyperparameters through a reparameterization trick.
