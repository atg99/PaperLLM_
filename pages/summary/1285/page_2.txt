The paper discusses advancements in the framework of Wasserstein AutoEncoders (WAE) and introduces the Sinkhorn AutoEncoder (SAE), which leverages Optimal Transport (OT) for generative modeling. The authors analyze the constraints of matching the aggregated posterior to the prior in latent space, highlighting the importance of divergence measures in this process. They establish that using the p-Wasserstein distance as a penalization term is advantageous for minimizing reconstruction errors while maintaining a connection to the original objective.

Key contributions include the demonstration that the minimization of the reconstruction error can effectively approximate the objective of WAE, and that classes of probabilistic encoders, including deterministic neural networks, can achieve the desired properties for approximation. The paper rigorously supports the claim that matching the aggregated posterior and prior is essential for accurately learning the true data distribution.

The SAE is described as a practical implementation that optimizes a sample-based cost function, allowing for the modeling of various prior distributions without requiring the reparametrization trick. The authors employ the Sinkhorn algorithm for efficient computation of the p-Wasserstein distance and correct for entropic biases.

Experimental results on benchmark datasets like MNIST and CelebA are presented, showing the performance of the SAE compared to traditional methods like VAE and WAE-MMD. The findings indicate that the SAE effectively accommodates different latent space geometries and prior distributions, reinforcing its versatility and efficiency in generative modeling tasks. Overall, the paper addresses and resolves open questions in the literature, providing a comprehensive framework for future research in generative autoencoders based on OT.
