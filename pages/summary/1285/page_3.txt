The paper discusses the Sliced Wasserstein AutoEncoder (WAE) framework, building on prior work by Kolouri et al. and Tolstikhin et al. The authors focus on aligning the aggregated posterior distribution QZ with a standard Gaussian prior PZ by utilizing the closed-form expression of the 2-Wasserstein distance for Gaussian distributions. They estimate the mean and covariance of QZ from minibatches and employ the Wasserstein distance as a loss function for backpropagation during training.

The paper outlines the principles of optimal transport (OT) and defines the cost of transporting distributions PX and PY. It introduces the concept of a latent variable model using a latent space Z and a prior PZ, with the goal of approximating the true data distribution PX through a generative model defined by a decoder G. The learning objective is to minimize the Wasserstein distance between the true distribution PX and the generated distribution PG.

The authors present a theoretical contribution that improves the analysis of generative autoencoders within the optimal transport framework. They establish a theorem that provides an equality relating the Wasserstein distance between PX and PG to the expected cost of transporting samples, alongside a term that accounts for the divergence between the aggregated posterior QZ and the prior PZ. This work emphasizes the importance of deterministic encoders and the role of Lipschitz continuity in achieving effective generative modeling.

Overall, the paper advances the understanding of Wasserstein autoencoding by integrating optimal transport principles and providing a clearer framework for generative modeling.
