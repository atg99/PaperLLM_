The paper discusses the use of Sinkhorn encoders for representation learning, particularly in unsupervised settings. It highlights the limitations of traditional Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) which typically utilize a Gaussian prior for tractability, and contrasts this with recent methods that employ more complex priors, such as the hyperspherical prior used by Davidson et al. The authors argue that their implicit encoder distribution simplifies the process of reparametrization and posterior sampling.

The paper references various related works, including the Sinkhorn algorithm, which has applications in multiple domains such as ranking and domain adaptation. The authors compare their approach to existing methods like Wasserstein Autoencoders (WAE) and Sliced Wasserstein AutoEncoders (SWAE), emphasizing the self-supervised nature of their encoder training that allows for independent sampling of pseudo labels.

The experimental section demonstrates the effectiveness of Sinkhorn distance as an objective for unsupervised feature learning. The authors present qualitative results from embedding tasks involving a Swiss Roll and MNIST dataset, showcasing the encoder's ability to preserve local data geometry while minimizing the specified loss function. The paper concludes that Sinkhorn encoders offer a promising direction for improving representation learning in generative models.
