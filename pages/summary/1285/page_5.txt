The paper discusses the computational aspects of optimal transport (OT) and presents the Sinkhorn AutoEncoder (SAE) framework. The exact solution for the empirical distributions ˆPZ and ˆQZ can be efficiently computed using the Hungarian algorithm with a time complexity of near O(M^3). However, the sample complexity of the Wasserstein distance between these empirical measures differs from the true Wasserstein distance by approximately O(M^(-1)k), where k is the dimension of Z. To address the computational inefficiencies, the paper introduces entropy regularization, which improves both computational and statistical properties.

The entropy regularized OT cost is defined, leading to the Sinkhorn divergence, which interpolates between OT divergences and Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD). The behavior of the Sinkhorn divergence varies with the regularization parameter ε, providing favorable sample complexity when ε is sufficiently large. The Sinkhorn algorithm allows for efficient computation of the Sinkhorn divergence with a time complexity close to O(M^2).

The SAE objective is formulated by minimizing a loss function that incorporates the Sinkhorn divergence in latent space and the original cost in data space. This leads to a framework that balances computational efficiency and adherence to the original objectives. The paper also details how to estimate the Sinkhorn divergence using the Sinkhorn algorithm, which operates on empirical distributions derived from samples of QZ and PZ, ultimately leading to a doubly stochastic matrix that minimizes the regularized OT cost. The Sinkhorn algorithm is noted for its speed compared to the Hungarian algorithm, making it a practical choice for the SAE framework.
