The paper presents a comparative analysis of various autoencoding methods applied to the MNIST and CelebA datasets, focusing on performance metrics such as Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD), Reconstruction Error (RE), and Fréchet Inception Distance (FID). The authors introduce their methods, including Wasserstein 2 Generative Adversarial Encoder (W2GAE), Hungarian Autoencoder (HAE), and Sinkhorn Autoencoder (SAE), and evaluate them against existing models like Variational Autoencoder (VAE) and its variants. The evaluation incorporates both Gaussian and hyperspherical priors, revealing that while FID scores for VAE may be marginally better, its reconstruction error is significantly higher compared to SAE and HAE.

The experiments demonstrate that the proposed methods, particularly SAE with a hyperspherical prior, achieve competitive or superior performance against other methods. The study highlights the importance of the latent space's geometry, with the Sinkhorn distance facilitating effective mapping of samples onto a hypersphere, thereby preserving local distances while ensuring a spread distribution.

Furthermore, the authors note that using the Hungarian algorithm for matching samples can yield suboptimal results in high-dimensional spaces due to the concentration of probability mass in isotropic Gaussian distributions. This phenomenon affects matching costs and can result in subpar performance when sampling from the trained model.

Overall, the findings suggest that while traditional methods like VAE have their strengths, the proposed techniques offer improved performance in terms of both reconstruction quality and representation in latent space, particularly for high-dimensional data.
