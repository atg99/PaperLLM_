The paper discusses advancements in the framework of Wasserstein Autoencoders (WAE) by introducing Theorem 2.1, which enhances the understanding of optimal transport in the context of neural networks. The main objective is to minimize the distance between distributions in a latent space defined by a deterministic encoder and decoder.

Key arguments supporting the theorem include:

1. **Monge-Kantorovich Equivalence**: This principle allows for the restriction to deterministic encoders when dealing with non-atomic distributions, which improves upon previous work by Tolstikhin et al. in 2018.

2. **Triangle Inequality**: The paper establishes that the Wasserstein distance can be bounded using a triangle inequality, which is not applicable to all divergences. This unique property facilitates the derivation of new optimization objectives.

3. **Data Processing Inequality**: The authors prove an inequality for the Wasserstein distance that incorporates the Lipschitz constant of the decoder, suggesting that using the p-Wasserstein distance in latent space is a beneficial choice.

4. **Universal Approximation**: Leveraging the universal approximation property of neural networks, the paper posits that neural networks can effectively approximate any measurable function, which is essential for ensuring the encoder can match the distributions adequately.

Theorem 2.1 concludes that failing to match the posterior and prior distributions leads to difficulties in learning the underlying data distribution. The authors emphasize that matching in latent space is crucial, akin to minimizing reconstruction error, and propose a min-min optimization objective for deterministic neural networks that incorporates the p-Wasserstein distance.

The paper further addresses the challenge of computing the p-Wasserstein distance, suggesting the use of sampled approximations to overcome computational difficulties. This establishes a foundation for the Sinkhorn Autoencoder, which utilizes entropy-regularized optimal transport to facilitate these computations effectively.
