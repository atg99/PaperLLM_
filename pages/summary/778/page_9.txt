The paper discusses various approaches to supervised feature extraction (FE) in high-dimensional data, specifically labeled hyperspectral images (HSI). It contrasts several methods, including Linear Discriminant Analysis (LDA), Constrained Graph Discriminant Analysis (CGDA), Locality Sensitive Discriminant Analysis (LSDR), and a Joint and Progressive Learning Strategy (J-Play).

The core focus is on Discriminant Analysis Feature Extraction (DAFE), which aims to find an optimal transformation matrix that maximizes class separation based on label information. This process derives a subspace from the original data, facilitating better classification. The paper elaborates on traditional LDA, which linearly transforms data to enhance discrimination by maximizing Fisher's ratio, balancing intra-class and inter-class scatter.

The paper identifies challenges with LDA, particularly its sensitivity to high-dimensional noise and the risk of overfitting due to the singularity of scatter matrices when sample sizes are small. To address these issues, Regularized LDA introduces a regularization term to enhance stability and generalization.

Additionally, the paper introduces Local Fisher Discriminant Analysis (LFDA), which extends LDA by considering local neighborhood relationships among samples, thereby capturing the underlying manifold structure of the data. LFDA modifies the scatter matrices to incorporate local weights, improving the method's robustness and effectiveness.

Overall, the paper provides a comprehensive overview of supervised FE techniques, highlighting their theoretical foundations, strengths, and limitations while suggesting potential improvements through regularization and local structure considerations.
