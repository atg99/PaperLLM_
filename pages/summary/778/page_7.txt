The paper discusses various feature extraction (FE) techniques for hyperspectral imagery (HSI), particularly those that utilize optimization frameworks and manifold learning methods. Rasti et al. highlight methods that find orthogonal subspaces by minimizing constrained cost functions, leveraging low-rank models and reconstruction-based approaches. These methods are robust to noise due to the low-rank model assumptions but often require significant computational resources due to their iterative nature.

One specific technique mentioned is Wavelet-based sparse reduced rank regression (WSRRR), which applies sparsity to wavelet coefficients, assuming that the data projected onto wavelet bases is sparse. WSRRR simultaneously estimates the low-rank projection matrix and wavelet coefficients while minimizing a cost function that incorporates a penalty on the wavelet coefficients.

Another technique discussed is orthogonal total variation component analysis (OTVCA), which models HSI as a combination of an unknown feature matrix and noise, assuming that the features are spatially piece-wise smooth. OTVCA employs a total variation penalty to capture spatial information.

Sparse and smooth low-rank analysis (SSLRA) is presented as an approach that extracts both sparse and smooth features from HSI data by combining their respective penalties in the optimization framework.

The paper also covers graph embedding and manifold learning techniques, which aim to capture the nonlinear characteristics of HSIs. These methods focus on the local geometric structure of neighboring pixels, emphasizing the importance of neighborhood pixel selection, weight selection, and embedding construction. Techniques like Isometric Mapping (ISOMAP) and Locally Linear Embedding (LLE) are explained in detail. ISOMAP works by constructing a neighborhood graph and computing geodesic distances, while LLE reconstructs data points using their neighbors through a constrained least-squares approach, preserving the intrinsic geometric properties of the data.

Overall, the paper provides an in-depth analysis of advanced FE techniques for HSIs, focusing on their mathematical foundations and computational methods.
