In this section, we discuss deep feature extraction techniques, emphasizing their advantages over shallow methods. Shallow feature extraction often requires extensive engineering and domain expertise, limiting their applicability. In contrast, deep learning techniques, such as autoencoders (AEs), convolutional neural networks (CNNs), and recurrent neural networks (RNNs), learn high-level features automatically from raw data, resulting in more robust and discriminative representations.

Autoencoders consist of an encoder that maps input data into a hidden space and a decoder that reconstructs the input. The training process minimizes the difference between the original input and its reconstruction. Stacked AEs (SAEs) enhance feature extraction by layering multiple AEs, allowing for better pre-training and reducing overfitting, especially in scenarios with limited training samples. SAEs can also generalize features across different images and effectively extract spatial features by processing local patches reshaped into vectors.

CNNs, on the other hand, are widely used for hyperspectral feature extraction due to their efficiency and effectiveness. They comprise convolutional layers for feature extraction, pooling layers for dimensionality reduction, and fully connected layers for high-level feature learning. CNNs can directly process spatial data without needing reshaping, which preserves spatial information and enhances feature extraction capabilities.

Overall, deep learning models like AEs and CNNs provide significant advancements in feature extraction from hyperspectral images, overcoming limitations associated with traditional techniques.
