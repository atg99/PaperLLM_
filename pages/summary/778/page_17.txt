The paper presents a comparative analysis of classification accuracies achieved through various shallow and deep feature extraction (FE) techniques applied to hyperspectral images from two datasets: Indian Pines 2010 and Houston University 2013. Tables V and VI detail the classification accuracies for different methods, including Unsupervised Feature Extraction (UFE) and Supervised Feature Extraction (SFE).

The results show that deep feature extraction methods generally outperform shallow methods, particularly in certain classes of the datasets. For instance, in the Indian Pines dataset, methods such as CNN and CRNN yield high accuracies, while PCA consistently underperforms compared to other techniques. PCA's limitation lies in its inability to effectively handle noise and its focus solely on spectral correlation without considering spatial information.

LPP emerges as a strong performer for the Indian Pines dataset, significantly improving classification accuracies. However, its performance declines in the Houston datasets, indicating variability in effectiveness based on dataset characteristics. The analysis emphasizes the importance of selecting appropriate feature extraction techniques tailored to specific datasets to enhance classification performance in hyperspectral image analysis.
