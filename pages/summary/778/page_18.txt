The paper presents a comparative analysis of classification accuracies obtained from various shallow and deep feature extraction techniques applied to the Houston University 2018 dataset. The results indicate that different feature extraction methods yield varying accuracies across multiple classes.

In shallow feature extraction (UFE), OTVCA is highlighted as a superior technique, particularly for the Houston datasets, demonstrating robustness to noise and the ability to exploit spatial correlations. This results in high signal-to-noise ratios and improved classification accuracies compared to other UFE methods.

For supervised feature extraction (SFE), the paper discusses the effectiveness of LDA over random forests, especially in individual material classifications. While CGDA offers automation in computing sample connectivity, its performance is generally inferior to LDA. LSDR is noted for its competitive performance but is limited by its time consumption and stability issues with small training sets. JPlay stands out for extracting meaningful features, although it incurs high computational costs.

In terms of deep feature extraction, the results indicate that spectral-spatial models (CNN, PCNN, CAE, CRNN) outperform purely spectral models (SAE, RNN). This suggests that incorporating spatial information alongside spectral data is crucial for effective hyperspectral image classification.

Overall, the findings underscore the importance of selecting appropriate feature extraction techniques based on the specific characteristics of the dataset and the objectives of classification tasks.
