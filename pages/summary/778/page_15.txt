The study compares six techniques from the subspace feature extraction (SFE) category and five deep feature extraction (FE) methods. The algorithms were set up with specific parameters that play a pivotal role in their performance. 

For unsupervised FE methods, parameters were either predefined or set through trial and error, with techniques like PCA being parameter-free, while MSTV, OTVCA, and LPP had specific parameter configurations for optimal performance. In supervised FE methods, cross-validation (CV) was employed for model selection, leading to the setting of parameters for LDA, CGDA, LSDR, and JPlay based on the datasets’ characteristics.

Deep FE techniques, including Stacked Autoencoders (SAE), Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN), and Recurrent Neural Networks (RNN), were also configured with specific architectures and parameters. The deep learning models were implemented using the PyTorch framework, optimized through the Adam algorithm, with standardized training settings to ensure consistency across runs.

Additionally, a Random Forest classifier was utilized for classification tasks across all non-deep FE techniques, with parameters adjusted to enhance prediction accuracy. The performance of these techniques was assessed across three diverse hyperspectral datasets, providing insights into their effectiveness and applicability in real-world scenarios.
