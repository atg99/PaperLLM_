The paper discusses the evolution and techniques of feature extraction in machine learning and deep learning, particularly in the context of hyperspectral data classification. Initially, it addresses the limitations of using raw pixel data as features, highlighting issues of redundancy and the neglect of spatial dependencies among pixels. To overcome these issues, the paper describes the process of feature reduction, which aims to minimize dimensionality while preserving essential spectral information. This is termed spectral feature extraction. 

Additionally, it introduces spatial feature extraction, which does not focus on dimensionality reduction but instead models spatial contextual information, often increasing the number of features. The paper emphasizes the significance of combining spectral and spatial features in hyperspectral data classification, referencing various studies in this area.

The paper further explores deep learning as a method that automates feature extraction and classification in an end-to-end framework, potentially outperforming traditional shallow machine learning approaches when sufficient training data is available. However, it raises a critical question regarding the effectiveness of advanced deep learning methods in scenarios where training data is limited, particularly within the remote sensing community.

The contributions of the paper include a comprehensive overview of hyperspectral feature extraction techniques categorized into shallow and deep methods. It provides a critical analysis of state-of-the-art techniques rooted in signal processing, statistical inference, and machine learning. The paper aims to determine whether deep learning-based feature extraction is superior to traditional shallow methods and identifies key factors for designing effective extraction techniques. 

Moreover, the paper offers publicly available resources, including codes and libraries for hyperspectral feature extraction. It also outlines future research directions and distinguishes its contributions from existing literature, particularly in terms of the breadth and depth of feature extraction techniques discussed. The dataset utilized in the study includes the Indian Pines dataset, a high-resolution hyperspectral image collected in 2010.
