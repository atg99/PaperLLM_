This paper discusses advanced methods for feature extraction and dimensionality reduction in hyperspectral imaging (HSI) through various statistical and mathematical approaches. The authors introduce the concept of Feature Spectral Discriminant Analysis (FSDA), which enhances class separability by maximizing the between-spectral scatter matrix before applying Linear Discriminant Analysis (LDA). The formulation of the scatter matrix is detailed, emphasizing the importance of the spectral representation of classes.

Furthermore, the paper outlines the General Graph Embedding (GGE) framework, which is critical in constructing various affinity matrices for different graph embedding techniques such as LDA, Locally Linear Embedding (LLE), and Sparse Graph Discriminant Analysis (SGDA). The authors illustrate how these affinity matrices can be derived from specific optimization problems, enabling the extraction of meaningful features from high-dimensional data.

The paper also addresses the challenges posed by the nonlinear distribution of HSI data, proposing Kernel Discriminant Analysis (KDA) as a solution. This method employs kernel tricks to project data into a higher-dimensional space, facilitating linear analysis of complex nonlinear relationships. The authors describe how existing LDA methods can be adapted to this kernelized context, leading to enhanced performance in classifying HSI data.

In summary, the paper provides a comprehensive overview of contemporary methods for feature extraction in HSI, focusing on FSDA, GGE, and KDA, while offering theoretical insights into the construction of affinity matrices and the application of kernel methods.
