The paper discusses the Houston University 2018 dataset, detailing the number of training and test samples for various land cover classes. The dataset includes 20 classes such as healthy grass, stressed grass, trees, soil, water, and various urban features, with a total of 504,712 samples.

The paper also introduces notations used in hyperspectral imaging (HSI), defining parameters such as the observed HSI matrix, training samples, class labels, and mathematical symbols relevant to the analysis.

The focus shifts to shallow feature extraction techniques, particularly unsupervised feature extraction (UFE). UFE does not require labeled samples, making it valuable for remote sensing applications. The paper categorizes UFE techniques into several groups, highlighting their reliance on intrinsic properties of HSI data, such as geometric and spectral information.

Three additional groups of feature extraction techniques are mentioned but not elaborated upon, including those based on indices like NDVI and NDWI, unmixing techniques, and mathematical morphology approaches. The paper further discusses conventional data projection techniques, which aim to reduce dimensionality by transforming the data into a lower-dimensional feature space using methods like principal component analysis (PCA). The transformation process is mathematically represented, emphasizing the importance of these techniques in HSI analysis.
