The paper discusses the challenges and advancements in applying deep learning models to hyperspectral image classification. It identifies two primary issues: the risk of overfitting due to a small number of training samples relative to high-dimensional spectral data, and the need for effective extraction of spectral-spatial joint features to enhance classification performance.

To address the overfitting issue, the paper categorizes strategies into four classes. The first class focuses on dimensionality reduction techniques, such as PCA, to simplify input data for CNNs while attempting to retain useful information. The second class involves data augmentation methods that generate virtual samples to enhance the training set. The third class emphasizes transfer learning, where models trained on one dataset can be adapted to another, often with minimal retraining. The fourth class explores semi-supervised and unsupervised learning approaches to leverage unlabeled data.

Regarding the extraction of spectral-spatial features, the paper discusses two main strategies. One involves using 3-D convolutional networks to process local cubes of hyperspectral images, which, while effective, increases computational complexity. The alternative approach decouples spectral and spatial feature extraction, employing different CNN architectures for each and then fusing the results.

Additionally, the paper introduces Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs), which are effective for sequential data analysis, as a promising method for hyperspectral image classification. RNNs leverage recurrent connections to capture temporal dependencies, making them suitable for modeling the sequential nature of hyperspectral data.

Overall, the paper highlights the importance of addressing both overfitting and feature extraction challenges in enhancing the performance of deep learning models in hyperspectral image analysis.
