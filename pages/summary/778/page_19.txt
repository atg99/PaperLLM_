The paper evaluates various feature extraction (FE) techniques for hyperspectral imagery (HSI) classification, focusing on performance comparisons among different models. Key findings include:

1. **PCNN vs. CNN**: PCNN consistently outperforms CNN across all datasets, particularly significant improvements are noted in the Houston 2013 and 2018 datasets. PCNN benefits from reduced redundancy through PCA, resulting in fewer parameters and better learning efficiency.

2. **UFE vs. SFE**: Unsupervised feature extraction (UFE) techniques yield superior classification accuracies compared to supervised feature extraction (SFE) methods across all datasets. UFE leverages spatial-spectral information more effectively, especially when ground samples are limited, as seen in the Houston 2013 dataset.

3. **Shallow FE vs. Deep FE**: Although shallow FE methods show slight advantages in specific datasets, deep FE techniques, particularly those based on CNN, demonstrate consistent performance across all datasets. The application of PCA before CNN enhances classification accuracy, especially in the Houston 2018 dataset due to its complexity.

4. **Land Cover Maps Comparison**: The paper presents visual comparisons of classification maps generated from various FE approaches. Methods like CNN and PCNN result in smoother maps compared to spectral classifiers, although some over-smoothing is noted, particularly in spatial-spatial FE methods.

5. **Impact of Training Sample Size**: The analysis of performance concerning the number of training samples indicates that while SFE techniques improve accuracies over spectral classifiers, they still lag behind UFE and deep FE methods. The limitations of JPlay, an SFE technique, are attributed to its inability to effectively model spatial and contextual information, particularly as sample sizes increase.

The findings emphasize the advantages of UFE and deep learning approaches in classifying HSI, particularly in scenarios with limited training data.
