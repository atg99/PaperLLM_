The paper discusses three different hyperspectral datasets utilized for land cover classification studies. 

1. **Indian Pines 2010 Dataset**: This dataset consists of 445 by 750 pixels with 360 spectral bands, a spatial resolution of 2 meters, and a spectral width of 5 nanometers. It includes 16 land cover classes, with detailed training and testing sample counts provided in Table I. The total number of samples is 198,074, with a significant number of training samples for classes like Soy-bean-mid and Hay.

2. **Houston University 2013 Dataset**: Acquired on June 23, 2012, by the Compact Airborne Spectrographic Imager, this dataset contains 349 by 1905 pixels with 144 spectral bands ranging from 0.38 to 1.05 micrometers. It features 15 classes of interest, and Table II outlines the training and test sample distribution, totaling 15,029 samples across various classes, including Grass Healthy and Residential.

3. **Houston University 2018 Dataset**: Collected on February 16, 2017, using the CASI 1500 imager, this dataset spans a spectral range of 380 to 1050 nm, comprising 48 bands and a ground sampling distance of 1 meter. It includes 20 land cover classes, with training and test samples detailed in Table III. The dataset was distributed for a data fusion contest organized by the IEEE Geoscience and Remote Sensing Society and the University of Houston.

Overall, the paper highlights the characteristics and sample distributions of these datasets, which are critical for advancing research in hyperspectral remote sensing and land cover classification.
