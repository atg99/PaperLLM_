This paper discusses the application of Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) in hyperspectral image feature extraction, emphasizing the construction of sequences for input. The hidden layer output at time t is determined by a function that considers the current input, the previous hidden layer output, and a bias vector. The paper outlines methods for sequence construction, including treating spectral bands as sequences and utilizing local patches or cubes of data. Challenges arise from the potentially long sequences, which can lead to issues like vanishing or exploding gradients. To address this, Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks and Gated Recurrent Units (GRUs) are proposed, as they handle long sequences more effectively.

The paper also suggests integrating Autoencoders (AEs) and RNNs with Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) to leverage their strengths. Two integration approaches are explored: independent processing followed by result combination, and embedding convolutional components within AEs or RNNs. Various frameworks are presented, including parallel and serial approaches for fusing spectral and spatial features. The authors highlight the effectiveness of convolutional operators in LSTM units for extracting both spectral and spatial characteristics.

Finally, the paper sets the stage for experimental evaluation of different feature extraction techniques, comparing methods like PCA and others within the Unsupervised Feature Extraction (UFE) category. The results aim to demonstrate the performance of these techniques in hyperspectral image analysis.
