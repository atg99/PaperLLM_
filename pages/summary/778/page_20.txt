The paper provides a comprehensive overview of feature extraction (FE) techniques for hyperspectral images, categorizing them into three main research lines: shallow unsupervised, shallow supervised, and deep feature extraction approaches. It highlights the evolution of these categories over the past decade, focusing on specific subcategories such as conventional data projection schemes, class separation discriminant analysis, and various deep learning models like CNN and RNN.

In the experimental section, the paper compares the performance of these techniques using three recent hyperspectral datasets, evaluating them based on classification accuracies and the quality of classification maps. Key findings include that deep learning techniques generally outperform shallow ones when sufficient training data is available, and that incorporating dimensionality reduction methods such as PCA significantly enhances the performance of deep learning approaches. Additionally, shallow unsupervised FE techniques are shown to be competitive with deep FE methods.

The paper acknowledges the limitations of its conclusions based on the specific datasets used and provides a substantial collection of codes and libraries in Python and Matlab to assist researchers in the field. The authors express gratitude to various institutions and individuals for their contributions and support.
