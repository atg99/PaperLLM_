This paper presents a comparative analysis of the computational characteristics of BERT and XLNet during inference, focusing on GFLOPS and memory bandwidth metrics. Key findings reveal that both models predominantly utilize matrix-matrix multiplication (Mat-Mat mul), which accounts for the majority of their execution time. The analysis distinguishes between two types of Mat-Mat mul: Mat-Mat mul (1) involves multiplying an input feature by a weight and is compute-intensive with high FLOP/B, while Mat-Mat mul (2) involves multiplying input features and has a lower FLOP/B due to its smaller input size and single-use nature. 

XLNet achieves superior benchmark scores compared to BERT, primarily due to its implementation of additional target position-aware representation and relative position encoding features, albeit with a performance cost of 1.2 times. The study highlights the importance of understanding these computational characteristics for optimizing model efficiency in natural language processing tasks.
