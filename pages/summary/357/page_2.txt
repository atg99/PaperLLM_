In the analysis of BERT's computational characteristics, it was observed that memory bandwidth requirements are notably higher for the attention score and output layers compared to others. Specifically, layer normalization also demands substantial memory bandwidth. The study further categorizes matrix multiplications into two types: Mat-Mat mul (1) and Mat-Mat mul (2), with the former being more compute-intensive and efficient in cache utilization.

The findings reveal that Mat-Mat mul operations dominate both the floating-point operations per second (GFLOPS) and execution time, accounting for 99% of total FLOP and 75% of the execution time in the encoder layers of BERT. The paper emphasizes the importance of understanding these computational characteristics for optimizing performance in transformer models, particularly in the context of memory bandwidth and operational efficiency.

In comparison to XLNet, which utilizes an autoregressive (AR) model and a permutation mechanism, BERT's masked language model approach presents a different trade-off in terms of capturing sequential dependencies. This distinction highlights the evolution of transformer-based architectures and their varying methodologies for handling input sequences.
