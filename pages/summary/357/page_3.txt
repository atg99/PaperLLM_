The paper discusses the architecture and performance of XLNet, a language model that outperforms BERT in various NLP tasks due to its unique permutation training method. This method shuffles input tokens and predicts words using an autoregressive approach, enabling the model to consider bidirectional context. However, shuffling leads to a loss of positional information, which XLNet addresses through a target position-aware representation and relative positional encoding, enhancing the model's ability to process long sentences effectively.

The paper compares XLNet with BERT, highlighting differences in their computational processes. XLNet employs two types of multiplications that incorporate relative keys and position scores, which contributes to its increased computational complexity. Experimental results indicate that XLNet requires 1.2 times more floating-point operations (FLOPs) and has a 1.5 times longer execution time compared to BERT.

A detailed analysis of the encoder layer reveals that both models share similar execution breakdowns, with XLNet's unique operations accounting for significant portions of its computational load. The paper concludes that despite the additional complexity, both XLNet and BERT exhibit comparable computational characteristics, suggesting that GPUs optimized for matrix multiplication are well-suited for their inference tasks.
