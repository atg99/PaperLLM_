3. Analyzing XLNet
XLNet builds on the strengths of both auto-regressive and auto-encoding models, enhancing contextual understanding while maintaining the capability for text generation. It introduces target-position-aware representations and relative position encodings, which improve performance on language tasks compared to BERT. Despite these enhancements, XLNet incurs higher computational costs, requiring approximately 1.2 times more arithmetic operations and 1.5 times the execution time of BERT on a modern CPU during inference.

4. Comparison of Computational Characteristics
Both BERT and XLNet are evaluated on the MPRC benchmark, demonstrating comparable computational characteristics overall. However, the unique features of XLNet, such as its handling of position encodings, contribute to its superior performance in certain benchmarks. The analysis highlights that while XLNet may offer better accuracy, it does so at the expense of increased computational demands.

5. Conclusion
In summary, BERT and XLNet represent advanced approaches to NLP tasks, leveraging the Transformer architecture. While BERT excels in context understanding with efficient use of resources, XLNet enhances this capability but at a greater computational cost. This study provides insight into the trade-offs between performance and resource utilization in contemporary NLP models, informing future research and development directions in the field.
