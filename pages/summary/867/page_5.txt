The paper presents an analysis of dynamic node embedding quality using the DySAT model, focusing on its performance in link prediction tasks. The experiments are guided by three research questions: comparing DySAT with state-of-the-art methods, evaluating its effectiveness for new links and unseen nodes, and assessing the efficiency and interpretability of its structural and temporal self-attentional layers.

The study utilizes four dynamic graph datasets: two communication networks (Enron and UCI) and two rating networks (Yelp and MovieLens). Summary statistics reveal variations in the number of nodes and links across these datasets. The authors compare DySAT against various static and dynamic graph embedding baselines, including node2vec, GraphSAGE, GCN-AE, DynamicTriad, DynGEM, and DynAERNN, to highlight the advantages of incorporating temporal information for link prediction.

For the link prediction tasks, both single-step and multi-step predictions are conducted. In single-step predictions, the model forecasts connections between nodes at the next time step, while multi-step predictions extend this to multiple future time steps. The evaluation employs the Area Under the ROC Curve (AUC) metric, indicating that DySAT consistently outperforms the best baseline by 4-5% in macro-AUC across all datasets. This performance improvement is significant when compared to the typical gains of about 2% reported in related literature.

Overall, the findings emphasize the effectiveness of DySAT in dynamic graph representation learning, particularly for link prediction tasks, showcasing its potential benefits over existing methodologies.
