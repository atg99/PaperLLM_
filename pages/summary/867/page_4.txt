The paper presents DySAT, a neural architecture designed for dynamic graph representation learning, utilizing structural and temporal self-attention mechanisms. The architecture operates on a series of graph snapshots, producing node representations at each time step.

DySAT is composed of two main components: a structural attention block and a temporal attention block. The structural block employs multiple stacked structural self-attention layers to extract features from nodes based on their local neighborhoods at each snapshot. Each layer is applied independently across snapshots, with shared parameters, allowing the model to capture the local structure around nodes over time.

The output from the structural attention block is then processed by the temporal attention block. This block incorporates position embeddings to capture temporal ordering and combines these with structural outputs to generate a sequence of input representations. It also consists of multiple stacked temporal self-attention layers, culminating in final node representations that are optimized to maintain local graph context.

The paper further details a graph context prediction objective, which encourages the learned representations to reflect the structural evolution of the graph. This is achieved through a binary cross-entropy loss that promotes similarity in representations for nodes that co-occur in fixed-length random walks, while also incorporating negative sampling to balance the training process. Overall, DySAT aims to effectively model dynamic graphs by leveraging both structural and temporal information.
