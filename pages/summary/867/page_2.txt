The paper introduces the Dynamic Self-Attention Network (DySAT), a novel neural architecture designed to improve dynamic graph representation learning. DySAT leverages joint self-attention mechanisms across structural neighborhoods and temporal dynamics to effectively learn latent node representations from evolving graphs. By employing structural attention, DySAT aggregates features from local node neighborhoods, while temporal attention captures the evolution of the graph over time by assigning adaptive weights to historical representations.

The architecture features multiple attention heads in both structural and temporal layers, facilitating the modeling of diverse variations in graph structures. Experiments demonstrate that DySAT outperforms several state-of-the-art methods in both single-step and multi-step link prediction tasks, achieving an average improvement of 4.8% in macro-AUC. An ablation study highlights the advantages of combining structural and temporal attention, and the visualization of temporal attention weights showcases DySAT's adaptability to different evolutionary trends in datasets.

Key contributions of the paper include:
- The proposal of DySAT, which utilizes joint structural and temporal self-attention for dynamic graph representation learning.
- The modular design of DySAT that enables efficient computational processes compared to recurrent neural network approaches.
- A comprehensive experimental validation showing DySAT's superior performance in dynamic link prediction tasks.

The paper also contextualizes DySAT within existing literature, discussing previous methods in static and dynamic graph representation learning, as well as the limitations of recurrent methods in capturing long-range variations in graph structures. The authors define dynamic graph representation learning as the task of learning latent representations that reflect both local structures and temporal behaviors of nodes across various time steps.
