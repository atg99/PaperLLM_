The paper presents a comparative analysis of various graph representation learning methods for single-step and multi-step link prediction tasks. The methods evaluated include node2vec, GraphSAGE (G-SAGE), G-SAGE combined with GAT, GCN-AE, GAT-AE, DynamicTriad, DynGEM, DynAERNN, and DySAT. The performance is measured using Micro-AUC and Macro-AUC metrics across multiple datasets: Enron, UCI, Yelp, and ML-10M.

Key findings indicate that DySAT significantly outperforms other methods, achieving an average Macro-AUC improvement of 4.8% across all datasets. Notably, DySAT maintains consistent performance over time, showcasing its ability to effectively utilize temporal context, which contrasts with the performance fluctuations observed in static embedding methods. In single-step predictions, GraphSAGE demonstrates comparable performance to dynamic methods, likely due to its use of trainable neighborhood aggregators. Additionally, node2vec shows stable performance, suggesting potential enhancements for DySAT through second-order sampling techniques.

In multi-step link prediction, DySAT again leads, exhibiting only slight performance decay over time, while other methods suffer from larger variances due to their lack of temporal awareness. The paper also investigates the impact of new graph elements, specifically focusing on link prediction for unseen nodes and new links, indicating DySAT's robustness in adapting to evolving graph structures.

Overall, the results highlight DySAT's superior capability in capturing temporal dependencies and its effectiveness in link prediction tasks compared to both static and dynamic graph embedding techniques.
