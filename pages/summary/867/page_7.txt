The paper presents a detailed analysis of various models for single-step link prediction in dynamic graphs, focusing on their performance measured by micro and macro averaged AUC scores across several datasets: Enron, UCI, Yelp, and ML-10M. The results indicate that DySAT consistently outperforms other baseline models, achieving relative gains of 3-5% in Macro-AUC on most datasets, although it is outperformed by DynAERNN on the Yelp dataset due to the latter's effectiveness in handling new links with limited historical context.

The evaluation emphasizes the challenge of predicting new links compared to all links at a given time snapshot, with all methods showing lower AUC scores in this restricted setup. DySAT's superior performance is attributed to its ability to incorporate the temporal evolution of neighboring nodes, even when dealing with unseen nodes that lack historical data. The paper also discusses the sensitivity of models to unseen nodes, highlighting DySAT's ability to characterize these nodes through indirect temporal signals from neighboring seen nodes.

Furthermore, the paper explores the role of self-attention in DySAT, conducting an ablation study to assess the impact of structural and temporal attention. The findings reveal that both attention mechanisms significantly enhance performance, with structural attention proving particularly effective when applied to snapshot-specific neighborhoods. Overall, DySAT demonstrates a robust framework for link prediction in dynamic graphs, leveraging joint structural and temporal self-attention for improved accuracy and interpretability.
