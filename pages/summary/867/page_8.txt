The paper presents an analysis of DySAT, a self-attentional architecture designed for dynamic graph representation learning, focusing on its performance, scalability, and attention mechanisms. The ablation study reveals that DySAT outperforms variants without structural or temporal attention layers, achieving an average improvement of 3% in Macro-AUC. The model's performance is enhanced by using multi-head attention, stabilizing with eight heads for both structural and temporal layers, which effectively captures graph evolution.

In terms of scalability, DySAT significantly reduces training time compared to DynAERNN, an RNN-based method for dynamic graph modeling. DySAT's training time per epoch is approximately 88 seconds with a temporal history window of ten on the ML-10M dataset, while DynAERNN takes 723 seconds. This advantage is attributed to the parallelization of self-attention mechanisms, contrasting with the sequential nature of RNNs.

The paper also visualizes temporal attention weights, showing distinct patterns between the UCI communication network and the Yelp rating network. In UCI, attention weights are uniformly distributed, while Yelp exhibits a bias towards recent snapshots, reflecting the bursty nature of user interactions in that dataset. This adaptability in attention weight distribution indicates DySAT's capability to learn from different temporal dynamics.

The authors discuss the architectural choices in DySAT, emphasizing the stacking of temporal attention over structural attention due to the instability of graph structures over time. They suggest future exploration of alternative architectures and highlight DySAT's potential for incremental learning in streaming environments, paving the way for further research in self-attentional models for dynamic graphs.
