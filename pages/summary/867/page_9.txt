In this paper, the authors present DySAT, a novel self-attentional architecture designed for dynamic graph representation learning. DySAT enhances the computation of dynamic node representations by employing joint self-attention across both the structural neighborhood and historical representations, effectively addressing the temporal evolution of graph structures. Experimental evaluations on four real-world datasets reveal that DySAT significantly outperforms various state-of-the-art static and dynamic graph embedding methods. The authors propose future research directions to explore continuous-time generalizations, which could allow for the incorporation of more nuanced temporal variations in graph representation learning.
