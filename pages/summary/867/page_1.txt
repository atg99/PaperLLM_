In this paper, we introduce DySAT, which leverages self-attention to effectively capture the dynamic nature of graphs. DySAT computes node representations by jointly considering structural neighborhoods and temporal dynamics through a self-attention mechanism. This approach offers a significant advantage over existing recurrent methods, which tend to be less efficient and often require extensive training data.

We evaluate DySAT's performance through link prediction experiments across two types of dynamic graphs: communication networks and bipartite rating networks. Our results indicate that DySAT consistently outperforms several state-of-the-art graph embedding techniques, demonstrating its efficacy in both single and multi-step link prediction tasks. Additionally, an ablation study reinforces the importance of jointly modeling structural and temporal aspects in dynamic graph representation learning.

In conclusion, DySAT presents a novel and efficient framework for learning dynamic node representations, addressing the challenges posed by evolving graph structures while achieving superior performance in predictive tasks.
