The paper discusses a Relation Encoder designed for visual question answering (VQA), focusing on the construction of graphs to represent relationships between objects in images. It introduces two types of graphs: a fully-connected relation graph and a pruned graph with explicit relations based on prior knowledge.

1. **Graph Construction**:
   - **Fully-connected Relation Graph**: Each object in an image is treated as a vertex in a fully-connected undirected graph, where edges represent implicit relationships between objects. Weights for these edges are learned through graph attention without prior knowledge, forming the implicit relation encoder.
   - **Pruned Graph with Prior Knowledge**: When explicit relationships are known, the fully-connected graph can be transformed into a sparse graph by removing edges that do not correspond to valid relationships. Each edge reflects specific inter-object relations, and self-loop edges are added to represent identical relations. This results in an explicit relation encoder that utilizes pre-trained classifiers to extract discrete class labels for object relationships.

2. **Types of Relations**:
   - **Spatial Graph**: Constructs a spatial graph to denote geometric positions of objects relative to each other, categorizing relationships into 11 classes. The edges are symmetrical, meaning if one relationship exists, the reverse is also valid but may have different predicates.
   - **Semantic Graph**: Focuses on semantic relations, which are not symmetrical. The construction involves classifying pairs of objects to identify their semantic relations through a trained classifier, taking into account features from both objects and their bounding box.

3. **Relation Encoder**:
   - The encoder employs a question-adaptive graph attention mechanism, allowing it to dynamically adjust the weights of relations based on the question being asked. This is done by concatenating question embeddings with visual feature vectors and applying self-attention to generate hidden relation features.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of both spatial and semantic relations in enhancing the capability of VQA systems by effectively encoding the dynamics between objects in images.
