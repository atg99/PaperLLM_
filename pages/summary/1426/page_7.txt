The paper presents a study on various fusion methods for visual question answering (VQA), highlighting the performance of the proposed ReGAT model compared to existing frameworks. The results demonstrate that ReGAT consistently outperforms baseline models across multiple datasets, including VQA 2.0 and VQA-CP v2.

Table 1 shows that ReGAT achieves significant improvements over traditional fusion methods like BUTD, MUTAN, and BAN, with the best results obtained through a weighted sum of the best single relation models. A statistical analysis confirms the significance of these improvements, with a p-value of 0.001459 indicating strong evidence against the null hypothesis.

In Table 2, ReGAT also outperforms the baseline on the VQA-CP v2 benchmark, achieving state-of-the-art results with a score of 40.30, surpassing the previous best of 39.54. This indicates the model’s robustness in handling datasets with different training-test distributions.

Table 3 provides a breakdown of the model's accuracy on the VQA 2.0 benchmark, showing that ReGAT surpasses all previous models, both with and without relational reasoning. The model utilizes bilinear attention with fewer glimpses compared to BAN, yet still achieves superior performance.

The ablation study in Table 4 assesses the importance of attention mechanisms and question-adaptive features in ReGAT. The findings reveal that incorporating attention mechanisms significantly enhances accuracy across various relational types, with question-adaptive features providing additional improvements.

Overall, the paper underscores the effectiveness of the ReGAT model in VQA tasks, demonstrating its capacity to leverage relational reasoning for enhanced performance while maintaining generalizability across different datasets.
