The paper presents a study on universal domain adaptation (UDA) methods, focusing on the performance of various algorithms, including UAN, DANN, IWAN, and OSBP, using the Office-31 dataset. The authors analyze the accuracy of these methods relative to the size of the target private label set (Ct) and the common label set (C). 

Key findings indicate that UAN consistently outperforms other methods, particularly in challenging UDA scenarios. Unlike other approaches that suffer from negative transfer—where performance degrades compared to models trained solely on source data—UAN effectively promotes positive transfer across all classes by employing a sample-level transferability criterion. This criterion allows UAN to distinguish between known and unknown classes more effectively than existing methods that rely on conformance to assumptions about label sets.

The analysis reveals that UAN maintains robust performance regardless of the size of Ct and Cs, demonstrating its versatility in varying UDA settings. In scenarios where the common label set is minimal or non-existent, UAN's performance remains superior, highlighting the limitations of other methods that depend on the overlap between source and target domains.

Overall, the results emphasize the effectiveness of UAN in UDA tasks, showcasing its ability to adapt across different configurations without prior knowledge of the target label set. The findings suggest that UAN is a promising approach for tackling the challenges inherent in UDA.
