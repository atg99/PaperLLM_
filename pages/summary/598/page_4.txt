The paper introduces the Universal Adaptation Network (UAN) aimed at addressing challenges in Universal Domain Adaptation (UDA). The architecture of UAN consists of a feature extractor, adversarial domain discriminator, non-adversarial domain discriminator, and label classifier. The system is designed to handle the complexities of domain adaptation, particularly the unknown relationships between source and target classes.

The UAN's training phase focuses on aligning the feature distributions of source and target data within a common label set, addressing both category and domain gaps. A significant challenge in UDA is the detection of "unknown" classes, which conventional confidence thresholding methods may struggle with due to overconfident predictions from neural networks.

The paper outlines the loss functions for the label classifier and both domain discriminators, emphasizing the need for a mechanism to detect the common label set. The training process is formulated as a minimax game, balancing transferability and discriminability through a hyper-parameter.

Overall, UAN aims to create domain-invariant features to improve classification performance in target domains while managing the complexities of unknown classes and domain differences.
