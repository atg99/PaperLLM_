Universal Domain Adaptation (UDA) addresses the challenge of transferring knowledge between source and target domains without prior knowledge of their label sets. Traditional domain adaptation methods often rely on well-defined relationships between source and target labels, which limits their applicability in real-world scenarios. UDA allows for a situation where both domains may have shared labels (common label set) and unique labels (private label set), leading to an additional category gap.

The core requirement for a UDA model is to classify target samples correctly if they belong to the common label set or to label them as "unknown" if they do not. This model must also perform reliably across various proportions of commonality, which is crucial for handling unknown target label sets in practical applications.

To address these challenges, the authors propose the Universal Adaptation Network (UAN). UAN quantifies the transferability of individual samples to identify both the common and private label sets, thereby enhancing adaptation for the discovered common labels and effectively recognizing unknown samples. 

The paper presents comprehensive evaluations demonstrating that UAN surpasses existing methods in closed set, partial, and open set domain adaptation within the new UDA framework. This advancement signifies a notable step forward in the field, enabling more robust domain adaptation in diverse real-world contexts.
