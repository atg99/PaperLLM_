The paper presents a novel Universal Domain Adaptation (UDA) framework, introducing the Universal Adaptation Network (UAN) that operates without prior knowledge of label set relationships across domains. It emphasizes the importance of a sample-level transferability criterion to effectively address UDA challenges. The study includes an ablation analysis demonstrating that both domain similarity and uncertainty criteria are essential for UAN's performance. Results indicate that UAN consistently outperforms existing methods, especially in scenarios where prior knowledge is unavailable.

The paper also investigates the sensitivity of UAN to threshold variations, revealing that its accuracy remains stable across a wide range of thresholds, further validating its robustness. The findings suggest that UAN can serve as a valuable tool for domain adaptation in new scenarios, providing insights into whether additional label collection is necessary based on its classification outcomes. Overall, UAN showcases state-of-the-art results, making it a promising candidate for practical applications in UDA.
