The paper discusses the intersection of reinforcement learning (RL) and receding horizon control (RHC), highlighting the potential for a middle ground between costly offline trajectory optimization and real-time RHC. It suggests that improving the modeling of the Q-function used in RHC can enhance performance, reducing the necessary time horizon for simulations and facilitating real-time operations. The ideal scenario of having a perfect Q-function model would allow for the optimal control policy to be derived from the Bellman equation.

The concept of iteratively refining the Q-function during RHC execution is explored through the "Learning MPC" approach developed by Rosolia, Carvalho, and Borrelli. This method utilizes a nearest-neighbor technique to learn the terminal cost associated with states, enabling exploration while ensuring safety by referencing previously visited states. This approach has demonstrated practical success, outperforming human operators in RC car demonstrations after limited training.

The paper also emphasizes the benefits of integrating hard-coded constraints and disturbance models into the optimization problem, striking a balance between safety and performance, especially in complex control tasks. It proposes further research in merging robust learning techniques with RHC.

Moreover, the author identifies key challenges at the interface of control and learning, particularly the integration of perception and control. The aspiration for end-to-end control in RL, akin to advancements in computer vision, presents significant opportunities and challenges, particularly regarding the complexities of nonlinear output feedback. The paper concludes by advocating for continued collaboration between the control and machine learning communities to address these challenges and enhance system capabilities.
