The paper investigates the performance of various reinforcement learning (RL) algorithms in the context of designing a controller for a double integrator model. The comparison focuses on the efficiency of different approaches in achieving optimal control with minimal simulations. 

Experiments were conducted using a small noise level, and the aim was to develop a controller that operates effectively over a long time horizon while minimizing the number of simulations needed. The nominal control strategy demonstrated strong performance, achieving nearly optimal results with just one simulation. This method, however, faced challenges when a least squares estimator was applied, as it disregarded prior information about sparsity in the state transition matrices.

The study also evaluated approximate dynamic programming and policy search methods, specifically using static linear policies and the Policy Gradient algorithm. While static linear policies performed comparably to more complex time-varying policies in this simple linear quadratic regulator (LQR) problem, the Policy Gradient algorithm struggled to converge without additional techniques like baseline subtraction to reduce variance.

Results showed that both nominal control and Least Squares Policy Iteration (LSPI) achieved high-quality controllers with only ten observations, while direct policy methods, particularly Policy Gradient, required significantly more samples—thousands more—indicating a clear advantage for simpler control methods in this scenario. The findings highlight the effectiveness of nominal control and LSPI in achieving optimal performance with fewer resources compared to more complex RL approaches.
