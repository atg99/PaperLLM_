This survey seeks to establish a common language for the control and reinforcement learning (RL) communities, emphasizing the potential benefits of cross-disciplinary communication. Both fields aim to design systems that utilize structured perception, adapt to environmental changes, and implement safeguards in unexpected scenarios. The paper highlights the need for insights from machine learning, optimization, statistics, and control theory to analyze and certify such systems effectively.

The author focuses on continuous control problems, aiming to clarify the similarities and differences between the methodologies of RL and control. The survey begins by framing RL within an optimization context, detailing the methodological tools relevant to contemporary RL. It distinguishes between model-free and model-based approaches and uses a case study of the linear quadratic regulator (LQR) with unknown dynamics to compare these techniques. The findings suggest that model-free methods prevalent in deep reinforcement learning are often less effective than simpler model-based approaches, a trend that persists even in more complex nonlinear applications.

The author proposes that model-free and model-based strategies can be integrated to leverage their respective strengths. The discussion concludes with an emphasis on the challenges at the intersection of control and learning, stressing the necessity of collaboration between the two communities to develop robust and safe learning systems capable of interacting with uncertain physical environments.

Reinforcement learning, as defined in the paper, involves using past data to improve the manipulation of dynamical systems, differentiating it from traditional machine learning by framing it as optimal control in scenarios with unknown dynamics. The goal is to determine a sequence of inputs that maximizes an objective, starting with minimal knowledge of the system's responses. The classic optimal control framework is presented, where the aim is to maximize expected rewards over time by optimizing control sequences while adhering to the defined state-transition dynamics.
