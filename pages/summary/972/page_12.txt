The paper discusses the formulation of optimal control problems using positive semidefinite matrices Q, R, and S. It emphasizes the transition from maximization to minimization, which is standard in optimal control. The state transitions are defined by a linear update rule involving matrices A and B. While linear dynamics may seem restrictive, they are relevant because many engineered systems operate linearly within their effective range. This linearity ensures that the constraint set remains convex, which simplifies analysis.

Cost functions, in contrast to dynamics, are at the discretion of the engineer. The design and refinement of cost functions are crucial in optimal control, as they can significantly influence performance. The paper highlights the challenge of creating effective cost functions, noting that quadratic costs are particularly beneficial due to their convex nature and favorable interaction with noise. The stochastic nature of problems affects the achievable cost but does not alter the selection of control actions.

When the parameters of the system are known, the standard Linear Quadratic Regulator (LQR) problem can be elegantly solved using dynamic programming. The optimal control action is expressed as a linear function of the state, with a fixed matrix K determined by the solution of the Discrete Algebraic Riccati Equation. As the time horizon approaches infinity, the control policy becomes static and linear.

The paper also discusses various methods to derive these results, including dynamic programming approaches. It mentions that the Q-function on a finite time horizon follows a recursive relationship, with the limit of specific matrices yielding the solution to the Riccati equation. This approach provides a systematic way to address optimal control problems in a linear context.
