The paper discusses the challenges and advancements in using reinforcement learning (RL) for controlling legged robots, particularly those with complex dynamics defined by Lagrange's equations. It highlights the difficulties in designing effective objective functions for locomotion planning due to the piecewise linear nature of these models, which change upon contact with solid objects, introducing normal forces.

Notable contributions in this field are mentioned, including the work of Tedrake, Zhang, and Seung, who successfully applied direct policy search to develop feedback control policies for legged robots. Levine and Koltun's pioneering use of the MuJoCo simulation environment for learning-based control is also noted, where they achieved complex walking without specialized techniques. This work has established a standard benchmark for continuous control in RL.

The effectiveness of random search methods, particularly by Salimans and his team at OpenAI, is highlighted, showing that simple random search can outperform policy gradient methods in certain applications. The study by Rajeswaran et al. demonstrated that Natural Policy Gradient could learn linear policies sufficient for controlling robotic simulators, raising the question of whether random search could similarly find linear controllers for MuJoCo tasks.

The authors, Guy, Mania, and others, explored this by implementing a basic version of random search with enhancements, leading to state-of-the-art results across MuJoCo benchmark tasks. Key takeaways from their findings suggest that these benchmarks may be easier than previously thought, indicating a potential overfitting issue to simulation approximations rather than genuine learning of walking policies.

Moreover, the paper emphasizes the high variance exhibited by model-free methods in these benchmarks. The authors observed that even with seemingly good parameters, the training process could yield inconsistent results, pointing to a broader issue of reliability in model-free methods across various control tasks. Overall, while direct policy search is straightforward to implement, its dependability in real-world applications remains uncertain.
