In this paper, the Q-function Q(x0;u0) is defined to represent the average reward obtained by starting from state x0 with action u0. The discussion highlights the challenges in applying dynamic programming directly to the problem at hand, prompting a shift to a discounted reward framework. The optimization problem is reformulated to maximize the expected discounted reward, governed by a discount factor that facilitates connection to the average reward scenario.

The Q-function is then expressed in a way that allows for the establishment of optimality conditions, leading to a recursive relationship that is central to dynamic programming. The optimal policy is derived simply as the action that maximizes the Q-function at any given state.

To estimate the Q-function, the paper proposes using stochastic approximation based on sampled trajectories, leading to an iterative update mechanism fundamental to Q-learning algorithms. The update rule incorporates a learning rate, allowing for convergence towards the true Q-function.

The paper contrasts the Q-function approach with the more conventional value function perspective in Approximate Dynamic Programming (ADP). It notes that while Q-learning focuses on Q-functions, methods for estimating value functions, including Temporal Difference algorithms, are also prevalent in reinforcement learning.

Finally, the authors caution that despite the model-free label often associated with certain reinforcement learning techniques, they still rely on the assumption that the functions being estimated possess a reasonable structure, highlighting the inherent need for effective parameterization.
