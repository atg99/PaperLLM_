The paper discusses the application of robust Linear Quadratic Regulator (LQR) in the context of control systems, particularly focusing on an unstable Laplacian dynamics scenario, exemplified by a data center cooling system. The model consists of three heat sources and their cooling devices, with the state variables representing the internal temperatures of these sources. The system is characterized by a linear dynamical model with an unstable open-loop system, indicating that any non-zero initial condition leads to an unbounded state vector.

The critical challenge highlighted is the necessity of accurately estimating the system's behavior to ensure optimal control and safety. The paper explores two versions of robust LQR: one with known model error and another where the error is estimated through bootstrap simulations. The results, presented in Figure 2, compare the cost of nominal control with the performance of robust LQR approaches. The blue curve illustrates the performance when the actual model error is known, while the green curve shows the performance with estimated errors.

The findings indicate that even when the model error is estimated, the performance of robust LQR remains only slightly inferior to the case where the true model error is known. This underscores the effectiveness of robust LQR in managing uncertainties in control systems, emphasizing the importance of robust methods in ensuring stability and safety in practical applications.
