While early results in reinforcement learning have shown potential in training optimal controllers directly from pixel data, they suffer from poor sample efficiency compared to state-based training methods. Guided Policy Search emerges as a promising technique, allowing for the iterative learning of a mapping from sensor inputs to optimal actions, thereby reducing reliance on precise state estimators over time. However, transitioning from fully observed to partially observed scenarios significantly complicates control problems, making the effective use of diverse sensor data a critical research challenge.

The paper also discusses the complexities of adaptive control, which differs fundamentally from episodic reinforcement learning. In adaptive control, learning occurs online with only one trajectory available, aiming for a long-term maximization of rewards. The best methodologies for adaptive control, even in simpler frameworks like Linear Quadratic Regulator (LQR), remain unresolved. While some advancements have been made using stochastic gradient techniques and online learning perspectives, no efficient method has yet been developed that guarantees optimal cost.

Finally, the interaction between humans and machines presents another significant challenge. The difficulty lies in modeling human behavior within robotic systems, as humans can be unpredictable. Game theoretic approaches have been suggested to frame human actions as disturbances in the control problem, yet capturing the complexities of human decision-making remains a formidable task. Overall, the paper underscores the need for new techniques that integrate insights from both machine learning and control theory to address these multifaceted challenges effectively.
