This collection of references encompasses a variety of studies and reports in the fields of computational complexity, reinforcement learning, adaptive control, and machine learning applications.

Blondel and Tsitsiklis provide a survey on computational complexity in systems and control, emphasizing the theoretical underpinnings of these fields. Bojarski et al. discuss end-to-end learning for self-driving cars, presenting a practical application of deep learning techniques. Bottou et al. explore counterfactual reasoning in computational advertising, illustrating the intersection of machine learning with real-world decision-making processes.

Bowling et al. demonstrate the resolution of heads-up limit hold'em poker, showcasing advancements in algorithmic strategies. Bradtke and Barto contribute to the literature on temporal difference learning and adaptive control through their works on linear least-squares algorithms and policy iteration.

Research by Campi and Weyer examines the finite sample properties of system identification methods, while Dann and Brunskill address the sample complexity in episodic fixed-horizon reinforcement learning. Dayan's studies delve into reinforcement comparison and the convergence of temporal difference methods.

Dean et al. investigate sample complexity and regret bounds in the linear quadratic regulator context, contributing to the foundational understanding of adaptive control. Efron introduces bootstrap methods, enhancing statistical analysis techniques.

Erez et al. present a real-time model predictive control system for humanoid robots, highlighting advancements in robotic control. Flaxman et al. discuss online convex optimization in a bandit setting, providing insights into algorithmic efficiency without gradient information. Gao and Jamidar focus on machine learning applications for optimizing data centers, demonstrating practical implications of these methodologies.

Goodwin et al. examine discrete-time stochastic adaptive control, enriching the control theory landscape. Hazan et al. propose near-optimal algorithms for online matrix prediction, further advancing learning theory. Finally, Henderson et al. emphasize the importance of impactful deep reinforcement learning, while Islam et al. address reproducibility in benchmarked tasks, underscoring the significance of validation in machine learning research.

Overall, these references reflect a diverse range of contributions to understanding and applying advanced concepts in systems, control, and machine learning.
