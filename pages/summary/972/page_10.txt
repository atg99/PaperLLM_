The section discusses two reinforcement learning (RL) methods: Policy Gradient and Pure Random Search. 

In Policy Gradient, the focus is on using probabilistic policies instead of deterministic ones, which are optimal for certain types of control problems. The probabilistic policy, parameterized by a vector #, generates a probability distribution over trajectories. The reward of a trajectory is defined as the sum of rewards at each time step. The optimization problem can then be framed using a trajectory sampled from this policy, and updates are performed using the REINFORCE algorithm. Notably, the gradient of the expected reward with respect to the parameters is independent of the underlying dynamics, which shifts the optimization focus to the sampling process.

Pure Random Search, an older method, involves perturbing the current decision variable by adding random noise and updating the model based on the reward received from this perturbed state. This method allows for the REINFORCE algorithm to be applied without knowledge of the underlying dynamics. It interprets REINFORCE as an approximate gradient ascent process, where a finite difference approximation of the gradient is computed. Variance in this estimation can be reduced by averaging results from multiple random perturbations.

Overall, both methods highlight different approaches to optimizing policies in reinforcement learning, emphasizing the flexibility in handling uncertainties in the underlying system dynamics.
