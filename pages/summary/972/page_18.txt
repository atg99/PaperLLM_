The paper presents an analysis of various control strategies applied to a Laplacian model, particularly focusing on the effectiveness of robust optimization methods compared to nominal control strategies. The results indicate that while nominal controllers frequently fail to stabilize the true system, robust LQR methods, including those using bootstrap techniques, consistently yield stabilizing solutions. This is crucial as the stabilization condition requires all eigenvalues of the matrix A + BK to be less than one.

The performance of model-free methods, such as LSPI and policy gradient approaches, is also compared, revealing that they significantly lag behind model-based methods. The paper highlights that LSPI, which previously excelled in simpler environments, struggles in this context, possibly due to its requirement for a stabilizing controller at all iterations and the sensitivity to hyperparameter tuning. It emphasizes that model-free methods are not only sample inefficient but also lack safety, a critical aspect in control applications.

In the latter part of the paper, the authors discuss the implications of their findings for advancing beyond the Linear Quadratic Regulator (LQR) framework. They explore derivative-free methods, noting that while random search performs well in simpler scenarios, its effectiveness diminishes in more complex problems. The discussion also references benchmarks from the deep reinforcement learning community, particularly those involving legged robot simulations, suggesting a pathway for future research in optimal control.
