The paper discusses the limitations of model-free reinforcement learning (RL) algorithms, particularly in the context of continuous control problems. It highlights that these methods do not effectively utilize samples due to their reliance on a single equation per time step, as opposed to multiple equations available when modeling state-transition functions. The introduction of discount factors complicates the Bellman equation and can lead to instability, requiring careful tuning to maintain performance.

The paper then shifts to direct policy search, a method that learns a policy function directly from experiences without constructing a model or relying on the Bellman equation. This approach reframes RL as a derivative-free optimization problem. 

A generic algorithm for sampling to optimize is presented, emphasizing the equivalence between optimizing a function and optimizing over probability distributions. The challenge lies in the intractability of optimizing all probability densities, necessitating a restriction to a parameterized family of distributions. This reparameterization allows for a robust optimization framework, although it may only provide a lower bound on optimal values if certain distributions, like Delta functions, are not included. The paper concludes by noting that this approach facilitates the computation of derivatives for optimization tasks.
