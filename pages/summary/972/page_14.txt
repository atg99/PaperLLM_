The paper discusses a robust variant of the standard Linear Quadratic Regulator (LQR) optimal control problem, focusing on the design of a stabilizing controller that minimizes worst-case performance amidst model errors. The authors utilize the System Level Synthesis (SLS) framework to address this robust optimization challenge, which allows for efficient controller search by elevating the system description into a higher-dimensional space. The approach guarantees finite performance over an infinite time horizon and establishes a quantitative relationship between the designed controller's performance and the optimal controller.

In the context of LQR with state dimension d and control dimension p, the analysis shows that after observing a trajectory of length T, a controller can be designed with an infinite-time-horizon cost that is bounded by a term related to the dimensions and the trajectory length. This method, termed Coarse-ID control, explicitly incorporates uncertainty in the least squares estimate, eliminating the need for perturbation studies of Riccati equations and making it adaptable to various optimal control problems.

The paper also addresses the sample complexity of model-free reinforcement learning (RL) for LQR. It notes that the Q-function for LQR is quadratic and discusses methods for estimating it via dynamic programming, referencing previous work that demonstrated the feasibility of estimating the value function with a specific sample complexity. The authors highlight the inefficiencies of current temporal-differencing methods compared to model estimation techniques, suggesting that estimating Q-functions may not be as straightforward as previously assumed, particularly in terms of data aggregation.

In summary, the paper presents innovative approaches to robust control and sample-efficient model-free RL for LQR, emphasizing the importance of accounting for uncertainty and the inherent challenges associated with estimating Q-functions.
