This paper discusses the challenges and methodologies in reinforcement learning (RL), particularly focusing on model-free methods, which include Policy Search and Approximate Dynamic Programming. The authors argue that it is often simpler to identify a policy for a task than to develop a general-purpose model of system dynamics. 

Model-free methods rely on previously collected data to improve reward outcomes. Policy Search aims to directly find optimal policies, while Approximate Dynamic Programming utilizes Bellman's principle of optimality to approximate control problems based on observed data. The paper emphasizes that all surveyed approaches ultimately involve function fitting from noisy observations, and the effectiveness of these methods can vary significantly based on the fitting process.

In model-based reinforcement learning, the authors discuss the strategy of estimating a predictive model for the dynamical process, referred to as the nominal model. This model serves as a baseline for control design, known as nominal control. System identification is highlighted as a crucial aspect of estimating dynamical systems, where careful selection of inputs is necessary to excite different system parameters.

The paper outlines a classic strategy for predicting future states using historical trajectories, which involves injecting a random probing sequence into the system and measuring responses. The state-transition function can be modeled through supervised learning techniques, such as least squares optimization. Overall, the main focus is on the comparison of different approaches in terms of sample efficiency and the speed of convergence to optimal policies.
