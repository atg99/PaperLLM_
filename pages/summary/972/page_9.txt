The paper discusses the "log-likelihood trick" for calculating the gradient of a function J with respect to parameters #. The derivation shows that the gradient can be expressed as the expected value of a function G(z;#) that involves the log of the probability density function p(z;#). This allows for an unbiased estimate of the gradient by sampling from the distribution defined by p(z;#).

The algorithm presented, referred to as REINFORCE, is straightforward to implement and operates through stochastic gradient descent. It is characterized by the following steps: initializing parameters, sampling from the distribution, and updating parameters based on the sampled values. The algorithm's simplicity makes it applicable to a wide range of problems, provided efficient sampling from p(z;#) is possible.

However, the REINFORCE algorithm has notable drawbacks. It relies on stochastic gradients, which can lead to high variance and necessitate many samples to converge to a stationary point. Additionally, since it uses function evaluations rather than actual gradients, it is less effective compared to methods that compute gradients directly, especially in scenarios with noisy evaluations.

The paper highlights two primary approaches in reinforcement learning that utilize this stochastic search framework: Policy Gradient methods and Pure Random Search. Despite the challenges associated with high variance and inefficiency, the ease of implementation remains a significant advantage of direct search methods like REINFORCE.
