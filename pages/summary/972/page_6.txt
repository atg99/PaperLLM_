The paper discusses the challenges of solving optimal control problems within the context of reinforcement learning (RL) and introduces approximate dynamic programming as a potential solution. The primary objective is to maximize the expected cumulative reward over time while adhering to specific state-transition constraints. However, the author notes that if the model used to derive control policies is incorrect, it may lead to suboptimal results, particularly if the noise process is not well-defined.

The paper further elaborates on the principle of optimality central to dynamic programming, which asserts that an optimal policy for a given time horizon can be recursively derived from any starting point. The Q-function is defined as a key component in this dynamic programming approach, representing the maximum expected cumulative reward for a given state and action. The optimal value of the control problem can be determined by maximizing the Q-function over possible actions.

The recursive definition of the Q-function is established, starting from a terminal condition and progressing backwards in time, encapsulated in Bellman's equation. The author emphasizes that the optimal policy can be derived from the Q-function at each state, thus allowing for efficient computation of control actions.

Approximate dynamic programming methods aim to estimate these Q-values from empirical data, often under the assumption of stationarity, which simplifies the problem when considering an infinite time horizon. The paper concludes with a formulation of the optimization problem in this limit, highlighting the potential for approximate dynamic programming to effectively address the challenges in optimal control within RL frameworks.
