The paper discusses the application of Receding Horizon Control (RHC), also known as Model Predictive Control (MPC), in humanoid simulations, specifically within the MuJoCo framework. It highlights the ability of RHC to plan actions based on short-term simulations and adapt to real-time feedback, which allows for corrections in case of model mismatches and unexpected errors. The authors emphasize the trade-off between the accuracy of the state-transition model and the Q-function in terms of learning efficiency and computational speed. 

Figure 4 illustrates the performance of the humanoid model, showing the variance in learning outcomes from random searches and demonstrating that while high rewards can be achieved, poor control performance is often observed. The right side of the figure showcases the capability of MPC to execute complex actions, such as climbing into a vehicle, even with a suboptimal model. 

The paper also references prior work where RHC was effectively utilized for robotic locomotion tasks, highlighting its robustness despite model inaccuracies. The authors note that the RHC approach has been able to generate real-time control solutions, achieving significant advancements in humanoid control performance over time.
