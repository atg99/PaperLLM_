The paper discusses various approaches to reinforcement learning (RL), particularly focusing on random search methods and their historical context. The random search algorithm, initially proposed by Rastrigin for reinforcement learning problems, is noted for its simplicity compared to policy gradient algorithms. Despite its simplicity, random search leverages less problem structure, making its effectiveness unclear in nonconvex RL scenarios. The paper highlights that multiple independent communities have developed similar random search strategies, suggesting their potential efficacy.

The section on deep reinforcement learning emphasizes that the primary distinction in these methods lies in the use of neural networks for function approximation. While the core algorithmic concepts remain unchanged, the convergence properties may differ, as discussed in Bertsekas and Tsitisklis' work on Neuro-dynamic Programming.

To evaluate the various RL approaches, the paper proposes focusing on the Linear Quadratic Regulator (LQR) as a simple yet non-trivial optimal control problem. The LQR framework, characterized by convex quadratic rewards and linear dynamics, serves as a foundational instance to assess the performance of different RL methods. The author argues that if complex methods struggle with simple problems like LQR, it indicates inherent flaws in those methods.
