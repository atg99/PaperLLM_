The paper discusses the challenges and methodologies involved in optimizing control policies within stochastic dynamical systems. It highlights that the control actions must be selected based on observed states and previous inputs, with the reward at each time step determined by the state and control action. The concept of a trajectory is introduced, defined as a sequence of states and control actions generated by the system.

The optimization problem is framed as maximizing the expected cumulative reward over time, subject to the dynamics defined by a function that incorporates the current state, control action, and disturbances. The paper emphasizes the importance of feedback in mitigating uncertainty, leading to the need for optimizing control policies rather than deterministic sequences of controls.

A key challenge arises when the state-transition dynamics are unknown, which is common in various complex systems. The paper posits that without a precise model of the dynamics, standard optimization methods cannot be employed effectively. Instead, it advocates for a reinforcement learning (RL) approach where a policy is defined and tested over a specified horizon length, allowing for the collection of trajectories and rewards. The ultimate goal is to derive a policy that maximizes the expected reward while minimizing the number of queries made to an oracle, which provides feedback in the form of states and rewards from the executed policy.

This approach to episodic reinforcement learning is presented as more intricate than traditional optimization oracle models, given the complexity of feedback signals received after each episode. The paper sets the stage for exploring effective learning strategies in environments where the dynamics and rewards may not be fully understood, stressing the importance of leveraging previously observed data to inform new policy decisions.
