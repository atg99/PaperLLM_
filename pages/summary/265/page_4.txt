The paper discusses the performance of Capsule Networks (CapsNets) in terms of routing procedures and their impact on model robustness, particularly focusing on affine transformations. It compares models with dynamic routing to those without it, revealing that both models show increased agreement on the target class with training time. The study highlights that different routing algorithms primarily differ in how they approximate coupling coefficients, CCC*. It is noted that CapsNets can achieve similar results by learning a transformation matrix without relying on explicit coupling coefficients, suggesting that these coefficients can be learned implicitly.

The paper introduces a no-routing approach, where coupling coefficients are set as a constant, and demonstrates through experiments that the agreement on the target class improves while the agreement on non-ground-truth classes decreases in both routing scenarios. This finding leads to the conclusion that the robustness of CapsNets does not significantly depend on the routing procedure.

The second part of the paper addresses the limitations of CapsNets regarding affine robustness. It explains that CapsNets consist of primary capsules that can only capture specific areas of input images, which restricts their ability to generalize to novel affine transformations. The transformation matrices learn to map low-level capsules to high-level capsules based on the training data, but when presented with novel inputs, the transformations become meaningless. Consequently, the paper emphasizes the need for robust affine capsule networks to enhance the generalization capabilities of CapsNets under varying affine transformations.
