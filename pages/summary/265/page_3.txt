The paper revisits the dynamic routing mechanism in Capsule Networks (CapsNets), focusing on its theoretical and empirical aspects. It begins by analyzing the backpropagation through routing iterations, where it presents a mathematical formulation of the forward pass during the iterative routing process. The authors demonstrate that the gradients through the routing procedure can be approximated, allowing the coupling coefficients to be treated as constants in the gradient backpropagation process. This leads to a significant reduction in computational cost and memory usage by rolling the routing iterations rather than unrolling them.

The empirical validation is conducted by training a CapsNet on the MNIST dataset, comparing the gradients obtained from unrolled and rolled routing iterations. The results show that the gradients are similar in both cases, supporting the claim that the routing procedure primarily identifies coupling coefficients rather than contributing significantly to gradient computation.

Furthermore, the paper formulates the forward pass through routing iterations as a function that maps capsule predictions to coupling coefficients, emphasizing the goal of maximizing agreement on the target class while minimizing agreement on non-target classes. Overall, the analysis suggests that the intrinsic role of the routing procedure is to optimize these coupling coefficients, thus highlighting its essential function in CapsNets.
