The paper presents a comparative analysis of various models, including CNNs and CapsNets, with a focus on their performance on the AffNIST and MNIST datasets. The results indicate that while traditional CNNs perform well, CapsNets, particularly those without dynamic routing, also achieve competitive results.

The discussion highlights the structural differences between regular CNNs, Aff-CapsNet, and CapsNets. CapsNets utilize capsules that connect to all elements of input capsules, potentially allowing for better feature extraction for tasks like affine-transformed image classification. The paper contrasts capsule layers with fully connected layers, emphasizing that capsule layers may introduce a new inductive bias in combining activations.

The authors suggest that CapsNets represent an architectural evolution parallel to CNNs and propose further exploration of CapsNet components rather than merely adopting CNN techniques. Their findings indicate that dynamic routing does not significantly enhance the generalization or robustness of CapsNets, raising questions about its necessity in certain tasks.

The paper concludes by proposing Aff-CapsNets, which demonstrate improved affine robustness with fewer parameters. It acknowledges the potential for CapsNets to be applied to various computer vision tasks and suggests that further investigation into their properties, including adversarial robustness and viewpoint invariance, will be addressed in future research.
