The paper discusses the limitations of Capsule Networks (CapsNets), particularly regarding the transformation matrices used for high-level capsules. Specifically, it highlights that the transformation matrix for primary capsules can fail when inputs undergo transformations such as rotation. To address this issue, the authors propose Robust Affine Capsule Networks (Aff-CapsNets), which utilize a shared transformation matrix across all primary capsules. This approach allows for effective transformations from local to global coordinate systems, enhancing the robustness of CapsNets to affine transformations.

The experiments conducted in the study consist of two parts: first, evaluating CapsNets with various routing mechanisms on standard datasets; second, demonstrating that Aff-CapsNets outperform traditional CapsNets, achieving state-of-the-art results. The authors analyze the effectiveness of different routing mechanisms, including standard dynamic routing, a rolled routing procedure, one-step routing with trainable coefficients, and a model without any routing.

Results indicate that while the performance variance among models is relatively small, those trained with no routing or trainable routing show slightly better results due to their ability to avoid issues related to coupling coefficient polarization. Overall, the findings suggest that the proposed Aff-CapsNets improve upon existing CapsNet architectures by providing a more robust solution to affine transformations.
