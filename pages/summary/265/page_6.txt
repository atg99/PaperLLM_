This paper investigates the performance of Capsule Networks (CapsNets) using different routing procedures across various datasets: MNIST, FMNIST, SVHN, and CIFAR10. The results indicate that the routing procedures (Dynamic-R, Rolled-R, Trainable-R, and No-R) do not significantly enhance the classification performance of CapsNets. The study concludes that the routing mechanism does not contribute to the generalization ability of CapsNets.

In the analysis of learned representations, the paper highlights that CapsNets reconstruct original inputs from the activity vector of high-level capsules, utilizing reconstruction as a regularization technique. It is shown that CapsNets with No-R can reconstruct inputs with lower error compared to those with Dynamic-R, demonstrating better performance in handling variations such as stroke thickness and rotation.

The paper also discusses the parallel attention mechanism in CapsNets, which allows for the recognition of overlapping objects. Notably, CapsNets without routing (No-R) achieved competitive accuracy (95.49%) on the MultiMNIST dataset, suggesting that the parallel attention mechanism can be learned even in the absence of a routing procedure.

Lastly, the robustness of CapsNets to affine transformations is examined, confirming that removing the routing procedure does not diminish their robustness. Overall, the findings suggest that while routing procedures may not enhance performance, CapsNets retain their beneficial properties without them.
