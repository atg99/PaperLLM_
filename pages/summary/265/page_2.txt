This paper presents several contributions focused on improving Capsule Networks (CapsNets). The authors revisit the dynamic routing procedure of CapsNets, identify limitations in the current architecture, and introduce a new model called affine Capsule Networks (Aff-CapsNet). They conduct extensive experiments to explore the properties of CapsNets trained without routing and demonstrate that Aff-CapsNet outperforms existing models.

The structure of the paper includes a review of CapsNets and related works in Section 2, followed by an investigation of the routing procedure's effectiveness in Section 3. Section 4 highlights the limitations of current CapsNets regarding affine transformations and details the proposed Aff-CapsNet, supported by experimental validation. The final sections offer discussions and conclusions.

Capsule Networks represent entities with capsules, using activity vectors to encode properties, where the length indicates the confidence of existence. The CapsNet architecture involves mapping raw input features to low-level capsules, which are then routed to high-level capsules. The routing process relies on coupling coefficients that are updated iteratively based on agreement measures.

The paper also reviews various routing algorithms and their improvements, noting that the routing procedure is computationally expensive. Some works have proposed methods to reduce this complexity or have eliminated the routing procedure altogether, yielding competitive performance. However, the impact of these changes on the robustness of CapsNets to affine transformations remains underexplored, which this paper aims to address.
