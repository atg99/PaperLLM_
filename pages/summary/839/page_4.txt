In the quantized matrix multiplication and convolution section, the paper describes how hidden activations and weight tensors are quantized to low-precision integer values, utilizing a formula to compute the output while maintaining precision through certain scaling factors. Notably, the use of dyadic numbers allows for efficient scaling without resorting to floating-point arithmetic, thus avoiding the higher latency associated with division operations.

The paper also addresses batch normalization (BN), emphasizing its significance in neural networks, particularly in computer vision. It critiques existing methods that maintain BN parameters in FP32 to preserve accuracy, which is incompatible with integer-only hardware. Instead, HAWQ-V3 proposes a method of fusing BN parameters with convolution operations and quantizing them using an integer-only approach, leading to improved accuracy.

Additionally, the paper discusses the challenges associated with quantizing residual connections. It highlights that prior methods that perform operations in FP32 can lead to signal loss due to the non-linear nature of quantization. HAWQ-V3 advocates for a method that quantizes residual connections effectively, ensuring compatibility with integer-only hardware while preserving accuracy.

Overall, HAWQ-V3 presents a comprehensive strategy for quantization in neural networks, emphasizing efficiency and accuracy through the innovative use of dyadic numbers and optimized methods for integrating batch normalization and residual connections.
