The paper presents HAWQ-V3, a method for dyadic neural network quantization that transitions from INT32 to INT4 precision. The approach involves quantizing inputs to INT4 before convolution layers, while maintaining the residual connections in INT32 to avoid significant accuracy loss. The authors emphasize the importance of dyadic arithmetic for residual addition, facilitating integer-only arithmetic operations. 

HAWQ-V3 also addresses the challenges of uniformly quantizing all layers to low precision, which can degrade accuracy. Instead, it selectively applies higher precision to sensitive layers while quantizing insensitive layers to lower precision. This mixed precision strategy considers hardware-specific metrics, such as latency, to optimize the bit-precision configuration. The paper highlights that not all layers benefit from low-precision quantization; some may not improve in latency and could negatively impact accuracy.

To formalize the optimal bit-precision selection, the authors propose using Integer Linear Programming (ILP) to navigate the vast search space of configurations. The goal is to balance trade-offs between model perturbation and various user-defined constraints, including model size and latency. Overall, HAWQ-V3 aims to enhance the efficiency of neural networks while preserving performance through intelligent quantization strategies.
