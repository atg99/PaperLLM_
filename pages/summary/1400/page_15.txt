The paper discusses various proposal generation methods for object detection, focusing on keypoints-based approaches and other innovative algorithms. 

Keypoints-based methods are categorized into corner-based and center-based techniques. Corner-based methods, such as Denet, utilize a probabilistic approach to predict bounding boxes by analyzing corners of objects on the feature map. This method eliminates the need for anchors, leading to high-quality proposals. Building on this, CornerNet employs novel feature embedding and corner pooling to effectively match keypoints, achieving state-of-the-art results. In contrast, center-based methods predict the center of objects directly from the feature map without anchor priors. The FSAF framework integrates online feature selection with multi-level center-based branches, dynamically assigning objects to suitable feature levels during training. Additionally, Zhou et al. applied center-based methods in advanced areas like 3D detection, while CenterNet merges corner and center-based techniques to enhance performance.

The paper also highlights other proposal generation algorithms not reliant on keypoints or anchors. AZnet, for instance, focuses on regions of interest by adaptively directing computational resources to promising sub-regions, improving detection of sparse and small objects compared to traditional anchor-based methods.

Finally, the paper emphasizes the importance of Feature Representation Learning in developing robust and discriminative feature embeddings, crucial for effective object detection in complex environments.
