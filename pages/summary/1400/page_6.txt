One-stage detectors, in contrast, streamline the detection process by eliminating the proposal generation phase. They evaluate all possible locations in the image to classify each region as either background or an object. This approach allows for faster processing speeds, making one-stage detectors more suitable for real-time applications, despite generally yielding lower accuracy than their two-stage counterparts.

In summary, the paper discusses the evolution of object detection methods, highlighting the distinctions between traditional bounding box detection and instance segmentation. It introduces the two main paradigms of object detection—two-stage and one-stage detectors—detailing the workings of two-stage methods such as R-CNN, which improved detection performance significantly through the use of deep learning and transfer learning techniques. While two-stage detectors excel in accuracy, one-stage detectors offer advantages in speed, catering to different application needs in the field of computer vision.
