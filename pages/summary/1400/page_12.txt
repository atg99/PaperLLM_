The evolution of deep learning architectures has led to various models that enhance training efficiency and performance. Residual networks (ResNet) introduced shortcut connections that facilitate gradient flow, enabling deeper networks to be trained effectively. The introduction of ResNet-v2 improved performance further by optimizing the order of Batch Normalization. DenseNet addressed the limitation of ResNet by preserving features from shallow layers through concatenation, enhancing information flow. However, concerns about computational costs led to the development of Dual Path Networks (DPN), which combined dense connections with element-wise operations. 

ResNeXt took a different approach by utilizing group convolutions to reduce computation while maintaining accuracy, and MobileNet was designed specifically for mobile applications, significantly lowering computational demands. 

In contrast, GoogleNet utilized inception modules to capture multi-scale features, and subsequent models improved upon this architecture by integrating residual blocks. While these architectures excelled in image classification, their application in object detection was less effective due to differing requirements for feature map resolutions and representations. To address these challenges, DetNet was proposed, which maintained high-resolution features critical for accurate detection while employing dilated convolutions to enhance receptive fields. 

Overall, these advancements reflect a continuous effort to optimize neural network architectures for various tasks while balancing computational efficiency and model performance.
