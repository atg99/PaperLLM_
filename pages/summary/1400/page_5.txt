3.2. Detection Paradigms
Object detection algorithms can be categorized into two primary paradigms: two-stage detectors and one-stage detectors. Two-stage detectors first generate region proposals and then classify these proposals, offering high accuracy but often at the cost of speed. One-stage detectors, on the other hand, streamline the process by predicting class labels and bounding boxes simultaneously, resulting in faster inference times. Each paradigm employs various deep learning backbone architectures, proposal generation techniques, and feature representation methods tailored to enhance detection performance.

In summary, the paper outlines a structured approach to object detection, emphasizing the dual tasks of recognition and localization, and introduces the foundational settings and paradigms that define the current landscape of deep learning-based detection methods. The importance of loss functions, evaluation metrics like IoU and mAP, and the balance between accuracy and inference speed are also highlighted as critical components in assessing the effectiveness of these algorithms.
