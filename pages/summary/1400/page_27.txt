The paper discusses advancements in face and pedestrian detection technologies, highlighting various methodologies and frameworks that enhance detection accuracy and robustness. The Level Feature Pyramid Network integrates high-level semantic and low-level facial features, enabling PyramidBox to predict faces across multiple scales in a single shot. Context-sensitive structures and data-anchor-sampling techniques are employed to augment training samples, particularly aiding in the detection of smaller faces.

Yu et al. introduced a context pyramid maxout mechanism for exploring image contexts and a cascade framework that optimizes anchor-based detectors. Zhang et al. proposed a two-stream contextual CNN that captures body part information and filters easy non-face regions for deeper analysis.

Wang et al. focused on loss function design by replacing the softmax loss in the Faster R-CNN framework with a center loss, reducing intra-class variance in face detection. Their enhancements to the framework, including online hard negative mining and multi-scale training/testing, led to significant improvements. The Face R-FCN framework further distinguished contributions from different facial parts and introduced position-sensitive average pooling, achieving state-of-the-art results in public benchmarks.

In pedestrian detection, the paper outlines its unique challenges, such as fixed aspect ratios and common issues like occlusion and crowding. Prior to deep learning, pedestrian detection relied on frameworks like Viola-Jones and features like histograms of oriented gradients (HOG). However, deep learning methods have since outperformed traditional approaches. Angelova et al.'s real-time framework employs a cascade of deep networks for efficient classification, while Zhang et al. utilize multiscale feature maps with boosted decision trees for improved detection performance. Overall, deep learning techniques have significantly advanced the state of pedestrian detection in various applications.
