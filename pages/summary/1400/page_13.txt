The paper discusses various architectures and methods used in object detection, highlighting their unique features and contributions. DetNet is introduced as an architecture that utilizes multi-scale feature maps, enhancing the richness of the information processed. It is pre-trained on large-scale classification datasets, with its structure specifically tailored for detection tasks.

The Hourglass Network, initially designed for human pose recognition, is characterized by its fully convolutional structure and hourglass modules. These modules effectively downsample and upsample images while employing skip connections to retain information, making the network adept at capturing both local and global information, thus proving beneficial for object detection. The Hourglass Network has become a prominent choice in advanced detection frameworks.

The paper also delves into the critical role of proposal generation in object detection frameworks. Proposals, which are rectangular bounding boxes that potentially contain objects, are generated for subsequent classification and localization refinement. The methods for proposal generation are classified into four categories: traditional computer vision methods, anchor-based supervised learning methods, keypoint-based methods, and others. A notable distinction is made between one-stage and two-stage detectors: the former evaluates each image region as a potential proposal, while the latter generates a sparse set of proposals focusing exclusively on foreground or background information.

Traditional computer vision methods for proposal generation are based on low-level visual cues, categorized into three main principles: computing an objectness score for candidate boxes, merging superpixels from segmentation results, and generating multiple foreground and background segments. Objectness score methods, such as those proposed by Arbelaez et al. and Rahtu et al., assess the likelihood of candidate boxes containing objects. Superpixel merging techniques, exemplified by Selective Search, leverage hierarchical segments based on visual factors to generate proposals. Seed segmentation approaches, like CPMC, use multiple seed regions to create overlapping segments for proposal generation but are noted for their time-consuming nature.

In summary, these traditional methods are praised for their simplicity and high recall in generating proposals, especially in medium-scale datasets, despite challenges in scalability and efficiency in larger datasets.
