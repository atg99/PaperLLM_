The paper provides a comprehensive survey of recent advancements in deep learning techniques for object detection tasks, structured into three major categories: object detector components, machine learning strategies, and real-world applications including benchmark evaluations. 

Key areas for future exploration include:

1. **Emerging Benchmarks**: The current MSCOCO benchmark, with only 80 categories, limits understanding of complex real-world scenes. The new LVIS benchmark offers a richer dataset with over 1000 categories and simulates low-shot scenarios, presenting opportunities for more challenging detection and segmentation tasks.

2. **Low-shot Object Detection**: This area addresses the need for detecting objects with limited labeled data, a challenge for deep learning models that typically require large datasets. Various methods, such as Multi-modal Self-Paced Learning for Detection and Low-Shot Transfer Detector, have been proposed but still face issues like overfitting and a lack of extensive research compared to classification tasks.

3. **Backbone Architecture**: Current practices often utilize classification models for detection tasks, leading to conflicts between the two. Developing detection-aware backbone architectures remains an important research direction.

4. **Other Research Issues**: Topics such as large batch learning and incremental learning are highlighted as open research areas. The impact of batch size on detection tasks and the problem of catastrophic forgetting in incremental learning require further investigation.

The survey aims to provide readers with an in-depth understanding of object detection in deep learning, encouraging further research and development in this critical field.
