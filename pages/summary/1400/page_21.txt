The paper discusses strategies for addressing difficulty imbalance in object detection by selecting negative proposals with higher classification loss for training. It highlights the importance of specialized loss functions, particularly in multi-class classification scenarios, where a classifier is trained over multiple categories including background. The standard loss function is defined by the negative log of the predicted probability for the ground truth class.

The concept of focal loss, introduced by Lin et al., aims to suppress signals from easy samples by assigning importance weights based on loss values, allowing the model to focus on harder samples. This approach is further extended by Li et al. with a gradient harmonizing mechanism that not only suppresses easy proposals but also mitigates the effects of outliers. Shrivastava et al. propose an online hard example mining strategy that emphasizes the significance of difficult samples over class imbalance.

The paper also addresses localization refinement, which is crucial for accurate object detection. It notes that precise localization can be challenging, especially when predictions do not encompass the entire object. Various methods, such as the R-CNN framework's use of L-2 auxiliary bounding box regressors and Fast R-CNN's smooth L1 regressors, are discussed for refining localization predictions. Advanced techniques, including an iterative bounding box regression method and LocNet, are mentioned for further enhancement of localization accuracy, although they require separate components in the detection pipeline.

Overall, the paper presents a comprehensive overview of the challenges and solutions in improving object detection through better handling of classification loss and localization refinement.
