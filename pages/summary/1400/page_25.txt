In this paper, the authors review various methods for accelerating object detectors, highlighting the differences between two-stage and one-stage detectors. Two-stage detectors, while more accurate, are inherently slower due to the separate proposal generation and region classification phases. Notable advancements include R-FCN, which utilized spatially-sensitive feature maps to optimize computation but faced challenges with increased channel numbers as categories grew. Li et al. introduced Light Head R-CNN, which reduced channel counts significantly, mitigating computational costs without sharing computations across regions.

The paper also discusses the importance of the backbone architecture in detection speed, suggesting the replacement of traditional backbones with more efficient models like MobileNet, which employs depth-wise convolution layers. Additionally, innovations such as PV ANet utilized CReLu layers to enhance inference speed. Offline optimization techniques, including model compression and quantization, further contribute to acceleration, while NVIDIA's TensorRT toolkit enhances model deployment efficiency.

The authors examine various testing strategies to improve detection accuracy, such as image pyramids that utilize multiple scales to enhance performance on diverse object sizes. However, these strategies can increase computational costs, making them less viable for real-world applications.

The paper concludes with an overview of real-world applications of object detection, particularly face detection. It highlights the unique challenges of face detection, such as greater scale variability and common issues like occlusion and blurriness. The authors argue that generic detection algorithms are often inadequate for face detection due to these specific challenges. The discussion suggests that tailored algorithms that leverage the structural characteristics of faces can lead to improved detection outcomes.
