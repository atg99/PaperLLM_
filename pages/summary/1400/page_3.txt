The paper discusses the evolution of object detection research utilizing deep convolutional neural networks (DCNNs) since 2012, highlighting significant models and their contributions. It begins with the introduction of AlexNet, which demonstrated the potential of DCNNs on the ImageNet dataset, overcoming previous limitations such as the need for large-scale annotated data and computational resources. The success of AlexNet led to a surge in the development of various architectures, including OverFeat, GoogleNet, VGGNet, and R-CNN, among others.

The paper categorizes current deep learning-based object detection frameworks into two main types: two-stage detectors and one-stage detectors. Two-stage detectors, like R-CNN and its variants, generate region proposals and classify them, typically achieving higher accuracy. In contrast, one-stage detectors, such as YOLO and SSD, offer faster performance by predicting object categories directly from feature maps, making them suitable for real-time applications.

The trend in recent research has shifted towards anchor-free and AutoML techniques, suggesting a promising direction for future advancements in object detection. The paper emphasizes that deep learning's hierarchical feature representation capability surpasses traditional hand-crafted descriptors, allowing for improved performance in complex visual contexts. Overall, the paper underscores the transformative impact of DCNNs on object detection methodologies and their ongoing evolution.
