The paper discusses various datasets and evaluation metrics used in object detection, face detection, and pedestrian detection tasks. It highlights key datasets such as Pascal VOC, MSCOCO, Open Images, LVIS, WIDER FACE, FDDB, and pedestrian detection datasets like Caltech, ETH, INRIA, CityPersons, and KITTI. 

Key definitions are provided for important evaluation metrics such as Frame Per Second (FPS), Intersection over Union (IoU) threshold, True Positive (TP), False Positive (FP), Precision (P), Recall (R), Average Precision (AP), mean Average Precision (mAP), and True Positive Rate (TPR). These metrics are essential for assessing the performance of detection models across various datasets.

For face detection, the paper notes that the evaluation typically employs mAP at a 0.50 IoU threshold, with specific metrics for different difficulty levels in WIDER FACE. For FDDB, the True Positive Rate is calculated at a threshold of 1,000 false positives, with evaluations based on bounding box and eclipse levels.

In pedestrian detection benchmarks, datasets like CityPersons provide comprehensive annotations for persons in urban settings, while Caltech is recognized for its challenging nature, derived from video recordings. The paper emphasizes the importance of these datasets and metrics in advancing the field of object detection.
