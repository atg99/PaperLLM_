The paper discusses strategies to enhance detection accuracy in object detection algorithms, focusing on duplicate removal and model acceleration. 

In the duplicate removal section, the necessity of Non-Maximum Suppression (NMS) is highlighted as a method to eliminate duplicate false positive predictions that arise from dense predictions in one-stage detection algorithms, such as SSD and DSSD, as well as from bounding box regressors in two-stage algorithms. NMS works by selecting the prediction box with the highest confidence score and removing other boxes that overlap significantly with it, using a predefined Intersection over Union (IoU) threshold. However, this method can lead to missed predictions in cases of clustered objects. To address this, Soft-NMS was introduced, which diminishes the confidence score of overlapping predictions rather than discarding them outright. This approach has shown to improve performance on various benchmarks. Additionally, a novel network architecture has been proposed that optimizes NMS separately from detector training, aiming to reduce duplicate detections by penalizing them and refining the scoring of detections based on proximity.

In the model acceleration section, the importance of efficiency in real-world applications of object detection is emphasized. Despite the strong performance of current state-of-the-art algorithms, their inference speeds are often inadequate for practical use, necessitating further evaluation on efficiency metrics to ensure their applicability in real-time scenarios.
