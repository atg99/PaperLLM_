The paper discusses the distinct characteristics of capsule networks (CapsNets) compared to conventional convolutional neural networks (CNNs), focusing on their behavior akin to a mixture-of-experts (MoE) and their noise filtering mechanisms. CapsNets utilize capsules that specialize in specific regions of feature space, making multiple predictions based on their localized information, similar to MoE. However, unlike MoE, capsules operate in disjoint feature spaces, which can reduce overfitting by aggregating predictions from various capsules with different parameters, despite potentially lower discriminative power.

The noise filtering mechanism in CapsNets is highlighted, where initial gating values are dynamically adjusted, allowing the network to minimize the influence of predictions that deviate from a general consensus. This effectively filters out noisy contributions, which is a limitation in CNNs that lack a systematic approach to distinguish between noisy and reliable channels.

The authors propose a new routing method that emphasizes the MoE-like behavior of CapsNets while addressing the drawbacks of existing routing mechanisms. They note that the traditional routing-by-agreement method leads to high computational complexity and relies on assumptions about the distribution of prediction vectors, which may not hold in real-world applications. The proposed method introduces learnable routing networks for each capsule, allowing for effective ensemble averaging that stabilizes outputs without the need for agreement-based clustering. This approach maintains the benefits of noise reduction while simplifying the routing process.
