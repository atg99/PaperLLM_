The paper "Self-Routing Capsule Networks" by Taeyoung Hahn, Myeongjang Pyeon, and Gunhee Kim proposes a novel routing strategy for capsule networks, addressing key limitations of the conventional routing-by-agreement method. Capsule networks are recognized for their robustness to input perturbations compared to traditional convolutional neural networks (CNNs). They operate using capsules that specialize in different regions of feature space and rely on dynamic routing mechanisms to filter out noise in predictions.

The authors highlight two main weaknesses of the routing-by-agreement approach: high computational complexity and the assumption of clustering, which can fail under significant input noise. To overcome these issues, the authors introduce self-routing, where each capsule is routed independently through a subordinate routing network, eliminating the need for agreement between capsules. This approach allows for the collection of poses and activations in a manner akin to Mixture-of-Experts.

Experimental results on datasets such as CIFAR-10, SVHN, and SmallNORB demonstrate that self-routing enhances robustness against adversarial attacks and affine transformations while reducing computational demands. The research emphasizes the importance of equivariance in neural networks for better generalization and effective learning of spatial relations.
