The paper presents a comparison of error rates on the SmallNORB test set using a 7-layer CNN as the base architecture. It evaluates the performance of capsule-based models against CNNs, specifically focusing on familiar and novel viewpoints. The results indicate that capsule networks, particularly those employing self-routing, demonstrate superior generalization capabilities compared to CNN baselines, especially in novel azimuth and elevation scenarios. The study highlights a weak correlation between model size and generalization performance, suggesting that larger models do not significantly outperform smaller ones.

In additional experiments with a smaller network comprising one convolution layer followed by three capsule layers, the self-routing method outperformed other routing techniques, showcasing its effectiveness in learning representations with shallow feature extractors. The conclusion emphasizes the efficiency and competitive performance of the proposed non-iterative self-routing method in adversarial defense and viewpoint generalization tasks. The findings suggest that routing-by-agreement may not be essential for the robustness of capsule networks. Future research directions include exploring the integration of residual connections to enhance model performance.
