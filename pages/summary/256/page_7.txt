The paper presents an analysis of the success rates of untargeted and targeted Fast Gradient Sign Method (FGSM) attacks against various routing methods of Capsule Networks (CapsNets) and Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs). The experiments focus on the robustness of these models, specifically highlighting the performance of self-routing CapsNets (SR-CapsNets) compared to CNN baselines. 

Figure 3 illustrates that CNNs exhibit greater vulnerability to FGSM attacks than CapsNets, with SR-CapsNets demonstrating the highest robustness. Figure 4 further explores the impact of increasing the number of capsules per layer, revealing that this addition enhances the robustness of SR-CapsNets, particularly against targeted attacks. However, it is noted that stacking more capsule layers only benefits performance against untargeted attacks. In contrast, other routing algorithms do not show a consistent pattern; performance often deteriorates with more capsules, indicating challenges in clustering predictions amidst noisy information.

Additionally, the paper discusses the robustness of CapsNets against affine transformations, showcasing their ability to generalize to novel viewpoints. This is evaluated using the SmallNORB dataset, where models are trained on specific azimuth angles and tested on others, further reinforcing the advantages of CapsNets in handling variations in input perspectives. 

Overall, the findings underscore the superior robustness of CapsNets, particularly SR-CapsNets, against adversarial attacks and their capability to generalize across different viewpoints.
