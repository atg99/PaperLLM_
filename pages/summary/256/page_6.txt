This paper presents a comparative analysis of routing algorithms in Capsule Networks (CapsNets) and Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) using ResNet-20 as the base architecture. The study evaluates parameter counts, floating point operations (FLOPs), and error rates on the CIFAR-10 and SVHN datasets. Different routing algorithms, including dynamic routing (DR), expectation-maximization (EM), and self-routing (SR), are assessed, with varying configurations based on the depth and width of capsule layers.

The results indicate that self-routing and dynamic routing achieve similar classification accuracies to CNN baselines, while EM routing shows degraded performance. Self-routing is noted for its lower computational overhead in FLOPs, as it does not require iterative computations, although EM routing is more efficient in terms of parameter size. However, training challenges arise with stacked EM layers on more complex datasets due to the constraints of weight matrix multiplications.

Furthermore, the paper discusses the robustness of CapsNets against adversarial examples, highlighting their resilience without requiring defensive modifications. The targeted and untargeted white-box Fast Gradient Sign Method (FGSM) is utilized for generating adversarial inputs, demonstrating the model's capability to withstand such attacks.

Overall, while CapsNet variants, particularly those utilizing self-routing, show advantages over CNN baselines, the performance differences are modest. The study suggests that the benefit of using ensemble submodules is limited for tasks with less pronounced specialization requirements.
