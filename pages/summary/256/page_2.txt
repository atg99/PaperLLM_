this work addresses the limitations of capsule networks (CapsNets) associated with the traditional routing-by-agreement mechanism. The authors propose a novel routing strategy termed self-routing, which eliminates the reliance on agreement among prediction vectors. Instead, the contribution of lower-level capsules to higher-level capsules is determined by their activation and the routing decisions made by a subordinate routing network. This approach draws parallels to the Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) framework, where units specialize in different input regions and contribute variably based on the input.

The authors argue that the robustness of CapsNets can be maintained without the traditional routing method, which has been shown to struggle in noisy environments. They evaluate the self-routing method against established routing-by-agreement techniques, specifically dynamic routing and EM routing, using datasets such as CIFAR-10, SVHN, and SmallNORB. The results indicate that self-routing not only improves classification accuracy but also enhances robustness against adversarial attacks and changes in viewpoint, all while reducing computational costs.

The paper also situates its contributions within the broader context of CapsNets and MoE, highlighting previous work on routing mechanisms and the fragility of CNNs. The findings suggest that self-routing is particularly beneficial as model sizes increase, contrasting with previous methods that may degrade under similar conditions. Overall, this work presents a significant advancement in the development of CapsNets, proposing a simpler yet more effective routing strategy.
