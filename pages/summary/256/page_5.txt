In SR-CapsNet, capsules autonomously determine their routing coefficients without needing to reach an agreement with other capsules, enhancing their modeling capabilities through a subordinate routing network. This routing network is designed as single-layer perceptrons, which can be extended to multi-layer perceptrons if desired. The routing coefficients function as predicted activations for the output capsules, meaning that an upper-level capsule is more likely to be activated when multiple lower-level capsules exhibit high routing coefficients towards it.

The self-routing mechanism employs two learnable weight matrices, Wroute and Wpose, to compute the routing coefficients and predictions of the capsules. The routing coefficients are computed using a softmax function applied to the pose vectors, allowing the upper-layer capsule's activation to be a summation of the weighted votes from lower-level capsules. The pose of the upper-layer capsule is derived from the weighted average of the prediction vectors.

The experiments conducted focus on comparing the self-routing approach with traditional agreement-based routing algorithms, examining image classification performance and robustness against unseen input perturbations, including adversarial examples and affine transformations. The datasets utilized include CIFAR-10, SVHN, and SmallNORB, with specific augmentation techniques applied during training. For a fair comparison, all routing algorithms utilized a consistent base CNN architecture, specifically ResNet-20, chosen for its effectiveness in various applications. The architecture was adapted to integrate capsule layers, replacing the final layers of ResNet-20 with a primary capsule and a fully-connected capsule layer. 

Additionally, performance variations were assessed based on the depth and width of the capsule layers, indicating a focus on optimizing the routing mechanism within the framework of SR-CapsNet.
