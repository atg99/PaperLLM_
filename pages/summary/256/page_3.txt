The paper discusses the challenges faced by convolutional neural networks (CNNs) in terms of fragility to adversarial attacks and explores capsule networks (CapsNets) as a potential solution. It highlights that traditional data augmentation techniques may not sufficiently enhance robustness against these attacks. The authors evaluate the effectiveness of CapsNets, particularly focusing on the impact of augmenting a single routing layer structured by capsules to improve robustness against adversarial perturbations and affine transformations.

The paper begins by reviewing the fundamentals of capsule networks and the two main routing algorithms: dynamic routing and EM routing. A capsule network consists of layers of capsules, each characterized by a pose vector and an activation scalar. The routing process involves adjusting routing coefficients to ensure that predictions from lower-level capsules effectively influence upper-level capsules based on the agreement of their pose vectors.

In dynamic routing, the agreement is measured using cosine similarity, while in EM routing, a probabilistic model is used to optimize the routing coefficients through an iterative process. The authors propose a self-routing approach designed to leverage the strengths of CapsNets while mitigating potential drawbacks, aiming for improved robustness in the face of adversarial attacks.
