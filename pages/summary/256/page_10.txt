This collection of references highlights significant advancements in neural network architectures and their robustness against adversarial attacks. Notably, Sabour, Frosst, and Hinton introduce dynamic routing between capsules, which enhances the capability of neural networks to understand spatial hierarchies. Saito and Roy investigate the influence of loss functions on adversarial robustness, emphasizing the importance of target representations in their effectiveness.

Shazeer et al. present the sparsely-gated mixture-of-experts layer, which allows for the development of outrageously large neural networks, optimizing computational efficiency. Su et al. demonstrate a one pixel attack that can deceive deep neural networks, showcasing vulnerabilities in current models. Szegedy et al. explore intriguing properties of neural networks, revealing unexpected behaviors that arise from their complexity.

Veit, Wilber, and Belongie analyze residual networks, suggesting they function similarly to ensembles of shallower networks, which may contribute to their performance. Verma and Zhang propose graph capsule convolutional neural networks, merging capsule networks with graph-based approaches for improved feature extraction. Lastly, Wang and Liu provide an optimization perspective on dynamic routing between capsules, further refining its application. Wang et al. focus on unsupervised feature learning for reading digits in natural images, demonstrating the versatility of neural networks in various contexts.
