The study focuses on the assessment of pulmonary involvement in COVID-19 pneumonia using computed tomography (CT). Two expert radiologists evaluated CT images for various alterations, including septal thickening and the presence of nodules, and assigned a radiological severity visual score ranging from none to critical involvement based on ground-glass opacities (GGO) and consolidations. The total score was calculated on a scale from 0 to 16.

CT post-processing involved the use of an artificial intelligence tool for automatic segmentation and quantification of thoracic disease, specifically targeting COVID-19 pneumonia. This software categorized lung regions based on Hounsfield Units to assess emphysema, healthy lung parenchyma, GGO, and consolidation.

Clinical and laboratory data were also collected, including symptoms like fever and dyspnea, comorbidities such as hypertension and diabetes, and various laboratory results including inflammatory markers and organ function tests. The study aimed to provide a comprehensive analysis of the radiological and clinical characteristics associated with COVID-19 pneumonia.
