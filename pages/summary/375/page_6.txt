The study analyzes the demographics, comorbidities, symptoms, and laboratory findings of 98 patients upon admission. Key findings indicate that the median age of patients is significantly higher in those hospitalized in serious condition or deceased (78.5 years) compared to those discharged at home (56 years) and those hospitalized and stable (62 years), with a p-value of less than 0.001. 

Gender distribution shows no significant difference between groups. Symptoms such as fever, cough, respiratory failure, dyspnea, asthenia, and other symptoms were prevalent across groups without significant variation. Comorbidities like hypertension, diabetes, and neurological issues also did not show significant differences.

Laboratory findings reveal that patients in serious condition had lower oxygen saturation (SpO2) and higher levels of high-sensitivity C-reactive protein (HS-CRP), LDH, d-dimer, troponin, creatinine, and azotemia compared to those discharged or hospitalized in stable condition, with p-values indicating statistical significance. Additionally, ALT and AST levels were higher in those in serious condition compared to the stable group.

Overall, the data suggest that age and specific laboratory markers are critical in assessing the severity of the patient's condition at admission.
