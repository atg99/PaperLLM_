The study focuses on analyzing the clinical and radiological characteristics of 98 patients diagnosed with COVID-19. The median age of the patients was 61 years, with 39.8% being female. Key findings indicated that certain clinical parameters, including oxygen saturation (SpO2) and various laboratory values, significantly correlated with patient outcomes. Specifically, lower SpO2 levels were noted in critically ill patients, while higher values of HS CRP, leukocyte count, and others were associated with worse outcomes.

CT imaging revealed ground-glass opacities (GGO) as the predominant pattern, often in combination with consolidations. The bilateral and peripheral distribution of these findings was common, although no significant differences were found in CT patterns across outcome groups. However, a high radiological severity score was linked to poor patient outcomes.

Logistic regression analyses identified several significant predictors for patient outcomes: age over 61 years, SpO2 levels at or below 93%, and elevated ALT levels indicated higher risks. Similarly, CT parameters like emphysema volume, residual healthy lung parenchyma volume, and consolidation volume also emerged as important predictors.

The model combining clinical and CT findings demonstrated the highest accuracy in predicting patient outcomes, achieving a sensitivity of 88%, specificity of 78%, and overall accuracy of 81%. This comprehensive analysis underscores the utility of integrating clinical, laboratory, and radiological data to improve prognostic assessments in COVID-19 patients.
