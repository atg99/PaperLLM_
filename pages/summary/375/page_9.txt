This study investigates the CT imaging patterns and prognostic factors in patients with COVID-19 pneumonia. Key findings indicate that ground-glass opacities (GGO) and consolidations were the predominant CT patterns, observed in 87.8% of patients. The disease was predominantly bilateral and peripheral, with a notable presence of crazy paving patterns and reversed halo signs. 

The research highlights a correlation between a visual severity score of ≥ 8 and patient outcomes, although no significant differences were found among various severity groups. The study emphasizes the importance of quantifying lung disease extent, linking visual and software-based CT assessments to clinical severity and prognosis. Specifically, a lower proportion of residual healthy lung parenchyma and higher volumes of emphysema and consolidation were associated with worse outcomes.

Clinical parameters such as age, inflammatory markers, and blood counts were also significant predictors of patient prognosis. The study acknowledges its limitations, including its retrospective and monocentric nature, focusing on a high-risk cohort. In conclusion, both visual and software-based quantifications of lung abnormalities on chest CT are valuable independent predictors of outcomes in COVID-19 patients, with specific thresholds indicating varying prognostic implications.
