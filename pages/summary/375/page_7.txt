The paper presents a study on computed tomography (CT) findings in patients with varying clinical outcomes. It includes a comprehensive analysis of categorical and continuous variables related to the presence of ground-glass opacities (GGO), consolidation, and other radiological features.

Key findings indicate that while the presence of GGO and consolidation was common across all patient groups, significant differences were observed in the volume of residual healthy lung parenchyma and the percentage of consolidation. Patients discharged at home had a higher percentage of healthy lung parenchyma compared to those hospitalized in serious condition or who died.

Univariable analysis revealed several clinical and CT parameters that significantly predicted patient outcomes. Factors such as age over 61 years, low oxygen saturation (SpO2 ≤ 93%), and elevated levels of inflammatory markers (e.g., high-sensitivity C-reactive protein, leukocyte count) were associated with poorer outcomes. The analysis also identified the percentage of residual healthy lung parenchyma and consolidation as critical predictors.

Overall, the study emphasizes the importance of radiological findings and baseline clinical parameters in predicting patient outcomes in a medical setting, particularly in the context of respiratory diseases.
