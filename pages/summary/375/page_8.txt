The paper discusses the role of chest X-rays and CT scans in diagnosing and managing COVID-19, emphasizing the unique radiological features of COVID-19 pneumonia, such as bilateral ground-glass opacities (GGO) and consolidation. It highlights the performance of radiologists in distinguishing COVID-19 from other viral infections.

A logistic multivariate analysis was conducted to evaluate the relationship between clinical and CT parameters and patient outcomes. Key findings include the significance of age over 61, low oxygen saturation (SpO2), elevated ALT levels, and the percentage of residual healthy lung parenchyma in predicting outcomes. The analysis showed that specific clinical and CT findings correlate strongly with patient prognosis.

Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) analysis results indicated the effectiveness of various models using clinical/laboratory data and CT findings in predicting patient outcomes. The combined model demonstrated improved accuracy in identifying patients at risk of hospitalization or death compared to models using clinical data or CT findings alone.

Overall, the study underscores the critical role of imaging in managing COVID-19 and identifies key parameters that can aid in predicting patient outcomes.
