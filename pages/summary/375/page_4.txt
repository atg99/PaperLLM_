The study focuses on the statistical analysis of medical imaging data, specifically related to lung conditions. Continuous data were summarized using median and range, while categorical data were expressed as counts and percentages. The Kruskal-Wallis test assessed differences among groups for continuous variables, and the Chi-square test with Yates's correction evaluated differences in percentages. The Spearman correlation coefficient was calculated to determine the relationship between volumes of residual healthy lung parenchyma, ground-glass opacities (GGO), and consolidations with a radiological severity visual score.

Outcomes were categorized into discharging at home, stable hospitalization, critical hospitalization, or death, utilizing the median value of continuous variables as thresholds. Both univariable and backward stepwise multivariable logistic regression analyses were employed to explore associations between potential predictors and outcomes, with factors showing p values under 0.1 in univariable analyses included in multivariable assessments.

Three models were developed for comparison: one using only clinical parameters, another with CT parameters, and a third combining both clinical and CT parameters. The R-squared values for each model were reported for performance comparison. Additionally, receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was conducted, with the area under the ROC curve (AUC) indicating the models' discriminatory power based on independent predictors. An example of lung volume calculation using an automatic segmentation tool on CT images was illustrated, showing segmentation of lung types based on specific Hounsfield Unit thresholds.
