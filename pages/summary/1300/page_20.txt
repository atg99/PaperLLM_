The paper investigates the use of unsupervised deep-learning algorithms, specifically autoencoders, for network intrusion detection systems (NIDS). These algorithms are advantageous for promptly detecting zero-day attacks and reducing the need for extensive labeling. The study focuses on optimizing model architecture, latent dimensions, model capacity, and depth to improve detection performance. Evaluations were conducted using a stacked autoencoder on three datasets: NSL-KDD, IoTID20, and N-BaIoT, revealing that model capacity and latent size significantly influence performance, with the best F1-score of 0.895 achieved on the NSL-KDD dataset. Notably, the depth of the model did not affect performance, and simple autoencoders performed exceptionally well on certain IoT datasets, suggesting potential for lightweight IDS solutions.

The results indicate that increasing model size and appropriately selecting latent size can enhance IDS performance without major model alterations. However, the impact of model depth remains inconclusive and warrants further investigation with larger configurations. Future research should explore diverse model designs and datasets, and develop methods to estimate optimal latent sizes efficiently.
