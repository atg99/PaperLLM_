The paper discusses the performance of different models in detecting attacks on various IoT devices using the N-BaIoT dataset. The analysis focuses on the reconstruction distance distribution for normal and attack data, illustrated through density plots and histograms. The best case scenario was identified with a latent size of 28, achieving a Matthews Correlation Coefficient (MCC) of 0.593, while a near-best case with a latent size of 9 had an MCC of 0.59.

Experiments were conducted on multiple IoT devices, including the Danmini Doorbell and Philips Baby Monitor, with results averaged over five runs for each device type. The findings indicate overall outstanding performance, with accuracies exceeding 99% and MCC values above 0.95. Notably, the Danmini Doorbell displayed the highest accuracy at 99.97% and an MCC of 0.996, indicating that attacks on this device are readily distinguishable by the neural anomaly detection algorithms used. The Philips Baby Monitor also performed well, achieving an MCC of 0.989, with no significant differences observed across various model configurations.

The paper presents performance trends as the latent size increases, revealing stable performance for the Danmini Doorbell, Philips Baby Monitor, and SimpleHome Security Camera. In contrast, the Provision PT-737E showed marked improvement in attack detection with increased latent sizes, particularly between sizes 1 and 4. The Ecobee Thermostat demonstrated significant improvements with specific model configurations, achieving the highest MCC of 0.959 with a latent size of 3 or 4, while the (7,64) model maintained stable performance regardless of latent size.
