The paper investigates the performance of different models in detecting various types of attacks using a machine learning-based network intrusion detection system (ML-NIDS). It analyzes the True Positive Rate (TPR) for specific attack types, noting that the (5,32) model performs well for Denial of Service (DoS) and Probe attacks, achieving TPRs of 0.892 and 0.904 at specific latent sizes. However, the model struggles with R2L and U2R attacks, reporting TPRs of only 0.335 and 0.646, respectively.

Similar trends are observed in the (5,64) and (7,64) models, with the (5,64) model reaching its peak Matthews Correlation Coefficient (MCC) at a small latent size of 3, particularly improving the TPR for R2L attacks. Despite this, R2L and U2R attacks still exhibit low TPRs. The (7,64) model also shows a high MCC at a latent size of 3 but fluctuates with increasing size, maintaining high detection rates for DoS and Probe attacks while struggling with R2L and U2R.

The study emphasizes the importance of selecting an appropriate latent size, as evidenced by the analysis of the reconstruction distance distribution for R2L attacks in the (5,64) model. It highlights that the choice of latent size significantly impacts the model's performance, with the best results obtained at a latent size of 3, where attack samples are effectively classified. In contrast, a latent size of 1 results in poor classification, with most attack data misidentified as normal. This reinforces the necessity of optimizing model parameters for effective anomaly detection in network security.
