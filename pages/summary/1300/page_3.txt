The paper identifies critical engineering issues in the application of autoencoders for anomaly-based network intrusion detection systems (NIDS). It emphasizes the need for optimal configurations regarding the architecture and hyperparameters of autoencoders to achieve superior detection performance. The authors point out that traditional methods often rely on manual tuning, which can be time-consuming and inefficient as data evolves.

Key model design decisions discussed include the appropriate number of hidden layers and neurons, the size of the latent layer for effective data representation, the setting of thresholds to distinguish normal from abnormal data without labeled datasets, and the selection of metrics to evaluate differences between inputs and outputs.

The study contributes by presenting these engineering challenges, investigating the effects of model and latent sizes on intrusion detection performance through experiments with benchmark datasets, and analyzing the results to draw conclusions.

The organization of the paper includes a review of related work, a description of the data and evaluation methods, presentation of experimental results, and a discussion followed by conclusions and future work suggestions. The related work section covers various deep learning and ensemble learning approaches for IDS, including specific architectures like recurrent neural networks and methods that leverage feature extraction through autoencoders.
