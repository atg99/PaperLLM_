The paper investigates various model structure configurations for intrusion detection systems (IDS) using the NSL-KDD dataset. Three configurations are evaluated: (5,32), (5,64), and (7,64), which vary in depth and size, with the number of neurons in each layer specified. The latent layer dimensions are varied to assess their impact on model performance, where the (5,64) model with a latent size of 3 achieved the highest Matthews correlation coefficient (MCC) of 0.712.

The results indicate that models with greater capacity, such as (5,64) and (7,64), generally outperform the (5,32) model in most evaluation metrics except for false positive rate (FPR). The (5,64) model, having twice the number of trainable parameters compared to (5,32), showcases the significance of model capacity on IDS performance. However, when comparing (5,64) and (7,64), the addition of hidden layers shows marginal differences in detection performance.

The best performance metrics for each configuration are summarized, with the highest F1-score of 0.895 recorded for the (5,32) model with a latent size of 4. The evaluation results highlight that the models tend to misclassify attacks as normal logs more frequently than the reverse. Overall, the findings underscore the importance of model structure and latent size in enhancing IDS effectiveness.
