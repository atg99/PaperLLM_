The paper discusses the selection and preprocessing of three labeled network traffic datasets used for evaluating intrusion detection systems (IDS): NSL-KDD, IoTID20, and N-BaIoT. 

The NSL-KDD dataset was derived from the KDD Cup 99 dataset by eliminating redundancy, making it a widely recognized benchmark for IDS performance. This dataset comprises four attack categories: DoS (denial of service), U2R (unauthorized access to local root privileges), R2L (unauthorized access from a remote machine), and Probe (surveillance and scanning). For validation, 10% of the training data was randomly sampled. The dataset statistics are summarized in a table, indicating the number of samples for benign and each attack category across training, validation, and test sets.

In the preprocessing stage, non-numerical data were converted into numerical formats suitable for input into the autoencoder. Categorical features such as Protocol, Service, and Flag were transformed into one-hot encoded vectors, while numerical features underwent normalization through min-max scaling after outlier removal. The final representation consisted of 114-dimensional vectors combining general features and one-hot encoded categorical features.

The IoTID20 dataset was collected from two IoT devices in a smart home network environment, consisting of 83 network features and three label features. The data was split into training, validation, and test sets in an 8:1:1 ratio, with details provided in a corresponding table.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of these datasets in evaluating the performance of IDS, particularly in the context of traditional and IoT environments.
