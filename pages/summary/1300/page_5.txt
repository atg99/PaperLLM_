This section of the paper discusses the use of autoencoder models for unsupervised anomaly detection in network intrusion systems (NIDS), specifically within Internet of Things (IoT) environments. Previous studies, such as those by Lopez-Martin et al. and Aygun et al., have employed various autoencoder architectures to classify traffic into different attack categories or detect unknown attacks, achieving notable F1-scores. For example, Lopez-Martin's model utilized conditional variational autoencoders for classifying five attack types, while Aygun's comparison of standard and denoising autoencoders yielded F1-scores of 89.51% and 89.28%, respectively.

Mirsky et al. introduced an ensemble approach called Kitsune, which integrates multiple autoencoders to analyze network packet features and determine anomalies based on reconstruction error. Recent applications of deep autoencoders for IoT botnet detection by Meidan et al. demonstrated a true positive rate of 100% with a false positive rate of 7%. Similarly, Shahid et al. designed a system using multiple sparse autoencoders tailored to specific device types, achieving true positive rates of 86.9% to 91.2% and low false positive rates.

Despite the promising results from various autoencoder implementations, previous research has not adequately addressed the impact of model architecture and hyperparameters on NIDS performance. This paper aims to evaluate how the size and latent dimensions of autoencoders affect their effectiveness in detecting intrusions, thereby filling a notable gap in the existing literature.
