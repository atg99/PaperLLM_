The paper details an experimental approach to assess how the design of deep-learning models, specifically autoencoders, impacts the performance of machine learning-based network intrusion detection systems (ML-NIDS). The study focuses on the structure of the autoencoder and the size of its latent dimension.

The process for building the optimal autoencoder model involves three main phases: preprocessing, training, model selection, and threshold selection. 

1. **Preprocessing**: The training dataset is filtered to exclude attack samples, ensuring that only normal samples are used for training. The data is then vectorized using min-max normalization to scale feature values based on the training set's minimum and maximum values. Additional preprocessing methods tailored to specific datasets are outlined in an earlier section.

2. **Training**: The autoencoder is trained to reconstruct inputs to closely match normal log patterns from the training set, utilizing L2 norm as the reconstruction loss. The expectation is that inputs from attack classes will differ significantly from their reconstructed outputs. The difference between the input and output is assessed against a threshold to classify the sample as either an attack or normal.

3. **Model Selection**: The performance of the model is evaluated using the Area under the Curve (AuC) score from the Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) curve during training. The highest AuC score and corresponding model weights are recorded to identify the optimal model.

4. **Threshold Selection**: The threshold for reconstruction distance is crucial as it separates normal from abnormal classes. The study evaluates various threshold values using a validation set to determine the most effective threshold, employing the Z-score of a standard normal distribution to ensure consistent classification performance across different datasets.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of model design and parameter selection in enhancing the effectiveness of autoencoder-based intrusion detection systems.
