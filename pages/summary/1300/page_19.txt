This section discusses the findings related to the model structure and performance of intrusion detection systems (IDS). The (5,64) model, featuring 5 hidden layers and 64 neurons in the first hidden layer, consistently exhibited the best performance across various benchmark datasets. This suggests that model capacity positively influences IDS performance, while model depth does not show a significant impact, as the (5,64) model outperformed the (7,64) model without a notable difference.

The analysis of latent size reveals that IDS performance improves with increasing latent dimensions up to a certain limit, beyond which performance declines. The optimal latent size varies by dataset and model configuration; for example, it was found to be 3 for NSL-KDD with the (5,64) model, 14 for IoTID20 with the (5,32) model, and 28 for N-BaIoT with the (5,64) model.

Additionally, the distribution of reconstruction errors was analyzed, highlighting that the threshold significantly affects IDS performance in unsupervised learning. Although previous works often used a Z-score of 1 as a threshold, the study found that optimal thresholds varied across datasets, with values around 3 for NSL-KDD and Philips datasets, while close to 0 for IoTID20.

The section also addresses potential threats to the validity of the experimental results, emphasizing the importance of carefully selecting model structures and latent dimensions for comparison, as well as the significance of the datasets used. To mitigate bias, experiments were conducted across three different types of datasets, with multiple iterations to ensure robust results.
