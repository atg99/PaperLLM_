This section of the paper presents findings on the performance of two models, (5,64) and (7,64), in classifying normal and attack data within the N-BaIoT dataset. Figures 13 and 14 illustrate the Matthews Correlation Coefficient (MCC) for each device using the respective models. Figure 15 compares the Z-score distributions of reconstruction distances for normal and attack data when employing the (5,64) model, highlighting both the best and worst performing scenarios.

In the best case scenario, represented in Figure 15a, the normal and attack data distributions show minimal overlap, contributing to a high MCC score of 0.976. The normal data predominantly fall within a narrow Z-score range, with very few exceeding the threshold. Conversely, Figure 15b illustrates the worst case, where a significant number of normal samples are incorrectly classified as attacks, resulting in a lower MCC score of 0.860. This scenario also features some overlap between normal and attack samples in the Z-score range of 1 to 2. The figures include density plots and histograms to visually represent the data distributions, with clear demarcation of the threshold that separates normal from attack classes.
