This paper discusses various machine learning and deep learning approaches for detecting webshell attacks and malware in IoT networks. The authors compare the effectiveness of ensemble learning methods that utilize either homogeneous or heterogeneous classification models. Hemalatha et al. introduced a method that converts program binaries into two-dimensional images, utilizing a pretrained deep-learning model, DenseNet, while addressing class imbalance through a reweighted cross-entropy loss function.

The paper highlights the use of autoencoders for feature reduction in network intrusion detection systems (NIDS). Li et al. demonstrated an ML-NIDS that employs an autoencoder for dimensionality reduction in conjunction with a deep belief network (DBN) for classification, achieving better detection accuracy compared to using the DBN alone. Tao et al. further explored data fusion techniques to enhance classification accuracy using deep autoencoders.

The KDD Cup 99 dataset, although widely used, has limitations due to duplicate records, leading to the creation of the NSL-KDD dataset which offers a cleaner benchmark. Various studies, including those by Javaid et al., Shone et al., and Al-Qatf et al., confirmed that integrating autoencoders with classification methods such as SoftMax regression and SVM improved NIDS performance significantly.

Madani et al. assessed the robustness of autoencoders against data poisoning attacks, finding them superior to PCA-based models in detection capability. Other studies utilized stacked autoencoders for feature extraction from diverse datasets, yielding high F1-scores in different network scenarios.

In IoT environments, Dutta et al. proposed an anomaly detection system leveraging a deep sparse autoencoder, deep neural networks (DNN), and LSTM, followed by logistic regression for classifying anomalous traffic, optimizing computational efficiency. Overall, the paper underscores the value of advanced machine learning techniques in enhancing cybersecurity measures within IoT networks.
