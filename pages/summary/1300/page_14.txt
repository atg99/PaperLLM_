This section presents the experimental results using the IoTID20 dataset, which was collected in IoT network environments. Each model configuration was trained for 50 epochs with a batch size of 512, and the average performance across 10 runs is reported. The three model structures achieved similar results, with a maximum Matthew's Correlation Coefficient (MCC) score of 0.595, indicating poor performance primarily due to a high False Positive Rate (FPR) exceeding 0.3, despite achieving a high accuracy of 94%. 

Table 8 details the average metrics for each model structure, highlighting the best MCC score. Table 9 provides the best performance metrics, where the highest F1-score of 0.974 was recorded for all models. The confusion matrix in Table 10 indicates that the models often misclassify attacks as normal logs rather than the reverse.

The analysis also examines the impact of latent size on model performance. As depicted in Figure 8a, the MCC for the (5,32) model improves with increasing latent size, while the True Positive Rate (TPR) for Scan attacks decreases. The (5,64) and (7,64) models show stable MCC scores with minor fluctuations, achieving close to optimal performance at smaller latent sizes. Detection rates were notably higher for DoS and MITM attacks compared to other types, with Scan attacks being the most challenging to detect.
