The paper discusses the evolution of network intrusion detection systems (NIDS) from traditional signature-based methods to more advanced anomaly-based approaches. Traditional methods, which rely on known attack patterns, struggle to detect new and sophisticated attacks due to their dependence on pre-labeled training data and the significant human effort involved in labeling.

To address these limitations, researchers have turned to anomaly-based detection, which learns normal behavior in an unsupervised manner and identifies anomalies that deviate from this norm. Techniques such as one-class SVM and clustering methods have been utilized for this purpose. A notable advancement in this area is the use of autoencoders, which are generative deep learning models that can effectively learn normal traffic patterns. By compressing input data and reconstructing it, autoencoders can identify attacks based on reconstruction errors that exceed a predefined threshold.

While autoencoders show promise in detecting unknown attack types, their performance is highly sensitive to the training process and hyperparameter selection. This emphasizes the need for careful tuning and evaluation in the development of effective NIDS using modern machine learning techniques.
