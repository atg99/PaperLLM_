The paper presents an analysis of different model configurations and their performance in detecting attacks using a confusion matrix and metrics like Matthews Correlation Coefficient (MCC). In the best-performing configurations, the (5,32) model achieved 10,920 true positives (TP), 636 false positives (FP), 1,913 false negatives (FN), and 9,075 true negatives (TN). The (5,64) model had slightly higher TP at 11,019 but also increased FP at 768, while the (7,64) model performed with 10,604 TP and lower FP at 428.

The second experiment investigates the relationship between the model's latent size and its performance. The (5,32) model showed optimal performance at a latent size of 4, with subsequent fluctuations. Similarly, the (5,64) model peaked at a latent size of 3, and the (7,64) model maintained an MCC close to 0.7, also at a latent size of 3. Figures provided illustrate these trends in MCC and true positive rates (TPR) across different attack types as latent sizes vary, emphasizing the importance of latent size in model performance.
