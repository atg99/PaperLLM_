The paper discusses strategies for managing hidden units in neural networks to address issues of bias and variance, which are critical for model performance. The first part focuses on a dynamic constant that adjusts the confidence level of the sigma rule based on bias conditions. A high bias indicates underfitting, necessitating increased network complexity, while a low bias suggests avoiding additional hidden units to prevent variance escalation. When adding a new hidden unit, parameters are randomly sampled to minimize reconstruction error, although this approach may not always guarantee model convergence. 

The second part introduces a hidden unit pruning strategy to combat overfitting caused by high variance in complex networks. This strategy evaluates model variance and employs a statistical process control method to identify when pruning is necessary. The pruning process involves assessing the significance of each hidden unit and discarding those that contribute little to the model's performance. The significance is determined through the average activation degree of the hidden nodes, which reflects their importance in the feature space.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the need for adaptive methods in managing hidden units to enhance model flexibility and performance, balancing complexity and generalization.
