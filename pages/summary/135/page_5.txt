The paper discusses the DEVDAN algorithm's learning mechanism, emphasizing its dual-phase learning strategy involving both generative and discriminative training. The algorithm iteratively processes data batches, first evaluating generalization performance through discriminative testing, then updating network parameters in an unsupervised manner via generative training, and finally refining the parameters through supervised discriminative training.

A critical aspect of the paper is the evaluation of the Denoising Autoencoder (DAE) through its reconstruction error, measured using mean square error (MSE). However, the MSE approach presents limitations, particularly in single-pass learning scenarios where it fails to account for the generalization capabilities of the DAE on unseen data. To address these limitations, the authors introduce a Network Significance (NS) formula that incorporates the expectation of the reconstruction error, allowing for a more comprehensive assessment of the DAE's performance.

The NS formula is derived mathematically, demonstrating the relationship between the reconstruction error and the DAE's generalization power. It highlights the decomposition of the error into bias and variance components, ultimately providing a robust framework for evaluating the efficacy of the DAE in learning tasks.
