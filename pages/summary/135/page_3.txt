DEVDAN is a novel algorithm for handling evolving data streams, characterized by its one-pass learning approach and a coupled-generative-discriminative training process. This dual-phase structure allows DEVDAN to effectively adapt to changes in data concepts, regardless of label availability. The paper presents a unique NS formula that governs network evolution during the generative training phase, emphasizing that the structural learning mechanism is independent of user-defined thresholds, making it a robust solution.

The effectiveness of DEVDAN is evaluated through extensive experiments on ten benchmark datasets, including Rotated MNIST, Permuted MNIST, and KDDCup, among others. The algorithm is compared with various state-of-the-art data stream methods, such as ADL and LEARN++NSE, as well as continual learning methods for deep networks like HAT and PNN. The evaluation follows the prequential test-then-train protocol, which involves assessing model performance across data batches and confirming the significance of results using the Wilcoxon test.

The paper is structured into several sections: it begins with problem formulation, followed by a detailed description of the DEVDAN algorithm, and then presents numerical studies that include comparisons and an ablation study. The final section provides concluding remarks on the findings. Additionally, a list of symbols is defined to aid understanding throughout the paper, covering key variables and functions used in the algorithm's framework.
