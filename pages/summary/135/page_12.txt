The paper discusses the DEVDAN framework, focusing on its time complexity during training phases and its application in continual learning scenarios. The time complexity for each training phase, including generative and discriminative processes, is outlined in a table, indicating the computational effort required for operations such as hidden unit growing, pruning, stochastic gradient descent (SGD), and parameter adjustment.

DEVDAN's implementation begins with a single hidden unit and operates without user-defined thresholds. It employs SGD for parameter adjustment, with specified learning rates and momentum coefficients to stabilize training and mitigate local minima issues. The framework uses masking noise on input data and is evaluated through a prequential test-then-train procedure, simulating real data stream conditions. The evaluation includes a windowing approach to assess model performance under concept drift.

DEVDAN is compared against several state-of-the-art algorithms in data stream and continual learning, ensuring a fair comparison by re-implementing these algorithms in the same environment and tuning hyperparameters. The performance metrics include classification rate, number of parameters, training and testing times, and the structure of hidden units and layers.

Overall, the paper highlights the effectiveness of DEVDAN in handling continual learning tasks, particularly in environments with limited labeled data, while providing a comprehensive evaluation against established algorithms. The source code is publicly available for further exploration.
