The paper presents a comprehensive numerical evaluation of various algorithms, including DEVDAN, ADL, HAT, PNN, and others, across different datasets such as MNIST, Permuted MNIST, and more. The results are summarized in Table 4, which lists classification rates (CR), training times (TrT), testing times (TsT), hidden nodes (HN), hidden layers (HL), and number of parameters (NoP) for each algorithm.

Key findings indicate that DEVDAN consistently outperforms other algorithms, particularly in classification rates across different datasets. The efficacy of the NS formula is highlighted, showing its ability to adjust network complexity dynamically, which helps mitigate issues of bias and variance. The analysis reveals that increasing the number of nodes generally improves predictive performance, while sudden increases in loss signal potential concept drift.

A statistical validation using the Wilcoxon signed-rank test confirms that DEVDAN's predictive accuracy is significantly better than its competitors, as indicated by the rejection of the null hypothesis based on residual error analysis. This suggests the robustness and adaptability of the DEVDAN algorithm in various contexts. Additionally, it is noted that the DEVDAN framework can be extended to deeper architectures through a greedy layer-wise learning approach, enhancing its applicability in complex scenarios.
