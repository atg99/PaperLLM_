In this paper, the authors develop a framework to analyze the performance of Denoising Autoencoders (DAE) through a novel formulation of the Noise Sensitivity (NS) metric. The NS metric is expressed in terms of bias and variance, denoted as Bias(z)² and Var(z), which are derived from the expectations of the recovered input attributes. The key equation (5) illustrates that NS can be decomposed into the squared bias and variance components.

The authors demonstrate how to compute the expectation of the recovered attributes, E[z], which captures both the contributions from previously seen training samples and unseen data. The expectation is influenced by the feature extractor and is modified to account for partially destroyed input features due to masking noise. The expressions for E[y] and E[z] are derived under the assumption of normal distribution, leading to the conclusion that E[z] is a function of two sigmoid functions.

Further, the authors clarify that the NS formula for DAE in the generative phase differs from that used in Adaptive Learning (ADL) as it is based on the expectation of the squared reconstruction error rather than output error. This distinction highlights the interconnected nature of the encoding and decoding processes in DAE.

Overall, the paper provides a comprehensive analysis of the NS metric in the context of DAE, emphasizing its role in assessing model performance regarding underfitting and overfitting, as well as its application in controlling hidden unit growth and pruning.
