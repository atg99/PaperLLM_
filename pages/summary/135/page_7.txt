The paper discusses the reliability of an encoder-decoder model, particularly focusing on the DEVDAN framework and its ability to balance bias and variance through a novel measure termed NS. A high NS value indicates potential overfitting or underfitting issues, which cannot be captured solely by traditional error metrics. The NS formula is advantageous as it is computationally efficient and does not require the storage of previous samples.

In the generative training phase, the algorithm iteratively processes input data while monitoring bias and variance. A hidden unit growing mechanism is implemented to address high bias problems by adding new hidden nodes based on specific statistical conditions. This is grounded in principles from statistical process control but tailored to focus on reconstructing corrupted input rather than binary classification.

The conditions for growing hidden nodes involve comparing the mean and standard deviation of bias at the current time with their minimum values recorded up to that point. If the conditions are met, the minimum values are reset, promoting adaptability to changing data distributions. Conversely, a pruning mechanism is also described, where hidden nodes are removed if they do not contribute to reducing variance, ensuring that the model remains efficient in its capacity. Overall, the paper outlines a structured approach to enhancing the performance of the encoder-decoder model through dynamic adjustments to its architecture.
