The paper presents an algorithm termed DEVDAN, which focuses on discriminative training in environments where data streams are received without true class labels. The algorithm operates through a structured training phase involving feedforward operations and error calculations. Key components include mechanisms for growing and pruning hidden nodes based on bias and variance metrics, optimizing the network's architecture dynamically.

The complexity analysis indicates that DEVDAN maintains a low computational cost, particularly in scenarios with large input dimensions and numerous hidden units. The time complexity for processing a single sample is expressed as O([12·[R·n] + 4·[R·m] + 4]·T·K), which is advantageous as it avoids quadratic growth. The space complexity is also reasonable at O([6·[R·n] + 3·[R·m] + 8]), primarily due to the storage of gradients and weights. These attributes point to DEVDAN's scalability and efficiency in fast data stream contexts.

To validate DEVDAN’s performance, the authors conducted experiments on standard supervised learning benchmarks, including statistical tests and an ablation study to assess the algorithm's effectiveness and robustness. Overall, DEVDAN demonstrates promising results when compared to other incremental learning methods.
