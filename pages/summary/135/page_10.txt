The paper discusses the DEVDAN algorithm, which operates through a two-phase approach: generative and discriminative learning. Initially, DEVDAN starts with a single hidden unit and can evolve its network structure by adding or pruning hidden units based on specific criteria. In the generative phase, DEVDAN leverages unlabeled data to condition network parameters, addressing the challenge of random initialization. The discriminative phase then refines the model using labeled data, optimizing the network with a focus on minimizing cross-entropy loss.

The algorithm employs a one-pass learning strategy, adjusting weights and biases using stochastic gradient descent with momentum. The paper emphasizes that DEVDAN's structure can adapt dynamically, enhancing its capacity to solve semi-supervised learning problems. The evolution of the network's hidden units is illustrated, demonstrating the algorithm's ability to grow and prune units as needed throughout the learning process. The integration of both generative and discriminative phases fosters a continual learning cycle, ultimately improving model performance.
