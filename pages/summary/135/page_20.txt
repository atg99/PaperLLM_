The paper introduces DEVDAN, an evolving denoising autoencoder designed for data stream analytics that integrates generative and discriminative training phases. The results presented in Table 9 highlight the effectiveness of a sample selection mechanism in improving classification accuracy on the Rotated MNIST dataset, achieving enhancements between 0.2% and 2%. This is attributed to the strategy of leveraging labeled data near the decision boundary, which bolsters the model's confidence during classification. However, in the Forest Covertype dataset, characterized by imbalanced class proportions, the same strategy led to a decrease in classification accuracy due to the exclusion of majority class samples that had already met a certain confidence threshold.

The findings underscore DEVDAN's potential in addressing semi-supervised learning challenges, with its dual training phases adept at managing concept drift regardless of label availability. The paper concludes that DEVDAN outperformed other algorithms in supervised learning across four of ten problems and excelled in all semi-supervised scenarios. Future work aims to explore a deeper version of DEVDAN for enhanced generalization, incorporate advanced semi-supervised learning techniques, and assess its applicability in transfer learning contexts. The source code and a demonstration video of DEVDAN's learning mechanism are provided for reproducibility.
