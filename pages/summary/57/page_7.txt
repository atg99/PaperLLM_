This paper explores the long memory characteristics of Recurrent Neural Networks (RNN) and Long Short-Term Memory networks (LSTM), particularly focusing on the Modified LSTM (MLSTM) model. The authors define long memory based on specific criteria and demonstrate that while LSTM and MLSTM both exhibit long memory in their cell states, simplifications to linear networks are not feasible due to their inherent nonlinearities. However, it is established that a constant-gates-LSTM lacks long memory, whereas a constant-gates-MLSTM qualifies as a long memory network.

The study includes extensive numerical experiments comparing the performance of these models on various time series forecasting tasks, utilizing both long and short memory datasets. The datasets include synthetic examples and real-world datasets like the Dow Jones Industrial Average and traffic volume data. The models were implemented in PyTorch and optimized using the Adam algorithm, with performance evaluated through metrics such as RMSE, MAE, and MAPE.

Key findings reveal that the MLSTM effectively captures long memory effects, as illustrated through autocorrelation plots of the datasets. The experiments indicate that the MLSTM outperforms traditional models like ARFIMA, emphasizing its utility in time series forecasting tasks. The paper provides insights into the implications of model parameters on forecasting performance and suggests further avenues for research in sentiment analysis tasks.
