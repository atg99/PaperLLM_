The paper explores the concept of long memory in time series analysis, particularly in the context of Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks. Long memory processes, such as fractionally integrated processes, are characterized by their dependency structure, where the memory parameter d indicates the degree of long-range dependence. Specifically, when d is between 0 and 0.5, the time series exhibits long memory, with larger values of d signifying longer memory.

In the context of multivariate time series, the definition of long memory is less straightforward, often requiring an assessment of each component's long-range dependency. The paper also discusses the concept of geometric ergodicity within Markov chains, indicating that a geometrically ergodic process has short memory since it forgets past information exponentially fast.

The authors introduce the notion of a recurrent network process, where the target sequence is generated from a hidden state and an input sequence. The absence of exogenous input is emphasized to maintain the focus on the network's ability to model long memory sequences. The transition function of the network is examined, and specific instances, such as vanilla RNNs, are presented to illustrate the modeling process.

Overall, the paper aims to assess whether RNNs and LSTMs can effectively model long memory sequences by analyzing their underlying processes and memory characteristics.
