The paper discusses the capabilities of Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks in handling long-term dependencies in sequential data. It highlights the inherent limitations of traditional RNNs in capturing long memory due to issues like vanishing gradients. In contrast, LSTMs are designed to address this limitation through their unique architecture, which includes memory cells and gating mechanisms that enable them to maintain information over extended periods.

The authors also reference various studies and methodologies that explore the generalization of filter designs and stability in systems with fractional-order elements, indicating a broader context in which RNNs and LSTMs can be understood. Additionally, the paper mentions advancements in machine learning techniques, including low-pass recurrent neural networks and tensor-train RNNs, which further enhance the ability to discover long-term correlations in data.

In summary, while RNNs struggle with long memory, LSTMs provide a more robust solution for learning from sequential data by effectively managing long-term dependencies.
