The paper investigates whether Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) and Long Short-Term Memory networks (LSTMs) possess long memory characteristics in their forecasting capabilities. The study evaluates multiple models, including RNNs, MRNNs, and LSTMs, and compares their performance through Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) metrics across various datasets, such as traffic and DJI.

Table 2 presents the overall performance results, highlighting that MRNN and MRNNF consistently achieve lower average RMSE values compared to other models. The analysis indicates that while MLSTM does not significantly outperform LSTM, it suggests challenges in training MLSTM effectively. Further, the boxplots illustrate the stability and variability of these models, particularly focusing on the traffic dataset.

In Table 3, the best RMSE performances are reported, showcasing that MRNN and MRNNF excel in datasets with long memory effects (ARFIMA, traffic, tree), while remaining competitive in others like DJI. The authors also conduct two-sample t-tests to rigorously compare the models, confirming that MRNN significantly outperforms standard RNNs and LSTMs across most datasets.

The paper also explores the performance of models on datasets lacking long memory effects, demonstrating that the proposed models maintain effectiveness without deterioration. A synthetic dataset generated by an RNN model serves as a basis for this analysis, revealing comparable performance levels for MRNN(F) and MLSTM(F) against the true RNN model.

Lastly, the research delves into the impact of the model parameter K on long memory datasets, indicating that careful tuning of K is essential for optimizing the performance of LSTM and MLSTM models. Overall, the findings suggest that MRNNs exhibit long memory capabilities and outperform traditional RNN and LSTM models in various forecasting tasks.
