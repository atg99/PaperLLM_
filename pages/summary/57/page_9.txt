The paper investigates whether RNN and LSTM possess long memory capabilities, concluding that they do not from a time series perspective. To address this limitation, the authors propose modified models, MRNN and MRNNF, which effectively handle long-range dependencies in data. The performance of these models is evaluated on two sentiment analysis datasets: CMU-MOSI and a paper reviews dataset.

In the CMU-MOSI dataset, the proposed models consistently outperform baseline models in terms of Mean Absolute Error (MAE), Root Mean Square Error (RMSE), and Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE) when using larger values of K. Notably, MLSTM and MLSTMF perform better with smaller K values, suggesting a need for further investigation into the balance between expressiveness and optimization for these models.

For the paper reviews dataset, performance metrics including accuracy, precision, recall, and cross-entropy loss reveal that MLSTMF significantly enhances performance across various measures compared to LSTM, while MRNNF achieves the highest accuracy. Statistical significance is noted in the results, with p-values indicating strong improvements over traditional RNN and LSTM models.

The conclusion emphasizes that MRNN and MRNNF models demonstrate advantages in forecasting long-term dependencies, and the combination of MLSTMF with LSTM layers shows substantial performance improvements on the paper reviews dataset. Future work is suggested to explore the application of memory filters to other recurrent and feed-forward networks, as well as the possibility of simplifying models for faster optimization. The authors also propose investigating other decay patterns for modeling long memory sequences and the potential of filter banks in extracting long memory features.
