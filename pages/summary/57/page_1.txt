The paper introduces a new definition of long memory networks, which requires model weights to decay at a polynomial rate. This definition addresses the limitations of RNNs and LSTMs, which are traditionally viewed as having Markovian update dynamics. The authors argue that these dynamics prevent the models from retaining long-term dependencies effectively.

To validate their theory, the authors modify RNN and LSTM architectures by incorporating a memory filter component. This filter allows for dynamic weightings of historical observations and enables the models to better capture long-term dependencies. The modified models, termed Memory-augmented RNN (MRNN) and Memory-augmented LSTM (MLSTM), exhibit improved performance in modeling long-range dependencies across various datasets.

The paper concludes that while RNNs and LSTMs have made significant advancements in machine learning applications, their inherent structure limits their ability to maintain long memory. The introduction of memory-augmented components provides a promising direction for enhancing their performance in this regard.
