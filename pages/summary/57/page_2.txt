The paper explores the limitations of traditional Recurrent Neural Networks (RNN) and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks in capturing long memory effects in time series data. It argues that while RNNs and LSTMs are typically designed for short memory tasks, they can be enhanced to model long memory processes effectively.

To address this, the authors propose a new definition of long memory network processes grounded in statistical theory and introduce a long memory filter component for network architecture. This modification allows RNNs and LSTMs to better accommodate sequences that exhibit long-range dependencies.

The paper reviews related works that discuss the long memory phenomenon in various datasets, including language, finance, and environmental science, noting the rigidity of traditional statistical models compared to the flexibility of neural networks. It also discusses the limited theoretical analysis of LSTMs, highlighting studies that examine their stability and components contributing to performance.

Furthermore, the paper reviews various recurrent network architectures designed to account for long-term dependencies, such as Hierarchical RNNs and Dilated RNNs, as well as memory networks that utilize external memory units like Neural Turing Machines and attention mechanisms.

The authors conclude by positioning their proposed memory filter as a special Finite Impulse Response (FIR) filter, linking it to existing literature on filters used in RNNs and signal processing. They provide a formal definition of long memory in the context of stationary time series and discuss the spectral properties associated with this phenomenon. Overall, the paper aims to bridge the gap between statistical long memory processes and neural network architectures.
