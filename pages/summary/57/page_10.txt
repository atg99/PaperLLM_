The paper explores the capacity of Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) and Long Short-Term Memory networks (LSTMs) to exhibit long memory characteristics. It acknowledges the challenges in learning long-term dependencies using gradient descent, as highlighted by earlier works. RNNs are traditionally known for their ability to process sequences; however, they struggle with long-term dependency retention due to issues like vanishing gradients. LSTMs were introduced to address these limitations through their unique architecture, which includes memory cells and gating mechanisms that enable them to maintain information over extended periods.

The paper references various studies on long-memory processes, emphasizing the significance of this property in time series analysis and its relevance in financial markets. The authors argue that while LSTMs are designed to capture long-range dependencies effectively, their performance can still vary based on the specific application and data characteristics. The research acknowledges the contributions of numerous scholars in the fields of neural networks and time series modeling, indicating a collaborative effort to deepen the understanding of memory in machine learning contexts.

In conclusion, the paper posits that RNNs and LSTMs can exhibit long memory properties under certain conditions, but further investigation is needed to quantify and optimize their performance in capturing these dependencies across different applications.
