The paper discusses the memory properties of Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks, focusing on their ability to handle long-range dependencies in time series data. It begins by defining the processes for RNNs and LSTMs, including their respective equations and hidden state calculations. 

A key aspect of the analysis involves two theorems under certain assumptions regarding the joint density function of the noise term. Theorems 1 and 2 establish that both RNN and LSTM processes are geometrically ergodic, implying they exhibit short memory rather than long memory. Specifically, the conditions for short memory are tied to the spectral radius of the weight matrix and the activation functions employed.

The paper concludes that RNNs, particularly with linear or ReLU activation functions, typically have short memory when weight magnitudes are constrained. It also emphasizes that LSTMs, despite their design to capture long-term dependencies, also fall short in this regard under the discussed conditions. Corollaries further reinforce the findings, indicating that both RNNs and LSTMs lack the capability to effectively model time series with stable long-range dependence.
