The paper presents a stochastic hard negative example mining method for optimizing triplet loss in machine learning models. The authors set the batch size to 60 and adjust hyperparameters based on dataset size, using η values of 10 for smaller datasets and 5 for larger ones, with different α and β values for each dataset. They employed the Adam optimizer with an initial learning rate of 1×10−4 and weight decay of 5×10−4, decaying the learning rate to 1×10−7 between epochs 200 and 400.

For evaluation, the Recall@K metric is utilized, measuring the proportion of correctly retrieved samples based on the nearest neighbors from the test set. The authors highlight the effectiveness of their stochastic hard negative mining method compared to existing methods, showing that it consistently identifies more desirable triplets with non-zero losses. Experimental results indicate that their approach improves accuracy over random sampling and other baseline methods, including variations that assess the impact of hard sample mining and class signature loss.

The paper concludes that their method effectively tracks neighbor relations during training, allowing for a coarse-to-fine search to identify hard examples, thereby enhancing model performance with minimal additional costs. The results demonstrate superior accuracy compared to state-of-the-art methods across various datasets, while maintaining comparable computational costs.
