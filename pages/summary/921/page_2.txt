This paper presents a novel approach to hard example mining in deep metric learning, focusing on the efficient identification of nearby neighbor classes from a stochastically sampled anchor class. The key innovation involves using class signatures, which monitor changes in the embedding function, allowing for adaptive updates of hard negative classes throughout training. By approximating sample-to-sample distances with sample-to-class distances, the method circumvents the computational burden of calculating distances between every sample pair, thus enhancing efficiency.

The experimental results demonstrate that the proposed technique significantly improves accuracy in image retrieval tasks across various benchmark datasets, including CARS-196, CUB-200-2011, In-shop retrieval, and Stanford online products. Furthermore, the incorporation of a cross-channel correlation technique enhances the representation power, leading to state-of-the-art performance.

The paper reviews existing hard example mining methods, noting that traditional approaches either rely on exact searches within minibatches or approximate searches across entire datasets. The authors emphasize the limitations of prior methods, such as the reliance on precomputed embeddings and coarse class-level approximations, which hinder effective mining of hard examples. In contrast, their method employs class signatures to efficiently select hard negative classes and refine searches, thus improving mining performance.

Overall, the proposed method effectively balances computational efficiency and mining accuracy, addressing key challenges in deep metric learning and contributing to advancements in image retrieval tasks.
