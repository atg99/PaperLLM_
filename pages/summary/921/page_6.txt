This paper discusses enhancements to a feature extraction method for image retrieval, particularly through the introduction of second-order pooling and cross-channel correlation. The feature extractor is built on the Inception v1 architecture, with modifications including a batch normalization layer and the implementation of second-order pooling, which utilizes cross-channel correlation to enhance performance in various computer vision tasks.

The second-order pooling aggregates features by considering the outer product of feature vectors, effectively capturing spatial relationships in the data. To mitigate computational demands, the authors employ a technique called Tensor Sketch. They also found that increasing image resolution improves accuracy without adding parameters, albeit with increased computational cost.

The image retrieval process relies on the similarity of image representations learned through triplet loss, which includes stochastic hard negative mining. The similarity metric emphasizes local features, promoting effective retrieval by minimizing the influence of background noise.

Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed method consistently outperforms the baseline across multiple datasets, including CARS-196 and CUB-200-2011. The results indicate improved accuracy with higher input resolutions, validating the effectiveness of the cross-channel correlation approach.

The experiments utilize standard benchmark datasets without ground-truth bounding box annotations, and detail the implementation of data augmentation techniques to enhance training. The paper concludes with a discussion of the method's performance relative to existing techniques, showcasing its potential in image retrieval tasks.
