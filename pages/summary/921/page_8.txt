The paper presents a comprehensive evaluation of various methods for fine-grained image retrieval across multiple datasets, including CARS-196, CUB-200-2011, In-shop retrieval, and Stanford Online Products. The results are summarized in tables comparing Recall@K and accuracy metrics for different algorithms. 

Table 2 highlights the Recall@K performance, where Stochastic Mining (Algorithm 2) consistently shows superior results across all datasets, achieving the highest recall rates at various K values. For instance, it reaches 83.4% at K=1 on CARS-196 and 91.7% at K=1 for the proposed Channel-correlation method.

Table 3 and Table 4 further detail accuracy comparisons on CARS-196 and CUB-200-2011, respectively. The proposed methods outperform existing baselines, with the proposed Channel-correlation method achieving 91.7% at R@1 on CARS-196 and showing significant improvements on CUB-200-2011 as well.

In In-shop retrieval (Table 5) and Stanford Online Products (Table 6), the proposed methods continue to excel, notably achieving 90.7% at R@1 for In-shop retrieval and 77.6% at R@1 for Stanford Online Products with the Channel-correlation approach.

Overall, the findings demonstrate that the proposed methods, particularly those utilizing channel-correlation, significantly enhance retrieval accuracy and recall compared to established baselines, indicating their potential effectiveness in deep metric learning for fine-grained categorization tasks.
