The paper presents a method for constructing minibatches in training deep learning models, particularly focusing on hard example mining to improve performance in tasks like In-shop retrieval and SOP datasets. 

Figure 3 demonstrates that the average sample-to-sample distance between classes correlates with their class-to-class distances, reinforcing the notion that closer classes yield more similar samples. Figure 4 illustrates the diversity of selected negative classes in minibatches, showing that a stochastic hard example mining approach leads to greater diversity across training iterations compared to a deterministic method.

The authors adopt a baseline protocol for batch construction based on Zhao et al., which involves randomly sampling a fixed number of classes and images to create minibatches. While this method is effective due to its simplicity, it lacks the capability to maximize the inclusion of hard triplets in the minibatch.

To enhance the baseline approach, the authors propose a method of hard class mining that focuses on selecting instances from an anchor class and its nearest classes. This selection process aims to increase the number of challenging examples within each minibatch. However, a challenge arises from high intra-class variation, which can distort class similarity measurements and reduce the diversity of sampled negative classes.

To address these limitations, the paper introduces a stochastic hard example mining strategy. This approach utilizes instance-to-class distances rather than solely relying on class signatures to identify nearest neighbor classes. By randomly selecting an anchor class and its instances, the method identifies a pool of nearest neighbor classes based on instance-to-class distances, thereby improving the robustness of the minibatch construction. This technique is particularly effective in scenarios with significant intra-class variation, leading to more effective training outcomes.
