The paper presents a novel approach to deep metric learning by introducing a stochastic class-based hard example mining technique. The authors, Yumin Suh, Bohyung Han, Wonsik Kim, and Kyoung Mu Lee, highlight that the performance of deep metric learning is significantly influenced by the effective mining of hard negative examples during training. Traditional methods often face a trade-off between training speed and prediction accuracy due to high computational costs associated with frequent feature computations and nearest neighbor searches in large datasets.

To address these challenges, the authors propose a method that utilizes class signatures to track feature embeddings online with minimal additional cost. This allows for the identification of hard negative candidates efficiently. The algorithm first selects hard negative classes based on class-to-sample distances and then refines the search at the instance level within those selected classes, significantly reducing the computational burden compared to exhaustive searches.

The proposed technique has demonstrated substantial improvements in image retrieval accuracy, achieving state-of-the-art performance on several benchmark datasets. The paper underscores the critical issue of computational feasibility in metric learning, particularly in the context of triplet loss, and positions their stochastic hard example mining method as a solution that balances efficiency and effectiveness in training deep metric learning models.
