This paper addresses this challenge by proposing a fully automated approach to evolving CNN architectures using a genetic algorithm that leverages ResNet and DenseNet blocks.

The proposed algorithm eliminates the need for pre-processing or post-processing stages, allowing users without extensive domain knowledge in CNNs or genetic algorithms to design CNN architectures effectively. The evaluation of this approach is conducted on CIFAR10 and CIFAR100 benchmark datasets, where it is compared against 18 state-of-the-art competitors. Results indicate that the proposed method surpasses both hand-crafted CNN architectures and those designed by other automatic methods in classification performance. Additionally, it demonstrates competitive accuracy against semi-automatic peers while requiring significantly fewer computational resources.

The study highlights the inherent challenges in CNN architecture optimization, including the non-convex and non-differentiable nature of architecture-related parameters, which complicates traditional gradient-based optimization methods. The paper emphasizes the limitations of existing architecture optimization techniques that assume a fixed number of parameters, thereby reinforcing the necessity for a more flexible and efficient approach like the one introduced.

In summary, this work presents a novel solution for CNN architecture design that is fully automated, efficient, and effective, thereby democratizing access to advanced CNN models for users without specialized knowledge.
