The paper presents a mutation operation and environmental selection process for an automatic convolutional neural network (CNN) architecture design algorithm. The mutation operation involves randomly modifying offspring individuals through adding, removing, or modifying units within the network. The process is guided by a mutation probability, and specific actions are taken based on randomly chosen types of mutation. The resulting mutated offspring are then returned for further processing.

In the environmental selection phase, a population of individuals is selected from the current generation and the newly generated offspring to create the next generation. The selection process aims to maintain both convergence and diversity within the population to avoid local minima and premature convergence. This is achieved through a binary tournament selection method that identifies individuals with the best fitness scores. Additionally, an elitism mechanism is incorporated, ensuring that the best-performing individual is included in the new population, thereby enhancing the overall quality of the evolutionary process.

The experiment aims to validate the performance of the proposed algorithm in image classification tasks by comparing it against various peer competitors, which include state-of-the-art CNNs designed with expert knowledge. The paper details the selection of these competitors, the benchmark datasets used for testing, and the parameter settings for the experiments.
