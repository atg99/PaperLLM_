The paper discusses the fundamentals of Genetic Algorithms (GAs), ResNet Blocks (RBs), and DenseNet Blocks (DBs) to provide context for the proposed algorithm, AE-CNN (Automatically Evolving CNNs), which is designed specifically for image classification tasks.

GAs are highlighted as a heuristic, population-based optimization method that mimics biological evolution. They are particularly effective in non-convex and non-differentiable optimization problems due to their gradient-free nature. The GA process involves initializing a population of candidate solutions, evaluating their fitness, selecting parents, generating offspring through crossover and mutation, and iterating this process until a termination criterion, often a maximum generation number, is met.

ResNet and DenseNet are presented as advanced Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) whose success is attributed to their respective building blocks. RBs consist of multiple convolutional layers with skip connections, which help mitigate the gradient vanishing problem and enhance learning efficiency. DBs feature connections from each layer to all previous layers, allowing for better reuse of features and improved classification accuracy.

The proposed AE-CNN framework consists of three main components: initializing a population of CNN architectures, evaluating their performance, and applying a genetic algorithm to evolve these architectures over a set number of generations. The best architecture is then selected based on its performance.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the synergy between GAs and modern CNN architectures, illustrating how these methodologies can be integrated to enhance image classification capabilities.
