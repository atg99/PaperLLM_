The paper discusses the development of a convolutional neural network (CNN) architecture design algorithm utilizing genetic algorithms (GAs). The algorithm aims to automatically design and evolve optimal CNN architectures for specific tasks while operating under limited computational resources. The findings indicate that the algorithm effectively improves classification accuracy across generations, demonstrating a trend of convergence with enhancements in both mean and best classification accuracy.

The results presented in the figures show that classification accuracy improves steadily, with some fluctuations, particularly at the 4th generation. The proposed algorithm achieves a significant increase in mean classification accuracy from about 30% to 79% across generations, while maintaining a consistent upper boundary for best accuracy through an elitism strategy that retains the fittest individuals for subsequent generations.

The paper provides detailed information on the best architectures discovered for the CIFAR10 and CIFAR100 datasets, highlighting that the architecture for CIFAR10 consists of nine units with 38 layers, while that for CIFAR100 comprises six units with 21 layers. Notably, the best architecture for CIFAR100 has fewer layers than that for CIFAR10, challenging the conventional wisdom that more complex tasks require deeper networks.

In conclusion, the study successfully demonstrates the potential of the proposed GA-based algorithm to discover efficient CNN architectures that outperform existing state-of-the-art models, suggesting that the evolutionary search process can yield valuable insights into effective architecture design. Future research may explore further enhancements to the algorithm and its application to more complex datasets.
