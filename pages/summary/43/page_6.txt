The paper discusses the use of evolutionary algorithms, specifically focusing on the AE-CNN algorithm, which aims to enhance population diversity and prevent premature convergence. In evolutionary algorithms, offspring are expected to inherit beneficial traits from parents, necessitating the selection of high-fitness individuals. However, selecting only the best individuals can lead to reduced diversity and local minima trapping. To mitigate this, the AE-CNN algorithm employs binary tournament selection to randomly choose parent individuals, allowing for a more diverse pool.

The crossover operation is crucial for generating offspring and is adapted in this algorithm due to the varying lengths of individuals, which represent CNNs with different depths. Traditional crossover methods are not applicable, so a one-point crossover operator, commonly used in Genetic Programming, is implemented. This operator allows for the creation of two offspring by combining parts of the parent individuals, with necessary adjustments made to ensure valid CNN configurations.

Additionally, mutation serves as a global search mechanism within the algorithm, introducing variations to offspring based on specific mutation types, such as adding, removing, or modifying components of the CNN. The mutation process follows a defined routine and includes automatic adjustments to maintain valid configurations.

Overall, the AE-CNN algorithm combines selection, crossover, and mutation strategies to explore the search space effectively while maintaining diversity and performance in the evolutionary process.
