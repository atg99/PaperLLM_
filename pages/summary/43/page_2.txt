The paper discusses the challenges in designing Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), particularly the need for expertise in both CNN architecture and specific problem domains, which often limits their application in fields such as medical imaging. There is a growing demand for algorithms that can automatically or semi-automatically design CNN architectures without requiring specialized knowledge. Recent advancements have resulted in various algorithms categorized into semi-automatic and fully automatic methods, utilizing either evolutionary algorithms or reinforcement learning.

Semi-automatic methods, like Genetic CNN and Hierarchical Evolution, still necessitate some level of expert knowledge in base network design. Fully automatic algorithms, such as Neural Architecture Search (NAS), often require substantial computational resources, which may not be accessible to all users. Furthermore, evolutionary algorithms used in CNN design may not fully leverage their potential, leading to suboptimal performance.

To address these limitations, the paper proposes a new genetic algorithm-based approach for automatically designing CNN architectures. The key objectives of this proposed algorithm include eliminating the need for prerequisite knowledge, employing a variable-length encoding scheme for adaptable CNN depth, and implementing a novel crossover and mutation operator to enhance search efficiency. The algorithm utilizes ResNet and DenseNet blocks but does not require user expertise in these architectures.

The paper is structured to provide background information on the proposed algorithm, detailed methodology, experimental design, and results, followed by conclusions and future work.
