The paper discusses various state-of-the-art convolutional neural networks (CNNs) including DenseNet, ResNet, Maxout, VGG, and others, highlighting their effectiveness in image classification tasks. Specifically, two versions of ResNet are utilized in experiments: ResNet with 101 layers and ResNet with 1,202 layers. The goal is to evaluate whether the proposed automatic CNN architecture design algorithm can compete with these established models.

The experiments are conducted using benchmark datasets CIFAR10 and CIFAR100, which are commonly used for recognizing natural objects. CIFAR10 consists of 10 classes with 50,000 training and 10,000 test images, while CIFAR100 has 100 classes with 50,000 training and 10,000 test images, but only 500 training images per class. The paper notes the challenges in recognizing objects due to variations in resolution and background interference.

In terms of methodology, the paper extracts results from peer competitors' original studies to ensure the use of optimal parameters. For the proposed algorithm, parameters such as population size, crossover, and mutation probabilities are set to standard values to facilitate ease of use for researchers. The training process uses Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD) with specific settings for momentum, learning rate, and weight decay.

The evaluation of the proposed algorithm involves training the best individual from the evolutionary process multiple times to ensure reliable results. Ultimately, the best performance is reported for a fair comparison against the established CNNs. The paper emphasizes the rigorous parameter settings and methodologies to ensure accurate evaluation of the CNN architectures.
