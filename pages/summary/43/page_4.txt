The paper presents a framework for an Adaptive Evolutionary Convolutional Neural Network (AE-CNN) designed to optimize CNN architectures through a genetic algorithm approach. The process begins by initializing a population of candidate CNN architectures, which are randomly generated in a uniform manner. Each individual in this population represents a unique CNN structure, constructed primarily from residual blocks (RBs), dense blocks (DBs), and pooling layers, excluding fully-connected layers to mitigate overfitting and reduce computational complexity.

The algorithm operates through several key steps: fitness evaluation of the population, selection of parent individuals via binary tournament selection, and the generation of offspring through crossover and mutation operations. After the offspring are created, their fitness is evaluated, and a new population is selected through environmental selection, which combines the current individuals and the newly generated offspring.

The population initialization phase is crucial, as it lays the foundation for the evolutionary process. It includes the calculation of the maximum number of pooling layers allowed based on the input dimensions, ensuring that the architecture remains valid. The use of pooling layers is constrained to prevent logical errors in dimension reduction.

Overall, the AE-CNN framework aims to efficiently discover optimal CNN architectures by leveraging evolutionary strategies while addressing common pitfalls associated with traditional fully-connected layers. Experimental results are anticipated to demonstrate the effectiveness of this approach in achieving high performance without the complexities introduced by fully-connected architectures.
