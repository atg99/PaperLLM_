The proposed algorithm employs a selection mechanism to choose parents from the current population, facilitating the creation of offspring that ideally inherit advantageous traits from their parents.

The offspring generation process involves crossover and mutation operations. Crossover combines the encoded information of two parent individuals to create one or more offspring, while mutation introduces random changes to an individual’s encoding to maintain diversity within the population. This diversity is crucial for exploring the search space effectively and avoiding premature convergence.

The encoding strategy outlined provides a flexible framework for modeling convolutional neural networks (CNNs) of varying architectures. By utilizing three types of units—RB Units (RBU), DB Units (DBU), and Pooling layer Units (PU)—the strategy allows for significant modifications in the depth and structure of CNNs without a fixed length limitation. This adaptability is essential for optimizing CNN architectures through genetic algorithms.

In summary, the proposed algorithm not only defines an innovative encoding strategy for CNNs but also establishes a comprehensive fitness evaluation process to assess individual architectures. The integration of selection, crossover, and mutation further enhances the algorithm's capability to evolve CNNs that achieve high classification accuracy in various tasks.
