The paper evaluates the performance of various BERT-based ranking models on two datasets: MS MARCO and ClueWeb. Table 1 presents comparative performance metrics, highlighting significant improvements of models over others. Notably, the Conv-KNRM model pre-trained on Bing user clicks outperforms all BERT models on ClueWeb, indicating that pre-training on user interactions is more effective for ad hoc tasks than on surrounding context.

The paper asserts that BERT-based rankers excel on MS MARCO due to their ability to leverage cross-query-document attentions, which is absent in simpler BERT applications. Despite the complexity of models like BERT (Multi-Int) and BERT (Term-Trans), the simpler BERT (Last-Int) achieves the best results, suggesting that excessive modification during fine-tuning may hinder performance.

The study also investigates the attention mechanisms within BERT, particularly focusing on the BERT (Last-Int) model. It categorizes terms in passages into three groups: Markers, Stopwords, and Regular Words. Results show that markers receive the most attention, crucial for distinguishing between text sequences, while stopwords are surprisingly given attention but do not contribute to performance. As layers deepen, attention becomes more distributed across tokens, enhancing contextual understanding but not necessarily improving global matching outcomes.

Overall, the findings emphasize the significance of pre-training strategies and the unique behaviors of BERT-based models across different ranking contexts.
