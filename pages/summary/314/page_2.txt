The paper discusses the implementation of BERT-based ranking models for text sequences, particularly focusing on the use of the “[CLS]” token for representation and the addition of task-specific layers during fine-tuning. Four models are explored: BERT (Rep), BERT (Last-Int), BERT (Mult-Int), and BERT (Term-Trans). Each model employs BERT's pre-trained embeddings to rank query-document pairs, with variations in how they utilize the “[CLS]” token and layer representations.

BERT (Rep) computes the cosine similarity between the last layer’s “[CLS]” embeddings of the query and document, making it a representation-based ranker. BERT (Last-Int) combines the last layer’s “[CLS]” embedding with weights to account for term pair interactions, functioning as an interaction-based ranker. BERT (Mult-Int) extends the Last-Int model by incorporating embeddings from all layers to capture diverse information. BERT (Term-Trans) enhances this by adding a neural ranking network that combines cosine similarities of contextual embeddings across layers.

The models are fine-tuned on the MS MARCO passage ranking task and the TREC Web Track ad hoc tasks, using classification loss to determine relevance. MS MARCO offers a large dataset for training, while ClueWeb provides queries and relevance judgments for validation. The evaluation metrics include MRR@10 for MS MARCO and NDCG@20 and ERR@20 for ClueWeb.

The paper compares BERT-based rankers against various baselines, including BM25 and neural rankers such as K-NRM and Conv-KNRM. Implementation details highlight the use of the Adam optimizer and specific learning rates, with convergence times varying across models. Overall, the study aims to enhance ranking performance through the strategic application of BERT embeddings and layers.
