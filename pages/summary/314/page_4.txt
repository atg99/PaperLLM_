This paper investigates the performance and behaviors of BERT in passage ranking tasks using the MS MARCO dataset and contrasts it with Conv-KNRM. The study focuses on how the removal of regular terms affects the ranking scores assigned by both models. The experiments reveal that BERT assigns more extreme ranking scores, with a tendency to categorize pairs into either high or low relevance, while Conv-KNRM's scores are more uniformly distributed. Notably, certain terms significantly influence BERT's scores, particularly those closely related to the query, indicating that BERT's training emphasizes semantic proximity.

Conversely, Conv-KNRM shows a preference for loosely related terms, suggesting a different matching strategy. The analysis of influential terms indicates that BERT excels in tasks with a question-answering focus, like MS MARCO, but struggles with TREC ad hoc ranking, where user click data may offer better pre-training signals. The findings underscore BERT's capability as a strong matching model, though its reliance on surrounding context may limit its effectiveness in certain retrieval scenarios. The paper concludes by proposing future research directions, particularly the need to explore deeper networks trained on relevance-based labels for improved performance in ad hoc ranking tasks.
