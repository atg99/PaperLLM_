This paper investigates the performance and behavior of BERT in ranking tasks, focusing on its application in the MS MARCO passage reranking and TREC Web Track ad hoc document ranking tasks. The authors demonstrate that BERT excels in question-answering tasks, showing strong effectiveness when fine-tuned for passage ranking, primarily due to its ability to model interactions between queries and documents. However, the results for TREC indicate that BERT's pre-training on surrounding contexts does not align well with the requirements of ad hoc document ranking, leading to inferior performance compared to traditional feature-based models and those trained on user interactions.

The study highlights BERT's unique properties, such as its large capacity and extensive pre-training on context signals from vast corpora, which contribute to its strong performance in certain ranking scenarios. The authors analyze BERT's attention mechanisms, revealing that it focuses on direct query-document term matches, reflecting a semantic matching approach that differs from the user click-driven relevance patterns typically learned by other neural rankers.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the strengths and limitations of BERT in different ranking contexts, suggesting avenues for future research to better adapt its capabilities to various ranking tasks.
