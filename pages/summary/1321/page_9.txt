The study evaluates the performance of models in classifying chest X-ray (CXR) images for COVID-19, various types of pneumonia, and healthy cases. The false positive rate (FPR), also referred to as specificity, indicates the proportion of negative samples incorrectly identified as positive. The evaluation involves binary classifications (COVID-19 vs. healthy, non-COVID-19 pneumonia vs. healthy, etc.) and multiclass classifications. 

Data was split into 70% for training and 30% for testing, using a dataset of 371 CXR images. Initial results showed low accuracy, sensitivity, and specificity due to limited data. After training with balanced datasets, the model achieved notable accuracy: 94.43% for non-COVID-19 viral pneumonia vs. healthy, 91.43% for bacterial pneumonia vs. healthy, and 99.16% for COVID-19 vs. healthy images. 

The model performed particularly well in distinguishing COVID-19 from non-COVID-19 viral pneumonia, achieving 99.62% accuracy. However, when evaluating a three-way classification (COVID-19, bacterial pneumonia, and healthy), the accuracy dropped to 94.00%. The hypothesis suggests that further classification into four categories will likely result in even lower performance due to the similarities between the pneumonia types.

Overall, the study highlights the challenges in accurately diagnosing COVID-19 through CXR images and the importance of training models with diverse datasets to improve classification performance.
