The paper presents a model for detecting COVID-19 pneumonia using chest X-ray (CXR) images, achieving significant performance metrics. Specifically, the model attained an accuracy of 91.94%, sensitivity of 100%, and specificity of 100% for distinguishing COVID-19 pneumonia from healthy CXR images. In comparisons of COVID-19 pneumonia with non-COVID-19 viral pneumonia, the model recorded a testing accuracy of 99.62%, sensitivity of 90.63%, and specificity of 99.89%. For multiclass classifications involving COVID-19, bacterial pneumonia, and healthy images, the model demonstrated a testing accuracy of 94.00%, with sensitivity and specificity values of 91.30% and 84.78%, respectively. In a four-way classification scenario, it achieved a testing accuracy of 93.42%, sensitivity of 89.18%, and specificity of 98.92%.

The findings suggest that computer-aided detection can serve as a viable alternative or supplementary method to RT-PCR testing, which has limitations in sensitivity and efficiency. However, the study acknowledges the limitation of a small dataset of COVID-19 pneumonia, which restricts the generalizability of results. Future work aims to enhance the model by utilizing larger datasets and deeper neural networks, such as GoogleNet and ResNet, and exploring hybrid models that combine CNNs with support vector machines and regression.

The paper concludes with a note on ethical standards, confirming that no human participant studies were involved.
