This paper discusses the methodologies used for feature extraction and image classification within a specific model, employing stochastic gradient descent with momentum. The training process is limited to a maximum of 20 epochs, with an initial learning rate set at 0.0001. 

To assess the trained models' performance, three key metrics are utilized: accuracy, sensitivity, and specificity. Accuracy is defined as the ratio of correctly classified images to the total number of images, also represented as the sum of sensitivity and specificity. The evaluation employs formulas for calculating loss and accuracy, with a confusion matrix serving as a primary tool for performance evaluation. This matrix categorizes model outcomes into true positives (TP), true negatives (TN), false positives (FP), and false negatives (FN).

True positive rate, or sensitivity, specifically measures the proportion of actual positive cases that are correctly identified. The document includes a dataset split for various categories, including bacterial pneumonia, viral pneumonia, and COVID-19, indicating the distribution of samples used for training and testing the models. The dataset consists of a variety of cases, highlighting the complexity and diversity of the images analyzed.
