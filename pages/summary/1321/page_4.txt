The paper discusses the application of deep learning (DL) models, particularly convolutional neural networks (CNNs), in the field of biomedical diagnostics, focusing on detecting diseases like cancer and COVID-19. DL models are noted for their ability to uncover hidden features in medical images that may not be discernible to human experts. The AlexNet model, introduced by Alex Krizhevsky, is highlighted as a significant advancement in this area, achieving 84% accuracy in the 2012 ImageNet competition. AlexNet employs a distinctive architecture featuring five convolutional layers, three fully connected layers, and utilizes the rectified linear unit (ReLU) activation function, which enhances the model's ability to learn complex patterns.

The paper identifies challenges in the current diagnostic methods for COVID-19, emphasizing the need for rapid and accurate detection systems amid rising patient numbers. Traditional molecular testing methods, while effective, are criticized for being costly and prone to errors, particularly in underdeveloped regions. The paper advocates for computer-aided detection systems using DL approaches as a more efficient alternative, capable of addressing the limitations of conventional methods. Although most existing research primarily focuses on binary classification (e.g., COVID-19 vs. normal), there is a call for more studies that explore multi-class classification to improve diagnostic accuracy across various pathogen strains.
