The paper discusses the emergence and impact of COVID-19, beginning with its identification in Wuhan, China, on December 31, 2019, and the subsequent declaration of a pandemic by WHO on March 11, 2020. COVID-19 is transmitted through respiratory droplets and primarily affects the lungs, leading to symptoms like cough, fever, and pneumonia. The pandemic has highlighted the absence of approved treatments and vaccines, prompting governments to implement measures such as lockdowns and social distancing to control the spread.

The paper also reviews past coronavirus outbreaks, including SARS-CoV-1 in 2002 and MERS-CoV in 2012, noting their global spread and significant mortality rates. The authors emphasize the urgent need for vaccines and treatments for COVID-19. Various vaccine technologies are under evaluation, and while some countries have approved candidates, concerns about their efficacy and testing processes remain.

Research is ongoing to repurpose existing antiviral drugs, with artificial intelligence (AI) and machine learning playing a crucial role in identifying potential treatments. Studies have highlighted drugs like Atazanavir and Nelfinavir as promising candidates against COVID-19. Additionally, AI-driven models have been employed for accurate data analysis and prediction of COVID-19 case numbers, enhancing the ability of medical experts to respond to the pandemic effectively.

The paper concludes with an overview of deep learning, a subset of machine learning inspired by the human brain's structure, which has gained prominence in analyzing health data and forecasting disease trends during the COVID-19 pandemic.
