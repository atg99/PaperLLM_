The paper discusses the effectiveness of various AI-driven models in diagnosing COVID-19 and differentiating it from other types of pneumonia using imaging techniques such as CT scans and chest X-rays. Key findings from multiple studies are summarized:

1. **COVID-19 Detection**:
   - Li et al. employed a three-dimensional deep learning framework (COVNet) on 4352 CT scans, achieving 90% sensitivity and 96% specificity for COVID-19 detection, and 87% sensitivity and 92% specificity for community-acquired pneumonia (CAP).
   - Apostolopoulos et al. used transfer learning on 1427 X-ray images, resulting in 96.78% accuracy and high sensitivity and specificity for both COVID-19 and bacterial pneumonia.

2. **X-ray Image Classification**:
   - Abbas et al. utilized the DeTraC CNN model, achieving 95.12% accuracy, 97.91% sensitivity, and 91.87% specificity using diverse datasets.
   - Narin et al. applied two deeper models to 100 X-ray images, recording 97% accuracy with InceptionV3 and 87% with Inception-ResNetV2.

3. **Comparative Accuracy of Models**:
   - The results indicate varying degrees of success across different models and datasets, with some achieving high accuracy in distinguishing between COVID-19, non-COVID-19 viral pneumonia, and bacterial pneumonia.

The paper concludes that AI-driven models demonstrate promising capabilities in accurately classifying and detecting COVID-19, highlighting the potential for these technologies in clinical diagnostics.
