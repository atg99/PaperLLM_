The paper presents a comparative analysis of a classification model designed to differentiate between COVID-19, bacterial pneumonia, non-COVID-19 viral pneumonia, and healthy chest X-ray (CXR) images. It evaluates the model's performance using three-way and four-way classifications, focusing on key metrics such as accuracy, sensitivity, and specificity.

For the three-way classification (COVID-19, bacterial pneumonia, and healthy images), the model achieved an accuracy of 94.00%, sensitivity of 91.30%, and specificity of 84.78%. In contrast, the four-way classification (including non-COVID-19 viral pneumonia) yielded an accuracy of 93.42%, sensitivity of 89.18%, and a notably high specificity of 98.92%.

The model's performance was benchmarked against state-of-the-art results. In the binary classification scenario of non-COVID-19 viral pneumonia versus healthy datasets, the model achieved a testing accuracy of 94.43%, sensitivity of 98.19%, and specificity of 95.78%. Additionally, for the classification of COVID-19 against healthy datasets, it recorded an accuracy of 99.16%, sensitivity of 97.44%, and perfect specificity of 100%.

Overall, the findings suggest that the proposed model demonstrates competitive performance in distinguishing between various pneumonia types and healthy cases, with results surpassing several existing benchmarks in accuracy and sensitivity.
