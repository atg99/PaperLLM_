7.2 Model Compression
Model compression techniques aim to reduce the size of pretraining models without sacrificing performance. This can be achieved through methods such as pruning, quantization, and knowledge distillation. Pruning involves removing less important parameters from the model, while quantization reduces the precision of the parameters, thereby decreasing the model size. Knowledge distillation entails training a smaller model (the student) to replicate the performance of a larger model (the teacher), allowing for a more compact model that retains much of the original's capabilities.

In summary, the advancements in masked data modeling for unimodal and multimodal data have led to the development of robust models like BEIT-3, FLA V A, and UNIMO, each utilizing unique methodologies to enhance performance on vision, language, and vision-language tasks. As the need for efficiency in parameter-heavy models grows, strategies focusing on model efficiency and compression are critical in making these powerful models more accessible and deployable in resource-constrained environments.
