The referenced works explore advancements in language and vision models, particularly focusing on alignment and pre-training techniques. Singh et al. propose FLAVA, a foundational model for language and vision alignment, while Wang et al. introduce BEiT pre-training for various vision tasks, treating images as a foreign language. Clark et al. present ELECTRA, a novel approach to pre-training text encoders as discriminators. 

Wallace et al. investigate adversarial example generation for question answering, and Nie et al. introduce a benchmark for natural language understanding through adversarial NLI. Niven and Kao probe neural networks' comprehension of natural language arguments, while Gordon et al. study the effects of weight pruning on BERT for transfer learning.

Other notable contributions include ALBERT for self-supervised learning, a survey on attention mechanisms in computer vision, and the use of transformers for image recognition at scale. The recent work by Mostafa Dehghani et al. discusses scaling vision transformers to 22 billion parameters, indicating a significant trend towards larger and more capable models in both language and vision domains.
