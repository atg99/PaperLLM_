The section discusses pre-trained feature models (PFMs) for speech and video, highlighting advancements in representation learning. For speech, models like wav2vec, vq-wav2vec, and wav2vec 2.0 leverage large-scale unlabeled datasets to capture contextual information, improving downstream tasks such as automatic speech recognition and speech synthesis. Joint pretraining of speech and text is exemplified by SpeechBERT, which employs masked language modeling on speech-text pairs, while SPLAT utilizes unlabeled data for label-level alignment. MusicBERT targets music data, incorporating unique encoding and masking strategies to enhance pretraining. Additionally, AudioTransformer improves Transformer architectures through techniques from convolutional networks.

In the realm of video, self-supervised learning techniques such as Odd-One-Out Networks and Shufﬂe and Learn focus on predicting sequences and temporal order, respectively. The Space-Time Cubic Puzzles task trains 3D CNNs to arrange permuted video segments, demonstrating effective representation learning. Contrastive learning approaches like the Inter-Intra Contrastive framework and Temporal Contrastive Pretraining apply positive and negative pair strategies to enhance video representation for various tasks. Sequence Contrastive Learning further innovates by addressing instance discrimination in sequence-based tasks. Overall, these models exhibit significant potential for advancing representation learning in both speech and video domains.
