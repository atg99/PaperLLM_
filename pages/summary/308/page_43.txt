The paper discusses the basic components of Natural Language Processing (NLP) and introduces common notations used in NLP and graph theory. Key notations include N for the length of input text, wi for the i-th word, and various graph-related notations such as G for the set of graphs and E for the set of edges.

The section on Language Models (LMs) highlights their growing importance in NLP, especially with the rise of deep learning. It differentiates between two main types of LMs: statistical and neural network LMs. The statistical LM focuses on the probabilistic characteristics of natural language, primarily through the N-gram model, which operates on the Markov hypothesis. This hypothesis posits that the probability of a word's occurrence depends only on the preceding n-1 words. The paper provides formulas for calculating the maximum likelihood probability of a word and emphasizes the trade-off between the value of N in N-gram models—larger N offers stronger constraints but leads to sparser data, while smaller N results in greater reliability and generalization.
