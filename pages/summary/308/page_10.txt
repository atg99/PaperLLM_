The paper discusses the use of contrastive learning for pretraining encoder networks, focusing on maximizing the distance between negative pairs and minimizing the distance between positive pairs. Specifically, the DeepClustering method is employed to categorize representations into clusters, providing supervised signals for training the backbone network.

In the context of Graph Learning (GL), the pretraining tasks are categorized into several types, emphasizing their design as either supervised or unsupervised. The tasks include:

1. **Graph Information Completion**: This involves masking part of the input graph and recovering the masked information based on the remaining data distribution, akin to tasks in computer vision and natural language processing.

2. **Graph Property Prediction**: This task utilizes node attributes and connectivity information to generate self-supervised signals, offering both regression and classification tasks through pseudo-labeling.

3. **Graph Consistency Analysis**: The objective here is to enhance consistency among semantically similar samples while reducing agreement among unrelated samples, with variations based on training strategies.

4. **Miscellaneous**: Some methods integrate multiple pretext tasks into a single framework to leverage their combined advantages, while others utilize unique self-supervised signals from specific graph data.

The paper highlights the transformer’s role in large model architectures for feature learning and intrinsic structure mining within data. It notes that different training mechanisms, particularly self-supervised learning (SSL) and reinforcement learning (RL), are crucial for effectively fine-tuning pre-trained models (PFMs) for downstream tasks. The design of efficient tasks for PFMs is identified as a significant research area.

The paper also outlines the relevance of PFMs in Natural Language Processing (NLP), which encompasses various tasks like part-of-speech tagging and machine translation. Initially popular in NLP, the PFM concept has been adopted in computer vision and graph learning, facilitating the training on large datasets followed by fine-tuning for specific tasks. This approach enhances the modeling of both syntactic and semantic representations of language.
