The paper discusses the conceptual architecture of Pretrained Foundation Models (PFMs) and their implications in the era of big data. It outlines the challenges and opportunities for future research, promoting the development of collaborative and interactive intelligence. The structure of the survey includes an introduction to basic components, a summary of PFMs in Natural Language Processing (NLP), Computer Vision (CV), and Graph Learning (GL), and advanced research topics such as model efficiency, compression, security, and privacy. 

Section 2 highlights the foundational aspects of PFMs, emphasizing their role as extensive neural network models that process information. The Transformer model is identified as a predominant architecture used in PFMs, particularly in NLP and CV. Training these large models necessitates diverse datasets for pretraining, followed by fine-tuning to meet specific requirements like efficacy and privacy. 

In discussing the Transformer, the paper explains its reliance on attention mechanisms to transfer knowledge among neural units, bypassing traditional recurrent or convolutional architectures. The attention mechanism plays a pivotal role by assigning weights to input representations, allowing the model to focus on the most relevant data. Various attention mechanisms, such as self-attention in NLP, are explored, underscoring their significance in generating effective representations. 

Overall, the survey aims to provide a comprehensive overview of PFMs, their architectures, and the associated challenges, setting the stage for future advancements in the field.
