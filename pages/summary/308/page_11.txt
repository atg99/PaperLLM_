Permuted Language Model (PLM) offers a different approach by rearranging the input sequence to predict masked words, allowing for a more holistic understanding of context. This model can leverage the relationships between words in a non-linear manner, which aids in tasks that require a deeper understanding of semantic relationships.

The paper discusses the advancements in pretrained language models (PFMs) and their effectiveness in various natural language tasks, highlighting the importance of word representation learning methods. It categorizes these methods into autoregressive, contextual, and permuted language models, each with distinct advantages. The autoregressive model, such as GPT, excels in generating coherent text but relies on sequential context. The contextual model, like ELMO, captures bidirectional context, improving word representation. The PLM uses masked prediction to understand word relationships more dynamically.

Additionally, the paper outlines the architectures and methods for enhancing PFM performance, including multi-task learning and instruction-aligned techniques like Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) and Chain-of-Thought prompting. These methods help align model outputs with human preferences, reducing harmful responses and improving overall utility in applications like ChatGPT. 

In summary, the paper emphasizes the evolution and capabilities of PFMs, showcasing their potential to surpass human performance in specific language tasks through advanced word representation techniques and innovative learning strategies.
