The paper discusses advancements in self-supervised learning (SSL) techniques for graph neural networks (GNNs), focusing on methods for enhancing feature representation through Graph Consistency Analysis (GCA). It highlights several specific tasks, such as GraphCompetion, AttributeMask, and EdgeMask, which aim to improve the model's ability to reconstruct masked information—node attributes and edges—by leveraging the underlying structure of the graph.

GraphCompetion utilizes unmasked node features to support GCNs, particularly in the context of second-order neighbors, to enhance feature extraction. AttributeMask involves randomly masking node attributes and requiring the model to reconstruct them, while EdgeMask focuses on the relationships between nodes by randomly masking edges and asking the model to predict these masked connections, thereby enhancing local connectivity learning.

The paper distinguishes GCA from previous methods by emphasizing the consistency of semantic distributions among graph elements. It proposes that elements with similar semantics should exhibit stronger consistency than unrelated ones, which can be harnessed for pretraining. The paper categorizes GCA methods into three types, with context consistency being a prominent focus. Here, techniques like DeepWalk and node2vec are discussed, which employ random walk strategies to capture node distribution characteristics effectively. These methods treat node sequences akin to sentences in natural language processing, facilitating unsupervised training for node representations.

Overall, the paper illustrates how these various approaches contribute to the development of more robust GNNs by enhancing their ability to learn from the graph's intrinsic structure and relationships.
