The paper discusses non-contrastive self-supervised learning (SSL) methods, highlighting their effectiveness compared to traditional contrastive learning. Notable methods like BYOL and SimSiam achieve state-of-the-art performance without relying on negative pair comparisons. Tian et al. analyze the eigenspaces and demonstrate that non-contrastive training benefits from both the predictor and stop-gradient signals, leading to the introduction of a new method called DirectPred.

Additionally, the paper categorizes pretext tasks used in computer vision into five groups: 

1. **Generation-Based Methods**: These include GANs and applications such as image colorization, super-resolution, and video generation tasks like future prediction.

2. **Transformation-Based Methods**: These methods augment training data through transformations, labeling transformed versions of the same image as positive samples for self-supervised pretraining. Common transformations include color and geometric adjustments.

3. **Context-Based Methods**: These involve artificial tasks like solving Jigsaw puzzles and context similarity comparisons, helping models learn effective feature representations.

4. **Semantic-Based Methods**: Tasks such as object detection and semantic segmentation fall under this category, utilizing pixel-based labels for robust feature learning, often using video datasets.

5. **View-Based Methods**: These methods generate positive samples from different viewpoints of single-modal data and can involve multi-modal data, exemplifying audio-video cooperative learning and cross-modal training.

The paper emphasizes the diversity of approaches within SSL and the potential of non-contrastive methods to advance the field.
