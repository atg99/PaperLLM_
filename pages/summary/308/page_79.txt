This compilation of references highlights significant contributions to the field of unsupervised and self-supervised learning for visual representations. Key works include local aggregation techniques for visual embeddings, the development of pretext-invariant representations, and momentum contrast methods that enhance unsupervised learning. Researchers have explored various frameworks, such as contrastive learning, which contrasts cluster assignments and improves generalization of visual features.

Several studies focus on self-supervised pretraining and representation learning, emphasizing mutual information maximization across different views and the importance of good view selection for contrastive learning. Noteworthy innovations include the Bootstrap Your Own Latent method and the exploration of simple Siamese representation learning.

The references also address practical applications, such as data augmentation techniques that enhance model training without extensive search efforts. Overall, these works collectively advance the understanding and implementation of representation learning in computer vision, paving the way for more robust and efficient unsupervised learning strategies.
