Attention-Based Networks have gained traction in computer vision (CV) by integrating attention modules with convolutional neural networks (CNNs). Notable examples include SENet, which introduced a channel attention module and achieved top results in the ILSVRC2017 competition, and CBAM, which generates attention maps in both channel and spatial dimensions. Several innovative models, such as GCNet and CCNet, leverage soft-attention mechanisms, yielding superior performance in recognition and segmentation tasks compared to traditional CNNs. The self-attention mechanism computes the relevance of each position in a sequence to others, enhancing feature map analysis. Key components like masked self-attention and multi-head attention have emerged to replace convolutions in transformer architectures.

Transformer-Based Networks have also emerged in CV, drawing inspiration from the self-attention mechanism and the success of transformers in natural language processing (NLP). These models typically employ a two-stage training process: pretraining on large, unlabeled datasets through pretext tasks, followed by fine-tuning on specific downstream tasks. The Vision Transformer (ViT) has set state-of-the-art (SOTA) benchmarks in CV, while Data-efficient image Transformers (DeiT) have optimized training efficiency. Other models like DEtection TRansformer (DETR) and LeViT have excelled in object detection and segmentation tasks. Additionally, DeepViT explores deeper architectures for improved performance, and specialized transformers like Pyramid Vision Transformer (PVT) and Medical Transformer (MedT) have been developed for specific applications, demonstrating the versatility and effectiveness of transformers in CV.

Traditional Graph Learning (GL) focuses on embedding graphs into low-dimensional representations while preserving essential properties. Classical GL methods often utilize statistical approaches or custom-designed components. Dimension reduction is a key technique in feature engineering, aiming to simplify high-dimensional graph data into lower-dimensional forms while retaining critical information, albeit at the expense of some attributes. Dimension reduction strategies can be categorized into two main types, emphasizing the balance between information retention and dimensionality reduction.
