In summary, the discussed methods leverage different strategies for enhancing node and graph representation learning through self-supervised and contrastive learning techniques. LINE employs first- and second-order proximity along with negative sampling to optimize node representation effectively. VGAE uses a variational autoencoder to encode graph data while modeling node relationships. Contrastive learning is applied in various studies, such as GCC, which focuses on subgraph instance discrimination, and GCA, which incorporates topological and semantic priors for adaptive augmentation. GraphCL introduces diverse augmentation strategies for graph-level tasks, demonstrating the importance of data augmentation in comparative learning. Additionally, methods like DGI and CMVRL maximize mutual information across different scales of graph data to enhance representation learning. Overall, these approaches contribute to a deeper understanding of graph structures and improve the performance of graph-based models.
