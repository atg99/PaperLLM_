Text summarization is a critical method for condensing single or multiple documents while preserving essential content. It can be categorized into two main types: extractive summarization, which selects and combines key sentences from the source text, and generative summarization, where a model creates summaries based on the content of the documents.

Several datasets are utilized in this field. The NYT dataset comprises 9,076 articles annotated with named entities using the Stanford NER tool, divided into training and validation sets. The CNN/Daily Mail dataset, popular for assessing automated text summarization systems, features news stories paired with human-generated summaries, totaling 287,226 training instances. The Gigaword dataset includes nearly 950 English news articles with concise headlines.

Machine translation, another essential area, involves translating text between languages while maintaining semantic equivalence. It encompasses three main approaches: rule-based, statistical, and neural network-based translation. The WMT datasets from various years provide resources for machine translation tasks, including news and biomedical translation, quality estimation, and automatic post-editing.

Dialogue systems, which facilitate human-machine interaction, are classified into task-oriented and non-task-oriented systems, such as chatbots. The DSTC2 dataset focuses on multi-round dialogues within the restaurant reservation context, accommodating changes in user goals, making it valuable for evaluating the adaptability of dialogue systems.
