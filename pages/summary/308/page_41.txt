The paper discusses the limitations and challenges faced by pretrained models (PFMs) in various fields, particularly in natural language processing (NLP). It highlights that small datasets hinder the stability and adaptability of models for different downstream tasks, compromising their effectiveness in real-world language applications.

One major challenge in model design is the balance between data availability, computational resources, and predictive performance. While there have been various model designs, such as generation-based models in computer vision, GAN-based approaches struggle due to issues like feature representation loss and mode collapse, which impede their effectiveness in self-supervised learning for pretraining.

Model compression is another significant concern, as the increasing complexity and size of models like BERT and GPT-3 require substantial hardware resources, creating barriers for researchers. Although models like ALBERT demonstrate improved efficiency with fewer parameters, they still necessitate powerful computing capabilities, complicating universal application.

Robustness is a critical issue, as existing pretext tasks may not adequately assess model performance before extensive computations. Additionally, NLP models are particularly susceptible to adversarial attacks due to their reliance on deep neural networks, which exhibit vulnerabilities not seen in computer vision tasks. This necessitates advancements in the robustness and anti-attack capabilities of PFMs.

Lastly, while pretrained models perform well in upstream tasks, achieving consistent results in downstream tasks during fine-tuning and prompting remains a challenge, underscoring the need for further research in this area.
