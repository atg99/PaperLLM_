The paper discusses various evaluation metrics used to assess the performance of algorithms in tasks such as Question Answering (QA) and Information Retrieval (IR). Key metrics include Precision (Pt), Recall (Rt), and Mean Reciprocal Rank (MRR), with MRR being calculated based on the ranks of ground-truth answers. Additionally, it highlights Generation Tasks, where Language Models (LMs) predict subsequent words or sentences. 

The paper also outlines the Bilingual Evaluation Understudy (BLEU) metric, which evaluates machine translation by comparing generated sentences with reference sentences, focusing on N-gram overlap. BLEU uses a penalty factor (BP) to address discrepancies in translation lengths. 

Furthermore, ROUGE (Recall-Oriented Understudy for Gisting Evaluation) is introduced, emphasizing N-gram co-occurrence statistics for automatic evaluation. ROUGE comprises different types, with ROUGE-N and ROUGE-L being the most utilized. ROUGE-N focuses on recall, contrasting with BLEU's accuracy approach, while ROUGE-L measures the Longest Common Subsequence between candidate and reference texts. These metrics collectively provide comprehensive tools for assessing the quality of language generation and translation tasks.
