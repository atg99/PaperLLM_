The paper discusses advancements in contrastive learning frameworks, specifically focusing on Momentum Contrast (MoCo), Bootstrap Your Own Latent (BYOL), and SimCLR. MoCo, developed by Facebook AI Research, employs a two-stream architecture with distinct parameters. It utilizes a dictionary look-up task for one encoder, generating a queue of encoded samples, while the second encoder produces encoded queries. The similarity between the new encoded query and the stored keys is measured using the dot product, treating the stored keys as negative samples. MoCo employs InfoNCE Loss to combine contributions from both negative and positive samples, with a key feature being the momentum update mechanism that ensures consistency between the encoders' parameters.

MoCo v2 enhances performance by introducing an MLP projection head and incorporating more data augmentation techniques. This approach bridges the gap between unsupervised and supervised learning through an embedded linear classifier and increases the number of contrastive samples by utilizing larger training batches and stronger augmentations.

BYOL, proposed by DeepMind, takes a different approach by eliminating negative samples entirely. It consists of representation, projection, and discrimination stages, focusing on preventing collapse during pretraining by comparing predictions from an online network against a regression target from a target network. The target network is updated similarly to MoCo's momentum encoder, and the decay rate controls parameter changes.

SimCLR, developed by Google Research, uses a hard parameter-sharing architecture, representing another variant in the contrastive learning landscape. Each of these frameworks showcases unique methods to improve representation learning while addressing challenges associated with negative sample reliance and the consistency of encoder parameters.
