Named Entity Recognition (NER) is a crucial task in Natural Language Processing (NLP) that involves identifying entities such as people, locations, organizations, and other relevant items within text. NER serves as a foundational tool for various NLP applications including information extraction, question answering, semantic parsing, and machine translation.

Several datasets are pivotal for NER tasks. The CoNLL 2003 dataset comprises 1,393 English and 909 German news articles, categorizing entities into four types: Location, Organization, Person, and Miscellaneous. OntoNotes 5.0 encompasses a broader spectrum with 174,500 English, 900,000 Chinese, and 300,000 Arabic texts, featuring 18 entity classes derived from diverse sources like telephone conversations and news articles. The MSRA dataset, focused on the Chinese language, includes three entity types and was utilized in the SIGNAN shared task in 2006.

In Question Answering (QA), two main system types exist: extractive and generative. The Microsoft Research Paraphrase Corpus (MRPC) contains 5,800 sentence pairs aimed at determining semantic similarity. The Stanford Question Answering Dataset (SQuAD) includes two versions: SQuAD 1.1, with 100,000 samples, and SQuAD 2.0, which expands this to 150,000 samples by introducing unanswered questions. The RACE dataset, derived from educational tests, consists of nearly 100,000 questions with an average text length exceeding 300 words.

For Dialog Act Classification (DAC), which categorizes dialogue based on meaning to interpret speaker intent, several datasets are essential. The Dialog State Tracking Challenge 4 (DSTC 4) focuses on human-human dialogues, featuring 89 training classes with 24,000 training and 6,000 test texts. The ICSI Meeting Recorder Dialog Act (MRDA) includes 75 hours of speech from 75 meetings, categorized into five types, with 51,000 training texts and additional validation and test texts. The Switchboard Dialog Act (SwDA) dataset extends dialogue labeling with discourse elements, containing 1,003,000 training texts and relevant test and validation sets across 43 categories.
