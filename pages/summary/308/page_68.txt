Graph Classification (GC) is a methodology employed in various fields such as social networks, molecular biology, and protein analysis, aimed at predicting the properties of specific communities, chemical compounds, and proteins. The paper presents statistics of multiple datasets utilized for GC, categorized into homogeneous and heterogeneous types. Key datasets include ZINC15 and ChEMBL for pretraining on molecular data, and several downstream datasets like MUTAG, PTC, and Tox21, which target different classifications of molecular properties. The paper also discusses citation and co-author networks, which reflect the relationships among research papers and collaborations, and their applications in node and graph classification tasks. Additionally, it elaborates on molecular networks that represent compounds for property prediction and protein networks that categorize proteins based on their structure. Overall, the paper underscores the significance of GC in predicting various properties across diverse datasets.
