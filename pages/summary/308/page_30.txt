The paper discusses techniques for enhancing graph neural networks (GNNs) through the use of pooled central node and subgraph representations. It highlights the importance of distinguishing positive samples, such as context subgraphs for specified nodes, from negative samples through contrastive loss in the latent space. This approach emphasizes the regional structural information of nodes.

The paper also explores learning via graph property prediction (GPP), which utilizes both attribute and structural information within a graph for pretraining. GPP can be categorized into two main tasks: property regression (PR) and property classification (PC).

In property regression, the focus is on uncovering the relationships between numerical structures and property attributes. For instance, the NodeProperty method aims to predict node characteristics like degree and local clustering coefficient, thereby encouraging GNNs to capture local structural information relevant to downstream tasks.

Conversely, property classification involves generating pseudo-labels based on distributions within the graph data. Clustering methods are particularly effective for this purpose. The paper references several strategies, including M3S, which uses graph clustering for iterative training, and Node Clustering, which assigns pseudo-labels based on attribute clustering. Additionally, Graph Partitioning divides nodes into subsets to minimize interconnections, aiding in the assignment of pseudo-labels.

The paper concludes by mentioning alternative methods for generating pseudo-labels based on statistical characteristics, specifically in molecular contexts where subgraph bonds and statistics guide GNN learning. This comprehensive approach to pretraining enhances the capability of GNNs in various applications.
