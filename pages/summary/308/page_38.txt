The paper discusses enhancements in the efficiency and security of pre-trained models (PFMs), particularly those based on the Transformer algorithm. It emphasizes that both effective training methods and more efficient model architectures can improve PFM efficiency by reducing the complexity of the Transformer.

Model compression is highlighted as a key strategy to decrease resource requirements and enhance computational efficiency. It is categorized into parameter compression and structure compression. Parameter compression techniques include:

1. **Parameter Pruning**: This involves removing redundant parameters by evaluating their importance, as demonstrated with BERT, which maintains performance while reducing size.
2. **Parameter Quantization**: This reduces the precision of model parameters, such as Q8BERT, which compresses parameters by fourfold with minimal performance loss.
3. **Low-Rank Decomposition**: This technique transforms high-dimensional parameter vectors into more efficient, sparse representations.
4. **Parameter Sharing**: This method reduces parameters through structured approaches, as seen in ALBERT, which employs cross-layer sharing and decomposition.

Structure compression encompasses compact networks, which involve designing new architectures to minimize parameters and calculations, and knowledge distillation, where a larger teacher model imparts knowledge to a smaller student model. An example is DistilBERT, which reduces BERT's size by 40% while retaining most of its language comprehension.

The paper also addresses security and privacy concerns associated with PFMs. It notes that deep neural networks, including PFMs, are vulnerable to adversarial samples that can mislead models into making incorrect predictions. Additionally, querying PFMs poses risks of data privacy leakage. The paper cites research showing that pre-trained language models can be attacked using natural adversarial samples, indicating a significant need for improving the security of language processing models.
