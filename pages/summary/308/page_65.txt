The paper discusses various datasets used for tasks in computer vision, specifically focusing on classification, detection, and segmentation. 

1. **VOC11 and VOC12**: These datasets are commonly utilized for segmentation tasks in the main competition.

2. **ADE20K**: This dataset includes 27,574 images from SUN and Places205, with comprehensive annotations for 707,868 objects across 3,688 categories. It also features detailed annotations for object parts and additional attributes, enhancing its value for research.

3. **NYU-Depth V2**: Comprising images and video sequences from 464 indoor scenes, this dataset includes 1,449 images with depth ground truth and RGB values, along with 407,024 unlabeled frames and additional class labels.

4. **Cityscapes**: This urban dataset contains dense semantic segmentation annotations for 5,000 images, focusing on vehicles, people, and construction, as well as coarser annotations for 20,000 images. It also includes videos with high-quality annotations.

5. **LVIS**: A large vocabulary instance segmentation dataset featuring over 1,200 categories from approximately 160,000 images, it includes more than 2 million high-quality instance segmentation masks.

6. **Densely Annotated Video Segmentation (DAVIS)**: Designed for video object segmentation analysis, this dataset features both semi-supervised and unsupervised video sequences with multiple annotated instances.

7. **Paris StreetView**: This dataset, aimed at image inpainting, has 14,900 training images and 100 testing images from street views in Paris, focusing on urban buildings.

8. **Moving-MNIST**: A video dataset based on MNIST for evaluating sequence prediction, it includes 10,000 sequences of two moving digits within a 64x64 patch.

9. **Yahoo Flickr Creative Commons 100 Million (YFCC100M)**: The largest public multimedia collection, it allows users to search for images and videos, with rich text information for each item, making it more of a multimedia library than a traditional dataset.

10. **Data in the Wild**: This concept reflects the shift towards generalized datasets in the self-supervised learning era, emphasizing the diversity and adaptability of data sources.

Overall, the paper highlights the significance of these datasets in advancing research in computer vision through varied tasks and annotated information.
