The paper discusses the significance of self-supervised learning (SSL) in mitigating the challenges posed by the high costs of data annotation in complex learning tasks. The proposed SSL framework enables the use of unlabeled data through self-supervision techniques, such as matching and contrasting data attributes. The general pipeline for SSL includes a pretraining phase where pretext tasks are established for encoder networks, allowing for the automatic generation of labels based on data characteristics.

In this pretraining stage, the encoder networks learn to solve pretext tasks, which can include various methods like Jigsaw puzzles or patch comparisons. The learned representations are then transferable to downstream supervised tasks like classification and detection. During the downstream phase, only a simple classifier, such as a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP), is trained while keeping the encoder parameters fixed, a process referred to as fine-tuning.

The section also highlights various specific pretext tasks used in computer vision (CV) for pretraining models, including context prediction, inpainting, and patch discrimination. These methods have been shown to effectively learn transferable representations, ultimately aiding in achieving comparable results across different tasks. The paper summarizes various pretext tasks and their applications in training powerful feature models (PFMs) in CV.
