The section on PFMs for multimodal data discusses two main categories: single-stream models and cross-stream models. 

Single-stream models integrate text and visual information at the outset. VisualBERT serves as an example, processing text and images simultaneously with a self-attention mechanism from Transformers. It utilizes masked language modeling (MLM) and sentence-image prediction for pretraining. Unicoder-VL follows a similar approach but enhances image label prediction by concatenating extracted features with position encodings. VL-BERT, another single-stream model, incorporates additional features such as region locations and fragment encodings in its image input.

Cross-stream models encode text and images separately before merging the information. ViLBERT exemplifies this approach, using a standard attention module based on Transformer architecture where each modality computes attention using its query. It employs two pretraining tasks: a mask task similar to BERT and a language-image matching task. DALL-E, developed by OpenAI, generates images from text prompts using a transformer-based architecture. Its successor, DALL-E 2, improves upon this with contrastive language-image pretraining (CLIP) and a diffusion model for image synthesis. OpenAI's GPT-4 represents a large-scale multimodal model showcasing advanced performance through reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF).

The paper highlights that multimodal data provides richer information, enhancing model performance, especially when combined with self-supervised learning (SSL) techniques. Cross and Learn introduces crossmodal information as a supervisory source, while Ren and Lee propose a multi-task network to leverage cross-domain data effectively, focusing on tasks like surface normal and depth predictions in RGB images, demonstrating superior performance across various benchmarks.
