The paper also examines the application of masked autoencoders (MAE) in graph learning, specifically through the MAGE framework. It highlights the effectiveness of the MGAE model, which employs partial network structures and a decoder that captures correlations between nodes. The GMAE model enhances this approach by integrating transformers and focusing on the reconstruction of masked node features. Additionally, MaskGAE provides theoretical support for masked graph modeling and proposes HGMAE, which introduces meta-path and adaptive attribute masking for heterogeneous graphs.

Further, the paper explores other innovative learning strategies, such as CG3, which uses semi-supervised consistency loss and graph generation loss to enhance node representation. Graph-Bert utilizes the attention mechanism to reconstruct node attributes and topological structures, while GMI extends mutual information concepts to graphs for improved representation learning. GPT-GNN proposes a self-supervised graph generation task that distinguishes between attribute and edge generation, thereby addressing the interplay between node attributes and graph topology.

In summary, the paper emphasizes the importance of addressing information loss in traditional feature learning methods and highlights the potential of these advanced techniques to enhance graph representation learning.
