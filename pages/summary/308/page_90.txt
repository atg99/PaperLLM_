The references listed encompass a range of studies focused on advancements in image and video processing through machine learning techniques, particularly generative adversarial networks (GANs) and reinforcement learning. 

Key works include Ledig et al. who propose a GAN for photo-realistic single image super-resolution, and Perarnau et al. who introduce invertible conditional GANs for image editing. Vondrick et al. and Tulyakov et al. explore video generation, with Tulyakov et al. specifically addressing motion and content decomposition in their work on MoCoGAN.

Wang and Gupta emphasize unsupervised learning of visual representations from videos, while Wei et al. and Ahsan et al. delve into unsupervised representation learning through jigsaw puzzles and spatiotemporal context in video action recognition, respectively. Pathak et al. and Croitoru et al. focus on learning from object movement and detecting foreground objects in single images.

Self-supervised learning is further highlighted by Sermanet et al. with time-contrastive networks, and Korbar et al. who investigate the synchronization of audio and video models. In reinforcement learning, Stadie et al. and Achiam et al. address intrinsic motivation and exploration strategies, with Pathak et al. discussing curiosity-driven exploration.

Overall, the references collectively showcase innovative methodologies that leverage self-supervised learning and reinforcement learning to enhance visual understanding and action recognition in complex environments.
