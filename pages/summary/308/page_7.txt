The paper discusses the significance of the Transformer architecture in various fields such as Natural Language Processing (NLP), Computer Vision (CV), and Graph Learning (GL). It highlights the use of a mask matrix within the self-attention mechanism of Transformers, which allows for selective visibility among words in a sequence. This capability addresses long-range dependency issues in NLP, exemplified by models like GPT-3, which operates on a transformer-based framework. In CV, the Vision Transformer (ViT) represents images as sequences of patches akin to word embeddings, while Graph Transformer Networks (GTN) facilitate learning complex graph structures and node representations without prior domain knowledge. The scalability of Transformers enables the development of powerful models, with examples like the ViT-22B and language models exceeding 100 billion parameters.

The paper also explores learning mechanisms for Pre-trained Foundation Models (PFMs), emphasizing the superiority of deep learning over traditional methods in tasks such as classification and detection across CV, NLP, and GL. It outlines the framework for supervised learning, where a model learns to minimize a loss function based on labeled data. Additionally, it introduces semi-supervised learning, which leverages both labeled and unlabeled datasets to enhance model training. The process integrates pseudo-labels for unlabeled data through a relation function, while unsupervised and self-supervised learning methods focus on extracting intrinsic data properties when labels are absent. The latter approach is the primary focus of the detailed discussion in Section 4.3.
