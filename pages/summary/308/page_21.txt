This section of the paper discusses advancements in generative adversarial networks (GANs) and their applications in representation learning. It highlights the limitations of traditional GANs due to the lack of a feature encoder, which led to the development of Bidirectional Generative Adversarial Networks (BiGANs). BiGANs enhance representation capabilities by mapping data back to the latent space, facilitating supervised discrimination tasks.

Building on BiGANs, BigBiGAN achieves state-of-the-art performance in unsupervised representation learning on ImageNet by incorporating an encoder and modifying the discriminator. The framework utilizes traditional GAN components to produce data-latent pairs, with the loss function comprising data-specific and data-joint terms. The discriminator distinguishes between pairs derived from raw data, latent distributions, and encoded vectors.

The paper also examines learning by reconstruction through models like iGPT and ViT, which adapt masked prediction tasks from language processing to images. BEiT is introduced as the first model to surpass conventional methods by employing a two-stage process involving token embedding and tokenizer training via masked image prediction. However, the separation of these stages limits the framework's end-to-end efficiency.

To address this, MAE proposes a straightforward end-to-end solution that predicts masked patches from unmasked ones using a Mean Squared Error loss, employing a higher masking ratio of 75% compared to typical ratios in NLP. An ablation study indicates that higher masking ratios enhance performance in fine-tuning and linear probing. SimMIM presents a similar autoencoder-based approach, confirming the effectiveness of this strategy in representation learning.
