The paper presents a comprehensive summary of Pre-trained Foundation Models (PFMs) in Graph Learning (GL) from various conferences spanning from 2014 to 2022. Key methods discussed include DeepWalk, LINE, VGAE, node2vec, and many others, each associated with specific tasks like Graph Classification (GC-C), Graph Information Classification (GIC), and Graph Property Prediction (GPP). The summary includes details on the year of publication, conference, method, pretext task, encoder type, and links to the respective code repositories.

The paper emphasizes the evolution of graph models, highlighting a shift towards self-supervised learning approaches that leverage the distribution of graph data attributes. These models, particularly when pretrained, have shown significant advantages in unsupervised and semi-supervised scenarios. The potential of federated training is also noted as a promising avenue for developing robust pretrained models.

Furthermore, the paper discusses advancements in PFMs beyond traditional domains like text and images, extending to modalities such as speech, video, and cross-data applications. The growing interest in unified PFMs that integrate multiple data types is highlighted, indicating a trend towards more comprehensive modeling techniques in the field.
