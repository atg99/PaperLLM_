One key issue is the generation of adversarial samples, which must be carefully crafted to maintain linguistic integrity while still influencing model output. Techniques such as adversarial attacks during the fine-tuning phase of models like BERT have shown effectiveness in text classification and entailment tasks. Additionally, methods that combine sememe-based word substitution with optimization algorithms have been developed to generate adversarial samples.

Model defects also pose significant challenges, as human factors and misleading statistical information can impair performance, particularly in reasoning tasks. Universal adversarial triggers can be introduced to manipulate outputs across various inputs, raising concerns about the security and reliability of PFMs.

Backdoor attacks represent another critical vulnerability, where attackers can inject malicious weights into pretrained models, leading to manipulated predictions after fine-tuning. Methods to modify the model corpus can also influence the meanings of words, further complicating the reliability of PFMs.

In response to these threats, various defense mechanisms have been proposed. The human-in-the-loop approach aims to create more effective adversarial samples, while auxiliary classifiers and multi-task learning techniques have been designed to combat adversarial attacks. However, inherited defects from PFMs during transfer learning remain a concern, prompting the development of model slicing techniques to minimize these vulnerabilities.

Data privacy is another pressing issue, as large language models (LLMs) trained on private datasets can inadvertently memorize sensitive information. Researchers have demonstrated that querying LLMs can reveal individual training examples, including personally identifiable information. The sheer scale of parameters in larger models increases the risk of privacy breaches, necessitating robust privacy-preserving measures throughout the entire PFM lifecycle, from data processing to deployment.

In conclusion, while PFMs represent a significant advancement in artificial intelligence, ongoing research is essential to address the challenges of adversarial attacks, model vulnerabilities, and data privacy to ensure their safe and effective application in various domains.
