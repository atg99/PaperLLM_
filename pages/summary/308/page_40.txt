Explicability Exploration As PFMs are increasingly used in critical applications, understanding their decision-making processes becomes essential. Current models often function as "black boxes," making it difficult to interpret their outputs. Developing methods for explaining how PFMs arrive at their conclusions is a pressing challenge that needs to be addressed to ensure trust and accountability in their use.

8.3 Challenges on Model Design
The design of PFMs must evolve to accommodate the complexities of various data types and tasks. Key issues include model scalability, adaptability, and efficiency. Existing models often struggle to efficiently process large datasets or adapt to new tasks without substantial retraining. Innovative architectures and training methodologies are required to enhance model performance while managing computational resources effectively.

8.4 Challenges on Upstream and Downstream Tasks
PFMs must be integrated into broader systems that involve both upstream data processing and downstream application tasks. This integration poses challenges in ensuring that models are not only accurate but also efficient and user-friendly. There is a need for research focused on optimizing the pipeline from data acquisition to model deployment, emphasizing the importance of seamless interaction between different components of the system.

In conclusion, while PFMs show great promise, significant challenges remain in the areas of data, theoretical foundations, model design, and task integration. Addressing these challenges will be crucial for advancing the field and unlocking the full potential of pretrained models across various applications.
