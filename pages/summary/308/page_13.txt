The paper discusses advancements in pre-trained language models (PFMs), focusing on the architectures of BERT, GPT, and BART. BERT utilizes a bidirectional encoder to predict masked words and assess sentence relationships, though it limits generation capabilities due to its independent token prediction. GPT, on the other hand, employs an autoregressive decoder that predicts the next word based solely on preceding words, making it more suited for text generation but lacking bidirectional context.

BART is introduced as an innovative noise-reducing autoencoder that combines the strengths of both BERT and GPT. It utilizes a sequence-to-sequence model with a bidirectional Transformer encoder and an autoregressive decoder. BART's pretraining involves corrupting text with various noise patterns, such as word masking and sentence rearrangement, which enhances its performance in sequence generation and natural language reasoning tasks.

The paper also touches on the design of masking methods and the attention mechanism employed in these models. While BERT's bidirectional nature allows for comprehensive context understanding, it poses challenges for natural language generation tasks. Overall, these models represent significant steps forward in the field of PFMs, each contributing unique strengths to the understanding and generation of language.
