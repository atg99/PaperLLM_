The discussion highlights the challenges faced in graph learning, particularly the need for large-scale labeled datasets which are often costly and time-consuming to create. Although semi-supervised methods offer some relief, they do not fully address the issue of label dependence. As a response, researchers are exploring the use of Pretrained Feature Models (PFMs) in graph contexts, inspired by their effectiveness in computer vision and natural language processing.

The paper emphasizes the potential of PFMs to significantly reduce computational overhead through a "pretraining once and transferring forever" paradigm. This is particularly relevant as transformer-based PFMs have shown superior performance compared to traditional training methods. The exploration of GIC represents a promising avenue for enhancing graph learning by leveraging inherent graph properties such as node attributes and topological relationships, thereby improving the performance of graph embeddings.
