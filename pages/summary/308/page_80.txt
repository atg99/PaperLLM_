The collection of references presents various studies on self-supervised learning, particularly focusing on vision transformers and graph neural networks. Key contributions include empirical studies on training self-supervised vision transformers, the exploration of invariant causal mechanisms for representation learning, and insights into the dynamics of self-supervised learning without contrastive pairs. Several papers propose innovative frameworks such as the Masked Self-Supervised Transformer for visual representation and BEIT, which adapts BERT for image transformers.

Additionally, the references discuss the application of self-supervised learning in graph neural networks, with significant works addressing strategies for pre-training and enhancing graph representations through contrastive coding and adaptive augmentations. The evolution of methods, from DeepWalk and node2vec to more recent approaches like graph contrastive learning, illustrates the ongoing development in the field of representation learning across different modalities. Overall, these studies highlight the potential of self-supervised techniques in improving model performance and representation quality in both visual and graph-based contexts.
