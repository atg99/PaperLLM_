The paper discusses Pretrained Foundation Models (PFMs), which are pivotal in Artificial Intelligence (AI) within the context of big data. PFMs, introduced as a broader class of models, are extensively utilized across three main AI domains: natural language processing (NLP), computer vision (CV), and graph learning (GL). They excel in learning feature representations for diverse tasks, such as text and image classification, text generation, and object detection, demonstrating impressive performance in training on large-scale datasets and adapting to smaller, related tasks.

PFMs leverage pretraining techniques, initially rooted in transfer learning from CV, to create general models that can be fine-tuned for specific applications. This approach is particularly beneficial in NLP, where vast amounts of unlabeled text data can be employed for training. Early static pretraining methods faced challenges in adapting to varied semantic contexts, leading to the development of dynamic techniques like BERT and XLNet.

The paper highlights the application of PFMs through examples like ChatGPT, which is fine-tuned from the GPT-3.5 model using reinforcement learning from human feedback to align large language models with human intent. This shift towards instruction-aligned training methods indicates a potential movement towards artificial general intelligence.

The review extends to PFMs across text, image, and graph domains, as well as advanced models for speech, video, and multimodal data. An example is GPT-4, a multimodal language model capable of processing text and image inputs, achieving human-level performance on various evaluations. The paper emphasizes the significance and versatility of PFMs in advancing AI technologies.
