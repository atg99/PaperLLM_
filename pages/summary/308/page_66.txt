The paper discusses the significance of images from multimedia platforms in advancing computer vision (CV) research, emphasizing the abundance of data and computational resources available. It explores various downstream tasks associated with pretraining graph models, categorized into node-level, link-level, and graph-level tasks.

Node-level tasks, such as Node Classification (NCF) and Node Clustering (NCI), focus on analyzing individual nodes within graphs. NCF is highlighted for its reliance on external labels, and is categorized into transductive and inductive types based on training visibility. NCI aims to group nodes into clusters based on similarity, serving as both a pretext and evaluation task for pretraining models. Top-K Search (TKS) is introduced as a task to identify the top K nodes related to a given node, useful for recommendation systems.

Link-level tasks address the connections between nodes, with Link Classification (LC) assigning labels to edges and Link Prediction (LP) forecasting the existence of edges in a graph. TKR mirrors TKS but focuses on the ranking of recommendations.

Graph-level tasks assess overall graph properties by analyzing the distribution of nodes and edges. The paper provides statistical data on various datasets relevant to these downstream tasks, underscoring the diverse applications of pretraining graph models across different fields.
