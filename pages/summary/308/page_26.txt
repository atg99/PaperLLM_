The paper provides a comprehensive overview of Progressively Fine-tuned Models (PFMs) in computer vision, specifically focusing on various pretext tasks utilized for representation learning from 2014 to 2023. It summarizes key methods introduced at major conferences, detailing the year, conference, method, pretext task, architecture, and downstream tasks for each model. 

The study highlights the evolution of pretext tasks, starting from early methods like Exemplar-CNN and Context to more advanced techniques including contrastive learning and clustering approaches. Notably, it discusses the integration of clustering with contrastive learning, exemplified by the SwAV and Prototypical Contrastive Learning (PCL) methods, which enhance the semantic encoding of representations.

The paper emphasizes that clustering can provide richer semantic information compared to instance discrimination, thereby benefiting downstream tasks. It also outlines the methodological frameworks and pipelines of the main approaches, aiming to equip new researchers with foundational knowledge and insights into potential research directions in this dynamic field of self-supervised learning.

The section concludes by expressing hope that the insights shared will aid newcomers in understanding this innovative area and exploring valuable research opportunities.
