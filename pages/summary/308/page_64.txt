The paper discusses various large-scale image datasets utilized in different domains of computer vision. 

1. **Sampled Subset**: It highlights a subset maintaining 397 categories, each with at least 100 images, while discarding categories with fewer unique photographs.

2. **Places205**: This dataset includes 2,448,873 images across 205 scene categories, providing a comprehensive resource for scene recognition tasks.

3. **Cars Dataset**: Comprising 16,185 color images spanning 196 car classes categorized by Make, Model, and Year, this dataset is divided into training and testing sets for ease of use.

4. **Aircraft Dataset**: Known as FGVC-Aircraft, it features a fine-grained classification structure with four hierarchies: Model, Variant, Family, and Manufacturer, enhancing detailed recognition.

5. **Pets Dataset**: The Oxford-IIIT Pet Dataset contains around 200 images for 37 pet categories, complete with annotations for breed classification and detection tasks.

6. **Flowers Dataset**: This includes Oxford-17 Flowers with 17 categories and Oxford-102 Flowers with 102 categories, serving as a valuable resource for floral classification.

7. **Describable Textures Dataset (DTD)**: A collection of 5,640 textural images across 47 categories, aimed at facilitating texture recognition.

8. **iNaturalist2018**: A large-scale species classification dataset with over 8,000 species categories and more than 450,000 images, gathered for a competition at CVPR 2018.

9. **JFT-300M**: An internal Google dataset containing 300 million images and over 1 billion labels, created for various classification tasks through human-computer interactions.

10. **Detection Datasets**: The paper emphasizes COCO and PASCAL VOC as pivotal datasets for object detection and segmentation. COCO features 330,000 RGB images with 1.5 million labeled object instances across 80 categories. PASCAL VOC has supported object class recognition challenges from 2005 to 2012 and includes standardized datasets for various tasks.

11. **Segmentation**: This section notes that segmentation involves pixel-level classification, which is challenging to obtain and annotate, making it a critical downstream task in computer vision.

Overall, the paper provides a concise overview of various datasets that support advancements in visual classification, detection, and segmentation tasks in computer vision research.
