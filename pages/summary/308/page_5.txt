The paper discusses the evolution of pretrained foundation models (PFMs), particularly focusing on the emerging trend of unified PFMs, which can process multimodal data such as text, images, and audio. It defines unified PFMs and reviews state-of-the-art models like OFA, UNIFIED-IO, FLA V A, and BEiT-3. The authors highlight two key advantages of PFMs: minimal fine-tuning is needed for improved performance on downstream tasks, and they have been validated for quality, allowing for their application to related datasets without starting from scratch. 

The paper also identifies a gap in existing survey studies, which often focus on specific areas like text generation or object detection, lacking a comprehensive review across various domains like computer vision (CV), natural language processing (NLP), and graphics and language (GL). This survey aims to fill that gap by tracing the evolution of PFMs in NLP and their adoption in CV and GL, while also comparing traditional static feature learning models with dynamic PFMs.

The authors cover advanced topics such as model efficiency, compression, security, and privacy in PFMs, and they outline future research challenges and open problems in these areas. They conclude by presenting a thorough review of PFMs across multimedia domains, contributing insights into the design and pretraining methodologies applicable to the three major domains. Additionally, evaluation metrics and datasets are detailed in the appendices.
