The paper discusses weakly-supervised learning, which sits between fully-supervised learning and self-supervised learning (SSL) based on reliance on human labels. While fully-supervised learning uses existing labels, SSL creates pretext tasks to derive supervised learning. In weakly-supervised learning, inaccurate K-labels may be assigned to data samples, and the goal is to minimize the overall loss across these labels. The loss function is structured for binomial classification, requiring computation for each entry.

SSL leverages inherent data characteristics to derive feature representations without extensive manual labeling. It utilizes pseudo labels and is particularly effective in NLP, where models predict masked elements. Generative methods like Variational Autoencoders (VAE) and Generative Adversarial Networks (GAN) reconstruct data, while contrastive learning, a discriminative method, organizes similar instances closely in a projected space.

Reinforcement Learning (RL) is another paradigm where an agent interacts sequentially with an environment to learn optimal decision-making policies. This process involves receiving states, selecting actions, and accruing rewards, with the aim of maximizing long-term returns.

Lastly, the paper highlights the importance of pretraining tasks for pre-trained feature models (PFMs). Pretraining serves as an initialization step, allowing models to capture essential attributes and structures, thus enhancing performance and convergence in subsequent fine-tuning tasks.
