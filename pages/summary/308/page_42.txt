The paper discusses the saturation phenomena observed in performance across upstream and downstream tasks in pretraining frameworks. It highlights that higher training accuracy on upstream tasks does not guarantee improved performance on downstream tasks, which challenges conventional beliefs about the pretraining process. 

The paper identifies the complexity of the relationship between various self-supervised pretext tasks and their corresponding downstream applications, noting that a multitude of pretext tasks complicates effective task matching. Additionally, it discusses the limitations of current pretraining on task graphs, which are necessary for reusing nodes across different tasks, thereby hindering the integration of extensive data as seen in natural language processing and computer vision.

It outlines several open problems for future pretraining frameworks (PFMs), including the need for a convergence of text, image, graph, and multimodal pretraining, as existing research primarily focuses on language and vision without adequately addressing graph data. The authors advocate for a unified backbone architecture for PFMs, emphasizing the potential of single-transformer models. They also call for a unified PFM capable of achieving state-of-the-art performance across all data domains, noting that most current models excel in only one area. The paper cites BEiT-3 as a notable example that performs well in both vision and vision-language tasks.

Lastly, the paper underscores the limited research on the application of reinforcement learning in PFMs, particularly in computer vision and graph learning, despite significant advancements in natural language processing with models like ChatGPT. The conclusion summarizes the survey's insights into existing PFMs across text, image, and graph domains, discusses model structures, and highlights challenges and future directions in PFM research.
