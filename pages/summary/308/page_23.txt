The paper discusses various two-stream models used in contrastive learning and memory-bank-based methods. It introduces the probability of recognizing a sample as a specific category using a softmax function, where representations of samples act as class prototypes. The temperature parameter, derived from knowledge distillation, influences this probability.

Local Aggregation (LA) is highlighted as a method that trains a CNN encoder to create lower-dimensional embeddings of raw images. This method emphasizes maximizing a metric that brings similar instances closer together while distancing dissimilar ones.

Pretext Invariant Representation Learning (PIRL) is introduced as a concept asserting that semantic representations remain consistent despite variations in pretext transformation tasks. The approach involves feeding both original and transformed views of images into a CNN encoder, with the aim of minimizing the empirical loss between similar views while maximizing the dissimilarity with other images. A memory bank is utilized to store previous representations, enhancing the scalability of the gradient and improving the encoder's representation capabilities.

Additionally, the paper outlines a learning strategy involving two encoder networks for different data augmentations, which pretrains parameters by manipulating distances between positive and negative pairs. The architecture is characterized by two types of shared encoders: Soft Sharing, where encoders have similar but distinct parameters, and Hard Sharing, where they share identical architectures and parameters. The interplay of MLP heads and the memory bank is discussed, indicating their optional presence based on specific settings.
