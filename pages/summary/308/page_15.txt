The paper discusses advancements in pretraining language models (LMs) and their application in downstream tasks, focusing on several models and techniques. ERNIE 2.0 employs seven pretraining tasks that enhance lexical, grammatical, and semantic understanding, utilizing continual learning to retain knowledge across tasks. It features a Transformer encoder with task embedding for effective task differentiation. UniLM integrates unidirectional, bidirectional, and encoder-decoder language modeling through a self-attention layer mask mechanism, training all tasks simultaneously to improve generalization in Natural Language Understanding (NLU) and Natural Language Generation (NLG).

The paper highlights the importance of adapting large pretraining models to specific downstream tasks, noting the emergence of specialized models like BERT-whole word masking (BERT-WWM) and ZEN, which enhance performance by addressing the challenges of semantic loss in languages like Chinese. It also mentions an oriented multilingual sequence labeling model that utilizes knowledge distillation for improved performance in low-resource languages.

Examples of practical applications include ChatGPT and Bard. ChatGPT is fine-tuned from GPT-3.5 using Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF), involving a two-step process of dataset creation and optimization via the Proximal Policy Optimization algorithm. Bard, developed by Google, is based on the LaMDA model, pretrained on extensive dialog data, and aims to tackle challenges related to safety and factual accuracy in conversational AI.
