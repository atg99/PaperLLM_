The paper discusses various modes of supervision in graph learning (GL), including supervised, semi-supervised, unsupervised, and self-supervised learning. In self-supervised learning, pretext tasks such as clustering and partitioning are employed to generate pseudo-labels from large-scale unsupervised graph data, allowing the GL model (MGL) to learn valuable features without manually labeled data. Semi-supervised learning combines both supervised and unsupervised approaches to predict unlabeled data, addressing challenges in acquiring labeled data in practical situations.

The learning modes are categorized into inductive and transductive learning. Inductive learning trains the model on labeled data and tests it on unseen samples, aiming to classify future graph data effectively. In contrast, transductive learning utilizes all available samples during both training and testing phases, learning to classify unlabeled data based on the entire dataset.

The optimization methods for classifiers under the supervised framework, including semi-supervised and self-supervised settings, are formulated with a cross-entropy loss function. The paper also highlights approaches that integrate multiple pretext tasks into a unified framework to leverage their combined advantages, improving the learning process.
