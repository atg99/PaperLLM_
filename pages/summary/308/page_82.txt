The cited works focus on advancements in unsupervised and self-supervised learning techniques applied to various domains, particularly graph representation learning and speech processing. 

In graph representation, several studies emphasize methods like mutual information maximization and contrastive learning to enhance graph-level embeddings, such as Infograph, which utilizes mutual information for representation learning. Other research explores self-supervised techniques for heterogeneous networks and bipartite graph embeddings, highlighting the importance of both local and global structures in graph data. 

In the realm of speech processing, works like wav2vec and its variants showcase frameworks for self-supervised learning that improve speech recognition and understanding by leveraging large-scale pre-training. Additionally, studies like SPLAT explore joint pre-training for speech and language tasks, demonstrating the potential of cross-modal learning approaches.

Overall, these papers contribute to the growing field of representation learning by presenting innovative methods that enhance model performance across different applications through unsupervised and semi-supervised paradigms.
