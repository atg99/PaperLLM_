The proposed U-Net model incorporates an attention mechanism in its skip connections, enhancing the detection of smaller needles while ensuring spatial continuity through total variation regularization. The training process employs deep supervision, generating needle mask patches to compute cross-entropy loss, leading to improved multi-needle segmentation performance.

Byra et al. introduced the selective kernel U-Net (SKU-Net) aimed at breast mass segmentation in ultrasound imaging, addressing the variability in breast mass size and image properties. By replacing each convolution layer with a selective kernel (SK) block, SKU-Net adapts the receptive field dynamically. This dual-path structure combines dilated and non-dilated convolutions, enhancing feature extraction and attention coefficient construction, resulting in significant performance gains over the traditional U-Net.

Punn and Agarwal enhanced the inception U-Net model with a residual cross-spatial attention (CSA) block in the skip connections, improving breast cancer segmentation accuracy. This CSA block utilizes multi-level encoded feature maps for better spatial correlation, leveraging depth-wise separable convolutions to mitigate computational costs, achieving promising results across various ultrasound datasets.

Yang et al. proposed a dual path U-Net (DPU-Net) for segmenting lumen and media-adventitia in intravascular ultrasound (IVUS) scans to assist in cardiovascular disease diagnosis. DPU-Net employs a real-time augmentor to generate diverse artifacts and utilizes multi-branch operations to effectively handle shape variations, achieving high Jaccard measures on different frequency frames from the IVUS dataset.

Wang et al. developed a multi-feature guided CNN model for the classification and segmentation of bone surfaces in ultrasound scans, beginning with a pre-enhancing network that synthesizes images to better highlight bone surfaces. This approach demonstrates the potential of U-Net variants in various biomedical imaging applications.
