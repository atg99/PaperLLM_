The paper discusses implementation strategies for segmentation architectures in biomedical image segmentation (BIS), categorizing them into two main approaches: training from scratch and transfer learning. The first approach requires significant labeled data and computational resources, utilizing initialization techniques such as Xavier or Kaming. In contrast, transfer learning leverages pre-trained models from benchmark datasets like ImageNet, often yielding superior results. Two schemes for transfer learning are identified: freezing the base model while training later layers or semi-freezing the base model with some high-level layers retrained.

Performance metrics are crucial for evaluating segmentation models, especially given the imbalance in biomedical datasets, where target regions are often underrepresented. Standard metrics may not apply, making the intersection-over-union (IoU) and dice similarity coefficient preferred choices for assessment.

Loss functions play a vital role in addressing class imbalance in BIS tasks. These functions are categorized into four types: distribution, region, boundary, and hybrid. The paper emphasizes the importance of tailoring loss functions to specific datasets and challenges, with a focus on semantic segmentation scenarios. Notably, the accelerated Tversky loss function is introduced to enhance training efficiency for biomedical image segmentation using the nnU-Net model.
