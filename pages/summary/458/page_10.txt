The paper discusses advancements in medical imaging and deep learning technologies, particularly focusing on the U-Net architecture, which has gained popularity for computer-aided diagnosis. Despite its success in tasks such as the ISBI cell tracking challenge, the traditional U-Net exhibits limitations, particularly in deeper layers where learning can slow down due to diluted gradients. This can lead to the model overlooking critical abstract features. Additionally, the localized convolutions of the U-Net restrict its ability to capture global and long-range dependencies effectively.

To address these shortcomings, the authors propose various U-Net variants aimed at enhancing segmentation performance across different imaging modalities, including X-ray, CT, MRI, PET, and ultrasound. The review categorizes these variants in two ways: inter-modality categorization, which highlights differences in segmentation approaches across modalities, and intra-modality categorization, which organizes U-Net variants within the same modality based on their technical contributions, such as better U-Nets, attention U-Nets, inception U-Nets, and ensemble U-Nets.

This structured approach not only clarifies the advancements made in U-Net variants but also provides insights into the specific challenges faced in segmentation tasks across various imaging modalities.
