This paper discusses advancements in medical imaging, particularly in the context of brain tumor segmentation and positron emission tomography (PET). Punn and Agarwal proposed a 3D U-Net framework for volumetric brain tumor segmentation, which includes three components: multi-modalities fusion, tumor extractor, and tumor segmenter. This architecture effectively merges MRI sequences using hierarchical inception convolution blocks and decodes multi-scale features into multi-class tumor regions. The use of weighted averages of dice and intersection over union (IoU) loss functions led to significant improvements in segmentation performance on BraTS 2017 and BraTS 2018 datasets, achieving DSC values of 0.86 and 0.88. The authors note that while inception convolutions enhance performance, they also increase computational complexity, which could potentially be mitigated by depthwise separable convolutions.

In the PET imaging segment, the paper highlights the importance of this technique in clinical applications and discusses the work of Blanc-Durand et al., who utilized a 3D U-Net model for 18F-FET PET lesion detection. Their approach achieved a DSC of 0.79 on training and validation sets, with suggestions for further improvement through GAN-based data augmentation. Additionally, Lu et al. introduced an automatic tumor segmentation approach using transfer learning with pre-trained VGG-19 blocks, which showed enhanced performance over traditional U-Net models by employing DropBlock for regularization and Jaccard distance as the loss function, validated on a dataset of 1,309 PET images.
