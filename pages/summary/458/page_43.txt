The paper discusses various U-Net variants tailored for biomedical image segmentation, highlighting their effectiveness and adaptability in processing medical images. It references foundational works, such as the original U-Net architecture by Ronneberger et al. and modifications like the Use-Net, which incorporates squeeze-and-excitation blocks for enhanced prostate zonal segmentation. Other notable contributions include the Modified U-Net (Mu-Net) for liver segmentation and the U-Next architecture designed for gallstone segmentation. The discussion spans a range of methodologies, including attention mechanisms and deep learning techniques, emphasizing the continuous evolution of U-Net models to address specific challenges in medical imaging. The paper also reviews diverse applications of these models across various imaging modalities, underscoring the importance of tailored approaches in improving segmentation accuracy and reliability in clinical settings.
