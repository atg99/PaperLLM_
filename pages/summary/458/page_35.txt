The paper discusses the challenges and solutions in biomedical image segmentation, particularly focusing on the limitations of deep convolutional neural networks (CNNs) in terms of transparency and interpretability. Unlike rule-based systems, CNN models are often viewed as black boxes due to the difficulty in understanding the processing within their hidden layers. To address this, various visualization techniques have been proposed, such as LIME, SHAP, PDP, and anchor methods, aimed at providing explanations for the outcomes produced by these models. However, the need for a standardized benchmarking scheme remains.

The paper also highlights specific challenges faced by different imaging modalities, including X-ray, CT, MRI, PET, and ultrasound (US). In X-ray imaging, for instance, the 2D projection of 3D anatomy leads to overlapping features, complicating the representation of physiological structures. This is particularly evident in chest X-rays where lung contours may be obscured by scarring. To tackle these issues, U-Net based models that employ long skip connections and adversarial learning strategies are utilized. Nonetheless, these models often demand substantial pixel-level annotated training data, which can be mitigated through data augmentation and self-supervised learning techniques.
