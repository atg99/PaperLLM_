The paper discusses several challenges in the realm of deep learning, particularly concerning computational power and data availability. One significant hurdle is the high computational requirements that can limit the feasibility of various approaches, prompting the development of cloud-based high-performance computing environments for efficient processing. Although advancements in model compression and acceleration techniques have been made, there is still a need for concrete benchmark results for real-time applications.

The EfficientNet framework proposed by Tan and Le offers a streamlined U-Net design for complex segmentation tasks while maintaining parameter efficiency. Additionally, attempts at automating model architecture design aim to optimize models for different applications, though progress remains ongoing.

The data-hungry nature of deep learning models poses another challenge, as the performance is heavily reliant on the quantity and quality of available data. Data acquisition costs and security concerns hinder data accessibility, which in turn affects the development of real-world systems. To mitigate this, various data augmentation strategies have been proposed, including geometric transformations and generative adversarial networks. However, the limited diversity of augmented data can lead to overfitting.

Transfer learning is another strategy employed to adapt pre-trained models to specific tasks with limited training data, categorized into four areas: instance-based, mapping-based, network-based, and adversarial-based. Self-supervised learning (SSL) is an emerging technique that utilizes unannotated samples for pre-training, allowing models to learn feature representations before fine-tuning for segmentation tasks, thereby advancing U-Net based approaches.

Moreover, the paper highlights the impact of low imaging quality due to noise and artefacts, which can obscure image features. Various pre-processing techniques such as wavelet thresholding and new reconstruction methods are discussed to minimize these issues. Overall, while there have been strides in addressing these challenges, significant work remains to enhance the robustness of deep learning models for real-world applications.
