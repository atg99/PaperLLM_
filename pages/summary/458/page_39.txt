This paper discusses various U-Net variants tailored for biomedical image segmentation, emphasizing their modality-specific adaptations. It cites significant works in the field, including studies on automatic multiorgan segmentation in thoracic CT images and the use of Siamese networks for knee cartilage tracking in ultrasound images. The authors also reference a range of methodologies for medical image segmentation, highlighting advancements in deep learning techniques and their application in improving segmentation accuracy.

Key contributions include the introduction of models like Inf-Net for COVID-19 lung infection segmentation and MA-Net for liver and tumor segmentation, showcasing the effectiveness of multi-scale attention mechanisms. The paper emphasizes the importance of evaluating segmentation algorithms and the impact of pre-trained networks in enhancing performance.

Additionally, the authors explore the integration of spatial attention modules for multimodal imaging and GAN-based approaches for synthetic image augmentation. The survey encapsulates the evolution of deep learning in medical imaging, underscoring its potential in diagnostics and treatment planning across various anatomical structures and imaging modalities.
