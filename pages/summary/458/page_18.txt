Additionally, the authors highlighted the challenge of limited data samples, which led to the use of data augmentation techniques like rotation and zooming to enhance model training. They implemented post-processing to refine the output masks, ensuring retention of the largest binary contours for accurate cardiothoracic ratio (CTR) calculation. The CTR, defined as the sum of distances from the heart's center to its boundaries divided by the maximum distance between lung boundaries, serves as an indicator of cardiomegaly.

In another study, Subramanian et al. introduced a dual U-Net model approach for classifying central venous catheters (CVCs), tackling class imbalance with exponential logarithmic loss. The first model focused on CVC segmentation, while the second extracted anatomical features to differentiate between ambiguous catheter types. The segmentation results were enhanced through clinician-annotated spatial priors, and a neural network random forest classifier was employed for final classification, yielding promising results on the NIH database.

Overall, the integration of deep learning techniques in medical imaging, particularly using U-Net architectures, has shown significant potential in improving diagnostic accuracy and efficiency in various applications, including lung nodule and CVC identification.
