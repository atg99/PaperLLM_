The paper discusses advancements in biomedical image segmentation using various U-Net based models. It highlights a classification embedded U-Net model (cU-Net) that utilizes B-mode ultrasound scans and three filtered image features—local phase tensor image, local phase bone image, and bone shadow enhanced image—to achieve segmentation and classification with F1-scores of 0.96 and 0.90 on SonixTouch and Clarius C3 datasets, respectively. Another model, Siam-U-Net, focuses on tracking knee femoral condyle cartilage during ultrasound guided procedures. It combines the U-Net with a Siamese framework to effectively localize cartilage in real-time ultrasound sequences, achieving a dice score of 0.70, with potential for improvement through 3D modeling.

The paper further explores various U-Net variants aimed at enhancing biomedical image segmentation. Notable mentions include RU-Net and R2U-Net, which integrate recurrent convolutional layers and residual units for improved performance across multiple modalities. The U-Net++ architecture utilizes nested skip connections and deep supervision to better capture details and outperforms existing models on various datasets. Lastly, BCDU-Net introduces bi-directional ConvLSTM with densely connected convolutions for enhanced segmentation capabilities. Overall, these models demonstrate significant advancements in the field, with each variant tailored to improve segmentation accuracy across different imaging modalities.
