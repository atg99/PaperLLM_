This paper provides a comprehensive review of Bayesian network structure learning methods, focusing on their applicability for various tasks such as classification and regression. It emphasizes the importance of selecting appropriate methods based on the specific goals, such as predicting outcomes or understanding independence relations among variables. The authors note that the methods discussed do not account for causality, highlighting a growing research interest in causal modeling, as reviewed by He et al. in 2017.

The paper further discusses technical considerations, such as the handling of missing data and continuous variables, indicating that certain methods are optimized for these scenarios. The authors point out that dataset size significantly influences the accuracy of constraint-based methods, where larger sample sizes can lead to an increased likelihood of false edges being included. Solutions to mitigate this issue have been proposed by Bacciu et al. in 2013.

Additionally, the availability of software tools for implementing these methods is addressed, with a detailed analysis provided in Section 4. The authors conclude by suggesting future research directions, including the development of scalable methods for large datasets and enhanced focus on causality for more interpretable models.
