This paper discusses various approaches to learning Bayesian networks, particularly in the context of regression problems and handling missing data. Fernandez et al. (2010) introduced a method that utilizes mixtures of truncated exponentials to represent the joint distribution of Bayesian networks. Their data augmentation algorithm iteratively re-estimates the model and imputes missing values, showing effectiveness on datasets with up to 16 variables. 

Nielsen et al. (2010) proposed a different approach for learning Probabilistic Decision Graphs in the presence of missing data. This representation can capture independence relations beyond those in Bayesian networks, offering potentially more efficient computational representations, although their experiments were limited to networks with up to 40 variables.

Scanagatta et al. (2018) introduced a method for structure learning on incomplete datasets using structural Expectation-Maximization (SEM). They addressed complexity issues through a bounded treewidth Bayesian network structure learning algorithm, enabling their SEM implementation to scale effectively to thousands of variables.

The paper also addresses methods for handling continuous variables in structure learning. While most techniques are initially designed for discrete variables, adaptations such as discretization allow for statistical independence tests. Score-based structure learning can accommodate continuous variables provided the scoring method aligns with the probability density model used. Romero et al. (2006) proposed using mixtures of truncated exponentials for this purpose. However, certain statistical models, like the conditional Gaussian (CG) model, impose restrictions that complicate this approach. Böttcher (2001) proposed a score-based learning solution for CG networks, which was later refined to enhance search efficiency (Böttcher and Dethlefsen; 2003).
