The paper discusses various algorithms and approaches for optimizing the identification of parent sets in Bayesian networks, with a focus on balancing computational efficiency and accuracy. Traditional rules proposed by de Campos and Ji in 2011 do not significantly reduce the search space, while the K2 algorithm by Cooper and Herskovits offers a greedy search but has been surpassed by more accurate modern methods.

The in-degree of nodes in a Bayesian network affects both the search space and computational time, leading to a trade-off for users, especially in large variable scenarios where a smaller in-degree is preferred for feasibility. The identification of parent sets is complex, likely not admitting a polynomial-time algorithm with guaranteed quality, which underscores the need for effective search heuristics.

Scanagatta et al. (2015) introduce a promising approach that leverages an approximated score function, BIC*, to guide exploration of parent sets without the constraint of a maximum in-degree, allowing scalability to thousands of nodes. The paper also reviews various exact algorithms for structure optimization, including dynamic programming methods by Koivisto and Sood (2004) and Silander and Myllymaki (2006), which are limited by their computational complexity.

Malone (2012) and others propose framing the learning goal as a shortest path problem, allowing for focused searches within the solution space, leading to improved performance on datasets with up to 26 variables. Additionally, de Campos et al. (2009) and de Campos and Ji (2011) present a branch and bound approach that identifies optimal parent sets by initially disregarding acyclicity constraints and later resolving cycles, offering an anytime behavior that allows users to manage the trade-off between solution quality and computational time for datasets with up to 100 variables.

Finally, the current leading method utilizes integer linear programming, as highlighted by Cussens (2011) and Jaakkola et al. (2010), which frames the learning problem in a relaxed manner, indicating its effectiveness in the optimization process.
