The paper discusses the reconstruction of graphs in the context of earthquake modeling, focusing on the scoring methods used for learning directed acyclic graphs (DAGs). It highlights two prominent scoring techniques: the Bayes Dirichlet equivalent uniform (BDeu) score and the Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC). Both scores have the property of decomposability, meaning the score of a DAG can be expressed as the sum of the scores of its subgraphs.

The learning process is divided into two distinct phases: parent set identification and structure optimization. Parent set identification generates a list of potential parent sets for each variable based on their scores, while structure optimization assigns these parent sets to maximize the overall score without creating cycles. There are two types of structure optimization: general, which imposes no restrictions, and bounded treewidth, which limits the treewidth of the resulting graph.

The paper notes the challenges of parent set identification, as it typically requires exhaustive exploration of the possible parent set space, which increases exponentially with the number of variables and maximum in-degree. This complexity is often managed by limiting the maximum number of parents per node, with existing studies showing that even with this constraint, the networks analyzed remain relatively small. The work by Cussens et al. is cited, illustrating the limitations of current methods in handling large datasets with numerous variables.
