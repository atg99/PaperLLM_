The paper discusses various methods for optimizing bounded treewidth structures in directed acyclic graphs (DAGs) by focusing on their moral graphs. It highlights the challenges posed by bounding treewidth as a global graph property, which complicates the learning process. 

Initial strategies involved heuristic edge additions, such as the method proposed by Elidan and Gould in 2008, which is polynomial in terms of the number of variables and treewidth. This method provides an upper-bound on treewidth, demonstrating superior model learning compared to other heuristic techniques on datasets with up to 200 variables.

Korhonen and Parviainen introduced an exact dynamic programming algorithm in 2013 that finds the optimal graph maximizing a score function but is limited to networks of up to 15 nodes due to its exponential time complexity. 

Subsequently, Parviainen et al. in 2014 employed an integer programming approach with cutting planes to enforce treewidth bounds, achieving better results on datasets with up to 60 nodes. 

Nie et al. proposed an approximation technique in 2014 that involves sampling k-trees, but faced issues with the vast sampling space, which hindered effective exploration for larger datasets. This was improved in 2015 with an A* guided exploration that utilized a heuristic evaluation called Informative Score to identify promising k-trees, although it did not fully resolve the sampling challenges.

Finally, Scanagatta et al. in 2016 introduced an iterative procedure that constructs a DAG by adding variables sequentially, ensuring that the moral graph remains a subgraph of a k-tree. This method effectively scales with the number of variables and treewidth, outperforming competitors and enabling the learning of bounded treewidth Bayesian networks from large datasets.
