The provided references highlight significant contributions to the field of Bayesian networks and their structure learning. 

Scanagatta et al. (2017) focus on enhancing local search methods for structure learning in Bayesian networks. Their work is presented at the 3rd International Workshop on Advanced Methodologies for Bayesian Networks. In a subsequent study (Scanagatta et al., 2018), they discuss efficient learning techniques for bounded-treewidth Bayesian networks using both complete and incomplete datasets, published in the International Journal of Approximate Reasoning.

Further, Scanagatta et al. (2015) address the challenge of learning Bayesian networks with thousands of variables, contributing to Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems. Schwarz (1978) introduces a method for estimating model dimensions, which is foundational in statistical modeling. 

Scutari (2014) presents parallel and optimized implementations of constraint-based structure learning algorithms within the bnlearn R package, while Silander and Myllymaki (2006) propose a straightforward method for finding globally optimal Bayesian network structures.

The seminal work by Spirtes et al. (2000) discusses causation, prediction, and search, laying the groundwork for many subsequent studies. Steck and Tresp (1996) explore the application of Bayesian belief networks in data mining, and Teyssier and Koller (2005) introduce an effective algorithm for learning Bayesian networks based on ordering.

Yuan and Malone (2012) improve admissible heuristics for optimal Bayesian network learning, while their earlier work (Yuan et al., 2011) utilizes A* search for this purpose. Lastly, Zheng et al. (2018) present a continuous optimization approach for structure learning in directed acyclic graphs (DAGs), further advancing the methodology in this area.

These works collectively contribute to the understanding and development of Bayesian networks, addressing various aspects from theoretical foundations to practical implementations.
