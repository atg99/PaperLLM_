This paper discusses various approaches to solving integer linear problems through the use of cutting planes, particularly focusing on breaking cycles to find optimal solutions. The success of this method hinges on identifying effective cutting planes, and experimental results show its capability to handle datasets with up to 200 variables.

Malone et al. (2018) introduce a supervised solver selection technique that utilizes features from the input data to predict the most suitable state-of-the-art solver for specific instances, shedding light on the strengths and weaknesses of different solvers.

Teyssier and Koller (2005) present an ordering-based search (OBS) that employs a Monte Carlo method to explore variable orderings, aiming to select the best network consistent with each ordering. This heuristic approach is competitive for complex problems, potentially scaling to datasets with thousands of variables. The OBS method uses a simple swap operator to escape local maxima, while Alonso-Barba et al. (2011) enhance this with an insertion operator, significantly improving the score of learned structures.

Lee and van Beek (2017) propose a meta-heuristic to improve greedy local search performance, introducing two strategies: an iterated local search with a perturbation factor and a genetic algorithm that utilizes crossover and mutation stages.

Scanagatta et al. (2017) introduce a window operator for local search, which enhances structure scoring through local changes in ordering. Their method consistently yields higher-scoring networks compared to existing competitors, effectively learning from datasets with thousands of variables. Additionally, Scangatta et al. (2018) focus on the significance of parent sets in directed acyclic graphs (DAGs), proposing a variable sampling strategy based on entropy to enhance performance in local search algorithms.

The paper also highlights local search over equivalence classes of network structures, leading to the GES algorithm, which has been improved for larger networks. Zheng et al. (2018) introduce a continuous optimization approach to structure learning, solvable by standard numerical algorithms, marking a novel direction in the field.
