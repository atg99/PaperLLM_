This section discusses constraint-based structure learning methods, which utilize statistical tests to determine independences among variables in a model. The PC algorithm is highlighted as a prominent approach, starting from a complete undirected graph and recursively deleting edges based on conditional independence tests. It typically operates in exponential time, but can perform in polynomial time under the assumption of a sparse underlying Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG). The robustness of the PC algorithm has been improved by modifications that render it order-independent. It primarily relies on tests such as Chi-square or mutual information, with a Bayesian score test alternative presented by Abellán et al. 

Recent advancements have incorporated parallel computing techniques for more efficient local causal discovery, notably Madsen et al.'s approach, which effectively handles large datasets. 

The challenge of incomplete data in structure learning is also addressed. The structural Expectation-Maximization (SEM) method is the conventional approach, employing an iterative two-step process to estimate missing values and perform structure learning. However, its high computational complexity limits its applicability to small datasets. An alternative method proposed by Adel and de Campos translates the problem of learning from incomplete data into one of learning from complete data by augmenting the network with additional variables. While promising on smaller datasets, this method also faces exponential computational costs with larger amounts of missing data.
