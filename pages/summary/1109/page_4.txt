The paper discusses the complexities of modeling and learning the structure of Bayesian networks, particularly in the context of earthquake prediction. It highlights that the problem of determining the structure from sampled data is NP-Hard, as established by previous research. A critical aspect of managing this complexity is the concept of treewidth, which measures the tree-likeness of a graph and affects the efficiency of inference algorithms. The authors introduce the concept of structure learning, where the goal is to infer the underlying structure of a Bayesian network from observed data rather than known configurations.

The task involves determining directed arcs in a directed acyclic graph (DAG) based on a given dataset. The challenge is compounded by the potential for missing data, which currently limits the effectiveness of structure learning methods. The paper emphasizes the computational difficulty of this task due to the vast number of possible graph structures, which grows super-exponentially with the number of nodes.

To address structure learning, the authors present score-based approaches as the most prevalent method. This involves identifying the best DAG by maximizing a score function that reflects the structure's fit to the observed data. The paper aims to provide insights into these methodologies and their implications for learning Bayesian network structures effectively.
