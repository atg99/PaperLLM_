Bayesian networks are a method for structured knowledge representation, where domain variables are represented as nodes in a graph that encodes their dependencies. A key challenge in this framework is structure learning, which involves determining the dependency graph from data. This task is classified as NP-hard, making it a significant area of research.

The process of structure learning can be viewed as selecting one graph from numerous candidates based on data samples from the distribution that generates the data. The complexity increases with the number of variables, leading to a challenging search space. This paper surveys various sophisticated algorithms proposed in the literature for this purpose.

The paper is organized into several sections: Section 2 provides a formal introduction to Bayesian networks, illustrated with an earthquake example. Section 3 reviews relevant literature on structure learning, while Section 4 discusses software tools that implement these learning techniques. Section 5 offers guidance on selecting appropriate methods based on specific applications, and Section 6 concludes the discussion.

In the earthquake example presented, five variables are identified: the alarm ringing, a burglar intrusion, the occurrence of an earthquake, the radio announcement, and the neighbor's call. Each variable is represented with a conditional probability table, detailing the distribution of the variable based on its parent variables. A Bayesian network is formally defined as a pair consisting of a directed acyclic graph and a collection of conditional mass functions, which together represent a joint probability distribution over a finite set of categorical variables.
