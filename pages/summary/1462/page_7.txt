The paper presents a comparative analysis of human performance and model accuracy on various tasks, specifically focusing on the CLUE benchmark. The results are summarized in Table 3, highlighting the performance of three annotators, their average scores, majority votes, and the best model performance. The annotators demonstrated varying scores across different tasks, with an average of 65.3 to 95.3, while the best model achieved scores between 58.61 and 82.13.

The models discussed include RoBERTa-wwm-ext-base and RoBERTa-wwm-ext-large, which are based on transformer architectures, with the latter being a larger version expected to yield better performance. The study also includes ALBERT-xxlarge and ALBERT-tiny to illustrate the impact of model size on performance, particularly in resource-limited scenarios.

Fine-tuning of these models was conducted separately for each task, with hyper-parameters selected based on development set performance. The paper emphasizes the importance of human annotation for establishing performance benchmarks, detailing a structured approach to training annotators and evaluating their results.

The analysis reveals that larger models, those trained on more data, and those employing whole word masking tend to perform better, with RoBERTa-wwm-ext-large and ALBERT-xxlarge being the top performers, especially in machine reading tasks. The paper concludes with an acknowledgment of the challenges presented by tasks with numerous classes, as seen in TNEWS and IFLYTEK, which affected human performance.
