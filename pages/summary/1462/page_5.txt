The paper discusses various datasets for machine reading comprehension (MRC) and the importance of large-scale language data for model pre-training, particularly in the context of Chinese language processing. 

CMRC 2018 is highlighted as a span-extraction dataset comprising approximately 19,071 annotated questions sourced from Wikipedia, where answers are text spans within the provided contexts. ChID is introduced as a cloze test dataset containing about 498,611 passages and 623,377 blanks, focusing on Chinese idioms, with each blank offering ten candidates for completion. C3 is identified as the first free-form multiple-choice MRC dataset for Chinese, featuring 19,577 problems across 13,369 documents, designed to evaluate reading comprehension similar to English datasets like RACE and DREAM.

The paper emphasizes the gap in available pre-training datasets for Chinese compared to English. It points out that existing Chinese datasets, such as the Wikipedia dataset, are significantly smaller. To address this, a large-scale crawled Chinese corpus of 214GB and approximately 76 billion words was compiled, incorporating three different corpora: CLUECorpus2020-small, CLUECorpus2020, and CLUEOSCAR. 

CLUECorpus2020-small includes 14GB of text from various genres, including news articles, web content from platforms similar to Reddit, Wikipedia, and e-commerce comments. CLUECorpus2020, a larger dataset of 100GB, is sourced from Common Crawl and is ready for pre-training without additional processing. The models discussed in the paper, including two ALBERT models and RoBERTa-large, were pre-trained on this comprehensive CLUE corpus.
