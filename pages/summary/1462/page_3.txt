The paper outlines the criteria and framework for the CLUE benchmark, aimed at enhancing Chinese natural language understanding (NLU) tasks. It emphasizes that tasks included should be of moderate difficulty, representative of common language understanding challenges, and tailored to the unique characteristics of the Chinese language. 

Given the scarcity of diverse publicly available NLU datasets in Chinese compared to English, the authors engaged with the Chinese NLP community to gather task proposals and also created two new datasets to address this gap. Ultimately, they compiled nine tasks, derived from existing datasets and new creations, which encompass various text genres, linguistic features, and task formats.

The paper details the creation of a large-scale pre-training dataset consisting of 214GB of raw corpus, containing approximately 76 billion Chinese words. Additionally, a diagnostic dataset was crafted to evaluate model performance on specific linguistic and logical phenomena.

To support the research community, the authors established a leaderboard for CLUE, allowing users to submit results and encouraging reproducibility by certifying open-source model scores. A toolkit named PyCLUE was also developed to facilitate quick performance validation on the benchmark, supporting various pre-training models and tasks.

The tasks within CLUE include single sentence classification, sentence pair classification, and machine reading comprehension, with specific examples provided for clarity. The TNEWS dataset is highlighted as an example of a single sentence task where users predict the category of news titles based on a classification of 15 categories.
