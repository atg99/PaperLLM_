The paper discusses the performance of various NLP models, particularly ALBERT-tiny and XLNet-mid, in comparison to BERT-base across different tasks. ALBERT-tiny, with significantly fewer parameters, shows a minor performance drop in single-sentence tasks like CLUEWSC2020, but struggles in tasks requiring global understanding, with up to a 40 percentage point drop in machine reading comprehension tasks like ChID. XLNet-mid performs poorly in token-level Chinese tasks, indicating that techniques effective in English may not translate well to Chinese due to its unique linguistic structure.

The analysis reveals that while humans excel in multiple-choice reading comprehension, machines, including ALBERT-tiny, perform poorly. Tasks such as CSL and ChID exhibit similar difficulty levels for both humans and machines, whereas TNEWS presents challenges for both due to its ambiguous categorization of news titles. The models still fall short of human performance, suggesting a need for improvement in NLP methods.

The paper also introduces a diagnostic dataset created to test models on linguistically significant phenomena in Chinese, diverging from English-based datasets. This dataset includes 514 handcrafted sentence pairs addressing unique aspects of the Chinese language, such as aspect markers and anaphora resolution. Evaluation of BERT-base, XLNet-mid, and RoBERTa-wwm-ext-large on this dataset reveals a maximum accuracy of only around 61%, indicating significant challenges in addressing these linguistic complexities, and suggesting further opportunities for model enhancement.
