This paper addresses the challenges in improving and comparing the performance of language models trained on limited and diverse corpora. To tackle this issue, the authors propose the creation of the CLUE pre-training dataset, which comprises over 214GB of raw text and approximately 76 billion Chinese words. Additionally, they introduce a diagnostic dataset crafted by linguists to evaluate models on linguistic and logical comprehension, similar to the GLUE framework.

The primary contributions of this work include: 
1. A comprehensive Chinese natural language understanding benchmark featuring various sentence classification and machine reading comprehension tasks that vary in difficulty, size, and format.
2. A substantial raw corpus intended for general-purpose pre-training in Chinese, facilitating meaningful comparisons across different model architectures.
3. A linguistically-informed diagnostic evaluation dataset that encapsulates multiple linguistic phenomena specific to Chinese.
4. A user-friendly toolkit and an online leaderboard equipped with an auto-evaluation system to support researchers in reproducing experimental results and comparing model performances effectively.

The paper also discusses the existing landscape of language representation evaluation, highlighting the inconsistencies arising from the use of different evaluation pipelines and datasets. Previous initiatives like SentEval, GLUE, and SuperGLUE have established standardized frameworks for evaluating models in English, underscoring the need for a similar approach in Chinese language understanding.

The CLUE framework includes nine distinct language understanding tasks designed to assess various facets of pre-trained models. These tasks were selected based on criteria such as diversity, clarity, and ease of processing, ensuring a robust evaluation of language understanding capabilities.
