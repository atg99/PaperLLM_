The paper introduces the Chinese Language Understanding Evaluation (CLUE) benchmark, which encompasses nine natural language understanding tasks and a diagnostic dataset informed by linguistic principles. It also provides an online leaderboard for evaluating model performance and releases a large, clean corpus for pre-training Chinese language models. CLUE is positioned as the first comprehensive benchmark for Chinese language understanding.

The results of various state-of-the-art language representation models are analyzed, revealing their challenges in addressing specific Chinese linguistic phenomena. For instance, the monotonicity task proves to be the most difficult, and models struggle with comparatives and lexical semantics, often relying on simple lexical overlap heuristics. The findings indicate that current models are approximately 10% below estimated human performance, suggesting significant potential for improvement in Chinese NLU.

The authors argue that while English benchmarks like GLUE and SuperGLUE have reached human performance levels, Chinese NLU remains an area with ample room for advancement. They envision future extensions of the CLUE benchmark to include new tasks and evaluation methods, emphasizing its community-driven and open-ended nature as a foundational step in enhancing Chinese language understanding capabilities.
