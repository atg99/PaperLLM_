The paper discusses the ChainerRL library, focusing on its impact on deep reinforcement learning (RL) research, particularly in the context of Atari evaluations. Key aspects of standard Atari evaluation protocols are outlined, including evaluation frequency, phase length, episode length, evaluation policy, and reporting protocols. The evaluation frequency determines how often evaluations occur during training, while the evaluation phase length affects the variance in performance results; longer phases yield more stable scores. Evaluation episode length can influence the agent's performance, as longer episodes allow for more points accumulation.

The paper highlights two reporting protocols: best-eval, which reports the highest mean score across all evaluations, and re-eval, which involves a re-evaluation of the best-performing model. These details are crucial for ensuring fair comparisons between different algorithms, as they significantly affect reported results. 

Tables within the paper present results from ChainerRL’s reproducibility scripts for various algorithms, including DQN, IQN, Rainbow, and A3C, against published benchmarks. For continuous-action algorithms, results from OpenAI Gym MuJoCo tasks for DDPG, TRPO, PPO, TD3, and SAC are also included. The authors emphasize that they provide models trained through their reproducibility scripts to aid further research. The development of these scripts involved thorough analysis of existing literature and communication with original authors to resolve any inconsistencies or ambiguities.
