In their paper, Fujita, Nagarajan, Kataoka, and Ishikawa discuss the challenges of reproducibility in research, particularly when original results cannot be directly compared to new implementations. They highlight the issue using the A3C algorithm as an example, where the original paper’s scores depend on demonstrations that are not publicly accessible. This lack of availability complicates accurate comparisons. To address this, the authors suggest utilizing high-quality published research that allows for reliable reproductions, referencing works by Fortunato et al., Henderson et al., and Fujimoto et al. to ensure valid comparisons in reproducibility efforts.
