The paper "Model-Centered Assurance for Autonomous Systems" by Susmit Jha, John Rushby, and Natarajan Shankar addresses the challenges of ensuring the safety and effectiveness of autonomous systems, particularly those utilizing Artificial Intelligence (AI) and Machine Learning (ML). The authors propose a framework that separates the functions of perception and action within these systems. Perception involves creating and maintaining an internal model of the environment, while action involves executing plans based on that model.

The assurance process is divided into two main components: ensuring the model accurately reflects the changing world and ensuring that actions taken based on the model are safe. The paper emphasizes the importance of the model in providing assurance, suggesting that traditional engineering techniques can be employed to monitor actions and ensure safety based on the model.

The authors introduce a two-level architecture for model assurance. The lower level focuses on "predictive processing," where the model is used to predict sensor interpretations. Small discrepancies indicate expected changes in the environment, prompting updates to the model, while larger discrepancies signal unexpected events, leading to error masking or model revision. The upper level engages in more reflective tasks, further supporting model maintenance, and aligns with theories of human cognition.

In summary, this work provides a structured approach to ensuring the accuracy of models in autonomous systems, highlighting the interplay between perception, action, and assurance in the context of AI and ML technologies.
