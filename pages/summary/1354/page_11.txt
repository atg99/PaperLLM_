The paper discusses a two-level architecture for predictive processing (PP) in human cognition, aligning it with the dual-process theory popularized by Kahneman. Level 1 focuses on basic model construction, which maintains a consistent world model, while Level 2 involves inferential refinement aimed at minimizing surprise through enhanced situation awareness. This refinement can incorporate hypothetical reasoning, adding "ghosts" to the model to account for unseen objects, thereby influencing action functions without committing to potentially hazardous choices.

For instance, reasoning about occluded vision allows for the addition of ghost vehicles, which helps prevent surprises when visibility changes. Additionally, detecting brake lights can signal problems ahead, increasing uncertainty in the world model. These adjustments lead to a more conservative model that limits action options, enhancing safety.

Level 2's primary objective is to minimize surprise, but it also addresses unexpected events through sensor, model, or world adjustments. This dual approach ensures that the model remains robust and safe, even in the face of uncertainty. Overall, the architecture emphasizes a hierarchical model structure that could be beneficial for autonomous systems, allowing for dynamic updates based on prediction errors.
