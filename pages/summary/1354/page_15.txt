This collection of references covers a wide range of topics related to automated driving systems, adversarial machine learning, predictive coding, and cognitive science. 

1. The NHTSA document outlines a vision for safety in automated driving systems, emphasizing the need for a structured approach to ensure safety in the development and deployment of these technologies.

2. Lin et al. discuss architectural implications and constraints of autonomous driving, suggesting that certain design considerations can accelerate the development of these systems.

3. Koller et al. present methods for model-based object tracking in traffic scenes, which is crucial for the functioning of autonomous vehicles.

4. Pearl and Mackenzie explore causation and the science of cause and effect, providing a theoretical framework that can be applied to understanding decision-making processes in autonomous systems.

5. Szegedy et al. and subsequent works by Moosavi-Dezfooli, Brown, and Carlini highlight the vulnerabilities of neural networks to adversarial examples, emphasizing the challenges in detecting and mitigating these threats.

6. The concept of novelty detection is reviewed by Pimentel et al., which is relevant for identifying unexpected events in driving environments.

7. Kingma and Welling introduce auto-encoding variational Bayes, a technique applicable in various machine learning contexts, including those relevant to autonomous systems.

8. The discussion on predictive coding by Wiese and others, including Friston's free-energy principle, presents a framework for understanding cognitive processes, which can inform the development of intelligent systems.

9. The dual-process theories of reasoning by Evans and Kahneman provide insights into decision-making, relevant for both human and machine cognition.

These references collectively contribute to the understanding of safety, machine learning vulnerabilities, cognitive theories, and the technical challenges in the development of autonomous driving systems.
