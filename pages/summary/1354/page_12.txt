The paper discusses the challenges and strategies involved in predictive processing within autonomous systems, particularly in the context of sensor errors and model updates. It posits that when significant prediction errors arise from a sensor or a group of similar sensors, it may be attributed to temporary issues such as sensor glitches or misinterpretations. In such cases, the system can rely on continuity in the environment or the redundancy of other sensors to maintain an accurate model.

If prediction errors are persistent or affect multiple sensors, it could indicate a hardware malfunction or environmental challenges, prompting potential actions, such as changing lanes to avoid direct sunlight affecting camera performance. The paper highlights that when numerous sensors report large errors, it signifies that the world has diverged from the expected model, necessitating adjustments to the model, which may include a complete rebuild based on new data.

The authors introduce a model-centered approach emphasizing predictive processing and a dual-process architecture, which allows autonomous systems to maintain a comprehensive and evolving model of the world. This model is regularly updated based on feedback from sensors, which report prediction errors. Small errors suggest a well-aligned model, while large errors indicate potential issues that warrant further investigation.

The assurance argument outlined in the paper focuses on ensuring that small prediction errors genuinely reflect a safe and accurate representation of the world. A primary concern is the possibility of systematic distortions, or "hallucinations," where the system fails to recognize certain objects, leading to unsafe scenarios. The paper underscores the importance of rigorous validation to prevent such occurrences and ensure the reliability of the autonomous system's perception.
