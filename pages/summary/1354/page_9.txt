The paper discusses the concept of predictive processing (PP) in the context of brain function, emphasizing the flow of predictions from higher to lower levels of processing and the correction feedback that flows in the opposite direction. The theory posits that the brain continuously predicts sensory input and adjusts its models to minimize prediction errors, a process that aligns with the notion of free energy. The architecture presented illustrates a two-level model where top-down predictions inform sensory processing, while prediction errors guide model refinement. This framework parallels methods from various fields such as neuroscience, control theory, and signal processing, all of which share similarities with PP despite differing terminologies and historical contexts. The model-centric approach highlights the importance of predictions in understanding sensory interpretation and action planning.
