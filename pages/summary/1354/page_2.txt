The paper discusses the challenges in ensuring the safety of autonomous systems, particularly self-driving cars, through extensive testing and the use of monitoring software. Traditional testing methods, referred to as "collecting miles," require an infeasible number of test miles to substantiate safety, as highlighted by studies from the RAND Corporation. 

An alternative approach involves utilizing conventional software as a monitor for autonomous functions, which can override the autonomous system when potential safety violations are detected. This monitoring process necessitates a clear understanding of the system architecture, which distinguishes between perception and action functions. Perception focuses on building an accurate model of the environment, while action pertains to executing safe maneuvers based on that model.

The paper emphasizes that both perception and action are critical for safety assurance. While AI and machine learning can aid in developing action plans, conventional software is essential for safety verification. Mobileye's "Responsibility-Sensitive Safety" model is presented as an example, wherein the action software must not contribute to accidents regardless of other vehicles' behavior. 

However, the complexity increases as monitors also require environmental models, which can be rudimentary and derived from sensor data. This may lead to limitations, as monitoring functions like "Forward Collision Warning" and "Automatic Emergency Braking" may not effectively address unpredictable failures in autonomous systems. Additionally, these reactive measures might conflict with the primary system's planned actions, potentially resulting in counterproductive outcomes.
