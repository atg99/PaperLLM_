The paper discusses methods for assessing the confidence in learning-enabled judgments by evaluating the similarity of current inputs to training data. One prevalent approach utilizes variational autoencoders to compress training data into a latent space, allowing reconstruction of inputs. If the current input closely resembles the training data, the reconstruction will also be similar, with Mahalanobis distance serving as a measure for this comparison. Another technique focuses on identifying key input features that influence judgments, although both methods can be vulnerable to minor alterations in input.

The paper also explores methods based on diversity and fault tolerance, emphasizing that redundancy is essential for fault tolerance. Ensemble systems, where multiple models collaborate or compete, can enhance performance but do not guarantee assurance as component failures may not be independent. Conversely, employing diverse sensor types in sensor fusion can potentially mitigate failures, though this is complex, as illustrated by the Uber self-driving car accident in Arizona. The incident highlights issues with object classification and tracking, where the car's sensor systems inconsistently identified a pedestrian as various object types in the moments leading up to the crash. This inconsistency in classification demonstrates the challenges of achieving reliable sensor fusion in critical applications.
