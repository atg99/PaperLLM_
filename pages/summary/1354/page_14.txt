The paper presents an architecture for enhancing the safety and assurance of autonomous systems, emphasizing the importance of a "safely approximate" accuracy in the world model maintained by perception functions. The architecture operates by utilizing a top-down approach for sensor interpretation, contrasting with traditional methods. Predictive processing plays a critical role; small prediction errors validate the model and sensor accuracy, while large errors signal potential faults or unexpected changes in the environment.

A key feature of this architecture is its reliance on uniform prediction errors as the foundation for runtime assurance and fault detection, thereby enabling autonomous assurance mechanisms. Additionally, AI functions at a secondary level provide situational awareness and strategies to mitigate surprises.

The authors acknowledge the relevance of their dual-process architecture to human cognitive theories and highlight ongoing experimental evaluations aimed at refining the approach and addressing associated challenges.

The paper concludes with acknowledgments of funding sources and contributions from reviewers and colleagues, while clarifying that the views expressed are those of the authors and do not reflect official positions of the funding agencies.
