The paper discusses a model-centered approach to assured autonomy, emphasizing the importance of prediction error in assessing model accuracy and detecting failures in autonomous systems. It argues that small prediction errors indicate satisfactory operation, while large errors signal unexpected developments or model flaws, thereby providing a method for runtime verification and failure detection.

The text introduces a dual-process architecture where an object detector may misidentify a painted bicycle on a van due to a prediction error. The paper suggests that if the object detector had access to contextual knowledge, such as traffic laws, it could more accurately identify the object. This leads to the idea that higher-level knowledge and inference capabilities should not be integrated into individual sensors but rather managed by a separate model refinement function. This function would analyze and augment the world model, allowing for more informed responses to situations that generate surprise.
