Model-Centered Assurance focuses on ensuring the safety of actions planned using a model of the real world. The key requirement for such a model is its fidelity to the real-world conditions necessary for planning safe actions. If M is a model of world W, actions deemed safe in M should also be safe when executed in W, although the reverse is not necessarily true. The model can be a conservative or safely approximate representation rather than a strictly accurate one.

In the context of self-driving cars, the model must be naturalistic to align with monitors expressed in everyday language, such as avoiding pedestrians. The model's content is dictated by the system's purpose, encompassing elements like a detailed map of the surroundings, nearby vehicles, pedestrians, fixed objects, road signs, and traffic signals. This can be structured as a single comprehensive model or as a coordinated collection of submodels.

A typical submodel might track detected objects, including their location, size, velocity, type, and inferred intent, while another could represent traffic lanes. The world model is constructed by interpreting and fusing inputs from various sensors, with a focus on image data from cameras due to its complexity and significance.

Lin and colleagues describe a vision processing pipeline for self-driving cars where image data is processed in parallel by an object detector and a localizer. The object detector identifies relevant objects, which are then tracked for movement prediction. The localizer establishes the vehicle's location, and the information from both is integrated into a unified model used by action functions, typically utilizing deep learning architectures like convolutional neural networks for object detection.
