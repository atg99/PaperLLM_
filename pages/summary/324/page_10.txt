The paper presents a comprehensive overview of various studies and advancements in active learning, sentiment analysis, and natural language processing (NLP). It references key works that address practical challenges in deploying active learning techniques, such as those by David Lowell et al. (2018) and Ameya Prabhu et al. (2019), which explore sampling biases and methodologies.

Pang and Lee's contributions (2004, 2005) are highlighted for their innovative approaches to sentiment analysis, utilizing subjectivity summarization and class relationships for categorization based on rating scales. Additionally, advancements in deep learning and NLP are surveyed, notably through the work of Xipeng Qiu et al. (2020) on pre-trained models.

The paper also discusses active learning strategies for various applications, including named entity recognition (Shen et al. 2017) and intent classification (Zhang and Zhang 2019), emphasizing the importance of deep learning frameworks in these contexts. The literature survey by Burr Settles (2009) provides foundational insights into active learning mechanisms.

Datasets employed in the research include Subjectivity, Polarity, AG's News, and others, which are crucial for training and evaluating the proposed models. This synthesis of literature underscores the evolving landscape of active learning and sentiment analysis, revealing both theoretical underpinnings and practical implementations in NLP.
