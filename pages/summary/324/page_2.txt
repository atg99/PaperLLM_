The paper investigates the effectiveness of various active learning (AL) strategies when applied to the BERT transformer model for text classification, particularly in scenarios with class imbalance and limited labeled data. The authors aim to assess the potential of traditional and advanced AL methods in enhancing BERT's classification performance across different datasets and settings. They define three scenarios: a balanced setting, an imbalanced setting with access to unbiased positive samples, and a more practical imbalanced setting with only biased samples available.

The findings reveal that AL strategies can significantly improve BERT's performance, especially in practical situations with constrained annotation budgets and skewed data. The research also includes a comprehensive framework, which is made publicly available, encompassing datasets, implementations of various AL strategies, and an automatic evaluation system to encourage further exploration in this area.

The paper highlights the evolution of active learning, particularly its adaptation to deep learning challenges, such as the computational demands of training deep neural networks (DNNs) and the need for batch mode active learning. While previous works have primarily focused on image classification, this study uniquely addresses BERT's application in text classification and systematically evaluates several advanced AL techniques, contributing valuable insights to the field.
