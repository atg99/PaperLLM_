The paper examines the effectiveness of various active learning (AL) strategies in selecting diverse and representative batches of data across different scenarios. The analysis reveals that batch-aware strategies, particularly DAL and Core-Set, excel in diversity, with DAL achieving the highest diversity scores. This success is attributed to the use of mini-queries which reduce redundancy in selected examples. Conversely, other strategies tend to select more redundant examples, especially in imbalanced scenarios, suggesting that combining these methods with diversity-enhancing techniques could improve prediction performance.

In terms of representativeness, DAL consistently outperforms other strategies as it is specifically designed for this purpose. The greedy core-set method, however, tends to select outliers, resulting in lower representativeness scores, particularly in imbalanced scenarios where query matches create bias. The paper notes that other strategies have low representativeness scores compared to DAL, indicating potential for improvement through the integration of representativeness-focused techniques.

The study also explores the synergy between different AL strategies by measuring the overlap of batches selected by paired strategies. Results indicate a generally low overlap, particularly in imbalanced scenarios, with higher overlap observed among uncertainty-based strategies. Notably, the highest overlap is between EGL and LC, suggesting future research could benefit from exploring combinations of strategies with low overlap to enhance classification performance.

In conclusion, the paper highlights the evolving landscape of natural language processing (NLP) driven by pre-trained models like BERT, which effectively utilize vast amounts of unlabeled data. The integration of active learning strategies raises important questions regarding their ability to enhance performance beyond the capabilities of these pre-trained models. This work is positioned as a pioneering systematic study in this intersection of active learning and NLP.
