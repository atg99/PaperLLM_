The paper discusses the performance of various active learning (AL) strategies in improving the efficiency of BERT training and inference. It provides runtime data for different AL strategies, highlighting that most runtimes are influenced by BERT inference, particularly for EGL and Dropout strategies which require more inference cycles or gradient calculations.

BERT was fine-tuned with specific parameters, including a batch size of 50 and a maximum sequence length of 100 tokens, which were determined to yield optimal results despite GPU memory constraints. An open-source AL research framework was introduced, allowing users to experiment with different strategies and assess their effectiveness on various datasets.

Results indicated that all AL strategies outperformed the Random baseline, particularly in imbalanced scenarios, where improvements in F1 scores ranged from 4 to 8%. This suggests that AL can significantly enhance BERT's performance when working with limited annotation budgets, especially in datasets with a low prior for positive examples. The statistical significance of these improvements was verified through Wilcoxon p-value calculations and Bonferroni corrections, confirming the robustness of the findings across different scenarios and datasets.
