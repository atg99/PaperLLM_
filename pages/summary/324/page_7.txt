Notably, no single AL strategy consistently excels across all scenarios, aligning with findings from previous research which highlighted the variability in AL effectiveness. A significance analysis confirmed no substantial performance differences among the AL strategies, although efficiency in terms of runtime varied.

The study also explored the impact of starting with biased versus unbiased positive examples. Initial models derived from biased seeds exhibited lower F1 scores compared to those starting with an unbiased set. However, through iterations, AL strategies effectively improved classification performance, particularly in imbalanced scenarios, where recall drove the enhancement in F1 scores. This underscores the capability of AL strategies to generalize beyond initial biases.

Further analysis focused on the comparative advantages of different AL strategies, particularly in terms of diversity and representativeness. Diversity, defined as the selection of varied examples, and representativeness, which assesses how well the selected examples reflect the overall data distribution, were measured. The findings indicated that while some strategies prioritized diversity, they may not always ensure that selected examples represent the dataset well, particularly in uncertainty-based approaches.

In conclusion, the application of AL strategies to BERT can enhance model performance, even when starting with biased examples. The analysis of diversity and representativeness can provide insights for developing more effective AL strategies in future research.
