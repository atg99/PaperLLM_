this paper investigates the integration of Active Learning (AL) techniques with BERT for binary text classification tasks, particularly in scenarios characterized by limited labeled data and class imbalance. The authors highlight the challenges faced in real-world applications, where the cost of labeling data is high and the desired class may be underrepresented.

The study presents a comprehensive empirical analysis of various AL strategies applied to BERT, demonstrating that these techniques can significantly enhance the performance of the model, especially when the initial labeled dataset is derived from biased keyword-based queries. The research underscores the effectiveness of AL in optimizing the labeling process by prioritizing the most informative instances, thus maximizing the utility of a small annotation budget.

The authors argue that while AL has been successfully utilized in conjunction with traditional classifiers, its application alongside modern deep learning models like BERT has not been sufficiently explored. This work aims to fill that gap, providing insights and a research framework to facilitate further investigations in this area. The findings indicate that employing AL with BERT can lead to improved classification outcomes in challenging data conditions, thereby contributing to the advancement of NLP methodologies.
