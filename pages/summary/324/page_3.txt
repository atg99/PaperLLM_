The paper discusses the characteristics and experimental setup of various datasets used for binary classification tasks, specifically focusing on class imbalance issues. Table 1 summarizes ten datasets, detailing their sizes, target classes, and prior probabilities. Datasets like AG's News and CoLA have higher positive class priors, while others like ISEAR exhibit lower priors, indicating varying levels of class imbalance.

The authors employed pool-based active learning (AL) with BERT as the classification model, exploring seven selection strategies across the ten fully labeled datasets. The initial annotation budget allowed for the labeling of 100 examples, forming a seed set L. This set is expanded in subsequent iterations, where batches of unlabeled instances are selected for labeling and added to L. The methodology emphasizes starting from scratch in each iteration to prevent overfitting.

Three scenarios for AL strategy implementation are considered: Balanced, Imbalanced, and a third scenario not explicitly detailed in the excerpt. The Balanced scenario applies to datasets with a positive class prior of 20% to 50%, where random sampling from the pool is feasible. For datasets with a prior of less than 15%, the authors adopt an Imbalanced approach, where the initial 100 labeled examples are exclusively positive, drawing attention to the challenges of training on skewed datasets.

Overall, the paper highlights the importance of dataset characteristics and selection strategies in active learning scenarios, particularly in addressing class imbalance in classification tasks.
