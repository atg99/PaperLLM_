The paper discusses the challenges and methodologies associated with active learning (AL) in the context of imbalanced datasets, particularly focusing on generating initial seeds of positive examples. It emphasizes the limitations of high-precision heuristics in real-world scenarios, where they may not yield a large training set but can still provide a small, biased sample to initiate active learning.

In the imbalanced-practical scenario, the authors simulate a realistic setting where a user aims to maximize the number of positive examples within a budget of 100 annotations. They design keyword-based queries tailored to specific datasets to retrieve instances likely enriched with positive examples. These queries are simple and practical but inherently biased since they rely on the keywords chosen.

The paper outlines several active learning strategies for selecting instances to label, including:

1. **Least Confidence (LC)** - Chooses instances for which the model shows the least certainty.
2. **Monte Carlo Dropout** - Similar to LC, but utilizes Monte Carlo Dropout to estimate uncertainty.
3. **Perceptron Ensemble (PE)** - Uses an ensemble of models to select instances with the highest uncertainty.
4. **Expected Gradient Length (EGL)** - Focuses on instances expected to influence the model significantly.
5. **Core-Set** - Selects instances that best cover the dataset in the learned representation space.
6. **Discriminative Active Learning (DAL)** - Aims to make the model representative of the entire pool.

The authors highlight the importance of these strategies as they span various state-of-the-art approaches in the AL domain, including uncertainty-sampling and diversity sampling.

In total, the study involves 2,520 fine-tuning experiments across multiple dataset-scenario combinations, initial seeds, and selection strategies, conducted on high-performance computing resources to facilitate parallel experiments.
