The paper discusses the concept of non-stationarity in stochastic processes and its implications for time series prediction, particularly in the context of sequence-to-sequence models. It introduces the notion of discrepancy as a measure of non-stationarity, demonstrating through various propositions that under certain conditions, such as covariance stationarity and specific hypothesis spaces, the discrepancy can be negligible. 

For instance, if the process is stationary or covariance stationary with a linear hypothesis space, the discrepancy is shown to be zero. Additionally, the paper highlights that even in non-stationary scenarios, such as periodic time series, the discrepancy remains negligible if all periods are observed with equal probability.

The paper then transitions to generalization bounds for time series prediction, focusing on the gap between the generalization error and empirical error. It outlines a methodology for partitioning the training set into collections where correlations between different time series are minimized, allowing for effective learning and generalization. 

Theorem 4.1 presents a bound on the generalization error, indicating that as the number of time series increases, the expected error decreases, suggesting that large datasets are advantageous for sequence-to-sequence models. It emphasizes the importance of balancing model complexity with data fit to control both discrepancy and Rademacher complexity.

The analysis further extends to independent time series, illustrating how the proposed bounds can be applied across different contexts, such as neuroscience and reinforcement learning. Overall, the paper provides insights into the relationship between non-stationarity, model complexity, and generalization in time series forecasting.
