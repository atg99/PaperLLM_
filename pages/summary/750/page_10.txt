The paper "Foundations of Sequence-to-Sequence Modeling for Time Series" by Mehryar Mohri and Afshin Rostamizadeh explores the theoretical underpinnings of sequence-to-sequence (seq2seq) models in the context of time series data. It builds upon previous works that established Rademacher complexity bounds for non-i.i.d. processes and stability bounds for stationary mixing processes. The authors emphasize the importance of understanding the complexities and limitations of seq2seq models when applied to time series forecasting.

Key concepts discussed include the application of neural networks for sequence learning, highlighting advancements made in the field, such as the use of recurrent neural networks (RNNs) and other deep learning architectures. The paper references various studies that address challenges in time series forecasting, including low-rank matrix estimation, norm-based capacity control, and probabilistic forecasting for hierarchical time series.

The authors argue for a rigorous theoretical framework to guide the development and evaluation of seq2seq models, ensuring their robustness and effectiveness in practical applications. They also discuss recent innovations in forecasting techniques, such as tensor-train RNNs and temporal regularized matrix factorization, which enhance predictive performance in high-dimensional settings.

Overall, the paper serves as a foundational text that connects theoretical insights with practical applications, advancing the understanding of seq2seq modeling in time series analysis.
