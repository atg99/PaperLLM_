The paper presents a comprehensive exploration of sequence-to-sequence modeling for time series, introducing a framework that enables models to learn mappings from past sequences to future values. It establishes the first generalization bounds for these models, utilizing new concepts of discrepancy and expected mixing coefficients to assess learning performance across various stochastic process families, including stationary and spatio-temporal time series.

A significant focus is given to hybrid models that blend local and sequence-to-sequence approaches by training a single model on a combination of local training sets. The authors derive learning guarantees for these hybrid models, demonstrating their effectiveness under conditions where the number of time series significantly exceeds the length of each time series or vice versa, while also noting potential costs associated with non-stationarity reflected in the new discrepancy term.

The paper contrasts the sample complexity of sequence-to-sequence models, which scales as O(√T/m) and is favorable when the number of time series is large compared to their length, against local models that scale as O(√log(m)/T), which are preferable under the opposite circumstances. The findings suggest that while hybrid models offer a favorable balance between complexity and performance, they do not inherently outperform local models.

Finally, the analysis indicates that the established results can be extended to scenarios involving test data with new, unseen time series, emphasizing the practical implications for real-world applications in time series forecasting.
