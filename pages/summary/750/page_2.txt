The paper explores the foundations of sequence-to-sequence modeling for time series forecasting, addressing when this approach is preferable over local modeling and vice versa. It provides the first generalization guarantees for sequence-to-sequence models, expressed through intuitive measures of non-stationarity and correlation strength among different time series. This allows for explicit dependency on the critical components of the learning problem.

The paper is structured as follows: Section 2 defines sequence-to-sequence and local modeling; Section 3 outlines the necessary analytical tools; Section 4 presents generalization bounds for sequence-to-sequence models; Section 5 compares the two modeling approaches; and Section 6 discusses hybrid models that combine elements of both.

In sequence-to-sequence modeling, the learner receives a multi-dimensional time series and aims to predict future values based on past sequences. This method contrasts with local modeling, which focuses on correlations among nearby observations in time by training separate hypotheses for each time series. The objective in both frameworks is to minimize generalization error.

The authors argue that their theoretical findings serve as a quantitative guide for practitioners in selecting the appropriate modeling approach based on the characteristics of their time series data. They also note that hybrid models leverage aspects of both methodologies, potentially benefiting from their respective strengths.
