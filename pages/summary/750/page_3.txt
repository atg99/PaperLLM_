The paper discusses various learning models for time series forecasting, focusing on local models, sequence-to-sequence models, and hybrid models. Table 1 summarizes these models, highlighting their training sets and hypotheses. Local models operate on individual time series, while sequence-to-sequence models train on pairs of input and target sequences. The hybrid model combines aspects of both approaches.

The authors provide the first generalization bounds for sequence-to-sequence and hybrid models, comparing these with local models. This comparison helps identify scenarios where each methodology is more effective. The paper emphasizes the practical considerations for choosing a model, noting that local models are easily parallelizable, whereas sequence-to-sequence models handle situations where historical data is absent, addressing the cold start problem.

The paper also addresses challenges in time series forecasting, such as correlations and non-stationarity, which violate the common assumption of independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) training and test data. To quantify these issues, the authors introduce concepts like mixing coefficients and discrepancy, which are essential for analyzing sequence-to-sequence learning.

Additionally, the paper defines expected mixing coefficients to measure dependencies between time series, along with unconditional coefficients. These metrics allow for a deeper understanding of the relationships between different series, which is vital for improving the accuracy of time series forecasting models. The authors plan to explore these coefficients further in subsequent sections, providing upper bounds for various stochastic processes.
