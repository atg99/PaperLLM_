The paper discusses the foundations of sequence-to-sequence modeling for time series, focusing on the implications of independence and correlation among dimensions in high-dimensional time series data. It presents Theorem 4.2, which establishes that when time series are independent, learning is primarily obstructed by non-stationarity, represented by a term ∆. In independent identically distributed (i.i.d.) scenarios, ∆ equals zero, leading to standard generalization results typical in regression.

The paper also explores correlated time series, which are common in various applications such as financial markets and climate observations. It introduces an autoregressive (AR) model for generating correlated time series, emphasizing the significance of the covariance structure in analyzing dependencies.

A key example provided is hierarchical time series, where energy demand forecasting illustrates a binary tree structure with time series generated at each leaf. The independence of time series within specific partitions is crucial for deriving effective bounds on learning. The paper proposes a method for constructing these partitions to ensure independence among the time series within each group.

Additionally, the paper addresses spatio-temporal processes, where historical data from different geographic locations are utilized for forecasting. It highlights the expectation that correlations between time series diminish with increasing geographical distance, suggesting a method for organizing time series based on their spatial relationships.

In summary, the paper lays the groundwork for understanding how to model time series data effectively, considering both independent and correlated structures, and discusses practical applications in hierarchical and spatio-temporal contexts.
