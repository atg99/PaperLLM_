The paper by Zelda Mariet and Vitaly Kuznetsov discusses the application of sequence-to-sequence models in time series forecasting, a field gaining traction due to the availability of extensive time series data and advancements in deep learning. The authors provide a theoretical analysis of this framework, contrasting it with classical time series models to guide practitioners in selecting appropriate methodologies.

The focus is on forecasting the next several steps of a multi-dimensional time series, where the number of dimensions can be substantial. Traditional approaches often treat each dimension as a separate univariate forecasting task, utilizing models like autoregressive and state-space models, as well as non-parametric methods such as recurrent neural networks (RNNs). These models typically analyze correlations between observations at consecutive time points.

In contrast, sequence-to-sequence modeling treats the multivariate time series as a collection of samples from an unknown distribution. In this framework, a unified model learns to predict future values based on past observations from multiple dimensions. Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks and RNNs are commonly employed in this context.

Despite advancements in understanding local models, the authors note a gap in the literature regarding the generalization properties of sequence-to-sequence models, particularly how these properties are influenced by the statistical characteristics of the underlying processes, such as non-stationarity and correlations. This raises important questions about the effectiveness and applicability of sequence-to-sequence approaches in practical forecasting scenarios.
