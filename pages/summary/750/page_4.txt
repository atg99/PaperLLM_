The authors define two measures of dependence: the mixing coefficients, which quantify the strength of dependence conditioned on past observations, and the unconditional dependence coefficients. They present a lemma that establishes an upper bound relating these two measures, indicating that the conditional dependence can be bounded by the unconditional dependence plus an expectation term involving covariances.

A significant concept introduced is that of tangent collections, where time series are considered independent. This allows for the simplification of analyses involving correlated time series by transforming them into independent series, facilitating more straightforward modeling.

The paper also introduces a novel notion of discrepancy tailored for sequence-to-sequence modeling, which quantifies the non-stationarity of stochastic processes by taking into account both a hypothesis set and a loss function. This discrepancy is shown to form a pseudo-metric and can be upper bounded by other well-known divergence measures.

Furthermore, the authors define a symmetric discrepancy that can be computed directly from data without needing test labels, making it a practical tool for assessing non-stationarity. The paper provides propositions that relate the symmetric discrepancy to expected discrepancies, establishing bounds that incorporate Rademacher complexity and other terms related to the mixing coefficients.

Overall, the paper lays a theoretical foundation for understanding dependence and non-stationarity in sequence-to-sequence modeling of time series, offering tools for analyzing and addressing challenges in this area.
