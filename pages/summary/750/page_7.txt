This paper discusses the learning guarantees for sequence-to-sequence models in comparison to local models, particularly focusing on the generalization gap. The generalization gap for local models is defined in relation to empirical error and discrepancy measures. 

The authors leverage existing results to derive a high probability bound for local models, incorporating the concept of sequential covering numbers. They establish that when the number of time series is relatively small compared to their length, treating each time series as an independent problem leads to better generalization bounds. Conversely, when the number of time series significantly exceeds the number of time steps, sequence-to-sequence learning tends to outperform local models.

The paper highlights the complexity of directly comparing the bounds of both models due to different discrepancies involved. Specific examples illustrate scenarios where either discrepancy measure may be negligible, suggesting a nuanced approach to model selection based on empirical estimates.

In conclusion, the authors note that similar results to those of sequence-to-sequence models can be extended to multivariate local models, with the sample complexity scaling differently. This work provides insights into selecting the appropriate modeling approach based on the characteristics of the data at hand.
