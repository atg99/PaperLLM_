(4) Pruning schedule. The pruning schedule dictates when and how often pruning occurs during the training process. This can vary from one-time pruning at the end of training to iterative pruning, where weights are removed in stages throughout the training cycle. The choice of schedule can significantly impact the performance and efficiency of the pruning process.

The paper provides an extensive overview of the pruning paradigm in neural networks, highlighting its historical context and future directions. It discusses the generic formulation of pruning, the significance of model sequences, and the essential elements of a pruned model, including topology and parameter values. The authors categorize pruning approaches into four classic topics: sparsity structure, pruning ratio, pruning criterion, and pruning schedule, each with its implications for model efficiency and performance. The analysis emphasizes the balance between model compression and acceleration, with structured pruning being favored for speed and unstructured pruning for flexibility in sparse training methods. The paper concludes with a call for further exploration of open problems within the pruning landscape, setting the stage for future research endeavors.
