The paper categorizes pruning methods into two main groups: sparse training and sparse selection, with a focus on the evolution of these techniques since their inception. The core principle driving most pruning criteria is the selection of weights whose removal leads to minimal change in loss. This concept has historical roots in previous work and continues to be relevant in current discussions of pruning strategies.

Pruning schedules are also a critical aspect of these methods, with three primary types identified: one-shot, progressive, and iterative pruning. Progressive and iterative pruning are generally found to outperform one-shot methods, as they afford the network more time to adapt during the training process.

The paper highlights the initial works of Lottery Ticket Hypothesis (LTH) and SNIP, which demonstrate that non-trivially sparse networks can achieve full accuracy. LTH utilizes masks derived from pretrained models while SNIP employs masks from initialized models. This distinction has led to subsequent research aimed at refining pruning criteria, including the exploration of dynamic masks that adapt during training.

Additionally, the discussion of sparse selection reveals that randomly selected networks can exhibit surprising accuracy without training, leading to new avenues of research in this field. The paper concludes by outlining the major methods in both sparse training and sparse selection, emphasizing the ongoing relevance and development of pruning techniques in neural network optimization.
