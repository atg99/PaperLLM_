The paper discusses the Lottery Ticket Hypothesis (LTH) and its follow-up works, which are categorized into two main groups: extensions and sanity-checks. 

1. **Extensions**: This group explores broader applications and validations of LTH beyond the initial small datasets (MNIST and CIFAR-10). Notable contributions include:
   - LTH+ which applies LTH to ResNet50 on ImageNet.
   - OneTicket demonstrating that winning tickets generalize across various datasets.
   - Research showing the phenomenon's presence in Reinforcement Learning and Natural Language Processing tasks.
   - Lifelong learning methods that identify winning tickets through bottom-up and top-down pruning.
   - Innovations like Pruning Aware Critical (PrAC) set, which reduces training data while maintaining effectiveness.
   - Early-bird tickets identified early in training, achieving energy savings while preserving performance.
   - Theoretical explorations aimed at understanding the dynamics of LTH and its validity through various frameworks.

2. **Sanity-checks**: This group critically evaluates the validity of LTH, highlighting ongoing debates regarding experimental setups. Key findings include:
   - Contradictory conclusions drawn by different studies, particularly regarding the comparison of winning tickets to randomly initialized subnetworks.
   - Observations about the correlation between initialized and final weights, suggesting that the winning property may relate to insufficient pretraining.
   - The introduction of static masks, such as SNIP and GraSP, which propose new criteria for pruning based on loss preservation and gradient signal preservation.

In summary, the extensions focus on applying and understanding LTH in diverse contexts, while the sanity-checks critically assess its validity and explore alternative approaches to pruning and weight initialization.
