This paper presents a comprehensive survey on the emerging paradigm of neural network pruning at initialization (PaI), which diverges from the traditional approach of pruning after training (PaT). The authors begin by establishing a general framework for neural network pruning and then delve into a structured literature review that categorizes PaI methods into two main tracks: sparse training and sparse selection.

The concept of PaI involves pruning a randomly initialized neural network, aiming to create a sparse network that can achieve performance comparable to that of a fully trained dense network. This approach challenges the conventional wisdom that pruning should occur post-training, as previous research suggested that training a sparse network from scratch often results in inferior performance compared to pruning a pre-trained model.

The review highlights significant methods within the PaI landscape, including the Lottery Ticket Hypothesis (LTH) and Sparse Neural Network Initialization (SNIP), both of which have shown promise in demonstrating that sparse networks can be effectively trained from initialization. The paper also contrasts PaI with PaT, emphasizing the differences in weight sourcing and training methodologies.

In conclusion, the authors underscore the growing interest and potential of PaI while identifying open questions and challenges that remain in this domain. Additionally, they provide a code base to facilitate benchmarking and validation of various PaI techniques, contributing to the advancement of research in this area.
