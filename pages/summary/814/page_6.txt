The paper discusses the concept of pruning at initialization (PaI) in neural networks and contrasts it with traditional pruning after training (PaT). The key shift in approach is that PaI uses a randomly initialized model to inherit weights for a sparse network, aiming for efficient training rather than inference. 

The authors highlight that while there is significant overlap between PaI and PaT, particularly in sparsity techniques like magnitude pruning, PaI introduces new considerations regarding weight initialization and knowledge transfer. Unlike PaT, which builds on knowledge from a redundant model, PaI questions whether early-stage weights contain sufficient information for effective pruning, suggesting a paradigm shift in understanding neural network training.

The paper identifies several open problems shared with PaT, such as optimizing sparsity structures, refining pruning criteria, and improving the scheduling of the sparsification process. Despite the theoretical promise of PaI, practical performance still lags behind PaT methods, as evidenced by experiments showing traditional methods outperforming newer ones. The authors also note the underdevelopment of sparse training libraries, which limits practical speedup benefits from sparse training.

To support future research, the authors provide a code base designed to facilitate experimentation with various PaI methods. The paper concludes by emphasizing the need for deeper insights into the performance gaps between PaI and PaT, as well as advancements in sparse matrix libraries, to realize the potential of PaI in practical applications.
