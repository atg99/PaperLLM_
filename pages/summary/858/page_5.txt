The paper presents a parameter-free convolutional self-attention model aimed at enhancing feature extraction of neighboring elements in machine translation tasks. Experimental results on various translation benchmarks, including WMT14 English to German, WMT17 Chinese to English, and WAT17 Japanese to English, demonstrate that the proposed model consistently outperforms existing models, thus confirming the importance of modeling locality and distance information. 

The findings indicate that the convolutional self-attention networks (CS ANs) yield better performance than traditional convolutional neural networks (CNNs), emphasizing the advantages of dynamic weight extraction over fixed parameters. The model's effectiveness is validated across different language pairs, showcasing its universality and capability to improve translation accuracy, particularly in capturing phrasal patterns through locality modeling.

Further analysis reveals that the proposed models exhibit significant improvements in larger n-gram granularities, which highlights the enhanced ability of self-attention mechanisms to capture phrasal information. The interactions among different heads during attention processing are also found to complement locality modeling, suggesting that feature interaction is crucial for performance enhancement.

In conclusion, the empirical results affirm the benefits of locality modeling in self-attention networks, the effectiveness of feature interaction across multiple heads, and the superiority of dynamic weights for local feature extraction. The authors suggest that the model's application could extend beyond machine translation to other sequence modeling tasks, paving the way for future research in diverse fields such as reading comprehension and sentiment analysis.
