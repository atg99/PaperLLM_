The paper introduces Convolutional Self-Attention Networks (CSANs), an enhancement of Self-Attention Networks (SANs) aimed at addressing their limitations in modeling dependencies among neighboring elements and interactions across multiple attention heads. While SANs have demonstrated strong performance in various natural language processing tasks, they often overlook the relationships between nearby elements and fail to exploit interactions among different linguistic properties captured by multi-head attention.

CSANs enhance locality by restricting the attention scope to a window of neighboring elements using a parameter-free 1-dimensional convolution. Additionally, the model incorporates a 2-dimensional convolution to facilitate interactions between features learned by different attention heads, thereby improving the overall representation of the input sequence.

The authors evaluate the CSANs on three translation tasks: WMT14 English-to-German, WMT17 Chinese-to-English, and WAT17 Japanese-to-English. The experimental results indicate that CSANs consistently outperform the strong Transformer baseline across various language pairs, demonstrating improvements in translation quality and training efficiency. Overall, this work suggests that incorporating locality and inter-head interactions can significantly enhance the performance of SANs without introducing additional parameters.
