The paper presents a comparative analysis of various model enhancements to the Transformer architecture, specifically for the WMT14 English-German translation task. It evaluates the impact of different approaches on model parameters, training speed, and BLEU scores, which measure translation quality. The baseline model is the original Transformer with 88 million parameters and a BLEU score of 27.31. Subsequent models introduced various techniques, such as Bidirectional attention, Relational Positional encoding, and Local Attention mechanisms, which generally resulted in incremental improvements in BLEU scores, with the best performing model being the 2D-Causal Self Attention Networks (2D-CS ANs) achieving a BLEU score of 28.18.

The study also discusses the training data used for different translation tasks, including English-German, Chinese-English, and Japanese-English, highlighting the importance of using large corpora for effective model training. It notes the use of byte-pair encoding for vocabulary reduction, enabling more efficient training.

Furthermore, the paper investigates the effects of window size and area size on translation quality, identifying that a local size of 11 tokens for the 1D-CS ANs yields the best results. It also explores the number of attention heads in 2D-CS ANs, noting that while increasing heads can enhance translation quality, there is a threshold beyond which performance declines, suggesting that too many heads may lead to overly similar contributions from different attention mechanisms.

Overall, the findings underscore the significance of locality modeling in enhancing translation performance while providing insights into optimal configurations for attention mechanisms.
