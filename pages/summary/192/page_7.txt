The paper presents HiGAN+, an enhanced handwriting imitation GAN that employs disentangled representations for improved style transfer in handwritten text generation. A key challenge addressed is the lack of precise labels for various writing styles, such as stroke thickness and character shape. To circumvent the need for extensive manual annotations, the authors utilize writer identity as a proxy for calligraphic style, assuming that each individual’s writing style is unique and consistent. 

The model optimizes a writer identifier to minimize cross-entropy loss while maximizing adversarial loss, allowing it to discern which writer a reference image belongs to. The style-conditioned synthetic images are enforced to closely match the style of reference images to facilitate effective disentanglement of calligraphic styles.

The paper introduces several regularization techniques to enhance performance. The style reconstruction loss promotes a direct relationship between synthetic images and style features, encouraging diversity in outputs. Content reconstruction loss aids in aligning content and style, while KL-divergence loss regularizes the latent space to follow a normal distribution, crucial for stochastic style sampling.

To refine local details in generated handwriting, the authors critique traditional style transfer methods, which often fail with handwriting due to the lack of spatial alignment. They propose a contextual loss that evaluates high-level feature similarity without requiring spatial correspondence, allowing for slight spatial deformations. Additionally, a local patch loss is introduced to enhance local consistency by evaluating the fidelity of image patches, addressing issues of blurred or distorted characters in the generated images.

Overall, HiGAN+ demonstrates an innovative approach to handwriting imitation by leveraging writer identity and advanced regularization techniques, resulting in improved style transfer and fidelity in synthetic handwriting generation.
