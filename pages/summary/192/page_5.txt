The paper presents HiGAN+, an advanced generative model for handwriting imitation that enhances visual quality, scalability, compactness, and style transferability compared to existing GANs for handwritten text generation. HiGAN+ builds upon the previous HiGAN model, improving handwriting style transfer accuracy and visual fidelity.

The methodology begins with the problem formulation, where the authors aim to synthesize realistic handwriting images based on arbitrary textual content and calligraphic styles. The generator, denoted as G, can produce handwritten texts that are not limited to predefined corpora or out-of-vocabulary words. It employs a style encoder, E, to either randomly sample styles or extract them from reference images.

The network architecture includes a style-controlled handwritten text generator that mimics the local sequential writing process observed in human handwriting. This generator creates character-level embeddings rather than a fixed-size representation, allowing it to handle variable-length images. The character embeddings are individually processed and combined into a text map, which serves as a style-invariant representation. The generator then applies conditional batch normalization to inject style features, enabling the synthesis of long handwritten texts with varied styles.

Additional components enhance the training process, including a global discriminator for overall image fidelity, a patch discriminator for local texture detail verification, a style encoder to extract calligraphic styles, a writer identifier to condition the synthesis on specific styles, and a text recognizer to ensure the generated handwriting is readable.

Overall, HiGAN+ significantly advances handwriting imitation through its innovative architecture and training strategies, allowing for the generation of diverse and realistic handwritten texts.
