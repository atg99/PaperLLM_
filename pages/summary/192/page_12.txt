The paper presents a quantitative comparison of different configurations for handwritten text synthesis, analyzing various methods and their performance metrics such as Inception Score (IS), Fréchet Inception Distance (FID), Kernel Inception Distance (KID), Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR), Mean Structural Similarity Index (MSSIM), Word Image Error Rate (WIER), and Word Error Rate (WER). The configurations are identified as AL, BA, CB, DC, ED, and the ground truth, with the best results highlighted in bold.

The authors emphasize the importance of specific losses like writer identification loss (Lid) and style reconstruction loss (Lstyle) in achieving accurate handwriting style transfer. Configuration (C), associated with HiGAN, showed improved performance in terms of lower WIER and higher FID scores, indicating better style transfer and visual quality. 

To enhance style consistency, the contextual loss (Lctx) was introduced in configuration (D), addressing the challenges posed by arbitrary handwriting and the lack of texture in handwritten images. The results demonstrate the effectiveness of this loss in measuring style similarity without requiring spatial alignment.

Further refinement was achieved by adding local patch loss (Lpatch) in configuration (E), leading to HiGAN+. This approach focuses on local texture details, crucial for high-resolution images, and ensures better style consistency by evaluating image patches. Figures illustrate the improvements in character clarity and overall visual quality.

Lastly, a comparison between PatchGAN and LPL is made, highlighting that while PatchGAN operates on the entire image, LPL allows for a more complex patch discrimination process by physically splitting the image into patches. The authors suggest that LPL, with its flexible architecture, can outperform PatchGAN, supporting their hypothesis with quantitative data from the study.
