The paper discusses the capabilities of HiGAN+, a model designed for handwriting synthesis, particularly focusing on its ability to perform one-shot style transfer and generate handwritten images from arbitrary-length texts. HiGAN+ effectively imitates the stylistic elements of reference samples, such as writing slant, thickness, and character shape, while maintaining the integrity of the textual content. The model can generate images conditioned on various text lengths without being limited to predefined corpora, allowing for the synthesis of long text strings by omitting spaces and converting text into individual character embeddings. Additionally, HiGAN+ demonstrates proficiency in generating complete handwritten paragraphs that closely resemble real handwriting. The results indicate that the synthesized handwriting is highly realistic and largely indistinguishable from authentic samples, showcasing the versatility and effectiveness of HiGAN+ in the domain of handwriting synthesis.
