In this paper, J. Gan et al. discuss advancements in handwriting synthesis, focusing on their proposed model HiGAN+. Previous work, including HiGAN, struggled with generating high-quality handwritten images, often producing blurred textures and distorted characters. The paper highlights the limitations of existing GAN-based style transfer methods, which typically require paired training data and are not fully effective for text- and style-conditioned handwriting synthesis.

The authors outline key differences between handwriting synthesis and glyph font synthesis, emphasizing that handwriting involves more variability in style and does not require extensive annotations. They identify three main areas for improvement in HiGAN+: generation quality, model compactness, and comprehensive evaluation metrics.

To enhance generation quality, HiGAN+ introduces a local patch loss to refine image details and a contextual loss to improve style consistency. For model compactness, HiGAN+ reuses components from the writer identifier, eliminating the need for separate style encoding modules. The evaluation metrics are expanded to include Inception Score, Kernel Inception Distance, Peak Signal to Noise Ratio, Mean Structural Similarity, and a newly proposed metric for style transferability, WIER.

The authors present extensive experimental results demonstrating that HiGAN+ significantly outperforms previous models, achieving state-of-the-art performance in handwriting synthesis tasks. The research showcases the potential of HiGAN+ to advance the field and addresses the challenges of generating realistic handwritten text from arbitrary-length inputs.
