The paper presents a method for improving the synthesis of handwriting images using a model termed HiGAN+. It introduces a patch discriminator that processes image patches rather than full images, allowing for greater flexibility and potentially enhanced synthesis performance compared to existing methods like PatchGAN. The overall objective of the model is structured around a min-max adversarial game, where various components, including a global discriminator, a patch discriminator, a text recognizer, and a writer identifier, are optimized. 

The training strategy involves pre-training the writer identifier and text recognizer separately from the adversarial training. This pre-training enhances their robustness and speeds up the overall training process for HiGAN+. Furthermore, the paper suggests reusing the writer identifier as a style encoder, benefiting from shared parameters to create a more efficient model while avoiding reliance on large VGG backbones that are not optimized for handwriting.

To optimize the model effectively, the paper employs a gradient balancing strategy that dynamically adjusts the hyper-parameters associated with different loss terms, thereby stabilizing the training and improving the synthesis results. This approach allows for a more refined optimization process and helps achieve better performance in handwriting generation tasks.
