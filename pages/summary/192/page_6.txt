The paper introduces HiGAN+, a model designed for synthesizing handwritten text by leveraging disentangled styles. During training, HiGAN+ performs three main functions: it synthesizes handwritten texts based on different styles, applies reconstruction regularization, and refines local image details to enhance visual quality. At test time, the model can generate diverse handwritten texts by sampling styles from a prior distribution or imitate calligraphic styles from reference samples. 

To train HiGAN+, a multi-writer handwriting dataset is utilized, which includes handwriting images, labeled texts, and corresponding writer identities. The model is trained using a large open corpus to allow for arbitrary text generation. The training process employs various objective functions, including adversarial loss, text recognition loss, and writer identification loss. 

The adversarial loss uses a generative adversarial network (GAN) framework, where the generator synthesizes images that are indistinguishable from real handwriting samples. The text recognition loss ensures the generated images maintain the desired textual content by utilizing a handwriting recognizer. The writer identification loss focuses on disentangling calligraphic styles to enable the generation of diverse images with different textual contents while maintaining stylistic consistency. Overall, HiGAN+ aims to improve the realism and readability of synthetic handwritten images.
