The paper addresses the challenges in synthesizing realistic handwriting images, a task that remains difficult for machines despite advancements in handwriting recognition technologies. The authors highlight the importance of handwriting imitation (HI), which involves generating diverse handwriting images based on arbitrary textual content and mimicking the calligraphic styles of reference samples. They emphasize that humans can easily visualize and create new handwriting styles after limited exposure, suggesting that machines could achieve similar capabilities if they learn to disentangle styles from content.

The paper contrasts HI with conventional image generation (CIG), noting three key differences: the need for variable-sized outputs, the requirement for precise textual content, and the challenges of style transfer due to the sparse graphical nature of handwriting. These differences complicate the application of traditional methods used in CIG.

Several existing methods for handwritten text synthesis using Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) are reviewed, revealing that none have fully addressed the complexities of HI. Previous approaches, such as ScrabbleGAN and TS-GAN, have limitations in style imitation and quality when handling out-of-vocabulary words or when using a single reference sample. The authors conclude that current state-of-the-art GANs have not successfully resolved the challenges posed by HI, indicating a need for further research in this area.
