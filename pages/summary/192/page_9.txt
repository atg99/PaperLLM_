The paper presents HiGAN+, an enhanced handwriting generation model that utilizes a disentangled representation approach to improve the quality and diversity of generated handwritten text. The authors highlight the distinct challenges of handwritten imitation compared to character generation, emphasizing the need for varied output sizes, arbitrary text content, and effective style transfer.

To evaluate the performance of HiGAN+, the authors propose comprehensive metrics that assess visual quality, readability, and style transferability. Visual quality is measured using metrics like Inception Score (IS), Fréchet Inception Distance (FID), Kernel Inception Distance (KID), Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR), and Mean Structural Similarity Index (MSSIM). Readability is assessed through Word Error Rate (WER), while style transferability is evaluated using Writer Identification Error Rate (WIER), a novel metric introduced in this work.

The experimental setup utilizes the IAM dataset, consisting of 63K handwritten English words from 500 writers. The authors detail their implementation specifics, including the use of a GeForce RTX 3090 GPU and various hyperparameters for model training. HiGAN+ is compared with several state-of-the-art handwritten text generation methods, showing its efficacy in generating high-quality, diverse, and readable handwriting.

Qualitative analysis demonstrates HiGAN+'s ability to generate arbitrary handwritten words in various styles, with a focus on both latent-guided and reference-guided synthesis techniques. The results indicate that HiGAN+ can produce human-readable text with stylistic variations while maintaining the integrity of cursive ligatures. Overall, the paper contributes significantly to the field of handwriting generation by addressing previous limitations and introducing effective evaluation metrics.
