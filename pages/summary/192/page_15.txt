The paper presents HiGAN+, an advanced handwriting imitation model that utilizes disentangled representations for improved handwritten text synthesis. It compares HiGAN+ with various existing GAN models like ScrabbleGAN, ST-GAN, GANwriting, and HWT across multiple quantitative metrics, showcasing its superior performance in Inception Score (IS), Fréchet Inception Distance (FID), Kernel Inception Distance (KID), Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR), and Mean Structural Similarity Index (MSSIM). HiGAN+ achieves the highest IS and lowest FID, indicating its capability in generating high-quality synthetic handwriting.

The study also addresses the limitations of HiGAN+, noting difficulties in synthesizing realistic punctuation marks and digits due to their rarity in the training data. Additionally, it prefers generating neat handwriting over extremely scribbled characters, which can be adjusted through hyperparameter tuning during training.

Human evaluations, including a user plausibility study and a user preference study, confirm that HiGAN+ generates plausible handwriting indistinguishable from real samples. Participants were able to discern between genuine and generated images, with HiGAN+ receiving favorable comparisons against competing GANs in terms of visual quality.

Overall, HiGAN+ demonstrates significant advancements in handwriting synthesis, although it recognizes inherent challenges in capturing the full variability of human handwriting.
