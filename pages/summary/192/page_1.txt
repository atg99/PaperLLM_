The paper presents HiGAN+, a generative adversarial network designed for handwriting imitation that leverages disentangled representations. The authors, Ji Gan, Weiqiang Wang, Jiaxu Leng, and Xinbo Gao, highlight the disparity between human and machine learning capabilities, particularly in the context of handwriting. Humans can quickly learn to imitate handwriting styles from few examples and can generate realistic handwritten text, a task that remains challenging for machines.

HiGAN+ aims to bridge this gap by enabling machines to mimic human-like handwriting through a novel approach that incorporates writer-specific auxiliary loss and contextual loss for effective one-shot handwriting style transfer. This method enhances both global and local consistency in synthetic handwriting images by refining local details.

The authors conducted extensive experiments, including human evaluations, demonstrating that HiGAN+ outperforms existing state-of-the-art GANs in visual quality, scalability, compactness, and style transferability. The findings underscore the potential of HiGAN+ for realistic handwritten text synthesis, thus contributing significantly to the field of handwriting imitation and generative models.

The work is supported by various grants from the National Nature Science Foundation of China and other projects. The paper was published in ACM Transactions on Graphics in September 2022.
