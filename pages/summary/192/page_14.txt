The paper discusses the performance of various Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) in synthesizing handwritten paragraphs and long texts. It highlights the advantages of HiGAN+, which incorporates a patch discriminator to enhance the texture details and achieve better global and local consistency in the generated handwriting images. Qualitative results, shown in Figures 14 and 15, indicate that HiGAN+ produces clearer and more precise handwriting images compared to other GANs.

A quantitative evaluation is presented in Table 5, comparing different GANs on visual quality across the entire test set. Additional metrics are provided in Table 6, assessing the generated samples against real ones under various settings. While ScrabbleGAN and ST-GAN exhibit high Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) and Word Error Rate (WIER), indicating poor visual quality and inability to mimic calligraphic styles, HiGAN shows improvements over GANwriting, achieving lower WIER and better readability.

The results demonstrate that HiGAN+ significantly outperforms other state-of-the-art GANs in handwriting synthesis, achieving superior visual quality and effective one-shot handwriting style transfer. The model's compactness is noted, as HiGAN+ utilizes a specifically designed style encoder instead of a large pre-trained VGG backbone, as seen in Table 7.

Lastly, the paper includes a failure case analysis (Figure 16) to identify the limitations of HiGAN+, providing insights into areas for potential improvement.
