The paper presents HiGAN+, a novel generative model designed for handwriting imitation based on disentangled representations. HiGAN+ is capable of generating diverse and realistic handwritten texts that are conditioned on arbitrary textual content and calligraphic styles. The authors address limitations of conventional style transfer techniques by introducing a contextual loss to enhance style consistency in synthetic images. Additionally, they refine local details of generated handwriting using a Local Patch Loss (LPL) and optimize the architecture by reusing early layers of a writer identifier for style encoding.

The effectiveness of HiGAN+ is demonstrated through extensive experiments, including user studies, where it outperformed competitive GANs in visual quality, scalability, compactness, and style transferability for handwritten text synthesis. Despite its limitations in synthesizing meaningful handwriting due to the arbitrary nature of human writing, HiGAN+ represents a significant step toward realistic handwriting generation, contributing to advancements in artificial intelligence.

The study's findings are summarized in user plausibility and preference studies, where HiGAN+ received the majority of votes, indicating its superiority over other methods. The source code for HiGAN+ is publicly available for further research and application.
