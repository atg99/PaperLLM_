The paper presents HiGAN+, an advanced handwriting imitation GAN that utilizes disentangled representations for improved performance. The results indicate that HiGAN+ can achieve high-quality one-shot handwriting imitation, demonstrating its capability to generalize beyond mere memorization of training data.

In the generalization analysis, two main methods are employed: style interpolation and text editing. The style interpolation process showcases the model's ability to smoothly transition between different calligraphic styles while maintaining the original text, confirming the continuity of the latent style space. For text editing, HiGAN+ can modify text by changing one character at a time, producing continuous changes in handwriting while preserving the calligraphic style. It successfully generates out-of-vocabulary words, indicating its ability to create novel handwriting images.

The study also includes UMAP visualizations of latent vectors, revealing that HiGAN+ can effectively cluster handwriting styles from the same author while separating different authors, demonstrating its ability to disentangle handwriting styles.

An ablation study further validates the contributions of various components in HiGAN+. The baseline model struggles with style imitation and produces poor-quality images due to the absence of a style encoder. Enhancements to the baseline, such as incorporating a style encoder and regularizing the latent space, improve performance but still yield high word error rates, indicating that further refinements are necessary for precise handwriting imitation.

Overall, HiGAN+ represents a significant advancement in handwriting generation, balancing quality and generalization through its innovative architecture.
