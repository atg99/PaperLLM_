The paper discusses HiGAN+, an advanced handwriting generation model that utilizes disentangled representations to improve the synthesis of handwritten text. It specifically compares the performance of different methods, including PatchGAN and LPL, on the IAM dataset, highlighting that LPL demonstrates superior capabilities with its more powerful patch discriminators.

In the context of handwriting imitation in diverse environments, the authors showcase HiGAN+'s ability to replicate various calligraphic styles found in the GNHK dataset, emphasizing its effectiveness in generating extreme handwriting styles without additional training images. Qualitative results indicate that HiGAN+ significantly surpasses baseline models in terms of visual quality.

A comprehensive comparison with state-of-the-art GANs reveals that while models like ScrabbleGAN and HTW struggle with style imitation and character clarity, HiGAN+ excels by employing a contextual loss to enhance style consistency and local texture details. The paper presents quantitative comparisons showing HiGAN+ achieves higher Inception Score and lower FID and WER, indicating its superiority in generating realistic and stylistically accurate handwritten text.

Overall, HiGAN+ represents a significant advancement in handwriting synthesis, demonstrating improved visual quality and the ability to capture complex calligraphic styles effectively.
