The paper presents HiGAN+, an enhanced version of the previously proposed handwriting imitation GAN (HiGAN). HiGAN was designed to generate diverse and realistic handwriting images based on arbitrary text and calligraphic styles, but it suffered from issues like blurred and distorted characters. HiGAN+ addresses these problems significantly, improving the visual quality of synthetic images and the accuracy of handwriting style transfer.

Key contributions of HiGAN+ include the introduction of contextual loss for better style consistency, local patch refinement for improved local consistency, and a more compact architecture that reuses the writer identifier for style encoding. The paper also introduces comprehensive performance metrics for evaluating GANs in handwritten text synthesis, including a novel writer identification error rate (WIER) to assess handwriting style transferability, which has not been previously examined.

Extensive experiments, including Turing tests, demonstrate that HiGAN+ outperforms other state-of-the-art GANs in terms of visual quality, scalability, compactness, and style transferability. The paper highlights the limitations of traditional and recent approaches to handwriting synthesis, emphasizing the advantages of using image-based methods over trajectory-based ones, particularly given the practical challenges of collecting handwriting trajectories.

Overall, HiGAN+ represents a significant advancement in the field of handwriting synthesis, overcoming limitations of earlier models and achieving superior results in generating handwritten text images.
