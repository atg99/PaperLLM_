The paper presents findings on the comparative performance of memory slots versus original contexts in language models, specifically evaluating the Integrated Contextual Attention Encoding (ICAE) method against traditional approaches. In Table 4, various systems are assessed based on their performance in the P WC test set, revealing that models utilizing ICAE, such as Llama-7b and StableLM-7b, consistently outperform those relying on original contexts, particularly GPT-4, in terms of win/lose ratios.

Table 5 further examines the effectiveness of different memory slot lengths and pretraining setups. The results indicate that pretrained ICAE models exhibit significantly lower hallucination rates compared to their non-pretrained counterparts. The study suggests that pretraining enhances the model's memory capabilities, mirroring human memory training. Notably, memory slots demonstrated superior performance over 128-token summaries generated by GPT-4, establishing their compactness and informativeness.

The analysis section discusses the scalability of ICAE, confirming that more powerful target language models yield better compression performance. Additionally, empirical tests show that ICAE enhances inference efficiency, achieving over a 2x speedup in processing time with context compression.

Overall, the findings underscore the advantages of ICAE in memory utilization, compression efficiency, and the importance of pretraining in improving model performance.
