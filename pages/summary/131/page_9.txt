In this paper, the authors introduce the In-context Autoencoder (ICAE), a method designed to enhance the capacity of large language models (LLMs) to manage long contexts. The ICAE achieves this by generating compact memory slots that effectively summarize original contexts, allowing LLMs to retain more information or convey the same content with shorter inputs. This method is presented as a scalable and parameter-efficient alternative to existing techniques, which often require fine-tuning of LLMs or involve complex recursive compression processes.

The authors highlight the limitations of current methods, such as Mu et al. (2023), which necessitate tuning LLMs to work with summary vectors, and other approaches that address context limits through divide-and-conquer strategies. They also reference related work on compressing concepts into tokens and general-purpose compression methods, while positioning their approach as more straightforward and applicable across various prompting scenarios.

The conclusion outlines the potential applications of ICAE, particularly in contexts like Retrieval Augmented Generation and advanced prompting methods, emphasizing its ability to reduce computational and memory burdens. The authors note that their experiments were limited to Llama models with up to 13 billion parameters and express optimism that ICAE will perform even better with more advanced LLMs. Future research directions include exploring ICAE's applicability in multimodal LLMs, where the need for compact representations is even greater due to the longer contexts associated with images, videos, and audio.
