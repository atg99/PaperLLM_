The paper presents findings on the performance of the ICAE (Incremental Context-Aware Encoder) model based on Llama-7b, focusing on its autoencoding capabilities and memorization patterns. Results indicate that increasing memory length (k) enhances performance metrics such as BLEU scores and Exact Match (EM) rates, particularly at k=128 where BLEU scores exceed 95% for context lengths up to 500. In contrast, lower memory lengths (k=64 and k=32) result in significantly poorer performance, demonstrating challenges in retaining original context and achieving high compression ratios.

Table 1 shows a clear correlation between higher compression ratios and increased perplexity (PPL) in text continuation tasks. For example, reducing context length from 512 to 128 (4× compression) leads to a noticeable rise in PPL, indicating a loss in language modeling quality. 

Further analysis in Table 2 illustrates the model's text restoration errors, which mirror human-like mistakes, suggesting that the ICAE selectively emphasizes certain information during memorization. This aligns with findings from Peng et al. (2023) that stronger language models require less memorization effort, akin to human learning behaviors.

Additionally, the paper explores the model's restoration performance across different text types. Results indicate that while normal texts can be effectively compressed and restored, less common or random texts present significant challenges, reflected in inferior loss and BLEU scores. These observations reinforce the notion that LLM memorization patterns closely resemble those of humans, highlighting the complexities involved in language modeling and information retention.
