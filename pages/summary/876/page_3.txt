The paper provides an extensive taxonomy of Neural Style Transfer (NST) techniques, categorizing methods into various types such as example-based techniques, model-optimization-based methods, and parametric neural methods. It highlights the limitations of traditional algorithms that do not utilize Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), particularly in terms of flexibility and style diversity, thus necessitating the development of NST.

The derivations of NST are explored, emphasizing the importance of accurately modeling and extracting style from images. The discussion begins with visual texture modeling, which is foundational to texture synthesis. Two main approaches are identified: parametric texture modeling using summary statistics and non-parametric texture modeling utilizing Markov Random Fields (MRFs). 

The paper details the parametric texture modeling approach, notably the Gram-based representation introduced by Gatys et al. This method captures the correlations between filter responses in CNNs, specifically using the VGG network, to effectively model various textures. However, it also notes that while the Gram-based approach excels in capturing global statistics, it may overlook spatial arrangements, which can hinder its performance in certain texture representations. 

Overall, the paper underscores the evolution of NST techniques and the ongoing quest to refine texture modeling to accommodate a broader range of artistic styles while preserving image content.
