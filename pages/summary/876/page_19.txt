In this paper, the authors discuss a user study conducted to evaluate various neural style transfer (NST) algorithms, highlighting the variability in ratings from eight raters, despite them sharing the same demographic characteristics. The lack of a gold standard for assessing non-photorealistic rendering (NPR) and NST algorithms poses significant challenges for aesthetic evaluation, suggesting that collaboration with professional artists may be necessary to establish underlying aesthetic principles.

The paper also notes the absence of a standardized benchmark image set for NST evaluation, with many authors using their own images. The authors utilized the NPRgeneral benchmark image set for their content images but acknowledge that their selection of style images does not constitute a standard benchmark. They emphasize the importance of developing a comprehensive NST benchmark style set that captures a wide range of properties and includes a detailed description of principles and measurements.

Additionally, the paper addresses the interpretability of NST algorithms, which currently operate as black boxes, complicating control over the output. The authors focus on three key issues: representation disentangling, normalization methods, and adversarial examples. They describe representation disentangling as a technique to learn interpretable representations that allow for precise control over various factors of variation in the stylization process.

The discussion includes an overview of normalization methods in NST, beginning with instance normalization, which has been shown to improve convergence and visual quality in style transfer networks. The authors suggest that instance normalization helps simplify the learning process by discarding contrast information in content images.

Overall, the paper identifies critical gaps in the evaluation and interpretability of NST algorithms, proposing that addressing these issues is essential for advancing the field.
