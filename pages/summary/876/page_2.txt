The paper discusses the advancements in Neural Style Transfer (NST), which utilizes Convolutional Neural Networks to render images in different artistic styles. It highlights the foundational work of Gatys et al., which opened the field of NST and has led to numerous academic and industrial developments, including applications like Prisma and Deep Forger. The authors aim to provide a comprehensive overview of NST, focusing on three main contributions: categorizing recent advances in NST, evaluating different NST algorithms, and identifying challenges within the field.

The paper is organized into several sections. It begins with a review of previous artistic rendering methods that do not employ CNNs, followed by an exploration of the foundations of NST. The authors categorize existing NST algorithms and discuss improvement strategies. They also present evaluation methodologies to establish standardized benchmarks for future studies and examine commercial applications of NST. Lastly, the paper addresses current challenges in NST and suggests potential future research directions.

The review of non-CNN artistic rendering methods includes stroke-based rendering, region-based techniques, example-based rendering, and image processing and filtering. Each method has its strengths and weaknesses, primarily in flexibility and style diversity. For instance, stroke-based rendering effectively simulates specific styles but lacks versatility, while example-based rendering relies on training data pairs, which are often unavailable.

Overall, the paper aims to summarize the current state of NST, its methodologies, applications, and the challenges it faces, providing a roadmap for future research in this evolving field.
