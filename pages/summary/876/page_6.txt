The paper discusses various neural style transfer (NST) methods, highlighting their strengths and weaknesses. Risser et al.'s algorithm enhances style transfer stability through histogram matching of feature activations but incurs high computational complexity. It still suffers from limitations present in Gatys et al.'s approach, such as neglecting depth and detail coherence. 

To address the preservation of fine structures, Li et al. introduce a Laplacian loss that compares low-level features in pixel space, which improves detail retention but lacks semantic and depth considerations. Non-parametric methods, such as the one developed by Li and Wand, operate on local patches using Markov Random Fields (MRFs). This patch-based approach excels in photorealistic styles but struggles with significant perspective differences between content and style images, limiting detail and depth preservation.

The paper also introduces model-optimization-based offline neural methods (MOB-NST), which aim to enhance efficiency. These methods include Per-Style-Per-Model (PSPM) techniques, which utilize pre-trained networks for real-time style transfer. While these methods achieve faster results, they inherit issues from Gatys et al.’s algorithm, including coherence and depth problems. Notably, Ulyanov et al. improve stylization quality through instance normalization, which allows for faster convergence.

Additionally, Li and Wand's non-parametric PSPM method also employs MRFs, achieving better texture coherence in complex images, though it underperforms with non-texture styles. Overall, the paper indicates a progression towards more efficient and effective style transfer methods while acknowledging ongoing challenges in detail preservation and semantic coherence.
