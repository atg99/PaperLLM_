The paper presents a comparative analysis of various Neural Style Transfer (NST) algorithms, focusing on their outputs, efficiency, and the subjective nature of style in artistic creation. 

1) **IOB-NST Results**: The IOB-NST algorithm by Gatys et al. is highlighted for its high visual quality despite being computationally intensive. It is considered a gold standard in NST.

2) **PSPM-MOB-NST Results**: The Per-Style-Per-Model approach shows results similar to those of Ulyanov et al. and Johnson et al., reflecting their shared foundational ideas. Li and Wand's GAN-based method yielded less impressive results due to instability during training, although GANs are viewed as a promising direction in NST.

3) **MSPM-MOB-NST Results**: Multiple-Style-Per-Model algorithms tie a limited number of parameters to each style, resulting in visually similar outputs. However, models like Zhang and Dana's and Li et al.'s, which use a single network for multiple styles, face issues with style interference that affect quality.

4) **ASPM-MOB-NST Results**: The Arbitrary-Style-Per-Model algorithms generally produce less impressive results compared to other categories. Chen and Schmidt's patch-based method struggles with style element integration, while Ghiasi et al. and Huang and Belongie’s methods depend on the variety of training styles, affecting their performance on complex patterns.

A **saliency comparison** reveals the subjective nature of style and the challenges in establishing aesthetic criteria for NST outputs. Saliency maps show that IOB-NST and PSPM-MOB-NST effectively preserve content structure, while some ASPM-MOB-NST methods struggle with coherence.

In the **quantitative evaluation**, five metrics are analyzed, focusing on stylisation speed. The results indicate that efficiency is a critical aspect of MOB-NST algorithms, with a table provided that compares the average time to stylise images of different resolutions across various algorithms. The findings highlight the trade-offs between speed, flexibility, and quality inherent in NST research.
