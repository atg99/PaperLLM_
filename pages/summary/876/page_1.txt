The paper "Neural Style Transfer: A Review" by Yongcheng Jing et al. provides a comprehensive overview of Neural Style Transfer (NST), a method that uses Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) to create artistic images by separating and recombining content and style from different images. The authors begin by discussing the historical context of painting and the evolution of techniques aimed at automating the creation of artwork, noting limitations in traditional non-photorealistic rendering methods and earlier style transfer approaches that relied on low-level image features.

The review emphasizes the seminal work of Gatys et al., who demonstrated that CNNs could effectively extract content from photographs and style from artworks, leading to the development of NST. They describe the algorithm's iterative optimization process, which aligns CNN feature distributions to generate stylized images. The paper proposes a taxonomy of current NST algorithms and evaluates them through qualitative and quantitative comparisons.

Additionally, it discusses various applications of NST and identifies open problems for future research. The authors provide a resource link for access to discussed papers, codes, and models, enhancing the paper's contribution to the field.
