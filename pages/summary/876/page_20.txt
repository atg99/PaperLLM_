The paper discusses various normalization techniques, including instance normalization, conditional instance normalization, and adaptive instance normalization, which help in normalizing the style of images effectively. While these methods have shown superior performance, the underlying reasons for their success remain unclear, highlighting a gap in theoretical understanding.

The paper also addresses the phenomenon of adversarial examples, which can mislead generative style transfer networks despite being recognizable to humans. This raises questions about the robustness of NST models and emphasizes the need for further investigation into how perturbations affect stylization outcomes.

Additionally, the authors outline a three-way trade-off in NST between speed, flexibility, and quality. Different models are evaluated based on these criteria, with some achieving high quality at the expense of speed, while others offer real-time stylization but lack flexibility. The challenge of hyperparameter tuning in NST is also noted, as optimal results are difficult to achieve without a deeper understanding of the optimization process.

In conclusion, the field of NST is rapidly evolving, driven by both scientific inquiry and practical applications. The review underscores the importance of continued research to enhance the understanding and capabilities of NST, which not only advances its own domain but also influences related areas such as image reconstruction.
