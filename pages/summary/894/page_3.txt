NeRF-Art proposes a novel approach to text-driven neural radiance fields stylization through a two-stage pipeline. In the first stage, the method reconstructs a NeRF model of the target scene using multi-view inputs, optimizing it with a reconstruction loss. The second stage involves stylizing this reconstructed model based on a text prompt, utilizing a combination of directional loss and contrastive loss in the CLIP embedding space, alongside regularization and perceptual losses. This approach allows for both appearance and geometric stylization, enhancing the realism and adherence to the desired style.

The paper discusses the limitations of existing 3D stylization methods, particularly those reliant on explicit representations like meshes and point clouds, which struggle with complex scenes. In contrast, NeRF offers a more expressive implicit representation, enabling richer scene details. Previous works have attempted to integrate NeRF for stylization but have mainly focused on appearance without modifying density parameters. NeRF-Art distinguishes itself by allowing stylization from simple text prompts rather than relying on image references, making it more intuitive and user-friendly.

Overall, NeRF-Art advances the field of 3D stylization by combining the strengths of implicit neural representations with the flexibility of text-driven guidance, thus paving the way for more dynamic and versatile applications in 3D content creation.
