In the proposed NeRF-Art framework, a novel directional CLIP loss is introduced to facilitate the transfer of a source image to a target domain based on a pair of text prompts. This contrasts with previous methods that used a single text prompt. The framework also employs a contrastive learning strategy to control the strength of the stylization, using both global and local contrastive losses to ensure balanced stylization across the entire scene while minimizing artifacts.

To address potential issues with geometric noise during the stylization process, a weight regularization loss is applied, guiding the density distribution towards a more realistic appearance. The overall training objective combines stylization losses, a content-preservation loss based on perceptual features, and the artifact suppression loss.

The training approach optimizes memory usage by rendering images at high resolution and calculating gradients efficiently, allowing for effective stylization while maintaining high fidelity and consistency across views. The experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed methods in achieving high-quality stylization results.
