The paper presents NeRF-Art, a novel approach for text-driven neural radiance fields stylization that leverages CLIP. The study involved a user evaluation where participants assessed various methods based on content preservation, style fidelity, and view consistency. NeRF-Art demonstrated superior performance compared to existing techniques like StyleGAN-NADA and CLIP-NeRF, achieving higher user preference rates.

An ablation study highlighted the importance of global-local contrastive learning. While directional CLIP loss can guide stylization direction, it often fails to provide sufficient magnitude. The introduction of a global contrastive loss ensures the proper magnitude while local contrastive loss addresses the balance of stylization across different scene regions. Combining both losses with directional CLIP results in uniform stylization.

The paper also discusses weight regularization to mitigate geometric artifacts during stylization, as shown in experimental results. Generalization evaluations indicate that NeRF-Art can adapt effectively to various NeRF-like models, maintaining stylization quality.

Geometry evaluation revealed that NeRF-Art correctly modulates geometry to align with desired styles, as demonstrated through mesh comparisons before and after stylization. The method successfully achieves visual enhancements in geometry and texture with only text guidance.

Despite its strengths, the method faces limitations, particularly with ambiguous text prompts leading to unexpected results. Linguistic ambiguities and semantically meaningless words can confuse the model, resulting in undesired outputs. These challenges present opportunities for future research.
