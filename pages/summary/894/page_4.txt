The paper presents a novel approach to 3D scene stylization using NeRF (Neural Radiance Fields) and a text-guided system based on CLIP (Contrastive Language-Image Pretraining). The authors highlight the limitations of existing methods that rely exclusively on mesh inputs for stylization and propose their method as a solution that offers improved visual quality and consistency.

The methodology consists of two main stages: reconstruction and stylization. Initially, the 3D scene is reconstructed using NeRF, which operates by predicting density and radiance from spatial coordinates and view directions. The reconstruction is trained through a supervised loss that compares predicted colors to ground truth pixel colors.

For the stylization phase, the authors leverage CLIP's capabilities to align image and text semantics. They introduce a directional CLIP loss that measures the cosine similarity between stylized renderings and target text prompts, enabling the model to maintain content from the original scene while adapting to the desired style. Additionally, a global-local contrastive learning framework is implemented to manage the strength of the stylization, and a weight regularization term is added to mitigate artifacts and noise during the stylization process.

The paper concludes with a comprehensive training strategy that integrates these components, ensuring that the stylized NeRF model effectively balances style adherence, content preservation, and cross-view consistency.
