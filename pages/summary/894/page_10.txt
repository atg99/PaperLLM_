The paper discusses a novel method called NeRF-Art for text-guided stylization of 3D avatars. This approach simultaneously modulates geometry and color of a pre-trained Neural Radiance Field (NeRF) to align with styles specified by text prompts. The authors highlight that while existing methods, such as AvatarCLIP, utilize 3D mesh inputs and often produce artifacts and noise due to limited sampling, NeRF-Art employs a memory-saving technique that allows for training on all rays, thereby enhancing detail in texture and geometry.

Through ablation studies, it is shown that weight regularization is crucial in reducing geometric noise and artifacts. The user study further validates the effectiveness of NeRF-Art, revealing that it consistently outperforms state-of-the-art methods in user preference ratings. The authors conclude that their contrastive learning strategies enable superior stylization without the need for mesh guidance, ultimately yielding finer details in the rendered avatars.
