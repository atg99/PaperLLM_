Implicit Neural Representations and NeRF. Recent advancements in neural radiance fields (NeRF) have revolutionized the field of 3D representation by enabling high-quality novel view synthesis. However, the implicit nature of NeRF complicates the process of stylization, as the appearance and geometry are entangled within densely parameterized networks. Existing NeRF stylization approaches have primarily relied on image references for style guidance, which can limit their effectiveness and applicability in various scenarios.

Proposed Method: NeRF-Art. In response to these limitations, the authors introduce NeRF-Art, a text-driven stylization method that leverages a pre-trained NeRF model along with a single text prompt. The method innovatively integrates the large-scale Language-Vision model CLIP to facilitate consistent novel view synthesis while transforming both appearance and geometry according to the specified style. To enhance stylization, a CLIP-based contrastive loss is implemented, ensuring that the stylized results align closely with the target style while maintaining a distinction from other styles.

The approach also utilizes a hybrid global-local framework to enforce uniformity in style across the entire scene and includes weight regularization to mitigate artifacts and noise during density field adjustments. Evaluation of the method demonstrates its effectiveness across various styles, with user studies indicating superior visual appeal compared to related techniques. The ability to extract a mesh from the stylized NeRF further showcases the method's potential for geometry modulation.

In conclusion, NeRF-Art represents a significant step forward in 3D content stylization, providing a flexible and intuitive means of achieving artistic transformations through natural language prompts, thereby addressing the challenges posed by previous methods.
