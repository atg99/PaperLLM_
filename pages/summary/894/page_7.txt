The paper presents NeRF-Art, a method for text-driven stylization of Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF). It compares its performance against existing techniques such as CLIP-NeRF and DreamField in generating stylized outputs based on textual prompts. The authors highlight that vague terms like “Fauvism” can lead to limited stylization effects, often affecting only specific areas of the output, while more precise descriptions yield clearer results. For instance, specific references like “Lord Voldemort” lead to consistent stylization outcomes, demonstrating that clarity in language significantly enhances the effectiveness of the stylization process. The findings suggest that the specificity of text prompts is crucial for achieving desirable artistic results in neural rendering.
