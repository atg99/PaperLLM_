The paper discusses various audio signal processing techniques utilized for speaker and language identification, speech segmentation, and the analysis of pathological speech. Key methods include:

1. **Cepstral Smoothing and GDF Computation**: This technique smooths intrinsic spikes in the audio signal, enhancing the performance of the Gaussian Density Function (GDF) used in speaker identification and related tasks.

2. **Envelope Modulation Spectrum (EMS)**: The EMS captures slow amplitude modulations in audio signals. The original signal is filtered into nine octave bands, and the envelopes of these signals are extracted using the Hilbert transform. Six EMS metrics are calculated, including peak frequency, peak amplitude, and energy ratios across specific frequency bands. These features are effective for classifying pathological speech.

3. **Long-term Average Spectrum (LTAS)**: LTAS helps in classifying atypical speech by analyzing variations in nasality, breath control, and loudness. It employs nine octave filters and produces a comprehensive 99-dimensional feature vector based on various statistical measures of the RMS values of the filtered signals.

4. **Chroma Features**: These features represent audio in terms of the twelve semitones of a musical octave, computed using the short-time Fourier transform. Chroma energy distribution statistics help identify similarities in musical interpretations.

5. **Tonality Features**: These features focus on the harmonic structure of audio signals, with the fundamental frequency (F0) being crucial for music onset detection and sound recognition. Additional tonality features include pitch histograms, pitch profiles, harmonicity, and jitter, which are significant for analyzing music and pathological voices.

6. **Spectrum Shape Features**: The spectral centroid indicates the center of mass of the audio spectrum, providing insight into the frequency distribution within the signal.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of these features in improving the classification and understanding of various speech and audio signals, particularly in distinguishing between normal and pathological speech.
