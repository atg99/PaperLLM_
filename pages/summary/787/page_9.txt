The paper discusses various advanced features used in the analysis of motor vibrations and audio signals, focusing on two primary methodologies: the Shortened Method of Frequencies Selection MULTIEXPANDED (SMOFS-MULTIEXPANDED) and time-frequency analysis.

SMOFS-MULTIEXPANDED is a handcrafted feature extraction technique aimed at diagnosing faults in motors. The algorithm involves capturing vibrations, extracting Fast Fourier Transforms (FFTs) from healthy and faulty motor sounds, calculating their absolute differences, and selecting frequency components based on a variable threshold. This approach emphasizes identifying common frequency components that signify motor health.

Time-frequency analysis, particularly through Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT), allows a comprehensive representation of signals in both time and frequency domains. This method overcomes the limitations of traditional time or frequency analysis by providing insights into non-stationary aspects such as trends and patterns. The paper highlights the conversion of time-domain signals into time-frequency representations (TFR), which often involves large data matrices. To manage this complexity, various decomposition techniques are introduced, including linear, quadratic, positive time-frequency distributions, and matching pursuit TFDs, each with its applications in audio signal analysis and industry diagnostics.

Additional features discussed include sub-band energy ratios for audio segmentation, spectral envelopes for accurate representation of audio signals, stereo panning spectrum features to utilize stereo information, and group delay functions that extract phase information crucial for identifying spectral events. These features are utilized in various applications, including environmental sound classification, music genre classification, and detecting faults in industrial machinery.
