This collection of references encompasses various studies and advancements in audio signal processing, focusing on applications such as speaker verification, synthetic speech detection, acoustic scene classification, emotion recognition, and fault diagnosis using acoustic signals. 

Key contributions include:

- Liu et al. propose a method that utilizes both spectral magnitude and phase information to create supervectors for speaker verification, enhancing anti-spoofing capabilities.
- Sahidullah et al. compare features for detecting synthetic speech, aiming to improve detection accuracy.
- Wu et al. explore methods to differentiate between converted and natural speech to counter anti-spoofing attacks.
- Das et al. introduce modified gammatone frequency cepstral coefficients for improved spoofer detection.
- Studies by He and Cao apply convolutional neural networks for automated depression analysis from speech, showcasing the intersection of audio processing and mental health assessment.

Additional works focus on the use of deep learning techniques for feature extraction from audio signals, including multi-stream hierarchical deep neural networks and local binary patterns for acoustic scene classification. 

Research on fault diagnosis emphasizes the application of acoustic signals for detecting machine failures, highlighting the potential of sound analysis in predictive maintenance.

Overall, these studies reflect a diverse range of methodologies and applications in audio signal processing, demonstrating its relevance in both technological and healthcare domains.
