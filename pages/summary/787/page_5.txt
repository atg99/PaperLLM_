The paper discusses the Zero Crossing Rate (ZCR) as an effective method for voice activity detection, categorizing speech frames as voiced, unvoiced, or silent. It highlights that ZCR is typically higher in unvoiced segments compared to voiced ones, and ideally, should be zero during silent portions of clean speech. Additionally, ZCR can estimate the fundamental frequency of speech, as it is twice the frequency of the signal, making it useful for designing discriminators and classifiers.

The paper also presents a modified version of ZCR that incorporates a detrending technique. This modified ZCR, along with linear prediction ZCR, which compares the ZCR of the original signal to that of the prediction error, broadens the applicability of ZCR in various tasks such as music/speech discrimination, music genre classification, voice activity detection, and vowel analysis.

Furthermore, amplitude-based features are introduced, focusing on the temporal envelope of signals. Key features include the Amplitude Descriptor (AD), which differentiates sound envelopes through energy and duration variability, and the ADSR envelope, utilized for music analysis and genre classification. The paper illustrates these concepts with figures and provides a MATLAB pseudo code for calculating ZCR.
