The paper discusses the classification of audio signals, particularly focusing on the differentiation between speech, music, and environmental sounds. It emphasizes the significance of audio feature extraction methods, which have evolved over time to enhance audio analysis and classification.

The evolution of audio features is categorized into four domains: time domain, frequency domain, joint time-frequency domain, and deep features. Time domain features, which are the oldest, have been fundamental to audio analysis since the late 1950s. Frequency domain features emerged from the analysis of the spectrum of audio signals during the 1950s to 1960s, while joint time-frequency features were developed in the late 1960s, contributing to audio signal processing algorithms.

With the advent of deep learning, deep features have gained prominence since 2010 in applications such as acoustic scene classification, speaker recognition, and audio-video analysis.

The paper also highlights the concept of windowing in time domain analysis, which is crucial for dealing with non-stationary audio signals. Windowing allows for the analysis of short segments of a long signal, treating them as quasi-stationary for more accurate analysis. The simplest window type mentioned is the rectangular window, which isolates specific regions of interest in the signal for further examination.
