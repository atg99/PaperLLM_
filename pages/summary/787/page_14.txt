The paper discusses various audio feature extraction techniques, focusing on Greenwood function cepstral coefficients (GFCC), gammatone cepstral coefficients (GTCC), discrete wavelet transform (DWT) features, and texture-based features such as local binary patterns (LBP) and local ternary patterns (LTP).

GFCCs, a generalized form of mel-frequency cepstral coefficients (MFCCs), utilize mel-scale features to effectively represent vocal characteristics across numerous terrestrial mammals. They are derived from the Greenwood equation, which accurately maps cochlear-frequency positions for different species, making them valuable for environmental sound recognition, particularly in animal and bird sound classification.

GTCCs address noise reduction challenges in automatic speech recognition (ASR) systems. They are extracted using gammatone filter banks, which produce a cochleagram representing the frequency-time characteristics of sound. The extraction process is akin to that of MFCCs, with the gammatone filter bank replacing the mel filter bank. GTCCs can also include delta and delta-delta features, enhancing their applicability in environmental sound and speech recognition.

The discrete wavelet transform provides a robust method for transforming audio signals into a time-frequency representation, effectively handling non-stationary signals. DWT offers varying resolutions for different frequency ranges, generating wavelet features that can be utilized in a range of applications, including audio analysis, classification, and emotion recognition.

Texture-based features like LBP and LTP are primarily used in computer vision but have been adapted for audio signal processing. LBP captures local spatial information from spectrograms for tasks like audio scene classification and emotion detection. LTP extends LBP by using a threshold constant to categorize pixels into three values, enhancing its application in various audio analysis contexts.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the significance of these feature extraction methods in advancing audio processing and recognition technologies.
