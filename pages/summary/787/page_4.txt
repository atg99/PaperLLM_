The paper discusses the concept of windowing in signal analysis, specifically using a rectangular window. It explains how the window slides over the signal to convert long non-stationary signals into smaller quasi-stationary segments. However, the rectangular window introduces distortion at the edges due to the Gibbs phenomenon. To mitigate this, the paper suggests using smoother window functions like Hamming or Hanning windows, which reduce edge effects by gradually transitioning from zero to one.

Additionally, the paper defines the zero-crossing rate (ZCR) of an audio frame, which measures the rate at which the signal changes sign within a frame. ZCR is calculated as the number of sign changes divided by the frame length, providing insight into the signal's characteristics. The mathematical formulation for ZCR is presented, highlighting its relevance in analyzing audio signals.
