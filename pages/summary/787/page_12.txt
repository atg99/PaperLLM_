The paper presents various algorithms for analyzing audio signals using spectral features. Key concepts include:

1. **Spectral Kurtosis**: Measures the tailedness of a probability distribution, calculated in the frequency domain using parameters like spectral centroid and spread. It is useful in identifying characteristics of audio signals.

2. **Spectral Slope**: Evaluates the slope of the amplitude of the signal through linear regression, aiding in speech analysis and speaker identification.

3. **Spectral Decrease**: Represents the average spectral slope in a rate-map representation, focusing on low frequencies.

4. **Bandwidth**: A second-order statistical measure that differentiates low bandwidth sounds from high frequency sounds, applicable in music classification and environmental sound recognition.

5. **Spectral Flatness**: Indicates the uniformity of the power spectrum's frequency distribution. It distinguishes between harmonic sounds (low values) and noise-like sounds (high values), and is used in music onset detection and audio fingerprinting.

6. **Spectral Crest Factor**: Measures the peakiness of the power spectrum, with higher values indicating harmonic or tonal sounds and lower values indicating noise-like sounds. It is relevant in audio fingerprinting and music classification.

7. **Entropy**: Assesses the uniformity of flatness using Shannon’s or Renyi entropy, applicable in automatic speech recognition.

8. **Spectral Flux**: Quantifies the changes in frequency energy distribution, useful for speech/music discrimination and genre classification.

9. **Octave Based Spectral Contrast (OBSC)**: Measures the difference between peaks and valleys in sub-bands, applied in music classification and mood classification.

The algorithms detailed in the paper provide systematic approaches to extract these spectral features from audio signals, enhancing the understanding and classification of various audio types.
