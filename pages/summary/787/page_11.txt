The paper discusses various spectral features of audio signals that contribute to the analysis of sound characteristics, particularly in music and speech. Key features include:

1. **Spectral Centroid**: This feature measures the brightness of a sound signal, calculated as a weighted average of frequencies based on normalized amplitude. It is instrumental in timbre measurement, music classification, and mood classification, with music typically exhibiting a higher spectral centroid than speech.

2. **Spectral Center**: Related to the spectral centroid, this feature represents the median frequency of the signal spectrum, balancing higher and lower energy levels. It is useful for tracking rhythm in music.

3. **Spectral Roll-off**: This point indicates the frequency below which a specified percentage (usually 95%) of the total signal energy is contained. It is essential for classifying audio signals, distinguishing between speech and music, and identifying music genres.

4. **Spectral Spread**: Also known as spectral dispersion, this feature quantifies the bandwidth of the signal. Noise-like sounds exhibit a wider spectral spread than pure tonal sounds. This spread is generally broader for music and environmental sounds compared to speech.

5. **Spectral Skewness**: This third-order statistical measure assesses the symmetry of the spectrum around its mean. A skewness of zero indicates symmetry, while positive or negative values indicate asymmetry, with applications in mood detection, music genre classification, and medical diagnostics.

6. **Spectral Kurtosis**: This fourth-order statistical measure describes the flatness of the spectral distribution. It indicates how peaked or flat the distribution is around its mean, with applications similar to skewness in music and medical contexts.

The algorithms for calculating these spectral features are outlined, emphasizing the transformation of audio signals into the frequency domain for analysis. Each feature provides unique insights into the audio characteristics, aiding in various applications such as music classification, mood detection, and fault detection in mechanical systems.
