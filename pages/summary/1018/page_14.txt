The paper discusses the optimization of weight and bias parameters in neural networks, aiming to minimize an error function E. For models with fixed basis functions, the error is a quadratic function of the weights, allowing for rapid minimization through linear equations. However, such models struggle with input dimensionality, necessitating the consideration of adaptive basis functions. This change leads to a non-linear error function, requiring advanced optimization methods.

The paper explores differentiable error functions and network mappings, establishing that the error function can be differentiated with respect to weights, enabling gradient-based methods for minimization. A computationally efficient procedure called back-propagation is introduced, which allows for the evaluation of required derivatives for any feed-forward network topology.

In a general feed-forward network, each unit computes a weighted sum of its inputs, and biases can be incorporated by adding a fixed activation unit. The error functions are expressed as a sum of individual pattern errors, leveraging the independence of data points. The process of forward propagation is described, where input vectors are fed into the network to calculate activations.

To evaluate the derivative of the error with respect to a weight, the chain rule is applied, leading to a simplification that expresses the derivative as a product of the error at the output unit and the activation at the input unit. This relationship facilitates the computation of derivatives necessary for optimization.
