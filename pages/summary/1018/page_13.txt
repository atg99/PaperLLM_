The paper discusses the training of a multi-layer perceptron (MLP) using data generated from specific density functions. It presents the output of the trained network as a function of the input variable x and compares this with the true posterior probability calculated using Bayes' theorem. The authors emphasize the importance of selecting an appropriate output-unit activation function to align with the chosen error function. 

Using Bayes' theorem, the posterior probability of a class Ck is derived from class-conditional densities, leading to the formulation of the softmax function. This function normalizes the output probabilities, ensuring they are between 0 and 1 and sum to 1, which is essential for probabilistic interpretations. The paper notes that, under certain conditions, minimizing the error function will yield outputs representing posterior probabilities for any input vector.

However, it warns that the outputs of the trained network may not closely approximate 0 or 1 when class-conditional densities overlap. The authors advise against heuristic procedures that alter the training distribution, as these may disrupt the network's ability to produce accurate Bayesian probabilities. Finally, the paper outlines the principle of maximum likelihood in the context of error back-propagation, framing the learning problem as one of error function minimization related to the network's weight vector.
