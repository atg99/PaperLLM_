The paper discusses the process of training neural networks, specifically through the back-propagation algorithm. This method allows for the evaluation of gradients of the error function for all units—output and hidden—within a feed-forward network, independent of its topology. Once the gradient of the error function is determined for a specific pattern, the process is iteratively applied across all patterns in the dataset, summing the resulting derivatives to compute the overall error gradient.

The back-propagation algorithm enhances the efficiency of gradient evaluation, which is crucial for minimizing the error through weight adjustments. The paper highlights that finding a weight vector to minimize error falls under the broader category of unconstrained non-linear optimization, where several robust algorithms exist. The initial weight vector can be randomly selected, followed by iterative updates based on the gradient descent method.

However, standard gradient descent is noted as inefficient for highly non-linear problems like neural network optimization. To improve efficiency, modifications such as incorporating a momentum term are often used, though they introduce additional parameters that require tuning. The paper references several advanced optimization techniques, including conjugate gradient methods and quasi-Newton algorithms, which have shown greater efficacy in solving non-linear optimization problems.

The term back-propagation itself can refer to different concepts within neural computing, including the architecture of multi-layer perceptrons and the specific training process using gradient descent on a sum-of-squares error function. The training process is characterized by two distinct stages: the first involves evaluating the derivatives of the error function, where back-propagation plays a key role, and the second involves using these derivatives to adjust the weights, which can be done through various optimization schemes beyond basic gradient descent. The paper emphasizes the versatility of the back-propagation technique, as it is applicable to various network types and error functions, and it can also facilitate the evaluation of second derivatives like the Hessian matrix.
