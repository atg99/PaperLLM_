The paper discusses neural networks as a framework for representing nonlinear mappings from multiple input variables to output variables. The parameters in these networks, referred to as weights, are determined through a process called learning or training, which relies on a training set of examples. The authors highlight that neural networks can be viewed as specific choices for functional forms used in mapping, with optimization procedures for parameter values.

The concept of polynomial curve fitting is introduced as a simpler analogy to explain important issues related to neural networks. The authors describe how a polynomial can be used to fit data points by minimizing an error function, illustrating that the polynomial's coefficients are analogous to neural network weights. They emphasize that while polynomials can approximate functions accurately, extending this to higher dimensions introduces complexity due to the curse of dimensionality, where the number of adjustable parameters increases dramatically with the number of input variables.

This growth in parameters necessitates a larger training set to ensure model accuracy, as having too few data points compared to parameters can lead to poor predictions. The paper concludes by underscoring the significance of neural networks and similar techniques in addressing the challenges of representing nonlinear mappings in high-dimensional spaces.
