The paper discusses the challenge of scaling with dimensionality in neural network models by representing non-linear mapping functions as linear combinations of basis functions, also known as hidden functions or units. The mapping is expressed in a form that incorporates a bias term, allowing for a constant in the function expansion. The authors emphasize that when representing functions of multiple input variables, a large number of basis functions and adaptive parameters may be required. However, due to significant correlations between input variables, the effective dimensionality of the data can be much lower than the number of inputs, known as intrinsic dimensionality.

To exploit this phenomenon, the paper suggests adapting the basis functions during the training process, which enables the number of functions to increase with problem complexity rather than merely with input dimensionality. This approach results in a model where the number of free parameters grows at a linear or quadratic rate with respect to input dimensionality, compared to the exponential growth associated with general polynomial representations.

The authors present a two-layer feed-forward network model, or multi-layer perceptron, where the basis functions contain adaptive parameters. The model incorporates an activation function, which must be non-linear to retain general approximation capabilities. The paper highlights that if the activation function were linear, the model would reduce to a linear mapping. The most common activation functions discussed include sigmoidal functions, particularly the logistic sigmoid.

Finally, the paper combines the discussed concepts to provide a complete expression for the function represented by the two-layer network, emphasizing its applicability to regression problems while noting necessary modifications for classification tasks. The authors also point out that adaptive basis function models are not exclusive to neural networks, having been explored in various contexts for many years.
