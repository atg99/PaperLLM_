The paper discusses the computational efficiency of the back-propagation algorithm in neural networks, emphasizing its significance in training multi-layer perceptron networks. It highlights that evaluating the derivatives of the error function directly requires O(W^2) operations, where W is the total number of weights. In contrast, back-propagation reduces the computational cost to O(W) by evaluating all derivatives through a single forward and backward propagation.

The paper further explores the concept of generalization in neural networks, stating that the goal is not merely to replicate the training data but to model the underlying process generating the data. This is crucial for making accurate predictions on new inputs. The complexity of the model must align with the data set, which is illustrated through polynomial curve fitting. The author explains that increasing the polynomial order improves flexibility and fitting accuracy, but excessive complexity can lead to overfitting.

To evaluate model performance, the paper introduces the root-mean-square error (ERMS) for both training and test sets, demonstrating that while training error decreases with higher polynomial orders, test error reaches a minimum at a certain point before increasing again. The optimal polynomial order corresponds to the complexity that best captures the underlying function without overfitting.

In summary, the paper underscores the importance of computational efficiency in the back-propagation algorithm and the balance between model complexity and generalization in neural networks, using polynomial fitting as an illustrative analogy.
