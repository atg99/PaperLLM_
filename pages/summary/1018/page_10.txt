The paper discusses the formulation of error functions for regression problems where output variables are continuous. It begins by assuming that the target variable is determined by a deterministic function of the input, with Gaussian noise added. This leads to the conclusion that the errors have a normal distribution with a mean of zero and a constant standard deviation. The probability distribution of the target variables is then modeled using a neural network, which allows for the derivation of an error function based on the sum of squared differences between the predicted and actual target values.

The derived error function can be simplified for optimization purposes, resulting in the familiar sum-of-squares expression. The paper highlights that models with fixed basis functions are linear in parameters, enabling efficient parameter determination through linear algebraic methods. However, if the basis functions contain adaptive parameters, the error function becomes highly non-linear, complicating the minimization process.

The sum-of-squares error function is shown to represent the conditional mean of the target data. Yet, the assumption of Gaussian distribution for target data may not always hold true, especially in cases of multi-modal distributions common in inverse problems. To address this, the paper suggests combining a feed-forward network with a Gaussian mixture model, allowing for more flexible modeling of conditional distributions.
