The paper discusses the logistic sigmoid activation function and its application within feed-forward neural networks, particularly focusing on a two-layer network structure. It compares the network's mapping function to projection pursuit regression, noting that although both share a similar form, their parameter determination methods differ significantly. The authors highlight the utility of representing the network's function through a diagram, which includes bias parameters modeled as weights from fixed inputs.

The simplicity of the two-layer network allows it to approximate any continuous mapping with arbitrary accuracy, provided the number of hidden units is sufficiently large. This characteristic is supported by various authors in the field. Additionally, it is noted that two-layer networks with sigmoidal hidden units can approximate both a function and its derivatives simultaneously, as established by Hornik et al. This emphasizes the versatility and effectiveness of the discussed network architecture in modeling complex functions.
