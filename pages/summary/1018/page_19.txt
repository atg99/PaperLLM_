The paper discusses the impact of polynomial fitting on training and test data in the context of a specific dataset. It illustrates that fitting a 10th-order polynomial to the data results in a perfect fit for the training data but introduces significant oscillations, leading to a poorer representation of the underlying generator function compared to a cubic polynomial. The analysis is further supported by presenting plots of the root mean square (RMS) error as a function of polynomial order. The findings reveal that while the RMS error for the training set decreases with increasing polynomial order, the error for the test set exhibits a minimum at a polynomial order of 3. This indicates that overly complex models may not generalize well to new data, highlighting the importance of balancing model complexity and predictive performance.
