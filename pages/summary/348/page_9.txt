The paper presents a performance comparison between the publicly released GPT-4 model and its base version, GPT-4-base, focusing on their effectiveness in medical assessments, specifically the USMLE exams. The results indicate that both models perform well across various datasets, with GPT-4-base showing a 3-5% improvement in accuracy compared to the release model.

The analysis highlights that the alignment process, which aims to enhance safety and instruction-following, may negatively impact raw performance. This finding suggests a need for further research into fine-tuning methods that could better balance safety and accuracy. Potential avenues for improvement include incorporating expert knowledge and utilizing specialized medical datasets.

Additionally, the paper outlines benchmarks for several medical challenge datasets from MultiMedQA, noting that some datasets were not tested due to their complex answer formats requiring in-depth expert evaluation. Overall, the research emphasizes the importance of refining model training techniques to achieve optimal performance in medical applications.
