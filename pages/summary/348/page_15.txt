In this clinical scenario, a 28-year-old man presents with hematuria and elevated blood urea nitrogen and creatinine levels, indicating impaired kidney function. His history of living in sub-Saharan Africa until recently is significant, as this region is known for a high prevalence of schistosomiasis, a parasitic infection caused by Schistosoma species. The laboratory findings, including a leukocytosis with a normal white blood cell distribution, further support this diagnosis.

The other potential causes are less likely for the following reasons: 

- Exposure to a chemical toxin lacks specific evidence in the patient's history.
- Interstitial cystitis typically does not cause significant kidney dysfunction.
- Malacoplakia, while it can cause hematuria, is rare and inconsistent with the presented lab results.
- Vesicoureteral reflux generally affects children and is less relevant here.
- Acute kidney injury is a consideration but usually presents with a more rapid decline in kidney function.

Overall, the most likely diagnosis for this patient, given the history and lab results, is schistosomiasis.
