The paper discusses the performance of GPT-4 in regenerating questions from various datasets, particularly SQuAD 2.0 and USMLE examinations. The findings indicate that GPT-4 can regenerate SQuAD 2.0 questions with a 99% overlap 17% of the time, but struggles significantly with USMLE datasets, showing less than 50% overlap. This suggests that official USMLE content is unlikely included in GPT-4's training data, despite some noted contamination in publicly available benchmarks not impacting performance.

The focus is primarily on multiple-choice questions, which form a large part of the USMLE exams. However, the analysis does not encompass the interactive case simulations present in Step 3 of the USMLE, and the lack of access to actual exam questions means that reported metrics may not accurately reflect GPT-4's true performance.

The paper also explores GPT-4's capabilities beyond just correct answers by conducting interactive sessions. An illustrative case study highlights how GPT-4 can engage in a dialog with a medical student, providing explanations for errors and conducting counterfactual analyses to simulate different clinical scenarios. However, the authors caution that the accuracy of information generated in these interactions requires expert verification. The paper emphasizes the need for careful review of the model's outputs in educational and clinical applications.
