1. **Detailed Patient History**: Gather comprehensive medical, travel, and social history, including medication or supplement use, recent illnesses, and potential exposures to infections or toxins.

2. **Urinalysis**: Conduct a thorough urinalysis to assess protein levels, perform a microscopic examination for blood cell casts, and carry out a urine culture to identify possible infections.

3. **Serology for Schistosomiasis**: Given the patient's background of residing in sub-Saharan Africa, testing for Schistosoma antibodies can help confirm or rule out schistosomiasis.

4. **Kidney Ultrasound**: Imaging can reveal structural abnormalities, kidney size, shape, and conditions such as hydronephrosis or kidney stones.

5. **Additional Blood Tests**: Measure serum complement levels and test for autoimmune markers such as ANA, ANCA, and anti-GBM antibodies to evaluate for glomerulonephritis.

6. **Cystoscopy**: If prior tests do not clarify the diagnosis, a cystoscopy may be performed to visualize the bladder and check for conditions like interstitial cystitis or malacoplakia.

7. **Kidney Biopsy**: In cases where the diagnosis remains unclear, a kidney biopsy can provide insights into underlying renal pathologies.

The sequence and choice of tests should be tailored to the individual case, relying on the patient's history and clinical presentation to prioritize the most relevant investigations.
