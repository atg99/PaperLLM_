This paper discusses the significant improvement in performance of GPT-4 on MedQA and MedMCQA tasks using a simpler zero-shot prompt, indicating that the effort required for high performance decreases with successive model generations. It emphasizes the importance of calibration in GPT-4, which refers to the alignment between the predicted probabilities of correctness and the actual outcomes. Calibration is crucial in high-stakes fields like medicine, where accurate probabilities can guide decision-making processes such as treatment plans. A well-calibrated model offers reliable confidence levels, while a poorly calibrated one can mislead users, potentially leading to harmful outcomes. The paper also highlights that good calibration differs from high predictive accuracy, as models can be accurate yet poorly calibrated. The authors utilize calibration plots to assess the calibration of GPT-4 compared to GPT-3.5 on the USMLE Self-Assessment and Sample Exam, illustrating the model's performance.
