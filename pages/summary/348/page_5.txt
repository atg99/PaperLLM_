The paper aims to assess the baseline performance of GPT-4 on medical multiple-choice questions (MCQs) using a straightforward approach, intentionally avoiding complex strategies like chain-of-thought prompting and retrieval augmented generation. Despite prior research indicating that such methods significantly enhance performance, the authors demonstrate that GPT-4 achieves impressive results independently, surpassing human performance as well as other models employing advanced prompting techniques.

The evaluation includes comparisons between GPT-4 and its predecessor, GPT-3.5, across various benchmark datasets. Both zero-shot and 5-shot prompting methods are utilized, with accuracy reported for zero-shot evaluations and leave-one-out cross-validation for the 5-shot setting. The results also incorporate findings from other models such as ChatGPT, InstructGPT, Flan-PaLM 540B, and Med-PaLM, although the latter two are not publicly accessible.

The paper further analyzes GPT-4's performance on official practice materials for the United States Medical Licensing Examination (USMLE), a crucial three-step assessment for medical competency in the U.S. Each step of the USMLE tests different aspects of medical knowledge and skills, with Step 1 focusing on core clinical knowledge, Step 2 on clinical understanding, and Step 3 on the application of medical knowledge in practice. The materials used for benchmarking are officially sourced from the National Board of Medical Examiners (NBME), ensuring authenticity and reliability in the evaluation process. This contrasts with other studies that may rely on unofficial datasets, thus emphasizing the rigor of their methodology and the significance of their findings. Further details on the datasets and concerns regarding model memorization are discussed in the appendix.
