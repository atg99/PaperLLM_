The paper discusses three biomedical question-answering datasets: PubMedQA, MedMCQA, and MMLU. 

PubMedQA requires models to answer yes, no, or maybe to biomedical questions based on context from PubMed abstracts. It features two testing settings: reasoning-required, where only abstracts are provided for answers, and reasoning-free, which includes long-form answers with explanations. The reasoning-required setting aligns with the multiple-choice format established by previous research.

MedMCQA consists of multiple-choice questions from Indian medical school entrance exams, with a development set of 4183 questions and a test set of 6150 questions, each offering four choices.

MMLU is a comprehensive multiple-choice dataset covering 57 subjects, with specific results reported for medicine-related topics.

The paper also introduces the Memorization effects Levenshtein detector (MELD) method to analyze memorization in models. This method partitions dataset content, measures the similarity of generated text to held-out data using Levenshtein distance ratio, and defines a near-exact match as having a ratio of at least 0.95. The rate of near-exact matches across datasets is used to infer whether the model has previously encountered the data, providing empirical evidence of memorization effects in the model's training process.
