The paper evaluates the capabilities of GPT-4, a large language model (LLM), in addressing medical challenges by assessing its performance on medical competency examinations and benchmark datasets. The analysis focuses on the United States Medical Licensing Examination (USMLE) and the MultiMedQA suite, revealing that GPT-4 surpasses the USMLE passing score by over 20 points without specialized training or prompt crafting. The model outperforms earlier iterations like GPT-3.5 and other models fine-tuned for medical knowledge, such as Med-PaLM.

The study also examines how GPT-4 performs with test questions that include text and images, investigates its memorization of training content, and assesses the calibration of its probability predictions, which is crucial in medical contexts. Findings indicate that GPT-4 exhibits better calibration than GPT-3.5, enhancing its ability to predict the accuracy of its responses.

Qualitative analysis further demonstrates GPT-4's ability to explain medical reasoning, tailor explanations for learners, and create counterfactual scenarios in medical contexts. The implications of these results suggest potential applications of GPT-4 in medical education, assessment, and clinical practice, while also highlighting the importance of addressing accuracy and safety challenges in these areas.
