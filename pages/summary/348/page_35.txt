The paper discusses schistosomiasis, a parasitic infection caused by various species of flatworms known as Schistosoma. The primary treatment involves antiparasitic medications, with praziquantel being the drug of choice for all major forms of the disease, including those caused by Schistosoma mansoni, Schistosoma haematobium, and Schistosoma japonicum. Praziquantel works by paralyzing the parasites, making them vulnerable to the host's immune response, and can be administered as a single oral dose or divided into multiple doses depending on the species and severity of the infection.

Oxamniquine serves as an alternative treatment specifically for Schistosoma mansoni infections in areas where praziquantel resistance is noted, though it is ineffective against other Schistosoma species. In addition to antiparasitic medication, supportive care may be necessary in severe cases, including analgesics and anti-inflammatory medications for pain management, corticosteroids for severe complications, and specific treatment for issues like anemia or organ damage.

Post-treatment monitoring is essential to confirm symptom resolution and infection clearance, with potential repeat treatments if necessary. Preventive measures, such as access to clean water, sanitation improvements, and snail control, are crucial in reducing transmission risk in endemic areas. Mass drug administration with praziquantel is also used as a public health strategy.
