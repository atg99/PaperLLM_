This paper examines the capabilities of the recently released GPT-4 model in addressing medical challenge problems, comparing its performance to its predecessor GPT-3.5 and the Flan-PaLM 540B model. The authors highlight the advancements in large language models (LLMs) that have emerged from extensive cross-disciplinary training, emphasizing methods such as fine-tuning with specialized datasets and reinforcement learning with human feedback to enhance model performance in specific domains.

The research draws from a historical context of AI in medicine, noting the evolution of computational methods aimed at assisting healthcare professionals. This includes various approaches, from probabilistic methods to deep learning models, particularly in the realm of medical diagnostics and reasoning.

Key findings indicate that GPT-4 demonstrates significant improvements in performance without requiring elaborate prompting or domain-specific fine-tuning. Using simple zero-shot and five-shot prompts, GPT-4 achieved an average score of 86.65% on the USMLE Self-Assessment and 86.7% on the Sample Exam, markedly surpassing GPT-3.5's scores of 53.61% and 58.78%. Additionally, GPT-4 outperformed GPT-3.5 and Flan-PaLM 540B on the MultiMedQA benchmark, reinforcing its potential as a powerful tool in medical problem-solving. The study establishes strong baseline performance metrics for GPT-4, contributing valuable insights into its applicability in the medical field.
