The paper introduces the Memorization effects Levenshtein detector (MELD), an algorithm designed to evaluate the performance of a generative language model in producing responses that align closely with ground truth answers. The algorithm operates on a dataset of question-answer pairs and utilizes a tokenizer to process the questions.

The process begins by initializing an empty list to hold overlap metrics. For each question-answer pair, the question is divided into two halves, which are then tokenized. The model's temperature is set to zero to ensure deterministic output, and the first half of the question is used as context for generating tokens based on the second half.

The core of the algorithm involves calculating the Levenshtein distance ratio between the generated text and the actual answer. This ratio reflects the degree of overlap, and if it exceeds a predefined threshold, the value is added to the list. Finally, the mean of the collected values is computed to produce a metric that measures the overall percentage of completions that surpass the threshold.

In summary, MELD provides a quantitative measure of the overlap between generated responses and correct answers, leveraging the Levenshtein distance to assess performance in a structured manner.
