The paper evaluates the performance of GPT-4 in answering multiple-choice questions on the USMLE medical licensing examination and compares it to GPT-3.5. The findings indicate that GPT-4 demonstrates significantly better calibration, with a higher correlation between assigned probabilities and correct answers. Specifically, GPT-4's average probability of 0.96 aligns with a 93% correctness rate, whereas GPT-3.5's similar probability corresponds to only 55% accuracy. This suggests that the calibration of language models may improve with increased scale.

The authors acknowledge limitations in their study, emphasizing that they aimed to establish baseline performance metrics without utilizing advanced prompting techniques. They note that richer prompting strategies, such as chain-of-thought prompting and information retrieval tools, have previously enhanced model performance and may yield even better results for GPT-4 with further exploration. Preliminary experiments with chain-of-thought prompting did not show performance improvements, but the authors recognize that variations in prompt structures could lead to different outcomes in future research. Overall, the paper highlights both the potential of GPT-4 and the need for continued investigation into effective prompting methods.
