This paper investigates the performance of the text-only version of GPT-4 in the medical domain, focusing on various aspects of its behavior. It examines the model's ability to answer text-centric examination questions compared to those that require image interpretation. A critical component of healthcare is the reliability of the probability of correctness assigned to answers, so the study assesses the calibration of these probabilities. The authors also explore whether GPT-4 has memorized examination content through its training data.

A case study is presented to illustrate GPT-4's capability to explain medical reasoning and engage students in discussions about counterfactual scenarios. The implications of the findings are discussed in terms of GPT-4's potential to aid medical education and support healthcare professionals, while addressing concerns about accuracy, fairness, and the broader impact on medical practice.

The paper critiques benchmark-based performance evaluations, highlighting the need for careful assessment before deploying models like GPT-4 in real-world settings. While caution is warranted, the authors anticipate several practical applications, especially in lower-stakes scenarios involving expert oversight. They express optimism about the long-term positive influence of GPT-4 and its successors on the field of medicine.

In terms of methodology, the study focuses on the text-only GPT-4, referred to as GPT-4 (no vision). Six datasets were utilized to evaluate the model, including the USMLE Sample Exam and USMLE Self Assessments from the National Board of Medical Examiners, alongside four publicly available benchmarks—MedQA, PubMedQA, MedMCQA, and MMLU. These datasets encompass various facets of medical knowledge and reasoning, forming a significant part of the newly introduced MultiMedQA benchmark. Further details on each dataset are included in the appendix.
