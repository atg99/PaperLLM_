The paper discusses the potential of GPT-4 in clinical applications, particularly in enhancing the quality of patient care while reducing costs. A revised clinical case highlights how altered lab results can indicate a higher likelihood of acute kidney injury (AKI) in a patient, underscoring the importance of accurate data interpretation. The paper notes that significant elevations in urea nitrogen and creatinine levels, along with the patient's recent NSAID use, support AKI as the primary diagnosis.

It emphasizes the gap between proficiency on medical benchmarks and real-world clinical application, suggesting that while GPT-4 shows promise in assisting healthcare professionals—especially in clinical reasoning and daily workflows—there are risks associated with erroneous outputs. The introduction of automation and machine learning in healthcare requires careful consideration to mitigate potential errors. Overall, the paper posits that with well-calibrated outputs, GPT-4 and similar technologies could significantly impact medical practice and research in the future.
