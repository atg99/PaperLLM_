The paper explores the impact of style variation and content preservation in images stylized using different artistic styles. It begins by extracting activations from the first fully connected layer of a neural network for real artworks, allowing for the approximation of style variation distributions. The study compares stylizations produced by a model with a disentanglement loss against one without, revealing that the former better captures the selected style. 

Further, the research investigates how changes in style affect content preservation through L2 distance measures in the feature space of a pretrained VGG16 network. A baseline distribution of content similarity is established using nearest neighbors from the ImageNet dataset. The analysis shows that stylizing images with different art styles introduces less perturbation to content than the baseline distance to nearest neighbors within the same class.

Qualitative results demonstrate the model's ability to discern subtle variations between styles and to learn fine artistic properties while minimizing artifacts and disentangling style and content. Overall, the findings indicate that the disentanglement loss significantly enhances the model's performance in maintaining style fidelity and content integrity during the stylization process.
