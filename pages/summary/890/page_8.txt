The paper presents a novel approach to style transfer that effectively disentangles content and style, allowing for nuanced variations within and between styles. The method utilizes a fixpoint triplet loss to enhance the model's ability to represent different styles while preserving content. The experiment evaluates the effectiveness of the approach through metrics such as deception rate, classification accuracy, and style divergence. Results indicate that the model incorporating the LFPT-style loss significantly improves the alignment with real artwork distributions compared to models without this loss. The findings suggest that understanding the relationship between style and content is crucial for art historians, as the method allows for detailed analysis of stylistic developments. Overall, the proposed approach enhances the stylization process, offering better control and fidelity in the representation of artistic styles.
