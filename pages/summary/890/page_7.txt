The paper discusses the performance of a new stylization model compared to existing models such as AST, Gatys et al., and CycleGAN. Figure 6 illustrates the qualitative results, showing that the proposed model (in red) produces higher quality stylizations with fewer artificial structures and artifacts. This indicates a successful disentanglement of style and content, especially notable in the stylized example of Monet. In contrast, the AST model generates elements that do not appear in the original content image, such as flowers in landscape paintings.

The paper also examines the distribution divergence of styles represented by the model. It measures how well the stylized images capture the variability of the intended style distribution using Kullback-Leibler divergence (DKL). The model's style distribution is approximated through activations from a network trained to classify painting styles. The divergence is computed between the style distributions of real artworks and those of the stylized images, including comparisons with a model that lacks the fixed-point triplet style loss.

To visualize the style distributions, the authors train models with and without the fixed-point triplet style loss and stylize the same set of content images. By analyzing the activations of the network, they conduct a PCA to visualize the projections of the style distributions for selected artists.
