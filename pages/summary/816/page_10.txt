The references provided encompass various methods and advancements in the field of deep neural network compression and optimization. Key contributions include:

1. **Thinet** (Luo et al., 2017) proposes a filter-level pruning technique aimed at reducing the size of deep neural networks while maintaining performance.
2. **ShuffleNet V2** (Ma et al., 2018) offers practical guidelines for efficient CNN architecture design, emphasizing the balance between efficiency and accuracy.
3. **Random Pruning** (Mittal et al., 2018) explores the resilience of deep convolutional networks to random pruning, highlighting their plasticity.
4. **Human-level Control** (Mnih et al., 2015) discusses achieving advanced control through deep reinforcement learning, showcasing the capabilities of neural networks in complex tasks.
5. **Pruning for Resource Efficiency** (Molchanov et al., 2016) focuses on pruning CNNs to enhance inference efficiency without significant loss in accuracy.
6. **Collaborative Channel Pruning** (Peng et al., 2019) introduces a method for joint channel pruning across multiple layers to optimize network performance.
7. **MobileNetV2** (Sandler et al., 2018) presents a lightweight architecture with inverted residuals and linear bottlenecks for efficient mobile applications.
8. **NISP** (Yu et al., 2018) utilizes neuron importance score propagation for effective network pruning.
9. **Multi-layer Pruning** (Zeng and Urtasun, 2018) automates the compression of neural networks across multiple layers.
10. **Discrimination-aware Channel Pruning** (Zhuang et al., 2018) emphasizes the importance of maintaining discrimination capability while pruning channels.

These studies collectively contribute to the understanding and development of techniques that enhance the efficiency and performance of deep learning models, addressing the challenges of model size and computational resource requirements.
