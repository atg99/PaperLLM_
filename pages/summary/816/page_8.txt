The paper discusses a novel approach to network pruning that focuses on structural redundancy reduction in convolutional neural networks (CNNs). It emphasizes the importance of pruning filters from layers with high redundancy rather than solely targeting the least important filters across all layers. 

The authors analyze the impact of different redundancy measurement strategies on pruning performance, specifically comparing three methods: SRR-NOF, which uses the number of filters as a measure of redundancy; SRR-PCA, which employs principal component analysis on feature maps; and SRR-GR, which utilizes graph redundancy. Experimental results indicate that pruning 44% of FLOPs from ResNet50 yields competitive performance, with SRR-PCA achieving minimal accuracy drops compared to SRR-NOF. The graph redundancy-based method significantly enhances pruning performance.

Additionally, the study explores the influence of the distance threshold γ on filter selection, revealing that an appropriate γ value allows for more effective identification of redundant layers. The findings support the conclusion that structural redundancy reduction is a viable strategy for channel pruning, and the proposed graph redundancy approach shows promise for further improving pruning techniques.

The paper concludes by asserting that understanding redundancy in CNNs can lead to more efficient pruning methods, suggesting potential applications in more complex tasks like object detection and image synthesis for future research.
