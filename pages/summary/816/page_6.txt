The paper presents a comparison of various channel pruning methods applied to ResNet architectures on CIFAR-10 and ImageNet datasets. The focus is on the effectiveness of the proposed approach, which emphasizes redundancy identification before filter pruning. Results indicate that the proposed method achieves significant reductions in FLOPs while maintaining or improving accuracy compared to state-of-the-art methods.

For ResNet20 on CIFAR-10, the proposed method prunes 45.8% of FLOPs and improves accuracy by 0.21%, while for ResNet56, it reduces FLOPs by 53.8% with a 0.37% accuracy increase. In contrast, other methods often resulted in accuracy drops exceeding 1% when pruning similar amounts of FLOPs. On ImageNet, the proposed method maintains high accuracy with only a 0.37% drop in top-1 accuracy after a 44.1% reduction in FLOPs, outperforming prior approaches.

An ablation study shows that the l-covering number estimation is effective and computationally feasible, with negligible deviations observed across experiments. The findings underscore the proposed approach's ability to prune networks efficiently while preserving performance, establishing it as a leading method in channel pruning.
