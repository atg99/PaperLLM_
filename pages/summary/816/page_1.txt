This paper investigates convolutional neural network (CNN) pruning, emphasizing the importance of structural redundancy over merely removing the least important filters. The authors argue that pruning filters from layers with higher structural redundancy yields better performance than traditional methods focused on filter importance. They present a statistical model that supports this claim, demonstrating that pruning in redundant layers can be more effective, even when filters are selected randomly.

The proposed approach, named layer-adaptive channel pruning based on structural redundancy reduction (SRR), involves creating a graph for each convolutional layer and measuring redundancy using the ℓ-covering number and quotient space size. This method allows for the identification of unimportant filters in the most redundant layers, leading to improved network compactness and performance.

Key contributions include a theoretical analysis of network pruning from a redundancy perspective and a novel pruning method that significantly outperforms existing state-of-the-art techniques across various benchmark architectures and datasets.
