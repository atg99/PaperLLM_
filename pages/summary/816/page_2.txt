This paper presents a new approach for pruning convolutional neural networks (CNNs) by analyzing redundancy in each layer. Instead of focusing on the least important filters across all layers, the method targets unimportant filters in the most redundant layers. The authors validate their approach on various architectures and datasets, achieving state-of-the-art results in channel pruning. Notably, their pruned ResNet50 model on ImageNet reduces 44.1% of floating point operations (FLOPs) while only losing 0.37% in top-1 accuracy.

The paper reviews existing work on network pruning, highlighting early weight pruning methods that create unstructured sparsities, and discussing channel pruning techniques that remove entire filters without requiring specialized hardware. These existing methods typically focus on identifying and removing the least important filters based on various criteria, such as weight magnitudes and activation values.

The authors argue that the structure of the network post-pruning is crucial for maintaining performance and suggest that pruning filters in layers with high redundancy is more effective than targeting the least important filters. They provide a theoretical analysis that formulates the channel pruning problem from a redundancy reduction perspective, showing that pruning in redundant layers improves overall network performance.

The findings are substantiated through statistical modeling, demonstrating that pruning strategies can be optimized based on layer redundancy rather than solely filter importance. This approach offers a novel perspective on network pruning, emphasizing structural redundancy as a key factor in enhancing CNN efficiency.
