The paper discusses network performance in the context of redundancy and filter pruning in convolutional neural networks (CNNs). It presents equations that quantify the probabilities of filter contributions exceeding certain thresholds, emphasizing that as long as the total contribution of filters in a layer is above a threshold, performance remains intact. The authors argue that layers with excessive redundancy can afford to prune filters without performance loss.

Key findings include the bounded nature of filter contributions and the convergence of the average filter contributions to a limit as the number of filters increases. The paper establishes that pruning filters from layers with higher redundancy leads to better performance compared to removing the least important filters across all layers. This conclusion holds regardless of the distribution of random variables involved, provided the number of filters is sufficiently large.

Empirical results demonstrate that even simple pruning strategies outperform more complex methods, especially in networks with high redundancy. The methodology section outlines the setup for network pruning, focusing on the number of layers and filters in the CNN. Overall, the study suggests a strategic approach to filter pruning that leverages redundancy for improved network efficiency.
