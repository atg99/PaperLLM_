The paper discusses a novel approach to redundancy identification and filter pruning in convolutional neural networks (CNNs). The method involves establishing a graph for each layer of the CNN, where vertices represent filters and edges are defined by the distances between filter weights. The goal is to measure structural redundancy in each layer and prune filters from the layers with the highest redundancy.

Key steps in the approach include:

1. **Graph Establishment**: Filters are normalized and represented as points on a unit sphere. An edge set is created based on the Euclidean distance between filters, indicating their similarity.

2. **Redundancy Measurement**: The redundancy in each layer is quantified using the quotient space size and the ℓ-covering number, which helps in assessing the structural redundancy of the filters.

3. **Pruning Process**: Filters are randomly removed from the layer identified as having the most redundancy. After each removal, the graph is reconstructed, and redundancy is recalculated until a target number of filters is pruned.

4. **Layer-Adaptivity**: Filters are ranked and pruned separately in each layer rather than globally, allowing for a more tailored pruning strategy based on the redundancy identified in each layer.

The overall workflow emphasizes that pruning filters from layers with higher redundancy yields better performance than simply removing the least important filters across all layers. This layer-adaptive approach aims to optimize the network while maintaining its accuracy and efficiency.
