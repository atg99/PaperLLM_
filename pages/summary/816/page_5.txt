The paper discusses the mathematical framework of equivalence relations and their application in defining quotient spaces, specifically focusing on graph redundancy. The authors introduce the concept of graph redundancy, defined through a formula that considers the size of the quotient space and the number of filters in a neural network. They assert that higher values of the quotient space size and covering number indicate more complex data structures with reduced redundancy.

To estimate the 1-covering number, a lightweight method is proposed due to the NP-hard nature of the ℓ-covering number calculation. This method involves constructing a sequence of nodes based on their degrees until a 1-cover set is formed. The authors provide bounds for the estimated covering number and validate their approach through extensive experiments on various networks.

The paper also outlines a filter selection strategy, emphasizing the pruning of unimportant filters based on their absolute weights. This strategy aims to enhance computational efficiency by avoiding extensive training for filter ranking.

In the experimental section, the authors evaluate their pruning approach using two benchmark datasets, CIFAR-10 and ImageNet, on ResNet architectures. They assess the performance in terms of accuracy and computational efficiency after pruning and fine-tuning the networks. The results indicate the effectiveness of their proposed method in reducing redundancy while maintaining performance.
