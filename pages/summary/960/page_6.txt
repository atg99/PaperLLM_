The paper discusses advancements in verification schemes for deep neural networks (DNNs), particularly focusing on the Marabou tool. Marabou enhances DNN-level reasoning by storing the initial network topology and utilizing deduction steps in its iterative search process. Key features include the integration of DNN-level reasoners with real-time updates from the search, and the implementation of a symbolic bound tightening method. This method expresses upper and lower bounds for hidden neurons as linear combinations of input neurons, allowing for tighter bounds to restrict piecewise-linear activation functions to specific linear segments.

The core component of Marabou is the Engine class, which manages the solution process through a structured main loop. This loop involves various steps, including case splitting for repeatedly fixed constraints, handling unsatisfiable conditions, addressing violated linear and piecewise-linear constraints, and confirming satisfaction of all constraints. The Engine also initiates deduction steps based on heuristics.

Additionally, Marabou features a divide-and-conquer (D&C) mode, which partitions the input region into sub-regions for independent property checks. This mode supports parallel execution, significantly enhancing performance. Even in sequential execution, solving smaller sub-regions typically results in lower overall solving times due to improved deduction efficiency. The paper highlights ongoing efforts to implement further DNN-level reasoning operations within Marabou.
