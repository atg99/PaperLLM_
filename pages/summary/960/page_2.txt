The paper introduces Marabou, a tool designed for the verification and analysis of deep neural networks (DNNs). It builds on previous work from the Reluplex project, which utilized Satisfiability Modulo Theories (SMT) techniques for DNN verification. Marabou processes verification queries that consist of a neural network and a property to check, aiming to confirm whether the network adheres to the specified property or to find a counter-example if it does not.

A DNN operates through layers of neurons, where the output is computed by iteratively applying weighted sums and non-linear activation functions to the inputs. This structure gives rise to a set of linear and non-linear constraints, forming the foundation of the verification problem. The goal is to identify neuron assignments that satisfy all constraints or to prove that no such assignments exist.

Marabou represents a significant advancement over Reluplex by incorporating several enhancements: it supports fully connected and convolutional DNNs with various piecewise-linear activation functions, implements a divide-and-conquer strategy for solving queries that promotes parallel processing, and replaces the previous external solver with a complete simplex-based linear programming core for improved efficiency. These improvements enable Marabou to handle a broader range of DNN architectures and enhance verification performance.
