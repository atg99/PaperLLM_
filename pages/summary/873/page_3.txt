The paper discusses a novel approach to enhancing the performance of Transformer models through the implementation of stochastic residual connections. The inner function F can represent various components such as self-attention, feed-forward layers, or decoder-encoder attention. To maintain the hidden layer magnitudes, layer normalization is applied. Stochastic residual connections introduce a mask M that determines whether the inner function F is activated or skipped, based on Bernoulli distribution values. This allows for diverse sub-network configurations during training while presenting the full network during inference, effectively ensembling different sub-networks.

The methodology involves setting a probability p for dropping layers, which is adjusted according to the layer depth. Lower layers, handling raw-level features, have a lower probability of being dropped. The output of selected layers during training is scaled to compensate for those not skipped.

The experimental setup involved training on the Switchboard-1 corpus and the TED-LIUM 3 dataset, utilizing 40 log mel filter-bank features. The models were based on configurations from the original Transformer paper, with specific hyperparameters such as embedding dimension and hidden state size. The results indicate that deeper models significantly reduce word error rates (WER), with stochastic connections particularly enhancing performance in deeper architectures. The findings suggest that while shallow configurations are insufficient, deeper models with stochastic layers improve generalization and performance on test sets, demonstrating a promising direction for future Transformer model enhancements.
