The paper presents a model architecture for transforming acoustic features into character-level transcriptions, primarily utilizing an encoder-decoder framework with attention mechanisms. The model consists of an encoder that processes the source sequence to create a high-level representation, and a decoder that generates the target sequence as a conditional language model. The decoder's output probability is conditioned on previously generated tokens and the encoder's representation. Both components rely on neural networks to learn relationships between input and output sequences, with attention mechanisms replacing traditional recurrence.

Multi-head attention is a central feature of the model, allowing for content-based information extraction via queries, keys, and values. This method improves upon standard dot-product attention by scaling queries and projecting them into multiple heads, enabling simultaneous attention operations. Unlike recurrent and convolutional connections, self-attention aggregates information across all time steps without requiring intermediate transformations.

The architecture includes layers composed of self-attentional sub-layers and feed-forward neural networks. To handle long speech utterances, the input features are grouped into steps, and sinusoidal positional encoding is added to maintain the sequence's integrity. Residual connections are employed to facilitate training, complemented by layer normalization to stabilize neuron values.

The decoder mirrors the encoder's structure but incorporates masked self-attention to preserve the auto-regressive property. An additional attention layer connects the decoder's hidden states to the encoder's outputs. The design offers advantages over RNNs and CNNs, including efficient parallelization of computations and the ability to train deeper networks effectively due to the combination of residual connections and layer normalization.

To combat overfitting and optimization challenges inherent in deep models, the authors propose stochastic residual layers, inspired by dropout techniques. This approach allows for random layer omission during training, enhancing the model's robustness while minimizing redundancy in its architecture.
