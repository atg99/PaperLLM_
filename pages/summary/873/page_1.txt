The paper "Very Deep Self-Attention Networks for End-to-End Speech Recognition" by Ngoc-Quan Pham et al. introduces a novel approach to automatic speech recognition (ASR) using deep Transformer networks. Unlike traditional methods that typically rely on time-delay neural networks (TDNN) or long short-term memory (LSTM) networks, the authors leverage self-attention mechanisms inherent in the Transformer architecture. This shift aims to enhance performance by enabling direct connections between sequence elements, allowing for efficient handling of long-range dependencies without the constraints of recurrent structures.

The authors conducted extensive experiments, training models with up to 48 Transformer layers, combined with stochastic residual connections to improve generalizability and training efficiency. Their results indicate that these deep models surpass previous end-to-end ASR methods and reach competitive performance levels with conventional hybrid systems. Specifically, they achieved word error rates (WER) of 9.9% on the Switchboard test set and 17.7% on the CallHome test set, outperforming existing models and demonstrating the effectiveness of deep self-attention networks in ASR tasks.

The paper emphasizes that depth is a crucial factor for achieving competitive performance in end-to-end ASR models. Additionally, the proposed stochastic depth variation helps regularize the training of very deep configurations, contributing significantly to the state-of-the-art results on the Switchboard benchmark. The findings suggest that the Transformer architecture is a viable alternative for ASR, potentially simplifying model structures while maintaining high performance.
