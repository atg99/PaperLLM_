This paper discusses the application of linear regression methods for analyzing binary outcomes in relation to treatment effects. The authors emphasize that simple linear regression of a binary outcome on a binary treatment yields unbiased estimates of the average treatment effect (ATE), as indicated in Equation 3. The inclusion of discrete covariates can enhance statistical power and precision in both experimental and quasi-experimental designs, allowing for more accurate causal effect estimates.

In the context of continuous covariates, the authors note that the conditional expectation function (CEF) may be nonlinear, raising questions about the appropriateness of linear models. While ordinary least squares (OLS) regression may not capture the CEF accurately, it can provide a reasonable approximation near the center of the distribution. The paper also highlights the violation of the homoskedasticity assumption in binary outcomes, recommending the use of heteroskedasticity-robust standard errors to address this issue.

The authors argue for the preference of linear regression analysis when the target estimand is the average causal effect, as OLS coefficients directly relate to percentage point changes in probabilities. In contrast, coefficients from nonlinear models such as logistic regression are less interpretable, often requiring additional steps for meaningful communication of results.

Overall, the paper advocates for the use of linear regression for binary outcomes due to its interpretability and direct estimation of causal effects, while acknowledging the limitations and complexities associated with nonlinear modeling approaches.
