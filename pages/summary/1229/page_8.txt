The paper discusses the implications of using odds ratios (OR) in logistic regression compared to linear regression, particularly in the context of binary outcomes. The authors argue that the relevance of ORs is contingent on the mean of the dependent variable in the control group, suggesting that the same OR can represent different effect sizes depending on this mean. This highlights the importance of effect size interpretation, as ORs can be misleading.

The findings indicate that both logistic and linear regression yield similar estimates for average treatment effects (ATE) in the analyzed dataset, which consists of a large number of observations (24,191 students) and a limited number of clusters (14). Despite the common use of nonlinear models like logit and probit in psychological research, the authors advocate for the use of linear regression as it provides more interpretable results in terms of probabilities, which is essential for effective communication of effect sizes.

The paper emphasizes that the statistical significance of results remains consistent across both methods, supporting the recommendation for researchers to adopt linear regression as the standard practice for estimating causal effects on binary outcomes. In cases where multiple regression analysis is conducted, the authors advise performing sensitivity analyses to ensure robustness against other analytic methods. The paper concludes that the simplicity and interpretability of linear regression make it a preferable choice over more complex models in psychological research.
