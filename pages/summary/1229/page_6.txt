This paper investigates the estimation of the Average Treatment Effect (ATE) in both experimental and quasi-experimental settings. It utilizes binary and discrete variables to analyze the impact of a treatment on participant outcomes. The treatment is shown to have an average effect of 0.08, equivalent to 8 percentage points. Simulations are performed using Declare Design, with varying sample sizes and outcomes modeled through linear and logistic regression.

In the experimental context, both regression methods are found to be unbiased and consistent in estimating the ATE. The simulation results indicate that the linear and logistic regression approaches yield similar distributions for the ATE.

In the quasi-experimental framework, the analysis includes two covariates: college degree status and religiosity level. The potential outcomes are generated from a logit model, resulting in an ATE of 0.38. The paper presents visual illustrations demonstrating the unbiasedness and consistency of regression estimators across different sample sizes and baseline probabilities, reinforcing the effectiveness of these estimation methods in capturing the ATE. 

Overall, the findings contribute to the understanding of treatment effects in experimental and quasi-experimental designs, emphasizing the reliability of regression techniques in estimating causal impacts.
