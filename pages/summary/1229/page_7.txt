The paper investigates the impact of an anticonflict intervention in 56 New Jersey middle schools, utilizing a dataset comprising survey and administrative data for 24,191 students. The study aims to compare the effectiveness of linear and logistic regression in estimating the average treatment effect (ATE) using both models. The dataset includes instances of disciplinary events and was structured into treatment and control groups through random assignment.

The analysis employs 12 binary outcome variables, reporting results from both linear and logistic regression models. The findings indicate that both models yield comparable p-values, suggesting that the choice of analytic strategy does not significantly affect statistical significance. However, the interpretation of results differs markedly. Linear regression provides results in terms of ATE expressed as probabilities, making them straightforward to interpret, while logistic regression results are presented as odds ratios (ORs), which can be more complex to understand.

The paper also highlights that logistic regression may perform poorly with fixed effects, as suggested by prior research. The results demonstrate that while logistic regression coefficients can be challenging to interpret, the linear regression outputs offer more direct insights into the average treatment effects. Overall, the study underscores the importance of analytic strategy selection in behavioral research and its implications for interpreting intervention impacts.
