This paper discusses the causal effects of variables with any distribution on binary outcomes, employing the Neyman-Rubin causal model to demonstrate that linear regression can yield unbiased estimates of causal effects, even when outcomes are binary. The author argues for the advantages of linear regression over nonlinear models like logit or probit, particularly in experimental contexts. 

One argument for nonlinear models is their ability to constrain predictions between 0 and 1, preventing impossible forecasts that linear regression might produce. Additionally, predictions outside this interval can lead to biased and inconsistent estimates in OLS regression, as shown by Horrace and Oaxaca (2006). The paper also highlights that binary outcomes introduce heteroskedasticity, violating OLS assumptions and further complicating analysis.

In psychology research, which often emphasizes causal mechanisms over predictions, the need to worry about out-of-bound predictions from linear regression is minimized, especially in experimental designs where random assignment mitigates bias. The paper compares linear and logistic regression, indicating that while both methods can provide unbiased and consistent estimates, linear regression offers advantages in interpretability and immediate estimates of average treatment effects. 

Overall, the author advocates for the use of linear regression as a straightforward and effective analytical strategy for binary outcomes, particularly in the context of experimental research.
