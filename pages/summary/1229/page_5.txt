In nonexperimental settings, causal inference relies on the assumption that the potential outcomes and treatment assignment are independent when conditioned on observed covariates. The treatment effect is estimated by conditioning on these covariates. The paper presents a formula expressing the treatment effect, highlighting the importance of using linear regression as a smoothing tool to approximate the expected outcome given the covariates and treatment status.

The author conducts simulations to compare the performance of linear and logistic regression in estimating the average treatment effect (ATE) across different sample sizes and baseline probabilities for binary outcomes. The simulation generates potential outcomes for six binary variables with varying probabilities of success, alongside two covariates. Results show that linear and logistic regression estimators can be unbiased and consistent in estimating the ATE, with graphical representations illustrating the estimates across multiple scenarios.

The findings emphasize the utility of linear regression in analyzing binary outcomes, particularly in contexts where random assignment is not feasible, thus contributing to the understanding of causal inference methodologies.
