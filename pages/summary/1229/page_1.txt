In the paper "Logistic or Linear? Estimating Causal Effects of Experimental Treatments on Binary Outcomes Using Regression Analysis," Robin Gomila argues that linear regression is generally the superior method for estimating causal effects of treatments on binary outcomes, contrary to the common reliance on nonlinear models like logit and probit. Gomila highlights that while nonlinear models are often used in psychology research, they can complicate the interpretation of coefficients and are not always justified based on statistical advancements.

The paper reviews the Neyman-Rubin causal model, demonstrating analytically that linear regression provides unbiased estimates of treatment effects on binary outcomes. Gomila emphasizes the advantages of linear regression, such as direct interpretability of coefficients as probabilities and suitability for including interaction terms and fixed effects. In contrast, converting logit and probit coefficients into probabilities requires complex methods, making them less practical.

Gomila supports his argument with simulations and empirical analysis of data from 24,191 students across 56 middle schools, illustrating the effectiveness of linear regression. The conclusion recommends that psychologists adopt linear regression for estimating treatment effects on binary outcomes, given its clarity and reliability in interpretation.
