The paper discusses a method for generating PI-explanations for Decision Trees (DTs) by transforming the problem into the enumeration of minimal hitting sets (MHS). The focus is on predicting outcomes for specific points in feature space that are consistent with certain tree paths. Each path in the set is inconsistent with at least one literal associated with either the input point or the corresponding tree path. 

For a subset of literals to entail the prediction, it must intersect with each set of literals that are inconsistent with the paths. The minimal hitting sets among the possible subsets represent the PI-explanations. The examples provided illustrate how to identify the sets to hit for given inputs and the resulting MHS that indicate PI-explanations.

The paper also includes an experimental evaluation of explanation redundancy in two prominent heuristic DT classifiers, ITI and IAI. ITI is utilized with a pruning option to prevent overfitting and produce shallow trees, while IAI is configured to generate shallow trees with high accuracy. The experiments involve 80 publicly available datasets from various repositories, although results are reported for only 30 due to space limitations. Findings suggest that increasing the maximum depth of the trees typically raises the percentage of redundant paths, while a smaller depth does not enhance accuracy. Other DT learning tools were excluded from the study due to their limitations with data types.
