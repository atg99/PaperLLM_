The paper "On Explaining Decision Trees" by Yacine Izza, Alexey Ignatiev, and Joao Marques-Silva discusses the interpretability of decision trees (DTs) in machine learning, challenging the prevailing notion that they are inherently interpretable. The authors argue that in certain cases, the paths in a DT can be significantly longer than the minimal set of feature values required to explain a prediction, termed PI-explanations. 

To address this, the paper introduces a new model for computing PI-explanations efficiently in polynomial time. It also establishes a connection between the enumeration of PI-explanations and the enumeration of minimal hitting sets. The experimental results, derived from various publicly available datasets, demonstrate that the majority of DT paths are indeed larger than the corresponding PI-explanations.

The introduction highlights the historical context of DTs as interpretable models and notes the extensive research focused on enhancing their interpretability through structural properties. However, the paper emphasizes the need to reassess the interpretability of DTs, suggesting that a deeper understanding of their structure is necessary to provide succinct explanations for predictions.
