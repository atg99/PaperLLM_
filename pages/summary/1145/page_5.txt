The paper discusses the limitations of decision trees (DTs) in providing interpretable explanations for their predictions, particularly in relation to PI-explanations, which are succinct and constant in size. The authors demonstrate that there exist Boolean functions for which decision trees contain paths that include all features, resulting in explanations that can be significantly larger than the corresponding PI-explanations.

The key argument is outlined in Proposition 1, which states that for certain Boolean functions, an irreducible decision tree will have paths that are a proper superset of a PI-explanation. The difference in the number of literals can be substantial, specifically n-k, where n is the total number of features and k is the constant size of the PI-explanation.

An example is provided using a specific Boolean function defined over n features, illustrating that while the decision tree may yield a long path containing multiple literals, the actual PI-explanation can be represented with just two literals. This highlights a fundamental limitation of decision trees in succinctly representing certain functions, regardless of whether the decision trees are redundant or not.

The paper concludes that decision trees are inherently less succinct than other models such as decision lists and decision sets, which can lead to the inability of decision trees to provide compact explanations for certain classes of functions. This raises important considerations for the interpretability of AI and the use of decision trees in practice.
