The paper discusses the distinction between path-restricted and path-unrestricted explanations in the context of decision trees and feature analysis. The analysis focuses on the case of feature x1, where all paths are deemed inconsistent due to certain literals, leading to the conclusion that x1 is redundant. A PI-explanation for the prediction P2 is identified as the literals (x3=1) and (x4=1).

Path-restricted explanations consider only the literals specified by the instance, while path-unrestricted explanations incorporate all literals consistent with the instance, including those not present in the path. An example illustrates this difference: with a given point (x1,x2,x3,x4)=(1,1,1,1), the path-restricted PI-explanation is limited to {(x1=1),(x3=1)}, but the path-unrestricted explanation expands to include additional literals, such as {(x2=1),(x3=1),(x4=1)}.

The paper also highlights the importance of enumerating multiple PI-explanations to aid human decision-making and enhance understanding of machine learning models. Recent advancements allow for the compilation of decision functions into Sentential Decision Diagrams (SDDs), facilitating polynomial-time enumeration of PI-explanations. However, the representation can be exponentially larger than the original model. Alternative methods utilizing iterative entailment checks with an NP-oracle are discussed, though they do not address the enumeration challenge.
