The paper discusses the analysis of decision trees (DTs), specifically focusing on explanation redundancy within the paths of a DT illustrated in Figure 6. The analysis reveals three distinct paths based on the conditions of variables y and x. 

1. The path where y is greater than 0.73 is identified as explanation-irredundant, meaning it contributes uniquely to predictions.
2. The path where y is less than or equal to 0.73 and x is greater than 0.64 is deemed explanation-redundant, indicating that if y is fixed, x does not influence the prediction.
3. The path where both y is less than or equal to 0.73 and x is less than or equal to 0.64 is also explanation-irredundant.

From this analysis, it is concluded that 1 out of the 3 paths exhibits explanation redundancy, leading to a determination that the decision tree has a 33.33% rate of explanation redundancy. The paper notes that similar findings of explanation redundancy are present in various decision trees referenced in multiple studies. Additionally, a full table of experimental results on an extended set of datasets is provided, showcasing further findings in the analysis.
