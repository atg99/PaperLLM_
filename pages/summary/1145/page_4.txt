The paper discusses the concept of PI-explanations in the context of decision trees (DTs) and their role in providing interpretability for machine learning predictions. It defines a PI-explanation as a set of literals that entails a specific prediction while any proper subset of these literals does not. The conditions for a set E to qualify as a PI-explanation for a prediction π given a value v are outlined, emphasizing the necessity for all literals in E to support the prediction and the inability of any smaller subset to do so.

The paper introduces the notion of explanation-redundancy, where a path Rk in a decision tree is deemed explanation-redundant if it fails to serve as a PI-explanation for the associated prediction π. This redundancy is illustrated through an example involving a DT for the function f(x1, x2) = x1 ∨ x2, demonstrating that while a specific path may provide an explanation, it may not be minimal or necessary for justifying the prediction.

The authors also reference related work that explores the interpretability of decision trees but note that the specific evaluation of paths in terms of PI-explanations is a relatively unexplored area. The paper sets the stage for a deeper examination of explanation-redundancy and its implications for interpreting machine learning models.
