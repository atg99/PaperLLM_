The paper presents an analysis of decision trees (DT) based on examples from Poole & Mackworth's and Z.-H. Zhou's books. The first case study examines a DT that predicts whether a person reads an article based on three features: Author, Thread, and Length. The analysis reveals that two out of four paths in the decision tree exhibit explanation redundancy, resulting in a 50% redundancy rate. Specifically, certain combinations of features render others irrelevant for prediction, indicating that the decision-making process could be streamlined.

In the second case study, the DT focuses on predicting the type of a drawing, categorized as either cross or circle, using two features: whether x is greater than 0.64 and whether y is greater than 0.73. The analysis of this tree is not detailed in the provided text but suggests a similar approach to evaluating the relevance of features in making predictions.

Overall, the study emphasizes the importance of understanding feature relevance in decision trees, which can enhance predictive accuracy and efficiency.
