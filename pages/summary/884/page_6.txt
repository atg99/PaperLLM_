The paper discusses the challenges and solutions related to stylization in neural networks, particularly those utilizing residual connections like ResNet. It highlights how deeper layers tend to exhibit peakier activations with lower entropy, which can negatively impact stylization performance. The authors propose a method called Stylization With Activation Smoothing (SWAG) to address this issue by applying a softmax-based smoothing transformation to the activations. This transformation reduces large peaks and enhances smaller values, resulting in a more uniform distribution of activations.

The paper notes that while altering the architecture can improve stylization, it may compromise classification performance, as demonstrated by experiments showing significant drops in accuracy for modified ResNet architectures. The proposed SWAG method, however, maintains the original network structure while improving stylization outcomes, as evidenced by qualitative evaluations on various networks, including Inception-v3 and Wide ResNet. The results indicate that SWAG enhances the transfer of high-level style patterns, showing that SWAG models can approach or even outperform traditional VGG models in stylization tasks.

Overall, the study emphasizes the effectiveness of activation smoothing in enhancing stylization without sacrificing the classification capabilities of widely used network architectures.
