The paper presents an analysis of stylization using various neural network architectures, specifically focusing on how different configurations impact activation statistics. It categorizes the architectures into pre-trained (denoted by 'p-') and randomly initialized (denoted by 'r-'), with an additional distinction for models that utilize SWAG (indicated by '∗').

Figures 2 and 3 illustrate the effects of these architectures on activation maxima and entropy across different layers. The results indicate that as depth increases, activation maxima tend to decrease while entropies remain relatively constant and high. Notably, the pseudo-ResVGG architecture exhibits significantly large maxima and near-zero entropies in deeper layers, suggesting a dominance of single feature channels and deterministic correlations.

This analysis highlights the varying behavior of activations in response to architectural changes, providing insights into the effectiveness of different models in stylization tasks.
