The paper investigates the differences in style transfer results between different neural network architectures, specifically comparing ResNet with VGG. It highlights that deeper layers of ResNet exhibit activations characterized by large peaks and low entropy, indicating a dominance of a few feature channels. This leads to a bias in the style transfer optimization process, causing ResNet to replicate only a few dominant style patterns while neglecting a broader range of styles, particularly high-level patterns like strokes.

In contrast, VGG activations are more uniformly distributed across layers, allowing for a richer diversity of style patterns to be captured. The authors attribute the peaky activations observed in ResNet to the presence of residual connections, which are common in modern architectures. This characteristic contributes to the robustness problem, where style transfer does not perform optimally.

To address these issues without altering the network architecture, the authors propose a method called Stylization With Activation smoothing (SWAG). This approach involves smoothing the activations used in loss computations through a softmax transformation. The experiments demonstrate that SWAG enhances the performance of various stylization algorithms across different architectures, including ResNet and Inception. Furthermore, it shows that random networks can achieve performance levels comparable to pre-trained models when SWAG is applied, and non-VGG models with SWAG can even surpass VGG in terms of stylization performance.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the significance of network architecture in style transfer and presents a novel method to improve robustness and performance across diverse neural networks.
