The section discusses the impact of weight factors on layer contributions to loss in neural networks, specifically in the context of image stylization using VGG and ResNet architectures. It highlights the use of pre-trained models and random weight initialization, noting that the performance of stylization varies significantly between these network architectures.

The authors emphasize the importance of residual connections in ResNet, which are traditionally considered beneficial for tasks like classification. However, they found that removing these connections, as demonstrated by creating a 'NoResNet' version of ResNet-50, significantly improved stylization performance. Modifications to further align the NoResNet with VGG architecture, such as changing convolution kernel sizes and adding maxpooling layers, resulted in even better performance, indicating that the main issue with ResNet's performance in stylization tasks stems from its residual connections.

The authors conducted an ablation study to investigate this phenomenon, providing evidence that the removal of residual connections leads to a more effective style transfer. They also examined the statistics of network activations and Gram matrices, revealing that networks with residual connections exhibited increasing maximum values and decreasing entropy in activation distributions as layer depth increased. This behavior contrasts with networks lacking residual connections, which maintained a more stable profile.

In conclusion, the paper argues that the poor performance of ResNet in stylization tasks is primarily due to its residual connections, which hinder the network's ability to effectively transfer styles.
