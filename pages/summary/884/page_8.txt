The paper investigates the performance of neural style transfer algorithms, comparing standard implementations to those enhanced by a technique called SWAG. The results indicate that models utilizing SWAG consistently received higher preferences than their non-SWAG counterparts, particularly outperforming standard VGG implementations. This suggests that SWAG mitigates the reliance on VGG architecture for stylization tasks.

An ablation study was conducted to evaluate the contributions of SWAG to content and style loss, using image reconstruction and texture synthesis tasks with random initial images. The findings revealed that SWAG improved image reconstruction quality and texture diversity, indicating that residual connections may detract from the effectiveness of both content and style losses. SWAG was shown to facilitate better style matching at deeper layers, enhancing overall performance.

The conclusion emphasizes the limitations of traditional stylization algorithms with non-VGG architectures, attributing some issues to residual connections that reduce activation entropy. The introduction of SWAG, which incorporates activation smoothing via a softmax function, presents a promising solution, allowing lightweight non-VGG architectures to become viable alternatives for stylization tasks.
