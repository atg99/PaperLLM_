The paper presents a comparison of two models, LXMERT and LOL, trained on Visual Question Answering (VQA) data, particularly focusing on their performance when combined with different training strategies such as Compose, Supplement, and Frechet-Compatibility (FC) Loss. Table 2 summarizes the validation accuracy across various configurations, revealing that LOL consistently outperforms LXMERT in several instances, particularly in the context of the VQA-Supplement dataset, which incorporates additional data from MS-COCO.

The authors highlight that both models maintain a similar training dataset size and distribution, ensuring a fair comparison. The results indicate that the attention modules used in LOL achieve a near-perfect accuracy of 99.9% in understanding the types of questions and the logical connections involved.

In the section discussing compositional generalization and commutative properties, Table 3 shows validation accuracies for both models across single and multiple question formats. Overall, the findings suggest that LOL shows superior performance in handling complex question structures, underscoring the advantages of its training methodology over LXMERT. The paper also raises the question of whether simply parsing questions into components could suffice for understanding, acknowledging the complexity inherent in question composition.
