The paper discusses the challenges and advancements in Visual Question Answering (VQA), particularly focusing on the ability of models to handle logical transformations and negations in questions. It highlights previous research indicating that BERT-based models struggle to differentiate between negated sentences and their affirmative counterparts. The authors reference VQA-v2, a dataset designed to balance the types of questions asked, ensuring equal representation of "YES" and "NO" answers, which comprises 204,000 images and 1.1 million questions.

The paper emphasizes the effectiveness of cross-modal pre-trained models in various vision-and-language tasks, while also noting the limitations of neuro-symbolic approaches in dealing with natural images. It explores recent efforts to incorporate various forms of reasoning into VQA, including commonsense reasoning and spatial reasoning. 

The authors advocate for a thorough analysis of existing VQA models, particularly their robustness to logical transformations, as a crucial aspect of enhancing VQA systems. They introduce a "lens of logical composition" to investigate and identify shortcomings in current VQA models, exemplified by an experiment where a simple antonym substitution leads to incorrect answers. This analysis aims to improve the understanding and performance of VQA systems in handling complex question structures.
