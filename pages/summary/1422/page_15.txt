In this supplementary material, we expand on our investigation into visual question answering (VQA) with a focus on logical transformation. Our findings indicate that current state-of-the-art VQA models struggle with questions that involve logical operations such as negation, conjunction, and disjunction. To address this, we have developed two new datasets, VQA-Compose and VQA-Supplement, designed to facilitate training models to understand and apply logical operators in questions.

The dataset creation process is grounded in existing annotations from the VQA-v2 dataset and the MS-COCO dataset, which provides the images used in VQA-v2. We specifically filter out "yes-no" questions, which make up 38% of the VQA dataset, retaining only those with a single valid answer—these constitute 20% of the VQA data. These questions, deemed "closed questions," serve as the foundational elements in our data generation.

To create logically composed questions, we utilize pairs of closed questions related to the same image and apply Boolean operators: negation, conjunction, and disjunction. The negation process involves modifying the original question by inserting "no" or "not" at strategic points, while conjunction and disjunction are achieved by inserting "and" or "or" between two closed questions. This method ensures that the newly formed questions maintain clear, unambiguous answers, which can be effectively treated as Boolean variables. 

Overall, this approach not only enhances the complexity of the questions but also aims to improve the performance of VQA models when faced with logical reasoning tasks. Further sections will provide detailed analyses of the datasets, the training processes, model parameters, and insights derived from our experimental results.
