The paper discusses the training datasets used for developing models capable of answering logical questions with a focus on those containing a maximum of one connective. It outlines the distribution of various training datasets, including VQA-Other, VQA-Number, VQA-YesNo, Comp-Single, and Supp-Single, detailing the proportions of samples and total training samples for each configuration. 

To ensure a fair comparison, the authors limit the training dataset size to match the original VQA dataset, employing the same question-type proportions: 38% yes-no, 12% number, and 50% other questions. This approach enhances the diversity of yes-no questions by integrating those from VQA-Compose and VQA-Supplement.

The paper further examines the training of models exclusively on closed questions, comparing three scenarios: using only yes-no questions from VQA, combining these with questions from VQA-Compose, and including questions from both VQA-Compose and VQA-Supplement. This comparison aims to evaluate model performance across different types of yes-no questions.

Additionally, the effect of logically composed questions is analyzed by progressively adding these questions to the training data and observing the learning curves based on the number of logical samples added. The goal is to determine how many logically composed questions are necessary for the models to achieve robustness.

Finally, the concept of compositional generalization is addressed, where models are trained on questions with a single logical connective or no connective at all and tested on more complex questions featuring multiple connectives. This methodology aims to assess the models' ability to generalize from simpler to more complex logical structures.
