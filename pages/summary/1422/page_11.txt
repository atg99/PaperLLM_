The paper discusses the performance comparison of two models, LXMERT and LOL, in the context of Visual Question Answering (VQA) with a focus on logically composed questions. It presents learning curves that illustrate the effectiveness of each model when trained on different datasets, specifically highlighting the combination of VQA with composition and supplementary data.

An oracle parser, which utilizes ground truth component questions, serves as a benchmark, achieving an exact-match accuracy of 85%. However, it struggles with determining the number of operands, with only 72% accuracy overall. The paper notes that the trained parser shows better performance with simpler questions but falters with more complex compositions. In contrast, end-to-end models like LOL do not require parsing, allowing them to sidestep these issues, although they still must comprehend logical operations.

The results indicate that both the oracle and trained parsers used with LOL outperform those with LXMERT. Notably, the LOL model without any parser surpasses both LXMERT and LOL with the trained parser, demonstrating a significant advantage.

Additionally, the paper explores whether models trained solely on the VQA-v2 dataset can effectively address logically composed questions. It finds that LXMERT performs poorly on these questions when only trained on VQA-v2. However, when models are explicitly trained with logically composed questions alongside VQA-v2 data, their accuracy improves on the composition datasets, though there is a decline in performance on yes-no questions from VQA. The incorporation of attention modules in their models helps maintain VQA-v2 performance while enhancing results across all validation datasets.
