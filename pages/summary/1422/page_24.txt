The paper discusses the performance of a parser model on Visual Question Answering (VQA) tasks, specifically focusing on question types with more than two operands. The model was trained for 20 epochs with a batch size of 32 and a learning rate of 1e-5. Results indicate a decline in parser accuracy as the number of operands in questions increases, highlighting a significant limitation of parser-based approaches.

The analysis reveals two main observations based on the heat-map and tables presented. In Figure 4a, the hardest question categories identified are Q1_Q2 and Q1^:Q2, while the easiest are Q1^Q2 and :Q1_:Q2. This distinction is further clarified using DeMorgan's laws, which reformulate the logical expressions associated with these question types. Figure 4b reinforces these findings, noting that questions derived from factually valid statements (e.g., about objects in an image) consistently yield "Yes" answers, while those involving conjunctions that combine negated statements lead to "No" answers. The categories mentioned include Q_B, Q_C, :Q_B, :Q_C, and various combinations involving conjunctions and negations.
