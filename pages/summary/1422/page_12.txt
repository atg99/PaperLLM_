The paper investigates the performance of models trained with different types of questions in Visual Question Answering (VQA), specifically focusing on closed questions and logically composed questions. The analysis reveals that models trained solely on closed questions from VQA datasets achieve optimal accuracy on logically composed questions. The study further examines the impact of increasing the number of logical samples in the training data, demonstrating that models trained on combined datasets (VQA + Comp + Supp) maintain performance on validation data, while those trained only on VQA + Comp show a decline. 

Additionally, the models exhibit improved performance on Yes-No questions when trained with more logically composed samples, indicating effective transfer learning capabilities. The results illustrate that a significant number of logical questions are essential during training for models to effectively answer them during inference, particularly highlighting the superiority of the model trained on VQA + Comp + Supp.

The paper also addresses compositional generalization, where models trained on questions with a single connective performed better on questions with multiple connectives compared to the baseline. Lastly, the study assesses inductive generalization by testing models on questions with more than two components, showing that the end-to-end models outperform the baseline LXMERT, reaffirming their enhanced capability in handling complex question structures.
