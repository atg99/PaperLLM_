The paper discusses the development of a new model called Lens of Logic (LOL) designed for vision-and-language tasks, particularly focusing on answering logically composed questions. The model is built on insights derived from the nature of closed questions, the compatibility of predicted answers with question types, and the identification of logical connectives within questions. 

The LOL model incorporates two key modules: the Question Attention module (qATT) and the Logic Attention module (`ATT). The qATT module processes cross-modal embeddings to determine the type of question (Yes-No, Number, or Other) and outputs probabilities for each type. Meanwhile, the `ATT module identifies logical connectives (AND, OR, NOT, or no connective) in the question using a sigmoid function to accommodate multiple connectives. 

The model's architecture allows it to generate embeddings for questions and images, culminating in a cross-modal representation that informs the answer prediction process. The training of LOL employs a joint loss function that combines Answering Loss, Attention Losses, and additional components to enhance performance.

This approach positions LOL as a robust model for tasks such as visual question answering, with the potential to outperform existing models like MCAN and UpDn by focusing on the logical structure of questions and their corresponding answers.
