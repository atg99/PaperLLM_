The paper presents the VQA-LOL model architecture, which incorporates a cross-modal feature encoder along with Question-Attention (qATT) and Logic Attention (`ATT) modules. The concatenated output from these modules is utilized by the Answering Module to predict answers. 

A key aspect of the study involves analyzing the model's performance with an increasing number of logically composed questions to understand how many logical samples are necessary for the model to grasp logic in questions. An ablation study is conducted wherein the training data is limited to closed questions, allowing the model to focus on "Yes-No" VQA questions, VQA-Compose, and VQA-Supplement.

The paper explores compositional generalization, investigating whether training on closed questions with a single logical operation can extend to questions with multiple operations. Additionally, inductive generalization is examined to see if training on compositions of two questions can be generalized to compositions involving more than two questions. 

The methodology section discusses the LXMERT model, a state-of-the-art Visual Question Answering (VQA) framework that integrates visual feature extraction with language processing capabilities. It introduces the Lens of Logic (LOL) model and its attention modules designed to learn question types and logical connectives, alongside the Fréchet-Compatibility (FC) Loss. The focus is on the composition of two questions, which can be extended to multiple questions.
