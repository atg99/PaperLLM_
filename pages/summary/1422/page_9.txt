The paper presents a novel approach to visual question answering (VQA) that incorporates a Fréchet-Compatibility Loss to enhance the model's predictions for component and composed questions. The training process minimizes a multi-label classification loss using Binary Cross-Entropy for various labels, including answer, question type, and connective. The Fréchet-Compatibility Loss is designed to ensure that the predicted answers for individual questions align with the answer for the composed question, adhering to Fréchet inequalities that provide bounds for the probabilities of conjunction and disjunction of answers. 

The implementation details include using the LXMERT feature encoder, which outputs a 768-dimensional vector for the attention modules. The training process involves an initial phase without the Fréchet loss, followed by fine-tuning with the loss to improve accuracy. The models are optimized using the Adam optimizer with specific parameters and trained on multiple GPUs over a period of 24 hours for 20 epochs. The experiments aim to assess the model's logical robustness and transfer learning capabilities across three datasets, including VQA v2.0 and a combination of VQA and VQA-Compose datasets. 

Overall, the introduction of the Fréchet-Compatibility Loss is central to enhancing the model's performance by ensuring coherent predictions between related questions.
