The paper discusses various approaches to selecting training sets for support vector machines (SVM), highlighting their application in different domains including disease classification and network intrusion detection. 

In disease classification, the authors note the challenge posed by imbalanced datasets, where healthy examples significantly outnumber pathological cases. They reference the work of Oh et al. (2011), who utilized SVM training set selection methods on datasets related to conditions such as leukemia, diabetes, and Parkinson's disease, among others. These datasets varied in size, with the diabetes dataset containing up to 800 vectors and the leukemia dataset featuring nearly 7200 attributes.

For network intrusion detection, the work by Yu et al. (2003) is cited, focusing on building classifiers to differentiate between normal and malicious connections. They employed a dataset with 4 million vectors and 42 features, which was used as a benchmark in a significant data mining competition. A noteworthy point is that the test data exhibited a different probability distribution compared to the training data, containing attack types not represented in the training set.

Overall, the review emphasizes the importance of effective training set selection in improving the performance of SVMs across various applications, especially when dealing with large-scale and imbalanced datasets.
