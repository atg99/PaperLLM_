The paper reviews methods for selecting training sets for support vector machines (SVMs), emphasizing the importance of efficient data selection in enhancing SVM performance. It is structured into several sections: the introduction to SVM theory, the classification of training data selection methods, and a conclusion that looks towards future research.

Section 2 provides foundational knowledge on SVMs, focusing on linear SVMs that utilize a decision hyperplane to separate data in a D-dimensional space. The hyperplane is defined mathematically, and conditions for linear separability are laid out, emphasizing the maximization of the margin between classes. The equations presented detail how the hyperplane is positioned to ensure that the closest data points from each class are as far apart as possible, establishing a framework for the optimal margin.

In Section 3, the paper proposes a taxonomy for categorizing various data selection techniques, discussing their characteristics and effectiveness in reducing training set sizes. This section aims to identify both promising and less fruitful areas for future exploration in the context of SVM training.

The review concludes with a summary of findings and suggestions for future research directions, advocating for further investigation in the identified areas to improve SVM training set selection practices.
