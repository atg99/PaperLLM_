The paper discusses the challenges and advancements in training Support Vector Machines (SVMs), particularly when dealing with large and complex datasets common in various engineering domains. SVMs function by identifying a hyperplane to separate data from two classes, relying on a subset of training data known as support vectors. While SVMs can handle non-linear problems through kernel functions, they face significant limitations due to their high computational complexity—specifically, O(t^3) time and O(t^2) memory, where t represents the size of the training dataset.

The review highlights the need for improved methods in selecting SVM training sets and addresses broader issues such as hyperparameter selection and handling data of questionable quality. Proper model selection, which involves determining appropriate hyperparameters and kernel functions, is essential for optimizing SVM performance. The paper references various techniques for automatic model selection, including covariance matrix adaptation evolution strategies and hybrid genetic algorithms, which have shown promise in enhancing the efficiency of SVM training.

Overall, the review aims to consolidate existing knowledge on methods for selecting SVM training sets, marking it as the first comprehensive summary of this subject in the literature. The findings emphasize the importance of addressing these challenges to effectively utilize SVMs in emerging big data contexts.
