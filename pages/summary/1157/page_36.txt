This paper discusses various approaches to selecting training data for Support Vector Machines (SVMs) from large datasets, highlighting the challenges posed by the increasing volume of data in fields such as text classification and credit screening. It emphasizes the effectiveness of clustering-based techniques over random sampling in training set selection, as demonstrated by Yu et al. (2003). The significance of text classification is underscored, with references to studies by Schohn and Cohn (2000) and Tong and Koller (2002) that utilized the Reuters-21578 dataset to evaluate active-learning SVM methods.

The paper also addresses the application of SVMs in credit screening, citing work by Lyhyaoui et al. (1999) that achieved over 90% classification accuracy despite using a noisy dataset. The authors note that while the abundance of data presents opportunities for extracting valuable insights, it also poses challenges in terms of time and memory complexities associated with SVMs, particularly as dataset sizes increase.

In conclusion, the paper reviews advancements in SVM training data selection, categorizing methods based on their underlying approaches and characteristics. It advocates for a taxonomy that can aid in understanding the strengths and weaknesses of emerging techniques, focusing on the importance of selecting only significant training vectors to optimize the SVM's performance.
