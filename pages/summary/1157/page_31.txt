The paper reviews the selection of training sets for support vector machines (SVMs) and discusses the methodologies for evaluating algorithm performance. It highlights the common practice of using statistical tests such as the two-tailed Wilcoxon signed-rank test and the Friedman test to compare the effectiveness of different algorithms across multiple datasets. 

The effectiveness of training set selection algorithms is examined using three types of datasets: 

1. **Artificially Generated Datasets**: These datasets are created to follow known distributions, allowing researchers to analyze algorithm behavior under controlled conditions. They facilitate visualization and understanding of the positioning of vectors relative to the decision hyperplane.

2. **Benchmark Datasets**: These are widely utilized to compare the performance of various training set selection algorithms. The paper notes that benchmark datasets are referenced in over 70% of the reviewed literature and are available from various repositories, including the UCI machine learning repository, KEEL, and LibSVM. The paper provides a summary of the characteristics of the ten most frequently used benchmark sets.

3. **Practical Applications and Real-Life Datasets**: While the volume of available data is increasing, the paper points out that less than 45% of the reviewed studies focus on real-world datasets, indicating a gap in practical applications for SVMs.

In summary, the paper emphasizes the importance of rigorous testing and evaluation of training set selection algorithms in the context of SVMs, using a variety of datasets to ensure comprehensive performance analysis.
