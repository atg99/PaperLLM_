The paper discusses various methods for selecting training sets for support vector machines (SVM), focusing on techniques aimed at refining training data to enhance SVM performance. One highlighted technique is the Minimum Enclosing Ball (MEB) clustering, which creates a refined set of training vectors by including all vectors from mixed-class clusters and the centroids of one-class clusters. After initial SVM training, a de-clustering step is employed to recover vectors near the decision hyperplane for potential inclusion in the refined set.

Another method, SebSVM, involves selecting convex hull vectors in a higher-dimensional kernel space by solving the MEB problem independently for each class. This approach incorporates a safety region to prevent the removal of useful training vectors.

Wang and Shi's SR-DSA algorithm utilizes Ward-linkage clustering to form ellipsoidal clusters, where each vector starts as an individual cluster and clusters are merged based on a linkage criterion. The refined training set is created by removing interior vectors and those distant from other-class clusters, although this method requires careful parameter selection, such as the number of clusters.

Lastly, Li et al. proposed a technique that combines k-means clustering with edge detection to improve the training set selection process. Overall, the paper emphasizes the significance of clustering and refinement techniques in enhancing the effectiveness of SVMs through careful training set selection.
