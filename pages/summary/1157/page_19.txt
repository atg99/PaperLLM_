This paper reviews various methods for selecting training sets for support vector machines (SVMs), highlighting advancements aimed at improving classification accuracy and efficiency. One approach discussed involves enhancing SVM algorithms by removing overlapping patterns using Bayes posterior probabilities, where vectors are eliminated if they are more likely to belong to a different class. This pre-processing step, which includes the removal of duplicated patterns, results in a competitive performance compared to established techniques.

Cervantes et al. (2015) introduce a method that employs an induction tree to refine training sets, enabling SVMs to be trained on smaller, more relevant subsets. Their technique classifies datasets based on imbalance ratios and applies different sampling strategies accordingly to ensure that the training set maintains a representative distribution of classes.

He et al. (2011) propose a neighborhood-based rough set model, FARNeM, which categorizes vectors into positive, noisy, and boundary regions. This model also incorporates feature selection to identify the most relevant attributes, focusing on minimizing the number of features while preserving classification performance. The authors emphasize the importance of tuning specific thresholds to optimize the algorithm's effectiveness.

The paper indicates that evolutionary algorithms have shown promise in various pattern recognition tasks, suggesting potential avenues for further exploration in training set selection for SVMs. Overall, the review highlights the importance of addressing class imbalance and refining training sets to enhance the performance of SVM classifiers.
