The paper discusses methods for refining training sets in machine learning through the selection of T-vectors, focusing on edge detection and convex-concave hull analysis. It highlights the use of image processing techniques to identify significant changes in pixel brightness and color, which may indicate edges. The approach by Li et al. (2009) analyzes neighboring vectors to determine their classification and retain those that are distinct, while rejecting others.

Chau et al. (2013) introduce the convex-concave hull analysis algorithm (CCHSVM) to address the issue of overlapping convex hulls in linearly non-separable cases. Their method involves generating convex hulls for each class independently and utilizing concave hulls to extract vectors closest to the exterior boundary of the convex hulls. The selection process is influenced by the choice of k-nearest points, which can affect the final shape of the hull.

The paper also mentions the application of convex hull analysis in various algorithms, including those for artificial neural networks and online classifiers. These techniques dynamically update support vector machines (SVMs) with new incoming vectors, although they note challenges with noisy datasets and the need for de-noising methods.

Finally, Shen et al. (2016) propose a redundant data reduction algorithm that removes unnecessary training vectors by analyzing cluster boundaries and inter-cluster relationships using k-means clustering. Overall, the methods discussed emphasize the importance of refining training sets to improve classification performance while addressing challenges such as noise and data distribution.
