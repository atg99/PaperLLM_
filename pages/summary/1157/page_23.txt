The paper reviews various methods for selecting training sets for support vector machines (SVMs), focusing on the challenges posed by large and noisy datasets. One approach discussed is random sampling, where a subset of the training set is drawn based on assigned weights, allowing for the inclusion of misclassified vectors in subsequent rounds. This iterative method aims to refine the training set by emphasizing important vectors, particularly support vectors (SVs). However, determining the optimal size of the refined set can be time-consuming and may lead to the omission of valuable relationships within the dataset.

The paper categorizes SVM training set reduction methods based on optimization strategies, highlighting key characteristics such as the type of approach (one-pass or iterative), source of information used in the optimization process, whether the method is randomized or deterministic, and its dependence on the training set size. Additionally, it notes the types of datasets used to validate these methods and provides insights into their scalability.

Overall, the review emphasizes the importance of selecting appropriate training sets to enhance SVM performance, especially in the context of large datasets, while also acknowledging the limitations of current methods.
