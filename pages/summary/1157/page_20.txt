The paper discusses various evolutionary algorithms (EAs) designed to enhance the selection of refined training sets for Support Vector Machines (SVMs). It begins by referencing Nishida and Kurita's hybrid algorithm (RANSAC-SVM), which combines random sampling and evolutionary techniques to identify optimal training sets. This approach involves generating small, random refined sets and using SVM classification scores to determine the best set through a consensus mechanism.

Building on this, Kawulok and Nalepa introduced the genetic algorithm for SVM (GASVM), which evolves a population of chromosomes representing refined training sets through standard genetic operations. The fitness of these individuals is determined by their classification accuracy. However, a challenge arose regarding the selection of individual sizes, which led to the development of an adaptive genetic algorithm (AGA) that dynamically adjusts parameters based on evolutionary progress.

The dynamically adaptive genetic algorithm (DAGA) further refined this concept by allowing for real-time updates to adaptation schemes based on the characteristics of the best individuals. Additionally, memetic algorithms (MAs) were introduced, combining EAs with refinement techniques to leverage knowledge gained during evolution. Nalepa and Kawulok's MASVM utilized a pool of support vectors (SVs) to create super individuals comprised solely of SVs, enhancing the solution process.

The adaptive MA (PCA2MA) introduced a preprocessing step to analyze the geometry of the training data, identifying valuable vectors to create a higher-quality initial population. This method also included mechanisms to maintain diversity during evolution. Notably, PCA2MA employed a parameter-less adaptation scheme, eliminating the need for prior parameter tuning.

Overall, the paper illustrates the evolution of algorithms for selecting refined SVM training sets, emphasizing the effectiveness of evolutionary techniques in improving classification accuracy across various datasets.
