The paper by J. Nalepa and M. Kawulok discusses the challenges and advancements in training Support Vector Machines (SVMs) using large datasets, particularly when dealing with noisy or weakly labeled data. It highlights various strategies for data cleansing, including embedded data cleansing, filtering algorithms, and evolutionary techniques that aim to identify and remove noise. The authors emphasize the importance of handling poor quality data, which includes detecting ambiguities, duplicates, and outliers.

The motivation behind this research stems from the increasing scale of datasets, which complicates the training process for SVMs. The paper reviews existing training set selection techniques tailored for SVMs, noting their strengths and weaknesses. The authors aim to provide a comprehensive understanding of these methods to improve the selection process for real-life applications and to inspire the development of new algorithms. They propose that a taxonomy of current techniques can aid in recognizing potential pitfalls and facilitate the combination of methods to enhance the training of SVMs on large datasets.
