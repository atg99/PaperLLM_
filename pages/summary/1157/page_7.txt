The paper discusses the selection of training sets for support vector machines (SVMs) and presents the mathematical foundation for both separable and non-separable cases. In SVMs, support vectors (SVs) are critical as they influence the position of the hyperplane. The decision function is defined using Lagrange multipliers, where only the SVs with non-zero multipliers contribute to the hyperplane's position.

For non-separable cases, the constraints are relaxed by introducing slack variables (ξi) to account for classification errors. The modified objective function aims to minimize the norm of the weight vector while considering the cost of misclassifications, with a parameter C that balances the trade-off between maximizing the margin and minimizing classification errors. This leads to the formulation of soft-margin SVMs.

The dual form of the problem is derived and maximized under specific constraints, allowing for the calculation of the weight vector w based on the support vectors. The paper concludes by presenting the Lagrangian in its primal form, incorporating the KKT conditions that apply to both separable and non-separable cases, thus providing a comprehensive understanding of SVM training set selection.
