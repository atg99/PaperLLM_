The paper by J. Nalepa and M. Kawulok discusses various sources of information used to generate refined training sets for Support Vector Machines (SVM). The authors categorize these sources into five distinct types: 

1. **Local** - This method analyzes the local neighborhood of a training vector to determine its importance, classifying it as either significant or irrelevant for inclusion in the refined set.
2. **Global** - This approach examines the overall characteristics of the training set to identify critical vectors for inclusion.
3. **Wrapper** - In this technique, the SVM is trained using the refined set to evaluate its quality based on classification performance, treating the classifier as a "black box."
4. **SVM** - Similar to wrapper techniques, this method assesses the importance of training vectors based on features of the trained SVM, particularly focusing on the vectors identified as support vectors during training.
5. **Theory** - This source relies on theoretical principles related to SVM classifiers or desired characteristics of training data to evaluate the significance of training vectors without prior training.

The authors highlight the challenges in assessing SVM training set selection algorithms, noting that evaluations can be both quantitative and qualitative. They discuss various quantitative measures commonly used in the literature to gauge the effectiveness of these selection techniques, emphasizing the importance of maximizing classification performance metrics based on true positives, true negatives, false positives, and false negatives. The paper concludes by presenting practical applications where different algorithms for selecting refined SVM training sets have been implemented.
