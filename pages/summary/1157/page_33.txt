The paper reviews various applications of support vector machines (SVMs) in the context of training set selection, particularly focusing on different domains such as hand-written digit classification, skin detection, hand pose estimation, face detection, and deceptive facial expression analysis.

In hand-written digits classification, the authors referenced previous works that utilized the MNIST dataset, consisting of 600,000 images across 10 classes. This application is significant for tasks like bank check processing and biometric authentication.

For skin detection and segmentation, the challenge lies in identifying human skin pixels in color images, which is essential for applications such as content filtering and human-computer interaction. The Skin dataset created by Kawulok and Nalepa includes over 4 million pixels, enabling testing of various SVM training set selection algorithms.

In hand pose estimation, the authors described a method using shape context descriptors to classify hand shapes for gesture recognition. Due to the potential size of training sets resulting from multiple gesture combinations, a genetic technique was employed to refine the selection process.

Face detection was explored by Kawulok and Wang, who tested their SVM algorithms on datasets with 1,000 to 3,500 images. This task is crucial for applications in surveillance and human-computer interaction.

Lastly, the paper discusses the detection of deceptive facial expressions, where Kawulok et al. utilized SVMs to differentiate between posed and spontaneous smiles using features derived from facial dynamics. They addressed the challenge of large training sets containing irrelevant data by implementing a memetic training set selection algorithm.

Overall, the review highlights the importance of effective training set selection in enhancing SVM performance across diverse applications.
