The paper provides a comprehensive review of various state-of-the-art methods for reducing the cardinality of training sets used in support vector machines (SVMs). It highlights a range of algorithms categorized under data geometry analysis and clustering-based methods, detailing their characteristics, including the year of publication, references, type, source, and some specific parameters.

Key methods discussed include:

1. **Clustering and 1-nearest neighbor cluster analysis (1999)**: A one-pass global method that does not rely on random sampling.
2. **k-means cluster analysis (2000)**: Another one-pass global approach utilizing random sampling.
3. **Hierarchical micro-clustering (2003)**: An iterative global method that utilizes SVM without random sampling.
4. **Minimum enclosing ball clustering (2008)**: A one-pass method that incorporates both global and SVM features.

The review also addresses more recent advancements, such as the analysis of cluster boundaries and independencies (2016), which employs an iterative global approach grounded in theoretical concepts.

Overall, the paper systematically categorizes and summarizes these methods, providing insights into their operational frameworks and applications in enhancing SVM training efficiency.
