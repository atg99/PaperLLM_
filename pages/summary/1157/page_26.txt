The paper by Nalepa and Kawulok discusses the optimization of training set selection for Support Vector Machines (SVMs) with a focus on minimizing execution times for training set selection, SVM training, and classification. The authors argue that these times are closely linked to the size of the refined training set and the number of support vectors (SVs). 

They propose a combined quality measure that addresses the dual objectives of maximizing classification accuracy and minimizing the number of SVs, particularly in scenarios where rapid SVM performance is crucial, such as real-time systems. This is represented by a quality function Q(AUC, s) that incorporates the area under the curve (AUC) and the number of SVs, allowing for a balanced assessment of performance based on user-defined importance.

The experimental setup for evaluating new training set selection algorithms involves comparisons with existing techniques and SVMs trained on complete datasets. The methodology includes multiple executions of randomized approaches for averaging results, as well as k-fold cross-validation to assess generalization capabilities. Two main groups of experiments are outlined: sensitivity analysis to assess the impact of algorithm components on performance, and comparative analysis against state-of-the-art methods and SVMs trained on the entire dataset. The authors emphasize the importance of statistical testing to validate the significance of their findings due to the extensive number of algorithms and datasets involved.
