The paper discusses the application of Support Vector Machine (SVM) active learning training set selection algorithms in various fields, particularly focusing on image retrieval and biomedical applications. Tong and Chang demonstrated that their SVM-based method effectively selects the most informative images for user queries, outperforming other contemporary image retrieval techniques using real-life datasets. This approach is relevant in industries such as textiles, nudity detection, art archives, and medical diagnostics.

In the biomedical domain, the paper highlights the challenges of data quality and volume, emphasizing the importance of selecting appropriate training sets. It notes the successful application of SVMs in RNA classification, specifically in identifying non-coding RNAs in large datasets. Cervantes et al. utilized a clustering-based training set selection algorithm for RNA datasets, demonstrating competitive performance despite the large sizes of the datasets involved. This underscores the significance of effective training set selection in enhancing SVM performance in complex biomedical applications.
