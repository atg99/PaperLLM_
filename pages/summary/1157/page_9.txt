The paper reviews methods for selecting training sets for support vector machines (SVMs), particularly in the context of large datasets. It highlights the challenges associated with SVM training, including the infeasibility of processing massive datasets and the memory burden introduced by existing acceleration techniques such as decomposition, parallel, and approximation methods. The classification time of SVMs is directly related to the number of support vectors (SVs), which are influenced by the size of the training set. To improve efficiency, it is crucial to minimize the number of SVs.

The paper categorizes algorithms for managing training sets into two main types: those that speed up SVM training and those that reduce the training set size by selecting candidate vectors likely to become SVs. The focus is on the latter, which aims to form significantly smaller training sets from larger datasets. Various techniques for reducing the cardinality of training sets are classified based on their optimization strategies, including data geometry analysis, neighborhood analysis, evolutionary methods, active learning, random sampling, and clustering-based methods. Each category is explored in detail in subsequent sections of the paper.
