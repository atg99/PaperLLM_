The paper reviews various methods for selecting training sets for support vector machines (SVMs), emphasizing both clustering and non-clustering approaches. It discusses a technique where unnecessary training vectors are removed from one-class clusters using distance density calculations. This involves determining which vectors are dense near the cluster centroid and which are sparse, with the sparse vectors being retained for further analysis. Fisher’s discriminant analysis is employed to delineate dense from sparse vectors.

Another technique highlighted involves the removal of redundant clusters through k-means clustering, which categorizes initial clusters into one-class and heterogeneous clusters. The heterogeneous clusters are further sub-clustered to identify and eliminate redundant one-class clusters, thereby streamlining the training set.

The paper also notes the computational challenges of clustering methods, suggesting the use of parallel architectures to expedite the training set selection process. It emphasizes the importance of selecting proper parameters to refine training sets and acknowledges that analyzing the entire training set may still be necessary for extracting valuable information.

In addition to clustering methods, non-clustering approaches are discussed, such as the method proposed by Abe and Inoue, which utilizes geometrical information based on Mahalanobis distance to identify vectors near the SVM decision boundary. This method is particularly effective for polynomial kernel functions, as it calculates the relative difference of distances for vectors to assess their proximity to the decision boundary. 

The review encapsulates key methods for optimizing training set selection in SVMs, addressing both theoretical and practical implications of these techniques.
