The paper discusses methods for selecting training sets for support vector machines (SVMs), focusing on performance metrics and optimization strategies. It highlights the importance of true positive rate (TPR) and false positive rate (FPR) in evaluating SVM performance, often visualized through receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves. The area under the ROC curve (AUC) is emphasized as a key performance indicator, where higher values indicate better classification performance.

The paper outlines several objectives for training set selection algorithms, including minimizing the size of the refined training set while maintaining classification performance. The reduction rate (R) is defined to measure the efficiency of training set selection, aiming to maximize this rate. Additionally, the paper discusses the need to minimize the number of support vectors (SVs) to enhance the speed of SVM operations and the importance of maximizing the percentage of selected vectors that become SVs to ensure the inclusion of significant data points. However, it warns against overfitting, as selecting too many vectors as SVs may indicate poor generalization. Overall, the paper presents a comprehensive review of performance measures and strategies for optimizing training sets in SVM applications.
