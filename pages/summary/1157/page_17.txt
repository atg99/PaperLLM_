The paper discusses various methods for selecting training sets for support vector machines (SVMs), emphasizing the importance of refining training sets to improve classification performance. One key approach is the neighborhood analysis method, which utilizes the statistical properties of training set vectors. Shin and Cho (2002) introduced a k-nearest neighbors (k-NN) based pattern selection algorithm that identifies correctly labeled patterns near the SVM decision hyperplane by analyzing their proximity and correctness. They defined proximity using the entropy of neighboring vectors, allowing for the selection of vectors with positive proximity that are close to the decision boundary. 

To enhance efficiency, Shin and Cho (2003) improved this method by reducing the search space based on the observation that neighbors of vectors near the hyperplane are closely located. The choice of the k parameter is crucial for optimal performance. 

Guo et al. (2010) presented an ensemble classifier approach for training set selection, using a modified margin definition to assess the classification certainty of vectors. The margin indicates whether a vector is correctly classified, with high positive margins suggesting central positioning within its class and negative margins indicating potential outliers. This innovative approach aims to refine the training set by omitting information about the correct class of samples, thereby focusing on the distribution of votes from base classifiers.

Overall, these methods contribute to the ongoing development of effective training set selection strategies for SVMs, highlighting the balance between computational efficiency and the quality of the training data.
