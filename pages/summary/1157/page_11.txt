The paper discusses various methodologies for selecting training sets for support vector machines (SVMs), focusing on the importance of critical centroids in the classification process. Critical centroids, identified through the 1-nearest neighbor algorithm, are those likely to be near the decision hyperplane. The authors propose four sample selection mechanisms to refine the training set, based on: (i) vector dispersion analysis, (ii) neighborhood analysis of the nearest opposite-class vector, (iii) a combination of the first two methods, and (iv) relationships between vectors and centroids. Although these methods do not significantly impact classification scores, the small size of the two-class training set used in the experiments may influence this finding.

The paper also highlights the SVM-KM algorithm introduced by Barros de Almeida et al., which employs k-means clustering for training set refinement. In SVM-KM, k clusters are formed from the entire training set, with one-class clusters discarded and centroids retained, while heterogeneous clusters contribute all their vectors to the refined set. The effectiveness of SVM-KM is contingent on data distribution, performing well with dense datasets, but potentially struggling with sparse ones. Additionally, careful selection of k is crucial to avoid algorithm performance issues.

Furthermore, the clustering-based SVMs (CB-SVMs) approach is presented, utilizing hierarchical micro-clustering to identify valuable vectors. CB-SVM constructs a clustering feature (CF) tree, which captures main data distribution patterns without allowing backtracking. Each CF is characterized by three components: the number of vectors in the cluster, and the linear and square sums of the vectors. The CF tree structure, resembling B+-trees, enables the handling of outliers and noisy vectors by treating leaf entries with significantly fewer vectors as outliers. In CB-SVM, separate CF trees are created for each class, and SVMs are trained using centroids from the root entries.
