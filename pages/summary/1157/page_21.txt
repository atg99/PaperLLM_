The paper reviews various methods for selecting training sets in support vector machines (SVMs), focusing on evolutionary algorithms (EAs) and their effectiveness in improving SVM performance. It highlights the PCA2MA approach, which outperforms other techniques by refining training sets to achieve high-performing SVMs with fewer support vectors. The authors discuss the multi-objective evolutionary technique by Fernandes et al., which creates balanced training sets from imbalanced datasets, leading to diverse and effective classifier ensembles. 

Pighetti et al. enhance genetic evolution with locality-sensitive hashing for multi-class classification, though they face challenges in determining optimal stopping points during optimization. Verbiest et al. investigate various evolutionary techniques, including standard and adaptive genetic algorithms, emphasizing the importance of fitness criteria that consider both classification accuracy and input reduction. 

Kawulok and Nalepa introduce a memetic approach that evolves training vectors and labels, effectively addressing weakly-labeled datasets, although it struggles with correctly labeled data. Lastly, Kawulok et al. propose an alternating genetic algorithm (ALGA) for optimizing SVM models alongside training sets, noting the influence of different kernel functions and hyper-parameters. Overall, the review underscores the potential of evolutionary techniques in enhancing SVM training set selection and classification accuracy.
