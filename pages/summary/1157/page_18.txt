The paper discusses various methods for refining training sets in machine learning, particularly focusing on support vector machines (SVMs) and ensemble classifiers. The authors introduce a margin-based measure, φ/prime M(a), which evaluates the closeness of a vector to the hyperplane, with smaller values indicating proximity. This measure helps in selecting support vector (SV) candidates from a set of training vectors (T) by sorting them based on their margin values.

Wang et al. proposed a neighborhood analysis method that identifies the largest sphere containing only same-class vectors for each training vector. The number of vectors within this sphere is assessed, and vectors with the lowest counts are selected for the refined training set, as they are less likely to be SVs.

Guo and Boukir extended ensemble margin-based algorithms, highlighting limitations of classic bagging trees with large datasets. They introduced more robust ensemble methods like random forests and a small votes instance selection (SVIS) to improve classifier effectiveness.

Li proposed a technique for one-class SVMs that can be adapted for two-class scenarios, focusing on creating a tight surface around class clusters. This surface is characterized by extreme points, and an approximation algorithm is used to find the normal vector of the tangent plane at these points, ensuring that extreme vectors are retained in the refined set.

Li and Maguire combined various techniques to select critical patterns, constructing surfaces passing through extreme points and containing one-class vectors, which allows for effective hyperplane placement within the dataset.

Overall, the paper presents a range of strategies for enhancing training set selection, emphasizing the importance of margin and neighborhood analyses in improving classifier performance, particularly in challenging data scenarios.
