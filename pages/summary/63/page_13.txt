This paper presents a novel hybrid model combining Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) and Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) to predict soybean yields across the Corn Belt in the United States. The model effectively captures both temporal dependencies in weather data and spatial dependencies in soil data, while also accounting for long-term yield trends due to advancements in breeding and management practices. The proposed methodology significantly outperformed traditional methods like LASSO and random forests.

A key innovation of this study is its ability to explain yield predictions by identifying the variable importance over time, thus addressing the common black box nature of deep learning models. Feature selection was conducted using backpropagation to evaluate the individual impact of weather, soil, and management factors, along with the timing of their significance.

The model's performance was sensitive to various environmental and management variables, and it successfully predicted yields in previously untested environments, indicating its potential for future applications in yield prediction. The research also suggests that similar methodologies could be adapted for classifying hybrid crops based on yield performance.

Data used in the study is publicly available, and the source code for the CNN-RNN model can be accessed on GitHub. The study was partially funded by the National Science Foundation under various programs.
