The paper discusses the development and application of a hybrid deep learning model for crop yield prediction, integrating remotely sensed images and various datasets. Kim et al. (2019) created a neural network model leveraging satellite and meteorological data for crop yield forecasting. Similarly, Wang et al. (2018) utilized deep learning for soybean yield predictions in Argentina and Brazil, achieving notable success with transfer learning. Yang et al. (2019) demonstrated the effectiveness of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN) in estimating rice yields, while Khaki and Khalilzadeh (2019) focused on predicting corn yield loss across North America using deep CNNs.

The paper is structured into several sections: Data, Methodology, Design of Experiments, Results, Analysis, and Conclusion. The Data section outlines the four datasets utilized: yield performance, management, weather, and soil, detailing their sources and characteristics. The yield performance dataset covers corn and soybean yields from 1980 to 2018 across 13 states in the Corn Belt. Management data tracks weekly planting percentages, while weather data encompasses daily records of six variables, and soil data includes various soil properties measured at multiple depths.

In the Methodology section, the authors propose a hybrid model combining CNNs, fully connected layers (FC), and Recurrent Neural Networks (RNN). The W-CNN and S-CNN components are designed to capture linear and nonlinear effects of weather and soil data, respectively. The FC layer integrates features from the CNN models, while the RNN captures the temporal dependencies of crop yields over time.

Overall, this research highlights the integration of advanced deep learning techniques and diverse datasets to enhance the accuracy of crop yield predictions, addressing the challenges faced in agricultural forecasting.
