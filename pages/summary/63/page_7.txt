In this analysis, the crop yield predictions for corn and soybean were based on input variables such as weather components, soil conditions, and management practices. To determine the relative importance of these factors, feature selection was conducted using a trained CNN-RNN model. The guided backpropagation method was utilized to identify input variables that maximize the activation of targeted neurons. This involved computing the average activation of neurons in the RNN output and backpropagating gradients to the input space, allowing for the ranking of input variables based on gradient magnitude.

The results highlighted the significance of various factors, with solar radiation being the most sensitive for corn yield, while snow was the least sensitive. This finding aligns with agronomic principles, as radiation is crucial for photosynthesis and biomass production. The analysis also indicated that snow-water impacts soil water balance but is less significant than precipitation and vapor deficit due to the latter's longer-lasting effects on crop yield, particularly in summer.

Key temporal periods for yield prediction were identified, with solar radiation showing critical sensitivity around week 15 (pre-planting) and week 30 (during the critical silking stage). This aligns with literature demonstrating a strong relationship between kernel number per plant and plant growth rate, driven by photosynthesis. Overall, the analysis provides valuable insights into the complex agricultural system and the temporal dynamics of yield prediction factors.
