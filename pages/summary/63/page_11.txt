The paper presents the CNN-RNN model for predicting crop yield, specifically focusing on corn and soybean, using various data inputs. It compares four models: CNN-RNN(W), CNN-RNN(S), CNN-RNN(M), and an Average model. The CNN-RNN(W) and CNN-RNN(S) models incorporate weather and soil data, demonstrating significantly higher prediction accuracies than CNN-RNN(M), which relies solely on management data. The findings indicate that weather and soil factors are crucial for yield prediction, influencing crop yield more than management practices, with planting dates having a greater impact on soybean than corn.

To assess the generalization capabilities of the CNN-RNN model, the researchers conducted experiments by excluding certain locations from the training dataset and predicting yields for these excluded locations in 2018. The model's performance was evaluated using k-fold cross-validation, revealing strong predictive accuracy across different folds. 

Performance metrics, including RMSE and correlation coefficients, were provided for each model, highlighting the CNN-RNN(W) and CNN-RNN(S) models as the most effective for both crops, while the Average model showed the least predictive power. The study concludes that incorporating comprehensive weather and soil data significantly enhances yield prediction accuracy compared to management data alone.
