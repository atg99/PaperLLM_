The study evaluates a hybrid CNN-RNN model for predicting corn and soybean yields, demonstrating its superior performance compared to LASSO, RF, and DFNN models. The LASSO model's linearity limited its effectiveness in capturing the nonlinear relationships between soil conditions and weather, while DFNN improved upon this by addressing these nonlinear effects. Although DFNN outperformed RF in most metrics, RF excelled in specific validation years for corn yield.

The CNN-RNN model consistently outperformed all other models across various metrics and years, achieving RMSE values of approximately 9% for corn and 8% for soybean yields. Key reasons for the CNN-RNN's success include its ability to capture yearly time dependencies through RNN, internal weather data dependencies via W-CNN, spatial soil data dependencies through S-CNN, and its consideration of nonlinear environmental effects.

The model also maintained consistent performance across validation years, with low prediction errors for most counties, as illustrated in the prediction error maps. Additionally, the model preserved some distributional properties of the ground truth yields, affirming its robustness.

The results are detailed in tables and figures, showcasing the performance of the CNN-RNN in comparison to other models for soybean yield predictions across the years 2016 to 2018, along with summary statistics for the validation data. Overall, the findings highlight the effectiveness of the CNN-RNN model in agricultural yield predictions.
