This paper introduces a deep learning framework that combines convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and recurrent neural networks (RNNs) for predicting crop yields, specifically corn and soybean, based on environmental data and management practices. The CNN-RNN model was tested against other methods like random forest, deep fully connected neural networks, and LASSO, using historical data from 2016 to 2018 across the Corn Belt in the United States. The CNN-RNN model outperformed these methods, achieving a root-mean-square-error (RMSE) of 9% and 8% of the respective average yields.

Key features of the CNN-RNN model include its ability to capture the temporal dependencies of environmental factors and the genetic improvements of crops without needing genotype information. It also demonstrated generalization capabilities to untested environments without significant loss in prediction accuracy. Furthermore, the model, through backpropagation, can elucidate how weather conditions, the accuracy of weather predictions, soil conditions, and management practices contribute to variations in crop yields.

The paper emphasizes the importance of accurate crop yield predictions for informed decision-making in agriculture, contributing to global food production and management strategies. The complexities involved in yield prediction, particularly the interactions between genotype and environmental factors, are highlighted as significant challenges in the field.
