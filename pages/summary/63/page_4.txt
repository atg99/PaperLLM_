The study investigates the increasing trends in corn and soybean yields over the past four decades, attributing this growth to advancements in genetics and management practices. However, the lack of publicly available genotype data necessitated a model that could indirectly reflect these effects. To address this, the researchers developed a specialized Recurrent Neural Network (RNN) model enhanced with Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) cells to capture the temporal dynamics of crop yield due to genetic improvements.

The RNN model utilized historical yield data, management information, and features extracted from weather and soil data to predict future crop yields. The input data included average yields from previous years, allowing predictions even in the absence of complete weather or soil data. The study detailed the experimental design, including hyperparameters for the CNN-RNN model, which included two convolutional neural networks (W-CNN and S-CNN) followed by fully connected layers. The RNN layer had a time length of five years, with LSTM cells comprising 64 hidden units.

The model was trained using stochastic gradient descent with a mini-batch size of 25 and employed the Adam optimizer. The training process lasted about an hour on a CPU. For comparative analysis, the researchers also implemented three other prediction models: Random Forest, Deep Fully Connected Neural Network, and Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator. The Random Forest model, optimized with 50 trees, demonstrated the best balance between accuracy and training time.

Overall, the proposed CNN-RNN framework represents a novel approach to yield prediction, leveraging historical data and advanced neural network architectures to model the complex relationships influencing agricultural productivity.
