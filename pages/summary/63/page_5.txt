The study investigates the effectiveness of various modeling techniques for predicting corn and soybean yields, focusing on the maximum depth of decision trees, deep fully connected neural networks (DFNN), and LASSO regression. The analysis reveals that a maximum tree depth of 10 achieves optimal prediction accuracy, while deeper trees lead to overfitting and shallower ones result in lower accuracy. The DFNN model, comprising nine layers with 50 neurons each, employs advanced deep learning strategies such as batch normalization and residual learning, which enhance prediction accuracy. LASSO serves as a benchmark to assess the linear and nonlinear impacts of soil and weather data, with the most effective L1 term coefficients identified between 0.3 and 0.5.

The models were tested on corn and soybean yield predictions for the years 2016, 2017, and 2018, utilizing training data from 1980 to the year prior to each validation year. Performance comparisons, presented in tables, highlight the root mean square error (RMSE) and correlation coefficients across different models. The results indicate that the CNN-RNN model and DFNN consistently outperform the random forest (RF) and LASSO models in both training and validation datasets, with notable improvements in predictions across the validation years. The average corn yield statistics for the validation years are also provided, underscoring the models' effectiveness in yield forecasting.
