The paper discusses the complexities involved in predicting crop yields due to the interdependence of environmental factors and their nonlinear effects. It highlights the application of various machine learning techniques, such as regression trees, random forests, and artificial neural networks, to predict yields for different crops. Studies have shown that models like random forest and artificial neural networks outperform traditional methods like multiple linear regression in yield prediction.

The paper proposes a deep learning framework that enhances yield prediction by utilizing advanced modeling techniques and data-driven feature learning. Deep learning methods, particularly those with multiple hidden layers, show improved performance but pose challenges in training due to high dimensional loss functions and potential vanishing gradient issues. To address these challenges, techniques such as residual connections, batch normalization, and dropout are employed.

The proposed hybrid model combines convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and recurrent neural networks (RNNs). CNNs are effective for processing various data formats, while RNNs excel in tasks involving sequential data, though they face training difficulties. Long short-term memory (LSTM) cells are introduced to enhance RNN performance by mitigating the vanishing gradient problem.

Recent applications of deep learning in crop yield prediction are noted, with examples demonstrating that these methods, including CNNs and RNNs, can surpass traditional models in accuracy. The findings suggest a promising direction for utilizing deep learning in agricultural yield forecasting.
