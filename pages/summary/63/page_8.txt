The paper investigates the impact of temperature, precipitation, and planting dates on corn and soybean yields through a CNN-RNN model. It highlights that temperature significantly influences seed germination and emergence, with high temperatures promoting faster and more uniform emergence. The model identifies that minimum temperatures are particularly critical around weeks 22 and 35, directly affecting corn yields during the grain fill period. This aligns with previous studies which emphasize the importance of temperature during these growth phases.

Furthermore, the analysis shows an increasing significance of precipitation during the grain fill period, corroborating findings from experimental studies. Soil variables also play a crucial role in yield predictions, impacting water and nitrogen supply to crops.

Regarding planting dates, the model indicates that corn yields are least sensitive around the optimal planting window of April 20 to May 15. In contrast, soybeans show higher sensitivity during late May, reflecting their respective optimal planting timelines.

The study also evaluates the effectiveness of a feature selection method by training the CNN-RNN model on historical data and testing it on subsequent yield predictions. The results demonstrate that the model's predictive accuracy remains largely unchanged when using a subset of the most important features, validating the feature selection approach.

Overall, the findings underscore the complex interplay between environmental conditions and management practices in influencing crop yields, providing insights for agronomic strategies.
