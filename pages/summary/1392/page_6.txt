The paper presents an ablation study of CFNet on the Pascal Context dataset, focusing on the impact of different modules and configurations on segmentation performance. Key findings include that the Aggregated Co-occurrence Feature (ACF) Module significantly enhances results, while the inclusion of global pooling features and the Context Encoding Module further improves performance. The experiments reveal optimal settings for multi-heads and mixtures, with H=4 and K=2 yielding the best results.

Technical implementation details are provided, including the use of ResNet as the backbone and various training strategies, such as dilation and bilinear interpolation for upsampling. The study employs a standard SGD optimizer and implements a specific learning rate schedule for different datasets. ACF is shown to be compatible with existing Fully Convolutional Network (FCN) approaches, and additional losses are integrated to enhance training.

The evaluation methodology involves multi-scale predictions to improve accuracy, using metrics of pixel accuracy and mean intersection over union (mIoU). The results indicate that CFNet achieves competitive performance on the Pascal Context dataset, with mIoU scores surpassing many baseline models. Overall, the findings underscore the effectiveness of the proposed CFNet architecture and its components in semantic segmentation tasks.
