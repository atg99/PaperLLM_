The paper discusses the challenges of accurately segmenting objects in images, particularly in complex scenes where fine-grained details are crucial for distinguishing similar categories. It highlights examples where traditional methods, such as Fully Convolutional Networks (FCN), struggle to correctly identify objects due to their reliance on global semantic context rather than detailed co-occurring features. The proposed CFNet addresses these shortcomings by focusing on the relationships among co-occurrent features before classifying each pixel, leading to improved segmentation results. The authors argue that predicting co-occurrent features is a high-rank problem due to the diverse nature of real-world images, which cannot be effectively captured by simpler models. To overcome these limitations, they introduce a contextual prior that enhances scene understanding. The effectiveness of CFNet is demonstrated through visual examples from the ADE20K dataset.
