This paper discusses the importance of co-occurrence in semantic segmentation, emphasizing that it extends beyond mere objects to include various parts of an object. For instance, distinguishing between an armchair and a standard chair requires recognizing the armrests. Co-occurring features significantly aid in identifying image pixel class labels, necessitating an approach that effectively captures these features and their dependencies.

Existing methods struggle to account for co-occurrence due to their fixed spatial structures, leading to limitations in capturing relationships among features, particularly over larger distances. While some recent approaches attempt to enhance receptive fields through multi-scale feature representations, they often apply uniform operations across the feature map, failing to adapt to the diverse distribution of critical features across different image regions.

The paper introduces the Co-occurrent Feature Model (CFM), which models feature co-occurrence as a probability distribution based on a target feature. This model learns inherent co-occurrence representations by measuring similarities between features, using a Softmax function that maintains spatial invariance. However, it recognizes a limitation in expressing contextual information solely through Softmax distributions. To address this, the authors propose a contextual prior as a conditional probability based on scene context, leading to a mixture of Softmax distributions with the contextual prior.

The second major contribution is the development of the Co-occurrent Feature Network (CFNet), a cutting-edge architecture for semantic segmentation, built on the proposed Aggregated Co-occurrent Feature Module (ACF). CFNet, utilizing a pre-trained ResNet base network, achieves state-of-the-art performance on several benchmarks, including 54.0% mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) on Pascal Context and 87.2% mIoU on Pascal VOC 2012.

In summary, the paper presents a novel framework for capturing co-occurring features and contextual information in semantic segmentation, demonstrating significant improvements in performance through the proposed CFM and CFNet architectures.
