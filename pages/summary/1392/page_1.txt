In the paper "Co-occurrent Features in Semantic Segmentation," the authors investigate the use of co-occurrent features to enhance semantic segmentation, which assigns per-pixel labels to images. They introduce the Co-occurrent Feature Model, focusing on the distribution of co-occurrent features relevant to a target object. To effectively utilize the semantic context derived from these features, they propose the Aggregated Co-occurrent Feature (ACF) Module, which aggregates the probability of co-occurrent features within their context, enabling a fine-grained spatial invariant representation.

The authors argue that while existing methods leverage global contextual information, they often fall short in complex scenarios where ambiguous object categories exist. For instance, in scenes where objects like water, river, and sea are visually similar and difficult to classify based solely on appearance, the presence of co-occurring objects (like a boat) can provide crucial context for disambiguation. The ACF Module addresses this by considering the relationships among co-occurrent features before prediction, thus improving segmentation performance.

The proposed method demonstrates significant improvements over the Fully Convolutional Network (FCN) baseline, achieving 54.0% mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) on the Pascal Context dataset, 87.2% mIoU on Pascal VOC 2012, and 44.89% mIoU on the ADE20K dataset. The authors plan to release the source code and complete system upon publication.
