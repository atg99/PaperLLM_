The paper discusses advancements in real-time object detection, focusing on the optimization of query initialization in various Detection Transformers (DETR). It highlights the limitations of current DETR models, which are computationally intensive and not suitable for real-time applications. The proposed RT-DETR aims to reduce computational costs while enhancing query initialization, thereby outperforming existing real-time detectors.

The analysis includes a detailed examination of the Non-Maximum Suppression (NMS) algorithm, which is essential for filtering overlapping detection boxes. It emphasizes the importance of two thresholds: the confidence threshold and the Intersection over Union (IoU) threshold. The paper presents empirical results demonstrating that the execution time of NMS is influenced by these thresholds, with a focus on the YOLOv5 and YOLOv8 detectors. As the confidence threshold increases, fewer boxes remain for processing, which reduces NMS execution time.

Additionally, the paper establishes an end-to-end speed benchmark for various real-time detectors, using the COCO val2017 dataset. This benchmark accounts for the average inference time of detectors while excluding I/O and memory operations. The findings reveal that inappropriate threshold settings can lead to significant false positives or negatives, and the paper advocates for a comprehensive speed benchmark to enable fair comparisons among different detection models.
