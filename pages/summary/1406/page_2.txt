The paper introduces the Real-Time Detection Transformer (RT-DETR), which is presented as the first real-time end-to-end object detector. The authors identify key challenges in existing models, particularly the high computational cost associated with multi-scale features in Transformer encoders and the difficulty in optimizing object queries. They propose a redesign of the encoder to improve speed and accuracy by implementing a hybrid encoder that separates intra-scale interaction and cross-scale fusion, significantly enhancing inference speed.

To address the issue of low localization confidence in object queries, the authors introduce an uncertainty-minimal query selection method that optimizes initial queries, resulting in better performance. RT-DETR allows for flexible speed tuning to adapt to various real-time scenarios without the need for retraining. 

Performance metrics demonstrate that RT-DETR outperforms advanced YOLO models in both speed and accuracy, achieving notable results on the COCO dataset. The paper also discusses the impact of Non-Maximum Suppression (NMS) on detection speed and accuracy, establishing a benchmark for end-to-end inference speed in real-time detectors.

Key contributions of the paper include the introduction of RT-DETR, a quantitative analysis of NMS effects, and the ability to adjust decoder layers for speed tuning. The authors highlight that RT-DETR not only enhances accuracy and speed but also eliminates the negative effects of NMS in real-time object detection.
