This paper presents a comparison of various state-of-the-art (SOTA) object detectors, focusing on real-time performance and accuracy. The table illustrates the specifications of different models, including their backbone architecture, epochs, parameters, GFLOPs, frames per second (FPS), and average precision (AP) metrics across various thresholds.

The results indicate that the proposed RT-DETR model outperforms existing YOLO detectors and other DETR models in both speed and accuracy. Specifically, RT-DETR-R50 achieves an accuracy improvement of 2.2% AP and a speed enhancement of 21 times over the DINO-Deformable-DETR-R50, marking a significant advancement.

The paper also includes ablation studies that evaluate the efficiency of a hybrid encoder designed to balance accuracy and computational cost. Variants of the encoder demonstrate varying degrees of accuracy improvements and latency, revealing that decoupling intra-scale interaction and cross-scale fusion can enhance performance while reducing computational expenses. The final variant, variant E, shows a notable 1.5% AP improvement while managing to decrease latency by 24%, highlighting the effectiveness of the hybrid encoder.

Additionally, an ablation study on query selection is conducted, focusing on the impact of selecting encoder features based on classification scores to optimize query performance. The results illustrate the effectiveness of the proposed query selection method in improving the model's accuracy on the COCO validation dataset.

Overall, the RT-DETR model demonstrates a strong performance improvement over existing methods, establishing it as a leading choice for real-time object detection tasks.
