The paper presents the Real-TimeDEtection TRansformer (RT-DETR), a novel end-to-end object detection framework that outperforms existing YOLO models in both speed and accuracy. While YOLO detectors are popular for their balance between these two metrics, they suffer from the limitations of Non-Maximum Suppression (NMS), which can slow down performance and introduce instability due to hyperparameter tuning. In contrast, RT-DETR eliminates NMS, leveraging a Transformer-based architecture that enhances computational efficiency.

The development of RT-DETR involves a two-step approach focused on optimizing both speed and accuracy. Initially, an efficient hybrid encoder is introduced to process multi-scale features effectively, decoupling intra-scale interactions from cross-scale fusion. This design significantly accelerates the processing speed. Subsequently, the paper details a method for uncertainty-minimal query selection, which ensures high-quality initial queries for the decoder, thereby boosting accuracy.

RT-DETR allows for flexible speed adjustments by varying the number of decoder layers without the need for retraining, making it adaptable to different operational scenarios. The experimental results indicate that RT-DETR-R50 and RT-DETR-R101 achieve 53.1% and 54.3% average precision (AP) on the COCO dataset, respectively, along with 108 and 74 frames per second (FPS) on a T4 GPU. These results not only surpass previous YOLO models but also significantly outperform the DINO-R50 in terms of accuracy and processing speed.

The findings suggest that RT-DETR, especially after pre-training on Objects365, achieves even higher AP scores of 55.3% and 56.2% for the R50 and R101 models, respectively. This research highlights the potential of Transformer-based architectures for real-time object detection, paving the way for future developments in the field.
