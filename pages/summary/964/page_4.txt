The paper discusses the representation of rotations in various dimensions, specifically analyzing the continuity of these representations. It begins by noting the absence of possible continuous representations for certain spaces, particularly highlighting that 3D rotations cannot be continuously represented in four or fewer dimensions.

In the section on rotation representation analysis, the authors examine both discontinuous and continuous representations. They start with discontinuous representations, using Euler angles and quaternions as examples. The Euler angle representation shows discontinuity at the identity rotation, where the azimuth angle can yield conflicting limits. The quaternion representation also reveals discontinuity, particularly around 180-degree rotations, where limits approach zero while the corresponding coordinates are non-zero.

The authors explain that the 3D rotation group SO(3) is homeomorphic to the real projective space RP3, which cannot be continuously embedded in dimensions less than five. This implies that there cannot be a continuous representation for 3D rotations in four or fewer dimensions.

In contrast, the section on continuous representations introduces a method to create continuous representations for n-dimensional rotations. The authors propose a representation that utilizes a Gram-Schmidt-like orthogonalization process to maintain continuity while avoiding excessive matrix sizes. This leads to the identification of continuous rotation representations for 3D rotations in 6D and 5D.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the significance of understanding the limitations and possibilities of rotation representations in various dimensions, providing insights that could guide choices in learning tasks and applications in graphics and vision.
