The paper presents empirical results focusing on the performance of various rotation representations in the context of 3D pose estimation tasks. Key findings include the mean, maximum, and standard deviation of errors for different representations, specifically 6D, 5D, quaternion, axis-angle, Euler, and matrix representations. The 6D and 5D representations demonstrate superior performance, converging faster and maintaining lower error rates compared to others, particularly the Euler representation, which exhibited the highest errors. 

Figures illustrate that while quaternion, axis-angle, and Euler representations yield errors predominantly below 25 degrees, some instances reach up to 180 degrees. In contrast, 6D and 5D representations consistently stay below 2 degrees. The authors argue that continuous rotation representations enhance training efficiency and accuracy, leading to lower errors and faster convergence.

In the context of 3D point cloud pose estimation, the study utilizes a weight-sharing Siamese network based on a simplified PointNet architecture. The network processes reference and target point clouds to estimate the rotation between them. The results reaffirm that continuous representations outperform traditional discontinuous methods, further solidifying the advantages of utilizing continuous rotation representations in neural network training for pose estimation tasks. Additional results in the appendix support these conclusions, indicating robust performance across various configurations.
