The paper discusses the concept of continuous representation within the context of neural networks, particularly focusing on the challenges posed by discontinuous mappings from original spaces to representation spaces. It uses the example of 2D rotations in the special orthogonal group SO(2) to illustrate these challenges. The representation of 2D rotations can be expressed as a matrix dependent on an angle θ, which can be mapped to the interval [0, 2π]. However, this mapping is discontinuous because at the identity matrix, the angle can yield two different limits: 0 and 2π. This discontinuity can complicate neural networks' learning processes.

The authors define a continuous representation as a mapping where a neural network produces an intermediate representation in a representation space R, which can be mapped back to an original space X. The mappings f and g must satisfy the condition f(g(x)) = x for every x in X, with g being continuous for the representation to be considered continuous.

The paper emphasizes the importance of continuity in mappings for neural networks, highlighting that if a connected set in the original space remains connected in the representation space, it leads to a continuous training signal for the network. Conversely, a discontinuous mapping can result in disconnected training signals, hindering the learning process.

Furthermore, the paper connects the concept of continuous representation to topology, stating that if (f, g) is continuous and g is a continuous one-to-one function, it implies a homeomorphism between the original space and a subset of the representation space. This connection underscores the geometric intuition that certain spaces can be transformed into each other through continuous and invertible mappings, allowing for effective learning in neural networks. The paper concludes by asserting the necessity of defining the mappings appropriately to ensure the neural network's outputs can be effectively related back to the original space.
