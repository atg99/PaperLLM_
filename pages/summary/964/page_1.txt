In the paper "On the Continuity of Rotation Representations in Neural Networks," the authors Yi Zhou, Connelly Barnes, Jingwan Lu, Jimei Yang, and Hao Li explore the significance of continuous representations in neural networks, particularly for 3D rotations. They highlight the common use of quaternions and Euler angles, which they demonstrate to be discontinuous in 3D and 4D spaces, making them challenging for neural networks to learn effectively. 

The authors introduce a new definition of continuity in representations and relate it to topological concepts such as homeomorphism and embedding. They analyze various rotation representations and show that all traditional representations are discontinuous in four or fewer dimensions, thus prompting the need for continuous alternatives. They propose continuous representations for 3D rotations in 5D and 6D spaces, which are shown to facilitate better learning outcomes for neural networks.

Furthermore, the paper extends the discussion to the n-dimensional rotation group SO(n) and other groups like the orthogonal group and similarity transforms. Empirical results presented in the paper substantiate the theoretical findings, demonstrating that neural networks utilizing the proposed continuous representations outperform those using traditional discontinuous representations across several applications in graphics and vision, including autoencoder tests, rotation estimation for point clouds, and inverse kinematics for human poses.
