The paper discusses a generalized Gram-Schmidt (gGS) process for obtaining orthogonal representations in n-dimensional spaces, particularly focusing on the rotation group SO(n). The method involves a normalization function and the use of a generalized cross product to compute the last column of the orthogonal matrix. The continuity of the gGS process is established, and it is shown that the process retains the first n-1 components of the original matrix during transformations.

For 3D rotations, the gGS method simplifies to the ordinary cross product, leading to a 6D representation that maintains orthogonality. This representation is advantageous for network applications, as it avoids unnecessary parameters and reduces computational error compared to direct predictions of 3x3 matrices.

The paper further explores group operations, emphasizing computational efficiency in multiplying representations. It introduces a dimensionality reduction approach for n-dimensional rotations while maintaining a continuous representation. This is achieved through stereographic projections, which normalize input points and project them to lower dimensions.

The concept of normalized projection is defined to ensure continuity, and a method for stereographic un-projection is also presented. The paper concludes that while the dimensionality reduction method does not outperform the original representation, it demonstrates the potential advantages of continuous rotation representations over discontinuous ones. Overall, the findings contribute to the understanding of efficient representations in higher-dimensional rotation groups.
