The paper explores the training of neural networks to approximate mappings between various rotation representations, focusing on 3D rotations. The authors trained a Siamese network using 2,290 airplane point clouds from ShapeNet and tested it on 400 held-out point clouds with random rotations. They minimized the L2 loss between the network output and ground-truth rotation matrices, achieving the lowest mean and standard deviation of errors with a 6D representation, outperforming 3D and 4D representations. The authors hypothesize that the gradient distortion from stereographic projection affects performance.

In a separate experiment on inverse kinematics for human poses, the authors trained a four-layer MLP network using joint positions as inputs to predict rotations. They employed the CMU Motion Capture Database, training on 1.14 million frames and testing on 107,000 frames of human poses. The results showed that the 6D representation yielded the lowest errors and fastest convergence, while 3D and 4D representations had higher average errors. The performance of the 3x3 matrix was poor when not orthogonalized, indicating potential scaling issues during forward kinematics.

The conclusion emphasizes that neural networks are more effective with continuous representations like 5D and 6D, which address the discontinuities found in traditional quaternion and Euler angle representations.
