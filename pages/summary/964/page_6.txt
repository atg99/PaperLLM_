The paper discusses methods for reducing the dimensionality of representations for special orthogonal groups, particularly SO(n), through normalized projections. It introduces a mapping function fP that reshapes the vectorized representation of an orthonormal basis generated by the Gram-Schmidt process, ensuring the preservation of the unit length property of basis vectors. The authors demonstrate that for 3D rotations, a 5D representation can be achieved using a 6D representation from previous cases, allowing for continuity in representation through up to n-2 projections.

Furthermore, the paper extends these representations to other groups, such as O(n) and similarity transforms, highlighting necessary modifications in the Gram-Schmidt process based on determinant values. It emphasizes the significance of continuity in rotation representations for effective learning, supported by empirical results from a sanity test and real-world applications using a multi-layer perceptron (MLP) network. The experiments compare various rotation representations, including quaternions, axis-angle, and Euler angles, evaluating their performance based on geodesic errors in reconstructed rotation matrices. The findings suggest that representations with better continuity properties yield improved learning outcomes.
