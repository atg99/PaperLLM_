The paper investigates the performance of discontinuous versus continuous representations in various rotation tasks, such as rotation autoencoders, 3D point cloud rotation estimation, and human pose inverse kinematics. It highlights that discontinuous representations can result in significantly higher mean errors—up to 14 times more—compared to continuous representations. Additionally, these representations converge slower and maintain large errors at specific rotation angles, attributed to their inherent discontinuities. 

The authors propose a direct regression approach using 3x3 rotation matrices but find it introduces larger errors than their 6D representation. They emphasize the necessity for orthogonal matrices in certain applications, suggesting an orthogonalization procedure like Gram-Schmidt, which effectively leads to their proposed 6D representation.

The contributions of the study include defining continuity for rotation representations suitable for neural networks, analyzing both discontinuous and continuous representations across various dimensions, presenting new formulas for continuous representations of SO(3) and SO(n), and providing empirical evidence that supports the superiority of continuous representations for learning tasks.

The related work section contextualizes the study within neural network approximation theory, noting that continuous functions with better smoothness yield lower approximation errors. It discusses previous findings on the limitations of traditional rotation representations, such as Euler angles and quaternions, in learning tasks due to their discontinuities. The paper argues that these discontinuities contribute to significant errors in applications like 6D object pose estimation and inverse kinematics, advocating for the use of continuous rotation representations to enhance performance across these tasks. 

In defining continuous representation, the authors establish terminology and analyze 2D rotations to develop a broader understanding of continuity in neural networks, linking it to topological concepts.
