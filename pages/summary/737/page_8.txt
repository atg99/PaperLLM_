The paper discusses the performance of the Optimistic Actor-Critic (OAC) algorithm compared to the Soft-Actor-Critic (SAC) algorithm in reinforcement learning, particularly in the Humanoid environment. The results indicate that OAC outperforms SAC, achieving a performance of 5033±147 compared to SAC's 4586±117, without any hyper-parameter tuning. This improvement suggests that optimism can enhance sample efficiency in model-free reinforcement learning.

A key component of OAC is the use of an epistemic uncertainty estimate derived from bootstrapping the critic network. The paper highlights the advantages of this approach by contrasting OAC with a modified version that does not utilize the uncertainty estimate but still employs a shifted exploration policy. The findings show that the bootstrapped uncertainty estimate significantly enhances performance in challenging environments like Humanoid, while having minimal effects in other settings. Given the low computational cost of obtaining the upper bound, the authors advocate for its use.

The paper also examines the sensitivity of OAC to the KL divergence constraint, controlled by the hyperparameter δ. The results demonstrate that a wide range of δ values can yield good performance, indicating robustness in the algorithm's design.

Additionally, the authors address learning stability, noting that despite initial concerns regarding the shifted exploration policy, both OAC and SAC demonstrate similar stability across individual learning runs. This finding alleviates worries about the potential instability often associated with deep off-policy reinforcement learning algorithms.
