The Optimistic Actor-Critic (OAC) algorithm is designed to enhance stability and performance in deep reinforcement learning. The algorithm initializes parameters for the critic and target policy, and iteratively collects transitions from the environment while updating the critic and policy. Key steps include sampling actions from an exploration policy, storing transitions, and updating critic weights using a lower bound estimate of the action-value function.

OAC incorporates a unique exploration policy that avoids pessimistic underexploration by sampling actions that are distant from spurious maxima of the lower bound estimate. This policy is not symmetric with respect to the target policy's mean, addressing issues of uninformed exploration. The algorithm demonstrates remarkable stability due to a Kullback-Leibler (KL) constraint that keeps the exploration policy close to the target policy. Empirical evaluations show that OAC's stability is comparable to established algorithms like Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) and Twin Delayed Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (TD3).

Furthermore, while OAC is optimistic, it avoids catastrophic overestimation by utilizing optimistic estimates solely for exploration. The updates for the critic and actor rely on lower bound estimates, ensuring that the upper bound does not directly influence the critic's learning process.

In comparison to other methods, OAC distinguishes itself by using a bootstrap approximation of the action-value function rather than a point estimate or a probabilistic model for exploration. It stands apart from other algorithms that incorporate uncertainty estimates, making OAC a unique contribution to the field of reinforcement learning.
