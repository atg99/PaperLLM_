The paper discusses the limitations of current actor-critic algorithms, specifically focusing on the suboptimal exploration behavior of policies with a small standard deviation around the mean. This underexploration can lead to missing the true maximum of the critic Qπ, as evidenced in the MuJoCo Ant environment. The authors argue that while upper bounds in the action space can encourage exploration and correct the critic, simply applying an upper bound does not resolve the issue of pessimistic underexploration due to overestimation.

The paper introduces the concept of directional uninformedness, where Gaussian policies sample actions symmetrically around the mean, leading to redundant exploration in areas already well-understood by the critic. This is illustrated through an example showing that while exploration to the left may be beneficial, exploration to the right is often unnecessary.

To address these issues, the authors propose the Optimistic Actor Critic (OAC) algorithm, which uses an exploration policy that maximizes an upper confidence bound of Qπ when the agent enters new states. This exploration policy is distinct from the target policy and is designed to mitigate both pessimistic underexploration and directional uninformedness, all while maintaining learning stability.

The paper outlines the process of deriving an approximate upper confidence bound, beginning with estimating epistemic uncertainty through a Gaussian model based on bootstrapped critic evaluations. The mean and standard deviation for the state-action value function Q are calculated, leading to the formulation of a tractable algorithm that enhances exploration efficiency.

Overall, the OAC approach is positioned as a more effective solution for improving exploration in reinforcement learning, especially in the context of actor-critic methods.
