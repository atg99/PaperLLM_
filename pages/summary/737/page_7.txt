The paper discusses the performance of the Optimistic Actor Critic (OAC) algorithm in comparison to state-of-the-art reinforcement learning methods, specifically Soft Actor Critic (SAC) and Twin Delayed Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (TD3), across five MuJoCo environments. The authors highlight that while OAC may appear similar to natural actor-critic methods due to its KL constraint, it fundamentally differs by constraining the exploration policy relative to the target policy, rather than focusing on small updates.

The experiments aim to evaluate OAC's competitive performance, the effectiveness of optimistic exploration based on bootstrapped uncertainty estimates, and the impact of this exploration on the stability of learning. The results indicate that OAC enhances performance, achieving state-of-the-art results on the Hopper environment and competitive results on Ant and Walker. In particular, OAC outperformed SAC on the Humanoid task when tested under conditions that maximized memory buffer usage.

Overall, the findings suggest that OAC's optimistic exploration can lead to improved performance in actor-critic methods, although the variance in results for the HalfCheetah environment complicates definitive comparisons. The paper provides detailed hyper-parameter tuning information and emphasizes the significance of improved exploration strategies in reinforcement learning.
