The paper introduces Optimistic Actor-Critic (OAC), an advanced reinforcement learning algorithm designed to enhance exploration efficiency in continuous control tasks. Traditional Gaussian policies often sample actions uniformly in opposing directions, which can be inefficient, particularly in previously explored action spaces. OAC addresses this issue by employing an off-policy exploration strategy that leverages upper confidence bounds derived from epistemic uncertainty estimates of the Q-function. This approach fosters more informed sampling, mitigating the risks of underexploration.

OAC incorporates a KL constraint between the exploration and target policies to maintain stability, especially in the context of off-policy reinforcement learning, which is prone to instability due to function approximation challenges. Similar to other contemporary methods like Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) and Twin Delayed DDPG (TD3), OAC further reduces overestimation by utilizing a lower confidence bound for updates to the target policy.

The empirical evaluation of OAC across various continuous control tasks demonstrates its superior sample efficiency, particularly on the Humanoid benchmark. The paper also conducts ablation studies to isolate the impact of bootstrapped uncertainty estimates on performance and confirms the stability of OAC through hyperparameter analysis.

In the context of reinforcement learning, the paper outlines the foundational concepts, including the Markov decision process framework, the definition of policies, action-value functions, and the general objectives of actor-critic methods. The algorithm aims to discover an optimal deployment policy that maximizes expected returns while maintaining exploration through a high-entropy target policy. OAC represents a significant advancement in the efficiency and stability of reinforcement learning strategies.
