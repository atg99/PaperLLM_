This paper discusses the development of an Optimistic Actor-Critic (OAC) algorithm, which enhances reinforcement learning by effectively managing uncertainty in action selection. The authors derive bootstraps for estimating value functions using existing algorithms, allowing for efficient computation of mean and standard deviation values, denoted as µQ and σQ. They demonstrate that employing a two-network bootstrap significantly improves performance, even when both bootstraps target the same value.

The upper bound on the action-value function is defined as ˆQUB(s,a) = µQ(s,a) + βUBσQ(s,a), where βUB controls the level of optimism. To facilitate tractable computations, the authors approximate this upper bound with a linear function ¯QUB, which is shown to be the best linear fit near the current policy mean by Taylor's theorem. 

The exploration policy πE is designed to balance maximizing the upper bound while constraining the KL divergence from the target policy πT, ensuring stability and preventing drastic actions that could hinder learning. The authors argue that this KL constraint is vital for maintaining similarity between the exploration and target policies, which enhances the reliability of the upper bound approximation.

The paper presents a closed-form solution for the exploration policy, which maintains the covariance structure of the target policy while adjusting the mean based on the gradient of the upper bound. The OAC algorithm utilizes this exploration policy to sample actions, storing them in a memory buffer, and subsequently training both the critic and actor components.

OAC is shown to mitigate issues related to greedy exploration, enabling swift corrections to the critic's estimates and preventing pessimistic underexploration. The exploration policy's asymmetry relative to the target policy's mean further helps avoid uninformed directional choices, ensuring a more effective learning process. The computational efficiency of OAC is highlighted, as it operates within the same time constraints as existing algorithms.
