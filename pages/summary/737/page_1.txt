The paper "Better Exploration with Optimistic Actor-Critic" by Kamil Ciosek and colleagues addresses the challenge of poor sample efficiency in actor-critic methods within model-free Reinforcement Learning. While these methods have shown success in continuous control tasks, their extensive requirement for environment interactions limits their real-world applicability.

The authors identify two key issues affecting exploration in existing algorithms like Soft Actor Critic (SAC) and Twin Delayed Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (TD3). First, they highlight the problem of pessimistic underexploration, where a greedy actor update combined with a pessimistic critic estimate leads to avoidance of less-known actions. Second, they point out that current algorithms are directionally uninformed, sampling actions equally in opposing directions, which is inefficient as certain directions are more relevant for exploration.

To overcome these challenges, the authors propose a new algorithm called Optimistic Actor Critic (OAC). This algorithm approximates both lower and upper confidence bounds on the state-action value function, allowing for directed exploration through the upper bound while mitigating overestimation risks with the lower bound. The empirical evaluation of OAC across various continuous control tasks demonstrates significant improvements in sample efficiency, establishing it as a state-of-the-art approach in this domain.
