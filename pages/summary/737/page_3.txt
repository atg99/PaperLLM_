The paper discusses exploration inefficiencies in actor-critic reinforcement learning methods, particularly focusing on the issues observed in contemporary algorithms like Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) and Twin Delayed Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (TD3). It highlights two main problems: pessimistic underexploration and directional uninformedness.

In the context of actor-critic methods, the algorithm aims to optimize a policy parameter vector to maximize expected returns. The paper introduces a reparametrized policy gradient approach that utilizes a random variable sampled from a multivariate Gaussian distribution, allowing for the computation of gradients with respect to the policy. To encourage exploration, an entropy term is added to the gradient, which helps balance the exploitation of known rewards with the exploration of new actions.

The authors argue that existing exploration strategies are inefficient, particularly due to the use of a lower bound approximation for the critic. This approach, while intended to improve sample efficiency and prevent overestimation of the critic's value function, can lead to policies that become overly concentrated around local maxima. This concentration can hinder exploration, especially when the critic's estimates are inaccurate.

The paper emphasizes that these inefficiencies arise from the algorithm's reliance on the lower bound for exploration, resulting in a tendency to exploit rather than explore, which can be detrimental to learning. The authors propose addressing these stability issues by imposing a Kullback-Leibler (KL) constraint between the exploration policy and the target policy, which they elaborate on in a subsequent section.

Overall, the paper aims to shed light on the exploration challenges inherent in current actor-critic methods and suggests potential directions for improvement.
