This paper discusses advancements in adversarial modeling, particularly addressing the limitations of likelihood-based models in fitting diverse data modes and the error accumulation in autoregressive models during long sequence sampling. The authors propose an automated method to balance sample diversity and quality, leveraging a pre-trained ImageNet classifier to score generated samples based on their alignment with true data distributions. This classifier is utilized solely for quantitative evaluation metrics such as FID, IS, Precision, and Recall, without influencing the sampling process.

The foundation of this work is based on the VQ-VAE framework, with enhancements drawn from Gated PixelCNN and self-attention mechanisms. The study acknowledges BigGAN as a benchmark for high-quality image generation, noting that the integration of self-attention and compute scaling can also enhance VQ-VAE sample quality. The paper contrasts its methodology with that of related works, emphasizing its unique hierarchical latent variable approach that extracts complementary information at each level, thereby avoiding issues seen in previous models.

The authors also review existing techniques for improving sample quality through rejection sampling and discuss the use of neural networks in learned image compression. The experiments section highlights the objective evaluation challenges in generative models, presenting both quantitative and qualitative results from their model trained on ImageNet 256x256. The findings indicate high-quality, sharp samples across various classes, showcasing the model's diversity in comparison to the established BigGAN-deep model.
