In the paper "Generating Diverse High-Fidelity Images with VQ-VAE-2," the authors Ali Razavi, Aäron van den Oord, and Oriol Vinyals from DeepMind investigate the capabilities of Vector Quantized Variational AutoEncoder (VQ-VAE) models for large-scale image generation. They enhance the autoregressive priors used in VQ-VAE, achieving synthetic image samples with significantly improved coherence and fidelity. 

The proposed model utilizes simple feed-forward encoder and decoder networks, making it efficient for scenarios where encoding and decoding speed are crucial. Notably, VQ-VAE samples an autoregressive model only in the compressed latent space, which is considerably faster than sampling in pixel space, especially for larger images. The authors demonstrate that a multi-scale hierarchical structure of VQ-VAE, combined with robust priors over the latent codes, can generate image quality that competes with state-of-the-art Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) on complex datasets like ImageNet. This approach avoids common GAN issues such as mode collapse and lack of diversity.

The paper highlights the advancements in deep generative models, facilitated by architectural innovations and computational improvements, allowing for training on larger datasets and model sizes. The applications of these models span various fields, including super resolution, domain editing, artistic manipulation, and generative tasks in music and speech.
