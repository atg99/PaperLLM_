This paper evaluates the performance of a multi-scale hierarchical model, specifically a Vector Quantized Variational Autoencoder (VQ-V AE), in generating high-resolution face images using the FFHQ dataset. The authors report training and validation negative log-likelihood (NLL) and Mean Squared Error (MSE) metrics to assess model performance, highlighting that the small differences in NLL and MSE indicate no overfitting for both the prior networks and the VQ-V AE.

The NLL values for the top and bottom priors are similar for the training and validation sets, suggesting effective generalization. The paper discusses the challenges of modeling high-resolution images, particularly the need to capture long-range dependencies, such as the correlation between features in face images.

For quantitative evaluation, the authors argue that traditional metrics like FID and Inception Score do not adequately address generalization, as they can reflect memorization of training data rather than true generative capability. Instead, they focus on NLL and propose Precision-Recall metrics as alternatives to evaluate the trade-off between sample quality and diversity.

The results indicate that while VQ-V AE shows slightly lower precision compared to BigGAN-deep, it achieves higher recall, suggesting a better balance in generating diverse high-quality samples. Overall, the findings support the effectiveness of the proposed model in generating realistic and diverse face images while maintaining generalization.
