The paper discusses a hierarchical approach to encoding images using a two-level latent hierarchy, specifically for 256x256 images. The encoder first downsamples the image to a 64x64 representation, which is quantized into a bottom-level latent map. A subsequent stack of residual blocks further reduces the representation to a 32x32 latent map at the top level. The decoder reconstructs the original image from these quantized latent maps through residual blocks and strided transposed convolutions.

To enhance image compression and facilitate sampling, the authors propose learning a prior over the latent codes during a second stage. This involves fitting prior distributions with neural networks, which improves the performance of latent variable models and minimizes the difference between the marginal posterior and the learned prior. This process is viewed as a form of lossless compression of the latent space, optimizing the distribution of latent variables to better reflect their true distribution, ultimately leading to more realistic image reconstructions.

In the VQ-VAE framework, the prior over the top latent map captures global structural information using multi-headed self-attention layers, while the bottom-level prior focuses on local information with a larger resolution. Although using self-attention layers for local information is not feasible due to memory constraints, the authors find that employing large conditioning stacks from the top prior yields satisfactory results. The hierarchical factorization allows for separate training of each prior, optimizing resource utilization.

Additionally, the paper addresses the challenge of balancing diversity with classifier-based rejection sampling. Unlike GANs, the maximum likelihood objective requires probabilistic models to account for all training data distributions. This requirement complicates the task but ensures comprehensive coverage of data modes, enhancing the model's robustness. The effectiveness of the hierarchical VQ-VAE is illustrated through reconstruction examples, demonstrating how each latent map contributes additional detail to the output.
