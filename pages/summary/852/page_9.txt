The paper discusses the challenges associated with evolutionary algorithms (EAs) in neural architecture search (NAS), particularly the high computational costs involved in evaluating the fitness of candidate networks. Evaluating the fitness can take substantial time, ranging from several hours to thousands of GPU days for large networks and datasets, which limits the practicality of these methods. 

To address this bottleneck, various acceleration techniques are proposed. Surrogate-assisted evolutionary algorithms (SAEAs) leverage cheaper models, such as radial basis function networks and Gaussian process models, to predict the fitness of candidates, significantly reducing the number of expensive evaluations needed. For example, a variant of AE-CNN that utilizes a performance predictor called E2EPP can reduce GPU days by 2.3x on CIFAR100 and 2.1x on CIFAR10.

Knowledge inheritance is another strategy discussed, where offspring networks inherit weights from parent networks, thereby lowering computation costs. Additionally, approaches using proxy metrics for training and evaluation are highlighted; these metrics can speed up the process but may introduce biases. The EcoNAS method implements a hierarchical proxy strategy to efficiently filter candidates while maintaining performance.

The paper also addresses evolutionary multi-objective NAS methods that optimize conflicting objectives, such as classification performance and inference time. Notable methods include NEMO, which uses NSGA-II for simultaneous optimization, and its extensions, which incorporate additional metrics for computational complexity. Other approaches, like LEMONADE, frame NAS as a bi-objective problem to balance performance with resource requirements.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the need for efficient strategies to enhance the usability of evolutionary NAS methods in practical scenarios.
