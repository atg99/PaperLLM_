The paper discusses the complexities and challenges of federated learning, particularly in the context of neural architecture search. Unlike distributed learning, which focuses on accelerating training speed, federated learning prioritizes the protection of user privacy and operates under unique constraints such as unstable connections and unbalanced data distribution across numerous client devices.  

The paper categorizes federated learning based on data distribution characteristics, distinguishing between horizontal federated learning—where clients have the same feature space but different samples—and instance distributed learning. Key differences highlighted include the nature of data distribution (IID vs. non-IID), the number of connected clients, and the mechanisms for global model updates. 

In horizontal federated learning, the central server cannot access raw data from clients, which complicates the learning process. The typical algorithm, such as FedAvg, involves initializing a global model on the server, distributing it to clients, and allowing those clients to train the model locally on their data for several epochs before sending updated parameters back to the server. 

The paper concludes by outlining remaining challenges in effectively implementing federated neural architecture search, emphasizing the need to balance multiple objectives such as accuracy, communication costs, model complexity, and memory requirements on local devices.
