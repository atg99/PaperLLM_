This paper discusses various methods for Neural Architecture Search (NAS), focusing on both gradient-free and gradient-based techniques. It highlights the evolution of NAS, particularly the increasing popularity of gradient descent (GD)-based methods due to their faster search speeds compared to reinforcement learning (RL) and evolutionary algorithms (EA). 

The authors reference several notable contributions in the field, including Lu et al.'s integration of multi-objective evolutionary algorithms with online transfer learning, which allows for efficient design of task-specific network architectures. They also discuss early GD-based methods that optimize layer hyperparameters and connectivity patterns.

Key algorithms mentioned include DARTS, which optimizes both network weights and architecture using relaxation tricks, and GDAS, which accelerates architecture search through a differentiable architecture sampler. Additionally, they introduce sequential greedy architecture search (SGAS) and Partially-Connected DARTS (PC-DARTS), which enhance performance and stability in the search process.

A significant challenge identified is the excessive GPU memory required by GD-based methods, which limits the search space. The authors propose DMaskingNAS as a solution, employing a masking mechanism to reuse feature maps and expand the search space without increasing memory costs. They also discuss ProxylessNAS, which designs networks directly based on the target task and hardware, circumventing the limitations of proxy tasks.

Overall, the paper emphasizes advancements in GD-based NAS methods, addressing their efficiency and memory constraints while showcasing a variety of innovative approaches to optimize neural network architectures.
