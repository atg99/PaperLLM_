The paper discusses an offline federated neural architecture search (NAS) framework that separates weight training and architecture search, aimed at reducing communication costs during federated learning. The procedure begins with removing global models with poor test accuracies, allowing the remaining clients to upload their model gradients for aggregation. This process is repeated for a predefined number of communication rounds, after which the global model is updated and stored.

Despite its effectiveness, this offline framework presents challenges, particularly in determining the optimal number of communication rounds and the additional communication resources required for training candidate neural network models. These issues highlight the need for an online federated NAS framework, which simultaneously trains models and optimizes architecture.

The paper introduces two approaches to online federated NAS: a gradient-based method and an evolutionary algorithm (EA)-based method. The gradient-based method utilizes a supernet composed of directed acyclic graphs (DAGs), enabling differentiable updates of architecture parameters through relaxation techniques. The process involves initializing the supernet, allowing clients to download and train it, and subsequently aggregating the model and architecture parameters at the server.

In summary, the paper outlines the development of federated NAS frameworks, addressing the limitations of offline approaches and proposing solutions for efficient model training and architecture optimization in a federated learning context.
