The paper discusses advancements in federated learning and neural architecture search (NAS), focusing on hybrid federated learning and the challenges associated with it. Hybrid federated learning is characterized by clients with varying sample and feature spaces, necessitating the sharing of data identity information, which poses privacy risks. Asymmetric federated learning is highlighted, where smaller companies prioritize ID protection while larger entities may not. The Secure ID alignment protocol, including Private Set Intersection (PSI), is essential for maintaining privacy during collaborative data sharing.

In the realm of NAS, the paper emphasizes the significance of network architecture in deep neural networks (DNNs) and the shift towards automated machine learning to facilitate network design for users lacking deep learning expertise. The NAS framework encompasses three dimensions: search space, search strategies, and performance estimation strategies. It categorizes search strategies into reinforcement learning, evolutionary algorithms, and gradient-based methods, among others. The performance estimation is critical but often computationally expensive. Recent efforts aim to minimize these costs through innovative methods like surrogate-assisted evolutionary algorithms and one-shot architecture search, addressing the limitations of traditional NAS approaches that require extensive computational resources.
