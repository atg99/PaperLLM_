This paper discusses advancements in Neural Architecture Search (NAS), particularly focusing on ProxylessNAS and MiLeNAS, as well as Federated NAS. ProxylessNAS enables the generation of network architectures for the ImageNet dataset without relying on proxy models, which allows for efficient use of GPU memory during training. 

He et al. highlight limitations in current Gradient Descent-based NAS methods, which often utilize heuristic bilevel optimization. They argue that these methods can suffer from gradient errors that hinder convergence to optimal solutions. To address this, they propose MiLeNAS, employing a first-order method that outperforms traditional bilevel optimization approaches in classification accuracy.

The paper further explores Federated NAS, which optimizes neural network architectures within a federated learning context. It distinguishes between offline and online optimization methods. Offline NAS separates architecture search and weight training, while online NAS integrates both processes, necessitating acceptable model performance during optimization. 

An example of an offline federated NAS framework is provided, which uses a multi-objective evolutionary algorithm involving initialization, offspring generation, environmental selection, and iterative training until convergence. The paper notes that this approach can be computationally and communicatively intensive since all participating clients must train models at each generation. To mitigate these costs, client sampling is suggested, where subsets of clients are used for training specific models. 

Overall, the paper emphasizes the evolution of NAS techniques and their application in federated learning, highlighting both challenges and innovative solutions.
