The paper discusses the current state and challenges of federated neural architecture search (NAS), highlighting the complexities of optimizing neural networks in a federated learning environment. It emphasizes the use of NSGA-II as a basic search algorithm for evolutionary optimization, which allows for the selection of well-trained subnets based on user preferences.

Key challenges in federated NAS include:

1. **Horizontally Partitioned Data**: The paper notes that non-IID (Independent and Identically Distributed) learning issues in horizontal federated learning remain unresolved. Previous research has attempted to address these issues through data sharing, which raises privacy concerns. Proposed statistical aggregation methods have shown promise over the traditional FedAvg algorithm, but their effectiveness in federated NAS is uncertain.

2. **Vertically Partitioned Data**: The paper highlights the difficulties in assessing data distribution in vertically partitioned federated learning. Current methods are primarily based on linear models and face challenges with loss calculation and gradient computation due to encryption, which complicates the use of complex models like deep neural networks (DNNs).

3. **Adversarial Federated Neural Architecture Search**: The paper discusses the vulnerabilities of federated learning to adversarial attacks, which can exploit model gradients to infer client data. Defense strategies have been categorized into gradient subsampling, differential privacy, and robust aggregation, but developing resilient architectures against such attacks remains challenging.

4. **Encrypted Federated NAS**: The use of homomorphic encryption to safeguard privacy in federated NAS is noted to be computationally intensive, posing additional challenges in practical implementations.

Overall, the paper identifies federated NAS as a nascent field with significant hurdles, particularly regarding data privacy, model robustness, and the complexity of implementing effective solutions.
