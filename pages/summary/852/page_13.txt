The paper discusses an online federated neural architecture search (NAS) framework, emphasizing its differences from traditional offline methods. Unlike offline frameworks that are population-based, this online approach jointly optimizes all candidate operations, allowing for architecture search and weight training of the supernet model to occur alternately during federated training. This method does not necessitate additional communication resources for candidate model training; however, it demands significant computation and memory resources, which may not be suitable for edge devices.

To address memory constraints, the paper introduces a lightweight real-time evolutionary NAS framework (RT-FedEvoNAS). This framework employs a model sampling technique, where only one path of repeated cells from the global model is sampled and sent to local clients, thus reducing local computation and upload costs.

The operational steps of RT-FedEvoNAS include initializing the supernet, client sampling, training subnets on local clients, generating offspring individuals through crossover and mutation, and evaluating objectives through aggregated uploads to the server. The framework allows for simultaneous architecture search and model training, eliminating the need for predefined fitness evaluation thresholds and additional communication resources.

The paper also highlights the multi-objective nature of federated NAS, which should optimize not only model performance but also minimize communication costs between the server and clients. RT-FedEvoNAS considers multiple objectives, including validation accuracy, model size, and floating-point operations per second (FLOPs), to achieve a trade-off between conflicting goals.
