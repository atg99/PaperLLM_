The paper discusses horizontal federated learning (HFL), a method that allows multiple clients to collaboratively train a global model while keeping their local data private. The process involves initializing global model parameters, clients updating these parameters based on their local data, and sending only the model weights or gradients back to a central server. This protects user privacy, as the server does not have access to raw data.

HFL faces several challenges compared to traditional centralized learning, including the need to minimize communication resources, improve convergence speed, and ensure privacy. Various approaches have been proposed to address these challenges, such as client update sub-sampling, model quantization, and layer-wise asynchronous updates to reduce communication costs. Additionally, techniques like the FedProx algorithm and attentive aggregation methods aim to tackle issues arising from non-IID data across clients.

The paper highlights concerns about potential data leakage through model gradients, leading to the adoption of encryption methods like additive homomorphic encryption and secure multiparty computation (SMC) to enhance privacy. However, these methods increase computational load and communication resource demands. Alternatively, differential privacy techniques, which involve adding noise to gradients, are suggested as lighter-weight privacy-preserving options, though they may impact the learning process if certain thresholds are exceeded.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the complexity of HFL compared to traditional distributed systems, focusing on balancing privacy preservation, communication efficiency, and model performance.
