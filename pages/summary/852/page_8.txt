The paper discusses various evolutionary algorithms (EAs) for neural architecture search (NAS) in the context of deep convolutional neural networks (CNNs). Real et al. propose a large-scale evolutionary NAS method that employs a variable-length encoding allowing network architectures to adaptively change their depths. Sun et al. introduce the AE-CNN algorithm, which automates CNN design without pre- or post-processing, utilizing predetermined building blocks inspired by ResNet and DenseNet. This approach includes a novel EA-based framework with variable-length encoding and repair mechanisms to avoid invalid architectures.

William et al. present a directed acyclic graph (DAG)-based encoding strategy that supports arbitrary CNN structures and depths. Suganuma et al. introduce the CGP-CNN algorithm that leverages Cartesian genetic programming to represent CNN architectures and their connections, facilitating variable-length architectures and skip connections. Sun et al. also propose EvoCNN, which automatically designs CNN architectures and initializes connection weights, optimizing search space using statistical measures and an incomplete training scheme to enhance fitness evaluation.

Additionally, the CNN-GA utilizes a genetic algorithm with a novel skip layer structure to mitigate overfitting risks by discarding fully connected layers. The paper notes that recent EA-based NAS algorithms have successfully employed micro-search spaces, exemplified by Real et al.'s AmoebaNet, which outperformed hand-designed methods on ImageNet. Overall, the paper highlights advancements in automated CNN architecture design through evolutionary strategies, focusing on optimizing both topology and weight initialization.
