The paper discusses various approaches to Neural Architecture Search (NAS), particularly focusing on Reinforcement Learning (RL)-based and Evolutionary Algorithms (EAs). It highlights the effectiveness of RL-based NAS methods in transferring cell structures to new tasks and presents a comparison between random search and Efficient NAS (ENAS) methods. A new reward function is introduced to enhance network quality and simplify hyperparameter tuning. Additionally, the paper describes architecture-aware knowledge distillation (AKD), which utilizes RL-based NAS to identify the best student models for a given teacher model.

In terms of EA-based NAS, the paper outlines the advantages of population-based, gradient-free heuristic search paradigms for optimizing deep neural networks (DNNs). It explains the four-step process of EA algorithms: population initialization, offspring generation, fitness evaluation, and environmental selection. The genetic CNN method is noted as an early example of using EAs for optimizing convolutional neural networks (CNNs), achieving competitive results on various datasets. 

The CoDeepNEAT method is also introduced, which extends the NEAT algorithm to DNNs by employing a coevolutionary approach. This method evolves two populations—modules and blueprints—allowing for more complex network structures. The paper concludes by mentioning the limitations of fixed-length chromosome encoding and the promise of variable-length encoding schemes in enhancing NAS performance.
