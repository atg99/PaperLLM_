The paper discusses vertical federated learning, a technique distinct from horizontal federated learning, where datasets share the same sample space but differ in feature space. This is exemplified by scenarios where different financial institutions serve the same customers but offer varying services. In vertical federated learning, the training data is partitioned by features, and a central server, referred to as a coordinator, is responsible for calculating the total loss rather than aggregating uploaded weights.

Initially introduced in prior research, the framework involves a trusted coordinator and two parties, each representing a client. The coordinator computes training loss and generates encryption key pairs, utilizing homomorphic encryption to preserve privacy. A recent advancement involves a two-party architecture that eliminates the need for a trusted coordinator, thus simplifying the system.

The typical process in this framework includes several steps: Party A, holding data labels, creates a homomorphic encryption key pair and shares the public key with Party B. Both parties compute local inner products of their data and model parameters, with Party B sending its results to Party A. Party A then calculates the encrypted loss and model gradients, which are communicated back to Party B for further calculations. This iterative process continues until model convergence.

The paper highlights the key differences between vertical and horizontal federated learning, particularly in the roles of the central server and the handling of data labels. While horizontal federated learning focuses on model aggregation, vertical federated learning emphasizes loss calculation and feature coordination among parties. Most existing studies primarily address two-party systems using binary logistic regression, although recent work has explored multi-participant frameworks and improved algorithms to enhance communication efficiency.
