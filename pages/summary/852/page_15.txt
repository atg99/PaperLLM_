This paper provides an overview of federated learning and neural architecture search (NAS), introducing the concept of federated NAS. It highlights significant challenges in federated NAS, as many techniques from centralized NAS do not apply due to the constraints of the federated learning environment. 

The paper discusses two approaches to federated NAS: offline optimization and online optimization. It points out that offline evolutionary NAS methods are often unsuitable for real-world applications due to their high communication costs associated with separate architecture search and weight training processes. The RT-FedEvoNAS method is mentioned as a potential solution, although it operates within a limited search space.

The paper also addresses the computational intensity of encrypting parameters in federated learning, noting that homomorphic encryption struggles with real numbers and requires modulus calculations with large prime numbers. The authors critique the security of traditional federated encryption methods, advocating for a more secure secret key management approach using secret sharing. However, this method increases communication costs due to the need for frequent gradient transfers between the server and clients.

In conclusion, while federated NAS presents significant challenges, it holds practical importance for real-world problems where traditional deep neural networks may fail. The authors aim to stimulate further research into federated NAS, contributing to advancements in AI applications where data privacy is critical.
