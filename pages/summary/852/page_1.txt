This paper provides a comprehensive survey of federated learning and federated neural architecture search (NAS), addressing the growing need for privacy-preserving machine learning techniques. The authors introduce federated learning as a distributed paradigm that maintains data privacy by allowing model parameter communication instead of sharing raw data. They categorize federated learning into horizontal, vertical, and hybrid forms.

The survey delves into various NAS methodologies, including those based on reinforcement learning, evolutionary algorithms, and gradient-based techniques. A significant focus is placed on federated NAS, which is further divided into online and offline implementations, as well as single- and multi-objective search strategies. The authors emphasize that online federated NAS poses unique challenges due to performance requirements and computational constraints.

Finally, the paper outlines unresolved research questions and suggests promising areas for future exploration in the realm of federated learning and NAS, underlining the importance of optimizing neural architectures within a federated context. Overall, this work highlights the intersection of privacy preservation and deep learning optimization, an area ripe for further investigation.
