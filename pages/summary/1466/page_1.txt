The paper presents a novel approach for end-to-end spoken language understanding (E2E SLU) that integrates a well-optimized end-to-end automatic speech recognizer (ASR) encoder with a pre-trained language model encoder into a transformer decoder. This unified model, termed the speech-language pre-trained model (SLP), is designed to enhance performance on limited labeled data by employing a conditional masked language model (MLM) objective. The model aims to effectively generate sequences of intent, slot type, and slot value from input speech during inference.

The authors discuss the limitations of traditional SLU systems, which rely on a cascaded ASR and natural language understanding (NLU) framework, citing issues such as error propagation between components and the challenge of obtaining sufficient paired data for training. The paper highlights the advantages of E2E modeling, which allows for reduced dependency on prior knowledge and simplifies the training process by combining ASR and NLU into a single model.

The proposed method not only addresses data scarcity through transfer learning but also innovatively formulates slot filling as a conditional sequence generation problem rather than a classification task. This approach enhances the model's generalization capabilities and performance in SLU tasks.

Experimental results on two public datasets demonstrate that the proposed method outperforms conventional cascaded approaches as well as current state-of-the-art E2E SLU methods, achieving superior results with significantly less paired training data. The paper's contributions emphasize the effectiveness of unifying speech and language encoders and rethinking slot filling within the context of sequence generation, positioning the SLP model as a promising solution for E2E SLU challenges.
