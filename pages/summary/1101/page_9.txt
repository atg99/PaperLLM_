This paper discusses the performance of various deep learning networks for pulmonary nodule classification and liver tumor segmentation tasks. It utilizes the LIDC dataset for the pulmonary nodule classification, where nodules from 1,010 CT scans were annotated by radiologists. The malignancy levels were simplified into benign and malignant categories. The study focuses on comparing the effectiveness of different initialization methods, with a particular emphasis on the pre-trained Med3D networks.

Results indicate that Med3D networks significantly outperformed Kin and TFS networks, demonstrating superior feature extraction capabilities. As the depth of the Med3D networks increased, performance improved, while Kin and TFS exhibited a decline in performance with increased complexity. The training loss curves showed that models using Med3D pre-trained weights converged faster and more stably compared to those trained from scratch.

In the LiTS challenge segment, the paper assesses the Med3D network's ability to segment liver and liver tumors from a challenging dataset with 201 CT scans. It highlights the difficulties posed by the small training dataset and variability in data quality. The Med3D network, initialized with pre-trained weights, achieved a Dice score of 94.6%, closely rivaling state-of-the-art techniques that employ ensemble methods. This finding underscores the potential of the Med3D framework to enhance performance in complex medical imaging tasks.
