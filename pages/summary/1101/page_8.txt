The paper presents a comparative analysis of various networks for lung segmentation and pulmonary nodule classification using the Med3D framework, as shown in Table 3. The results indicate that Med3D consistently outperforms other networks, including Kin and TFS, across both Dice and accuracy metrics for segmentation and classification tasks. Specifically, Med3D achieves the highest performance with a Dice score of 93.82% and an accuracy of 91.92% for lung segmentation.

The study utilizes the Visceral dataset, comprising 80 volumes with annotations across four modalities. For training, 72 volumes are selected, while 8 are reserved for testing. The networks are initialized using different pre-trained models, including Med3D, Kin, and TFS, with optimization performed using the Adam algorithm.

The findings confirm the hypothesis that Med3D networks, which are better suited for capturing 3D medical volume data, yield superior performance compared to TFS networks, which are less effective in this context. The training curves illustrated in Figure 5 show that while all networks stabilize over time, Med3D networks achieve lower loss values and demonstrate faster convergence compared to the others. This emphasizes the importance of using models pretrained on relevant medical data to enhance performance in 3D medical tasks.
