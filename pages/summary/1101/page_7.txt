The paper presents a study on the performance of the Med3D model in medical image segmentation, focusing on the impact of training sample size and data variety. The experiments demonstrate a correlation between the amount of training data and the Dice scores achieved across various organs, such as the brain, hippocampus, prostate, liver, heart, pancreas, vessels, and spleen. Specifically, results indicate that higher percentages of training samples, from 10% to 100%, lead to improved performance in segmentation tasks.

Furthermore, the paper explores the effect of training Med3D on different combinations of datasets. The findings reveal that training with multiple datasets (up to eight) enhances performance significantly compared to using single datasets. This improvement is attributed to the complementary information provided by diverse data sources.

Additionally, the study investigates the transfer learning capabilities of Med3D using the 3DSeg-8 dataset, which includes organs and tissues from different datasets with varying scales. Experiments conducted with the lung segmentation tasks, utilizing the Visceral dataset and LIDC dataset, confirm that the pre-trained Med3D network generalizes well to unseen data, outperforming models trained from scratch.

Overall, the results underscore the importance of both training data magnitude and variety in enhancing the segmentation performance of the Med3D model.
