The paper introduces 3D-PointCapsNet, a novel deep learning architecture for processing 3D point clouds, which enhances existing methods like PointNet, FoldingNet, and AtlasNet. PointNet operates with a single latent vector and lacks a grid structure, while FoldingNet employs a two-stage MLP to warp a fixed grid. AtlasNet advances this by utilizing multiple MLPs to reconstruct different local surface patches, thus improving learning capacity. However, these methods are limited by their reliance on a single latent feature vector.

The authors argue that point clouds consist of multiple basis functions, necessitating a set of latent features to capture these distinct representations. This leads to the development of a new decoder formulation, which allows for multiple latent representations that can fold distinct 2D grids onto specific local patches.

The architecture of 3D-PointCapsNet is described as follows: it begins with an encoder that processes an N×d point cloud, using a point-wise MLP to extract local feature maps. These maps are then fed into multiple independent convolutional layers to promote diverse learning, followed by max-pooling to create a global latent representation. This representation is organized into primary point capsules, which are further enhanced using dynamic routing to form higher-level latent capsules.

The paper emphasizes the potential of capsule networks to learn collections of capsules through dynamic routing, motivating the design of 3D-PointCapsNet as a promising approach for point cloud auto-encoding, ultimately unifying the methodologies of existing architectures while addressing their limitations.
