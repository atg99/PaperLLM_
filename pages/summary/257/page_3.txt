The paper introduces a novel approach to 3D point cloud processing through the development of a capsule network specifically designed for this purpose, termed 3D PointCapsNet. This network utilizes the sparse and unstructured characteristics of 3D point clouds to effectively represent 3D surfaces. The architecture comprises a capsule-encoder that processes an input point cloud, generating features through a multi-layer perceptron (MLP) and independent convolutional layers. These features are pooled and clustered into primary point capsules, which are then refined through dynamic routing into latent capsules.

The decoder reconstructs point sets from the latent capsules by generating multiple point patches targeting different regions of the shape. This reconstruction process is guided by minimizing the Chamfer distance between the generated point cloud and the input. The authors assert that their approach is the first to leverage capsule networks in the context of 3D point clouds, highlighting its advantages over previous models.

The methodology begins with foundational concepts related to 3D surfaces and point clouds, defining terms such as diffeomorphism, charts, and parameterization. The paper formulates the problem of learning to generate 2-manifolds and establishes the theoretical framework supporting the reconstruction capabilities of MLPs. The authors emphasize the importance of selecting appropriate parameterization functions and distance metrics for effective reconstruction, setting the stage for their contribution to 3D auto-encoders.
