The study evaluates the performance of four natural language understanding (NLU) systems—IBM Watson, Dialogflow, Rasa, and LUIS—by analyzing their confidence scores when classifying intents from queries related to two tasks: Repository and Stack Overflow.

The methodology involved querying each NLU and recording the highest confidence score associated with the correct intent classification. The analysis aimed to determine the reliability of these confidence scores by comparing their distributions for correctly and incorrectly classified intents. Ideally, a reliable NLU should exhibit high confidence scores for correct classifications and low scores for incorrect ones, with distinct score distributions for each.

The results indicated that Rasa consistently achieved the highest median confidence scores, exceeding 0.91 for both tasks, along with the most compact score distribution, indicating a strong correlation between high confidence scores and correct classifications. Statistical analysis using the Mann-Whitney U test confirmed significant differences in confidence scores between correctly and incorrectly classified intents for all NLUs, except for Dialogflow in the Repository task.

Overall, the findings suggest that developers can generally trust the confidence scores provided by these NLUs to determine the correctness of intent classification, aiding in the decision-making process for chatbot fallback actions when intent determination fails.
