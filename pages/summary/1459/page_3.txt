The study aims to investigate the performance of various Natural Language Understanding (NLU) systems in classifying intents, extracting entities, and assessing confidence scores, which are crucial for enhancing chatbot interactions. Misclassifications of intents lead to fundamental misunderstandings, while errors in entity recognition result in the chatbot providing irrelevant information. A confidence score, ranging from 0 to 1, indicates the NLU's certainty in its classifications, guiding chatbot responses or triggering fallback intents when scores fall below a certain threshold. 

The paper evaluates four popular NLUs: IBM Watson, Dialogflow, Rasa, and LUIS, chosen for their widespread use and integration capabilities. Each NLU has unique features, such as prebuilt models, language support, and deployment options, which influence their performance. The evaluation uses two distinct data corpora: a Repository corpus for queries related to software repositories and a Stack Overflow corpus for development-related questions. This setup aims to provide insights into the effectiveness of each NLU in real-world applications, thereby contributing to the optimization of chatbot functionalities.
