Three key factors influencing NLU performance are identified: the number of training samples, the presence of exclusive words in intents, and the inclusion of distinct entity types in queries. Intents with more training data, like LookingForCodeSample, tend to have higher classification accuracy. Intents with exclusive words, such as 'fail' and 'crash' in FacingError, also show improved performance. Additionally, intents with unique entity types, like FixCommit and BuggyCommit, benefit from better classification due to the NLUs' ability to leverage these entities.

Overall, the findings highlight that while IBM Watson leads in intent classification, the effectiveness of NLUs is significantly affected by the characteristics of the intents, including training sample size, word exclusivity, and distinct entity types.
