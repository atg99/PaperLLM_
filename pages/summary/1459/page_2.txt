This paper presents the first study assessing the performance of various Natural Language Understanding (NLU) systems in the context of software engineering (SE) tasks, particularly focusing on chatbot development. It evaluates four widely-used NLUs: IBM Watson, Google Dialogflow, Rasa, and Microsoft LUIS across two key SE tasks: exploring repository data and answering technical questions from Stack Overflow.

The evaluation criteria include intent classification accuracy, confidence scores for correct and incorrect classifications, and entity extraction performance. Results indicate that IBM Watson excels in overall performance with an F1-measure exceeding 84% for intent classification. However, Rasa shows the highest median confidence score (over 0.91) for correctly classified intents. Performance in entity extraction varies, with LUIS leading for repository tasks (F1-measure of 93.7%) and IBM Watson for Stack Overflow (F1-measure of 68.5%).

The study highlights that each NLU has unique strengths across different tasks, emphasizing the need for a nuanced approach when selecting an NLU based on specific requirements. It also explores the impact of confidence score thresholds on classification performance and provides actionable recommendations for chatbot practitioners aiming to enhance NLU effectiveness. Additionally, the paper contributes a publicly available labeled dataset to facilitate future research in this domain.

The structure of the paper includes sections on chatbot definitions and terminology, the methodology for evaluating NLU performance, detailed results, discussions on findings and recommendations, related work, threats to validity, and a concluding summary.
