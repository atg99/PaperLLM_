This paper discusses the development and evaluation of a Natural Language Understanding (NLU) platform designed for interacting with developers about software repository issues, specifically focusing on bug tracking and fixing. The interaction between users and the chatbot is highlighted through examples, such as queries related to specific Jira tickets.

The paper categorizes various intents that the chatbot can recognize, detailing their definitions and distribution in training and test datasets. A total of ten intents are identified, including BuggyCommitsByDate, FixCommit, and OverloadedDev, with varying percentages of representation in training and testing sets. These intents reflect common queries developers and project managers might have regarding the state of a project repository.

Additionally, the paper emphasizes the significance of using two distinct tasks—Repository and Stack Overflow discussions—to better evaluate the NLU's performance across different contexts. The Repository task focuses on project-specific inquiries, while the Stack Overflow task leverages a diverse corpus of developer discussions from a popular Q&A platform.

The entity distribution within the Repository task is also presented, showcasing different entity types such as FileName, JiraTicket, and CommitHash, which are crucial for the chatbot's functionality. The findings suggest that the NLU model can effectively address realistic developer queries, enhancing its applicability in software engineering environments.
