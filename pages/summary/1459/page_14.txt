The paper evaluates the performance of four widely-used Natural Language Understanding (NLU) systems—IBM Watson, Google Dialogflow, Rasa, and Microsoft LUIS—in the context of software engineering chatbots. The study highlights the importance of selecting an appropriate NLU for effective chatbot functionality, particularly focusing on intents classification, confidence scores, and entity extraction across two tasks derived from Repository and Stack Overflow contexts.

Despite potential limitations in the MSRBot dataset, which may not fully represent the diversity of real-world queries, the selection of questions is based on common inquiries from software practitioners. The study addresses replicability validity by providing scripts and datasets to facilitate future research, acknowledging that changes in NLU implementations could affect results over time.

The findings indicate that IBM Watson performs best overall in intents classification, while Rasa is noted for its trustworthy confidence scores. Both LUIS and IBM Watson excel in entity extraction, with results emphasizing the impact of training sample quantity and unique entities on NLU performance. The paper calls for further investigation into NLU performance across diverse datasets and tasks, suggesting that enhanced training through dataset augmentation could improve outcomes.

In conclusion, the research serves as a foundation for chatbot practitioners in selecting suitable NLUs for software engineering tasks and encourages ongoing studies to benchmark NLU systems in this domain. The dataset used in the study is made publicly available to support future research efforts.
