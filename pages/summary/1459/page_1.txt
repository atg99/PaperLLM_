This paper evaluates and compares four Natural Language Understanding (NLU) platforms—IBM Watson, Google Dialogflow, Rasa, and Microsoft LUIS—for their effectiveness in powering chatbots within the Software Engineering (SE) domain. The motivation behind this study stems from the growing utilization of chatbots in SE, facilitating natural language interactions and automating routine tasks. 

The authors analyze the performance of these NLUs based on three criteria: intent classification, stability of confidence scores, and entity extraction, using two datasets that reflect common tasks in SE—interacting with chatbots about software repositories and querying development questions on Q&A forums like Stack Overflow. The findings indicate that IBM Watson excels overall, achieving the highest F1-measure for intent classification at over 84%. However, Rasa surpasses the others in terms of confidence scores, with a median score greater than 0.91. For entity extraction, both IBM Watson and Microsoft LUIS demonstrate superior performance across the SE tasks.

The results of this study provide valuable insights for software engineering practitioners in selecting the most appropriate NLU for their chatbot implementations, addressing a significant gap in the existing literature regarding the performance of NLUs in the specialized context of Software Engineering.
