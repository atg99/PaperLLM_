Each category includes various examples, such as Java for Programming Languages and Maven for Frameworks. The study also highlights the importance of semantic understanding in user queries, with various intents representing different ways users may ask similar questions. 

The Repository corpus, used for training a chatbot, includes labeled questions defined by developers, while the Stack Overflow corpus, which lacks intent labeling, required manual classification of intents through thematic analysis. This involved categorizing queries into 19 initial intents, which were later refined by merging similar categories to ensure a sufficient number of examples for training. 

The validation of the intent classification was conducted by two additional annotators, achieving substantial inter-rater agreement as indicated by Cohen's Kappa coefficients. The final dataset for evaluation consisted of 215 queries, with five intents clearly defined, such as FacingError, which pertains to questions seeking solutions for encountered errors. This structured approach aims to enhance the performance of Natural Language Understanding systems in recognizing and responding to software engineering-related inquiries.
