The paper provides a comprehensive tutorial and survey on Boltzmann Machines (BM), RBMs, and Deep Belief Networks (DBNs). It begins by addressing the historical challenges faced by neural networks, particularly the vanishing gradient problem that limited the depth of networks. This issue was notably mitigated by the introduction of RBMs, which Hinton and collaborators advanced through a maximum likelihood estimation training method. The RBM's structure enables it to serve as a generative model, allowing for the sampling of new data points.

In 2006, Hinton proposed the DBN, a stack of RBMs trained using a greedy layer-wise approach. This innovation provided a robust initialization for backpropagation, overcoming the vanishing gradient problem and enabling deeper networks capable of handling more complex data. The paper discusses various applications of DBNs, including speech and action recognition.

Furthermore, the paper highlights the emergence of two pivotal techniques: the ReLU activation function and dropout regularization, which further enhanced the performance of neural networks by preventing overfitting and addressing gradient issues without relying solely on RBM pre-training.

The remainder of the paper is structured as follows: it reviews background concepts such as probabilistic graphical models and Gibbs sampling in Section 2, details the structure and training of BMs and RBMs in Section 3, discusses different unit states in RBMs in Section 4, and explains Conditional RBMs and DBNs in Sections 5 and 6. The paper concludes with Section 7, summarizing the insights presented throughout.

Overall, this paper serves as a valuable resource for understanding the evolution and mechanics of BMs, RBMs, and DBNs in the context of deep learning. It assumes the reader has foundational knowledge in calculus, probability, linear algebra, and optimization, while providing necessary background on statistical physics.
