This paper serves as a tutorial and survey on Boltzmann Machines (BM), Restricted Boltzmann Machines (RBM), and Deep Belief Networks (DBN). It begins with foundational concepts in probabilistic graphical models, including Markov random fields, Gibbs sampling, and the Ising model, which are essential for understanding the subsequent discussions on BMs and RBMs. The authors detail the structures of BMs and RBMs, explaining the conditional distributions of visible and hidden variables, as well as the Gibbs sampling process used for variable generation. They also cover the training of BMs and RBMs through maximum likelihood estimation and contrastive divergence, alongside various discrete and continuous distributions applicable to the models.

The paper introduces conditional RBMs and elaborates on their training methods. It culminates with an explanation of deep belief networks as a hierarchical arrangement of RBM models. The paper asserts the relevance of Boltzmann machines across diverse fields such as data science, statistics, neural computation, and statistical physics.

The historical context of the Boltzmann distribution is presented, tracing its evolution from early statistical physics applications to its adaptation in neural networks, specifically through the work of Hinton et al. on BMs and RBMs. The paper contrasts the learning methodologies used in Hopfield networks with those in BMs and RBMs, highlighting the advantages of maximum likelihood estimation over the Hebbian learning approach. It also references the development of backpropagation as a significant advancement for training neural networks, enhancing their ability to generalize beyond training data.
