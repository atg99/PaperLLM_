The paper discusses the log-likelihood of visible data in the context of Restricted Boltzmann Machines (RBMs) and Deep Belief Networks (DBNs). It begins by representing the log-likelihood as a sum over the visible data, where the contribution of each visible unit is expressed through the expectation of hidden variables. The use of Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE) for parameter optimization is highlighted, with the log-likelihood's derivative with respect to the parameters being derived.

The first term of the derivative involves the expectation of the gradient of the energy function conditioned on the visible data, while the second term involves a similar expectation over all possible visible data. The resulting expression for the derivative is a combination of these two expectations, indicating that parameter updates require both data and model expectations.

Since a closed-form solution is not feasible, the paper suggests using iterative gradient ascent for parameter learning. The derivatives for each parameter (weights, biases, and hidden units) are derived, showing how they relate to the expectations of the visible and hidden variables. The paper concludes with the definition of the expectation for the hidden variables given the visible data, setting the stage for practical implementation of the RBM framework.
