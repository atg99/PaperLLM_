The paper discusses the mathematical framework of Restricted Boltzmann Machines (RBMs) and Deep Belief Networks (DBNs), focusing on the distributions of visible and hidden variables. It begins by establishing that the conditional distributions can be chosen from the exponential family and explores different cases based on whether the variables have discrete or continuous values.

In the case of binary states, both hidden and visible variables are binary, leading to simplified joint probabilities. The conditional probabilities are expressed using the sigmoid function, which allows for the sampling of hidden states based on visible states. The paper provides equations that demonstrate how these probabilities are derived and emphasizes the conditional independence of the units, allowing the joint distribution to be expressed as a product of individual conditional distributions.

For continuous values, the hidden units are represented as continuous variables, and the paper discusses how to derive the corresponding conditional probabilities. It presents the relationship between the hidden and visible variables using a softmax function that approximates a Gaussian distribution.

Overall, the paper provides a comprehensive tutorial on the mathematical underpinnings of RBMs and DBNs, detailing how to model the relationships between hidden and visible units through both discrete and continuous frameworks.
