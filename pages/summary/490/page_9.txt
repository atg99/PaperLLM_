The paper discusses Restricted Boltzmann Machines (RBMs) and Boltzmann Machines (BMs), focusing on their training algorithms and distribution modeling. It presents the gradients for RBM training through contrastive divergence, emphasizing the importance of Gibbs sampling in obtaining approximations that lead to convergence. The gradients are derived from the expected values over data and reconstructions, demonstrating the relationship between observed and hidden variables.

For BMs, the paper highlights their more complex structure with additional links and weights, which are also trained using gradient descent. The gradients for updating these additional parameters are introduced, maintaining a similar form to those for RBMs.

The paper further explores the distributions of visible and hidden variables, suggesting that they can be modeled using exponential family distributions. It formulates the joint distribution of visible and hidden variables, incorporating a quadratic term for cross-interaction, and establishes the conditional independence of these variables. This allows for the conditional distributions to be expressed as products of exponential family distributions, facilitating the modeling process.
