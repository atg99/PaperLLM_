This paper investigates the capability of generative adversarial networks (GANs) to perform basic visual transformations by navigating their latent space. The authors aim to determine whether GANs can be considered "steerable" in this context. Through their experiments, they find that while it is possible to shift the distribution of generated images, complete extrapolation outside the dataset’s support is not achievable. The degree of transformation correlates with the variability of attributes in the training data.

Key findings include:
- Simple navigation in the latent space can effectively achieve transformations such as camera motion and color changes without the need for labeled data.
- Linear paths in the latent space yield results comparable to more complex non-linear paths, indicating that the models inherently learn to linearize these transformations.
- The extent of transformation is limited and is influenced by the diversity of the training dataset.
- The proposed transformation framework is applicable across various GAN architectures, each exhibiting different disentanglement characteristics.
- Enhancements in steerability can be achieved through data augmentation and by jointly optimizing the generator and latent trajectory during training.

The paper also contextualizes its contributions within the broader landscape of latent space manipulations, noting that traditional methods often rely on linear directions tied to specific attributes. However, this work emphasizes the potential for more nuanced transformations that extend beyond simple interpolation, thereby enriching the discourse on the capabilities of generative models.
