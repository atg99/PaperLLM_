The paper investigates the effects of various transformations on generated images from a model, focusing on shifts and zoom levels. It quantifies how these transformations influence the quality of images by measuring the intersection between the original model output and a learned transformation model, as well as calculating the Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) to assess image realism. The findings indicate that excessive transformations, such as vertical shifts beyond 150 pixels or zooms greater than 8x, lead to unrealistic images, evidenced by increased FID values and convergence of distribution modes. 

The research further explores how the variability of the underlying dataset affects the extent of transformations. By comparing the means of transformed models across different classes with the spread of the dataset, the authors hypothesize a correlation between the degree of transformation and the dataset's standard deviation. The paper concludes that while transformations can be increased, there is a limit beyond which the generated images deviate from the natural image manifold, emphasizing the importance of dataset characteristics in determining transformation capabilities.
