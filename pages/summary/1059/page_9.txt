This paper explores the trade-offs in image transformations using generative models, focusing on the effectiveness of linear versus nonlinear walks in modifying latent spaces. The authors hypothesize that linear walks act as implicit regularizers, leading to more realistic outputs despite undershooting the intended transformations. They present data showing the correlation coefficients for various transformations, indicating that linear transformations yield more consistent results across luminance, shifts, and zoom operations.

The study also investigates the generality of these findings using the StyleGAN model, which separates its latent space into two components, z and W. The authors find that while StyleGAN can manipulate colors without altering the overall structure of the images, it struggles with maintaining geometry under transformations like zoom and shift, unlike BigGAN, which changes semantics with color adjustments.

In an effort to enhance the steerability of generative models, the authors examine the impact of dataset biases through experiments with a class-conditional DCGAN trained on MNIST digits. They compare results from a standard DCGAN and one augmented with additional data, concluding that data augmentation significantly improves the model's ability to approximate target images during transformations.

Overall, the paper highlights the importance of understanding latent space organization and the potential benefits of data augmentation in steering generative models for better image editing capabilities.
