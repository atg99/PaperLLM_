This paper investigates the "steerability" of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) in producing realistic images and their ability to generalize beyond the training data. While GANs have made significant advancements in generating high-quality images, the authors argue that they still struggle to represent the full diversity of the visual manifold. 

The study focuses on simple transformations, such as camera movements and color changes, revealing that GANs often reflect the biases present in their training datasets, which typically consist of centered objects. However, the authors also demonstrate that by manipulating the latent space of GANs, it is possible to achieve some degree of generalization and shift the distribution of generated images while maintaining realism.

The research hypothesizes that the extent of these transformations is linked to the diversity of the training data. To support this claim, the authors conduct experiments to quantify the limitations of GAN transformations and propose techniques to address these challenges. 

Overall, while GANs show promise in generating realistic images, their capabilities in navigating and transforming these images are limited by the biases in their training data. The paper contributes to understanding the constraints of GANs and opens avenues for improving their steerability in generating diverse visual content.
