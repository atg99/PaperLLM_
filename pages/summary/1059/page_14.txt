The paper discusses additional experiments conducted to analyze the performance of generative models, particularly BigGAN and StyleGAN, in matching dataset distributions and the effects of transformations in latent space. 

In Section B.1, the authors examine how well the generated images approximate the variability of the ImageNet dataset. They observe some bias in the model-generated images and hypothesize that additional biases arise from variability in the training data.

Section B.2 focuses on quantifying transformation limits, noting that increasing the transformation magnitude in latent space leads to less realistic images. The authors compute the LPIPS perceptual distance to assess how transformation magnitude affects image similarity, finding that larger magnitudes result in more similar images.

In Section B.3, the authors utilize a pre-trained MobileNet-SSD model to evaluate the effectiveness of zoom and shift transformations by analyzing detected bounding boxes. This quantification is limited to ImageNet classes that contain recognizable objects.

Section B.4 presents alternative approaches for latent space walks, including using the LPIPS perceptual similarity metric instead of Euclidean loss for training the latent space walk. The authors also introduce a nonlinear approach to transformations, parameterizing the function with a neural network and demonstrating its effectiveness through qualitative results.

Overall, the experiments highlight the complexities of generative model transformations and the importance of understanding the relationship between model outputs and training data distributions.
