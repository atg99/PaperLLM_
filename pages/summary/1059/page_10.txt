This paper investigates the capabilities of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) in generalizing beyond training data by examining latent space walks. The authors compare different training methods, including vanilla GANs and those enhanced with data augmentation. They find that models trained with data augmentation exhibit lower L2 reconstruction errors and maintain structural integrity in generated images, particularly when jointly optimizing the generator and walk parameters. 

The results show that while GANs can simulate transformations like zoom, movement, and recolorization, their ability to extrapolate is limited to the support of the training data. The study highlights the importance of training data bias on the steerability and generalization of GANs, suggesting that joint optimization and data augmentation can extend the range of generated images. The paper identifies the challenges of applying effective steering across different datasets and GAN architectures as an avenue for future research.
