The paper discusses the effects of latent space transformations on the generated images of various generative models, including BigGAN, StyleGAN, and Progressive GAN. It finds that while both linear and nonlinear transformations can modify the generated image distribution, nonlinear transformations lead to a greater increase in Fréchet Inception Distance (FID), indicating that these images appear less realistic compared to natural images.

For StyleGAN, experiments in the W latent space demonstrate improved attribute disentanglement, allowing for effective transformations such as color, shift, and zoom. Qualitative results show that the model can localize color changes to specific object regions, supporting the notion of disentanglement within the latent space.

In Progressive GAN, the study notes that achieving visual effects through linear walks requires significantly more training samples compared to StyleGAN, suggesting that the W latent space is more flexible for transformations. However, it also observes some entanglement in transformations, where changing zoom levels inadvertently affects lighting.

The paper further explores the impact of dataset variability on transformation capabilities by comparing models trained with and without data augmentation. Results indicate that models with data augmentation and joint optimization maintain better structural integrity of generated images, while those trained without it tend to distort the image content as transformations increase in magnitude.

Overall, the findings emphasize the significance of latent space properties and training strategies in enhancing the steerability and realism of generated images across different GAN architectures.
