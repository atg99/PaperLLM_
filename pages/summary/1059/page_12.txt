The paper discusses methodologies for learning transformations in the latent space of generative models, specifically focusing on the application of linear walks to manipulate images through unsupervised techniques. The authors utilize mini-batch stochastic gradient descent with the Adam optimizer to train a walk vector across various categories in the BigGAN model, using a dataset of 20,000 unique samples from the latent space.

Key transformations explored include shifts and zooms of images. For shifts, the model learns to adjust images horizontally and vertically based on a defined parameter that allows for controlled adjustments. The training process involves applying a mask to ensure that only visible portions of the source image are utilized in the loss calculation, promoting effective extrapolation from obscured regions. 

For zoom transformations, the model is optimized to either zoom in or out of images, with careful management of the scaling parameters to facilitate both positive and negative adjustments. The authors emphasize that these transformations can be learned without direct supervision, relying instead on the application of desired edits to the source images.

Overall, the paper presents a systematic approach to leveraging latent space manipulations in generative models, highlighting the effectiveness of unsupervised learning techniques in achieving nuanced image edits.
