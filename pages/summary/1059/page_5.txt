The paper presents a method to enhance the transformation capabilities of generative models by utilizing data augmentation and a modified objective function. The authors focus on understanding the organization and limitations of the latent space in models like BigGAN and StyleGAN. They propose augmenting the training dataset by editing images according to specific transformations, which helps the generative model learn more effective latent space representations.

The optimization process involves minimizing a combined loss function that includes an edit loss, which measures the L2 error between the learned transformation and the target image, and a GAN loss that optimizes the discriminator's ability to distinguish between real and generated images. This approach encourages the generator to align transformations along linear paths in latent space, thereby expanding the range of achievable transformations.

Experiments are conducted using BigGAN, trained on 1000 ImageNet categories, to learn a shared latent space walk and analyze how different classes respond to transformations. The paper details the types of transformations achievable, such as rotation and shifting, and highlights the model's ability to maintain contextual consistency in generated images. Qualitative results demonstrate how the model can extrapolate from source images, adapting elements like sky and grass based on the transformations applied. Overall, the findings indicate that the proposed method significantly enhances the model's transformation capacity while maintaining realism in the generated images.
