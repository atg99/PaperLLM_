The paper by Gronauer and Diepold presents a comprehensive survey of Multi-Agent Deep Reinforcement Learning (MADRL), addressing the complexities and challenges faced in this domain. The authors categorize the training of agents into two paradigms: distributed and centralized. They explore emergent patterns of agent behavior influenced by reward structures, language, and social contexts. Key challenges in MADRL are identified, including non-stationarity caused by co-adapting agents, the learning of communication protocols, the necessity for coherent coordination of actions, the credit assignment problem, scalability to numerous decision-makers, and handling non-Markovian environments resulting from partial observations. The paper concludes by discussing trends in recent literature and proposing directions for future research.
