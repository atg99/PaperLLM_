The paper surveys advancements in multi-agent deep reinforcement learning (MARL), focusing on strategies to enhance training efficiency and coordination among agents. One key approach discussed is the concept of experience replay, where Palmer et al. (2018) introduced leniency to improve value function updates and mitigate over-generalization. Following this, Palmer et al. (2019) proposed a method to filter out detrimental data from replay buffers to address coordination issues. Similarly, Lyu and Amato (2020) developed decentralized quantile estimators to manage non-stationary transitions, while Zheng et al. (2018a) introduced auxiliary mechanisms like lenient reward approximation to bolster independent learners.

The paper also explores centralized training paradigms, where agent policies are refined using mutual information during training, although this information is not utilized during testing. Centralized training can be further categorized into centralized and decentralized execution. The Centralized Training Centralized Execution (CTCE) scheme enables a joint policy to be learned from distributed observations, allowing the application of single-agent training methods to multi-agent contexts. However, the exponential growth of state-action spaces poses challenges, which Gupta et al. (2017) addressed by factoring joint policies into individual sub-policies and local value functions. Despite these advancements, issues such as lazy agents can arise in the CTCE framework, where one agent's success may inadvertently disincentivize another's learning efforts.
