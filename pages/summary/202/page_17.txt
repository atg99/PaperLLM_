When the reward for collaborative efforts exceeds individual rewards, agents are more inclined to develop policies that promote teamwork. Research by Leibo et al. indicates that higher numerical team rewards enhance collaborative tendencies among agents. Conversely, punishing the entire team for one agent’s failure, as demonstrated by Tampuu et al., fosters cooperation by driving agents to support each other and prevent individual faults, thereby improving overall team performance.

In competitive scenarios, where individual performance is prioritized, agents are motivated to outdo their opponents. For instance, Tampuu et al. manipulated reward structures in the Pong video game, finding that increased competition rewards led agents to adopt advanced strategies to outperform rivals. Similarly, Bansal et al. showed that adversarial training in a 3D environment facilitated the learning of complex behaviors, underscoring the significance of reward structure in competitive settings.

To address the challenges posed by sparse rewards, intrinsic motivation can be employed, allowing agents to receive denser feedback and encouraging exploration of novel states. This intrinsic motivation focuses on maximizing internal reinforcement signals through the discovery of surprising patterns. Two primary categories exist: one encourages exploration of unknown states based on novelty, while the other aims to reduce uncertainty about action consequences by modeling environmental dynamics. Recent advancements in intrinsic motivation, including its application to multi-agent contexts, have been explored in various studies, reinforcing its importance in guiding agent behavior in MADRL environments.
