The paper surveys various approaches in multi-agent deep reinforcement learning (MARL) focusing on communication strategies among agents. It discusses independent Q-learning where agents learn action-value functions based on observation histories and messages from others, employing parameter sharing through a single Q-network. The differentiable inter-agent learning (DIAL) method merges centralized learning with deep Q-networks, utilizing discrete message delivery that is made differentiable.

The CommNet architecture, introduced by Sukhbaatar et al., facilitates learning communication through continuous protocols, emphasizing the necessity of communication in complex environments. The bidirectionally-coordinated network (BiCNet) by Peng et al. enhances communication among heterogeneous agents in games like StarCraft by using a vectorized actor-critic framework.

The paper also explores targeted communication protocols using attention mechanisms. ATOC, proposed by Jiang and Lu, enables agents to communicate selectively, improving performance in large-scale scenarios. Similarly, VAIN enhances CommNet with attention mechanisms, focusing on relevant agents. TarMAC by Das et al. emphasizes active message passing between agents, while TBONE addresses visual navigation in cooperative tasks. Singh et al. further expand this by analyzing mixed settings where agents operate individually.

Overall, the survey highlights the evolution of communication methods in MARL, showcasing the significance of targeted and efficient communication strategies for enhancing agent coordination and performance.
