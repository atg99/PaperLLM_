The paper discusses strategies to enhance the robustness of agents in multi-agent environments, particularly addressing overfitting, adversarial training, and partial observability. It highlights the use of regularization techniques, such as policy ensembles and best responses to policy mixtures, to prevent agents from overfitting to the behavior of others. Adversarial training methods are also emphasized, where agents face targeted disturbances during training, compelling them to develop more resilient policies.

Furthermore, the concept of self-play is examined, showing its effectiveness in learning robust strategies and approximating Nash equilibria in imperfect-information games. While self-play enhances robustness, the paper notes that agents can still be vulnerable to simpler strategies, especially in high-dimensional observation spaces.

The challenge of partial observability is addressed, indicating that agents often lack access to complete environmental information, leading to non-Markovian conditions. The paper introduces frameworks like POMDPs and decentralized POMDPs (dec-POMDPs) for handling such scenarios, although they remain computationally intensive. It suggests that information exchange among agents can mitigate the effects of partial observability, allowing agents to compensate for their limited knowledge.

Overall, the paper underscores the importance of robust training techniques and communication among agents to navigate the complexities of multi-agent environments effectively.
