The emergence of language among autonomous agents has gained significant attention, with studies showing that agents can learn to communicate through interaction. In referential games, agents convey objectives through messages, evolving semantic properties in their language. For instance, Lazaridou et al. (2017) demonstrated that agents developed communication protocols based solely on their interactions, while Mordatch and Abbeel (2018) explored how agents learned vocabulary and syntax structures through navigation tasks.

Research also indicates that structured compositional languages facilitate communication more effectively than less structured ones, as shown by Li and Bowling (2019). The development of language grounded in visual perception has been a common theme in multiple studies, highlighting the role of social interactions in language emergence.

Conversely, dialogues, particularly negotiations, represent another aspect of language development. In these scenarios, agents negotiate to maximize the value of shared resources. Lewis et al. (2017) found that agents could master linguistic and reasoning challenges, employing strategies like deception to enhance their outcomes. Additional studies in this area include the works of Cao et al. (2018) and He et al. (2018), which further investigate negotiation dynamics among agents. Overall, the exploration of language emergence in both referential games and dialogues is critical for understanding the communication capabilities of autonomous agents.
