The paper discusses how communication capabilities among agents can be enhanced through mechanisms such as attention, allowing agents to exchange information on-demand rather than at fixed intervals. It highlights the significance of memory mechanisms, particularly deep recurrent neural networks, in addressing partial observability by enabling agents to retain and utilize past experiences. Despite advancements, issues like long-term dependencies complicate decision-making and the memorization challenges increase with the number of agents involved.

The discussion section identifies trends in recent literature that could address current challenges in MADRL, emphasizing the importance of reproducibility and standardized frameworks for evaluating algorithms. The authors stress that insights from multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) can inform MADRL approaches, particularly in simplifying complex problems. They also call for greater transparency in reporting hyper-parameters and experimental methods to enhance the credibility of research outcomes. The paper concludes by noting the need for continued exploration of under-researched problems within the MADRL field.
