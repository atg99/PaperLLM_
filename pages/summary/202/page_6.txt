This paper discusses the action-value function Q, which evaluates the utility of being in a specific state and performing a given action while following a policy thereafter. In deep reinforcement learning, policies and value functions are often represented using neural networks.

The paper then introduces multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL), utilizing Markov Games as a framework for sequential decision-making involving multiple agents. Originating from Littman's work in 1994, Markov Games generalize Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) to account for multiple interacting agents in a shared environment. The formal definition includes a tuple that outlines the set of agents, state observations, joint action spaces, transition probabilities, and individual reward functions.

At each time step, agents select actions based on their policies, and the system transitions to a new state while agents receive immediate feedback through their reward functions. Each agent's objective is to optimize its policy to maximize long-term rewards.

The paper also highlights a special case of Markov Games known as the strategic-form game, which involves one-shot interactions where agents simultaneously execute actions and receive rewards based on their joint actions. Research within the MARL community continues to explore this simplified setting, addressing various challenges and pathologies.
