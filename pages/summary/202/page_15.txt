This survey on multi-agent deep reinforcement learning (MADRL) explores various approaches and methodologies that enhance agent coordination and behavior. It highlights how agents can operate effectively with local observations while sharing information through centralized critics or master-slave architectures. 

Centralized critics, as discussed in works by Bono et al. and Foerster et al., improve performance by providing a counterfactual baseline, which helps to mitigate variance in action evaluation, especially in environments with multiple agents. Wu et al. proposed an action-dependent baseline that incorporates data from other agents to further reduce estimation variance. The master-slave architecture, as shown by Kong et al., facilitates coordination by using a centralized master to guide decentralized agents, allowing them to compute actions based on both local observations and shared internal states.

The survey also delves into the emergent behavior of agents, emphasizing how their policies adapt based on interactions and the reward structures in place. It discusses the balance between cooperation and competition among agents, illustrating that while incentives for cooperation can lead to collaborative strategies, competitive incentives may drive agents toward individualistic behaviors. This dynamic interplay can result in complex behaviors where temporary cooperation may yield long-term benefits.

The literature review categorizes emergent behaviors based on reward structures, inter-agent communication, and social contexts, focusing on analyses rather than new methodologies. This structured examination aims to provide insights into the factors influencing agent behavior in multi-agent settings.
