The paper by Gronauer and Diepold presents an overview of multi-agent deep reinforcement learning (MADRL) research that examines emergent patterns in agent behavior. The literature is categorized based on the emergence setting, including reward structures, language use, and social contexts. 

In the cooperative reward structure, key studies include those by Diallo et al. (2017), Leibo et al. (2017), and Tampuu et al. (2017). For competitive settings, notable works are by Bansal et al. (2018) and Liu et al. (2019). The exploration of intrinsic rewards is covered by several authors, including Baker et al. (2020) and Jaderberg et al. (2019).

Language use is analyzed through referential games, with contributions from Choi et al. (2018) and Lazaridou et al. (2017). Dialogues are addressed in studies by Cao et al. (2018) and Das et al. (2017).

In social contexts, the paper discusses commons dilemmas through works like Foerster et al. (2018a) and public good dilemmas as explored by Pérolat et al. (2017). This comprehensive overview highlights the diverse approaches within MADRL to understand how agent behaviors can emerge from various settings and frameworks.
