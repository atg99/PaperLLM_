In the paper by S. Gronauer and K. Diepold, the authors introduce concepts of Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) based on the taxonomy from Busoniu et al. (2008). They categorize tasks into three main types based on the reward structure: 

1. **Fully Cooperative Setting**: All agents share the same reward, which encourages collaboration to prevent individual failures and enhance team performance. This is a cooperative environment where agents work together towards a common goal.

2. **Fully Competitive Setting**: Characterized as a zero-sum Markov Game, where the total rewards sum to zero after state transitions. Agents focus on maximizing their own rewards while minimizing those of their competitors, reflecting a competitive dynamic.

3. **Mixed Setting**: Also known as a general-sum game, this setting does not strictly conform to cooperation or competition, allowing for more flexible agent goals without imposed restrictions.

Additionally, the authors discuss the distinction between independent learners, who do not consider the actions of other agents, and joint-action learners, who observe and adapt based on the actions of all agents involved. 

The paper also highlights a key challenge in multi-agent systems: the non-stationarity issue. In contrast to single-agent scenarios where state transitions are directly linked to the agent's actions, in multi-agent environments, agents update their policies simultaneously, resulting in a perceived non-stationary environment. This situation violates the Markov assumption of a Markov Decision Process (MDP), creating a moving target problem for individual agents. 

The Nash equilibrium is presented as a solution concept, where each agent's strategy is the optimal response to the others. However, it is noted that agents can converge to sub-optimal solutions even amidst randomness in their action choices, indicating the complexity of achieving optimality in multi-agent settings.
