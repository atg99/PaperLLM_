This survey on multi-agent deep reinforcement learning discusses key concepts and methodologies employed to improve agent collaboration and performance in dynamic environments. 

The experience replay mechanism is highlighted as a crucial element for reinforcement learning, particularly in multi-agent settings where past experiences can quickly become irrelevant due to policy adaptations. Foerster et al. proposed two strategies to address this challenge: decaying outdated transition samples and enhancing state spaces with estimates of other agents' policies, termed "fingerprints." These approaches help stabilize learning and reduce non-stationarity. Palmer et al. introduced leniency in experience replay, associating temperature values with stored transitions to manage the relevance of past experiences.

The centralized training with decentralized execution (CTDE) paradigm is another focus, where agents share information during training to mitigate non-stationarity. Various methods are explored, including centralized critics that provide agents with a stationary environment during training while allowing decentralized actions at test time. Notable works demonstrate how centralized critics can utilize joint actions and observations to compute individual advantages, thus improving the learning process.

Additionally, the paper touches on meta-learning, which enables agents to adapt to the behavioral changes of other agents. This approach, referred to as "learning-to-learn," has shown promise in single-agent scenarios and is gaining attention for its potential in multi-agent contexts.

Overall, the survey encapsulates the advancements in multi-agent deep reinforcement learning, emphasizing the importance of experience replay, centralized training schemes, and adaptive learning strategies.
