The paper discusses the Centralized Training Decentralized Execution (CTDE) paradigm, which is a prevalent framework in multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL). It highlights the advantages of CTDE, such as improved learning efficiency through shared information and reduced non-stationarity during training. The authors categorize the literature based on agent structure, distinguishing between homogeneous and heterogeneous agents. 

Homogeneous agents share a common structure and can benefit from parameter sharing, leading to faster learning and more efficient behavior acquisition. The paper cites various studies demonstrating that shared policies can enhance learning outcomes compared to independently trained agents. Additionally, it discusses the value-based methods employed by homogeneous agents, where mutual information is utilized to improve value function approximations.

In contrast, heterogeneous agents possess different structures and skills, which can be addressed through centralized critic methods. These methods allow for individualized policy improvement while still sharing critical information during training. By employing a centralized critic, agents can better navigate the complexities of multi-agent environments.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of the CTDE framework in advancing multi-agent learning strategies, showcasing how both homogeneous and heterogeneous agents can effectively learn and adapt through shared insights and structured training environments.
