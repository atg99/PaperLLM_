The paper provides a comprehensive survey on multi-agent deep reinforcement learning (MARL), focusing on the formalism of multi-agent interactions modeled as extensive-form games. In this context, the value function for each agent is influenced not only by its individual policy but also by the policies of other agents, leading to a joint policy framework. The optimal policy for an agent is determined by its own strategy and the strategies of others, with the concept of "best response" being central to this analysis. 

A best response for an agent is defined as the strategy that maximizes its utility given the fixed policies of other agents. The paper introduces the Nash equilibrium, which occurs when each agent's policy is the best response to the others' policies, indicating that no agent can unilaterally improve its outcome. However, multiple Nash equilibria may exist, prompting the need for the concept of Pareto-optimality. A Nash equilibrium is Pareto-optimal if no other policy provides a higher payoff for at least one agent without reducing the payoff for another.

The survey categorizes the classical MARL literature based on various features, such as task type and the information available to agents. Overall, the paper emphasizes the complex interactions in MARL and the foundational concepts from game theory that underpin these dynamics.
