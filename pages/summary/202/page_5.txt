The paper provides a comprehensive overview of multi-agent deep reinforcement learning (MADRL), beginning with foundational concepts in reinforcement learning (RL). It introduces the Markov decision process (MDP) as a framework for single-agent learning, which encompasses an agent that interacts with an environment to optimize a control policy through trial and error. The MDP is formally defined by a tuple consisting of state and action spaces, a transition function, a reward function, and a discount factor. The agent's objective is to maximize expected long-term performance by learning an optimal behavior policy.

The discussion progresses to multi-agent scenarios, introducing the concept of Markov Games and highlighting the complexities that arise in this domain, such as non-stationarity, relative over-generalization, and the credit assignment problem. These challenges are critical for understanding the state-of-the-art approaches in MADRL, which are rooted in classical multi-agent reinforcement learning research. The background section aims to provide insights into early works that form the basis for modern methodologies in the field.
