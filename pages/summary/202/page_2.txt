The paper outlines the rapid advancements in multi-agent deep reinforcement learning (MADRL), emphasizing its significance in complex real-world applications involving multiple interacting decision-makers. Despite the successes of single-agent reinforcement learning, the paper argues that many real-world scenarios require a multi-agent approach. The authors provide a comprehensive review of recent literature in MADRL, noting the shift from traditional tabular methods to more complex problems facilitated by deep learning techniques.

The review of related work highlights the historical evolution of research at the intersection of multi-agent systems and reinforcement learning. Early surveys categorized multi-agent systems based on agent structures and communication skills, while later critiques called for clearer problem definitions and a focused research agenda. The authors also discuss challenges faced in MARL, such as generalizing to continuous action spaces, computational complexity, and the need for effective coordination among agents.

Overall, the paper aims to clarify the current state of MADRL research and its implications for future studies, acknowledging both the benefits and challenges of deploying multi-agent systems in various domains.
