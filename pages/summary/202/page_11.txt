The paper provides a comprehensive survey of multi-agent deep reinforcement learning (MARL) with a focus on the Distributed Training Decentralized Execution (DTDE) paradigm. In DTDE, each agent operates independently, mapping local observations to action distributions without sharing information. This independence leads to a perception of a non-stationary environment for each agent, as they lack knowledge about others' actions, a challenge first explored by Tan in 1993. Early studies indicated that independent learners perform slower in deterministic settings compared to cooperative agents.

Subsequent research demonstrated that both independent and joint-action learners can converge to equilibrium in deterministic games. However, studies have shown that distributed training methods suffer from scalability issues as the number of agents increases, leading to greater sample complexity and inferior performance compared to centralized training paradigms. For example, Gupta et al. (2017) and Foerster et al. (2018b) highlighted the reduced effectiveness of independently learning actor-critic methods.

The paper also discusses the pathologies faced by independent learners, such as non-stationarity and stochasticity in transitions and rewards, which complicate the learning process. Recent advancements have sought to address these challenges, including the introduction of Concurrent Experience Replay Trajectories (CERT) by Omidshafiei et al. (2017), which aims to enhance cooperation among independent learners.

The paper illustrates different training schemes in MARL: centralized training with a joint policy, DTDE where agents update individual policies, and Centralized Training Decentralized Execution (CTDE), where agents can share information during training but operate independently during testing. Overall, the survey highlights the complexities and evolving strategies within the field of multi-agent deep reinforcement learning.
