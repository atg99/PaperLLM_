The paper provides a comprehensive survey of multi-agent deep reinforcement learning (MARL), focusing on two primary approaches: independent learners and model-based coordination.

Independent learners treat each agent as an individual, disregarding the presence of other agents in their learning process. This naïve approach can result in challenges like action shadowing, where agents unintentionally influence each other's decision-making. Research has shown that while independent learners can converge to solutions in deterministic settings, they struggle in stochastic environments. Factors such as stochasticity, over-generalization, and non-stationarity have led to the development of new learning techniques, including hysteretic learning and leniency. Hysteretic Q-learning employs dual learning rates to counteract value function overestimation, while leniency adjusts optimism levels based on the frequency of state-action pair visits.

To enhance the performance of independent learners, researchers have proposed various advanced techniques, including improved experience replay mechanisms and specialized estimators. However, studies indicate that without these enhancements, independent learners perform poorly in cooperative tasks.

The paper also discusses model-based approaches for achieving coordination among agents. By constructing models that predict the behavior of other agents based on past interactions, learners can better navigate the dynamics of multi-agent environments. Early deep learning applications in this area utilized neural networks to capture opponents' strategies and Q-values. Techniques have evolved to account for the impact of one agent's actions on others, using methods like maximum likelihood estimation to infer opponent policies and address non-stationarity.

Overall, the survey highlights the challenges and advancements in MARL, emphasizing the need for sophisticated strategies to improve coordination and learning efficiency among multiple agents.
