The paper provides a comprehensive survey of multi-agent deep reinforcement learning (MADRL), highlighting key strategies for improving agent performance and learning efficiency. 

One significant approach discussed is parameter sharing, which has been effective in various applications including communication and modeling agents. This method allows agents to leverage shared parameters to enhance learning outcomes in cooperative environments. 

Another important strategy is knowledge reuse through transfer learning. Experience gained from one task can inform and enhance the learning of related tasks. The paper cites examples where agents utilize a knowledge database or engage in peer advice to facilitate knowledge transfer, improving overall learning speed and efficiency.

Curriculum learning is also emphasized as a strategy for scaling MADRL systems. By initially training a small number of agents and gradually increasing their count, agents can achieve better performance and faster convergence compared to training from scratch. This approach can promote improved generalization and allow for automatic curriculum generation in some cases.

The survey addresses the challenge of complexity reduction in environments with numerous interacting agents. Techniques like mean-field methods and problem factorization help manage the curse of dimensionality by simplifying interactions and making large-scale problems more computationally feasible.

Finally, the paper discusses the importance of robustness in learned policies, particularly in the face of environmental perturbations caused by numerous agents. It highlights the risk of overfitting in reinforcement learning and suggests various methods to ensure robustness throughout the training process.

Overall, the paper underscores the significance of these strategies in advancing the field of MADRL and addresses the challenges posed by complex multi-agent environments.
