The paper discusses the pathologies encountered in multi-agent systems during cooperative, competitive, and mixed tasks, highlighting issues such as relative over-generalization, credit assignment, and miss-coordination, which are particularly prevalent in cooperative contexts. The authors emphasize the importance of continuing research in simpler environments, like tabular worlds and variations of the climbing game, where unresolved challenges such as reward stochasticity persist. 

The analysis of training schemes for multiple agents reveals that training is a complex computational task due to the exponential growth of state and action spaces with the number of agents. The authors categorize training into centralized and distributed paradigms. In centralized training, agents share information to update their policies, while in distributed training, agents operate independently without exchanging information. 

Furthermore, the paper distinguishes between centralized execution, where a central unit guides actions, and decentralized execution, where agents act based on individual policies. The exploration-exploitation dilemma is also introduced, illustrating the challenge agents face in balancing the acquisition of new experiences against optimizing known actions. Overall, the paper presents an organized overview of training strategies and their implications for multi-agent learning.
