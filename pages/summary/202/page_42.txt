This paper presents a comprehensive review of various studies in the field of multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL), highlighting key contributions and methodologies. It references foundational works such as Kaelbling et al.'s survey on reinforcement learning and explores advancements in communication among agents, particularly in the context of learning communication codes and emergent language from interactions. The review discusses collaborative strategies, including payoff propagation and decentralized planning, emphasizing the importance of cooperation in social dilemmas.

Several studies are noted for introducing novel algorithms and frameworks, such as the master-slave architecture and hierarchical control in MARL. The emergence of linguistic communication through referential games and the role of imitation learning in coordination among agents are also examined. The paper underscores the significance of multi-agent interactions in fostering innovation and cooperation, supported by empirical results from various conferences and journals.

Overall, the synthesis of these works illustrates the ongoing evolution in MARL, with implications for artificial intelligence and social dynamics, paving the way for future research in this interdisciplinary field.
