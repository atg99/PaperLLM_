The paper discusses the absence of a consistent benchmark for fair comparisons among various algorithms in multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL), akin to the OpenAI Gym for single-agent problems. It identifies four key trends in current literature addressing challenges in this domain.

The first trend is curriculum learning, which breaks down the learning process into manageable stages. This approach allows for scalability by starting with a small number of agents and gradually increasing their quantity or complexity of tasks, facilitating large-scale training. It also fosters the investigation of emergent behaviors through engineered changes in the environment, leading to adaptive behaviors among agents.

The second trend involves the use of deep neural networks with recurrent units, which help agents memorize their experiences. This capability is crucial for managing the non-stationarity introduced by multiple decision-makers and partially observable states, particularly in complex environments.

The third trend focuses on enhancing communication skills among agents. Advances in deep learning enable the study of language emergence between agents, allowing them to express intentions, coordinate actions, and share information. This communication helps mitigate challenges related to non-stationarity and partial observability.

Overall, these trends reflect a concerted effort to tackle scalability, non-stationarity, coordination, and partial observability challenges within multi-agent deep reinforcement learning (MADRL).
