The paper provides a comprehensive survey of challenges and approaches in multi-agent deep reinforcement learning (MADRL). It identifies key challenges such as non-stationarity, coordination, credit assignment, and scalability, alongside various strategies that have been proposed to address these issues.

For non-stationarity, approaches include experience replay, centralized training, and meta-learning, with contributions from numerous studies including works by Foerster and colleagues. Communication challenges are tackled through broadcasting, targeted, and networked communication methods, highlighting works by Das and others.

Coordination is addressed through independent learners, model construction, and hierarchical methods, with significant contributions from researchers like Barde and Jaderberg. The credit assignment problem is approached through decomposition, marginalization, and inverse reinforcement learning, as seen in studies by Castellini and Barrett.

Scalability issues are tackled through knowledge reuse and complexity reduction, with notable contributions from Baker and Chen. The paper emphasizes the importance of robustness in MADRL, citing various studies that propose solutions to enhance the stability and performance of multi-agent systems.

Overall, the survey consolidates a wide range of literature, providing insights into the evolving landscape of MADRL and the strategies being explored to overcome its inherent challenges.
