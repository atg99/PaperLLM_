This paper surveys multi-agent deep reinforcement learning (MADRL), focusing on key concepts such as equilibrium, partially observable Markov games (POMG), and the credit assignment problem. It discusses the notion of shadowed equilibrium, where one joint policy is shadowed by another if an agent's unilateral deviation from the first policy does not yield a better outcome than deviating from the second. This concept highlights the potential for agents to converge on sub-optimal Nash equilibria due to relative over-generalization, where policies perform adequately with arbitrary actions from others.

In the context of Markov games, the paper notes that agents may not have complete information about the state of the world, leading to partially observable environments. POMGs are introduced as a framework for these scenarios, defined by a tuple that includes the set of agents, unobserved states, individual action spaces, observation spaces, transition probabilities, and reward functions. When agents share a reward function in cooperative tasks, the framework becomes a decentralized POMDP.

The credit assignment problem is also examined, particularly in fully cooperative settings where agents struggle to determine the impact of their actions on overall team success, complicating the reward attribution process. The paper cites various studies to support these discussions, emphasizing the complexities inherent in designing effective policies in multi-agent systems.
