The paper by Gronauer and Diepold discusses the challenges and advancements in multi-agent reinforcement learning (MADRL), particularly in heterogeneous environments where agents operate with limited and local information. It highlights the curse of dimensionality as a fundamental issue, where the complexity of state-action spaces and agent interactions grows exponentially with the number of agents, complicating exploration and decision-making.

Despite the historical limitations of MADRL in handling complex environments, the advent of deep learning has transformed the field, making previously intractable problems more manageable. The authors review recent advancements that leverage deep learning to tackle real-world complexities and analyze training schemes for policy learning, as well as emergent agent behaviors during interactions.

The paper systematically investigates the challenges present in multi-agent contexts and outlines trends in the current literature, proposing directions for future research. The authors aim to equip readers with insights into contemporary challenges in MADRL, emphasizing its potential while acknowledging its limitations. They anticipate a surge in literature and encourage ongoing development in this dynamic and evolving research area. The authors also express gratitude to reviewers and colleagues for their feedback and support during the manuscript's preparation.
