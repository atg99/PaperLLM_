This paper provides a comprehensive survey of recent research in multi-agent deep reinforcement learning (MADRL). The authors discuss the unique challenges faced by independent learners in fully cooperative Markov Games, including issues like non-stationarity and shadowed equilibria, and analyze conditions under which algorithms can effectively address these coordination challenges. They critique the narrow focus of previous surveys that predominantly intersect reinforcement learning and game theory, emphasizing the need for scalable solutions in complex environments.

The survey identifies five technical challenges in MADRL: nonstationarity, partial observability, continuous spaces, training schemes, and transfer learning, as highlighted by Nguyen et al. The authors categorize existing literature into four main areas: emergent behaviors, communication, cooperation, and agent modeling. They also reference recent works that explore specific sub-fields, such as cooperative settings and knowledge reuse.

The paper aims to provide a broad overview of contemporary challenges and state-of-the-art solutions in MADRL, contrasting with earlier surveys that covered narrower topics. It serves as an introduction for readers interested in understanding the historical context, current developments, and future research directions in MADRL. The structure of the paper includes a formal introduction to single-agent and multi-agent reinforcement learning, an analysis of training architectures, and a review of literature on emergent agent behaviors, categorized by reward structure, communication, and social context. Lastly, it enumerates the current challenges in the multi-agent domain, setting the stage for further research in this evolving field.
