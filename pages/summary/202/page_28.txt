The challenge of non-stationarity due to rapidly adapting agents is addressed by Everett and Roberts (2018) with the switching agent model (SAM), which learns multiple opponent models and selects the best response based on observed behaviors.

The paper also notes advancements in cooperative tasks and imitation learning (Barde et al. 2019; Grover et al. 2018) and emphasizes the role of model construction in stabilizing environments by clarifying transition dynamics attributed to behaviors of other agents. Hierarchical methods are proposed as a solution to the complexities of coordination among multiple decision-makers. By abstracting coordination tasks to higher levels, as seen in earlier single-agent frameworks, the learning process is simplified. Early efforts in hierarchical multi-agent reinforcement learning (RL) are noted, with Kumar et al. (2017) introducing a higher-level controller for agent communication and Han et al. (2019) implementing dynamic termination criteria in Q-learning.

Ahilan and Dayan (2019) explored two-level abstractions based on rewards for decentralized control, while Jaderberg et al. (2019) demonstrated the capability of agents to solve mixed tasks through hierarchical representations. Lee et al. (2020) applied hierarchical methods in robotic tasks, learning primitive skills at lower levels guided by higher-level policies. Overall, the paper presents a comprehensive overview of strategies to improve coordination in multi-agent systems through innovative modeling and hierarchical approaches.
