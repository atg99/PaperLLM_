The paper provides a comprehensive survey on multi-agent deep reinforcement learning, focusing on the emergence of communication and social context in agent interactions. It discusses scenarios where agents communicate in a question-and-answer format, highlighting studies that demonstrate improved task performance through communication channels. However, it notes that while numerical performance metrics are useful, they do not adequately reveal the communication abilities developed by agents. To address this, Lowe et al. (2019) suggested metrics to assess communication protocols, which Eccles et al. (2019) built upon by advocating for the incorporation of inductive bias in learning objectives to foster meaningful communication.

The survey also explores agent behavior in social contexts, emphasizing the importance of cooperation versus defection in social dilemmas. It categorizes literature based on different social dilemmas and psychological variables influencing agent decisions, such as individual benefits, fear of future consequences, trust, and emotional impacts. The paper distinguishes between commons dilemmas, where agents must balance short-term individual gains with long-term collective interests, and public goods dilemmas, where resource sustainability is crucial. Key studies in these areas are referenced, emphasizing the challenges agents face in achieving socially optimal outcomes in shared resource scenarios.
