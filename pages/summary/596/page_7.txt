The paper discusses deep-learning domain adaptation techniques aimed at enhancing credit card fraud detection, specifically comparing methods for e-commerce (EC) and face-to-face (F2F) transactions. Two primary methods, AugDNN and AdvDNN, are introduced, featuring distinct architectures that incorporate additional features from EC to improve detection accuracy. 

The experimental setup utilizes a substantial dataset from an industrial partner, comprising over 37 million EC transactions and nearly 48 million F2F transactions, with varying fraud ratios. The evaluation metric employed is Precision@100, which measures the accuracy of identifying compromised cards among a sample of 100, aligning with the operational practices of fraud investigators.

Results indicate that F2F fraud detection accuracy is significantly lower than that for EC transactions, with a noted discrepancy attributed to the inherent lower fraud probability in F2F transactions. Statistical analysis through the Friedman/Nemenyi test reveals that the differences in performance among the methods are statistically significant, emphasizing the challenges faced in adapting models from one domain to another.

The findings underscore the complexities of domain adaptation in fraud detection, highlighting the need for tailored approaches to effectively address the varying characteristics of transaction types.
