The paper presents a comparative analysis of various methods for card-based precision@100 in fraud detection, specifically focusing on five algorithms: BDNN, NDNN, FEDA, AugDNN, and AdvDNN. The boxplots indicate a significant relative mean increase in precision for NDNN compared to AugDNN, with a notable difference of 59%. 

The Friedman/Nemenyi test results show that BDNN and NDNN are closely related, suggesting that the tasks they address share similarities. In contrast, the domain adaptation methods (FEDA, AugDNN, AdvDNN) demonstrate improved accuracy over the baselines. Among these, AugDNN is identified as the superior method, significantly outperforming AdvDNN as per Wilcoxon test results with p<0.05.

Execution times for the algorithms vary, with BDNN taking 12 minutes and AdvDNN taking 46 minutes. The findings highlight a trade-off between accuracy and execution time, with AugDNN achieving a balance of better performance and efficiency compared to AdvDNN. Overall, AugDNN is recommended as the most effective method based on the experimental results.
