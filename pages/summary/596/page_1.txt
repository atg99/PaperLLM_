This paper addresses the critical issue of credit card fraud detection, highlighting the significant financial losses associated with fraudulent transactions despite their low incidence. The authors emphasize the necessity for automatic Fraud Detection Systems (FDS) that can efficiently identify fraud across various transaction types and contexts, underscoring the heterogeneous nature of fraudster behavior influenced by factors such as payment systems and demographics.

The focus of the study is on deep transfer learning techniques to adapt classification models from one transaction domain (e-commerce) to another (face-to-face). The paper presents two domain adaptation strategies within a deep neural network framework: the first involves generating additional features to facilitate property transfer from the source to the target domain, while the second is an extension of a method proposed by Ganin et al. 

The effectiveness of these techniques is evaluated using a substantial dataset comprising over 80 million transactions from a major card issuer, covering both e-commerce and face-to-face transactions. The study also benchmarks these methods against three state-of-the-art approaches, contributing valuable insights into the application of deep learning for enhancing credit card fraud detection systems. 

Overall, the research highlights the importance of leveraging existing data-driven systems through transfer learning to address the evolving challenges of credit card fraud.
