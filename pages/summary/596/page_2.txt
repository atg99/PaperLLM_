The authors emphasize that while existing fraud detection systems (FDS) have predominantly concentrated on EC fraud, F2F fraud, although less frequent, poses significant risks and requires dedicated attention.

The paper outlines the challenges inherent in fraud detection, such as the unbalanced nature of transaction data, concept drift influenced by changing fraud strategies, and the complexities introduced by big data dynamics. These factors can lead to high false alert rates and diminished detection accuracy if not adequately addressed.

The authors propose the application of transfer learning strategies to leverage the knowledge gained from EC fraud detection to improve F2F fraud detection. They review state-of-the-art domain adaptation methods and introduce two novel contributions: the development of specialized features aimed at enhancing transferability between domains and the adaptation of an adversarial deep learning approach to address specific challenges in credit card fraud detection.

Overall, this paper represents a pioneering effort to evaluate the effectiveness of transfer learning techniques in the context of credit card fraud detection, highlighting the necessity for tailored approaches that consider the unique characteristics of both F2F and EC transactions.
