The paper introduces GroupViT, a novel architecture designed for zero-shot semantic segmentation using text supervision. This model effectively groups visual information into segments of arbitrary shapes through a hierarchical structure, progressively merging smaller segments into larger ones to yield distinct semantic groups for images. 

The paper discusses representation learning with text supervision, highlighting its success in various downstream tasks, such as visual question answering and reasoning. Notably, it references previous work where ConvNets were pre-trained on image captioning and then fine-tuned for tasks like object detection. Radford et al.'s contrastive learning method is also mentioned, which allows for zero-shot transfer to ImageNet classification without fine-tuning. GroupViT extends this concept by exploring zero-shot transfer specifically for semantic segmentation tasks solely with text supervision.

Visual grounding, which focuses on establishing correspondence between image regions and text, is contrasted with GroupViT's approach. While visual grounding often relies on curated datasets and bounding box annotations, GroupViT utilizes large-scale noisy image-text pairs from the web, enabling a bottom-up mechanism for visual grouping without needing external annotations.

The paper further discusses semantic segmentation methods with reduced supervision, including few-shot, active, and weakly-supervised learning. Unlike these approaches, GroupViT eliminates the need for human annotations entirely, demonstrating the ability to generalize to any category set in a zero-shot manner.

The architecture of GroupViT employs a hierarchical grouping Transformer that leverages global self-attention to partition images into progressively larger groups. The model's training involves contrastive losses applied to image-text pairs, facilitating the transfer to zero-shot semantic segmentation without additional fine-tuning.

In summary, GroupViT presents an innovative approach to semantic segmentation that harnesses the power of text supervision and a novel grouping mechanism, significantly reducing reliance on traditional annotation methods.
