The paper presents GroupViT, a novel architecture designed for visual processing using a hierarchical grouping approach. GroupViT organizes Transformer layers into stages that progressively handle larger visual segments. Each grouping stage begins with pixel-level segments that are merged into object parts, such as the legs and noses of elephants, and then further combines these into complete objects, including the elephant and its background.

The architecture features a Grouping Block at each stage, which computes the similarity between learned group tokens and image segment tokens. This is achieved through a Gumbel-softmax operation, allowing for a differentiable assignment of segment tokens to group tokens. The assignment is transformed into a one-hot representation, facilitating the merging of segment tokens based on their similarity in the embedding space.

The process starts with initial image patches serving as segment inputs. Each grouping stage updates these segments through Transformer layers, followed by a Grouping Block that reduces the number of segment tokens while increasing their size. After the final grouping stage, the model applies additional Transformer layers to the remaining segment tokens and averages their outputs to create a final global image representation.

The paper emphasizes that GroupViT is capable of handling arbitrary image segments rather than being restricted to a regular grid structure, enhancing its flexibility and effectiveness in visual tasks. The use of the straight-through trick ensures that the assignment process remains differentiable, allowing for end-to-end training of the model.
