This compilation of references covers various advancements in the fields of computer vision and language processing, particularly focusing on methodologies for semantic segmentation, weakly-supervised learning, and vision-language tasks. 

Key contributions include the introduction of Adam for stochastic optimization by Kingma and Ba, which provides an effective optimization framework. The Visual Genome project connects language and vision through dense image annotations, facilitating improved understanding between the two modalities. FickleNet offers a method for weakly and semi-supervised semantic segmentation, while VisualBERT presents a baseline for vision and language integration. 

Further developments include the OSCAR model, which aligns object semantics for pre-training in vision-language tasks, and advancements in zero-shot segmentation techniques. The Swin Transformer introduces a hierarchical approach to vision transformers, enhancing performance in various vision tasks. 

Additionally, methods like Vilbert and the Few-shot segmentation techniques emphasize task-agnostic learning and classifier weight transformation, respectively. The work on conditional image-text embedding networks and the use of active learning strategies highlight the ongoing evolution in effective model training and data utilization.

Overall, these references underscore a significant trend towards improving model efficiency and performance across tasks that bridge visual and textual understanding, with a focus on leveraging weak supervision and innovative learning paradigms.
