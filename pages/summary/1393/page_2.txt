The paper presents a novel approach to semantic segmentation using a model called GroupViT (Grouping Vision Transformer), which learns purely from text supervision without requiring pixel-wise annotations. This model is designed to generalize to various object categories in a zero-shot manner, inspired by prior work that successfully transferred learned models to tasks like ImageNet classification.

GroupViT incorporates a grouping mechanism into the Vision Transformer (ViT) architecture, allowing it to dynamically form irregular-shaped visual segments instead of relying on regular grids, as seen in traditional convolutional neural networks (ConvNets). The model is organized hierarchically, with multiple Transformer layers facilitating information propagation among visual segments, resulting in semantically meaningful groupings during training.

Training utilizes large-scale paired image-text datasets with contrastive losses, where image embeddings are compared to textual embeddings derived from a jointly trained Transformer model. This process enables the model to learn to associate visual segments with corresponding textual labels, even when only image-level supervision is provided.

During inference, GroupViT extracts visual groups from input images and assigns category labels based on their similarity in embedding space, achieving competitive results on semantic segmentation tasks like PASCAL VOC and PASCAL Context without any fine-tuning. The model reports a mean intersection over union (mIoU) of 52.3% on PASCAL VOC 2012 and 22.4% on PASCAL Context, demonstrating its effectiveness in zero-shot transfer for semantic segmentation.

The paper claims several contributions: introducing the GroupViT architecture for irregular grouping of visual concepts, successfully performing zero-shot semantic segmentation with only text supervision, and establishing a baseline for future research in this area. The work highlights the potential of leveraging the flexibility of Transformers to enhance segmentation tasks beyond traditional methods.
