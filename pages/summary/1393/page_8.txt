This paper presents GroupViT, a novel approach for zero-shot semantic segmentation that leverages text supervision without requiring explicit human annotations. The authors compare GroupViT's performance against existing zero-shot baselines and fully supervised transfer methods on the PASCAL VOC 2012 and PASCAL Context datasets.

The study reveals that GroupViT significantly outperforms traditional ViT models trained with CLIP, achieving a mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) of 52.3% on PASCAL VOC 2012 without any fine-tuning, closely approaching the performance of fully supervised models (53% mIoU). The results indicate that the hierarchical grouping mechanism in GroupViT enhances the model's ability to transfer learned representations from large-scale noisy image-text pairs to semantic segmentation tasks.

Furthermore, the paper highlights the effectiveness of the two-stage model, which shows a 7% improvement in mIoU values when trained on larger datasets compared to the one-stage model. The authors conclude that their work opens new avenues for employing text supervision in fine-grained vision tasks, suggesting that it is a viable alternative to traditional pixel-level annotations.

Overall, GroupViT demonstrates promising capabilities for zero-shot semantic segmentation and emphasizes the potential of text-based learning in the field of computer vision.
