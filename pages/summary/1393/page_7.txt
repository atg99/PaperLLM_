The paper presents an ablation study of the GroupViT model, focusing on the impact of various components on its performance in semantic segmentation tasks. The experiments are conducted primarily on the PASCAL VOC 2012 validation set, using mean intersection over union (mIoU) as the evaluation metric.

Key findings from the study include:

1. **Hard vs. Soft Assignment**: The comparison between hard and soft assignment techniques within Grouping Blocks shows that hard assignment significantly outperforms soft assignment by over 10% mIoU. This is attributed to the exclusive nature of hard assignment, which reduces ambiguity in label assignments.

2. **Multi-Label Contrastive Loss**: The introduction of multi-label contrastive loss enhances performance for both hard and soft assignments, with improvements of 13.1% and 2.6% mIoU, respectively. This consistency in input text format during training and inference aids in better classification of image segments.

3. **Group Tokens**: An increase in the number of group tokens leads to improved performance, suggesting that each group token captures distinct semantic concepts. The optimal number of output tokens is determined to be 8.

4. **Multi-Stage Grouping**: The comparison between 1-stage and 2-stage GroupViT architectures reveals that the 2-stage model yields smoother segmentation maps and better boundary mIoU, improving mask mIoU by 1.8% and boundary mIoU by 1.9%.

Overall, the study highlights the effectiveness of hard assignment, the benefits of multi-label contrastive loss, and the advantages of multi-stage grouping in enhancing the semantic segmentation capabilities of the GroupViT model.
