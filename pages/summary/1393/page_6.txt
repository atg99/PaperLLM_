The paper presents the multi-label contrastive loss function for training the GroupViT architecture, which is an extension of the standard image-text contrastive loss. This multi-label loss is composed of two components, each representing a two-way contrastive loss, aimed at maximizing the similarity between image and text embeddings while minimizing the similarity among irrelevant pairs.

GroupViT is designed to facilitate zero-shot transfer to semantic segmentation tasks. It effectively groups images into semantically similar segments, which can then be mapped to a predefined set of object classes without requiring additional fine-tuning. The process involves computing similarity scores between the embeddings of the output segments and text embeddings of the semantic classes, assigning each segment to the class with the highest similarity score.

The architecture utilizes a Vision Transformer (ViT-S) backbone with 12 layers, processing images of size 224x224 with a patch size of 16x16. Two configurations of GroupViT are explored: a one-stage and a two-stage setup, both producing eight segment tokens after the final grouping stage. The training is carried out on large datasets with a specific learning rate schedule and optimizer settings.

The effectiveness of GroupViT in zero-shot transfer is evaluated using the PASCAL VOC 2012 and PASCAL Context datasets, focusing on foreground class predictions based on a similarity threshold applied during inference. The results indicate GroupViT's capability to perform semantic segmentation tasks without prior fine-tuning, highlighting its potential for practical applications in visual understanding.
