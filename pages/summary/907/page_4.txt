This paper discusses the limitations of the CutMix data augmentation technique and introduces an improved method called Attentive CutMix. CutMix randomly selects and pastes image patches, which can lead to associating irrelevant background regions with the labels of the objects in the foreground. This undermines the effectiveness of the augmentation. Attentive CutMix addresses this by utilizing a pretrained model to identify the most important regions for the classification task, thereby enhancing patch selection and placement.

The authors conducted extensive experiments using various models and datasets, specifically CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100, to evaluate the performance of Attentive CutMix. They trained multiple variants of ResNet, DenseNet, and EfficientNet architectures to ensure a comprehensive assessment across different model depths and sizes. The models were trained from scratch, except for EfficientNet due to its high computational cost, allowing for unbiased results.

Training parameters included 80 epochs for CIFAR-10 and 120 epochs for CIFAR-100, with consistent batch sizes and learning rates. The results demonstrated that Attentive CutMix outperformed CutMix, Mixup, and baseline methods across all tested models, indicating its effectiveness as a data augmentation technique. The findings are detailed in two tables that summarize the comparative performance on both datasets.
