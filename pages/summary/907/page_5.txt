The paper presents a comparative analysis of various augmentation methods, including Mixup, CutMix, and a proposed technique called Attentive CutMix, on two datasets: CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100. The results, summarized in Tables 1 and 2, illustrate that Attentive CutMix outperforms both Mixup and CutMix across multiple architectures, including ResNet and DenseNet, with average accuracy improvements of around 1.5%. 

An ablation study focused on the number of patches (N) cut from the first image revealed that cutting out six patches yielded optimal performance. Fewer patches did not sufficiently occlude the main subject, while more than six caused excessive occlusion, hindering the model's learning.

The discussion emphasizes that while Attentive CutMix requires a pretrained feature extractor, the computational cost is minor compared to the performance benefits. The authors suggest that this method could enhance performance in other computer vision tasks, such as object detection and instance segmentation, due to its reliance on robust feature extraction.
