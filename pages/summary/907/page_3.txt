The paper introduces the Attentive CutMix framework, which enhances the training of deep learning models through an attention-based region selection method. The core concept involves generating new training samples by combining two distinct samples using a binary mask to determine which pixels are taken from each image. This process involves creating a heatmap from the first image using a pretrained model to identify the most discriminative regions, which are then cut and pasted onto a second image.

The algorithm starts by obtaining a 7x7 feature map from the first image, from which the top N patches are selected based on their response. These patches are then mapped back to their original locations on the second image, allowing for a composite training sample that retains spatial relevance. The augmentation process is random, with each training iteration potentially involving different images and patches.

The paper argues that while CutMix has shown empirical success in improving classification accuracy, it lacks strong theoretical justification. The Attentive CutMix method addresses this by systematically selecting the most relevant features, which may lead to better learning outcomes by reducing random occlusions that can confuse the model. The authors plan to explore the impact of varying the number of selected patches in their ablation studies, further validating the effectiveness of their approach.
