The paper "Cycle-Consistency for Robust Visual Question Answering" addresses the challenges of robustness in Visual Question Answering (VQA) models, which struggle with linguistic variations in questions. The authors introduce a new evaluation protocol and dataset, VQA-Rephrasings, which includes 3 human-provided rephrasings for 40,000 questions across 40,000 images from the VQA v2.0 validation dataset. This dataset highlights the brittleness of state-of-the-art VQA models to variations in question phrasing.

To enhance robustness, the authors propose a model-agnostic framework that utilizes cycle consistency. This approach involves training a model to answer a question while also generating a question based on the provided answer, ensuring that the answer for the generated question matches the ground truth answer of the original question. Their findings demonstrate that this method significantly improves robustness to linguistic variations compared to existing VQA models, without the necessity for additional annotations.

Furthermore, the proposed framework outperforms state-of-the-art models on both standard VQA and Visual Question Generation tasks within the VQA v2.0 dataset. The paper emphasizes the importance of robustness in VQA applications, particularly as they transition from controlled datasets to real-world scenarios where users may phrase questions differently. The results indicate that the cycle-consistency approach effectively addresses the limitations of current VQA systems, promoting better understanding and consistency across varied linguistic inputs.
