The paper presents a model-agnostic training framework that integrates cycle consistency into Visual Question Answering (VQA) models, enhancing their robustness to linguistic variations and their awareness of prediction failures. The authors evaluate the Visual Question Generation (VQG) component, highlighting that even when using unattended image features, the model outperforms baseline metrics, although question generation quality declines without attention mechanisms.

The VQG module is assessed against state-of-the-art answer-conditional VQG models, showing superior performance across standard image captioning metrics such as CIDEr, BLEU, METEOR, and ROUGE-L. The study also includes an analysis of failure prediction capabilities, demonstrating that models trained with the cycle-consistent framework exhibit improved calibration, enabling them to better identify instances of incorrect predictions.

The results indicate that both naive confidence thresholding and a dedicated failure prediction module enhance the models' ability to detect failures, with models utilizing the cycle-consistent training achieving higher F1 scores compared to their baselines. The paper concludes that the proposed framework not only improves robustness to linguistic variations but also fosters a greater awareness of potential failures in VQA models, achieving state-of-the-art results on the VQA v2.0 dataset.
