This paper introduces a model designed not only to answer questions but also to generate diverse, semantically similar variations of those questions based on the provided answers. The approach ensures that the answer for the generated question aligns with the original question's ground truth answer. This training method enhances the model's ability to generalize to unseen variations of questions during testing.

The proposed method has two main advantages. First, by maintaining consistent correctness across different question rephrasings, the model can better adapt to diverse linguistic variations. Second, the model's generative training fosters a robust multi-modal understanding of language and vision, reducing biases in question interpretation. The authors demonstrate that models trained using this approach outperform existing state-of-the-art models in Visual Question Answering (VQA) and Visual Question Generation (VQG) tasks, particularly on the VQA v2.0 dataset.

The paper also identifies a gap in the assessment of VQA models' robustness to linguistic variations due to the absence of a benchmark. To address this, the authors created a large-scale dataset called VQA-Rephrasings, which includes three human-generated rephrasings for approximately 40,000 questions across the validation split of the VQA v2.0 dataset. They propose metrics to evaluate the robustness of VQA models against these variations and benchmark several existing models, revealing significant performance drops when consistency is required in addition to correctness.

The contributions of this work include:
1. A model-agnostic cycle-consistent training scheme that enhances VQA models' robustness to linguistic variations in open-ended questions.
2. The introduction of the VQA-Rephrasings dataset and associated scoring metrics for evaluating robustness.
3. Evidence showing that models trained with this approach surpass state-of-the-art performance on standard VQA and VQG tasks while demonstrating greater resilience to linguistic variations.

The paper also reviews related work in VQA, robustness studies, and visual question generation, emphasizing the importance of addressing linguistic and compositional variations in multi-modal tasks.
