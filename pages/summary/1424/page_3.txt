The paper presents a cycle-consistent training scheme aimed at enhancing Visual Question Answering (VQA) models through a novel Visual Question Generation (VQG) component. The approach utilizes a triplet comprising an image, a question, and a ground truth answer, facilitating the transformation of the question and image into a predicted answer. The VQA model predicts an answer based on the original question and image, while the VQG model generates a rephrased question based on the predicted answer and the image.

The authors argue that the integration of cycle-consistency—a technique previously applied in various domains like object tracking and machine translation—can significantly improve robustness in multi-modal tasks, which has not been extensively explored in VQA. This consistency acts as an online data-augmentation technique, allowing the model to learn from multiple generated question rephrasings.

The proposed framework is built on two key beliefs: first, that a model capable of generating semantically and syntactically correct questions based on answers and images demonstrates a better understanding of the relationships among these modalities; and second, that a robust VQA model should produce the same answer for both the original and the rephrased questions.

Challenges in enforcing cycle-consistency in VQA arise from the disparity in information content between modalities. To address this, the VQG model is provided with additional supervision through attention mechanisms, directing it to focus on relevant image regions used by the VQA model in its answer prediction process. This enhances the generation of meaningful question rephrasings, ultimately contributing to improved VQA accuracy.
