The paper presents a detailed analysis of various Visual Question Answering (VQA) models evaluated on the VQA-Rephrasings dataset, focusing on their consensus performance and robustness. The consensus score, CS(k), is defined to reflect the accuracy of models when at least k rephrasings of a question are answered correctly, with results shown in Table 1. It demonstrates that models incorporating a cycle-consistent (CC) training framework consistently outperform their baseline counterparts across all k values. 

The study highlights several models, including MUTAN, BUTD, Pythia, and BAN, specifying their architectures and performance metrics. Notably, Pythia and BAN models achieved significant results in previous VQA challenges, with BAN achieving a state-of-the-art accuracy of 69.64% on the VQA v2.0 test-dev without additional training data.

Implementation details are provided, indicating that models were trained using specific parameters and that the VQA-Rephrasings dataset was solely used for evaluation. The robustness of models is further assessed through ablation studies, as shown in Table 2, which illustrates the impact of adding components such as Q-consistency, A-consistency, and gating mechanisms to baseline models. 

The findings reveal that all models experience a drop in performance when assessed for consistency across rephrasings, particularly for higher values of k. The BAN model trained with the CC framework outperforms all other models, demonstrating the effectiveness of this approach. Qualitative comparisons of attention mechanisms across different models are also presented, highlighting variations in how models focus on image regions in response to rephrased questions. Overall, the paper underscores the importance of consistency in VQA and the benefits of a cycle-consistent training methodology.
