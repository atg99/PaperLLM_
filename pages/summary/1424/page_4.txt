The paper presents a method for generating questions that are semantically and syntactically coherent with given answers in the context of visual question answering (VQA). The question generation module is designed similarly to a conditional image captioning model, utilizing two linear encoders to transform image features and answer distributions into lower-dimensional feature vectors. These vectors are combined with additive noise and processed through an LSTM that reconstructs the original question, optimized through a negative log likelihood loss function. The approach diverges from previous methods by using the predicted distribution over answers instead of one-hot vectors, allowing the model to learn from its confidence in the answers.

The paper introduces two consistency measures: Q-consistency and A-consistency. Q-consistency involves generating rephrasings of questions from images and predicted answers, while A-consistency assesses the generated questions' ability to produce correct answers when passed back to the VQA model. The overall loss function combines these aspects, incorporating hyperparameters for tuning.

To address potential issues with the quality of generated questions, a gating mechanism is proposed. This mechanism filters out undesirable questions based on their coherence with the original question and the VQA model's ability to answer them correctly. Additionally, the paper discusses the late activation of cycle-consistency, which prevents mode collapse during training by ensuring that the VQA and question generation models are sufficiently trained before enforcing consistency.

The VQA-Rephrasings dataset is introduced as a tool for evaluating the robustness and consistency of VQA models against different rephrasings of the same question. The dataset is derived from the validation split of VQA v2.0, containing a sampled subset of questions for which human annotators generated rephrasings. The findings suggest that careful design choices in the training process enhance the robustness of VQA models, allowing them to better handle variations in question phrasing and to predict their own failures.
