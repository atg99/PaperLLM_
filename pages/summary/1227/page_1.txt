This editorial serves as a guide for clinicians on interpreting regression analyses, specifically focusing on linear and logistic regression, which are commonly utilized in ophthalmology research. 

Linear regression analysis quantifies the relationship between a continuous dependent variable and one or more independent variables. The dependent variable, such as intraocular pressure, must be continuous, while independent variables can be continuous, binary, or categorical. The editorial explains key outputs of linear regression, including the estimated regression coefficient, which indicates the change in the dependent variable for a one-unit change in the independent variable, confidence intervals that reflect the precision of the coefficient estimates, and p-values that assess statistical significance.

Logistic regression, on the other hand, is utilized for binary dependent variables, allowing for the assessment of the association between independent variables and outcomes that can take on two values. The effect size is represented by odds ratios, which compare the likelihood of the outcome occurring in different groups. An odds ratio of 1 indicates no difference between groups, while values greater or less than 1 suggest higher or lower odds, respectively.

The editorial includes a clinical example where Sen et al. examined factors affecting visual acuity in patients undergoing anti-VEGF therapy for diabetic macular edema using both regression methods. 

Overall, the editorial emphasizes understanding these statistical techniques to improve the interpretation of clinical study results and enhance patient care.
