The study investigates the relationship between age, ellipsoid zone status, and best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA) at 100 weeks in patients undergoing treatment for macular edema due to central retinal vein occlusion. Results indicate a significant negative association between age and BCVA, with a regression coefficient of -0.33, suggesting that BCVA decreases by 0.33 for each additional year of age. Multivariable logistic regression analysis shows that older age and compromised ellipsoid zone status are significant predictors of achieving a BCVA letter score greater than 70 letters at 100 weeks. Specifically, patients aged 75 years and older have a decreased odds of achieving this score (OR 0.96) compared to those under 50. Similarly, patients aged 50-74 years also show lower odds (OR 0.15). In contrast, patients with an ungradable/questionable ellipsoid zone have increased odds (OR 2.26) of achieving a BCVA letter score >70 compared to those with an intact ellipsoid zone. The study underscores the utility of linear and logistic regression in clinical practice to identify risk factors associated with visual acuity outcomes.
