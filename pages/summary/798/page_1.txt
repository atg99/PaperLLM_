This paper presents a comprehensive study on the performance evaluation and optimization of model compression techniques in deep learning, focusing on bridging traditional deep learning approaches with large language models (LLMs). As deep learning models have grown in size and energy demands, deploying them on low-compute devices has become challenging. The authors highlight the need for compressed models that can operate effectively on devices with limited computational ability and power.

The study reviews various compression techniques, including weight quantization, parameter pruning, network pruning, low-rank representation, weight sharing, neural architecture search, and knowledge distillation. The authors implemented these techniques on popular models used for image classification, object detection, and generative tasks, as well as on various LLMs. They utilized standard evaluation metrics such as model size, accuracy, and inference time to assess performance.

The introduction discusses the rapid increase in the size of deep learning models, particularly in natural language processing and computer vision, which has led to significant memory and compute requirements. It explains the role of quantization, which reduces model size by mapping large values to smaller ones with minimal information loss. The paper further distinguishes between two quantization methods: Quantization Aware Training and Post-Training Quantization, with tools provided by frameworks like TensorFlow and PyTorch.

Pruning is also explored as a method to enhance model efficiency by removing less significant neurons or layers, thereby increasing sparsity without significantly impacting performance. The paper concludes by discussing the challenges associated with model compression and suggests areas for future research. Overall, the research emphasizes the importance of optimizing deep learning models for deployment in resource-constrained environments.
