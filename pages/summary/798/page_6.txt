In the object detection model, we examined the effects of unstructured pruning at different percentages (0%, 60%, and 80%) on the MAP metric, model size, inference time, and frames per second (FPS). The results indicated a significant performance drop with increased pruning, particularly at 60% and 80%, while the model size remained unchanged. The inference time increased slightly with higher pruning percentages.

For language models, we explored structured pruning techniques on XLM-Roberta and BERT-small. By reducing the number of attention heads and optimizing feed-forward networks, we aimed to enhance model compression with minimal performance loss. The importance of attention heads was evaluated based on their contribution to the loss function, allowing for the removal of less critical components. 

In our experiments, the pruned XLM-Roberta model maintained accuracy while reducing inference time, with a configuration of 2048 neurons and 8 attention heads. However, further pruning led to significant accuracy degradation. The BERT-small model, trained on disaster tweets, was also pruned successfully, demonstrating comparable results.

Additionally, we applied the parameter-aware sparse training method to the GPT-2 small model, leveraging both data-free and data-driven approaches to assess weight importance for effective pruning. These findings contribute to the understanding of model compression in deep learning, specifically in the context of transformers and their application in various tasks.
