Structured pruning involves removing entire structures such as layers or channels, while unstructured pruning targets individual weights that contribute less to the model's performance. Both methods are supported by frameworks like TensorFlow and PyTorch.

The paper reviews various related works focused on optimizing deep neural networks (DNNs) for deployment on devices with limited computational resources. Jacob et al. explored reducing model size through integer-only arithmetic operations, while Krishnamoorthi et al. demonstrated effective post-training quantization of convolutional neural networks, achieving minimal accuracy loss. Dubhir et al. evaluated quantization features in popular frameworks, and Luo et al. proposed metrics for comparing AI capabilities across devices. Blalock et al. highlighted the lack of standardized benchmarks in neural network pruning research, emphasizing the benefits of pruning for improving the tradeoff between accuracy and computational efficiency. Srinivas et al. introduced a novel technique for neuron pruning that maintains performance while achieving significant sparsity.

The methods section details how TensorFlow models can be quantized post-training to enhance performance with minimal accuracy degradation. The TensorFlow Lite Converter facilitates this transition, optimizing models into FlatBuffer format. Various quantization methods are discussed, including dynamic range quantization, float16 quantization, and int8 quantization, each with distinct benefits and requirements for deployment on specific hardware.

In summary, the paper presents a comprehensive overview of model optimization techniques, highlighting the importance of pruning and quantization in making deep learning models more efficient for deployment on resource-constrained devices.
