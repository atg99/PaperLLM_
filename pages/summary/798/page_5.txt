The paper presents a detailed analysis of various quantization and pruning techniques applied to different deep learning models, including YOLO v4, FRCNN, DistilBERT, CycleGAN, MobileNetV2, and ResNet50. The results are summarized in several tables that outline the models' sizes, compression times, loading times, performance metrics (such as mean average precision and F1 scores), and inference latencies.

In the quantization experiments, dynamic range and float16 quantization generally resulted in minimal performance degradation for YOLO v4 and FRCNN. However, the int8 quantization led to significant drops in performance, particularly for FRCNN, which failed to yield valid results due to runtime errors. For DistilBERT, while the dynamic range and float16 quantized models showed acceptable performance, the int8 model was impractical for larger datasets due to excessive processing time. CycleGAN also demonstrated minimal changes in FID scores with dynamic range and float16 quantization, but the int8 optimization resulted in a notable decline in performance.

The pruning experiments conducted on MobileNetV2 and ResNet50 revealed that pruning could enhance performance while reducing model size, although excessive pruning (around 90%) led to performance degradation for MobileNetV2. The results indicate that a careful balance must be struck when applying pruning techniques to maintain model efficacy.

Overall, the findings underscore the importance of selecting appropriate quantization and pruning strategies to optimize model performance while minimizing size and inference time across different applications in object detection, language processing, and generative tasks.
