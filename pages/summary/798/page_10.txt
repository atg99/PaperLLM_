The paper discusses the optimization of TensorFlow-based models through post-training quantization and pruning, focusing on their implications for model performance and deployment in edge environments. It highlights that pruning can significantly impact performance depending on the model's architecture; models with many contributing neurons are more sensitive to pruning, while larger networks can tolerate more pruning with minimal performance loss, as observed with the RoBerta model.

Quantization after pruning is recommended for edge use cases, although not all models are suitable for these processes due to potential incompatibilities with custom layers not supported by TensorFlow. The findings indicate that the effectiveness of quantization is influenced by the original model size, where larger models experience less performance degradation post-quantization compared to smaller networks.

The paper emphasizes the benefits of pruning insignificant weights to streamline models while maintaining performance, suggesting that gradual pruning is preferable to avoid drastic performance drops. It advocates for integrating pruning into the training process to optimize model size efficiently, as post-training pruning can be time-consuming. The research indicates that TFLite quantized models perform best on devices with ARM architecture, such as Google Coral and Raspberry Pi.

Overall, the paper contributes to the ongoing research in model optimization, underscoring the importance of careful pruning and quantization strategies for effective deployment in resource-constrained environments.
