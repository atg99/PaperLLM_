This paper discusses the effects of quantization and pruning on deep neural network (DNN) models using TensorFlow. Key findings include:

1. **Quantization**: 
   - Dynamic range quantization reduces model size by nearly 25%, while Float16 quantization reduces it by about 50%.
   - Contrary to expectations, the latency of quantized models can be significantly higher than that of original models due to incompatibilities between TensorFlow Lite and Intel's i9 processor instruction sets.
   - Different TensorFlow versions can affect the size and compatibility of quantized models.
   - Not all neural network operations can be quantized due to limitations in TensorFlow Lite's support for a subset of operations.
   - Input and output shapes may differ in quantized models, necessitating specific parameters during quantization.

2. **Pruning**: 
   - Overparameterized models can be pruned effectively to enhance sparsity with minimal performance impact.
   - Retraining pruned models with original data may improve performance.
   - There is a threshold for effective pruning; exceeding it can degrade model performance.
   - Unstructured pruning does not reduce model size as zeroed weights still occupy memory, unlike structured pruning, which can reduce size by removing entire neurons.
   - Pruning does not guarantee latency reduction, and both TensorFlow and PyTorch acknowledge that pruning may not lead to performance improvements.

3. **Recommendations**: 
   - Quantization is ideal for edge deployment where integer calculations are optimized.
   - Expect significant size reductions in pre-trained models with quantization.
   - Networks like ResNet, MobileNet, and VGG16 can be effectively quantized or pruned using TensorFlow.
   - While quantization slightly affects performance, pruning can have a more drastic impact.
   - The effectiveness of pruning depends on the number of insignificant weights present in the model. 

Overall, the paper emphasizes the trade-offs between model size, performance, and latency when applying quantization and pruning techniques.
