The paper discusses advancements in one-dimensional convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for audio tagging, particularly focusing on adapting the LeeNet architecture. The authors enhance LeeNet from 11 to 24 layers by introducing convolutional blocks, each containing two convolutional layers. They further propose a one-dimensional residual network named Res1dNet, which incorporates residual blocks to improve the model's depth and complexity. The Res1dNet architecture includes configurations with 31 and 51 layers.

The paper also critiques previous one-dimensional CNN systems for their inability to effectively capture frequency information, which is essential for recognizing audio patterns. To address this limitation, the authors introduce two new architectures: Wavegram-CNN and Wavegram-Logmel-CNN. Wavegram is a novel feature representation that combines time and frequency information, akin to log mel spectrograms but learned through a neural network. This representation is designed to enhance audio pattern recognition by capturing frequency patterns that traditional methods might miss.

The Wavegram is constructed using a one-dimensional CNN applied to the time-domain waveform, followed by multiple convolutional blocks and downsampling layers to reduce memory usage while preserving essential audio features. The resulting Wavegram is then processed with a two-dimensional CNN to capture invariant time-frequency patterns.

The Wavegram-Logmel-CNN integrates both Wavegram and log mel spectrograms, leveraging the strengths of both representations to improve audio tagging performance. The architecture allows for a comprehensive analysis of audio data by combining time-domain and frequency-domain information.
