This paper presents advancements in audio tagging using Pretrained Audio Neural Networks (PANNs) trained on AudioSet, which includes 1.9 million audio clips classified into 527 sound categories. The study primarily explores the balance between audio tagging performance and computational complexity across various PANN architectures. A notable contribution is the introduction of the Wavegram-Logmel-CNN system, which achieves a mean average precision (mAP) of 0.439, surpassing previous state-of-the-art systems and Google's model.

The organization of the paper includes a detailed discussion on audio tagging and convolutional neural networks (CNNs) in Section II, followed by an introduction to the proposed Wavegram-CNN systems in Section III. Section IV outlines data processing techniques such as data balancing and augmentation, and Section VI presents experimental results, culminating in the conclusions drawn in Section VII.

The paper emphasizes the evolution of audio tagging from traditional methods using manually designed features and generative models to contemporary neural network-based approaches, particularly CNNs. These CNNs, which often utilize log mel spectrograms, have demonstrated state-of-the-art performance in various audio tasks. The authors adapt CNN architectures for large-scale audio tagging, enhancing their representation capacity by adding fully connected layers and utilizing advanced pooling techniques.

The paper also highlights the significance of global pooling methods and the incorporation of dropout layers to mitigate overfitting. By employing complete audio clips for training rather than segments, the proposed systems aim for more accurate tagging outcomes. The authors have made their source code and pretrained models publicly available, encouraging further research and development in the domain of audio pattern recognition.
