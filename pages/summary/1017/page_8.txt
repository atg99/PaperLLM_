The paper examines various factors influencing the performance of audio tagging systems using the CNN14 model. Key findings are summarized as follows:

1. **Embedding Dimensions**: Increasing embedding dimensions from 32 to 2048 improves mAP from 0.364 to 0.431, indicating that higher dimensional features enhance model performance.

2. **Partial Training Data**: Training with 100% of the data yields the best performance (mAP of 0.431), while using 50% results in a significant drop to 0.406, emphasizing the importance of sufficient training data.

3. **Data Balancing**: Implementing data balancing accelerates training convergence and improves mAP from 0.375 to 0.416, demonstrating its effectiveness in optimizing training efficiency.

4. **Data Augmentation**: The use of mixup data augmentation raises mAP from 0.416 to 0.431, particularly benefiting training on smaller datasets, suggesting that augmentations can significantly enhance model robustness.

5. **Hop Sizes**: Smaller hop sizes correlate with better mAP scores, with the optimal size of 320 samples yielding a score of 0.431, thus highlighting the importance of time resolution in audio processing.

6. **Sample Rates**: The model trained at 16 kHz achieves an mAP of 0.427, closely matching that at 32 kHz, while 8 kHz results in a lower score of 0.406, indicating that higher sample rates capture more relevant audio information.

7. **Mel Bins**: Increasing the number of mel bins from 32 to 128 improves mAP from 0.413 to 0.442, suggesting that finer frequency representations enhance tagging performance.

8. **CNN Layers**: Deeper CNN architectures (14 layers) outperform shallower ones (6 and 10 layers) with mAPs of 0.431 compared to 0.343 and 0.380, respectively, reinforcing the advantage of complexity in model design.

Overall, the results underscore the significance of embedding dimensions, training data quantity, data balancing and augmentation, hop sizes, sample rates, mel bin counts, and network depth in developing effective audio tagging systems.
