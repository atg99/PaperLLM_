This paper discusses the adaptation of various convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for audio set tagging, focusing on ResNets, MobileNets, and one-dimensional CNNs. 

**ResNets for Audio Set Tagging**: The paper presents three types of ResNets—22-layer, 38-layer, and 54-layer—each designed with varying depths and configurations. The architecture utilizes log mel spectrograms as input, employing basic and bottleneck blocks to facilitate better gradient propagation through shortcut connections. The loss function used for training is binary cross-entropy, optimizing the model to predict sound class presence probabilities.

**MobileNets for Audio Set Tagging**: MobileNets are introduced as a more computationally efficient alternative to traditional CNNs and ResNets, particularly for portable devices. The architecture employs depthwise separable convolutions to reduce parameters and computational load. The paper outlines adaptations for MobileNetV1 and MobileNetV2, highlighting their block structures and configurations for audio tagging tasks.

**One-dimensional CNNs**: The paper also explores one-dimensional CNNs that directly process time-domain waveforms, moving beyond traditional features like log mel spectrograms. Two specific models are discussed: DaiNet, which uses larger kernels for robustness against phase shifts, and LeeNet, which employs smaller kernels to replace the short-time Fourier transform (STFT) for spectrogram generation.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the advancements in deep learning architectures for audio classification tasks and the importance of optimizing models for both performance and computational efficiency.
