The paper presents results from experiments on audio tagging using various architectures and configurations, particularly focusing on the CNN14 system and its performance in comparison to other models like Wavegram-Logmel-CNN and MobileNetV1. The results are illustrated through multiple figures and tables that analyze different factors influencing the mean Average Precision (mAP), such as architecture choice, data balancing, augmentation methods, embedding size, training data volume, sampling rate, and mel bin numbers.

Key findings include that the CNN14 system achieves an mAP of 0.431, surpassing previous systems. The Wavegram-Logmel-CNN further improves this performance. The paper highlights class-wise performance variations, showing that some sound classes like "Music" and "Speech" achieve high APs, while others, despite having a large number of training clips, perform poorly. For instance, "Inside, small" has a low AP of 0.19 despite 70,159 training clips, while "Hoot" achieves an AP of 0.86 with only 106 clips.

Data balancing and augmentation techniques are explored, with results indicating that the combination of balanced data and mixup augmentation yields the highest mAP of 0.431 when using 1.9 million training clips. The analysis of different hop sizes reveals that adjustments in time resolution can also impact performance, with a hop size of 320 ms achieving the best mAP of 0.431.

Overall, the results underscore the complexity of audio tagging performance, which does not always correlate with the volume of training data or label quality, highlighting the effectiveness of the proposed systems against existing state-of-the-art models.
