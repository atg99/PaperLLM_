The paper discusses the development and evaluation of deep learning (DL) models for plant disease detection and classification. It emphasizes the importance of selecting an appropriate model architecture, highlighting convolutional neural networks (CNN) as the most commonly used for feature extraction in this domain. 

The training process involves setting various hyperparameters and employing grid search to optimize them. During training, data is fed into the model, and weights are updated through back-propagation until the model learns new capabilities. Model performance is assessed using metrics such as accuracy, precision, recall, and F1 score, which are derived from a confusion matrix that categorizes predicted outcomes into true positives, false positives, true negatives, and false negatives.

Accuracy is the primary evaluation metric in plant disease classification, with higher values indicating better performance. The paper also explains the inference process, where the trained model applies its learned capabilities to new, unseen data. Successful deployment of the model in agricultural settings can occur on mobile devices, cloud platforms, or edge devices. To enhance the model's generalization ability, continuous updates with new labeled datasets are recommended.
