The paper discusses the challenges and advancements in deep learning (DL) methods for plant disease detection and classification. It highlights the limitations posed by insufficient datasets and tedious labeling work, suggesting that unsupervised and semi-supervised models, such as generative adversarial networks (GANs) and variational autoencoders (VAEs), could be effective solutions. These models can leverage normal samples or a combination of few annotated and many unannotated samples to improve training efficiency.

The paper also notes the evolution of network designs from 2017 to 2021, emphasizing a focus on reducing network parameters and enhancing training capabilities with small datasets and field images. The trend towards computationally efficient classification networks is expected to continue.

Furthermore, the integration of intelligent devices like smartphones and UAVs is making image classification more accessible. The paper presents a scheme involving mobile clients and server-side processing, allowing users to upload images for classification. It mentions the "Insect Prophet" product developed by Turui Information Technology, which utilizes cloud platforms for pest identification.

The paper also explores the potential of using electrical signals generated by plants for real-time disease detection, suggesting that features extracted from these signals could aid in classification efforts. An example study achieved an 80% accuracy rate in classifying plant states based on electrophysiological signals.

In conclusion, the paper asserts that DL methods have significantly advanced plant disease detection and classification, addressing many issues faced by traditional machine learning approaches. It reviews the latest convolutional neural network (CNN) developments and summarizes the principles and challenges associated with DL in this field.
