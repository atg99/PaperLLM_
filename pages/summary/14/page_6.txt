The paper discusses the critical role of data preparation and preprocessing in deep learning (DL) models, emphasizing that the quality of input data directly affects model accuracy. It outlines standard data splits for training, validation, and testing, typically in ratios such as 70:20:10 or 80:10:10. The authors highlight the importance of using balanced datasets to avoid bias towards any single disease category in plant disease classification tasks.

The paper identifies several public datasets available for plant disease classification, including those from Kaggle and BIFROST, which provide a variety of images for training DL models. For effective classification, the authors stress the need for preprocessing techniques like image resizing, random cropping, and normalization.

In terms of model architecture, the paper notes that convolutional neural networks (CNNs) are the most commonly used for plant disease detection. The authors describe the process of training a neural network, where hyperparameters are adjusted using grid search methods, and performance is evaluated through metrics such as accuracy, precision, recall, and F1 score. The confusion matrix, which outlines true and false positives and negatives, is introduced as a foundational tool for calculating these performance metrics. Overall, the paper underscores the importance of data quality, model architecture, and evaluation metrics in developing effective DL models for plant disease classification.
