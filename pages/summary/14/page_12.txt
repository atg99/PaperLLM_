The paper discusses various methods for image segmentation and plant disease identification using deep learning (DL) techniques. It highlights the limitations of different segmentation methods, including Deep Learning Fully Convolutional Networks (DL FCN) and watershed segmentation. While FCN offers pixel-level classification, it may overlook pixel relationships. Watershed segmentation is effective but prone to over-segmentation, prompting the development of optimized algorithms such as hierarchical and marker-based watershed segmentation. The authors suggest that combining multiple methods can yield better results and present a fully automatic segmentation approach for medicinal plant leaf images, which outperformed existing methods.

In the discussion section, the paper provides a comparative analysis of various convolutional neural network (CNN) models applied to plant leaf disease identification. A table summarizes key studies, detailing tasks, datasets, methods, accuracy, and pros and cons. The findings indicate that many studies utilize similar architectures, leading to similar results. The authors advocate for new tests with challenging datasets and innovative DL architectures to avoid redundancy in research. Overall, the paper emphasizes the need for diverse and robust methodologies in image segmentation and plant disease detection to enhance practical applications.
