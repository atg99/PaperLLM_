This review paper discusses the application of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN) in the classification of plant leaf diseases, highlighting the importance of accurate disease detection for food security. Traditional methods of classification, such as visual inspections and laboratory tests, are often time-consuming and subjective, leading to a need for more efficient solutions. The authors emphasize that deep learning techniques, particularly CNNs, have emerged as state-of-the-art tools that address the limitations of traditional methods.

The paper summarizes the principles of deep learning relevant to plant disease classification, reviews the latest CNN architectures utilized for this purpose, and identifies key challenges along with potential solutions. It also outlines the future directions for the development of plant disease classification technologies.

The introduction underscores the urgency of addressing plant diseases, citing the significant loss in crop production attributed to these issues, which affects global food supply. The authors reference historical examples, such as the Irish potato famine, to illustrate the severe impact of plant diseases on agriculture. The paper ultimately advocates for the integration of advanced CNN-based methods in agricultural practices to enhance disease detection and improve food security.
