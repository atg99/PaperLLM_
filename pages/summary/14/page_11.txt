In summary, the detection of plant diseases is complicated by symptom variations influenced by the interplay of diseases, plants, and environmental factors. Variations in symptoms can arise from the disease's development stage, the presence of multiple diseases, and similarities in symptoms across different diseases. This complexity presents challenges for image capture and annotation. To address these challenges, researchers can either attempt to collect a comprehensive range of disease images, which is often impractical, or focus on gradually enhancing the diversity of image databases, a more feasible approach currently in use.

The background of images also plays a crucial role in classification accuracy. Regularized image collection can yield homogeneous backgrounds that may enhance classification performance, while real-time images with busy backgrounds necessitate effective leaf segmentation to prevent erroneous classifications. Various segmentation methods exist, each with its own strengths and weaknesses, emphasizing the importance of selecting an appropriate technique for accurate disease identification.
