This paper discusses advancements in deep learning (DL) methods for plant disease and pest classification, emphasizing the application of few-shot learning (FSL) and citizen science. The FSL approach, utilizing a Siamese network with Triplet loss, showed that dataset sizes could be reduced by 89.1% with only a 4% drop in accuracy, proving effective for limited training sets. Citizen science, introduced in 1995, enables non-professionals to contribute to data collection, exemplified by the Plantix app, which aids farmers in identifying plant diseases.

The paper also highlights the importance of data sharing to enhance dataset representativeness in global disease classification studies. Various data augmentation techniques were reviewed, showing significant improvements in dataset sizes and accuracies across multiple studies.

Furthermore, the paper addresses the challenge of nonideal robustness in DL models, where discrepancies between training and test set distributions lead to overfitting. Notable examples include the significant accuracy drop observed when models trained on the PlantVillage dataset were tested on images from different conditions. To enhance robustness, the paper suggests methods such as using compressed models, unsupervised DL approaches, multicondition training (MCT), and continuously enriching dataset diversity. MCT, in particular, was shown to improve robustness in a study involving a tea disease dataset.
