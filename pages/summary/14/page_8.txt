The paper discusses the advancements and challenges in applying deep learning (DL), particularly convolutional neural networks (CNNs), to plant disease classification. Before 2015, significant progress was lacking, but since then, CNN-based models have become the leading technology in this area. The authors identify key issues affecting the effectiveness of these models, which arise from both extrinsic and intrinsic factors.

One major problem highlighted is the insufficiency of datasets regarding size and diversity. This limitation affects model accuracy, especially when tested under varied conditions compared to the training environment. For instance, experiments by Mohanty et al. showed high accuracy in controlled settings, but performance dropped significantly in real-world applications due to the lack of diverse training data. Fuentes et al. also noted that practical applications suffer from insufficient sample sizes, which are further complicated by environmental factors impacting data collection.

To address these dataset challenges, the paper presents five potential solutions: transfer learning, data augmentation techniques, few-shot learning, citizen science, and data sharing. Transfer learning, in particular, allows for the adaptation of pretrained models to new tasks with fewer data requirements. Various studies cited in the paper demonstrate the effectiveness of transfer learning, achieving high accuracy rates in recognizing plant diseases using different pretrained models like ResNet50 and VGG16.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the critical role of sufficient and diverse datasets in improving the practical application of CNN-based DL methods for plant disease detection and classification.
