This paper discusses the limitations of existing gradient descent optimization techniques, namely Adam and AMSGrad, particularly in their handling of learning rates and friction in the optimization landscape. The authors propose a new optimization method called diffGrad, which dynamically adjusts the learning rate based on the behavior of short-term gradients.

The paper explains that the DiffGrad Friction Coefficient (DFC) is introduced to manage the learning rate, where a DFC of 1 indicates no friction and 0 indicates infinite friction. The DFC is defined using a non-linear sigmoid function, AbsSig, which responds to changes in gradients. Large changes in gradients result in less friction, facilitating faster updates, while small changes increase friction, slowing down the learning process.

The update rule for the diffGrad method is similar to Adam, incorporating bias-corrected first and second moments. The learning rate is adjusted based on the DFC, ensuring smaller updates in regions of low gradient change and larger updates in regions of high gradient change. This approach aims to prevent overshooting optimal solutions, a common issue in existing methods.

Overall, diffGrad offers a more nuanced approach to gradient descent optimization by adapting the learning rate based on real-time gradient changes, which may improve convergence in various deep learning applications.
