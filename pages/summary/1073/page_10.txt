The paper discusses the convergence guarantees of the proposed diffGrad optimizer in the context of stochastic optimization. It establishes conditions under which the gradients of the function are bounded and provides a theoretical framework for analyzing the optimizer's performance. The key theorem demonstrates that under specific assumptions about the gradients and the distance between iterates, the optimizer can achieve a bound on the regret over time.

The proof leverages existing results from the Adam optimizer to derive the update rules and show how the parameters evolve through iterations. The analysis highlights the importance of bounded gradients and the distance between parameter updates, ensuring that the optimizer maintains stability and converges effectively.

The findings contribute to the understanding of adaptive learning rates in optimization algorithms and offer insights into improving convergence properties in machine learning applications. The work is relevant for practitioners looking to implement robust optimization techniques that can handle non-convex problems effectively.
