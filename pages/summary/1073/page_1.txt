The paper presents diffGrad, a novel optimization method designed to enhance the performance of Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD) for training Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs). Traditional SGD applies uniform step sizes across all parameters, which can hinder optimization due to varying gradient behaviors. To address this limitation, diffGrad adapts the step size for each parameter based on the difference between the current and immediate past gradients. This allows for larger step sizes when the gradient changes rapidly and smaller step sizes when changes are minimal.

The authors conduct a convergence analysis using a regret bound approach within an online learning framework and rigorously test diffGrad against three synthetic complex non-convex functions. They also perform empirical evaluations on the CIFAR10 and CIFAR100 datasets, utilizing a ResNet-based architecture to compare diffGrad's performance with other state-of-the-art optimizers such as SGDM, AdaGrad, AdaDelta, RMSProp, AMSGrad, and Adam. The results indicate that diffGrad consistently outperforms these optimizers, demonstrating robust performance across various activation functions.

The paper highlights the effectiveness of adapting step sizes based on gradient changes, contributing to improved convergence rates in training CNNs. The source code for diffGrad is publicly available, facilitating further research and application in the field.
