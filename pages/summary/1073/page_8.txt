The paper presents a comparative analysis of the diffGrad optimization technique and its variants in terms of validation classification accuracy on the CIFAR10 and CIFAR100 datasets. The results highlight the performance of diffGrad against several established optimization methods, including SGDM, AdaGrad, and ADAM, across different batch sizes of 32, 64, and 128. It is found that diffGrad consistently outperforms other techniques, demonstrating its effectiveness in minimizing noisy oscillations near optimal solutions.

In addition, the study investigates various modifications of the diffGrad Friction Coefficient (DFC) to enhance performance. The results indicate that the original diffGrad method excels with larger batch sizes on CIFAR10 and smaller ones on CIFAR100, while mean and standard deviation-based variants perform better at specific batch sizes. 

Furthermore, the paper explores the impact of different activation functions—ReLU, Leaky ReLU, ELU, and SELU—on the performance of the ResNet50 model. The findings reveal that ELU, ReLU, and Leaky ReLU yield the highest validation accuracies across all tested batch sizes.

Overall, the study confirms that the diffGrad optimization technique is a robust method for improving classification accuracy in convolutional neural networks, with promising results from its variants and the exploration of various activation functions.
