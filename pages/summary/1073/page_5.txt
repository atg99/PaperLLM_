The paper discusses the proposed diffGrad optimizer, which incorporates a diffGrad friction coefficient (DFC) to manage oscillations in the optimization process. The DFC adjusts the learning rate based on gradient changes, effectively reducing fluctuations near local optima and enhancing convergence towards the optimization goal. The optimization landscape illustrated in Figure 3 highlights how the DFC helps control the learning rate, allowing for a higher rate in areas of steep descent while reducing it in flatter regions to prevent overshooting.

The paper contrasts diffGrad with existing optimizers like Adam and AMSGrad, noting that while Adam addresses the diminishing learning rate issue with two gradient moments, it can still overshoot local optima. AMSGrad mitigates this by introducing a maximum parameter. However, both methods overlook the influence of the first moment on the learning rate. The diffGrad optimizer addresses this gap by introducing the DFC, which maintains a sufficient step size to escape flat local optima.

The convergence analysis for diffGrad is conducted within the online learning framework, demonstrating a regret bound comparable to other convex online learning methods. Theoretical guarantees are provided, indicating that diffGrad achieves an O(sqrt(T)) regret bound while effectively managing the learning process in high-dimensional spaces. The paper concludes that diffGrad enhances performance in optimization tasks by balancing learning rates according to gradient dynamics, thus improving convergence rates and solution accuracy.
