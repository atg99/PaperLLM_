The paper discusses the convergence of the diffGrad optimization method, demonstrating that it achieves a bound on average regret. According to Corollary 1, under the assumption of bounded gradients and bounded distances between iterations, the average regret R(T) of diffGrad is shown to be O(1/√T), leading to the conclusion that as T approaches infinity, R(T)/T approaches zero.

An empirical analysis is conducted to validate the effectiveness of diffGrad compared to the Adam optimization method. The analysis involves three one-dimensional non-convex functions, F1, F2, and F3, where F1 exhibits one global and one local minimum, while F2 and F3 have one global and two local minima. Both optimization methods were tested over 300 iterations with specific hyper-parameter settings. The results indicate that Adam tends to overshoot the global minimum due to accumulated momentum, resulting in local minima traps, whereas diffGrad effectively controls momentum and reaches the global minimum without overshooting, achieving zero loss.

The paper also outlines the experimental setup for image categorization tasks using Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN), specifically employing the ResNet architecture. ResNet, known for its residual units that facilitate the training of deeper networks, is implemented in the experiments. The paper details the architecture of ResNet50 utilized for CIFAR datasets, emphasizing its components such as convolutional layers and batch normalization.

Overall, the findings highlight the advantages of the diffGrad method in optimizing non-convex functions and its effectiveness in image classification tasks using advanced CNN architectures.
