In this paper, the authors introduce a novel stochastic gradient descent optimization method named diffGrad. This method integrates short-term gradient change information from the current and previous iterations with Adam optimization techniques to adaptively control the learning rate based on the optimization stage. diffGrad allows for a higher learning rate when the optimization is far from the optimal solution and a lower rate when close to it, effectively navigating local optima and saddle points through the use of historical gradient data. Theoretical guarantees of convergence are supported by regret bound analysis.

Empirical evaluations on three synthetic non-convex functions demonstrate that diffGrad effectively manages update steps, preventing overshooting and oscillation around the global minimum. Additionally, tests on the ResNet50 model for image classification tasks using the CIFAR10 and CIFAR100 datasets show that diffGrad significantly outperforms other state-of-the-art SGD optimization techniques such as SGDM, AdaGrad, and Adam. Furthermore, employing the mean and standard deviation of the absolute gradient of a batch with diffGrad enhances results on the CIFAR10 dataset.
