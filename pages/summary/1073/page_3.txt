The paper introduces diffGrad, a novel optimization method designed to enhance the performance of stochastic gradient descent (SGD) by allowing a high learning rate while adapting to the optimization landscape. It automatically reduces the learning rate when gradient changes are minimal, indicating proximity to an optimal solution. The method also leverages the accumulation of past gradients to effectively navigate saddle points.

A convergence analysis is performed within an online learning framework, establishing a regret bound for diffGrad and providing a proof of its convergence. Empirical evaluations demonstrate diffGrad's advantages through regression modeling of optimization problems, particularly showcasing its effectiveness on three synthetic complex non-convex functions.

The experimental component includes testing diffGrad on an image categorization task using a ResNet-based convolutional neural network (CNN), where improved performance is observed. Various diffGrad variants and activation functions are also explored.

The paper is structured as follows: Section II discusses SGD optimization preliminaries; Section III details the diffGrad method; Section IV presents the convergence analysis; Section V focuses on empirical analysis; Section VI outlines the experimental setup; Section VII discusses results, comparisons, and analysis; and Section VIII concludes the findings.
