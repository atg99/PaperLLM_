This paper discusses advancements in optimization methods for neural networks, particularly focusing on Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD) and its variants. It highlights challenges faced by traditional SGD, including the slow change in loss in certain dimensions and issues with local minima and saddle points. Several SGD variants have been proposed to enhance optimization, such as SGD with momentum (SGDM), which incorporates past gradients to maintain momentum and improve convergence. Nesterov’s Accelerated Gradient (NAG) further enhances SGDM by predicting future parameter positions for gradient calculations.

The paper also explores AdaGrad, which adapts learning rates based on parameter frequency, and its limitations due to a diminishing learning rate over time. To address this, AdaDelta and RMSProp were developed, both of which manage the learning rate more effectively. The Adaptive Moment Estimation (Adam) method is noted for its practical performance, though it lacks the incorporation of immediate past gradient information.

The recent AMSGrad variant improves upon Adam by utilizing a long-term memory of past gradients to prevent overshooting minima. In contrast, the proposed diffGrad method enhances Adam by considering short-term gradient changes between iterations to better control the learning rate based on the optimization landscape. The paper introduces diffGrad as a new gradient descent optimizer for Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), emphasizing its ability to adapt learning rates in relation to the proximity to optimal solutions. Overall, the contributions of this research aim to improve the efficiency and effectiveness of gradient-based optimization methods in deep learning.
