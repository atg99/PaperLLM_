The paper presents a comparative analysis of optimization techniques, particularly focusing on the proposed diffGrad method against established methods such as Adam, SGDM, AdaGrad, AdaDelta, RMSProp, and AMSGrad. The experiments utilize three synthetic non-convex functions (F1, F2, F3) to illustrate the performance of these optimizers through visual representations of regression loss and parameter values over iterations. The results indicate that diffGrad consistently outperforms other methods in terms of regression loss and parameter convergence.

The hyper-parameter configurations for the experiments include the use of Stochastic Gradient Descent with momentum, batch sizes of 32, 64, and 128, a learning rate of 0.001 for the first 80 epochs and 0.0001 for the last 20 epochs, and a momentum coefficient set to 0.9 for SGDM. 

For image classification tasks, the CIFAR10 and CIFAR100 datasets are employed, both containing 60,000 images (50,000 for training and 10,000 for validation). The CIFAR10 dataset is categorized into 10 classes, while the CIFAR100 dataset consists of 100 classes. The results from the validation classification accuracy demonstrate that diffGrad yields superior accuracy in both datasets across various batch sizes, significantly outperforming the other optimization methods. 

Overall, the empirical results highlight the effectiveness of the diffGrad optimization technique, particularly in non-convex scenarios and image classification tasks.
