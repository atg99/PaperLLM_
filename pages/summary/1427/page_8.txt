In this paper, the authors explore the performance of the B2T2 model in visual question answering (VQA) tasks, particularly in comparison to other models. The findings indicate that while B2T2 outperforms models that lack image or bounding box access, its effectiveness is limited by the model's inability to recognize human expressions and activities. For instance, in specific examples, the model fails to identify smiles or the activity of sitting, which leads to incorrect answers.

The authors also discuss related work in the field, highlighting how B2T2 is similar to other models like VideoBERT and the Bottom-Up Top-Down attention model. However, B2T2 utilizes an early fusion approach by treating image regions as visual tokens integrated with text, in contrast to the late fusion method used by the Bottom-Up Top-Down model. Other models, such as the Neuro-Symbolic Concept Learner and MAC, are also mentioned, with MAC noted for its explicit multi-step reasoning process.

The paper concludes by emphasizing the significance of combining text and images effectively, using BERT-Large as the text model and ResNet-152 as the vision model, with the VCR dataset serving as the primary benchmark. The B2T2 model, which links sentences with bounding boxes, is shown to yield the best results in the VQA domain, underscoring the importance of visual context in improving language understanding and model performance.
