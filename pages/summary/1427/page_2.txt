This paper introduces the Bounding Boxes in Text Transformer (B2T2), a neural architecture designed for multimodal encoding of natural language and images. The model is evaluated on the Visual Commonsense Reasoning (VCR) benchmark, which is particularly suited for testing multimodal representations due to its complex image content and human-annotated questions and answers. The authors highlight that VCR features longer questions and answers compared to other Visual Question Answering (VQA) datasets, necessitating enhanced modeling capabilities.

Key findings from the experiments indicate that early fusion of co-references between text and visual features significantly improves performance on VCR. The inclusion of more visual object features, even when not directly related to the text, and the positional context of objects in images further enhanced the model's effectiveness. Additionally, initializing the models with pre-training on the Conceptual Captions dataset led to more reliable training outcomes. As a result of these improvements, the B2T2 model achieved state-of-the-art performance on the VCR benchmark, reducing error rates by over 25% compared to the previous best model.

The problem formulation involves data represented as 4-tuples comprising images, bounding boxes, tokenized text, and binary labels. The integration of explicit references to bounding boxes within the text is justified as a natural way to discuss objects in images, aligning with the VCR dataset's annotation style. The paper provides a detailed mathematical framework for the model's components, including image and text representation functions, and outlines the dimensions and structures of the various elements involved. Full encoding details are provided in subsequent sections.
