The paper presents a study on enhancing performance in the Visual Commonsense Reasoning (VCR) task by exploring various model training techniques and the importance of bounding box features. It details the training of B2T2 models with linear learning rate decay over multiple epochs, utilizing different random seeds for initialization to boost performance. An ensemble approach involving five identical models trained for three epochs further optimized the results.

VCR, introduced by Zellers et al. in 2019, involves reasoning based on stills from movies, where questions require external knowledge beyond the images. Each sample consists of an image, identified objects, a question, multiple answers, and rationales supporting the answers. The tasks include selecting the correct answer (Q!A), selecting the correct rationale (QA!R), and a combined pipeline (Q!AR).

The authors adapted VCR to create multiple instances for each task, employing BERT's special tokens to structure the input sequences. Their experimental results demonstrated that the Dual Encoder model significantly outperformed previous baselines, even without using bounding boxes. The ensemble of B2T2 models, pretrained on Conceptual Captions, achieved notable accuracy improvements across all tasks compared to existing benchmarks.

Ablation studies revealed that the inclusion of bounding boxes was crucial for improving accuracy, with variations in the number and type of bounding boxes yielding different performance metrics. The findings underscore the effectiveness of the proposed models and the critical role of bounding box information in enhancing visual commonsense reasoning tasks.
