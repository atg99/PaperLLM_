The paper presents a control model that utilizes late fusion for grounding words in visual context, which performs significantly worse compared to other methods that implement early fusion. This finding emphasizes the importance of early integration of visual context for effective word grounding. Furthermore, the authors demonstrate that their Dual Encoder model achieves competitive results on the Visual Commonsense Reasoning (VCR) dataset, even when disregarding textual references to image bounding boxes. They enhance the Dual Encoder model by integrating visual features from the entire image and bounding boxes into the textual embeddings, resulting in substantial performance improvements. Additionally, pretraining the deep model on Conceptual Captions using a Mask-LM loss leads to further enhancements and more stable fine-tuning outcomes. Overall, the research highlights the critical role of visual context in language processing tasks and the benefits of early fusion and comprehensive feature integration.
