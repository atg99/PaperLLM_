The paper discusses two primary architectures for processing image and text data: the Dual Encoder and the B2T2 model. 

The Dual Encoder architecture encodes images and text separately and computes answer scores through an inner product, ignoring co-reference information. Despite this limitation, it performs competitively on visual question answering tasks, potentially due to the use of strong pretrained models. 

In contrast, the B2T2 model employs early fusion by integrating visual features at the level of non-contextualized token representations. This model combines text, images, and bounding box information, allowing for a more nuanced understanding of the relationships between visual and textual elements. The representation of bounding boxes is achieved through learned embeddings that capture their position and size. 

Both models are trained using binary cross-entropy loss, and the B2T2 model undergoes pretraining on image-caption pairs with a Mask-LM technique to improve its performance on downstream tasks. The pretraining includes tasks like impostor identification and masked language model prediction, but does not incorporate bounding boxes at this stage. 

Overall, the paper sets the groundwork for evaluating these architectures and their effectiveness in visual question answering, with a detailed exploration of their respective methodologies and training processes.
