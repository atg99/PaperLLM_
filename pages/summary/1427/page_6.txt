The paper presents experimental results on visual commonsense reasoning using various models on the Visual Commonsense Reasoning (VCR) dataset. It compares the performance of different models, including the proposed B2T2 model and its 5-Ensemble version, against existing models reported by Zellers et al. and others. The B2T2 model demonstrates superior performance, achieving significant accuracy improvements over both published and unpublished benchmarks on the VCR leaderboard.

Key findings include the effectiveness of the B2T2 architecture, particularly in how it integrates visual information through bounding boxes. The study reveals that the model's accuracy is sensitive to architectural choices, such as the timing of visual information integration (late vs. early fusion) and the size of the text and visual models used.

Ablation studies highlight that using BERT-large instead of BERT-base contributes to improved performance, though some accuracy is lost when switching to a smaller visual model like ResNet-50. The effects of pretraining are also explored, indicating that while it yields modest accuracy gains, it significantly reduces variance in results.

Error analysis demonstrates how the B2T2 model utilizes bounding box information effectively, as illustrated by specific examples where correct answers rely on interpreting visual cues from bounding boxes.

Overall, the paper underscores the importance of model architecture and pretraining in enhancing visual commonsense reasoning capabilities, while also identifying areas for potential improvement in representing complex visual features beyond mere object detection.
