This paper discusses enhancements made to the generator architecture in StyleGAN3, focusing on improving translation and rotation equivariance while reducing artifacts in generated images. A learned affine layer is introduced to adapt global translation and rotation parameters based on input Fourier features, initially set to identity but evolving to improve performance over time. 

The paper identifies that visible artifacts persist due to insufficient filter attenuation in lower-resolution layers. It proposes a flexible specification of filter parameters that varies per layer, allowing for stronger attenuation in lower-resolution layers to mitigate aliasing and enabling better matching of high-frequency details in higher-resolution layers. The results show significant improvements in stopband attenuation, leading to better translation equivariance and elimination of remaining artifacts.

For rotation equivariance, the network architecture is modified by replacing 3x3 convolutions with 1x1 convolutions, compensating by increasing the number of feature maps. A radially symmetric jinc-based downsampling filter replaces the sinc-based filter, enhancing equivariance without negatively impacting the Fréchet Inception Distance (FID). A Gaussian blurring technique is also employed during early training to stabilize the discriminator's performance.

The results demonstrate that both StyleGAN3-T and StyleGAN3-R generators maintain competitive FID scores compared to StyleGAN2, with StyleGAN3-R achieving rotation equivariance. The paper concludes that the modifications allow for a more flexible number of layers and improved training dynamics across varying output resolutions.
