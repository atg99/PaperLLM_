This paper discusses the discrete and continuous representations of network layers in practical neural networks, focusing on operations such as convolution, upsampling, downsampling, and nonlinearity. It establishes a relationship between discrete feature maps and their continuous counterparts, denoting discrete operations as F(Z) and continuous operations as f(z). The paper emphasizes the importance of maintaining bandlimit constraints during these operations to ensure faithful representations in both domains.

The concept of equivariance is central to the discussion, particularly translation and rotation equivariance. The paper highlights that for an operation to be equivariant, it must commute with spatial transformations in the continuous domain. Specifically, the authors note that for rotation, the spectral constraints are stricter, necessitating that the spectrum remains limited to a disc with a radius of s=2.

Convolution is examined in detail, where the authors clarify that convolution with a discrete kernel K can be seen as sliding the kernel over the continuous representation of the feature map without introducing new frequencies, thus satisfying bandlimit requirements. The paper asserts that translation equivariance is guaranteed through this operation, while rotation equivariance requires the kernel to be radially symmetric.

In discussing upsampling, the authors state that ideal upsampling does not alter the continuous representation but increases the output sampling rate, thereby allowing for additional spectral content in subsequent layers. Both translation and rotation equivariance are preserved in this process.

Downsampling, on the other hand, requires low-pass filtering to remove high-frequency components to faithfully represent the signal in a coarser discretization. The paper details the continuous operation of downsampling and its discrete counterpart, noting that translation equivariance is automatically achieved. However, for rotation equivariance, a radially symmetric filter must be employed.

Overall, this paper provides a comprehensive analysis of the interplay between discrete and continuous operations in neural networks, emphasizing the significance of equivariance and bandlimit considerations in the design of network layers.
