In the paper "Alias-Free Generative Adversarial Networks," the authors Tero Karras et al. discuss the limitations of traditional generative adversarial networks (GANs) in image synthesis, specifically addressing the issue of aliasing that affects the hierarchical processing of images. They identify that current GAN architectures rely too heavily on absolute pixel coordinates, leading to a phenomenon known as "texture sticking," where fine details appear fixed to certain coordinates rather than being appropriately linked to the underlying surfaces of objects.

To address this problem, the authors propose small architectural modifications that treat all signals as continuous, preventing unwanted information from contaminating the synthesis process. These changes result in networks that maintain performance comparable to StyleGAN2, achieving similar Fréchet Inception Distances (FID), while exhibiting fundamentally different internal representations. Importantly, the proposed networks are fully equivariant to translation and rotation at subpixel levels.

The findings suggest a path toward improved generative models that are more suitable for applications in video and animation, enhancing the ability to create coherent and dynamic visual content. The paper emphasizes the need for a more natural hierarchical synthesis process that accurately reflects the relationships between different scales of detail in real-world observations.
