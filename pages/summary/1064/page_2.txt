The paper discusses advancements in the StyleGAN framework, specifically addressing issues of "texture sticking" and aliasing in image generation. The authors illustrate that StyleGAN2 suffers from unwanted sharpness in generated images, where details like fur remain fixed in pixel coordinates rather than animating naturally. They identify two main sources of aliasing: non-ideal upsampling filters and the nonlinear activation functions used in neural networks, which can inadvertently preserve positional references.

To tackle these issues, the authors propose a comprehensive redesign of the StyleGAN2 generator, emphasizing the need for high-quality upsampling filters that effectively suppress aliasing. They argue that by enforcing continuous equivariance to sub-pixel translation, the network can generate details more naturally, improving the hierarchy of feature representation. The proposed changes lead to the development of StyleGAN3, which maintains performance comparable to StyleGAN2 while being slightly more computationally intensive.

The authors assert that their modifications will enhance the generation of video and animations by allowing details to align correctly with underlying surfaces. They also highlight that their findings contribute significantly to the understanding of translation equivariance in convolutional neural networks, particularly in the context of generative models. The implementation and pre-trained models of StyleGAN3 are made available for public use.
