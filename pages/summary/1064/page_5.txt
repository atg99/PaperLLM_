This paper discusses advancements in the architecture of generative networks, specifically focusing on enhancing equivariance to translations and rotations in the StyleGAN framework. The study evaluates various configurations and parameters through metrics such as Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) and equivariance metrics (EQ-T and EQ-R). The results demonstrate that certain configurations, like the use of filtered nonlinearities and transformations, significantly improve performance, as indicated by lower FID scores and higher equivariance metrics.

The authors explore the challenges of maintaining equivariance while applying nonlinearities, noting that pointwise nonlinear functions do not commute with transformations in the discrete domain. To mitigate this, the paper proposes a method of upsampling to a higher resolution before applying nonlinearities and then downsampling, ensuring that high-frequency content is controlled through an ideal low-pass filter. This approach allows for novel frequency generation while adhering to bandlimit constraints.

The practical application of these theoretical insights is illustrated by modifying the StyleGAN2 generator architecture. The mapping network transforms latent variables, while the synthesis network processes these through a series of layers to generate images. The goal is to ensure that each layer is equivariant to transformations, ultimately leading to a generator network capable of producing images that maintain fine details consistent with coarser features.

The evaluation of architectural changes is performed through systematic experimentation, with the discriminator remaining unchanged. The findings highlight that by implementing these modifications, the generator network can achieve improved equivariance, which is crucial for generating high-quality images that are robust to spatial transformations.
