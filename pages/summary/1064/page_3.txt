The paper discusses the relationship between discrete representations and continuous signals in the context of convolutional neural networks (CNNs), particularly focusing on the concept of equivariance. It highlights that while data in CNNs is stored in pixel grids, these values should not be directly interpreted as signals due to the limitations in representing operations like translation. The Nyquist-Shannon sampling theorem is referenced to explain how a regularly sampled signal can represent continuous signals within certain frequency limits.

The authors present a two-dimensional discretely sampled feature map, denoted as Z[x], which consists of Dirac impulses spaced apart according to the sampling rate. They employ the Whittaker-Shannon interpolation formula to derive the continuous representation, z(x), through convolution with an ideal interpolation filter. This continuous signal captures all frequencies that can be represented within the sampling rate.

The paper also emphasizes the inadequacy of simply storing a pixel feature map, proposing that for accurate representation, one would theoretically need to consider an infinite grid of values. However, a practical approach involves storing a two-dimensional array that slightly exceeds the unit square of interest. The authors advocate for interpreting z(x) as the actual signal processed by the network, while Z[x] serves as a convenient encoding, thereby shifting focus away from a pixel-centric view to a more comprehensive understanding of signal processing in CNNs.
