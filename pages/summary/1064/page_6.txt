This paper discusses the implementation of a synthesis network that employs a random amount of transformations, specifically focusing on translation and rotation, as defined by Zhang. The network utilizes an equation to quantify image quality based on a dynamic range and spatial translation operator, allowing for the processing of color channels independently. The authors introduce Fourier features to enable continuous transformations, improving the Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) while maintaining equivariance metrics. 

The paper outlines a step-by-step redesign of the architecture to enhance translation equivariance while simplifying the network. Initial modifications include removing per-pixel noise inputs and reducing the depth of the mapping network, which maintains FID levels akin to the original StyleGAN2. An explicit margin around the target canvas is introduced to prevent the leakage of absolute coordinates into internal representations, and bilinear upsampling is replaced with a windowed sinc filter to improve boundary handling.

The results indicate that while some changes improve translation equivariance, they may compromise FID, suggesting a trade-off between image quality and transformation robustness. The authors also explore the effect of filtered nonlinearities on the network's performance, aiming for a balance that enhances both equivariance and overall output quality.
