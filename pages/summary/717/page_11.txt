The paper discusses the unbiasedness of the Q-estimate in the context of reinforcement learning. It establishes that for any state-action pair (s,a) and parameter θ, the expected value of the Q-estimate \( \hat{Q}^{\pi_\theta}(s,a) \) equals the true Q-value \( Q^{\pi_\theta}(s,a) \). This is shown through a mathematical derivation involving the random horizon \( T' \) and the boundedness of rewards, which allows for the interchange of limits and expectations using the monotone convergence theorem.

The authors demonstrate that the inner expectation over \( T' \) can be interchanged with the summation in the expression for \( \hat{Q}^{\pi_\theta}(s,a) \). They leverage the independence of \( T' \) from the system evolution and its geometric distribution to derive that the expected value of the sum converges to the true Q-value.

Similarly, they prove that \( \hat{V}^{\pi_\theta}(s) \), the value estimate, is unbiased for \( V^{\pi_\theta}(s) \). The paper concludes with the assertion that both \( \hat{Q}^{\pi_\theta}(s,a) - \hat{V}^{\pi_\theta}(s) \) and \( R(s,a) + \gamma \hat{V}^{\pi_\theta}(s') - \hat{V}^{\pi_\theta}(s) \) serve as unbiased estimates of the advantage function \( A^{\pi_\theta}(s,a) \). This establishes a foundation for evaluating policy gradients in reinforcement learning.
