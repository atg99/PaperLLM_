This paper discusses the formulation of reinforcement learning (RL) within the framework of Markov decision processes (MDPs). It establishes the notation and key components involved in RL, including state and action spaces, transition probabilities, and reward functions. The primary goal of an RL agent is to find an optimal policy that maximizes the long-term expected return, defined by a value function.

The paper introduces the value function and action-value function, alongside the advantage function, which helps in evaluating the performance of a given policy. It highlights the challenge of solving the optimization problem of maximizing the value function due to its nonconvexity concerning policy parameters. This nonconvex nature implies that finding a global optimum is NP-hard, with most approaches converging to stationary points—either local optima or saddle points.

The authors emphasize the importance of developing methods to escape saddle points, as these can hinder the training process. They reference recent advancements in both first-order and second-order methods aimed at addressing this issue in nonconvex optimization contexts. Overall, the paper sets the stage for exploring policy-search methods, particularly those parameterized in a way that allows for effective optimization in RL scenarios.
