This paper presents an unbiased method for estimating the Q-function, crucial for policy optimization in reinforcement learning. The algorithm, referred to as EstQ, initializes the Q-value and collects rewards over a randomly drawn horizon T, which follows a geometric distribution. The rewards are updated iteratively based on the instantaneous rewards received and the discount factor, culminating in a final reward update at the end of the horizon.

The paper also articulates the Policy Gradient Theorem, which provides a framework for deriving the gradient of the objective function J(θ) in terms of the policy's parameters θ. It emphasizes the importance of the state-action occupancy measure and the advantage function, which differentiates the Q-value from the baseline value function.

To perform stochastic gradient ascent, unbiased samples of the gradient must be obtained. This involves sampling state-action pairs from the occupancy measure and estimating the Q-function or advantage function. The paper outlines a mechanism for achieving this through the use of finite sample trajectories, ensuring that the sampled state-action pairs correspond to the desired distribution.

The goal of these methods is to effectively converge to a stationary point solution for a non-convex optimization problem while potentially reaching a local optimum through the introduction of controlled perturbations.
