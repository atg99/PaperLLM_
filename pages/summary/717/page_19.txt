The paper discusses the convergence properties of a stochastic optimization algorithm, specifically focusing on the behavior of iterates under different regimes of gradient magnitude and Hessian curvature. It establishes that the algorithm can approximate the solution of a stochastic optimization problem with high probability after a certain number of steps, which is influenced by parameters such as the Lipschitz constant of the Hessian and various scaling factors associated with the stochastic policy gradients.

The analysis is divided into three regimes, with the first regime addressing scenarios where the gradient is large. A lemma is introduced, demonstrating that when a stochastic gradient ascent step is taken, the expected value of the function increases, provided the gradient norm is sufficiently large. This is formalized in another lemma that guarantees a positive increase in the expected value of the function when the gradient norm exceeds a threshold.

Overall, the paper provides a rigorous framework for understanding the convergence behavior of stochastic optimization algorithms, particularly in relation to the properties of the gradient and the underlying function being optimized. The proofs leverage concepts from smoothness and stochasticity to establish bounds on the expected increases in function values, thereby ensuring the effectiveness of the optimization process.
