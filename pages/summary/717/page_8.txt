The paper presents the Random-horizon Policy Gradient (RPG) algorithm, designed for optimizing policies in reinforcement learning. The algorithm operates by initializing parameters and iteratively drawing samples from a geometric distribution to determine the horizon for each iteration. At each step, it simulates actions and states while estimating the action-value function using a separate estimation algorithm. The policy is updated based on the estimated value, utilizing a step size that can be either constant or diminishing.

Additionally, the paper discusses modifications to the RPG algorithm, referred to as Modified RPG (MRPG), which aim to enhance performance by reducing variance in policy gradient estimates. This is achieved through the application of baselines in the gradient evaluations, which help in estimating the state-value function. The authors assert that under certain conditions, the algorithm can converge to a stationary policy and reach approximate local extrema, thereby addressing potential saddle point issues in optimization. The structure and methodology outlined suggest a robust approach to policy optimization in complex environments.
