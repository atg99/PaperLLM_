The paper investigates the convergence properties of policy gradient (PG) methods in reinforcement learning (RL), highlighting the distinction between local and global convergence. While existing literature claims that PG methods can converge to locally optimal policies, the authors argue that this assertion is limited by the theory of stochastic approximation, which allows for local optimality only when starting near a local minimum. The authors emphasize the need for a more thorough examination of global convergence.

To bridge the gap between nonconvex optimization theory and PG methods, the paper establishes conditions under which PG methods can escape saddle points and reach approximate second-order stationary points (SOSPs) with high probability, particularly when employing a periodically enlarged stepsize strategy. This leads to the introduction of modified RPG (MRPG) methods that incorporate baseline adjustments to mitigate variance in gradient estimates.

The main contributions of the paper include: 
1. The proposal of RPG methods that provide unbiased estimates of the true policy gradient for infinite-horizon discounted Markov Decision Processes (MDPs), facilitating convergence to stationary-point policies.
2. The development of modified RPG methods with guaranteed convergence to locally optimal policies under specific conditions related to reward functions and policy parameterization.
3. A connection between reward function conditions and the reward-reshaping technique commonly used in empirical RL, supported by a unique proof technique that addresses previous analytical flaws.

Overall, the authors believe that their findings open new avenues for leveraging advances in nonconvex optimization to enhance the convergence behavior of PG methods in RL.
