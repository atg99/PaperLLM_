This paper explores the global convergence of policy gradient (PG) methods in reinforcement learning (RL), emphasizing their optimization perspective. PG methods, which focus on direct policy search, have gained popularity due to their scalability to large and continuous spaces, multiagent settings, and compatibility with deep networks. However, their global convergence in infinite-horizon discounted settings remains poorly understood.

The authors identify key challenges, including the difficulty of obtaining unbiased estimates of the policy gradient. The Policy Gradient Theorem requires that the state-action pairs are sampled from the correct occupancy measure and that the action-value function estimates are unbiased. Additionally, the value function in RL is generally nonconvex concerning the policy parameters, complicating convergence analysis.

While some recent works have addressed convergence in specific RL settings, this paper aims to tackle a broader regime where nonconvexity is prevalent, and approximation errors cannot be easily quantified. The authors propose a solution to the bias introduced by finite-horizon Monte Carlo rollouts by using random geometric time horizons, which allow for unbiased estimates of the Q-function and facilitate unbiased sampling from the discounted occupancy measure.

This leads to the introduction of the random-horizon policy gradient (RPG) algorithm, which connects PG methods to classical stochastic programming algorithms, highlighting the importance of unbiased stochastic search directions. The paper further analyzes the implications of policy parameterization on both limiting behavior and finite-iteration performance, noting that convergence to stationary points with zero gradient-norm is the best achievable outcome in nonconvex optimization without additional structure. Overall, the findings enhance understanding of PG methods and their convergence properties in RL.
