This paper addresses the global convergence of policy gradient (PG) methods within the context of reinforcement learning (RL), particularly focusing on infinite-horizon problems with discounted factors. Despite their empirical success, there has been a lack of rigorous understanding regarding the global convergence of PG methods. The authors propose a new variant of PG methods that utilizes a random rollout horizon for the Monte Carlo estimation of the policy gradient, resulting in an unbiased estimate with bounded variance. This approach allows for the application of nonconvex optimization techniques to establish global convergence.

The authors introduce a modified PG method that incorporates a periodically enlarged stepsize rule, which has been shown to help escape saddle points under certain assumptions about the reward functions and policy parameterization. They connect this to the correlated negative curvature condition, asserting that the reward must be strictly positive or negative for the method to converge to locally optimal policies. The findings provide a rigorous justification for the previously claimed benefits of reward reshaping in escaping saddle points, thus enhancing the theoretical foundation of PG methods in RL.

The paper emphasizes key concepts such as the importance of the Markov decision process framework, the role of exploration in learning optimal policies, and the implications of nonconvex optimization in establishing convergence guarantees for PG methods. The research contributes to the understanding of the conditions under which PG methods can effectively converge to optimal solutions in RL scenarios.
