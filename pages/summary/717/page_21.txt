The paper discusses the bounds and expectations related to the convergence of a sequence of parameter estimates in an optimization context. It establishes conditions under which the total expectation of the gradient norms can be bounded. The authors derive inequalities involving the expectations of the differences between the estimates and their true values, leveraging properties such as the telescopic nature of sums and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality.

Key findings include the relationship between the threshold function Jthre and the expected gradients, highlighting how the stochastic error from gradient estimates can be managed. The paper emphasizes that the distance between the estimated parameters and the true parameters can be decomposed into manageable components, allowing for upper bounds to be established.

The analysis concludes that under specific assumptions regarding boundedness and independence of the stochastic errors, the derived inequalities provide a framework for understanding the convergence behavior of the iterative optimization process. Overall, the results contribute to the theoretical underpinnings of optimization algorithms by providing insights into the relationship between gradient estimates and convergence thresholds.
