The paper aims to develop policy gradient (PG) methods to maximize the objective function J(θ) while thoroughly examining the relationship between its limiting properties and the need for enhancements in algorithm updates, reward functions, and policy parameterization to avoid undesirable limit points. This issue was previously addressed by incorporating random perturbations in actor-critic reinforcement learning algorithms, based on asymptotic convergence results. The authors provide a contemporary viewpoint informed by advancements in nonconvex optimization.

In the section on policy gradient methods, the authors link stochastic gradient ascent in stochastic optimization with PG methods as a direct policy search approach in reinforcement learning. They establish a standard assumption regarding the regularity of the Markov Decision Process (MDP) and the parameterized policy πθ, which includes the uniform boundedness of the reward function and the differentiability of the policy with respect to θ. The boundedness of the reward function is a common standard in policy gradient and actor-critic literature, implying that the Q-function's absolute value is also upper-bounded.

The conditions set forth regarding the policy's score function, which must be Lipschitz and have a bounded norm, are shown to be satisfied by typical parameterized policies such as the Gaussian and Gibbs policies. For continuous action spaces, the Gaussian policy is parameterized to allow exploration around a mean action, while in discrete spaces, the Gibbs policy is characterized by the exponential of a feature vector. Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of these assumptions and conditions for ensuring the convergence and effectiveness of policy gradient algorithms.
