The paper discusses the behavior of the expected value of the action-value function Qπθ(sT,aT) when the reward function R(s,a) is strictly positive, bounded within [LR, UR]. It establishes that Qπθ(sT,aT) is a scalar that lies within the range [LR/(1 - γ), UR/(1 - γ)], indicating that as the number of time steps T’ increases, the expected cumulative reward increases as well.

An important finding is the existence of a finite threshold T* such that the expected value of the cumulative reward from time 0 to T* is less than Qπθ(sT,aT), while the expected value from time 0 to T* + 1 is greater than or equal to Qπθ(sT,aT). This leads to the conclusion that the variance of the estimated action-value function, denoted Var[ˆQπθ(sT,aT)], can be expressed in terms of these expectations.

Further analysis involves applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and manipulating the expressions to demonstrate that this variance is influenced by the lower bound of the reward function LR, as well as the geometric distribution of the time steps. The paper highlights that the variance can be decomposed into contributions from the time steps below and above T*, reinforcing the notion that the reward structure and the distribution of time steps significantly affect the estimation accuracy of Qπθ(sT,aT). 

Overall, the findings underscore the relationship between the reward function, time steps, and the estimation of action-value functions in reinforcement learning contexts.
