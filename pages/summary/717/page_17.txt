The paper discusses the variance of estimated action-value functions and state-value functions in a reinforcement learning context. It derives bounds on the variance of these estimates, highlighting the role of parameters such as the discount factor gamma and the constants L and R. The analysis demonstrates that the variance of the estimated action-value function Q is lower-bounded by a function of these parameters, leading to an expression for the expected squared gradient of the objective function J with respect to the policy parameters θ. 

Key findings include:

1. The variance of the estimated action-value function Q is shown to be at least proportional to a combination of L, R, and powers of gamma, specifically indicating that it is influenced by the discount factor's behavior as it approaches its limits.

2. Similar bounds are established for the state-value function V, indicating a consistent structure in the variance across both value functions.

3. The results culminate in a conclusion about the expected squared gradient of the objective function, establishing a positive lower bound that indicates the presence of a sufficient signal for policy improvement.

The analysis further extends to cases where the reward function is negative, noting that the general structure of the arguments remains applicable with minor modifications. The paper concludes with a discussion on the implications of these findings for the optimization of policy parameters in reinforcement learning frameworks.
