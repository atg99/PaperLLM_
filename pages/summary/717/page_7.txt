The paper discusses a method for estimating the Q-function value \( Q^\pi_\theta(s,a) \) using a sampling approach that employs a geometric distribution with a discount factor of \( \gamma^{1/2} \). This approach allows for unbiased estimates of the Q-function in an infinite-horizon setting through Monte Carlo rollouts of finite horizons. The authors highlight that the use of random horizons, as opposed to fixed-length rollouts, mitigates bias in the Q-function estimate, which is critical for accurate policy gradient estimates.

The proposed stochastic estimate of the policy gradient \( \nabla J(\theta) \) is calculated using the Q-function estimate and the log-derivative of the policy. Two additional stochastic policy gradients are introduced that utilize advantage functions to further refine the estimates, resulting in smaller variances compared to the initial method.

The paper also outlines an update rule for the policy parameters \( \theta \) based on the estimated gradients, allowing for iterative improvement of the policy. The use of \( \gamma^{1/2} \) in the discount factor is emphasized as it ensures almost sure boundedness of the Q-function estimate, which is crucial for the convergence analysis of the proposed methods. Overall, the proposed techniques enhance the efficiency and accuracy of policy gradient methods in reinforcement learning.
