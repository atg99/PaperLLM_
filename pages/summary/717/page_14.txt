This paper discusses the implications of Assumption 4.5 in the context of policy gradient (PG) methods, emphasizing its relationship with Assumption 3.1. The authors highlight that the strict positivity of the reward function can be achieved by adding an offset to the original rewards without affecting the optimal policy, thus enabling better convergence results for the MRPG algorithm to the Strong Optimal Stationary Policy (SOSP). This reshaping of rewards is presented as a novel theoretical contribution to the understanding of PG methods, despite its practical popularity.

The paper also addresses the variance introduced by the positivity of the Q-function, necessitating the use of a baseline function for variance reduction. The authors note that many commonly used policies, such as Gaussian policies, satisfy the conditions outlined in Assumption 4.5, which support Lipschitz continuity of the Hessian matrix of the objective function. This is crucial for establishing convergence in non-convex optimization scenarios.

Furthermore, the paper presents Lemma 4.7, which establishes that the stochastic policy gradients satisfy the Correlated Negative Curvature (CNC) condition. This condition is essential for subsequent analyses and shows that there exist constants ensuring a minimum expected value for the squared gradients, reinforcing the theoretical foundation for the proposed methods. The proofs for these results are deferred to the appendix, indicating a thorough and formal approach to the analysis.
