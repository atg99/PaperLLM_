The paper discusses the establishment of lower bounds for the expected value of squared gradient estimates in the context of reinforcement learning. It demonstrates that under certain conditions, specifically when the reward function R(s,a) is consistently positive or negative, the expected value of the square of the inner product of the gradient estimates can be uniformly bounded. 

The authors derive inequalities that express these bounds, showing that they depend on parameters like the learning rate (LR) and the discount factor (γ). They also explore the variance of the estimates, indicating that the variance terms need to be uniformly lower bounded. The paper outlines the mathematical steps taken to derive these inequalities, including the use of independence and unbiasedness of the estimates involved.

The analysis is supported by rigorous mathematical reasoning, highlighting how the results hold for any unit-norm vector and for eigenvectors associated with the maximum eigenvalue of a specific Hessian matrix. The findings contribute to understanding the stability and convergence of policy gradient methods in reinforcement learning.
