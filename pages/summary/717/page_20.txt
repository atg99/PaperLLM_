The paper discusses the convergence of policies in the context of optimization, particularly focusing on the behavior of iterates near saddle points. The authors present a method involving the use of larger stepsizes to navigate towards directions of positive curvature, followed by smaller stepsizes to refine the search. 

The proof demonstrates that, under certain conditions regarding the Hessian matrix's maximum eigenvalue, the expected increase in the objective function value after a specified number of iterations is guaranteed. Specifically, if the maximum eigenvalue is sufficiently large, the iterates will escape saddle points effectively, ensuring that the function value increases by at least a predefined threshold.

The authors utilize a contradiction strategy to establish their results, asserting that if the objective value does not increase significantly, the distance between successive iterates can be constrained, leading to a polynomial upper bound. This approach is supported by previous results in the literature, reinforcing the validity of their findings on the convergence behavior of the proposed optimization strategies.
