The paper discusses the convergence properties of the Reinforcement Policy Gradient (RPG) algorithm. It establishes bounds on the gradient of the objective function J(θ) using assumptions about the reward function R(s,a) and the policy gradient norm. The results indicate that the RPG algorithm converges almost surely to stationary policies under certain conditions related to the stepsize sequence.

Theorem 4.4 states that under Assumption 3.1, if the stepsize satisfies specific summability conditions, then the sequence of parameters converges to the set of stationary points of the objective function. The proof relies on supermartingale convergence arguments, differing from other methods like ODE-based analyses used in actor-critic algorithms.

The paper also introduces Assumption 4.5, which outlines conditions necessary for the finite-iteration convergence of the Modified RPG (M RPG) algorithm. Key requirements include bounded rewards, the existence and boundedness of the score function's gradient, and positive-definiteness of the Fisher information matrix associated with the policy. These conditions ensure that the algorithm can effectively optimize the policy within the defined parameters.
