The paper discusses the convergence of a sequence of policies to local optimal policies in a stochastic optimization context. It begins by establishing an upper bound on the expected distance between the current policy parameter θp and the optimal parameter θ0, leveraging properties of the gradient and the Hessian of the objective function J. Specifically, it shows that the expectation E(‖θp - θ0‖²) can be bounded in terms of several parameters, including the step size α and the characteristics of the objective function.

The authors introduce a quadratic approximation of the objective function, denoted as Q(θ), which allows for a clearer analysis of the gradient differences. This approximation leads to a Lipschitz condition on the Hessian of J, facilitating the derivation of further bounds on the distance between parameters over iterations.

The paper also explores the implications of the CNC (Conditioned Non-Convexity) condition, which ensures that the distance between successive parameter estimates can be lower-bounded by an exponential function of a threshold parameter kthre. This analysis culminates in a decomposition of the update from θp to θp+1, illustrating how various terms contribute to the overall convergence behavior.

In conclusion, the authors provide a rigorous framework for understanding the convergence properties of their proposed algorithms, demonstrating both upper and lower bounds on the expected distances between policy parameters across iterations, thus reinforcing the theoretical foundations of their approach in optimization settings.
