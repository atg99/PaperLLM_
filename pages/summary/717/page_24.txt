The paper discusses the global convergence of policy gradient (PG) methods in reinforcement learning (RL), specifically focusing on their ability to escape saddle points and converge to approximately locally optimal policies. The authors present Theorem 4.8, which demonstrates that the probability of retrieving a saddle point optimal solution policy (SOSP) uniformly over the iterates can be bounded, allowing the PG algorithm to escape saddle points efficiently within a polynomial number of steps. The proof improves upon previous work and emphasizes the relationship between the gradient and Hessian in convergence criteria.

The authors introduce modified random-horizon policy gradient (MRPG) algorithms that utilize periodically enlarged step sizes to enhance convergence properties. These algorithms are shown to effectively escape saddle points and reach locally optimal policies under standard assumptions in RL. A noteworthy finding is that reward reshaping, specifically shifting rewards by an offset, can increase the variance of the stochastic policy gradient, aiding in the escape from saddle points without altering the problem's landscape.

The paper highlights the need for a deeper understanding of PG methods from a nonconvex optimization perspective, marking a significant contribution to the literature by distinguishing between convergence to stationary points and local extrema. Future research directions are suggested, emphasizing the potential for further enhancements by linking policy search techniques with nonconvex optimization strategies. The authors also acknowledge previous work in the field and clarify corrections made to earlier proofs related to convergence rates.
