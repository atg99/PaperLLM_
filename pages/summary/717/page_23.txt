The paper discusses the behavior of a stochastic optimization algorithm as it approaches second-order stationary points (SOSP). It presents a key lower bound derived from equation (4.44), which establishes that the expected function value increases significantly after a certain number of steps (kthre) if the parameters are appropriately chosen. Specifically, Lemma 4.11 is highlighted as crucial, affirming that after kthre steps, the expected function value will increase by at least Jthre, provided that the iterate does not satisfy the SOSP condition.

When the iterate nears an SOSP, both the gradient's norm and the largest eigenvalue are small, yet function value decreases can still occur due to the variance in the stochastic policy gradient. However, this decrease is bounded, as shown in equation (4.45). The analysis further combines results from Lemmas 4.10 and 4.11 to establish a probabilistic lower bound on the expected return.

The expected change in the function value is calculated based on the occurrence of certain events, denoted as scrE m and its complement scrE c m. By considering the probabilities of these events, the overall expectation of the function value change is derived in equation (4.48). Finally, the paper concludes that by running the iterate for K steps, one can accumulate results over M iterations of the process, thereby reinforcing the theoretical underpinnings of the algorithm's performance in optimization tasks.
