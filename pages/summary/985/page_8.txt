This paper investigates the effectiveness of MacBERT, a pre-trained language model, through various ablation studies. The evaluation across fine-tuning tasks reveals that every component of MacBERT contributes positively to its performance, with the most significant enhancements stemming from N-gram masking and similar word replacement techniques. N-gram masking is particularly beneficial for text classification tasks, while similar word replacement improves reading comprehension tasks. The combination of both methods results in superior performance across different task genres.

The study also highlights the lesser importance of the next sentence prediction (NSP) task compared to the masked language model (MLM) task. Notably, the sentence order prediction (SOP) task outperforms NSP, as it simplifies the task for machines by requiring the identification of the correct order between two sentences. The absence of SOP leads to significant performance declines in reading comprehension tasks, emphasizing the need for effective relation learning tasks.

Additionally, the paper delves into the MLM task, focusing on the selection and replacement of masked tokens. The analysis reveals that the method of replacing tokens greatly influences the performance of pre-trained models. MacBERT employs a strategy where 80% of masked tokens are replaced with similar words, while 10% are replaced with random words. This approach is contrasted with other methods, including random replacement and partial masking, indicating that token replacement strategies are crucial for optimizing model performance.
