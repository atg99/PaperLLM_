This paper presents a comparative analysis of several pre-trained language models, including BERT, ERNIE, XLNet, RoBERTa, ALBERT, ELECTRA, and MacBERT. These models are categorized by their types (Auto-Encoder or Auto-Regressive), embedding types (Token, Segment, Position), and various masking and language modeling tasks, as summarized in Table 1. 

The key contributions of the paper include:

1. Comprehensive empirical studies that reassess the performance of Chinese pre-trained language models across various tasks, accompanied by detailed analyses.
2. Introduction of MacBERT, a new pre-trained language model that addresses the discrepancies between pre-training and fine-tuning stages by employing a novel masking strategy that replaces words with their semantically similar counterparts, enhancing performance on downstream tasks.
3. Development and release of a series of Chinese pre-trained language models to support ongoing research in Chinese natural language processing.

The paper also reviews the foundational techniques of prominent models, starting with BERT, which utilizes a bidirectional approach for pre-training through the Masked Language Model (MLM) and Next Sentence Prediction (NSP) tasks. BERT's optimization techniques, such as whole word masking, are highlighted for their effectiveness in improving model performance without increasing computational burdens.

The review continues with ERNIE, which enhances the masking process by incorporating entity-level and phrase-level masking, improving upon the random word selection in BERT.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the advancements in pre-trained language models and their implications for future research in the field of natural language processing.
