The paper discusses various masking strategies used in language models, particularly focusing on MacBERT, a modified version of BERT designed to enhance performance on fine-tuning tasks. 

The authors highlight that whole word masking affects token selection during pre-training, while still utilizing the WordPiece tokenizer as in the original BERT. They introduce MacBERT, which employs whole word and N-gram masking strategies, adjusting the percentage of masked tokens based on their type. Instead of using the standard [MASK] token, MacBERT replaces masked tokens with similar words obtained through a synonym toolkit based on word2vec similarity. Specifically, 15% of input words are masked, with 80% replaced by similar words, 10% by random words, and 10% remaining unchanged.

For the next sentence prediction task, MacBERT utilizes sentence-order prediction, creating negative samples by altering the order of consecutive sentences. The experimental setup involves pre-training on a substantial dataset derived from Wikipedia, using both Simplified and Traditional Chinese, supplemented by additional training data from varied sources to enhance the model's capabilities.

The paper concludes with a focus on the contributions of each modification through ablation studies, aiming to demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed changes in MacBERT compared to earlier models.
