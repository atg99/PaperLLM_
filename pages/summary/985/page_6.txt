The paper presents a comparison of various Chinese pre-trained language models in the context of Machine Reading Comprehension (MRC), specifically evaluating their performance on three datasets: CMRC 2018, DRCD, and CJRC. The models assessed include BERT, BERT-wwm, BERT-wwm-ext, RoBERTa-wwm-ext, ELECTRA, and MacBERT, with results summarized in tables.

In the CMRC 2018 dataset, which focuses on span extraction similar to SQuAD, the performance of MacBERT-large stands out, achieving a state-of-the-art F1 score of 60%. The results indicate that models utilizing extended pre-training data, such as RoBERTa and ELECTRA, show significant improvements over their base counterparts.

For the DRCD dataset, which is a traditional Chinese span-extraction MRC dataset, MacBERT-large again demonstrates superior performance, achieving an F1 score of 95.6%. The findings emphasize that additional training with large-scale simplified Chinese data positively impacts performance on traditional Chinese tasks due to the shared character set.

In the CJRC dataset, which contains various question types, similar trends are observed, with MacBERT-large outperforming other models. The results across all datasets confirm that MacBERT consistently yields improvements, highlighting the effectiveness of the model in enhancing comprehension tasks.

The paper concludes that leveraging additional pre-training data and optimizing model architecture significantly boosts the performance of Chinese language models in reading comprehension tasks.
