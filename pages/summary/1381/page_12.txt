While ML has gained significant traction due to its extensive study and application in various fields, MAS remains underexplored despite its potential to effectively coordinate complex tasks across supply chains.

The paper argues that combining ML and MAS can yield enhanced solutions for OGI applications, particularly in dynamic environments where predicting agent behavior is challenging. It highlights that agents can benefit from learning and adapting to their surroundings, which is where ML plays a crucial role. The complexity of agent interactions increases with the number and sophistication of agents, necessitating the use of ML to facilitate adjustments in agent behavior.

Among the different ML approaches, reinforcement learning is identified as particularly suitable for agent learning due to its alignment with the agent paradigm. However, its application in OGI is limited, with most existing MAS applications relying on supervised or unsupervised ML for predictive or descriptive analytics rather than enabling agent learning.

The paper cites an example of a MAS utilizing reinforcement learning, where Aissani et al. developed a model for dynamic maintenance task planning in a petroleum refinery. This model employed a modular Markov decision process architecture and the SARSA algorithm to allow agents to learn from their experiences. The approach demonstrated effectiveness in providing online scheduling solutions for maintenance tasks, although challenges persisted in scheduling during unexpected failures.

Overall, the integration of ML and MAS holds promise for improving operational efficiency in the OGI, but further exploration and experimentation are necessary to fully realize its potential.
