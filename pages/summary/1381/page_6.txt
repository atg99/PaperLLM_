The paper discusses the application of various machine learning models in predicting oil and gas production and detecting anomalies. The LS-SVM model was found to have the highest accuracy in predicting gas-to-oil ratios across different scenarios, suggesting its promising application in production and reservoir engineering tasks. Chaki et al. applied a Modular Artificial Neural Network (MANN) to estimate sand fractions in a reservoir using seismic properties from a hydrocarbon field in India. Their approach yielded accurate predictions with efficient processing and enhanced visualization.

Recent advancements in Deep Learning (DL) are highlighted, showcasing its potential to transform the oil and gas industry by automating risk detection in pipelines and identifying anomalies that traditional systems may overlook. Two DL models were developed for predicting nuclear magnetic resonance T2 time distribution from well logs, utilizing a Variational Auto-Encoder and a Convolutional Neural Network, alongside a Long Short-Term Memory network. These models demonstrated robust performance and good accuracy, even in noisy environments.

Additionally, Genetic Algorithms (GA) have been effectively employed in oil field development, production scheduling, reservoir characterization, and seismic inversion. The paper also emphasizes the role of machine learning in anomaly detection, with Jinet al. demonstrating the effectiveness of LS-SVM in identifying leak levels in gas pipelines using acoustic wave methods. Their experiments confirmed the model's ability to accurately discern leakage levels based on acoustic signals within a short timeframe.
