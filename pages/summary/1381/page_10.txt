This paper discusses the implementation of Multi-Agent Systems (MAS) in the oil field and network management operations, emphasizing their role in enhancing efficiency and reducing costs. It highlights a study where agents communicated using FIPA's agent communication language within a MySQL 5.0 database connected to SGCP. The findings suggest that MAS facilitated collaborative problem-solving, minimizing the need for extensive data analysis across various systems.

The authors present the NM-MAS, designed for network management in a gas-oil separation plant (GOSP), where agents performed tasks like symptom detection and fault diagnosis. The system's effectiveness was evaluated using metrics such as the success rate of repair plans and fault-tolerance indicators, although the authors acknowledge challenges posed by wireless networks that can impact data accuracy and real-time control.

Additionally, the paper introduces the ICAM system, proposed by Sayda and Taylor, aimed at intelligent asset management in the petroleum industry. The authors developed a conceptual model and conducted real-time simulations, which demonstrated positive performance improvements in the MAS design. The system's ability to react to faulty sensors and actuators was particularly noted, and further testing revealed limitations in the prototype. Recommendations for overcoming these limitations to enhance system performance were also provided.

In the context of Supply Chain Management (SCM), the paper points out the complexities faced by the oil and gas industry due to increasing global demand and dynamic conditions, indicating a need for more research on MAS applications in SCM.
