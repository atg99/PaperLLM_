The proactive agents focused on risk assessment and vulnerability analysis, while the reactive agents were responsible for immediate threat response. The authors demonstrated that their approach significantly improved the detection and mitigation of vandalism incidents compared to traditional monitoring methods.

In another study, Mikkelsen et al. developed a multi-objective control model for offshore oil and gas production that addressed conflicting control concerns in dynamic environments. Their stratified multi-agent system (MAS) operated at three decision layers—strategic, tactical, and operational—allowing agents to negotiate and coordinate their actions effectively. By leveraging historical data and online feeds from the Siri oil and gas field, the authors found that their system outperformed manual operations, meeting both current and future operational requirements while minimizing environmental impacts.

Dobrescu et al. introduced a MAS framework for shared control in onshore oilfields, proposing a distributed control strategy aligned with the IEC 61499 standard. They transformed traditional production units into production unit agents to enhance system dynamics and load distribution. Their approach aimed to identify supervisory-level modifications needed for effective remote execution, with plans for future implementation in real-world scenarios.

Overall, these studies indicate a growing trend in the use of multi-agent systems in the oil and gas industry, emphasizing improved efficiency, safety, and adaptability in production and maintenance operations.
