Robots play a crucial role in offshore fields for tasks like drilling, inspection, and damage control, thereby improving efficiency and safety. The integration of micro- and nanotechnologies has advanced sensor-based applications in OGI, enabling detailed and accurate information gathering. Wireless sensor networks facilitate the monitoring of oil assets, such as pipeline integrity and equipment status, in real time. Additionally, radio frequency identification (RFID) technology aids in asset management and safety protocols.

The paper emphasizes the significant challenges posed by the vast amounts of data generated from various sensors, which are often too complex for human operators to interpret. Intelligent systems, particularly machine learning (ML), emerge as a solution for real-time data analysis and decision-making. ML mimics human learning by improving performance through repeated tasks and error correction, focusing on predictions, clustering, and decision-making from data.

In the context of OGI, ML has shown promise in optimizing drilling plans, monitoring operations, and conducting real-time optimizations at lower costs. Various ML algorithms, including Support Vector Machine, Artificial Neural Networks, Deep Learning, and Genetic Algorithms, have been effectively employed in OGI applications.

Specifically, ML enhances oil and gas production rates and reduces lifting costs. It supports reservoir modeling and preventive maintenance by determining optimal maintenance schedules to avoid failures. For instance, Qiao et al. introduced a hybrid forecasting model combining Particle Swarm Optimization and Least Squares Support Vector Machine, which successfully analyzed production factors using historical data. Similarly, Panja et al. compared different models to predict oil production and gas-to-oil ratios, demonstrating the efficacy of ML in addressing OGI challenges.
