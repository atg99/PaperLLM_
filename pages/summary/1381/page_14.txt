This paper examines the application of machine learning (ML) and multi-agent systems (MAS) in addressing challenges within the oil and gas industry (OGI). The OGI faces issues such as dwindling oil and gas reserves, rising prices, theft, vandalism, and a lack of transparency, which necessitate innovative solutions for managing extensive data to enhance prediction, prevention, and optimization efforts. A staged approach to investment is recommended to mitigate risks, allowing for the development of specific predictive and descriptive ML models that can be integrated into a scalable MAS for comprehensive oversight of OGI operations.

The review highlights the complexity of the OGI, which encompasses upstream, midstream, and downstream sectors involving numerous stakeholders. While ML has been recognized for its potential in extracting patterns from data to aid decision-making, and MAS for managing complex relationships, most existing studies focus on isolated tasks rather than a holistic approach to automating the entire supply chain and information governance. Furthermore, many proposed MAS solutions remain confined to simulated environments, with limited real-world application due to high implementation costs, associated risks, and the absence of standardized tools.

To advance the integration of ML and MAS in OGI, the paper suggests that industry leaders adopt a phased strategy for embedding these technologies into existing processes. This would involve developing distinct ML models for various tasks over time, ultimately merging them within a MAS framework. Enhancements in agent cooperation can be achieved through the incorporation of advanced deep reinforcement learning techniques. Additionally, promoting data openness and sharing can accelerate the development and integration of ML and MAS solutions, benefiting all stakeholders in the industry.
