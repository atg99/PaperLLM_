The paper discusses various loss functions used in metric learning, particularly focusing on Siamese networks and their adaptations. It highlights the SoftTriple loss, which is designed to train Siamese networks by adjusting intra-class and inter-class distances through a specific optimization formula. The paper also introduces Fisher Siamese losses, which leverage Fisher Discriminant Analysis (FDA) principles to minimize intra-class variance while maximizing inter-class variance. This approach involves projecting features through a matrix and optimizing a loss that incorporates regularization to address ill-posed problems.

Additionally, the paper presents Deep Adversarial Metric Learning, where a generator is utilized to create negative examples through adversarial learning. The generator's loss function aims to ensure that the generated negative points are close to real negatives while maintaining distance from anchor points. The overall goal of these methodologies is to enhance the performance of metric learning by refining distance measures between data points in a learned feature space.
