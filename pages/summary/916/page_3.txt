The paper discusses the concept of generalized Mahalanobis distance, which is an extension of the traditional Mahalanobis distance. This distance is defined using a positive semi-definite weight matrix, allowing for the incorporation of correlations between dimensions of data points. The generalized Mahalanobis norm is also introduced, reflecting the weighted distance in a more structured way.

Key properties of the generalized Mahalanobis distance are established, including non-negativity, identity, symmetry, and the triangle inequality, thereby confirming it as a valid distance metric. The paper emphasizes the importance of the weight matrix, which influences the distance computation by considering the scatter of data points.

Additionally, the paper highlights that the Euclidean distance is a special case of the Mahalanobis distance, specifically when the weight matrix is the identity matrix. The notion of projection in metric learning is also explored, where the eigenvalue decomposition of the weight matrix allows for a linear projection that can transform the generalized Mahalanobis distance into a Euclidean distance in a reduced-dimensional subspace.

The main idea of metric learning is discussed, focusing on the identification of similar and dissimilar pairs of data points within a dataset. Similar pairs exhibit comparable characteristics, while dissimilar pairs do not, setting the foundation for metric learning approaches that aim to enhance the representation of these relationships in the learned metric space.
