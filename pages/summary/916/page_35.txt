The paper discusses various loss functions and sampling strategies for metric learning, particularly focusing on triplet loss formulations. It presents several approaches to optimize the distances between points in a feature space, aiming to enhance the performance of deep learning models.

1. **Triplet Loss Functions**: The paper outlines different triplet loss functions that leverage extreme distances. The first function incorporates both hard positive and negative samples, aiming to minimize the distance between positive pairs while maximizing the distance to negative samples. Another variant introduces logarithmic and exponential functions to address the same optimization goal.

2. **Hard Mining Center-Triplet Loss**: This method, proposed by Lv et al., uses the mean of each class as an anchor and selects the hardest positive and negative samples relative to this anchor. This approach focuses on challenging examples to improve the model's discriminative ability.

3. **Triplet Loss with Cross-Batch Memory**: This variation allows the model to utilize previously seen batches, enhancing the selection of positive and negative samples from a broader memory rather than just the current mini-batch. This method helps maintain a richer set of examples, potentially leading to better learning outcomes.

4. **Triplet Sampling Techniques**: Instead of relying solely on the hardest samples, the paper discusses triplet sampling methods that randomly select positive and negative points from mini-batches or distributions. This includes distance-weighted sampling, which selects points based on their pairwise distances, ensuring a more balanced representation of examples.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of carefully selecting triplet samples and loss functions to optimize metric learning in deep learning contexts, presenting a comprehensive survey of current methodologies and innovations in the field.
