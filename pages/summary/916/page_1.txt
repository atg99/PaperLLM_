This paper serves as a tutorial and survey on metric learning, categorizing algorithms into spectral, probabilistic, and deep metric learning. It begins by defining distance metrics, including the Mahalanobis distance and its generalization. The spectral methods section covers various techniques such as Fisher discriminant analysis, Relevant Component Analysis, and large-margin metric learning, alongside kernel spectral methods and geometric metric learning on Riemannian manifolds.

In the probabilistic methods section, the authors discuss collapsing classes, neighborhood component analysis, Bayesian metric learning, and empirical risk minimization. The deep learning methods section introduces reconstruction autoencoders, supervised loss functions, Siamese networks, triplet mining, and deep discriminant analysis methods based on Fisher discriminant analysis. The paper concludes with insights into multi-modal deep metric learning, geometric metric learning via neural networks, and few-shot metric learning.

The introduction emphasizes the role of dimensionality reduction and manifold learning in feature extraction, highlighting that metric learning aims to create a distance metric to differentiate between classes effectively. The paper acknowledges existing surveys on metric learning and provides a comprehensive overview of significant algorithms within the defined categories.
