In this framework, we define a triplet consisting of an anchor point (green circle), a positive point (blue circle), and a negative point (red diamond). The triplet loss aims to ensure that the distance between the anchor and positive points is minimized while maximizing the distance between the anchor and negative points.

The triplet loss function can be expressed as follows: for a given triplet (anchor, positive, negative), the loss is formulated to ensure that the distance from the anchor to the positive is less than the distance from the anchor to the negative by a margin. This is mathematically represented as:

minimize max(0, d(f(anchor), f(positive)) - d(f(anchor), f(negative)) + margin)

where d denotes the distance function, and the margin is a predefined threshold that enforces the separation of the embeddings. 

The advantages of the triplet loss include its ability to create a well-structured embedding space where similar items are clustered together while dissimilar items are pushed apart. However, it requires careful selection of triplets to ensure effective training.

In summary, both contrastive and triplet losses are essential components in metric learning, facilitating the development of robust embedding spaces through the use of similarity measures. While contrastive loss operates on pairs, triplet loss extends this to triplets, each offering unique advantages and considerations in implementation.
