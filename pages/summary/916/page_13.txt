The paper discusses the decomposition of the weight matrix in Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Space (RKHS) and its application to generalized Mahalanobis distance metrics. It presents the mathematical formulation for the distance metric as well as the kernelized versions of within-class and between-class scatter matrices. 

The generalized Mahalanobis distance is expressed using projections onto a subspace defined by a projection matrix T. The optimization problem is framed as maximizing the Rayleigh-Ritz quotient, leading to a generalized eigenvalue problem involving the scatter matrices. 

In addition, the paper elaborates on the kernel version of the metric learning method, highlighting how the objective functions transform in RKHS. It establishes that the optimization problem can be reformulated to minimize the trace of a matrix that represents the difference between the average within-class and between-class scatter matrices, subject to a constraint that ensures orthogonality. 

This analysis is closely related to Kernel Fisher Discriminant Analysis, emphasizing the method's ability to minimize intra-class variance while maximizing inter-class variance within the RKHS framework. Overall, the paper provides a comprehensive survey of spectral, probabilistic, and deep metric learning approaches, focusing on their mathematical underpinnings and applications in machine learning.
