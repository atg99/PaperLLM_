This paper serves as a comprehensive tutorial and survey on various metric learning methods, focusing on spectral, probabilistic, and deep metric learning approaches. It is structured into multiple sections, beginning with an introduction to distance metrics and the generalized Mahalanobis distance, followed by detailed discussions on the three primary metric learning methods, and concluding with a summary.

The paper assumes that readers have a foundational understanding of calculus, probability, linear algebra, and optimization basics. 

In Section 2, the concept of a distance metric is defined, highlighting essential properties such as non-negativity, identity, symmetry, and the triangle inequality. The Euclidean distance is presented as a classic example. The Mahalanobis distance is also introduced, providing a measure of distance between two points that accounts for the covariance of data sets. This section emphasizes the importance of understanding how variance influences distance measurements, illustrating that the Mahalanobis distance can outperform the Euclidean distance in certain contexts by reflecting the distribution of data points.

Overall, the paper aims to equip readers with a solid understanding of metric learning, its methodologies, and practical applications, supported by relevant examples and definitions.
