The paper presents a comprehensive survey and tutorial on spectral, probabilistic, and deep metric learning, highlighting the evolution and various methodologies employed in the field. It discusses foundational works, including those by Guillaumin et al. on face identification and Hadsell et al. on dimensionality reduction through invariant mappings. The authors explore geometric perspectives on metric learning, as proposed by Hauberg et al. and Harandi et al., emphasizing the integration of dimensionality reduction and metric learning.

Key contributions in deep metric learning are outlined, such as the triplet network approach by Hoffer and Ailon, and the application of Siamese networks for one-shot image recognition by Koch et al. Various techniques for learning distance metrics under contextual constraints and the importance of knowledge distillation in neural networks are also examined.

The survey addresses recent advancements in few-shot learning and unsupervised meta-learning, showcasing innovative methods like multi-scale metric learning and hard mining strategies for specific applications. The historical context is enriched by references to pioneering works in metric learning, including Mahalanobis' foundational concepts.

Overall, the paper serves as a valuable resource for understanding the landscape of metric learning, its challenges, and the promising directions for future research.
