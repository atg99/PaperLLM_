The paper discusses various loss functions utilized in metric learning, focusing on their mathematical formulations and applications. 

1. **Loss Functions**:
   - **L1 Loss**: Also known as Manhattan distance, this loss minimizes the absolute differences between predicted and true labels, promoting sparsity in embeddings.
   - **Huber Loss**: Combines Mean Squared Error (MSE) and Mean Absolute Error (MAE) to leverage the advantages of both, providing robustness to outliers.
   - **KL-Divergence Loss**: Aims to align the distribution of predicted labels with true labels, using a probabilistic approach.
   - **Hinge Loss**: Suitable for binary classification, it focuses on maximizing the margin between classes, penalizing predictions based on their margin from the decision boundary.
   - **Cross-Entropy Loss**: Used for multi-class classification, this loss function requires a softmax activation in the output layer, facilitating the separation of classes and yielding discriminative embeddings.

The relationship between cross-entropy and metric learning losses is highlighted, particularly how cross-entropy serves as an upper-bound on metric learning losses, thus ensuring the generation of effective embedding features.

2. **Siamese Networks**:
   - Introduced as a vital method in deep metric learning, Siamese networks consist of multiple identical sub-networks sharing weights. Typically, they are structured with two or three sub-networks, the latter forming a triplet network. 
   - These networks are trained to minimize intra-class variance while maximizing inter-class variance, effectively clustering similar points and distancing dissimilar ones. Applications of Siamese networks span across computer vision and natural language processing.

Overall, the paper outlines the foundational loss functions in metric learning and the role of Siamese networks in feature extraction and classification tasks.
