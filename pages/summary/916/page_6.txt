The paper discusses various approaches to metric learning, particularly focusing on spectral, probabilistic, and deep metric learning techniques. It introduces an optimization problem that minimizes the trace of a matrix involving covariance estimates from similar and dissimilar data points, framed as an eigenvalue problem. The two approaches presented involve either maintaining a standard trace constraint or imposing an orthogonal projection constraint. 

The paper also highlights the relationship of these methods with Fisher discriminant analysis (FDA), emphasizing the common goal of minimizing intra-class variance while maximizing inter-class variance. 

Relevant Component Analysis (RCA) is introduced as a metric learning method that applies Principal Component Analysis (PCA) to data, using covariance matrices for Mahalanobis distance calculations. 

Discriminative Component Analysis (DCA) is discussed as another spectral metric learning technique that focuses on maximizing inter-class variance and minimizing intra-class variance through a generalized Rayleigh-Ritz quotient optimization problem. The solutions to these problems are derived from generalized eigenvalue problems, establishing a connection to the Mahalanobis distance framework.

Overall, the paper provides a comprehensive survey of metric learning methodologies, illustrating the mathematical foundations and relationships between different approaches.
