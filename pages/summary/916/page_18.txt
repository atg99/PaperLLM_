The section discusses Adversarial Metric Learning (AML) and Probabilistic Metric Learning, highlighting their methodologies and objectives. AML, as introduced by Chen et al. in 2018, employs adversarial learning to enhance metric learning. It consists of two stages: the distinguishment stage, which aims to bring similar points closer and push dissimilar points apart, and the confusion stage, which seeks to mislead the metric learning by pulling dissimilar points closer and pushing similar points apart. This dual-stage approach is trained simultaneously, enhancing each stage's performance progressively. The process involves forming random pairs of points and generating new points iteratively to optimize the confusion stage, using specific loss functions from previous methods.

Probabilistic Metric Learning, on the other hand, utilizes probability distributions to learn the weight matrix in the generalized Mahalanobis distance. This method emphasizes collapsing similar points into the same class while distancing dissimilar points. A Gaussian distribution is employed to define the probability of one point being a neighbor of another, with the distance metric shaped by the generalized Mahalanobis distance. The goal is to minimize the Kullback-Leibler divergence between the desired and actual distributions of point neighborhoods, thereby ensuring that similar points converge to a single point in the projected subspace.

Together, these approaches illustrate advanced strategies in metric learning, focusing on enhancing the effectiveness of distance metrics through adversarial techniques and probabilistic frameworks.
