The paper discusses various loss functions used in metric learning, particularly in the context of deep learning. It begins with the triplet loss, which minimizes the distance between an anchor and a positive point while maximizing the distance from a negative point by a specified margin. This ensures that similar points are closer together than dissimilar points by at least the margin. The triplet loss is linked to spectral large margin metric learning and has been shown to enhance the robustness of networks against adversarial attacks when used alongside cross-entropy loss.

The tuplet loss extends this concept by allowing multiple negative points per anchor, thereby pushing several dissimilar points away simultaneously. Neighborhood Component Analysis (NCA) loss is introduced as a method to minimize the negative log-likelihood of similar points while maximizing the distance of dissimilar points within a mini-batch. However, NCA can be computationally intensive.

To address this, Proxy-NCA loss is proposed, which uses proxy points to represent classes in the embedding space, improving computational efficiency and memory usage. Proxies can be defined as the mean of embedded points or by clustering methods like K-means. The Proxy-NCA loss effectively functions as an upper bound on the NCA loss, allowing for faster and more efficient training while achieving similar goals. Overall, these loss functions play a critical role in enhancing the performance of metric learning frameworks.
