The paper discusses the derivation of gradients in the context of Spectral, Probabilistic, and Deep Metric Learning, drawing parallels to Stochastic Neighbor Embedding (SNE) and t-SNE methodologies. The derivation begins by defining the squared distance \( r_{ij} \) between data points \( x_i \) and \( x_j \) with respect to a weight matrix \( W \). It utilizes the chain rule to express the change in the cost function \( c \) with respect to changes in \( W \), leading to the formulation of \( r_{ij} \) and its derivatives.

The cost function is reformulated to isolate terms dependent on the probability distributions \( p_0(l|k) \) and \( p_W(l|k) \). The paper highlights the importance of the cyclic property of trace in simplifying the expressions. It further explains how to compute \( \partial c / \partial r_{ij} \) using the definitions of \( p_W(l|k) \) and its relationship to the exponential of the distances.

Through careful manipulation of these probabilities and their derivatives, the final expression for the gradient of the cost function is derived, showing a relationship between the true and learned probabilities. This comprehensive derivation illustrates the connections between distance metrics and probabilistic frameworks in machine learning, culminating in a formula that captures the essence of the optimization process in metric learning.
