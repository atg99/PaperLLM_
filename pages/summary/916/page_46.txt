This paper provides a comprehensive tutorial and survey on spectral, probabilistic, and deep metric learning, beginning with an introduction to the generalized Mahalanobis distance metric and its significance in metric learning. The authors elaborate on the Mahalanobis distance and its generalized form, emphasizing the foundational concepts of metric learning.

The paper explores various spectral metric learning methods, starting with those using scatter matrices and semidefinite programming, and discusses their connections to Fisher discriminant analysis and relevant component analysis techniques. It also addresses large-margin metric learning, imbalanced metric learning, and locally linear metric adaptation. The section on kernel spectral metric learning highlights the use of eigenvalue decomposition and regularization methods, including Laplacian regularization.

Geometric spectral metric learning is examined, including geometric mean metric learning across various manifolds. The paper also introduces adversarial metric learning, which adds a layer of complexity to traditional approaches.

In the probabilistic metric learning section, the authors discuss collapsing classes in both input and feature spaces, as well as neighborhood component analysis methods. This survey not only summarizes the advancements in metric learning but also highlights the interconnectedness of the various methods and their applications in machine learning.
