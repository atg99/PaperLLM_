This section discusses Bayesian metric learning methods, specifically focusing on two approaches: Bayesian metric learning using a sigmoid function and Bayesian Neighborhood Component Analysis (NCA).

In Bayesian metric learning with a sigmoid function, as proposed by Yang et al. (2007), a likelihood function is defined using a sigmoid model, where the binary values indicate whether pairs of data points belong to the same or different sets. The method utilizes a Gaussian distribution for the parameters and derives an evidence lower bound to maximize likelihood, enabling the estimation of the covariance matrix and mean parameters iteratively. The variational parameters are defined for both similar and dissimilar pairs, and the process continues until convergence. The learned eigenvalues from the posterior distribution are then used to derive the weight matrix necessary for the metric.

Bayesian NCA, introduced by Wang and Tan (2017), employs variational inference to improve the NCA framework. It establishes an evidence lower bound on the log-likelihood, incorporating the k-nearest neighbors of each data point. This method also assumes a Gaussian distribution for both prior and posterior parameters. The iterative updates for the variational parameters and the covariance and mean estimates are performed until convergence. Once learned, these parameters allow for sampling from the posterior distribution to derive the distance metric.

Both approaches highlight the effectiveness of Bayesian techniques in enhancing metric learning by enabling parameter estimation through variational methods, ultimately contributing to improved performance in various machine learning tasks.
