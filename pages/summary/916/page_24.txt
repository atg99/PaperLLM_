This section of the paper discusses various approaches in metric learning, particularly focusing on methods that utilize information theory to address challenges such as imbalanced data and relevant component analysis.

1. **Information Theoretic Metric Learning for Imbalanced Data**: The Distance Metric by Balancing KL-divergence (DMBK) method is introduced, which is designed for scenarios where class distributions are unequal. It assumes Gaussian distributions for classes and computes the KL-divergence between projected class probabilities. To mitigate the impact of class cardinality, a normalized divergence is proposed, which is then maximized to enhance class separation in the metric space. Regularization and constraints are implemented to manage distances between similar and dissimilar points.

2. **Probabilistic Relevant Component Analysis (RCA)**: This method extends the traditional RCA by emphasizing the maximization of mutual information between original and projected data. The goal is to maintain a margin constraint on distances of points within clusters from their respective means. The optimization problem focuses on maximizing entropy related to the projected data, ensuring that the information is preserved post-projection while controlling inter-cluster variances.

3. **Metric Learning by Information Geometry**: This approach incorporates information geometry principles, utilizing kernels based on data and labels. It introduces a kernel matrix that enhances label representation, ensuring full rank by adding a small positive constant. The projection matrix is used to derive the metric subspace, facilitating effective data representation.

These methodologies collectively contribute to advancing metric learning, particularly in handling complex data distributions and improving classification tasks through enhanced distance metrics.
