The paper discusses metric learning on Stiefel and symmetric positive definite (SPD) manifolds, focusing on optimization approaches in these contexts. It highlights how the weight matrix can be decomposed, allowing for the learning of matrices through optimization. The optimization problem involves minimizing a loss function that incorporates both pairs of similar and dissimilar samples, subject to constraints defining the Stiefel and SPD manifolds. Riemannian optimization methods are suggested for solving this problem.

Additionally, the paper introduces Curvilinear Distance Metric Learning (CDML), which reformulates the generalized Mahalanobis distance to account for geodesic curves on manifolds. This method aims to find the nearest neighbor on a geodesic rather than a straight line, leading to a modified distance metric defined through the arc length of the geodesic. The optimization in CDML minimizes a loss function over point pairs while incorporating regularization and penalty terms. The paper references a follow-up study that builds upon CDML, indicating ongoing research in this area.
