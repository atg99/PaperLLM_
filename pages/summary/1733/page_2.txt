Existing zero-shot learning (ZSL) approaches struggle with generalized zero-shot learning (GZSL) tasks due to a tendency to overfit source classes, leading to inadequate knowledge transfer to target classes. This issue arises because these models are trained solely on source categories, neglecting the target categories, which results in poor performance when faced with domain shifts. To address this, the authors propose a novel transferable contrastive network aimed at effectively transferring knowledge from source classes to target categories. 

The core idea involves learning target classifiers using similar source images, such as using horse images to help classify zebras. The proposed network utilizes a contrastive learning process that assesses the consistency between images and their class semantics, focusing on two key properties: discriminative and transferable properties. The discriminative property ensures the model can distinguish between different classes, while the transferable property enables knowledge transfer to target classes, enhancing robustness despite the absence of labeled target images.

The paper highlights two main contributions: the introduction of a new network structure for contrastive learning in GZSL and the integration of both discriminative and transferable properties in the learning process. Experimental results on five benchmark datasets demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed method.

Additionally, the paper discusses the importance of semantic information in ZSL, particularly attributes and word vectors, with a preference for attributes due to their accuracy. It also reviews various visual-semantic transformation approaches, categorizing them into visual to semantic embeddings, semantic to visual embeddings, and latent space embeddings, each with distinct methodologies for bridging visual and semantic spaces.
