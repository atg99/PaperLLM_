The paper presents a novel transferable contrastive network (TCN) aimed at improving generalized zero-shot learning (GZSL). The TCN contrasts images with class semantics to evaluate their consistency, focusing on two key properties: discriminative and transferable. The discriminative property allows effective classification of different classes, while the transferable property enhances robustness to target classes, addressing the issue of overfitting to source classes.

The authors demonstrate the model's effectiveness through extensive experiments on five benchmark datasets, highlighting its ability to recognize target-class samples despite training only on source-class images. The results indicate that the TCN can differentiate between similar classes, such as bobcat and leopard, due to explicit knowledge transfer based on class similarities. This capability ensures that target-class images are effectively recognized when encountered.

The paper concludes that the proposed approach outperforms existing methods in GZSL tasks by leveraging the strengths of contrastive learning. The research is supported by various funding programs in China.
