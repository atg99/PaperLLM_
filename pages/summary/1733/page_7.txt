The paper investigates the effectiveness of knowledge transfer in zero-shot learning (ZSL) and generalized zero-shot learning (GZSL) models. It highlights the importance of balancing the focus between source and target classes to prevent overfitting. The authors demonstrate that their approach, which includes a knowledge transfer term, significantly improves GZSL performance, although ZSL improvements are modest.

The analysis of the parameter α reveals that a smaller value (0.01) yields the best results, indicating that class similarities are not precisely defined and that source images do not perfectly match target classes. As α increases, source class performance declines due to the model's increased focus on target classes.

Visualization of class similarities in datasets such as AWA1 and APY shows the potential for knowledge transfer, where similar source classes can aid in learning target classes. The paper concludes that while class similarities are beneficial for knowledge transfer, they can be inconsistent in coarse-grained datasets like APY, leading to lower recognition accuracy.
