The paper discusses zero-shot learning (ZSL) and generalized zero-shot learning (GZSL) using various datasets: APY, AWA1, AWA2, CUB, and SUN. It provides statistics for these datasets, detailing the number of images, attributes, and classes for training and validation.

In the context of ZSL, the authors propose a method that classifies images based on contrastive values against class semantics. For ZSL, it selects the class with the highest contrastive value among target classes, while for GZSL, it considers all classes. The experiments utilize ResNet101 for feature extraction and a two-layer neural network for class semantic transformation.

The performance of the proposed method, termed TCN, is evaluated against several state-of-the-art approaches. Results indicate that TCN achieves the best performance on three datasets and is competitive on SUN, demonstrating effective knowledge transfer to target classes. The success of TCN is attributed to the discriminative nature of contrastive learning and the effective transfer of knowledge due to the similarity of fine-grained classes. However, lower performance on the APY dataset is noted, attributed to weak relations between source and target classes.

Overall, the findings highlight the potential of the transferable contrastive network in fine-grained recognition tasks.
