The paper presents a comparison of various methods for Generalized Zero-Shot Learning (GZSL) across multiple datasets, including APY, AWA1, AWA2, CUB, and SUN. The results are summarized in tables showing Top-1 accuracy for target and source classes, as well as the harmonic mean (H) for each method. The authors argue that traditional methods often overfit source classes, leading to poor performance on target classes due to a lack of knowledge transfer during model training. 

In contrast, the proposed TCN (Transfer Contrastive Network) approach effectively mitigates this issue by explicitly transferring knowledge between similar classes, resulting in more balanced performance across both source and target classes. The results indicate that TCN outperforms baseline methods by achieving higher accuracy in both zero-shot and GZSL tasks, demonstrating its robustness and simplicity compared to other generative approaches. The authors emphasize the importance of knowledge transfer in improving model performance, suggesting that leveraging class similarities enhances the learning process for target classes.
