The paper discusses the performance of the MAD-GAN model in anomaly detection for the unbalanced SWaT and WADI datasets, highlighting significant challenges related to precision and subsequence resolution. The F1 scores across different principal spaces were notably low, attributed to a high number of false positives, particularly for the SWaT dataset. The authors emphasize the importance of selecting an optimal subsequence length, finding that a smaller length (sw=30) yielded better precision and recall metrics for the SWaT dataset compared to larger lengths, which resulted in worse precision due to increased false positives.

For the WADI dataset, F1 scores remained poor, below 0.2, while recall values were acceptable, reinforcing the notion that false alarms are tolerable in intrusion detection applications. The authors observed fluctuations in performance across various subsequence lengths, suggesting that a small subsequence length could be advantageous for systems with rapid data recording and response times.

The paper also addresses the stability of MAD-GAN across training epochs, noting large variances in recall values. Initial improvements in performance were followed by oscillations at higher recall levels, indicating instability in the GAN-based anomaly detection approach. The authors propose that further research should focus on developing methods to enhance the stability of GAN-trained models for anomaly detection. Overall, the findings advocate for a principled approach to determining optimal subsequence resolutions and improving the stability of GAN models in time-series analysis.
