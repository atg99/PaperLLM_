Probabilistic and density estimation-based methods involve modeling the data distribution and identifying outliers based on their likelihood of occurrence within that distribution.

Deep learning-based methods have gained traction due to their ability to capture complex patterns in data. These methods typically involve training neural networks to learn representations of normal data, which are then used to identify anomalies by measuring deviations from these learned representations.

The paper proposes a novel approach for detecting anomalies in multivariate time series data using a Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) framework, termed Multivariate Anomaly Detection with GAN (MAD-GAN). This method leverages the strengths of GANs, which include the simultaneous training of a generator and a discriminator, to model complex multivariate correlations effectively. The GAN-trained discriminator is particularly suited for this task as it learns to distinguish between real and generated data in an unsupervised manner.

The proposed method builds upon previous research that utilized GANs for generating time series data and exploring latent space representations for anomaly detection. By integrating both reconstruction and discrimination losses from the GAN framework, MAD-GAN aims to enhance the accuracy of anomaly detection in complex cyber-physical systems (CPS).

The structure of the paper includes an overview of related works, a detailed introduction of the MAD-GAN framework and the anomaly scoring function, experimental results on real-world CPS datasets, and a conclusion that discusses future research directions.
