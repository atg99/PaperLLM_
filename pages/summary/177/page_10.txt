The paper evaluates the performance of the MAD-GAN model for anomaly detection using two datasets, SWaT and WADI. To manage computational efficiency, the authors applied Principal Component Analysis (PCA) to reduce the dimensionality of the data based on the variance rate of the principal components. They compared MAD-GAN's anomaly detection performance with several popular unsupervised methods, including PCA, K-Nearest Neighbour (KNN), Feature Bagging (FB), and Auto-Encoder (AE), as well as a Genetic Algorithm-based method, EGAN. 

The results show that at early training stages, the samples generated by MAD-GAN were random and distinct from the actual data. However, as training progressed, the generated samples closely matched the distribution of the original data, indicating the effectiveness of the model in generating realistic multivariate data from the cyber-physical systems. The visualization emphasized that no PCA projection was applied to the real samples during this comparison.
