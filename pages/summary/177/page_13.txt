The paper discusses the performance of the MAD-GAN model for multivariate anomaly detection in time series data, specifically examining the impact of principal component (PC) resolutions on detection metrics. The analysis utilizes data from two datasets, SWaT and WADI, with a focus on how dimensionality reduction through principal components affects the model's effectiveness.

Table 3 presents key anomaly detection metrics, including precision, recall, and F1 score for various PC resolutions from 1 to 10, indicating that while the first principal component alone explained over 50% of the variance in the SWaT dataset, it resulted in unsatisfactory recall rates below 75%. The performance improved significantly when the second principal component was included, leading to recall rates exceeding 90% when using the first two components, which together explained over 80% of the variance.

Further analysis shows that using the first five principal components, which accounted for over 99.5% of the variance, achieved a recall rate higher than 95%. Although this was slightly lower than the recall with only two components, the precision was improved, suggesting that careful selection of feature subsets is critical to minimizing false positives in anomaly detection. Overall, the findings underscore the importance of balancing dimensionality reduction and the retention of critical information for effective anomaly detection using MAD-GAN.
