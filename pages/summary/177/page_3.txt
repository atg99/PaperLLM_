The paper presents MAD-GAN, a novel method for multivariate anomaly detection in time series data using Generative Adversarial Networks (GAN). Traditional methods like Partial Least Squares (PLS) and distance-based approaches like K-Nearest Neighbor (KNN) and Clustering-Based Local Outlier Factor (CBLOF) have limitations, particularly in their reliance on data distribution assumptions and prior knowledge about anomalies. Probabilistic and density estimation methods, while improvements, fail to account for temporal correlations in multivariate time series data.

In contrast, deep learning techniques, particularly unsupervised methods like Auto-Encoders and LSTM Encoder-Decoder, have shown promise. MAD-GAN builds upon this success by adapting the GAN framework for time series analysis through Long Short-Term Recurrent Neural Networks (LSTM-RNN) to capture temporal dependencies. The architecture includes a generator that creates synthetic time series data and a discriminator that distinguishes between real and generated sequences. 

MAD-GAN employs a novel anomaly scoring mechanism that integrates both the discriminator's outputs and the reconstruction errors of the generator. This approach allows for a more robust detection of anomalies, specifically in the context of cyber-attacks, outperforming existing methods on two Cyber-Physical System (CPS) datasets. The architecture emphasizes the importance of considering entire variable sets simultaneously to model their latent interactions effectively.
