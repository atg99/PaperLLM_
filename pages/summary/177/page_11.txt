The paper discusses the use of MAD-GAN, a Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) framework, for multivariate anomaly detection in time series data. The authors employ Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) as a metric to evaluate the model's ability to learn the distributions of training data. MMD values, plotted across GAN training iterations, demonstrate convergence to lower values after 30-50 iterations for both datasets, indicating effective training. Notably, multivariate samples showed lower early MMD values and faster convergence compared to univariate samples, suggesting that multiple data streams enhance GAN training.

In terms of anomaly detection performance, results are summarized in Table 2, highlighting MAD-GAN's superiority over traditional unsupervised methods such as PCA, KNN, FB, and AE. Specifically, for the SWaT dataset, MAD-GAN significantly outperformed AE by 26.34% in precision and 11.11% in recall, achieving nearly perfect detection without false alarms. For the WADI dataset, while MAD-GAN's recall was slightly lower than AE's, it still surpassed other methods by a substantial margin, achieving nearly 100% recall, which is crucial in cyber-attack scenarios where false positives can be tolerated. The authors note that the WADI dataset's imbalance and larger feature dimension compared to SWaT contribute to the differences in performance, with MAD-GAN performing notably better on the SWaT dataset.
