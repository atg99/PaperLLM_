The paper discusses a multivariate approach for anomaly detection in time series data using a model called MAD-GAN, which leverages Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs). It emphasizes the importance of considering correlations among multiple variables, as changes in performance across different sub-processes can aid in recognizing attacks more effectively.

In the experiments, the authors utilized the SWaT and WADI datasets, which include a significant amount of data collected under normal conditions and during cyber-attacks. For the SWaT dataset, 51 variables were measured over 11 days, while the WADI dataset involved 103 variables over 16 days. The initial samples during system stabilization were removed to ensure data quality.

The anomaly detection process involved segmenting the raw data into smaller time series using a sliding window technique, with various window sizes tested to optimize representation. The model employed a Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) network architecture, consisting of a three-layer generator with 100 hidden units and a simpler one-layer discriminator.

Evaluation metrics for the model's performance included Precision, Recall, and F1 scores. Given the focus on detecting cyber-attacks, Recall was prioritized as the primary metric, highlighting the model's ability to identify true anomalies, even at the cost of some false positives. This approach underscores the necessity of detecting all attacks in security contexts, making recall a critical measure for the success of the anomaly detection system.
