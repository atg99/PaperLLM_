The paper discusses the impact of cyber-attacks on water treatment systems, specifically focusing on the Reverse Osmosis (RO) feed tank and the water level sensor in different processing units. In the first example, an attacker manipulated the water level sensor LIT401, which led to a significant reduction in the RO feed tank level, causing the pump P401 to stop and decreasing the output flow rate of treated water. The required flow rate was approximately 1.2 cm/hr, equivalent to nearly 5 gallons per minute, but the attack resulted in a reduced output during the observation period.

In another instance, the attacker tampered with the water level sensor in P1, altering its reading from 76% to 10%. This false low reading prompted the PLC-1 to activate the intake water pump, while simultaneously cutting off the water supply from P1 to P2, leading to a critical reduction in water levels at P2. This resulted in potential overflow in P1 and a complete halt of water flow to consumer tanks in P2.

The paper presents datasets from two systems, SWaT and WADI, highlighting their characteristics, including the number of variables, types of attacks, and data collection duration. The SWaT/WADI data collection spanned 11 to 16 days, during which various cyber-attacks were executed with differing intents and durations. Notably, the effects of attacks on one sensor can influence the performance of others, and similar sensor types tend to respond similarly to attacks. The findings underscore the complexity of detection tasks in cybersecurity for water treatment systems.
