The paper presents MAD-GAN, a novel approach for multivariate anomaly detection in time series data using Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs). As cyber-physical systems (CPSs) such as smart buildings and power plants generate vast amounts of multivariate time series data, monitoring for anomalies is critical due to their vulnerability to cyber-attacks. Traditional methods, including threshold-based and supervised machine learning techniques, fall short in handling the dynamic complexities and lack of labeled data in these systems. Current unsupervised approaches also fail to leverage the spatial-temporal correlations among multiple variables effectively.

MAD-GAN addresses these limitations by employing Long-Short-Term-Memory Recurrent Neural Networks (LSTM-RNN) within the GAN framework to capture temporal relationships in the data. Unlike conventional methods that analyze each data stream independently, MAD-GAN evaluates the entire set of variables simultaneously, allowing for a more comprehensive understanding of latent interactions. The framework introduces a novel anomaly score, DR-score, which utilizes both the generator and discriminator from the GAN for anomaly detection through discrimination and reconstruction.

The efficacy of MAD-GAN was tested on two real-world CPS datasets: Secure Water Treatment (SWaT) and Water Distribution (WADI). Experimental results demonstrate that MAD-GAN effectively identifies anomalies related to various cyber intrusions within these complex systems. This work highlights the potential of GANs for enhancing anomaly detection capabilities in dynamic environments.
