In this paper, the authors present MAD-GAN, an unsupervised anomaly detection framework based on Generative Adversarial Networks (GAN). The framework consists of a generator (G) and a discriminator (D) that undergo iterative adversarial training. The generator captures the hidden multivariate distributions of normal training sequences, while the discriminator is trained to distinguish between real (normal) and fake (abnormal) data.

The anomaly detection process leverages both G and D, employing two main strategies: reconstruction and discrimination. The reconstruction aspect involves calculating residuals between real-time testing samples and their reconstructed counterparts generated by G. The discrimination aspect uses D to classify the time series data. Testing samples are processed by mapping them into the latent space, which allows for the computation of reconstruction loss and discrimination loss.

The authors introduce a novel metric called the Discrimination and Reconstruction Anomaly Score (DR-Score) that combines these losses to identify potential anomalies in the data. The problem is formulated with a training dataset assumed to contain only normal instances, and the goal is to assign binary labels to the testing dataset’s measurements, indicating whether they are normal or anomalous. The study highlights the use of a sliding window technique to segment multivariate time series into sub-sequences for effective analysis.
