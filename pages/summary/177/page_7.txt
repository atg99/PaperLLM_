The paper discusses the Secure Water Treatment (SWaT) system and the Water Distribution (WADI) testbed as platforms for studying cyber-attacks in water management systems. The SWaT system is a small-scale operational testbed designed to mimic real-world water treatment plants, with data collection spanning 11 days, during which 36 attacks were executed. These attacks targeted various sensors and actuators, with varying intents and durations. The water purification process in SWaT includes six key subprocesses, from raw water intake to storage for distribution.

WADI, an extension of SWaT, represents a more complex water distribution network that includes raw water intake and distribution processes. It features multiple tanks and can simulate both cyber and physical attacks, such as water leakage and chemical injections. Data collection for WADI lasted 16 days, with 14 days of normal operation and 2 days involving attack scenarios.

The paper highlights that attackers aim to disrupt normal operations, assuming remote access to the SCADA systems of both testbeds. A total of 36 attacks were conducted on SWaT and 15 on WADI, with one example being an attack on sensor LIT401 in SWaT designed to reduce water flow performance. The research emphasizes the importance of understanding these vulnerabilities to enhance system security.
