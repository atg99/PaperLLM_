The paper presents an anomaly detection strategy utilizing a Long Short-Term Memory Recurrent Neural Network Generative Adversarial Network (LSTM-RNN-GAN). The algorithm operates in a loop, iterating through a specified number of training epochs. During each epoch, the generator produces samples from a latent space, which are then evaluated by a discriminator.

The discriminator's parameters are updated through two loss minimization processes: one for the real samples and another for the generated samples. The process is designed to refine the discriminator's ability to distinguish between actual data and generated data.

For testing, the algorithm maps the testing data back to the latent space and calculates the residuals between the original testing data and the reconstructed data. Discrimination results from the discriminator are also computed. A combined anomaly score, referred to as the DR-Score (DRS), is derived by integrating the reconstruction residuals and the discrimination results across various sub-sequences of the time series data.

The anomaly detection loss is defined as a weighted combination of the reconstruction error and the discriminator output. The final DR-Score aggregates these losses for each test data sub-sequence, facilitating the identification of anomalies in the time series data. The model parameters are updated using mini-batch stochastic optimization methods, specifically the Adam Optimizer and Gradient Descent Optimizer.
