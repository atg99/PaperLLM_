For simpler problems like OMcT, HOpt provides some benefit, although the models already exhibit good robustness without it. Conversely, in more complex scenarios such as TqA, HOpt can potentially decrease model robustness. This issue can be mitigated through techniques like dimension reduction and the removal of mixed variables.

The relationship between HOpt and training costs is ambiguous; training time appears to depend more on MLA model architecture than on the specifics of the design problem. Understanding the architecture of MLA models can aid in estimating and minimizing training durations.

Overall, HOpt may be less reliable for complex design problems but can enhance modeling performance when fewer design variables are involved. Further dimension reduction may negate the necessity for HOpt. The study recommends using Gaussian Process Regression (GPR) for complex issues due to its efficiency and accuracy. The findings indicate that HOpt yields minimal improvements in simpler design challenges. The paper concludes with a proposed framework for optimizing hyperparameters in MLA surrogate models including GPR, SVM, RFR, and ANN.
