The paper discusses the role of optimizers in training artificial neural networks (ANNs) and the implications of various learning rates on convergence. Specifically, it highlights the stochastic gradient descent (SGD) method, which updates weights based on the current gradient, learning rate, and momentum. A large learning rate can enhance convergence speed but risks overshooting the optimum, while a small rate increases training costs. The study limits ANN training epochs to 2,000, demonstrating this is sufficient for convergence.

Additionally, the paper outlines measures of accuracy, emphasizing statistical indices like root mean square error (RMSE) and maximum absolute error (MXAE) as key metrics for evaluating surrogate modeling accuracy. These measures are derived from physical quantities relevant to design objectives, ensuring consistency across different scenarios. The paper employs RMSE and MXAE as objective measures within its optimization framework, aiming to assess the modeling accuracy of machine learning algorithms (MLAs).

The optimization formulation is discussed, with RMSE and MXAE serving as objectives for hyperparameter optimization (HOpt). For each HOpt task, 30 initial designs are generated, which sets the stage for further analysis on the impact of HOpt on computational costs and the selection of suitable MLAs.
