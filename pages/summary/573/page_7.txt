The paper discusses the optimization process for hyperparameters using a multi-objective hyperparameter optimization (HOpt) framework. The methodology involves an iterative approach where an optimal design is added to a hyperparameters dataset, updating a Random Forest Regression (RFR) surrogate model for subsequent iterations until a specified number of iterations is reached. A Pareto front is generated based on the optimal acquisition strategy.

Key to this process is the use of the Lower Confidence Bound (LCB) as an acquisition criterion, which balances exploration and exploitation in the search for optimal hyperparameters. The LCB is defined mathematically, incorporating the posterior mean and standard deviation of accuracy measures derived from cross-validation. The weight of the standard deviation is adjusted based on the nature of the hyperparameters (numerical or categorical).

The design space is refined iteratively through a shrinking procedure aimed at reaching the final optimum efficiently. Previous works cited in the paper have explored similar methodologies, including generative design systems and genetic algorithms, but the current study emphasizes a semi-automated process replacing human evaluation with surrogate models like RFR. The RFR method is particularly effective in managing categorical parameters while maintaining lower computational costs. The iterative design space shrinking is repeated with varied initial designs to enhance the likelihood of achieving the true optimum.
