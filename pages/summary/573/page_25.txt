This paper investigates the application of surrogate modeling and Hyperparameter Optimization (HOpt) methods on four benchmark examples to assess their impact on the accuracy, robustness, and computational costs of surrogate models. The findings suggest that HOpt generally enhances machine learning algorithm (MLA)-based surrogate models by reducing error rates. However, the effectiveness of HOpt varies based on the complexity of the specific problems being addressed. The performance of HOpt is less stable for complex problems involving mixed variables and higher-dimensional design spaces. 

Gaussian Process Regression (GPR) is recommended in conjunction with HOpt due to its low training cost and relatively high accuracy. In the analysis of training costs, it was found that Artificial Neural Networks (ANN) exhibited the highest sensitivity to HOpt, with training time closely linked to the learning rate and mini-batch size, where a suitable mini-batch size can enhance convergence and reduce costs. Random Forest Regression (RFR) showed a linear relationship between training costs and the scale of the forest. GPR and Support Vector Machines (SVM) displayed insensitivity to hyperparameter tuning, although SVM can incur high computational costs under specific conditions.

A parametric study highlighted the influence of hyperparameters on Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) and Maximum Absolute Error (MXAE), recommending polynomial and Laplacian kernels for GPR to achieve high modeling accuracy, alongside low-degree polynomial kernels and low sigma values for SVM. For ANN, the use of tanh or ReLU activation functions with stochastic gradient descent (SGD) or Adagrad optimizers yielded favorable performance.

Future work will focus on integrating the developed codes into software with a graphical user interface, with the potential for open-source availability. A code repository is provided for access.
