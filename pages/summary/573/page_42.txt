The paper discusses the performance of various activation functions in artificial neural networks (ANNs) and their suitability for modeling non-linear responses, specifically in the context of ShB. The ReLU activation function shows low RMSE accuracy due to its linear nature, which limits its effectiveness after passing through a hidden layer. In contrast, the other three activation functions, being non-linear, yield similar accuracy levels, with the tanh function achieving rapid and stable convergence due to its significant value variation within the range of [0, 1]. Consequently, tanh is preferred for predicting non-linear responses.

The study also emphasizes the advantages of multi-layered ANNs in learning complex non-linear responses. Four multi-layered models with optimal hyperparameters were developed to assess the impact of layer complexity on training outcomes. Among these, the model configuration 7-20-1 demonstrated the best performance. The 7-10-5-1 model exhibited good accuracy but slightly lower performance due to its limited number of weights compared to the training dataset size. The remaining models, which significantly increased the number of weights, experienced unstable convergence and reduced accuracy, indicating that the number of weights is critical for effectively training an accurate ANN, more so than simply increasing the number of layers. 

Overall, the findings illustrate the importance of activation functions and model architecture in optimizing ANN performance for non-linear response prediction.
