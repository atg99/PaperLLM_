This study focuses on optimizing machine learning algorithms (MLAs) through a multiobjective hyperparameter optimization (HOpt) framework, utilizing a sequential model-based optimization (SMBO) approach. The primary goal is to formulate the HOpt problem by identifying objectives and tuning hyperparameters within specified ranges. 

The methodology begins with the design of experiments (DoE), where combinations of hyperparameters are generated using Latin hypercube sampling (LHS). Each combination is evaluated by training the corresponding MLA, with model accuracy serving as the response variable. A Random Forest Regression (RFR) surrogate model is employed to capture the relationship between hyperparameters and accuracy measures, particularly due to its ability to handle categorical features directly, unlike other methods that convert categorical data into numerical values.

The optimization process continues iteratively, predicting optimal hyperparameters and evaluating them through MLA training until a termination criterion is met. This iterative refinement leads to the identification of the ultimate optimum hyperparameters for the specific MLA in question. The study demonstrates that the proposed framework efficiently navigates the hyperparameter space to enhance MLA performance.
