This paper investigates the impact of hyperparameter optimization (HOpt) on the accuracy and robustness of different MLA models, specifically comparing their performance before and after optimization.

The study finds that HOpt generally enhances the performance of MLA models, although the extent of improvement is influenced by the complexity of the model. For complex problems characterized by high-dimensional mixed-variable design spaces, the improvements in accuracy and robustness become unstable. Furthermore, the research examines the additional computational costs associated with HOpt, revealing that the training costs vary significantly among different MLA architectures. Specifically, artificial neural networks (ANN) and random forest regression (RFR) experience a greater increase in training costs post-optimization compared to Gaussian process regression (GPR) and support vector machines (SVM).

The findings of this study provide valuable insights for selecting appropriate HOpt methods tailored to different design challenges based on their complexity, which includes factors like design domain continuity and the number of design variables. The paper emphasizes the necessity of an effective surrogate modeling approach to support engineering design and optimization processes while acknowledging the limitations of traditional surrogate methods in providing universally accurate predictions.
