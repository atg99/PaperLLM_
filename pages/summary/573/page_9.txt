The paper discusses the importance of a sufficiently large training dataset for achieving convergence and modeling accuracy in surrogate models. It cites Yang et al. (2005) and Shi et al. (2012), who established that three times the number of design variables (VN) is the minimum sample size needed. Xu et al. (2016) introduced a polynomial coefficient metric to assess sample size adequacy based on the nonlinearity of design problems, concluding that 1,000 data points are adequate for the current study.

The paper also addresses the issue of varying magnitudes among design variables, which may skew the model's performance. To mitigate this, min-max normalization is applied to scale the design variables into a uniform range of [0, 1].

Further, the paper identifies the significance of hyperparameter optimization for machine learning algorithms (MLA) in enhancing model accuracy. It details kernel functions for Gaussian Process Regression (GPR), including radial basis, polynomial, hyperbolic tangent, and Laplacian kernels, which are crucial for modeling complexity and nonlinearity. Additionally, it mentions the tuning of control parameters for Support Vector Machines (SVM), which transform design variables into a higher-dimensional space for linear regression.
