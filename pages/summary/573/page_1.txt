This paper explores the impact of hyperparameter optimization on machine learning algorithms used as surrogate models in structural design problems. The authors highlight the importance of surrogate models in reducing the computational costs associated with expensive finite element simulations. Machine learning algorithms are effective in capturing complex relationships between design variables and responses from large datasets. 

The distinction between model parameters, which are derived from training data, and hyperparameters, which are predetermined by users, is emphasized. The study identifies a gap in systematic research regarding the influence of hyperparameters on the accuracy and robustness of surrogate models. 

To address this, the authors propose a hyperparameter optimization framework based on sequential model-based optimization. This method generates a Pareto front by iteratively updating the surrogate model through optimal acquisition and design space shrinking processes. The surrogate model, informed by hyperparameter combinations sourced via Latin hypercube sampling, is utilized to evaluate modeling accuracy.

The effectiveness of this framework is demonstrated through experiments involving four commonly used machine learning algorithms: Gaussian Process Regression, Support Vector Machine, Random Forest Regression, and Artificial Neural Network, tested across four benchmark structural design optimization scenarios. The study aims to provide a deeper understanding of how hyperparameter optimization influences model performance in engineering design contexts.
