The paper emphasizes the importance of hyperparameters in MLAs, which significantly influence model flexibility, accuracy, and robustness. Despite their critical role, systematic studies on the impact of hyperparameters in MLA-based surrogate modeling are lacking, particularly in the field of structural design.

Existing research often relies on default hyperparameter values or subjective trial-and-error methods, resulting in underutilization of MLAs' potential. To address this gap, the authors advocate for the use of Hyperparameter Optimization (HOpt) in structural design. HOpt treats hyperparameters as design variables and employs optimization methods to identify optimal values, leading to improved prediction accuracy, as demonstrated in various public datasets.

The study introduces a multi-objective HOpt framework to evaluate the surrogate modeling performance of four commonly used MLAs: Gaussian Process Regression (GPR), Support Vector Machine (SVM), Random Forest Regression (RFR), and Artificial Neural Network (ANN). These models are chosen for their relevance in structural engineering applications. The paper presents a comprehensive analysis of the models' accuracy, training cost, and robustness, both before and after applying HOpt. Additionally, it discusses the hyperparameter tuning of the two most effective MLAs.

In conclusion, the proposed method aims to enhance the surrogate modeling capabilities of MLAs in structural design, providing a pathway for more accurate and efficient engineering solutions.
