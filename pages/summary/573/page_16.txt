The study investigates the energy-absorbing efficiency of various thickness designs in a rigid wall impact scenario, utilizing a finite element (FE) model known as OMcT. The model simulates an impact at a velocity of 30 km/h with a mass block attached to reflect vehicle inertia. The coefficient force efficiency (CFE) is calculated to assess energy absorption, defined by the integral of average crushing force relative to peak crushing force. 

The impact speed is then increased to 50 km/h to align with the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration standards. Four models are simulated using both implicit and explicit solvers in ANSYS Workbench and LS-DYNA, respectively. A total of 1000 design cases are generated for machine learning algorithm (MLA) training.

Results indicate the generation of Pareto fronts through sequential model-based optimization (SMBO). While optimal hyperparameters may differ across datasets, the study finds that values from the Pareto front can be applicable to similar design challenges. The final optimization solution is selected based on user preferences, with a posteriori method employed to evaluate points on the Pareto front. Alternatives with high root mean square error (RMSE) or maximum absolute error (MAXE) are excluded, and a solution is randomly selected from the remaining options to ensure data generality.
