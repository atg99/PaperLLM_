The paper discusses the effectiveness of HOpt in enhancing the performance of various machine learning algorithms (MLAs) in structural design problems. HOpt demonstrates significant improvements in accuracy, particularly for the TqA and OMcT models when using Gaussian Process Regression (GPR) and Support Vector Machines (SVM). While HOpt also reduces errors in the TbPT and ShB models, the improvements are less pronounced. The degree of enhancement varies according to the complexity of the specific problem.

The robustness of MLAs is generally improved by HOpt, although there are exceptions for TqA and OMcT where the robustness of GPR, SVM, and Random Forest Regression (RFR) declines post-HOpt. Initial hyperparameter values for most MLAs are typically set based on prior knowledge or trial-and-error approaches. Figures presented in the paper illustrate the comparative accuracy of the models before and after applying HOpt, highlighting discrepancies between cross-validation errors and new dataset test errors, attributed to different training processes. Overall, the findings suggest that HOpt can effectively enhance both the accuracy and robustness of commonly used surrogate models in structural design.
