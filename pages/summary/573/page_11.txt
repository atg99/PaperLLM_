This paper discusses the suitability of artificial neural networks (ANNs) with a single hidden layer for modeling specific problems, particularly those related to structural design. It highlights that while deeper networks can enhance performance in tasks like vibration signal processing, natural language processing, and image processing, they are prone to overfitting in typical structural design scenarios. Consequently, a single hidden layer ANN structure is chosen for optimization.

The paper includes a detailed table of hyperparameters for four machine learning algorithms (MLAs), including Gaussian Process Regression (GPR), Support Vector Machines (SVM), Random Forest Regression (RFR), and ANN. Each MLA has specified initial values and ranges for various hyperparameters essential for model tuning. For the ANN, parameters such as the number of hidden neurons, activation functions, optimizers, mini-batch sizes, learning rates, and momentum are identified as crucial for training and optimization. The training process aims to adjust weights within a defined architecture to enhance model accuracy while managing the risk of overfitting. The mini-batch method is employed to expedite the training process by using subsets of the training data sequentially within each epoch.
