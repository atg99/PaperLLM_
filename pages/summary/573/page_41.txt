The paper discusses the effects of hyperparameters on a basic one-hidden-layer artificial neural network (ANN) using specific values: a batch size of 100, a learning rate of 0.1, 20 hidden neurons, Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD) as the optimizer, a tanh activation function, and a momentum of 0.9. Due to high computational costs, the ShB dataset is utilized exclusively, with all models trained over 10,000 epochs.

The study emphasizes the interaction between batch size and learning rate, represented in log scale contour plots. It finds that larger learning rates and smaller batch sizes accelerate convergence; however, excessively large learning rates can destabilize training and hinder optimal performance. In contrast, smaller mini-batch sizes, while potentially increasing convergence speed, raise communication costs related to synchronizing shared variables. The analysis identifies a learning rate of 0.1 as optimal, and a mini-batch size of 100 is selected to balance accuracy and communication costs.

Additional hyperparameters, such as the number of hidden neurons, optimizer type, activation function, and layer structure, are also evaluated. More hidden neurons can slow convergence, and 20 neurons are chosen for better accuracy compared to using 5. Among the optimizers tested, Adagrad demonstrates superior accuracy, stability, and convergence speed, making it a preferable choice over SGD, which, despite similar accuracy, suffers from slower convergence due to its complex training process. Overall, the findings provide insights into the selection and optimization of hyperparameters for effective ANN training.
