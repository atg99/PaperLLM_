This section of the paper focuses on the analysis of two vehicular components, specifically a thin-walled S-shaped beam (ShB) and a thin-walled octagonal multi-cell tube (OMcT), under dynamic loading conditions. These components are crucial for energy absorption during impacts in passenger cars, as they can undergo significant plastic deformation and dissipate kinetic energy effectively.

The ShB is characterized by seven design variables and has a total length of 1,000 mm. The finite element (FE) model of the ShB is subjected to a frontal impact at a speed of 10 m/s. The specific energy absorption (SEA) is defined in Equation (11), which integrates the impact force-displacement history to determine the energy absorbed relative to the structural mass.

The OMcT, measuring 310 mm in length, features a cross-section made up of inner and outer octagons with ribs. The dimensions for the inner and outer octagons are 30 mm and 60 mm, respectively.
