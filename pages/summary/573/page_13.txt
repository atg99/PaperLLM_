The paper discusses the construction of an initial surrogate model and the subsequent optimization of hyperparameters using 70 evaluations, totaling 100 iterations. The sufficiency of this sample size for ensuring the convergence of the Pareto front is validated through parametric analysis. A 5-fold cross-validation method is employed to train the model, which divides the dataset into five subsets for iterative training and validation. This approach helps mitigate bias and reduce overfitting compared to traditional methods. 

The objective is to minimize the Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) and Maximum Absolute Error (MXAE) subject to certain constraints related to integral, continuous, and categorical variables. These variables have defined domains as specified in the accompanying table. The datasets generated by Design of Experiments (DoE) are utilized for training Machine Learning Algorithms (MLAs), aimed at constructing accurate surrogate models for predicting structural responses.

The study also presents four benchmark engineering structures to illustrate the impact of hyperparameters through Hyperparameter Optimization (HOpt). The cases encompass various geometries, loading conditions, boundary conditions, and deformation modes, providing a broad applicability of the methods to different structural scenarios. Specifically, under static loading, the paper examines a ten-bar planar truss and a torque arm, commonly used to validate structural design algorithms.
