The paper discusses the evaluation of a model's robustness using the interquartile range, defined by the 25th and 75th percentiles. A smaller distance between the upper and lower bounds indicates higher robustness, while points outside this range are considered outliers. To validate the models, a new dataset generated by Latin Hypercube Sampling (LHS) includes 180 designs not present in the original cross-validation (CV) dataset. The distributions of this new dataset are compared with the CV datasets to ensure consistency.

Results indicate that the models trained with the original data exhibit improved prediction accuracy post-Hyperparameter Optimization (HOpt), as evidenced by reduced median Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) and Maximum Absolute Error (MXAE) values. The performance of the models is further assessed through test errors calculated with the new dataset, which shows varying degrees of performance across different machine learning algorithms, including Gaussian Process Regression (GPR), Support Vector Machines (SVM), Random Forest Regression (RFR), and Artificial Neural Networks (ANN). Figures and tables in the paper illustrate these findings, confirming the effectiveness of HOpt in enhancing model accuracy.
