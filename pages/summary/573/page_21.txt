The paper discusses the training time of various machine learning models, specifically Gaussian Process Regression (GPR), Support Vector Machine (SVM), Random Forest Regression (RFR), and Artificial Neural Networks (ANN), before and after hyperparameter optimization (HOpt). 

It highlights that GPR training is unaffected by hyperparameters regarding model size, while SVM training time increases significantly due to the influence of a large error penalization factor (C) and low epsilon bounds, which complicate the minimization of errors and raise training costs. Additionally, the training time for RFR is linked to the number of trees and the number of features (NF), with Min TS and Max TN having a lesser impact. A larger number of trees and features enhances prediction accuracy but also elevates training time due to increased model size.

Figures are referenced to illustrate the training time comparisons of the models across different tasks, emphasizing the differences before and after optimization. Overall, the findings underscore the trade-off between model complexity, training time, and prediction accuracy in machine learning frameworks.
