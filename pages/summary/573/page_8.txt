The paper discusses a framework for optimizing hyperparameters through a systematic iterative process. In each iteration, a group of design alternatives is sampled using Latin Hypercube Sampling (LHS) to ensure uniform distribution across the design space. The responses of these designs are predicted using a Random Forest Regression (RFR) surrogate model. The best alternative is then selected, and a smaller subspace is generated around this optimal point. This new subspace balances exploration and exploitation; a smaller subspace enhances convergence speed but risks missing the optimum, while a larger subspace captures the optimum but reduces convergence speed.

The (i+1)th subspace is defined as the intersection of the current hyperparameter space and an expanded area centered around the best design from the ith iteration. This process continues through a limited number of iterations until a termination criterion is met, resulting in an optimal design. To enhance robustness, the optimization process is repeated multiple times, generating designs from the Pareto front of each run. The overall Pareto front is then used to determine the hyperparameter values for corresponding machine learning algorithms.

The implementation of this hyperparameter optimization framework involves preparing the hyperparameter domain, measures, and formulation based on available datasets. The datasets for machine learning algorithm training are typically generated using design of experiments (DoE) methods, with LHS employed in this study to effectively fill the design space.
