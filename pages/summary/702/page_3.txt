The paper discusses the implementation of Q-learning and Deep Q-Networks (DQN) for optimizing the movement strategy of Unmanned Aerial Vehicle Base Stations (UAV-BSs). Q-learning aims to learn a policy that maximizes total rewards through a Q-Table, where the Q-value represents the total rewards from being in a specific state and taking an action. The Q-value is computed using the immediate reward and the maximum possible Q-value of the next state, adjusted by a discount factor for future rewards. The Q-Table is initialized to zeros, and an epsilon-greedy strategy is employed for action selection, balancing between exploring random actions and exploiting known Q-values.

However, traditional Q-learning struggles with large state spaces due to memory constraints. DQNs address this by utilizing neural networks to approximate Q-values, transforming the problem into a regression task. A target network with frozen parameters is introduced to stabilize training, and experience replay is employed to improve convergence by sampling from a memory pool of past experiences.

The paper further presents a dynamic movement strategy for UAV-BSs aimed at maximizing user data rates. The optimization problem is formulated to determine the optimal positions of UAV-BSs at each time instant, subject to constraints ensuring users are associated with the nearest base stations and only one base station at a time. The environment is characterized as partly random and partly stationary, following a Markov Decision Process (MDP) framework.

To implement the movement strategy, a reinforcement learning approach is proposed, considering the real-time locations of users following a random walk mobility model. This model allows for flexible adaptation of UAV-BS movements in response to user dynamics, aiming for optimal positioning to enhance overall data rates.
