The paper presents results on instance mask propagation and evaluates various methods on the DA VIS-2017 validation set, focusing on region similarity (J IoU) and contour-based accuracy (F). Table 2 summarizes performance metrics for different models, including SIFT Flow, Video Colorization, and V ADeR, under varying input resolutions. 

Table 3 details the performance of V ADeR across different view sampling strategies for semantic segmentation and dense correspondence, highlighting the effectiveness of fine-tuning methods. Figure 4 illustrates the results of fine-tuning for semantic segmentation in PASCAL VOC12 and depth prediction in NYU-d v2, demonstrating that V ADeR achieves performance comparable to MoCo when 100% of labeled data is used, while outperforming supervised ImageNet pretraining as labeled data decreases. 

In the context of object detection and instance segmentation, the paper employs Mask R-CNN with a Feature Pyramid Network (FPN) backbone, trained on COCO using standard metrics. Results in Table 4 show that V ADeR consistently surpasses both MoCo and the supervised baseline in object detection, instance segmentation, and keypoint detection tasks, emphasizing the advantages of learning dense representations over global ones. 

Overall, the findings support the assertion that self-supervised learning techniques like V ADeR are more effective in scenarios with limited labeled data compared to traditional supervised methods.
