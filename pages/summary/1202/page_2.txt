This paper introduces a novel method for unsupervised learning of dense visual representations, named View-Agnostic Dense Representation (V ADeR). The central concept of V ADeR is based on perceptual constancy, which posits that local visual features should remain consistent across varying viewpoints. This method contrasts local representations by ensuring that features corresponding to the same scene location are closely embedded, while those of different locations are distanced in the embedding space.

V ADeR employs an encoder-decoder architecture to compute pixel-level similarities, contrasting with existing methods that typically focus on global image-level representations. By utilizing known pixel correspondences from the view generation process, V ADeR effectively identifies matching features between views. This approach enhances the learning of dense features, making V ADeR applicable to various downstream tasks such as instance segmentation, object detection, and depth prediction.

The authors demonstrate that V ADeR outperforms traditional global contrastive self-supervised methods and even surpasses supervised pretraining on ImageNet across multiple tasks. The paper also reviews related work in self-supervised learning, highlighting the evolution of techniques from predictive tasks to contrastive learning, which distinguishes compatible and incompatible views of images.

Overall, V ADeR presents a significant advancement in the domain of unsupervised representation learning, focusing on dense features and leveraging perceptual invariants to improve performance across a range of visual understanding tasks.
