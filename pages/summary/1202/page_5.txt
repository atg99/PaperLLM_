The paper discusses the architecture and training methodology of the V ADeR model, focusing on its application in semantic segmentation and depth estimation as structured multi-class classification and regression problems, respectively. The model architecture utilizes a feature pyramid network (FPN) integrated with ResNet-50 to produce a multi-scale feature representation. This involves creating a pyramid of features with dimensions of 256 across four scales, followed by upsampling blocks to yield a final representation of dimension 128 at a scale of 1/4.

Training is conducted on the ImageNet-1K dataset with approximately 1.28 million images, employing stochastic gradient descent with specific weight decay and momentum settings. The model is trained using a batch size of 128 across 4 GPUs for roughly 6 million iterations, with distinct learning rates for the encoder and decoder. The training process incorporates a correspondence flow between pixel pairs from randomly sampled image views and applies loss functions to optimize the network parameters.

The authors initialize the encoder weights with MoCo to enhance training efficiency while the decoder weights are set randomly. The paper emphasizes the significance of MoCo as a baseline due to its state-of-the-art performance in unsupervised visual representation. The effectiveness of the learned features from V ADeR is evaluated across various downstream tasks, highlighting the model's potential for feature transferability in unsupervised learning contexts.
