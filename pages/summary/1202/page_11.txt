The referenced works span various approaches to visual representation learning, object recognition, and correspondence detection using both supervised and unsupervised techniques. 

Jakab et al. propose a method for unsupervised learning of object landmarks through conditional image generation, emphasizing the potential of generative models in landmark detection. Kanazawa et al. introduce WarpNet, focusing on weakly supervised matching for single-view reconstruction, showcasing the effectiveness of weak supervision in achieving accurate 3D reconstructions. Kim et al. present deformable spatial pyramid matching, which enhances the speed and accuracy of dense correspondences.

Kirillov et al. contribute to the field with Panoptic Feature Pyramid Networks, integrating features for object detection and segmentation. Kolesnikov et al. revisit self-supervised visual representation learning, highlighting its advancements and methodologies. Lai and Xie explore self-supervised learning for video correspondence flow, indicating its relevance in dynamic scenes.

LeCun et al. provide foundational knowledge on energy-based learning, while Li et al. propose a joint-task self-supervised learning approach for temporal correspondence. Lin et al. introduce Feature Pyramid Networks, which significantly improve object detection tasks. The Microsoft COCO dataset, discussed by Lin et al., serves as a critical benchmark for common objects in context.

Liu et al. present SIFT Flow, a technique for dense correspondence across scenes, with applications in various computer vision tasks. Long et al. investigate whether convolutional networks learn correspondence, contributing to understanding deep learning capabilities. Lowe's work on distinctive image features from scale-invariant keypoints remains a cornerstone in feature detection.

Mahendran et al. explore cross pixel optical-flow similarity for self-supervised learning, while Minderer et al. focus on unsupervised learning of object structure and dynamics from videos. Misra and van der Maaten advance self-supervised learning of pretext-invariant representations, emphasizing the importance of invariant features.

Nair and Hinton's research on rectified linear units enhances the performance of restricted Boltzmann machines, while Kohli et al. address indoor segmentation and support inference from RGBD images. Noroozi and Favaro's method for unsupervised learning through jigsaw puzzles demonstrates innovative approaches to visual representation. Novotny et al. present AnchorNet, a weakly supervised network for learning geometry-sensitive features, while Ono et al. introduce LF-Net for learning local features from images.

Lastly, van den Oord et al. discuss representation learning through contrastive predictive coding, and Pathak et al. present context encoders for feature learning via inpainting, showcasing the diverse methodologies employed in the field of visual learning.
