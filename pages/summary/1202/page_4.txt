In section 3.1, the paper introduces view-agnostic dense representations for pixels in images, denoted as (x, u), where x is the image and u is the pixel location. The authors build on self-supervised contrastive learning methods to define a view as a stochastic combination of data transformations that affect both the appearance and geometry of images. The goal is to create an embedding function using encoder-decoder convolutional networks, f and g, such that the embeddings of the same pixel across different views are invariant. This is mathematically represented by ensuring that the embeddings from different views are more compatible than embeddings from different pixels.

The paper proposes a contrastive learning approach utilizing a noise-contrastive estimation (NCE) loss, which treats the compatibility of pixel representations as a binary classification task. The loss function is constructed by comparing the embeddings of a pixel against a set of negative samples, ensuring that the model learns to distinguish between compatible and incompatible views.

In section 3.2, the authors detail the implementation of their approach. They describe the sampling of views through a combination of appearance and geometric transformations. Positive training pairs are created by sampling pairs of views from the same image, ensuring a sufficient number of matching pixels. The paper emphasizes the importance of negative samples, suggesting that using pixels from different images is more effective than using pixels from the same image. They employ a momentum contrast mechanism to manage a large number of negative samples efficiently, enhancing the training process.
