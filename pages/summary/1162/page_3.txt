This paper addresses the limitations of existing cost-sensitive Support Vector Machines (CS-SVMs) by proposing an alternative strategy that enhances their design without directly altering the standard SVM learning algorithm. The authors critique the biased penalties method, noting its inability to enforce cost-sensitivity effectively, especially when training data is separable, leading to a standard SVM optimization scenario.

The proposed method extends the SVM hinge loss and derives a cost-sensitive learning algorithm based on risk minimization principles, drawing on recent insights into probability elicitation. This innovative approach allows for the specification of desired predictor and conditional risk functions for the SVM classifier, leading to the development of a new hinge loss that optimally aligns with the cost-sensitive Bayes decision rule and approximates the cost-sensitive Bayes risk.

The paper provides sufficient conditions for achieving cost-sensitive Bayes-optimality and necessary conditions for approximating conditional risks. The authors demonstrate that the minimization of the new loss function generalizes the classic SVM optimization problem and can be solved using existing procedures. This results in a cost-sensitive algorithm that effectively addresses both separable and inseparable training data scenarios, yielding superior classification performance compared to previous methods.

Furthermore, the paper delves into the dual formulation of CS-SVMs, revealing that cost-sensitive learning manifests as regularization and adjustments in constraint upper bounds, particularly in the context of sensitivity analysis. The authors highlight the importance of incorporating priors and costs into performance metrics in cost-sensitive and imbalanced data settings. They propose a minimum expected cost-sensitive risk as a new performance metric and establish its relationship with the ROC curve. A robust measure for unknown costs is also introduced, aimed at reflecting classifier performance under specified tolerances for false-positive and false-negative errors.

The paper is structured into sections that first review loss function design through probability elicitation, then generalize these connections to the cost-sensitive framework, followed by the derivation of the new SVM loss in subsequent sections.
