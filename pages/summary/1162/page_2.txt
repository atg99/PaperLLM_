This paper addresses the challenge of designing cost-sensitive SVMs, highlighting that traditional SVMs rely on a symmetric hinge loss function, which lacks a clear cost-sensitive alternative.

The literature presents three main approaches to tackle this issue. The first involves data processing techniques, such as resampling methods that either under-sample the majority class or over-sample the minority class. However, these methods face challenges when class imbalance arises from differing misclassification costs or extreme probability disparities, and they may not influence the support vectors effectively.

The second approach modifies the SVM kernel through conformal transformations of the input space. While this method attempts to introduce cost sensitivity, it assumes that linear SVMs cannot be adjusted for cost sensitivity, a premise that remains questionable.

The third and most prevalent strategy involves modifying the SVM algorithm itself to incorporate cost sensitivity. This includes the boundary movement method (BM-SVM), which adjusts the decision boundary by changing the SVM threshold. Although theoretically optimal under Bayesian decision theory, this approach suffers from SVMs' inaccurate probability predictions and does not enhance cost-sensitive performance due to the nature of calibration techniques.

The second method within this category is biased penalties (BP-SVM), which introduces different penalty factors for positive and negative slack variables during training. This approach aims to directly address cost sensitivity in the learning process, marking a significant step forward in the development of cost-sensitive SVMs.
