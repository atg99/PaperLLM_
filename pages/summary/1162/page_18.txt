The paper discusses the performance of the Cost-Sensitive Support Vector Machine (CS-SVM) algorithm when applied to imbalanced datasets, specifically the covertype UCI dataset with a significant imbalance ratio of 1:211. It highlights how varying the regularization coefficient, denoted as ϕ, impacts the algorithm's behavior. When ϕ is set to 1, CS-SVM behaves like the Basic SVM (BP-SVM). As ϕ decreases, there is a noted reduction in the number of negative support vectors, which contributes to an improved balance in the ratio of support vectors, approaching 1 when ϕ is around 2.256.

The paper emphasizes the importance of selecting an appropriate value for ϕ. A very small value, such as 0.01, can effectively correct the decision boundary for highly imbalanced data, while values below 0.01 can lead to a preference for the majority class, thereby diminishing the algorithm's effectiveness. Figures presented in the paper illustrate these concepts, showing how the regularization impacts the sparsity of the solution and the distribution of support vectors across classes.

Additionally, the paper addresses cost-sensitive learning, noting that regularization can alter the margins for different classes based on their associated costs. Specifically, CS-SVM reduces the margin for the class with a lower cost and increases it for the class with a higher cost, thereby allowing for more effective handling of imbalanced datasets.
