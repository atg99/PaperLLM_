The paper by Masnadi-Shirazi, Vasconcelos, and Iranmehr discusses a performance measure for classifiers known as cost-sensitive zero-one risk, represented mathematically by RCS. This measure incorporates class priors and the rates of false negatives and false positives. The authors highlight that this performance measure simplifies to the well-known probability of error, referred to as cost insensitive risk.

To find the optimal cost-sensitive zero-one risk, the authors frame it as a vector optimization problem where classifiers produce vectors representing false positive and false negative rates. The minimal elements of these vectors form the Pareto optimal frontier, also known as the ROC curve. The optimization process involves minimizing a weighted sum of false positive and false negative rates, where weights correspond to the class priors and costs.

When class priors are known, they are utilized for classifier evaluation and threshold determination. However, in cases where costs or priors are unknown, relying on a single point on the ROC curve may not provide a robust measure. To address this, the authors propose evaluating the risk across various points on the ROC, leading to the introduction of TP-t-AUC and TN-t-AUC metrics, which assess the area under the ROC curve in specific true positive and true negative regions. These metrics are employed to demonstrate the capability of the CS-SVM in achieving high sensitivity and specificity in model learning.
