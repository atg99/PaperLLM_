The paper discusses cost-sensitive support vector machines (CS-SVM) and presents experimental results comparing three algorithms: BM-SVM, BP-SVM, and CS-SVM. The experiments are organized into four categories: cost-sensitive learning with known class-dependent costs, learning without class-dependent costs, example-dependent costs, and learning from imbalanced datasets.

The results are illustrated using ROC curves for the CI-SVM, BP-SVM, and CS-SVM on the German dataset, highlighting the performance of each model based on risk metrics. Key metrics include Risk*, TH Risk, and NOTH Risk, which represent different operational scenarios of the models. 

A total of 21 datasets, derived from 20 distinct sources, are analyzed to evaluate the effectiveness of the CS-SVM algorithm. Each dataset is tailored for specific experimental conditions, with the KDD98 dataset utilized for example-dependent cost experiments and others for imbalanced dataset learning. The study emphasizes the necessity of selecting the appropriate target class based on cost or data availability, with all multi-class datasets being converted into binary formats for analysis. 

Overall, the paper provides a comprehensive evaluation of cost-sensitive support vector machines, demonstrating their performance across various scenarios and datasets.
