The paper discusses the dual formulation of the CS-SVM (Cost-Sensitive Support Vector Machine) and its implications for imbalanced data learning and cost-sensitive learning. The dual variable is identified as z, with the dual decision function represented as g = K⁻¹z. The formulation presented in the paper contrasts the traditional SVM dual problem and highlights a unique duality property where the primal variable corresponds to the dual decision function.

The authors emphasize the role of the `1-norm regularization term and equality constraint in the dual problem, noting that these can apply to either the basis expansion coefficients or dual decision values. They argue that the CS-SVM's additional `1-norm regularization helps address the challenge of over-training on the majority class in imbalanced data scenarios by balancing the contribution of basis functions from each class.

In cost-sensitive learning contexts, where misclassification costs differ by class, the authors suggest that `1-norm regularization can be effectively employed to favor the learning of the lower-cost class. Overall, the paper illustrates how these modifications improve the SVM's performance in scenarios with imbalanced data and varying misclassification costs.
