The paper presents a novel approach for developing cost-sensitive Support Vector Machine (CS-SVM) classifiers. It extends the traditional SVM hinge loss to accommodate cost sensitivity, deriving the CS-SVM as the minimizer of the associated risk. This extension is grounded in recent insights linking risk minimization with probability elicitation, ensuring consistency with the cost-sensitive Bayes risk and the corresponding Bayes decision rule. Consequently, the optimal decision rules derived from this new hinge loss align with the Bayes-optimal cost-sensitive classification boundary.

The authors demonstrate that minimizing this new hinge loss generalizes the classic SVM optimization problem, allowing it to be solved using the same methodologies. A thorough examination of the dual problem of CS-SVM through regularization theory and sensitivity analysis is conducted, providing a solid foundation for the proposed algorithm. Additionally, the approach is adaptable to scenarios involving example-dependent costs.

The paper introduces the minimum cost-sensitive risk as a performance metric, linking it to Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) analysis via vector optimization. The proposed algorithm addresses limitations found in prior cost-sensitive SVM designs and exhibits enhanced performance across various cost-sensitive and imbalanced datasets. The introduction emphasizes the significance of cost-sensitive algorithms in applications such as medical diagnosis and fraud detection, where misclassification costs can vary significantly.
