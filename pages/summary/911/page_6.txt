Key findings include the effectiveness of different mini-batch mining strategies, where data diversity in mini-batches is critical, regardless of the training objective. The study reveals that simpler heuristics like SPC-2 can be just as effective as more complex methods, such as SPC-4, which is commonly used in literature.

The comparison of 14 DML objectives and four mining methods under fixed training conditions indicates that ranking-based models, particularly those utilizing distance-based tuple mining, demonstrate superior performance. The analysis showed that while margin loss and multisimilarity loss generally yield the best results, many simpler loss functions can achieve comparable, if not superior, outcomes under well-optimized training conditions. Notably, the performance saturation observed across methods challenges existing literature and highlights the importance of standard deviations in evaluating significance.

Finally, the paper discusses generalization within DML, linking it to the compression of learned representations. The study suggests that well-trained baseline models can outperform state-of-the-art methods, emphasizing the need for competitive baseline implementations in future evaluations. Overall, the research underscores the nuanced interplay between training strategies, model choices, and generalization performance in DML.
