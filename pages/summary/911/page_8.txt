The paper revisits training strategies and generalization performance in deep metric learning (DML). It examines the correlation between generalization and structural properties of learned representations, focusing on mean intra-class distances, inter-class distances, their ratio, and spectral decay. The findings indicate that generalization is inversely correlated with spectral decay and positively associated with embedding space density, suggesting that excessive feature compression negatively impacts representation learning, particularly when there are shifts between training and testing distributions.

To address this issue, the authors propose a simple method of ϵ-regularization for ranking-based DML approaches. This technique involves randomly altering tuples by switching negative samples with positive ones during training, which helps to prevent excessive compression and enables the model to capture additional non-label-discriminative features. The paper presents a toy example illustrating the benefits of this regularization method, highlighting how it allows for better separation of test classes and improved generalization.

The results demonstrate that ϵ-regularization reduces spectral decay and enhances recall performance across several benchmark datasets. The authors conclude that regulating the number of significant variance directions can lead to improved generalization in DML, ultimately contributing to better comparability and performance of DML approaches moving forward.
