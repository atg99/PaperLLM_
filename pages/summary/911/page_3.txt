In the paper "Revisiting Training Strategies and Generalization Performance in Deep Metric Learning," the authors focus on the training strategies for Deep Metric Learning (DML) models, emphasizing their impact on generalization performance. The section outlines the key components necessary for training DML models, categorizing them into three main areas: objective functions, data sampling, and model components.

In 3.1, the authors discuss various objective functions used in DML, which aim to learn an embedding function mapping data points into an embedding space. They categorize these functions into three families:

1. **Ranking-based:** These loss functions operate on pairs, triplets, or larger sets of data points, aiming to minimize distances between similar samples while maximizing distances between dissimilar samples.
2. **Classification-based:** These approaches derive from softmax logits, using techniques like regularization to maximize class margins through optimization over angles.
3. **Proxy-based:** These methods approximate class distributions by employing learned representatives, simplifying the training loss computation and alleviating tuple mining issues common in ranking-based methods.

In 3.2, the authors address the importance of data sampling strategies in DML, which has been underexplored in the field. They present two types of sampling methods: 

- **Label Samplers:** These heuristics select samples based on label information, allowing for control over class distribution within mini-batches.
- **Embedded Samplers:** These methods enhance data diversity by distilling information from larger sets into manageable mini-batches, utilizing a memory bank of embedded training samples to maintain effective training dynamics.

The paper highlights the significance of optimizing both the objective function and the data sampling process to improve the performance of DML models, ultimately contributing to a better understanding of their generalization capabilities.
