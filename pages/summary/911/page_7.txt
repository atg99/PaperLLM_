The paper "Revisiting Training Strategies and Generalization Performance in Deep Metric Learning" investigates the relationship between training strategies and generalization performance in deep metric learning (DML). It presents a comparative analysis of various DML approaches across three datasets: CUB200-2011, CARS196, and SOP, focusing on Recall@1 (R@1) and Normalized Mutual Information (NMI) as performance metrics.

Key findings indicate that while a strong emphasis on the most discriminative directions can enhance classification performance under independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) conditions, it may hinder generalization in scenarios with shifts between training and testing distributions. The authors hypothesize that retaining a broader range of directions of significant variance is essential for developing a well-generalizing embedding function.

To support this hypothesis, the paper employs singular value decomposition (SVD) to analyze the spectral decay of embedded training data. The researchers compute the Kullback-Leibler divergence of the sorted singular values against a uniform distribution, observing that lower divergence values correlate with better generalization performance. This relationship is substantiated by a significant inverse correlation between R@1 and the spectral decay of embedding spaces.

The paper also contrasts its findings with common training strategies that focus on maximizing inter-class distances and managing intra-class variance. It concludes that these distance-related measures do not consistently correlate with generalization performance, suggesting the need for a more comprehensive understanding of embedding spaces in DML.

Overall, the study emphasizes the importance of considering the diversity of variance directions in training to improve generalization capabilities in deep metric learning models.
