The paper "Revisiting Training Strategies and Generalization Performance in Deep Metric Learning" explores the relationship between training strategies and the generalization performance of deep metric learning (DML) models. The authors note that established metrics like inter-class margins and intra-class variance do not consistently correlate with generalization performance. Instead, they identify a strong correlation between generalization performance and the compression of learned representations, measured by spectral decay.

Key contributions of the paper include:

1. An extensive analysis of recent DML objective functions, training strategies, data-sampling methods, and model design choices, setting a benchmark for the field. The authors plan to make their code publicly available for further research.
   
2. New insights into DML generalization are provided, focusing on the relationship between embedding space compression, inter-class margins, and intra-class variance.

3. A proposed regularization technique aimed at reducing embedding space compression, which has been shown to enhance the generalization performance of ranking-based DML approaches.

The structure of the paper includes a review of related works, a discussion of the components analyzed in DML models, presentation of findings, an analysis of DML generalization, and a conclusion.

Additionally, the paper discusses various DML approaches, such as ranking-based objectives and classiﬁcation-based methods, highlighting the computational challenges of tuple-based methods and the introduction of various sampling strategies. It also emphasizes the importance of mini-batch selection and its impact on training efficiency and convergence.

Finally, the paper examines the generalization capabilities of DML representations, drawing on existing research to propose that excessive compression negatively impacts generalization. The authors aim to bridge the gap between theoretical insights and practical applications in DML.
