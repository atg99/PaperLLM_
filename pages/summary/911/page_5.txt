The paper "Revisiting Training Strategies and Generalization Performance in Deep Metric Learning" investigates the impact of various training strategies and parameters on the generalization performance of deep metric learning (DML) models. The authors utilize ResNet50 for their experiments due to its proven efficacy in recent state-of-the-art approaches. The training protocol includes resizing and cropping images to 224x224, applying random horizontal flipping, and optimizing with the Adam algorithm. They run experiments on multiple GPUs, ensuring results are averaged over different seeds to mitigate performance variability.

The study evaluates DML parameters and architectures by analyzing their influence on performance across five objectives. Key findings indicate that increasing batch size generally enhances results, particularly for the Stanford Online Products dataset. Weight decay shows variable effects based on loss and dataset, while data preprocessing methods significantly affect performance. Notably, larger images yield better results, and advanced augmentations like color jittering depend on the dataset. The authors emphasize the necessity of transparency in training protocols to improve reproducibility and comparability in DML research.

Additionally, the paper explores the effects of batch sampling strategies on DML training. By testing various objective functions and sampling methods, the authors find that the choice of sampling strategy can influence performance, with differences observed in mean performance up to 1.5%. Although no single sampling method emerged as the best across all datasets, the SPC-2 and FRD sampling strategies consistently performed well.

Overall, the paper underscores the importance of careful consideration of training strategies, data preprocessing, and sampling methods to enhance the performance and comparability of DML models.
