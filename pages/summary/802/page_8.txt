The paper discusses techniques for preserving the overall structure of deep neural networks (DNNs) while reducing storage and processing requirements through sparsity in weight matrices. Two primary reasons for exploiting sparsity are identified: the presence of zero or near-zero weights, which can be eliminated, and the similarity among maximum stored weights, allowing for multiplexing connections.

The literature is categorized into three main techniques: Quantization, Multiplexing, and Weight Sharing. 

1. **Quantization**: This technique involves reducing the number of bits required to represent weight matrices, which in turn decreases storage and computation needs. The authors propose methods for weight quantization that eliminate redundant weights and group similar weights together, thus facilitating smaller indices for storage in embedded devices. Jacob et al. introduce a quantization technique that employs integer arithmetic for inference, enhancing efficiency compared to floating-point operations while maintaining accuracy through a specialized training process.

2. **Multiplexing**: This approach focuses on reducing storage requirements by replacing multiple similar weights with a single multiplexed weight. The authors describe a lightweight neural multiplexer that chooses models based on input data and resource budgets, enabling efficient inference either on mobile devices or in the cloud. The use of a contrastive loss function during training helps optimize the performance of the multiplexed models.

The paper highlights the effectiveness of these techniques in compressing DNN models while noting gaps in addressing the negative consequences and complexities associated with weight quantization.
