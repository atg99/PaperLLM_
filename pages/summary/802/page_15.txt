Deepeye, as discussed in [75], aims to perform image recognition with significant accuracy in a short duration by optimizing the operations of convolutional and fully connected layers. This is particularly useful for applications requiring rapid image processing.

In [77], the authors introduced DeepApp, a framework designed to analyze mobile device usage patterns through historical data. This framework employs deep reinforcement learning to enhance the understanding of user interactions with mobile applications.

DeepFusion, as presented in [78], focuses on recognizing various IoT-based tasks by integrating values from multiple sensors into a unified framework, which improves the overall recognition accuracy.

The paper also discusses a technique for reducing resource requirements through distributed operations in [79], where the authors implemented a method to train DNN models in a distributed manner, thereby enhancing efficiency.

In [80], the authors address the challenges faced by DNN models running on high-end machines versus mobile devices, emphasizing the contradictions between the high resource demands of DNNs and the limited capabilities of mobile devices.

Lastly, Shufflenet, discussed in [81], seeks to reduce power consumption by minimizing floating-point operations while maintaining accuracy through pointwise group convolution and channel shuffle techniques.

Overall, these approaches highlight various strategies for optimizing DNN models for mobile and embedded devices, addressing challenges such as resource constraints, execution time, and accuracy.
