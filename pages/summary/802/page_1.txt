This paper presents a comprehensive survey on the compression of Deep Neural Networks (DNNs), highlighting the challenges and solutions associated with deploying DNN models in resource-constrained Internet of Things (IoT) devices. Despite their impressive performance due to automated feature extraction, DNNs require significant computational power, energy, and storage, making them impractical for many IoT applications. To address these limitations, various compression techniques have been proposed to reduce the resource requirements of DNNs while maintaining accuracy.

The authors categorize existing compression methods into five main types: network pruning, sparse representation, bits precision, knowledge distillation, and miscellaneous techniques. Each category is examined for its mechanisms and the specific challenges that arise. The paper underscores the importance of DNN compression for enabling efficient deployment on resource-constrained devices, which is crucial given the increasing demand for IoT applications.

Key benefits of DNN compression include reduced storage requirements, lower computational demands, faster training and inference times, enhanced privacy through in-situ processing, and decreased energy consumption, particularly beneficial for battery-operated devices. The authors aim to provide an organized overview of current literature and identify potential research gaps, paving the way for future advancements in DNN compression techniques.
