The paper discusses various techniques for knowledge distillation aimed at enhancing the performance of compressed deep neural network (DNN) models. It highlights how logits transfer can improve the generalization ability of student models, which are smaller and more resource-efficient than cumbersome teacher models. The authors propose that the cumbersome model, which integrates outputs from multiple models, serves as a source of knowledge that can be distilled into the smaller model.

The summary of contributions from various studies is presented in a table, illustrating different approaches to implementing knowledge distillation across various model categories such as CNN, FC, and RNN. Key techniques include self-knowledge distillation, the introduction of teacher assistants, and establishing relationships between compression rates and training epochs.

The paper elaborates on the mechanics of logits, defining them as output feature vectors from a layer before the softmax layer. It explains the calculation of logits and their transformation into predicted class probabilities using softmax. A temperature variable is introduced to soften the probability distribution, aiding in the matching of logits between the teacher and student models.

To quantify the performance of the compressed model relative to the cumbersome model, the paper proposes using cross-entropy loss computed with both soft targets and correct labels. The goal is to minimize the gradient that represents the difference in outputs between the two models, ultimately aiming for an equilibrium where their performance is similar. However, the paper acknowledges the challenge in achieving this state practically due to the infinite training required.

In summary, the research emphasizes the potential of knowledge distillation techniques to bridge the performance gap between large and small DNN models, making them suitable for deployment in resource-constrained environments.
