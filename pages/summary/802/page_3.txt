The method involves a two-step process: first, identifying and eliminating redundant channels, and second, fine-tuning the model to recover potential accuracy losses. The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of their approach through experiments on various datasets, showing improved performance and reduced computational demands.

The paper categorizes network pruning into four main types: Channel pruning, Filter pruning, Connection pruning, and Layer pruning. Each category addresses different aspects of model reduction by removing less significant components. The authors emphasize the importance of balancing pruning with accuracy retention and the challenges in fine-tuning the model post-pruning.

In addition to network pruning, the paper explores sparse representation and other compression techniques such as quantization, multiplexing, weight sharing, and knowledge distillation. These methods aim to further reduce the storage and computational requirements of deep neural networks, making them more suitable for deployment on mobile devices.

Overall, the paper presents a comprehensive overview of various DNN compression techniques, highlighting their relevance in optimizing models for practical applications while maintaining performance levels.
