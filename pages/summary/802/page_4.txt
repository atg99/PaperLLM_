DNN compression techniques are essential for optimizing deep learning models by reducing model size, runtime memory, and computational operations while retaining accuracy. Various methods of DNN compression are categorized, including channel pruning, filter pruning, sparse representation, quantization, knowledge distillation, and others.

Channel pruning techniques, such as those discussed in studies [25]–[29] and [83], focus on reducing the number of channels in convolutional networks. Notable approaches include network slimming, which introduces a scaling factor for each channel, allowing for joint training with sparsity regularization to maintain accuracy. VarGNet utilizes variable group convolution to balance computational cost and intensity, while MobileNets employs depth-wise separable convolutions and hyperparameters to create a lightweight model suitable for resource-constrained applications.

Filter pruning is also highlighted, where reducing the number of filters in a CNN can significantly decrease computational demands. The existing literature, such as ThiNet [30], demonstrates effective frameworks for filter pruning, aiming to enhance CNN performance while minimizing unnecessary filters.

Despite advancements in channel and filter pruning, challenges remain regarding accuracy compromises and the impact of eliminating channels on the performance of different layers within the DNN model. Further research is needed to address these gaps and improve the efficiency of DNN compression techniques.
