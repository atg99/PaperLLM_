The paper discusses various optimization techniques for deep learning models, particularly in the context of mobile devices and Internet of Things (IoT) applications. The authors highlight the significance of filter pruning and connection pruning as effective strategies for reducing the number of floating-point operations in Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs). However, they also point out that fully connected layers and recurrent layers contribute significantly to the overall computational load and should not be overlooked during the compression process.

Several approaches are examined, including DeepMon, which optimizes convolutional operations on mobile GPUs, and DeepIoT, which focuses on deploying deep learning models on IoT devices. The latter employs dropout techniques to eliminate unimportant connections while maintaining a balance between compression and accuracy.

The Energy Efficient Inference Engine (EIE) is introduced as a means to facilitate DNN inference on compressed networks, emphasizing sparse matrix multiplication and weight sharing. Additionally, the Multi-Tasking Zipping (MTZ) framework is presented, which merges multiple DNN models to achieve cross-model compression through layer-wise neuron sharing.

The paper concludes with a discussion on various methods that enhance the deployment of deep learning models on embedded devices, emphasizing the importance of connection pruning and the development of efficient compression techniques to ensure minimal accuracy loss while maximizing performance and energy efficiency.
