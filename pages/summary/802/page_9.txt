The paper discusses various deep neural network (DNN) compression techniques that utilize sparse representation, highlighting methods such as weight quantization, guided deployment, pruning, multiplexing, and weight sharing. Key contributions and methodologies from several studies are summarized:

1. **Weight Quantization**: This method reduces the storage requirements of DNN models by minimizing the bits needed for weight matrices representation.

2. **Guided Deployment**: A technique aimed at enhancing the selection mechanism for deploying DNN models, allowing for more efficient pruning.

3. **Pruning Methods**: Techniques like singular value decomposition are employed to facilitate the deployment of DNN models on embedded devices by removing redundant connections.

4. **Integer Arithmetic Inference**: A quantization technique that uses affine mapping to enable inference using integer arithmetic.

5. **Pruning and Weight Sharing**: Approaches that focus on pruning unnecessary connections while sharing weights among different elements in the network, thereby optimizing performance.

6. **Lightweight Neural Multiplexer**: A developed mechanism for determining which model to use for inference, enhancing efficiency across various tasks.

7. **Multi-Branch CNN Architecture**: This architecture allows simultaneous multiplexing of different recognition and prediction tasks using a single backbone network, which preserves time and energy while maintaining accuracy.

8. **Weight Sharing Techniques**: The paper emphasizes the importance of weight sharing in reducing both storage and computational needs. Techniques such as k-means clustering are utilized to identify shared weights across layers, which enhances efficiency while maintaining model performance.

9. **Data Sharing During Training**: A technique that allows for the sharing of training data to improve understanding of the DNN model's decision-making process, although it raises privacy concerns.

10. **Multitasking Learning Framework**: This framework optimizes resource use while achieving high accuracy by sharing hidden layers among different tasks, balancing performance improvements with the complexity of managing multiple losses.

The paper concludes that while existing literature has made significant strides in DNN compression through these techniques, there are still opportunities for further innovation, particularly in the area of multiplexing weights to reduce storage requirements.
