The paper discusses various techniques for DNN compression through bits precision, particularly focusing on the transition from floating-point to integer arithmetic to lower storage and computation needs. The authors highlight that while traditional floating-point representations require 32 bits, switching to integer representations can reduce this to as low as 8 bits. 

A significant challenge identified is the complexity of converting floating-point values to integers, which may also lead to accuracy loss. The literature reviewed includes strategies like Neuro.ZERO, which employs multiple mechanisms to enhance inference performance while utilizing integer arithmetic. This includes runtime adaptations based on resource availability, extended inference to boost accuracy, expedited inference to offload tasks, ensemble inference for simultaneous model execution, and latent training for unseen data on secondary processors.

Additionally, the paper explores low bits representation, extending beyond integers to include quantized neural networks (QNNs) that utilize weights represented in very low precision formats, such as 1-bit or 4-bits. This approach aims to significantly reduce memory requirements and improve computational efficiency by replacing floating-point operations with bit-wise operations. Two quantization schemes are discussed: linear and logarithmic quantization, both designed to optimize the representation of weights and activations while maintaining operational efficiency.

In summary, the paper emphasizes the importance of bits precision in DNN compression, detailing various methods and their implications for storage, computation, and accuracy. The findings suggest potential avenues for further research, particularly in exploring lower bit estimations beyond integers.
