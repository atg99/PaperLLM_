The paper discusses the implementation of federated learning to enhance a global model for detecting COVID-19 lesions using data from local hospitals. The federated approach involves averaging updates from local datasets weighted by their sizes, iterating this process until convergence. The study employs transfer learning from the DeepLesion dataset, which provides a vast collection of CT images, to address data insufficiency for developing a deep learning detector.

The model architecture is based on an improved version of RetinaNet, a CNN designed for object detection. It was pretrained on the DeepLesion dataset and fine-tuned with internal COVID-19 images. The model includes an auxiliary segmentation branch that, although frozen during fine-tuning, effectively produces lesion segmentation masks due to prior training on similar data.

Preprocessing steps involved clipping Hounsfield units and normalizing each volume individually, which improved the model's generalizability. During federated training, local clients used the Adam optimizer with specific hyperparameters to optimize their models for one epoch with data augmentation techniques applied to enhance training data. Convergence was monitored through local validation performance over successive federated rounds.

Post-processing utilized non-maximum suppression to refine bounding box predictions, while an existing lung segmentation model was employed to eliminate false positives outside lung regions. The paper outlines the methodology for computing ROC curves to evaluate model performance, identifying true and false positives based on the intersection over union metric.

Data availability is discussed, indicating that public dataset images and annotations can be accessed online, while certain datasets contain confidential information and require authorization for access. The study highlights the automated estimation of lesion burden changes over time, illustrating its application in monitoring hospitalized patients with COVID-19.
