This paper investigates the feasibility of training a deep convolutional neural network (CNN) model for automated detection of COVID-19 lesions in CT images. The approach focuses on identifying lesion bounding boxes instead of requiring dense pixel-wise labels, which are labor-intensive to obtain. The study employs a privacy-preserving federated learning method that does not necessitate data exchange or central storage, allowing for training on decentralized data from multiple centers.

The research involved a multicenter study with data collected from three hospitals in Hong Kong, comprising 75 patients confirmed with COVID-19. The CT images captured during early 2020 were annotated with bounding boxes around key indicators of the disease. To validate the model's robustness and generalizability, the study used external datasets from China and Germany, which included various patient demographics and imaging protocols.

The CNN model was developed using federated learning and transfer learning techniques, leveraging previously established models. Six experimental settings were created for comparison: three individual models trained at each internal center, an ensemble of these models, a centralized joint model, and the federated learning model. The evaluation of model performance included analyzing receiver operating characteristic curves and calculating area under the curve metrics.

The findings illustrate the potential of federated learning to create scalable and cost-effective AI tools for COVID-19 diagnosis and management, while maintaining patient privacy. The study provides a foundation for further research in automated image interpretation in clinical settings.
