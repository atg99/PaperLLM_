This study explores the feasibility of federated learning for combining COVID-19 data from different medical centers while protecting patient privacy. The decentralized training approach is particularly valuable during a pandemic, as it eliminates the need for complex data-sharing agreements. While federated learning has been used in fields like edge computing, its application in medical imaging faces unique challenges, such as high-dimensional data and imbalanced cohort sizes.

The research demonstrates that federated learning can enhance generalization performance compared to single-site models, indicating successful decentralized optimization with diverse training datasets. A proof-of-concept shows that a convolutional neural network-based federated deep learning model effectively detects chest CT abnormalities in COVID-19 patients. The model, trained on data from Hong Kong, performed well not only on internal tests but also on independent external datasets from various hospitals in Asia and Europe, despite differences in scanner brands and imaging protocols. The results, including ROC curves and AUC metrics, affirm the potential of federated learning in improving AI-based analysis during global health crises.
