The paper discusses the performance of various object detection models in image processing, particularly in the context of detecting lesions in CT scans of COVID-19 patients. The evaluation of models included metrics such as mean Average Precision (mAP), sensitivity, and precision, with statistical analyses performed using the Clopper-Pearson method and Student's t-test for significance.

In internal validation, Individual-model-2, trained on the largest dataset, achieved the highest Area Under the Curve (AUC) at 95.40% and an mAP of 89.98%. The ensemble of three models showed a slightly lower AUC, while the joint model had a further reduced performance. Notably, the federated learning model demonstrated comparable performance to Individual-model-2 with an AUC of 95.27% and a higher mAP of 91.28%, indicating its effectiveness in leveraging multicenter data.

The generalization performance on external cohorts was also impressive, with the federated learning model achieving the best results across all methods on External-Set-1, attaining an AUC of 95.66%. The paper highlights the importance of using diverse datasets for training to enhance model robustness and generalizability in clinical applications. The results underscore the potential of federated learning in developing privacy-preserving AI systems for medical imaging tasks.
