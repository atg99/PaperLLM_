The study investigates the performance of various AI models for lesion detection in COVID-19 CT scans, with a focus on a federated learning model. The federated model outperformed others on External-Set-1, achieving an AUC of 88.15%, mAP of 71.48%, sensitivity of 73.31%, and precision of 91.93%. The ensemble model, which aggregates predictions from three individual models, provided the second-best results. The federated learning model also excelled on External-Set-3 with an AUC of 91.99%. Statistical comparisons showed significant differences (p-value <0.05) between the federated model and other models across external sets.

An ablation study highlighted the benefits of transfer learning, demonstrating that initializing the federated model from a prior DeepLesion model improved mAP by 2.56% to 5.51% across various datasets compared to training from scratch. The model's clinical applicability was further evaluated through case studies on External-Set-4, where it estimated lesion burden from longitudinal scans. The automated estimates correlated with patients' clinical symptoms, suggesting that the model can aid in monitoring disease progression and treatment planning.

The results indicate that the federated learning model is effective in detecting lesions and can provide valuable insights into patient conditions through automated lesion burden estimation, potentially enhancing clinical decision-making.
