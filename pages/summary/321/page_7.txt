The paper discusses the processing and training of MIMIC notes, which are categorized into 15 distinct note types, many of which are semi-structured. The authors detail their methodology for extracting sentences from these notes using Scispacy and subsequently inputting them into BERT-Base and BioBERT models for pre-training on clinical text. 

In the BERT training section, they describe the pre-training parameters, including a batch size of 32, a maximum sequence length of 128, and a learning rate of 5e-5, with models trained for 150,000 steps. They found no significant performance improvements when extending training to 300,000 steps. For fine-tuning on downstream tasks, they experimented with various hyperparameters, including learning rates and batch sizes, while maintaining a maximum sequence length of 150.

The paper also explains the IOB (Inside-Outside-Beginning) format for encoding span-based Named Entity Recognition (NER) tasks, which enhances the labeling granularity. It classifies words within a span, outside any span, and at the beginning of a span into three subclasses: I-, O, and B-. An example illustrates how a text with specific NER labels can be converted into the IOB format. Overall, the paper highlights the effectiveness of these methodologies in processing clinical text for improved performance in NER tasks.
