The BERT training in this work focuses on utilizing standard procedures to create pre-trained embeddings for the clinical community. Two models were developed: Clinical BERT, based on BERT-Base, and Clinical BioBERT, based on BioBERT. Both models underwent fine-tuning for various downstream tasks, using a simple linear classification layer. Although this approach may limit performance compared to more complex models, the primary goal was to demonstrate the effectiveness of clinical-specific embeddings.

Training these models required substantial computational resources, taking around 17 to 18 days on a single GPU, which may be prohibitive for some institutions. This underscores the value of making the pre-trained models publicly available.

The models were assessed on several tasks, including MedNLI and four i2b2 named entity recognition challenges. The results indicated that Clinical BioBERT outperformed both BioBERT and general BERT on three out of five tasks, achieving a new state-of-the-art accuracy on MedNLI. However, no improvements were observed in the de-identification tasks, attributed to the synthetic nature of the PHI used in the datasets, which differed significantly from the clinical text the models were trained on.
