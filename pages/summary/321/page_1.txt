The paper by Emily Alsentzer and colleagues addresses the gap in publicly available pre-trained BERT models for clinical text. While models like ELMo and BERT have shown significant advancements in natural language processing tasks, their application in specialized domains such as clinical narratives has been limited. This study introduces two domain-specific BERT models: one for generic clinical text and another specifically for discharge summaries. 

The authors demonstrate that these clinical BERT models outperform general and biomedical BERT models (BioBERT) on various clinical NLP tasks, including named entity recognition (NER) and medical natural language inference. However, they note that the clinical models do not perform as well on de-identification tasks, which the authors attribute to differences between de-identified source text and non-de-identified task text.

Key contributions of the paper include the release of BERT-Base and BioBERT-finetuned models tailored for clinical notes and discharge summaries, along with empirical evidence of performance improvements on established clinical tasks. The research highlights the importance of developing specialized models to capture the unique linguistic characteristics of clinical text.
