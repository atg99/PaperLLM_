The paper discusses advancements in contextualized word embeddings, focusing on the superiority of BERT over ELMo and traditional non-contextual embeddings. ELMo generates context-sensitive embeddings based on a large text corpus, while BERT, being a deeper model with more parameters, allows for task-specific fine-tuning, making it particularly effective in various applications, including clinical NLP tasks.

The authors highlight several models tailored for clinical and biomedical domains, such as BioBERT, which improves performance on biomedical tasks by training on a corpus of biomedical texts. They also reference works that utilize ELMo and BERT for clinical text applications, noting improvements in tasks like de-identification and other clinical NLP benchmarks.

The methodology section outlines the use of approximately 2 million clinical notes from the MIMIC-III database for training two versions of BERT: Clinical BERT, which incorporates all note types, and Discharge Summary BERT, focusing solely on discharge summaries. The authors assert that the potential bias from overlapping notes in training and testing datasets is minimal due to the large size of the MIMIC corpus. Overall, the study emphasizes the need for tailored contextual embeddings to enhance performance in clinical NLP tasks.
