The paper presents results from several SSL experiments on various datasets, including DBLP, Pubmed, Cora, and Citeseer, with a focus on analyzing the effectiveness of different methods. Table 4 details mean test errors with standard deviations across 100 train-test splits, indicating that methods like HGNN and FastHyperGCN consistently outperform others, particularly in co-citation scenarios. The best-performing methods are highlighted, showing significant improvements, especially in handling noisy hyperedges.

Table 5 further explores performance on synthetic data and a subset of DBLP, emphasizing the robustness of these methods against noise in hyperedges. HGNN shows the best results across varying noise levels, while FastHyperGCN and HyperGCN also demonstrate competitive performance.

The paper includes a proposition that establishes a relationship between hypergraphs and their respective clique and mediator expansions, providing theoretical insights into the conditions under which these methods operate effectively. The findings suggest that HGNN is a strong baseline, particularly in co-citation tasks, while FastHyperGCN and HyperGCN also deliver notable results.

Overall, the experiments illustrate the advantages of the proposed methods in various contexts, emphasizing their applicability to noisy data and their competitive edge in hypergraph-based learning tasks.
