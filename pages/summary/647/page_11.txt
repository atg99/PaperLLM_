This collection of references primarily explores various aspects of graph and hypergraph theory, particularly in relation to machine learning and neural networks. 

Hammond, Vandergheynst, and Gribonval (2011) discuss wavelets on graphs through the lens of spectral graph theory, providing foundational insights into graph signal processing. Hein et al. (2013) revisit total variation on hypergraphs, contributing to the understanding of learning within hypergraph structures. Jiang et al. (2019) introduce dynamic hypergraph neural networks, emphasizing the evolving nature of relationships in data. Jin et al. (2019) present hypergraph-induced convolutional manifold networks, expanding on neural network architectures that can handle hypergraph data.

Kipf and Welling (2017) focus on semi-supervised classification using graph convolutional networks, a significant advancement in leveraging graph structures for predictive modeling. Lemos et al. (2019) merge graph coloring concepts with deep learning, proposing effective models for combinatorial problems. 

Li and Milenkovic (2018) contribute to the understanding of submodular function minimization in hypergraphs, while also addressing applications in clustering and spectral analysis. They further revisit decomposable submodular functions, providing new insights into incidence relations. 

Monti et al. (2018) introduce dual-primal graph convolutional networks, which enhance the capabilities of traditional graph convolutional approaches. The works of Marcheggiani and Titov (2017) highlight the use of graph convolutional networks for semantic role labeling, showcasing the versatility of these methodologies in natural language processing.

Overall, these references illustrate the diverse applications and theoretical advancements in graph and hypergraph methodologies within machine learning, paving the way for future research in this evolving field.
