The paper discusses the performance of HyperGCN, FastHyperGCN, and HGNN on various hypergraph datasets. It establishes that when the maximum size of a hyperedge is 3, the graphs produced by the three methods are essentially the same, leading to comparable accuracies on the Cora co-citation and Citeseer co-citation datasets. The mean hyperedge sizes in these datasets are close to 3, which minimizes deviations and results in similar graph structures.

However, HyperGCN demonstrates statistically significant superiority over HGNN on datasets such as Pubmed, DBLP, and Cora co-authorship, particularly due to the presence of large noisy hyperedges typical in real-world hypergraphs. The paper notes that in co-authorship networks, authors can work across different topics, contributing to noise in the hypergraph structure. Experimental results indicate that HyperGCN and FastHyperGCN outperform HGNN as noise levels in hyperedges increase, particularly in synthetic datasets where hyperedges are predominantly noisy.

The study also explores the use of HyperGCN for combinatorial optimization, specifically targeting the densest k-subhypergraph problem, which is NP-hard. Greedy heuristics like MaxDegree and RemoveMinDegree are compared to the learning-based HyperGCN approach. Experimental results show that HyperGCN outperforms other methods, except in cases with a specific structure in the Pubmed dataset.

Visualizations reveal that HyperGCN tends to form denser clusters of vertices, indicating more effective inclusion of hyperedges among closely connected vertices compared to HGNN. Overall, the findings support the preference for HyperGCN and FastHyperGCN in scenarios with large noisy hyperedges, highlighting their effectiveness in both classification and optimization tasks in hypergraph contexts.
