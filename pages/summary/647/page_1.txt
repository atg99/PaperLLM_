The paper introduces HyperGCN, a novel approach for training Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) specifically designed for semi-supervised learning (SSL) on hypergraphs. Hypergraphs extend traditional graphs by allowing relationships that involve multiple vertices, which makes them suitable for modeling complex real-world networks such as co-authorship and co-citation. The authors highlight the limitations of existing techniques that primarily focus on pairwise relationships and emphasize the need for methods that can effectively handle the intricate relationships present in hypergraphs.

HyperGCN utilizes spectral theory of hypergraphs to enhance the training of GCNs for SSL, aiming to assign labels to initially unlabelled vertices. The paper presents empirical evidence demonstrating the effectiveness of HyperGCN through detailed experiments on real-world hypergraph datasets, illustrating its superiority over state-of-the-art baselines in specific scenarios. The authors also provide insights into the conditions under which HyperGCN outperforms existing methods, contributing to the understanding of hypergraph-based learning paradigms. The source code for HyperGCN has been made publicly available to facilitate further research and application in this domain.
