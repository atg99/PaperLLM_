The paper discusses the performance of a zero-shot image classification model known as LSeg, which utilizes a transformer-based architecture for text encoding. The study compares various text encoders paired with different image encoders during the pretraining phase of CLIP. Notably, the RN 50-16 encoder demonstrated superior performance, outperforming the weaker ViT-B/32 text encoder by 2.5%, potentially due to its larger embedding size.

To evaluate the model's performance on a fixed label set, LSeg was trained on the ADE20K dataset. Results indicated that LSeg performed competitively against established semantic segmentation models, with only a minor performance drop when using the RN 50-16 text encoder compared to the best fixed-label method, DPT.

Additionally, the authors explored LSeg's ability to generalize to new, unseen classes. The model was trained on a combination of seven datasets without any relabeling. Results showcased LSeg's capability to adapt to semantically related labels, demonstrating effective segmentation even with labels not present during training.

The paper concludes with an ablation study and performance comparisons, highlighting LSeg's effectiveness in semantic segmentation tasks while maintaining flexibility in label interpretation.
