This paper presents LSeg, a model designed for zero-shot semantic segmentation, which can dynamically handle varying label sets during inference. The model initializes its image encoder backbone using pretrained weights from ViT or ResNet and trains the decoder randomly while keeping the text encoder frozen. This approach allows LSeg to synthesize arbitrary zero-shot segmentation models by adjusting the labels provided to the text encoder, offering greater flexibility compared to traditional methods that assign fixed channels for semantic classes.

The experiments focus on comparing LSeg against state-of-the-art few-shot segmentation models on established benchmarks like PASCAL-5i and COCO-20i. These datasets facilitate evaluation through metrics like mean intersection over union (mIoU) and foreground-background intersection of union (FB-IoU). The authors acknowledge that while few-shot methods typically perform better due to their access to labeled samples, they lack the flexibility of LSeg, which operates effectively in zero-shot scenarios.

The experimental setup includes using an LSeg model with CLIP-ViT-B/32 as the text encoder and DPT with a ViT-L/16 backbone for the image encoder. The model is trained with a specific learning rate schedule and batch size on high-performance GPU hardware. The results indicate that LSeg competes well with leading few-shot methods, showcasing its potential in semantic segmentation tasks without requiring prior exposure to the target classes.
