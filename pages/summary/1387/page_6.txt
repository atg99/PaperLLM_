The paper presents a comparison of various models for few-shot and zero-shot segmentation tasks, evaluated on the PASCAL-5i and COCO-20i datasets. The results highlight the performance of different model backbones and methods, showcasing metrics such as mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) and mean F-Beta Intersection over Union (FB-IoU), where higher values indicate better performance.

Table 1 shows that models like HSNet and PFENet achieve the highest FB-IoU on PASCAL-5i, with HSNet reaching 67.3, while zero-shot methods like LSeg with the ViT-L/16 backbone also perform competitively with a FB-IoU of 61.3. In Table 2, HSNet again leads with a FB-IoU of 69.1 on COCO-20i, outperforming other models significantly. The competitive performance of LSeg is noted when using a larger backbone.

The paper also discusses the experimental setup, aligning with previous works like ZS3Net and employing cross-validation across different folds of data. Furthermore, it references the FSS-1000 dataset, designed for few-shot segmentation, noting its unique challenge due to the inclusion of many unannotated object classes.

Overall, the findings indicate that the proposed model demonstrates substantial improvements over existing zero-shot baselines and is competitive with few-shot segmentation methods, emphasizing the importance of backbone architecture in achieving higher performance in segmentation tasks.
