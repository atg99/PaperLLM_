This paper presents LSeg, a novel approach for zero-shot semantic segmentation that utilizes state-of-the-art text encoders, such as CLIP, trained on visual data. The method allows for flexible synthesis of segmentation models by embedding labels from the training set into a shared embedding space, enabling the visual encoder to produce pixel-wise embeddings that align with corresponding label embeddings, even for classes not present in the training dataset.

The flexibility of LSeg allows users to dynamically adjust the label set at test time—adding, removing, or reordering labels as needed. This is demonstrated through various examples where LSeg successfully segments objects based on an arbitrary set of labels. For instance, the model can identify a "pet" class despite it not being included in the training data, showcasing its zero-shot capabilities.

Quantitative evaluations reveal that LSeg outperforms existing methods in zero-shot scenarios and remains competitive in few-shot tasks, all without the need for additional training samples. The integration of text embeddings results in minimal performance loss compared to traditional fixed-label segmentation methods.

In the related work section, the paper discusses generalized semantic segmentation, highlighting that most existing models are limited to a fixed label set defined by the training dataset. Few-shot methods attempt to address this limitation by allowing for some flexibility when few examples of new classes are available during testing.
