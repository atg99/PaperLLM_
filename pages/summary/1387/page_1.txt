The paper introduces LSeg, a model designed for language-driven semantic segmentation of images. LSeg utilizes a text encoder to create embeddings for descriptive labels, such as "grass" or "building," and pairs this with a transformer-based image encoder that generates dense per-pixel embeddings of the image. The image encoder is trained using a contrastive objective to align pixel embeddings with corresponding text embeddings, enabling a flexible representation where semantically similar labels are mapped closely in the embedding space.

This approach allows LSeg to generalize to new categories during testing without the need for retraining or additional training samples. The authors demonstrate that LSeg achieves competitive zero-shot performance compared to existing zero- and few-shot semantic segmentation methods and can match the accuracy of traditional segmentation algorithms when provided with a fixed label set.

The paper addresses the limitations of current semantic segmentation methods, which are constrained by the size of labeled training datasets and the labor-intensive nature of image annotation. By leveraging modern language models, LSeg enhances the flexibility and generality of semantic segmentation, drawing inspiration from the CLIP model. Overall, LSeg represents a significant advancement in enabling semantic segmentation with a broader set of labels while minimizing the need for extensive annotated data. Code and a demo are accessible online.
