The paper presents a method for semantic segmentation that integrates an image encoder and a text encoder to correlate pixel embeddings with label embeddings. The process begins with the image encoder extracting per-pixel embeddings from the input image while the text encoder converts labels into a vector space. The core of the approach is the creation of a word-pixel correlation tensor, computed through the inner product of pixel and label embeddings. During training, the model aims to maximize the correlation between the pixel embedding of a given pixel and the corresponding ground-truth label embedding, utilizing a pixelwise softmax objective with a temperature parameter to guide the learning process.

To address memory constraints, the model outputs lower-resolution pixel embeddings, which are then spatially regularized and upsampled to match the original image resolution. This upsampling is achieved through two proposed spatial regularization blocks: one that employs depthwise convolutions and another that enhances depthwise convolutions with max-pooling over the label set. The final predictions are refined using bilinear interpolation to ensure they align with the original resolution. This method effectively combines semantic segmentation with label correlation to improve prediction accuracy while maintaining equivariance with respect to label order.
