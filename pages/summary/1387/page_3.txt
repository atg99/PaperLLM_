The paper presents a novel approach called Language-driven Semantic Segmentation (LSeg) for zero-shot semantic segmentation, which seeks to accurately segment unseen objects without requiring additional samples of novel classes. Unlike previous methods that rely on labeled examples from target classes, LSeg leverages high-capacity language models to synthesize segmentation models that perform comparably to fixed-label and few-shot methods.

The authors highlight that existing zero-shot segmentation techniques primarily utilize text embeddings of class labels, demonstrating the potential of synthesizing visual features from word embeddings. Key works in this area have explored various strategies such as generative models, uncertainty-aware learning, and structured learning to enhance performance, but none have achieved the level of robustness demonstrated by LSeg.

LSeg operates by embedding both text labels and image pixels into a shared vector space, allowing for the assignment of the closest label to each pixel. The framework employs a text encoder, utilizing the Contrastive Language–Image Pre-training (CLIP) model to generate embeddings for a potentially variable number of labels. An image encoder, based on dense prediction transformers, similarly produces embeddings for input pixels. This dual-embedding approach enables effective correlation between visual features and text labels, facilitating accurate segmentation of novel categories.

The authors also discuss related works in open-set recognition and cross-domain adaptation, emphasizing that their approach addresses the limitations of these methods by focusing on label flexibility and zero-shot learning capabilities. Overall, the paper contributes to the field by demonstrating the feasibility and effectiveness of language-driven methods for semantic segmentation tasks.
