The study outlines the creation of two remote sensing-oriented Visual Question Answering (VQA) datasets, addressing the shortage of task-specific datasets for VQA. The datasets are generated from existing human annotations and are designed to enhance the training of the RSVQA model, which incorporates a non-spatial fusion step. A significant contribution of this research is the introduction of a method to create these datasets, along with the datasets themselves and the RSVQA model, which can be accessed online.

The dataset construction process begins with the formulation of questions derived from OpenStreetMap (OSM) data. The methodology involves four main components: selecting an element category, applying attributes to the selected element, determining relative positional relationships, and finally constructing the question. The element categories include roads, water areas, buildings, and various land uses. The attributes applied can relate to the shape or size of the elements, while relational positioning allows for spatial context in the questions posed.

This work builds upon previous studies by providing a larger dataset of very high-resolution images, which facilitates testing the spatial generalization capabilities of VQA systems. The research emphasizes the ongoing challenges in the field and contributes valuable resources for future developments in remote sensing and VQA.
