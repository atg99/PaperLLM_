The paper discusses a methodology for constructing questions and answers based on image datasets, specifically focusing on low and high-resolution imagery. It outlines how size thresholds for objects of interest are adapted to the resolution of images, differentiating between small, medium, and large scales. A key aspect of the approach is the generation of base questions from identified elements, which involves selecting from a catalog of question types and using predefined templates to formulate natural language questions.

The answer construction process involves extracting relevant objects from the OpenStreetMap (OSM) database based on the image footprint. The answers vary depending on the question type, including counts of objects, presence checks, area calculations, comparisons between elements, and rural/urban assessments based on building density.

The paper details two datasets created for this study: a low-resolution dataset using Sentinel-2 images with 10m resolution over the Netherlands, which resulted in 770,232 constructed questions and answers, and a high-resolution dataset derived from 15cm aerial imagery from the USGS. The datasets were split into training, validation, and test sets to ensure minimal spatial correlation, enhancing the robustness of the methodology.
