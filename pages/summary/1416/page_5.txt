The paper discusses the creation and analysis of two distinct image datasets for Visual Question Answering (VQA) based on satellite imagery, focusing on high-resolution (HR) and low-resolution (LR) datasets. The HR dataset includes images from urban areas like New York City, Philadelphia, and Portland, split into 100659 images of size 512x512, covering the North-East coast of the USA from 2000 to 2016. The dataset is categorized into training, validation, and testing sets, with specific attention given to the different regions covered by each set.

The key differences between the LR and HR datasets are highlighted. The LR dataset, featuring lower resolution (10m), allows for broader spatial and temporal queries but lacks the detail necessary to answer questions about small objects. In contrast, the HR dataset, while providing richer details suitable for answering specific queries about smaller objects, is less frequently updated and more costly to acquire.

To tailor the question types to the datasets, the authors developed distinct categories: questions about object area are exclusive to the HR dataset, while urban/rural classifications are reserved for the LR dataset. Additionally, the paper discusses the quantization of numerical answers to better accommodate the distributions observed in each dataset. For the LR dataset, answers are grouped into categories based on object counts, while the HR dataset uses similar quantization for area-related questions due to the heavy-tailed nature of the data distribution.

The distribution of answers for different question types is analyzed, revealing that while most question types are evenly distributed, specific responses dominate. The HR dataset shows a high frequency of 'no' answers (37.7%), while the LR dataset has a near-equal distribution of 'yes' (34.9%) and 'no' (34.3%) responses. This analysis provides insight into the types of questions that can be effectively answered using each dataset and the inherent challenges associated with each resolution type.
