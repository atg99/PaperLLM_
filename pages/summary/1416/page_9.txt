The paper presents results from testing a model designed for answering questions based on remote sensing images, utilizing both low and high resolution datasets. The results indicate promising performance, particularly in presence detection, with accuracies of 87.46% for the low resolution dataset and 90.43% for the first high resolution test set. However, counting questions yield lower accuracies of 67.01% and 68.63%, respectively. This discrepancy is attributed to the inherent complexity of counting tasks, especially when distinguishing between connected instances, such as buildings.

For area-related questions, the model performs well, achieving 85.24% accuracy on the first high resolution test set, illustrating effective detection of building presence through area coverage. Conversely, the model struggles with questions regarding relative positions of objects, reflecting challenges in handling high semantic level queries.

In the low resolution dataset, rural versus urban classification is generally accurate at 90%, aided by a clear ground truth definition based on building counts. However, the low resolution of the images affects the model's ability to identify smaller objects reliably.

Generalization to new geographic areas presents challenges, with a noted accuracy drop of approximately 5% on the second high resolution test set, particularly impacting more complex tasks like counting and area computation. This drop is linked to differences in urban organization and sensor characteristics between the areas studied, highlighting the model's limitations in adapting to diverse environments.
