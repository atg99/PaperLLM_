The paper discusses the development of a Visual Question Answering (VQA) system tailored for remote sensing images. It introduces a method for creating datasets focused on VQA, which can be adapted for various data sources, featuring two specific datasets: one based on Sentinel-2 images and another on high-resolution aerial orthophotos from USGS. 

The analysis employs a deep learning model that integrates convolutional and recurrent neural networks to interpret images and associated questions, ultimately selecting the most probable answer from a predefined set. The results indicate that while the model generally provides logical answers, it exhibits some language biases, relying more on the questions than the images for responses. The accuracy rates across different datasets show a slight decline when questions are decoupled from specific images, particularly in high-resolution contexts.

Additionally, the study highlights the importance of training sample size, demonstrating that even with a small fraction of training data, reasonable accuracy can be achieved, though numerical tasks require more extensive datasets for optimal performance. The limitations of the dataset's diversity and the types of questions addressed are acknowledged, suggesting that incorporating a broader range of objects and question types could enhance the system's applicability.

The conclusion emphasizes the potential of VQA systems for extracting information from remotely sensed data while outlining future research directions to address existing challenges, such as language biases and complex tasks like counting. Proposed solutions include implementing attention mechanisms and dedicated components for counting within a modular framework.
