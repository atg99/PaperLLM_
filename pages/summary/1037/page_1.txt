The paper by Alexa Hagerty and Igor Rubinov examines the ethical implications and social impacts of artificial intelligence (AI) globally, highlighting a significant bias in current analyses that predominantly reflect U.S. perspectives. The authors conducted a comprehensive literature review of over 800 academic sources across various languages, focusing on the social implications of AI in five global regions.

Key findings indicate that AI's social impacts vary significantly by geographic location, with local cultural and social contexts shaping perceptions and understandings of these technologies. Research from the U.S. suggests that AI exacerbates social divides and inequality, particularly affecting historically marginalized communities. This trend appears to be consistent globally, with low- and middle-income countries potentially facing greater risks and fewer benefits from AI advancements.

The authors advocate for extensive ethnographic research to assess the social impacts of AI worldwide. Such research is essential to identify AI systems that may worsen social inequalities and to develop responsible AI technologies. This understanding is crucial for creating effective regulations and ensuring that AI benefits all segments of society.
