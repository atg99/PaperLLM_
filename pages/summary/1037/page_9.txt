The paper discusses the plurality of ethical frameworks that exist globally, emphasizing that Western moral philosophy is primarily based on Aristotelian virtue ethics, Kantian deontology, and consequentialism. However, it acknowledges the richness of ethical thought from various cultures, including Confucian, Shinto, Hindu, and the religious frameworks of Judaism, Christianity, and Islam. The paper highlights the significant internal diversity within these frameworks, illustrated by the differences within Christianity and Islam.

Moreover, it argues that ethics cannot be divorced from culture; rather, they are interdependent. Culture encompasses a living system of ethics that includes not only formal moral frameworks but also politics, history, law, customs, and individual experiences. Understanding one requires understanding the other, as ethics are best studied within their cultural contexts.

In the conclusion of Part I, the paper reiterates that AI and ethics are shaped by cultural contexts and that ethics should be viewed as a dynamic system rather than a static set of rules. 

Part II raises critical questions regarding the ethical implications of AI technologies, particularly in light of the diverse cultural interpretations of concepts like fairness and privacy. It notes that while several countries have established national AI strategies with ethical principles, these principles may not translate universally. The paper calls for a nuanced understanding of how AI technologies affect different populations and questions whether local legal frameworks and NGO assessments suffice to measure these impacts effectively.
