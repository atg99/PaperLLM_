The paper discusses the implications of artificial intelligence (AI) on social organization and control, highlighting the potential for both positive and negative outcomes. It emphasizes that while AI can enhance governance and public services—such as improving election integrity and disaster response—it also poses risks of increased surveillance and social inequality. The Assistant Secretary-General for Strategic Coordination, Fabrizio Hochschild, raised critical questions about whether emerging technologies will promote peace and development or exacerbate inequality and human rights violations.

The paper points out that the impact of AI is not limited to specific social groups; rather, negative consequences can ripple through entire societies, particularly as social inequality increases. It illustrates the dual nature of AI through examples of government surveillance practices in the U.S. and China, where authorities often exaggerate their capabilities to project power while the actual effectiveness of such technologies may be limited.

A case study on Singapore highlights the normalization of state surveillance, where mass monitoring is largely accepted by the public despite concerns about privacy. This acceptance reflects a complex relationship between technological capability, governmental authority, and societal perception, suggesting that social control is influenced not only by technology but also by cultural beliefs about its use.
