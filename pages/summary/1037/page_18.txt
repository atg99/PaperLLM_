The paper examines the implications of AI technologies on society, with a specific focus on the evolving social credit system in China, which is still in development and not as pervasive as often portrayed. The authors emphasize that AI's social impacts will vary significantly across different regions, shaped by cultural contexts, and argue for the necessity of ethnographic research to understand these dynamics. They highlight the importance of empirical studies, as advocated by figures like Kate Crawford and organizations like Data & Society, to assess AI's effects from design to deployment. The authors caution that while AI technologies hold great promise for improving lives, they also risk exacerbating existing inequalities, particularly affecting vulnerable populations. They argue that understanding the impact of AI on marginalized groups is crucial for predicting broader societal effects, asserting that systems tested in less equitable environments will eventually influence all segments of society. The call to action is clear: continued research and critical engagement with AI technologies are essential to ensure that they contribute to human flourishing rather than deepen social divides.
