The paper discusses the vulnerabilities of low- and middle-income countries to the negative social impacts of artificial intelligence (AI) while highlighting their lesser likelihood of reaping positive benefits. It underscores the risk of social inequality being exacerbated by AI, which could lead to social instability and significant geopolitical ramifications. The authors argue for urgent action, emphasizing the need for comprehensive global understandings of AI, as current research is limited and lacks high-quality, on-the-ground studies. They call for rigorous ethnographic research to explore the ethical and social consequences of AI across diverse cultures, particularly to identify and mitigate harms arising from AI systems that may intensify social inequality.

The research project evaluated AI and related technologies in five global regions, focusing on fourteen countries. A team of social scientists reviewed over 800 academic articles, policy papers, and local media in multiple languages. Findings revealed significant gaps in existing scholarly literature regarding the social impacts and ethical implications of AI. The authors propose considering adjacent technologies, such as social media and surveillance systems, as proxies to understand AI's potential effects on society.

The article aims to initiate a serious dialogue about AI ethics across different cultures by defining key terms such as artificial intelligence, culture, and ethics. It posits that AI is influenced by its social context throughout its development and use, resulting in diverse manifestations in various locations. The authors advocate for a dynamic understanding of culture to avoid stereotypes and highlight the inseparability of ethics from cultural context. They argue that ethnographic research methods are particularly effective for analyzing the interplay between ethics and culture.
