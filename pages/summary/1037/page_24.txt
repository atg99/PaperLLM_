The referenced works explore the intersection of emerging technologies, artificial intelligence, and ethical considerations in various sectors, particularly in healthcare and governance in India. Basu and Majumdar discuss how AI can transform the Indian healthcare ecosystem, while Basu and Hickok examine AI's role in governance, highlighting the implementation of predictive policing in several Indian states. The use of biometric identification, particularly through the Aadhaar program, raises ethical concerns, especially regarding its impact on vulnerable populations, such as HIV patients facing coercion to submit identification for healthcare access.

The critiques of biometric systems include the potential for exploitation of centralized databases, as noted by the Electronic Frontier Foundation, and the alarming statistics linking Aadhaar to hunger-related deaths in India. The works also address broader implications of AI and big data, including issues of discrimination and bias in algorithms, which can perpetuate systemic inequalities in areas such as consumer lending and credit scoring.

The ethical discourse surrounding these technologies emphasizes the need for careful consideration of their societal impacts, particularly on marginalized communities, and advocates for policies that mitigate biases and protect individual rights.
