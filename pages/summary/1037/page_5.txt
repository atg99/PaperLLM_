In summary, the paper explores critical questions surrounding the development and implementation of AI technologies, emphasizing the global digital divide, which restricts participation in AI design, particularly affecting women from the global south. The lack of educational opportunities and systemic barriers, such as visa issues for African scholars, limit engagement in AI discourse. Additionally, the paper discusses the implications of data representation in AI, noting that populations in low- and middle-income countries often lack a digital footprint, resulting in their exclusion from datasets that inform AI systems. This absence may lead to further marginalization. The paper also points out that the context in which AI technologies are deployed is crucial; even well-designed systems can perpetuate social injustices when they are integrated into societies with pre-existing inequalities.
