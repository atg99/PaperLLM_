The paper discusses the critical issue of global AI ethics, emphasizing the risk of exclusion faced by communities lacking adequate ICT infrastructure, which could hinder their participation in the technological advancements brought about by AI. It points out that individuals from low- and middle-income countries are often underrepresented in the datasets used to train AI systems, leading to further marginalization. The lack of educational opportunities, particularly for girls and women, contributes to a skills gap that prevents these communities from becoming creators of AI technologies.

The anticipated economic benefits of AI are predicted to primarily favor the U.S. and China, while low- and middle-income countries may only experience modest gains and significant job losses. This disparity threatens to widen existing income inequalities and create a class of digital laborers, who are often poorly compensated. The paper warns that the uneven distribution of AI benefits could exacerbate current digital divides, leading to geopolitical instability.

Furthermore, the social impacts of AI are expected to vary across different regions, with the most vulnerable populations experiencing the negative consequences first. The amplification of social inequalities resulting from AI could increase social instability and pose risks to entire societies, as highlighted by interdisciplinary scholarly literature linking social inequality with political and economic unrest.
