The paper discusses the global implications of AI ethics, particularly highlighting how AI systems can exacerbate social inequalities, especially in low- and middle-income countries. It notes that marginalized groups, including religious, ethnic, sexual, and gender minorities, are at heightened risk due to AI technologies. The World Economic Forum has identified significant risks for discriminatory outcomes in these regions, where economic hardship, political corruption, and authoritarian governance are prevalent. 

The paper cites examples such as the use of social media algorithms in the oppression of Myanmar's Rohingya population and China's surveillance of Uighurs to illustrate the detrimental effects of AI on vulnerable communities. It emphasizes the necessity of considering cultural contexts when evaluating the ethics of AI and its potential to reinforce existing inequalities.

A case study on facial recognition systems in Brazil further exemplifies these concerns. Following the introduction of a bill to allow facial recognition technology for policing, the paper raises alarms about the lack of research on the social impacts of such technologies outside the U.S., particularly in light of known biases in AI systems that disproportionately affect African Americans in judicial contexts. 

The paper concludes by noting that despite advances in global connectivity, the digital divide remains a significant issue, and the rise of AI technologies may deepen existing inequalities and create new forms of exclusion.
