The paper discusses the ethical implications of AI technology, particularly in the context of the Saudi Ministry of Interior's surveillance practices. It highlights how the technology, while not inherently flawed, reinforces existing social dynamics, particularly regarding gender. Women's responses to these technologies, especially through platforms like Twitter, illustrate both resistance and adaptation in a complex social landscape.

The importance of understanding culture in relation to AI ethics is emphasized. The notion of culture is critiqued for its oversimplification, particularly through the "trait list approach," which assumes fixed beliefs among ethnic or national groups. This reductionist view leads to harmful generalizations and stereotypes, undermining a nuanced understanding of human experience.

Anthropologists caution against the misuse of cultural narratives to justify discrimination or neglect, as evidenced by a case in Venezuela where health officials attributed a cholera epidemic to "indigenous culture," deflecting responsibility from systemic issues like inadequate healthcare and sanitation. This highlights the danger of employing cultural stereotypes in political discourse, reinforcing inequality rather than fostering genuine understanding.
