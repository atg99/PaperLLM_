The paper discusses the cultural differences in the understanding of privacy and data protection, highlighting that these concepts are not merely semantic but reflect deeper societal values and relationships among individuals, governments, and corporations. The author emphasizes that the ethical frameworks guiding AI principles are influenced by social histories and cultural assumptions, which complicate their application across different regions.

In the section "AI Principles in Practice," the paper questions how AI principles manifest globally, noting a lack of comprehensive research on the social impacts of AI outside the U.S. and Western Europe. It suggests that AI technologies will evolve uniquely in various cultural contexts, leading to diverse interpretations and implementations of fairness and inclusivity.

The paper illustrates this with examples from various countries: in Brazil, an AI system called "Rosie" helps combat corruption by analyzing payment patterns to politicians; in India, AI is successfully used to screen chest X-rays for tuberculosis; in Nigeria, machine learning aids farmers in crop management; and a start-up in India employs an algorithm to assess student comprehension through facial expressions. These instances underscore the idea that AI technologies adapt to local cultures, resulting in new forms and applications that can have significant social implications.
