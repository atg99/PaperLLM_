The paper emphasizes the necessity for a global AI ethics that acknowledges the risks associated with AI development in an unequal world. Philosopher John K. Roth highlights that merely discussing ethics is insufficient; action is required to address the potential for disaster. There is a pressing need to consider not just what technologies can be created, but what should be created, as emphasized by Luciano Floridi. While AI has the potential to exacerbate inequalities, it also holds promise for bridging divides and fostering inclusivity, as noted by Uyi Stewart. Achieving this requires AI practitioners to commit to ethical, transparent, and intentional implementation of AI technologies. The paper calls for a comprehensive ethical framework that involves determining the appropriate technologies to produce, monitoring their societal impacts, and ensuring they contribute positively to the world we aspire to create for future generations.
