The paper provides a comprehensive survey of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN), focusing on their architectural evolution and applications, particularly in computer vision. It categorizes CNN architectures into eight major types, highlighting their fundamental structures, historical development, and advancements. The survey aims to enhance readers' understanding of CNN design principles, aiding in the creation of novel architectures.

Key contributions of the paper include:

1. A detailed review of CNN's history, architecture designs, advantages, disadvantages, applications, and future research directions in a single document.
2. Guidance for researchers in making informed decisions regarding their work in CNN for computer vision.
3. An analysis of existing CNN architectures and their limitations, which can inspire new designs.
4. A clear categorization of CNN architectures based on implementation criteria.
5. An exploration of various applications of CNN beyond computer vision.
6. A listing of future research trends in CNN.

The organization of the paper begins with an overview of CNN fundamentals, drawing parallels between CNN and the primate visual cortex. The literature review is divided into two subsections, detailing CNN components and architectural evolutions. Subsequent sections discuss CNN applications, current architectural challenges, and future research scopes, concluding with a summary of various CNN variants.

The paper emphasizes that while humans can recognize images intuitively, teaching computers to do the same involves training algorithms on vast datasets, making image recognition a complex challenge.
