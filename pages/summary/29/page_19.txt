In video analysis, several researchers have leveraged CNNs combined with LSTM architectures to enhance activity recognition, addressing the challenges of geographical and temporal data collection. Notably, Shi's three-stream structure effectively captures spatial-temporal features, while bi-directional LSTMs facilitate sequential data processing. 

For low-resolution images, CNN-based methods have been pivotal, with techniques like LR-CNN and deep CNNs improving object identification and action recognition in thermal images. 

In resource-limited environments, CNN architectures, such as those implemented on FPGA platforms, have shown promise in maintaining power efficiency while executing complex machine learning tasks. Innovations like MobileNet and ShufﬂeNet cater specifically to mobile devices, enabling efficient deployment of CNNs in embedded systems.

Moreover, the versatility of CNNs extends to 1D data, where they have outperformed traditional machine learning models in tasks like network intrusion detection and biomedical data analysis. The paper also highlights the growing importance of 3D data for classification tasks, emphasizing the need for enhanced volumetric and multi-view CNN architectures to fully exploit 3D representations.

Overall, the research underscores the adaptability and effectiveness of CNNs across various applications, driving advancements in machine learning and computer vision.
