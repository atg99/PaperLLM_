The paper discusses recent advancements in convolutional neural networks (CNNs), highlighting their significance in image processing and related tasks over the past decade. It addresses challenges in developing deep CNNs on resource-limited devices and suggests using pipeline parallelism, exemplified by Google's GPipe, to enhance training efficiency without extensive hyperparameter tuning. 

The authors emphasize the potential of cloud computing platforms, such as those provided by Amazon, Microsoft, Google, and IBM, in facilitating the training of resource-intensive CNN applications. They also advocate for the use of 1D-CNNs for sequential data, noting their effective feature extraction capabilities.

Additionally, the application of CNNs in high-energy physics, particularly at CERN, and their role in human activity recognition are highlighted as emerging research areas. 

The conclusion summarizes eight key architectural advancements in CNNs, including spatial and feature-map exploitation, and the modular/block-based architecture that enhances accessibility and performance. The paper suggests that further research in 3D object recognition and neural architecture search (NAS) techniques remains promising.

The authors contributed collaboratively, with specific roles outlined in the manuscript, and the research was conducted without external funding.
