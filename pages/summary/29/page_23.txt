The paper discusses various architectures of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), highlighting their merits and demerits, particularly focusing on feature-map-based, channel-boosting, and attention-based CNN architectures.

1. **Feature-Map-Based CNN Architectures**:
   - **Squeeze and Excitation Network**: This architecture is praised for its simplicity and ability to integrate easily into existing CNN models. However, it is criticized for its limited ability to prioritize important features based on residual information.
   - **Competitive Squeeze and Excitation Networks**: This variant utilizes feature-map statistics from both residual and identity mappings but lacks an attention mechanism.

2. **Channel-Boosting CNN Architectures**:
   - **Channel Boosted CNN using Transfer Learning**: This approach enhances the representational capacity of CNNs by increasing input channels through innovative transfer learning methods. The downside is a potential increase in computational burden due to the additional auxiliary channels.

3. **Attention-Based CNN Architectures**:
   - **Residual Attention Neural Network**: This network excels in creating attention-aware feature maps through residual learning, allowing for distinct representations of important image patches. However, it suffers from high model complexity.
   - **Convolutional Block Attention Module (CBAM)**: CBAM is designed as a generic building block that sequentially produces feature-map and spatial attention, improving information flow. Despite its advantages, it may lead to significant computational load.

Overall, the paper outlines the trade-offs associated with different CNN architectures, emphasizing the balance between enhanced representational capabilities and computational efficiency.
