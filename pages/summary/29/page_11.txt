The paper discusses the significance of both depth and width in the development of learning philosophies for neural networks, particularly emphasizing that wide architectures are essential for maintaining universal approximation properties while increasing depth. It highlights a critical issue where deep neural networks may fail to learn valuable features, indicating that merely stacking layers does not enhance learning power. Moreover, it asserts that a deep network cannot approximate continuous functions effectively if its width is not greater than the input dimension, prompting a shift in focus from deep and narrow to wide and thin architectures.

The paper also explores Exploitation-Based Feature-Map (ChannelFMap) CNNs, which are noted for their hierarchical learning capabilities in computer vision. The selection of features is crucial as ineffective feature maps can lead to overfitting due to excessive noise. The importance of feature map selection is underscored for improving network generalization.

Additionally, the paper discusses CNNs based on attention mechanisms, which enhance the network's discrimination power by mimicking human visual attention. This approach allows the network to focus on relevant areas of an image, improving its ability to recognize objects in complex environments.

Lastly, it addresses dimension-based CNNs, which improve computational efficiency through separable convolutions that handle spatial and channel-wise information separately. This method reduces computational costs but can create bottlenecks in point-wise convolutions. The paper concludes with a summary table of various CNN variants and their architectural characteristics.
