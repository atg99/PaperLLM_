This paper presents a performance summary of various Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) variants, detailing their architectural categories, main roles, parameters, and error rates. The architectures are categorized primarily into spatial exploitation, depth and width, multi-path, and feature-map exploitation. 

Key architectures include:

- **LeNet (1998)**: The first prevalent CNN architecture with a low error rate on MNIST.
- **AlexNet (2012)**: Introduced deeper and wider networks, utilizing RELU and dropout, achieving significant improvements on ImageNet.
- **VGG (2014)**: Known for its small-sized kernels and homogeneous topology, it achieved a notable error rate on ImageNet.
- **GoogleNet (2015)**: Introduced the block concept with split-transform and merge ideas, further reducing error rates.
- **Inception Variants (2015-2016)**: These architectures, including Inception V-3 and V-4, addressed bottleneck issues and utilized asymmetric filters, achieving low error rates on ImageNet.
- **ResNet (2016)**: Introduced skip connections and residual learning, significantly improving performance on both ImageNet and CIFAR datasets.
- **Xception (2017)**: Focused on depth-based and point-based convolution, achieving an impressive error rate on ImageNet.
- **Squeeze and Excitation Network (2017)**: Modeled interdependencies between feature maps, enhancing performance on ImageNet.

Overall, the paper highlights the evolution of CNN architectures and their respective advancements in error reduction across various datasets, showcasing the shift from spatial exploitation to more complex multi-path and attention mechanisms.
