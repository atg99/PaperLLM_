The paper discusses the advantages and disadvantages of various multi-path-based Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) architectures, focusing on their deployment challenges. 

In Table 4, the merits and demerits of specific architectures are outlined. Highway Networks enhance connectivity across layers, reducing deep network constraints, but may incur high costs due to data-dependent gates. ResNet utilizes identity-based skip connections, allowing for efficient information flow, although it can suffer from redundant feature maps. DenseNet improves data flow and feature accessibility but increases parameter count significantly.

Table 5 addresses width-based CNN architectures. Wide ResNet demonstrates the benefits of expanding breadth over depth, promoting feature reuse but risking overfitting. Pyramidal Net progressively increases width to minimize information loss, yet faces high complexity challenges. Xception simplifies learning through depth-wise separable convolutions but incurs high computational costs, while Inception's varying filter sizes enhance output at the expense of increased complexity. ResNeXt introduces cardinality for layer transformations but also has a high computational burden.

The paper emphasizes the importance of feature-map selection, as deep learning architectures generate numerous feature maps, not all of which contribute equally to classification tasks.
