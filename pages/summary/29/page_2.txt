an overview of convolutional neural networks (CNNs), highlighting their significance in computer vision and image processing. CNNs are a type of deep learning algorithm that effectively processes images by assigning importance to different objects within them, requiring minimal preprocessing compared to traditional methods. Their capabilities in image classification, recognition, segmentation, and retrieval have garnered attention from major technology companies, leading to ongoing research and development of various CNN architectures.

The paper outlines the evolution of CNNs, starting from LeNet, which was developed by Yann LeCun for handwritten digit recognition, to contemporary models that have significantly advanced due to improved hardware, particularly GPUs. The success of AlexNet in 2012 marked a turning point for CNN applications in computer vision, leading to the emergence of numerous CNN variants.

Key features of CNNs include automatic feature extraction, multitasking capabilities, weight sharing, and hierarchical learning, which collectively enhance their performance in image analysis. The manuscript also reviews significant survey papers published between 2012 and 2021, discussing various CNN architectures, their components, challenges, and future trends.

Overall, the paper provides a comprehensive introduction to CNNs, detailing their historical development, architectural characteristics, and the taxonomy of notable CNN designs, while emphasizing their transformative impact on the field of computer vision.
