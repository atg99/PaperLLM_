The paper discusses the mechanics of convolutional neural networks (CNNs), focusing on the convolution and pooling layers. During the forwarding pass, a kernel traverses the input image, producing an activation map that reflects the kernel's response across different spatial positions. The output volume's size can be calculated using the formula Wout = (W - F + 2P) / S + 1, where W is the input size, F is the kernel size, S is the stride, and P is the padding.

Following the convolution layer, a pooling layer is introduced to reduce the spatial dimensions of the feature maps. This dimensionality reduction decreases the computational power needed and enhances the extraction of key features that are invariant to position and rotation, which is crucial for effective training. Pooling also helps in reducing training time and mitigating overfitting. The paper outlines two types of pooling: maximum pooling and average pooling, with maximum pooling selecting the highest value within a specified kernel size as it moves across the input tensor.
