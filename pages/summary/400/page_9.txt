The paper discusses the performance of the YOLO and YOLO-UA models in traffic monitoring across different environmental conditions. It highlights the significance of the Generalized Intersection over Union (GIOU) metric during the training of deep neural networks, noting that as training progresses, the location gap between predicted and actual frames diminishes, leading to reduced loss and improved accuracy.

Statistical results presented in Tables 3 and 4 show that the YOLO-UA model significantly outperforms the YOLO model, with average accuracy and recall rates improving by 22% and 21%, respectively, under various detection conditions. This indicates that the YOLO-UA model exhibits better adaptability to changing scenes and weather, effectively fulfilling traffic flow monitoring requirements.

The paper also discusses challenges faced by the models, particularly in sunny conditions where the number of vehicles is high, leading to difficulties in detecting smaller and unclear vehicle targets due to increased distance from the camera. Conversely, at night, the sparse distribution of vehicles allows for easier detection. Observations reveal that the YOLO model suffers from considerable missed and false detections, adversely affecting statistical accuracy. Overall, the findings underscore the enhanced capabilities of the YOLO-UA model in diverse operational scenarios.
