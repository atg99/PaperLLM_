The paper discusses the limitations of the YOLO model in vehicle monitoring, particularly its inability to predict multi-scale anchor boxes, which restricts the detection capabilities for vehicles of varying sizes. This limitation can lead to missed statistics and reduced accuracy in monitoring results. The study introduces an optimized version, YOLO-UA, which addresses these issues by reducing false detections and improving overall network performance.

In the impact accuracy experiments, the paper evaluates the statistical accuracy of both YOLO and YOLO-UA models by manipulating the CT and NT values. The results indicate that the YOLO-UA model consistently outperforms the YOLO model across different datasets, especially as the NT value increases. The study highlights that when vehicles are closely positioned, the overlapping boundary frames can complicate detection; however, with higher NT values, the Non-maximum suppression algorithm effectively mitigates leakage detection issues. Additionally, increasing the CT value allows for better classification by distinguishing reliable frames from less reliable ones, thereby enhancing statistical accuracy. Overall, the findings underscore the importance of model optimization in improving vehicle monitoring accuracy.
