In the analysis of experimental results, the UA-CAR datasets were utilized to evaluate the effectiveness of the optimization through precision and recall metrics. Precision is defined as the ratio of correctly detected vehicles to the total detected vehicles, while recall is the ratio of correctly detected vehicles to the actual number of vehicles in the dataset. The findings revealed an inverse relationship between precision and recall; enhancing recall by increasing correct detections resulted in a decrease in precision due to higher misdetection rates. Conversely, reducing false detections improved precision but lowered recall.

Further experiments assessed the accuracy of the YOLO and YOLO-UA models, using default thresholds CT = 0.1 and NT = 0.08. The results indicated that the YOLO-UA model significantly improved accuracy and recall rates compared to the YOLO model, with increases of 13% in accuracy and 12% in recall, albeit at the cost of precision.

Lastly, scene and weather adaptation experiments demonstrated that the optimized YOLO-UA model exhibited superior adaptability across various conditions, with optimal detection results observed at threshold settings of CT = 0.1 and NT = 0.15. The results highlighted the YOLO-UA model's effectiveness in a range of environments, confirming its robustness over the traditional YOLO model.
