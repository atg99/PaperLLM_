The paper discusses the challenges in improving detection accuracy in computer vision, particularly focusing on the regression bounding box, which is crucial for target detection and tracking. It highlights the limitations of traditional methods, such as deeper backbone networks and local feature extraction techniques, which often neglect the optimization of loss functions. The Intersection over Union (IOU) metric is introduced as a common evaluation tool for measuring the accuracy of object detectors, but its shortcomings in optimizing loss functions and handling overlapping frames are addressed.

The authors propose using the Generalized IOU (GIOU) to improve location accuracy. GIOU is defined as the IOU minus the proportion of the non-overlapping area to the minimum enclosing area of the two frames. This metric ranges from -1 to 1 and aims to enhance the optimization process by addressing the limitations of traditional IOU calculations. The paper provides a series of equations to calculate the intersection area and the areas of both predicted and actual frames, establishing a framework to optimize the loss function based on GIOU. Overall, the proposed approach aims to enhance detection performance by refining the measurement and optimization processes involved in bounding box regression.
