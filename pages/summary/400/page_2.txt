This paper highlights the importance of accurate traffic flow statistics for urban road management and discusses various existing methods for vehicle detection and tracking, including the limitations of traditional algorithms such as the Vi Be method, virtual coil method, and target tracking techniques. 

The paper emphasizes the role of deep learning in enhancing detection capabilities, particularly through region-based methods like R-CNN and its variants. While these methods have improved detection speed and accuracy, challenges remain in achieving real-time performance. To address these challenges, the authors propose a novel method that combines histogram of oriented gradients (HOG) and discrete wavelet transform (DWT) for pedestrian feature detection, utilizing motion amplitude to enhance detection speed.

Furthermore, the introduction of a lightweight RFBNet model, which incorporates dilated convolution and Receptive Field Block (RFB) modules, aims to optimize both speed and accuracy in target detection. This research contributes to the development of more efficient traffic monitoring systems, ultimately enabling better management of traffic flow in urban environments.
