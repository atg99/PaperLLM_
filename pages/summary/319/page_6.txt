The paper discusses the performance of E-BERT, particularly the E-BERT-concat variant, in relation to existing models like BERT, KnowBert, and ERNIE. E-BERT-concat shows significant improvements in mean Hits@k, especially for k greater than 1, indicating its strength in providing nearly correct answers even when the top answer is not fully accurate. This suggests that even less salient factual knowledge can enhance performance in downstream tasks.

The paper outlines two main downstream tasks: relation classification (RC) and entity linking (EL). In RC, E-BERT is used to predict directed relations between entities based on text. The model is evaluated using a dataset from the FewRel corpus, employing various embedding techniques including E-BERT-concat, E-BERT-replace, and baselines like Wikipedia2Vec-BERT. The results show that E-BERT-concat outperforms both original BERT and ERNIE, with E-BERT-replace also demonstrating superior performance over original BERT, highlighting the value of aligned entity vectors.

In the EL task, the focus is on identifying entity spans in text and linking them to entity IDs. The approach typically involves span detection, candidate generation, and selection of the correct entity. Overall, the paper emphasizes the advantages of E-BERT, particularly E-BERT-concat, in improving relation classification and its potential in entity linking tasks.
