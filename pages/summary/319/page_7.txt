The paper discusses a method for entity selection using a model called E-BERT-MLM, which builds upon the BERT architecture. The process begins with the KnowBert candidate generator that identifies spans from an input sentence based on a precomputed span-entity co-occurrence table. Each span is annotated with potential candidates and their prior probabilities, which are not specific to the context, leading to potential over-generation of candidates. 

To address this, the E-BERT-MLM model learns to reject irrelevant spans and disambiguate candidates using context. The model repurposes the masked language model (MLM) capabilities of BERT to predict entities from context. It takes a tokenized input that includes a designated [E-MASK] token for entity selection. The embeddings of candidates and their priors are incorporated into the model to inform the selection process.

The output probability distribution is defined over the candidates and a null entity, which is trained from scratch. The model is fine-tuned using pairs of spans and their corresponding gold entities, aiming to minimize the negative log probability of the correct entity given the input.

Additionally, the authors introduce an iterative refinement process, enhancing predictions by sorting spans based on their probabilities and replacing the top candidates in a non-overlapping manner. This approach has demonstrated improved performance on datasets like AIDA, outperforming various baseline models and showing the effectiveness of the E-BERT-MLM framework in entity selection tasks.
