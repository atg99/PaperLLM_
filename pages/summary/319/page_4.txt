This paper discusses the evaluation of language models, particularly E-BERT, ERNIE, and KnowBert, in determining the native languages of French-speaking actors. The study highlights the models' performance based on their entity vocabularies, with E-BERT having 2.7 million, ERNIE 5 million, and KnowBert 470,000 entries. The evaluation metric used is Hits@k, which counts whether the correct answer appears among the top-k predictions, diverging from traditional precision definitions.

The authors illustrate a potential flaw in the models' reasoning capabilities using the example of a quiz about actor Jean Marais. They argue that models like BERT may generate plausible but incorrect answers based on name associations rather than factual knowledge. To address this, they introduce LAMA-UHN (UnHelpful Names), a subset of LAMA designed to eliminate questions where the correct answer can be inferred from the entity name, using two heuristics: a string match filter and a person name filter. These filters aim to ensure that the models demonstrate genuine factual knowledge rather than relying on superficial name-based reasoning.

Overall, this research emphasizes the need for refined evaluation methods that distinguish between true knowledge and heuristic guessing in language models.
