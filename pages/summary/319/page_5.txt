The paper presents an analysis of the performance of various models on the LAMA dataset, particularly focusing on the impact of filters applied to the dataset, such as the string match filter and person name filter. It details the percentage of questions deleted due to these filters and provides examples of such deletions, highlighting the significance of certain relations like manufacturer, named after, and jurisdiction.

The results indicate that the E-BERT-concat model achieves new state-of-the-art results on LAMA, outperforming the original BERT model significantly. The paper discusses the differences in performance between BERT and E-BERT models, noting that while BERT's success is partly attributed to its ability to leverage helpful entity names, E-BERT-replace relies more on factual knowledge. This suggests that E-BERT models, particularly E-BERT-concat, effectively combine entity names and vectors to enhance performance.

Further analysis illustrates the varying impacts of the filters on performance across different relations, with a particular focus on the performance drop of E-BERT-replace compared to BERT when the filters are applied. The findings emphasize the importance of understanding the role of entity names in model performance and suggest avenues for future research in improving knowledge representation in language models.
