The paper introduces E-BERT, which has two variants: E-BERT-concat and E-BERT-replace. E-BERT-concat combines entity IDs with wordpieces through string concatenation, using a slash as a separator. For instance, the input "The native language of Jean Mara ##is is [MASK]" transforms into "The native language of Jean Marais / Jean Mara ##is is [MASK]". The entity ID is embedded by EE-BERT, while wordpieces are embedded by EBERT, and the resulting vector sequence combines with position and segment embeddings before being processed by FBERT. This approach is compared to ERNIE and KnowBERT, though unlike these models, E-BERT does not alter or further pretrain the BERT encoder.

E-BERT-replace substitutes the entity surface form with the entity vector, simplifying the example to "The native language of Jean Marais is [MASK]".

The implementation of E-BERT involves training cased Wikipedia2Vec on a recent Wikipedia dump, resulting in an entity vocabulary of approximately 2.7 million. The training took around six hours on 32 CPUs without the need for a GPU, contrasting with the more resource-intensive pretraining of KnowBERT and ERNIE.

Additionally, the paper discusses the application of E-BERT in unsupervised question answering (QA) using the LAMA benchmark, which evaluates language models on factual and commonsense knowledge without supervised fine-tuning. The LAMA task involves predicting an object from a knowledge base triple using a cloze-style question, with the model generating a probability distribution over a limited vocabulary to fill in the masked object.

The primary baselines for comparison are cased BERT BASE and BERT LARGE, as utilized in previous studies. The study aims to demonstrate that E-BERT can provide competitive performance in QA tasks without the extensive resource requirements of other models.
