The paper discusses the critical aspects of ethical considerations in artificial intelligence (AI) and highlights the importance of privacy-by-design, the social impact of AI, and the need for ethical frameworks. It emphasizes that a deeper understanding of human rights laws can enable AI designers to mitigate issues related to discrimination and privacy invasion.

The social impact section addresses the contentious debate surrounding automation and job displacement. While some argue that AI will eliminate jobs, others believe it will create new opportunities. The paper raises concerns about the disruption of the labor market and the potential emergence of a "useless class," as noted by Harari. It mentions proposals like Universal Basic Income (UBI) as potential solutions to address unemployment caused by AI advancements.

Accessibility is highlighted as an ethical principle, stressing the need for technology to be available and suitable for all individuals, including marginalized groups. The paper questions the fairness of technological benefits if they are not equitably distributed.

Furthermore, the paper discusses the implications of unethical AI on democracy and civil rights, noting that misinformation and loss of trust can undermine democratic structures and civil liberties. It calls for AI ethics to prioritize these fundamental rights.

The discussion section outlines a framework for defining and implementing AI ethics, emphasizing that while the theoretical aspects are complex, practical application is equally challenging. Ethical AI is defined as AI that does no harm, yet the paper acknowledges the ambiguity surrounding concepts of harm and human rights, calling for ethical sensitivity training for those involved in AI development.
