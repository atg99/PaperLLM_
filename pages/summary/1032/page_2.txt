The paper by Keng Siau and Weiyu Wang explores the dual facets of AI ethics: the ethics of AI, which involves the study of ethical principles, guidelines, and regulations governing AI technologies, and ethical AI, which refers to the development of AI systems that operate in an ethical manner. The authors argue that understanding potential ethical and moral challenges posed by AI is crucial in formulating effective ethical frameworks.

Key issues highlighted include the low explainability of AI systems, data biases, and concerns regarding data security and privacy. The paper emphasizes that as AI technologies become more integrated into various sectors, addressing these ethical concerns is paramount to ensuring user safety and societal well-being.

The authors pose several critical questions regarding perceived ethical issues with AI, the common principles that could mitigate these issues, and the characteristics necessary for ethical AI. They advocate for a proactive approach to developing ethical guidelines that can inform the creation of AI systems, ultimately promoting responsible AI practices.

In summary, the paper underscores the importance of establishing a robust ethical framework for AI development and usage, which is essential for harnessing its benefits while minimizing potential risks to individuals and society.
