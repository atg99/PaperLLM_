The paper discusses ethical frameworks relevant to artificial intelligence (AI), highlighting key concepts such as beneficence, justice, privacy, and accountability. It references established frameworks by Ken Blanchard and Norman Vincent Peale, which pose three critical questions regarding legality, fairness, and personal feelings. The Markkula Center Framework offers five approaches to ethical issues, including utilitarianism and virtue ethics.

The paper differentiates between "ethics of AI," which addresses the moral implications of human interactions with AI and the societal impacts of AI technologies, and "ethical AI," which focuses on the moral behavior of AI agents themselves. Roboethics pertains to human responsibilities in designing and using AI, while machine ethics involves the moral conduct expected from AI as it evolves.

The paper emphasizes the need for ethical guidelines as AI becomes more sophisticated, referencing Isaac Asimov's Three Laws of Robotics. These laws advocate for the protection of humans and the robot's own existence, but the paper suggests these laws may need to be expanded to include ethical interactions among intelligent AI agents.

Ultimately, understanding the ethics of AI is crucial for establishing a framework for developing ethical AI systems that interact responsibly with both humans and society. The paper proposes an initial framework for creating ethical AI, addressing how AI should ethically engage with other AI, humans, and operate within society.
