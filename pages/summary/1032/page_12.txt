The paper discusses the ethical considerations surrounding artificial intelligence (AI) and categorizes ethical agents into three types: explicit, implicit, and full ethical agents. Explicit ethical agents clearly define permissible and forbidden actions, while full ethical agents possess consciousness and free will, a concept still in research and development. The paper raises critical ethical questions about the treatment of AI agents with consciousness and moral sense, particularly regarding the implications of shutting down such agents and deploying robots in hazardous environments.

The paper highlights the European Commission's commitment to ensuring that AI developed in Europe adheres to higher ethical standards compared to other regions. It notes that various countries, including the U.S. and China, are also developing AI ethics frameworks, with initiatives focused on responsible AI stewardship. Despite these efforts, the paper points out that the current focus among AI developers tends to be on performance metrics rather than ethical considerations, driven by competitive pressures.

Consumer behavior further complicates the ethical landscape, as many prioritize price and quality over ethical implications, leading to a potential tradeoff for companies between advancing AI technology and ensuring ethical practices. The paper concludes that understanding and addressing the ethical dimensions of AI is still in its early stages and emphasizes the need for ongoing discussion among stakeholders. It advocates for the integration of ethics into AI development as a fundamental consideration rather than an afterthought, asserting that the future of humanity may hinge on the proper establishment of AI ethics.
