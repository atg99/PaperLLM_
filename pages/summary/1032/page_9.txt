The paper discusses the criteria necessary for robots to be viewed as moral agents, which include autonomy, intentionality, and responsibility. It highlights the challenges of assigning accountability when AI agents fail, raising the question of who is responsible—the programmer, data owner, or end-user.

The issue of human bias in AI is also addressed, noting that biases related to gender and race can be inherited from the data used to train AI systems. This underscores the need for careful programming and training to avoid perpetuating these biases.

Furthermore, the paper emphasizes the difficulty of establishing ethical standards for AI, as these principles must be comprehensive and universally understood. It suggests that a global ethical framework is necessary to guide AI behavior, particularly regarding the concept of harm.

Lastly, the importance of educating software engineers about human rights laws is stressed to prevent unintentional violations during AI development. This education is critical in fostering ethical AI systems that respect and uphold human rights.
