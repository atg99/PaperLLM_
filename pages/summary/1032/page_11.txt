The paper discusses the challenges and importance of developing ethical artificial intelligence (AI). It emphasizes that while AI theoretically should recognize ethical issues, practical implementation is complex and requires sustained efforts. Institutions like Google, IBM, and Microsoft are working on ethical principles to guide AI development, including the FEAT principles launched by the Monetary Authority of Singapore, which focus on fairness, ethics, accountability, and transparency.

Collaboration among academics, practitioners, and policymakers is essential to establish ethical principles for AI design and usage. Good governance and legal frameworks are necessary to enforce these principles, and existing regulatory measures in the U.S. can inform the development of AI ethics policies.

The paper also highlights the role of self-regulation, communication, and public education in fostering trust and understanding of AI. It references Moor's (2006) framework for educating AI as ethical agents, proposing three categories: implicit ethical agents, explicit ethical agents, and full ethical agents. This approach aims to enhance the ethical sensitivity of AI systems and ensure their responsible deployment.
