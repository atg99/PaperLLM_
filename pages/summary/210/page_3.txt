This paper discusses the application of reinforcement learning (RL) in trading strategies, specifically focusing on the challenges and methodologies involved. Traditional trading strategies that rely on directional moves can incur significant losses during sideways market movements due to the unpredictability of signals. To address this, the authors propose using time series momentum features combined with technical indicators to create a state space for deriving trade positions through RL.

The literature on RL in trading is categorized into three primary approaches: critic-only, actor-only, and actor-critic. The critic-only method, particularly Deep Q-learning Networks (DQN), constructs a state-action value function (Q) to evaluate actions in a given state. However, this method struggles with large action spaces, which are necessary for scaling positions according to market conditions.

The actor-only approach optimizes the objective function directly without predicting outcomes for each action, allowing for continuous action spaces. However, it tends to learn slowly and requires substantial sample sizes to refine policies due to the potential misclassification of actions as beneficial based solely on total rewards.

The actor-critic approach aims to mitigate these learning challenges by updating two models simultaneously: the actor, which dictates the agent's actions based on the current state, and the critic, which evaluates the quality of those actions. Despite its potential, this method has been less explored in financial applications, which the authors seek to address.

The methodology section introduces the formalization of the trading problem as a Markov Decision Process (MDP), where the agent interacts with the environment over discrete time steps. The agent receives a state, chooses an action, and receives a reward, with the goal of maximizing expected returns over time. The paper also outlines the RL algorithms employed, including DQN, Policy Gradients (PG), and Advantage Actor-Critic (A2C), and emphasizes the importance of state, action spaces, and reward functions in the context of trading.
