In their paper, Zhang, Zohren, and Roberts explore the application of Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) algorithms in developing trading strategies for continuous futures contracts. They consider both discrete and continuous action spaces, integrating volatility scaling to enhance reward functions that adjust trade positions according to market volatility. The authors evaluate their algorithms on 50 highly liquid futures contracts from 2011 to 2019, examining performance across various asset classes, including commodities, equity indices, fixed income, and foreign exchange markets.

The study compares the proposed DRL methods against traditional time series momentum strategies, demonstrating that the DRL approach yields superior performance, achieving positive profits even in the face of substantial transaction costs. The results indicate that the DRL algorithms are capable of effectively following significant market trends without altering positions and can appropriately scale down or hold during periods of market consolidation.

The introduction highlights the evolution of financial trading methodologies, emphasizing the rise of algorithmic trading, which now constitutes a significant portion of trading volume in U.S. stock exchanges. The authors note the challenges posed by the low signal-to-noise ratio in financial data and the dynamic nature of markets, which complicates the design of predictive signals that can generate excess returns.

The paper argues for the use of Reinforcement Learning as a means to directly derive trade positions rather than relying on predictive forecasting, thus addressing the complexities associated with transforming predictive signals into actionable trading strategies. The authors aim to provide a more effective framework for maximizing returns within the constraints of modern portfolio theory.
