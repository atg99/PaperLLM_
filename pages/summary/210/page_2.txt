The performance of this wealth measure is influenced by sequences of interdependent actions that affect both immediate and future returns. This scenario aligns with optimal control theory, characterizing a classical sequential decision-making process. For risk-neutral investors, the utility function is linear, leading to a focus on maximizing expected cumulative trade returns, E(∑ Wt), fitting within the reinforcement learning (RL) framework aimed at maximizing expected cumulative rewards through agent-environment interaction.

The study employs state-of-the-art RL algorithms, including Deep Q-learning Networks (DQN), Policy Gradients (PG), and Advantage Actor-Critic (A2C), exploring both discrete and continuous action spaces. Reward functions are enhanced with volatility scaling to adjust trade positions according to market volatility. The robustness of these algorithms is demonstrated through testing on the 50 most liquid futures contracts from 2011 to 2019 across various asset classes, including commodities, equity indexes, fixed income, and FX markets. The proposed method outperforms classical time series momentum strategies, generating positive returns across all sectors despite transaction costs, while time series strategies show varied performance depending on market conditions.

The paper is structured as follows: Section 2 reviews existing literature, including classical trading strategies and the application of RL in trading. Section 3 details the methodology, while Section 4 compares the proposed method with baseline algorithms and examines trading performance across asset classes. The dataset and training methodology are also described in this section. Finally, Section 5 concludes the findings and discusses future work extensions.
