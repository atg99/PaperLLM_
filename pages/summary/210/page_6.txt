The paper presents a series of experiments utilizing a dataset of 50 ratio-adjusted continuous futures contracts sourced from the Pinnacle Data Corp CLC Database, covering the period from 2005 to 2019. The dataset includes diverse asset classes such as commodities, equity indices, fixed income, and foreign exchange. The model is retrained every five years using all available data up to that point, with parameters fixed for out-of-sample testing from 2011 to 2019.

The study compares various baseline algorithms, including classical time series momentum strategies like Long Only, Sign(R), and MACD Signal, with reinforcement learning (RL) algorithms: Deep Q-Network (DQN), Policy Gradient (PG), and Advantage Actor-Critic (A2C). The RL algorithms utilize Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) neural networks for both actor and critic components, leveraging LSTMs' proven effectiveness in financial time-series modeling.

The paper outlines the training schemes for the RL algorithms, detailing the architecture of LSTM networks and the choice of hyperparameters. Each asset class is trained with a separate model to enhance performance. The hyperparameters, including learning rates, batch sizes, and memory sizes, are summarized in a table, providing insight into the model configurations used for DQN, PG, and A2C. The Adam optimizer is employed for training, and specific parameters are set to regularize and optimize the agents' trading strategies.
