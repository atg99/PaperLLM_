This paper discusses the representation of state spaces, action spaces, and reward functions in the context of trading strategies. 

For state representation, the authors utilize a combination of a security's past price, returns over varying horizons, and technical indicators such as the Moving Average Convergence Divergence (MACD) and the Relative Strength Index (RSI). Specifically, they analyze the last 60 observations of these features to form a single state. The features include a normalized close price series, returns normalized by daily volatility over different time frames, and technical indicators calculated using established methodologies.

The action space is examined in both discrete and continuous formats. In discrete action spaces, three positions are defined: maximally short, no holdings, and maximally long, with the potential for transaction costs depending on changes in position. The continuous action space allows for trade positions to take any value between -1 and 1, providing flexibility in trading decisions.

The reward function is designed based on a utility function that focuses on profits, representing a risk-insensitive trader. The reward at any time is influenced by the trader's actions and is scaled according to market volatility, allowing for normalization across different futures contracts. The transaction cost is accounted for as a fraction of the traded value, ensuring that costs are appropriately represented based on the varying price ranges of contracts.

The authors emphasize the importance of using additive profits for their trading model, particularly when trading fixed quantities, while noting that multiplicative profits may be more suitable for trading fractions of wealth. This structure aims to enhance the efficacy of trading strategies across multiple contracts.
