The paper discusses various reinforcement learning (RL) algorithms with a focus on their implementations and improvements. It starts by highlighting the use of additive profits over logarithmic transformations to avoid penalizing large wealth growths in the RL setup.

1. **Deep Q-learning Networks (DQN)**: DQNs approximate the state-action value function using neural networks. The objective is to minimize the mean squared error between the current and target Q-values. To address training stability issues, the paper incorporates three strategies: fixed Q-targets, Double DQN, and Dueling DQN. Fixed Q-targets and Double DQN reduce policy variances and mitigate the "chasing tails" problem by employing a separate network for target values. Dueling DQNs enhance learning by separating state values and action advantages, allowing for more robust updates.

2. **Policy Gradients (PG)**: PG methods aim to maximize expected cumulative rewards by directly optimizing the policy. They generate trajectories from the environment and adjust the policy parameters using gradient ascent. While PG can effectively handle stochastic policies and continuous action spaces, it often suffers from slow training and potential convergence to local maxima due to its reliance on Monte Carlo methods for trajectory sampling.

3. **Advantage Actor-Critic (A2C)**: A2C improves upon PG by allowing real-time policy updates through two networks: an actor that outputs the policy and a critic that evaluates the chosen actions. The objective function maximizes the log probability of actions weighted by the advantage function, which measures the relative value of actions. The critic network helps calculate advantages and is updated using gradient descent to minimize temporal difference errors. A2C is particularly advantageous for continuous action spaces, reducing policy variance and permitting either synchronous or asynchronous training, with this work adopting the synchronous approach.

Overall, the paper outlines the strengths and weaknesses of each RL algorithm, emphasizing improvements in stability and efficiency in training processes.
