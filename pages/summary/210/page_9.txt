The paper explores the application of reinforcement learning (RL) algorithms in developing trading strategies for continuous futures contracts. It highlights the relationship between modern portfolio theory and the RL reward hypothesis, demonstrating their equivalence when using a linear utility function. The study evaluates three RL algorithms: Deep Q-learning Network, Policy Gradients, and Advantage Actor-Critic, and examines both discrete and continuous action spaces. By incorporating time series momentum and technical indicators into state representations and applying volatility scaling to enhance reward functions, the authors conduct tests on 50 liquid futures contracts from 2011 to 2019. The findings reveal that RL algorithms consistently outperform baseline models, generating profits even when accounting for significant transaction costs.

The paper also discusses the potential for future research, suggesting an exploration of various utility functions that reflect risk aversion in investors. The authors propose using distributional reinforcement learning to capture the entire distribution of Q-values, allowing for the selection of actions that maximize the Sharpe Ratio while minimizing risk. Additionally, they contemplate extending their methods to portfolio optimization by adjusting action spaces to allocate weights among individual contracts, integrating reward functions with mean-variance portfolio theory to achieve favorable expected returns with reduced volatility.
