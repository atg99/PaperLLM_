The paper presents a comparative analysis of various lightweight models for image classification in agriculture, specifically focusing on different attention mechanisms. The models evaluated include a base lightweight ResNet20 model and its variants enhanced with convolutional block attention module (CBAM), squeeze-and-excitation (SE), self-attention (SA), and dual attention (DA). 

The performance metrics, including precision, recall, F1 score, and average accuracy, are detailed in the results. Notably, the lw_resnet20_cbam model achieved the highest average accuracy of 99.69%, outperforming the base model, which recorded an average accuracy of 98.58%. The confusion matrices illustrate the models' effectiveness across various classes of plant diseases, such as bacterial spot and early blight.

Additionally, the paper provides information on network parameters, training and testing efficiency, disk size, and floating-point operations per second (FLOPs) for each model. The lw_resnet20 model has the lowest complexity with 1,424,043 parameters and an average accuracy of 98.58%, while the standard ResNet50 model, despite its higher accuracy of 98.74%, is significantly more complex with over 23 million parameters. 

Overall, the findings suggest that integrating attention mechanisms can enhance model performance while maintaining lightweight efficiency for practical applications in agricultural image classification.
