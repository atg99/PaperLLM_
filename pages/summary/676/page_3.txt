The study aimed to enhance the detection of plant diseases, specifically focusing on tomato diseases, by developing a lightweight convolutional neural network (CNN) integrated with various attention modules. The proposed model utilized a 20-layer ResNet architecture, which was optimized to have fewer trainable parameters. Attention mechanisms such as the convolutional block attention module, self-attention module, squeeze-and-excitation module, and dual attention module were incorporated to evaluate their influence on the performance of the CNN.

A comprehensive dataset consisting of 19,510 images representing 10 classes of tomato diseases, including one healthy class, was utilized for training, validation, and testing purposes, with a split ratio of 8:1:1. The images underwent preprocessing, including labeling, resizing, rescaling, and augmentation, to enhance the model's learning capability.

The design of the CNN emphasized computational efficiency while maintaining effective disease classification performance. The study included an ablation analysis to determine the optimal number and placement of attention modules within the network. Performance metrics such as accuracy, precision, recall, and F1 score were employed to assess model effectiveness. Additionally, the computational complexity and processing times were evaluated to ensure the model's practicality for real-world applications in plant disease detection. Overall, the research contributes to the development of more efficient deep learning models for agricultural applications.
