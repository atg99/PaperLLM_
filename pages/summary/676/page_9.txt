The paper discusses the training and evaluation of deep convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for agricultural applications, focusing on the impact of data augmentation and hyperparameter optimization on model performance. Various data augmentation techniques were applied exclusively to training datasets, significantly increasing the number of training images by eight times. The Adam optimizer, with a default learning rate, was selected to ensure effective convergence during training, which was conducted over 100 epochs. 

Models were saved at each epoch to minimize validation losses, and performance was measured using standard classification metrics, including precision, recall, F1 score, and accuracy. The computational complexity of the models was assessed through floating point operations (FLOPs). 

The training setup utilized a consistent hardware and software environment, featuring an Intel Core i9 processor, NVIDIA RTX 2070 GPU, and Keras with TensorFlow. The paper provides detailed specifications of the training hyperparameters, including batch size, learning rate, and specific data augmentation methods such as image zooming, flipping, and rotation. Overall, the study highlights the importance of data augmentation and careful hyperparameter selection in enhancing the performance of deep learning models in agricultural contexts.
