The paper presents a detailed analysis of a dataset used for training, validating, and testing models in agricultural disease detection, particularly focusing on various plant diseases. Table 4 outlines the statistics of the datasets, highlighting the original and augmented counts for each class, including bacterial spot, early blight, fusarium wilt, and others. Data augmentation was applied exclusively to the training datasets, resulting in a total of 15,603 training samples and 1,947 samples for validation and testing.

In the results section, the paper discusses the training and validation accuracy of different models. It notes that the base model without an attention module (lw_resnet20) trained more slowly compared to models with attention modules. Specifically, the lw_resnet20_se model, which incorporates a squeeze-and-excitation (SE) attention module, demonstrated quicker training capabilities. Despite showing the best training accuracy, the base model had lower validation accuracy compared to the lw_resnet20_cbam and lw_resnet20_da models, which achieved the highest validation accuracy and loss results.

Overall, the findings emphasize the importance of model architecture, particularly the role of attention mechanisms in enhancing training efficiency and validation performance in agricultural disease classification tasks.
