The evolution of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) began in the 1980s, but gained momentum after AlexNet's success with the ImageNet dataset. CNNs are now prevalent in agriculture for tasks such as quality inspection, disease identification, and crop monitoring.

Despite their effectiveness, accurately detecting plant diseases remains challenging due to factors like cluttered backgrounds and variations in disease symptoms. Recent advancements in hardware and software have led to increased implementation of deep CNNs for these tasks. For instance, researchers like Toda and Okura have utilized architectures like InceptionV3 to analyze large datasets, achieving significant parameter reductions through visualization techniques.

Traditional CNNs focus on global features, which can obscure important foreground details. To mitigate this, integrating attention mechanisms allows models to prioritize significant features, improving classification accuracy. Several studies have explored this approach, demonstrating enhancements in performance when attention modules are incorporated into CNN architectures. Researchers like Zeng and Li, as well as Zhao et al., have proposed various models that combine attention mechanisms with existing CNN frameworks, yielding better results in classifying plant diseases.

Nonetheless, these advanced models often require substantial computational resources and large datasets for training. As such, striking a balance between model complexity and the need for accessible hardware remains crucial for the practical application of deep learning in agricultural disease detection.
