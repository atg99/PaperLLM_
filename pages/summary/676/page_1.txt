The paper by Bhujel et al. presents a novel approach to tomato leaf disease classification using a lightweight attention-based convolutional neural network (CNN). The study addresses the critical challenge of plant disease identification, which is essential for mitigating crop losses. Traditional methods of disease detection, reliant on the experience of farmers, are often inefficient and prone to inaccuracies due to the complexity of symptoms and the variety of diseases.

To enhance classification performance while reducing computational demands, the authors designed a lightweight CNN that integrates various attention modules. The models were trained and evaluated on tomato leaf disease datasets, with a split ratio of 8:1:1 for training, validation, and testing. The effectiveness of the attention mechanisms was assessed based on standard metrics such as precision, recall, and F1 score.

Results indicated that the incorporation of attention mechanisms improved model performance, achieving over 1.1% higher accuracy compared to traditional models. The lightweight CNN significantly decreased the number of network parameters (approximately 16 times less) and complexity (around 23 times less) compared to the standard ResNet50 model. Among the proposed models, the convolutional block attention module (CBAM) yielded the highest average accuracy of 99.69%, followed by the self-attention mechanism at 99.34%.

In conclusion, the study demonstrates that attention-based lightweight CNNs can effectively enhance tomato leaf disease classification while maintaining a low computational footprint, thereby offering a promising solution for timely agricultural interventions.
