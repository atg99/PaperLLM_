The section discusses advancements in Connectionist Temporal Classification (CTC)-based automatic speech recognition (ASR) models, highlighting their structural evolution and the importance of large-scale data for effective training. One notable advantage of CTC is its elimination of the need for data segmentation, allowing for the integration of deep learning techniques, particularly Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN) and Recurrent Neural Networks (RNN).

Early works, such as a three-layer model for large vocabulary continuous speech recognition (LVCSR), demonstrated that increasing the network's depth and the number of hidden units improves recognition accuracy. Subsequent studies emphasized the necessity of deeper architectures, leading to the development of a five-layer bidirectional LSTM model that achieved state-of-the-art results. This sparked further innovations in five-layer networks.

As deep learning progressed, models began to incorporate various configurations. Some studies introduced CNNs alongside RNNs, while others focused solely on CNNs, demonstrating that deeper networks can effectively capture time-domain characteristics. Notably, a nine-layer RNN model outperformed human performance on specific tasks, and deep neural networks with substantial hidden units achieved optimal outcomes on extensive datasets.

However, the trend toward deeper networks does not universally guarantee improved results; some research opted for shallower architectures due to dataset limitations. For instance, a five-layer model was trained on a significantly smaller dataset compared to a seven-layer model that utilized a much larger dataset.

Furthermore, the success of deep ASR models is closely tied to the availability of large-scale training data. Effective training of complex models requires extensive datasets, as exemplified by a model that utilized over 7,000 hours of clean speech data, supplemented by additional datasets and synthetic noise, totaling over 100,000 hours of data.
