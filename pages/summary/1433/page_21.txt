The paper discusses various end-to-end models for speech recognition, focusing on their architectures and performance on a large vocabulary continuous speech recognition (LVCSR) task. The encoders utilize five bi-directional long short-term memory (BLSTM) layers with 350 dimensions each, while the decoders employ a beam search decoding method with a width of 15, without using additional language models or pronunciation dictionaries. A Connectionist Temporal Classification (CTC) model is trained to predict character outputs, and its encoder weights are used to initialize other sequence-to-sequence models to enhance convergence speed.

The RNN-transducer model features a single 700-dimensional Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) for the prediction network and a forward network with a tanh activation function in its joint network. Attention-based decoders are also tested, comparing one-layer and two-layer GRU models. The baseline for comparison is a context-dependent phoneme CTC model, which consists of five layers of 700-dimensional LSTM, predicting 8192 CD-phoneme distributions and optimized via CTC-criterion and state-level minimum Bayes risk (sMBR) training.

To train these deep networks, the study utilizes approximately 15 million hand-transcribed anonymized utterances, amounting to around 12,500 hours of data. Multi-condition training data is generated to enhance system robustness against noise and reverberation, using artificial distortions and noise samples from various sources, targeting an overall signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) of 0 to 30 dB.

Testing involves three categories of data: open-ended dictation, voice-search queries, and numeric instances, with additional noisy versions created for robustness evaluation. The performance of different models is summarized in a table showcasing word error rates (WER) across clean and noisy datasets for various model configurations. The results indicate notable differences in performance, with the baseline models generally outperforming the end-to-end systems in clean conditions, while certain RNN-based models perform competitively in noisy scenarios.
