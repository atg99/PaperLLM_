The paper discusses advancements in attention mechanisms, specifically addressing two key issues: the lack of location information and the inability to focus on significant key features. To tackle the first issue, a new attention mechanism was proposed that incorporates location information through a hybrid approach that utilizes convolution. This mechanism processes location data, enhancing the attention's effectiveness.

For the second issue, the authors critique the traditional softmax operation used in attention calculations, which retains noise and fails to adequately focus on meaningful features due to its characteristics. They highlight that softmax ensures all items have positive weights, leading to the retention of irrelevant information while concentrating attention on dominant features at the expense of others. To mitigate these problems, the authors introduced three sharpening techniques: scaling softmax values to enhance significant weights, retaining only the top k values post-softmax normalization, and employing a calculation window that limits attention to a subset of data during computation.

The implementation of these techniques resulted in a 17.6% word error rate (WER) on the TIMIT dataset, with a WER of 20% for extended sentences. Further improvements were made by incorporating window technology in the training phase, which adjusted the window selection process to reduce dependency on previous window quality. This adjustment, based on training data statistics, enhanced model performance and training stability.
