The paper compares various Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) models, specifically focusing on CTC-based, RNN-Transducer, and Attention-based models. Key characteristics evaluated include delay, computational complexity, language modeling ability, training difficulty, and recognition accuracy.

1. **Delay**: The CTC-based model exhibits the shortest delay due to its ability to adjust decoding results immediately after receiving encoding results. RNN-Transducer has higher delay because it incorporates previous decoding outputs into its calculations. The Attention-based model has the longest delay as it waits for all encoding results before starting decoding, limiting its support for streaming speech recognition.

2. **Computational Complexity**: CTC requires a softmax operation at each input time step, resulting in a complexity of O(T). RNN-Transducer and primitive attention share a complexity of O(TN) due to softmax operations on input/output pairs. However, more advanced attention models can reduce complexity to O(wN) by using window mechanisms.

3. **Language Modeling Capabilities**: CTC lacks the ability to model language due to its assumption of label independence. In contrast, RNN-Transducer incorporates language modeling via a joint network, while attention models achieve this through their decoder networks.

4. **Training Difficulty**: CTC is easier to train because it functions primarily as a loss function for an acoustic model. The RNN-Transducer and attention models are more complex due to their additional weights and joint training requirements, making them more challenging to optimize.

5. **Recognition Accuracy**: RNN-Transducer and attention-based models typically achieve higher recognition accuracy than CTC, with attention models generally performing better than RNN-Transducer due to their enhanced linguistic knowledge modeling.

The paper includes experimental comparisons using 80-dimensional log-mel features as input for all models, which utilize characters as output units, including English letters, numerals, spaces, and punctuation. These experiments aim to highlight the strengths and weaknesses of the different ASR methods.
