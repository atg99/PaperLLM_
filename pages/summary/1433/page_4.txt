The paper discusses the application of Bayesian theory in the context of Hidden Markov Models (HMM) for speech recognition, specifically focusing on the decomposition of the probability of a sequence of observed data given a model. The key equation presented is the maximization of the conditional probability p(L|X), which can be expressed in terms of joint probabilities and factors corresponding to the acoustic model, pronunciation model, and language model.

1. **Acoustic Model p(X|S)**: This represents the likelihood of observing the data X given the hidden state sequence S. The model is based on the assumption that observations depend only on the current hidden state. It can be decomposed into probabilities that can be calculated using Gaussian Mixture Models (GMM) or Deep Neural Networks (DNN). The transition from HMM-GMM to HMM-DNN is highlighted, showing that DNN provides better performance in estimating the posterior probabilities of the hidden states compared to GMM.

2. **Pronunciation Model p(S|L)**: This model serves as a dictionary that connects the acoustic sequences to the language sequences. It includes various levels of mapping, from pronunciation to phonemes and from phonemes to triphones, facilitating both structural mapping and probability calculations.

3. **Language Model p(L)**: This model is trained on extensive corpora using a Markov hypothesis to generate an n-gram language model, capturing the probability of word sequences based on previous words.

Overall, the transition from traditional methods to deep learning approaches in acoustic modeling represents a significant advancement in automatic speech recognition systems, enhancing their accuracy and effectiveness.
