The paper discusses the advantages of end-to-end large vocabulary continuous speech recognition (LVCSR) models, particularly focusing on the Connectionist Temporal Classification (CTC) approach. Unlike traditional Hidden Markov Model (HMM)-based systems, which require explicit internal representations for pronunciation and often involve complex processing for transcription, end-to-end models directly map acoustic signatures to text sequences, simplifying model construction and training.

The paper identifies alignment issues as a common challenge in sequence-to-sequence tasks, especially in speech recognition. Both HMM-based and end-to-end models face the problem of aligning label sequences with speech data. The end-to-end models employ soft alignment, where each audio frame is associated with all possible states according to a probability distribution, eliminating the need for forced alignments.

Three categories of end-to-end models based on their implementation of soft alignment are outlined: 

1. **CTC-based models**: These enumerate all possible hard alignments to achieve soft alignment, assuming independence among output labels.
2. **RNN-transducer models**: Similar to CTC, but they allow for dependencies among labels, altering the path definitions and probability calculations.
3. **Attention-based models**: These utilize an attention mechanism to compute soft alignment directly, without enumerating hard alignments.

The paper highlights that while HMM-DNN hybrid models currently achieve state-of-the-art results, their reliance on DNN is limited to modeling posterior state probabilities. CTC addresses the data alignment issue by allowing DNNs to model time-domain features effectively, thus enhancing their utility in LVCSR tasks. Additionally, CTC simplifies the output process by directly producing target transcriptions, eliminating the need for intermediary phoneme representations and streamlining the overall model training and construction.
