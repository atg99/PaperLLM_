The paper discusses the increasing interest in end-to-end speech recognition models that utilize attention mechanisms. These models consist of three main components: encoder, aligner, and decoder, with the aligner employing the attention mechanism. The encoder serves as the acoustic model and faces similar challenges as CTC-based, RNN-Transducer, and HMM-DNN hybrid models, but also introduces new issues when combined with attention.

One significant challenge is latency. The attention mechanism requires the entire encoding sequence to be completed before it can operate, leading to increased delays compared to models like CTC and RNN-Transducer that do not have this requirement. Additionally, the encoder's output sequence, which is often longer than the target label sequence, introduces information redundancy. This redundancy occurs because the speech input sequence is typically much longer than its transcription, resulting in excessive attention calculations and unnecessary information for the attention mechanism.

The paper highlights that early works in this area did not adequately address these sequence length issues. However, a notable work identified the problem and proposed a solution by implementing pooling between RNN layers to reduce the input sequence length. This approach involved decrementing the time steps between layers, effectively reducing the sequence length by a factor of four through methods like adjacent summation or subsampling. The model utilized a four-layer bidirectional gated recurrent unit (GRU) for this purpose.
