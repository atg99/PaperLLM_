This section of the paper presents a comprehensive list of references related to advances in speech recognition technologies, particularly focusing on methods involving neural networks and statistical approaches. Key contributions include:

1. Nagata et al. (1964) introduced a spoken digit recognizer for the Japanese language, marking an early exploration in speech technology.
2. Itakura (1970) developed a statistical method for estimating speech spectral density, which is foundational for analyzing speech signals.
3. Vintsyuk (1968) proposed dynamic programming for speech discrimination, contributing to algorithmic efficiency in recognition tasks.
4. Atal and Hanauer (1971) advanced speech analysis and synthesis through linear prediction, enhancing the understanding of speech wave patterns.
5. Lee (1988) focused on large-vocabulary speaker-independent continuous speech recognition, addressing scalability in recognition systems.
6. Recent works by Dahl et al. (2011) and Rao et al. (2017) explore deep learning architectures, emphasizing context-dependent modeling and RNN-transducer frameworks for streaming speech recognition.
7. The use of recurrent neural networks (RNNs) and convolutional neural networks (CNNs) has been highlighted in various studies, including those by Graves et al. (2014) and Hori et al. (2017), showcasing the evolution toward end-to-end speech recognition systems.

This compilation of references underscores the progression from traditional statistical methods to advanced neural network approaches, illustrating the significant advancements in the field of automatic speech recognition.
