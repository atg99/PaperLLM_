The paper discusses the limitations of HMM-based models in large vocabulary continuous speech recognition (LVCSR) and introduces the advantages of end-to-end models. HMM-based models face challenges such as complex training processes that hinder global optimization and reliance on conditional independence assumptions, which do not reflect the actual conditions of LVCSR. These models use different training methods for various modules, leading to optimization objectives that may not align with the overall performance criteria.

In contrast, end-to-end models, propelled by advancements in deep learning, provide a streamlined approach by directly mapping input audio sequences to word sequences. The end-to-end framework consists of three main components: an encoder that converts audio to feature sequences, an aligner that aligns these features with language, and a decoder that outputs the final identification result. This model consolidates multiple modules into a single deep network, facilitating joint training and allowing for a global optimization goal that is directly related to final evaluation criteria. This integration enhances the model's performance and simplifies the overall architecture, eliminating the need for extensive intermediate processing.
