The paper discusses the Connectionist Temporal Classification (CTC) process, which comprises two main sub-processes: path probability calculation and path aggregation. A significant aspect of CTC is the introduction of a blank label, represented as "-", and the concept of an intermediate path.

In the path probability calculation, an input sequence X of length T is encoded into a feature sequence F. Each feature vector has a dimension that is one greater than the vocabulary size. The CTC applies a softmax operation to convert F into a probability distribution sequence Y, where each element indicates the likelihood of outputting a specific label or the blank label at each time step. The conditional probability distribution of any sequence within the defined vocabulary is computed based on these probabilities.

An important assumption in this calculation is the independence assumption, which states that the output labels are independent of each other. However, the encoder in CTC considers acoustic context from both past and future, meaning it operates as an acoustic model rather than a language model.

The second sub-process, path aggregation, addresses the discrepancy between the length of the input speech sequence and the typically shorter transcription length. It involves mapping multiple paths to a shorter label sequence through two main operations: merging contiguous identical labels and deleting the blank labels. This results in a concise final label sequence that accurately represents the input speech. For instance, paths like "c-aa-t-" and "c-a-tt-" are aggregated to "c-a-t-", which then becomes "cat" after removing the blank labels.
