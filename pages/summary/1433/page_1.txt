This paper provides a comprehensive overview of end-to-end automatic speech recognition (ASR), emphasizing its significance in the field of machine learning, particularly large vocabulary continuous speech recognition. Traditionally, the hidden Markov model (HMM) combined with Gaussian mixed models (GMM) has dominated speech recognition frameworks. However, recent advancements have shown that the HMM-deep neural network (DNN) model and end-to-end models leveraging deep learning techniques outperform the HMM-GMM approach.

The authors highlight the limitations of the HMM-DNN model, which include data forced segmentation alignment and independent hypothesis testing, along with the complexities of multi-module training. In contrast, end-to-end models offer a simplified architecture, allowing for joint training and direct output without the need for forced data alignment, making them a promising direction for future research in speech recognition.

The review discusses the fundamental concepts, advantages, and disadvantages of both HMM-based and end-to-end models. It focuses on three specific end-to-end approaches: connectionist temporal classification (CTC)-based models, recurrent neural network (RNN)-transducers, and attention-based models. The paper provides detailed theoretical and experimental comparisons of these models, outlining their respective strengths and weaknesses, and concludes by suggesting potential future developments in end-to-end ASR.

Overall, the paper underscores the evolution of ASR technologies and the growing importance of end-to-end models in enhancing speech recognition capabilities.
