The paper discusses the process of path aggregation in the context of label sequences and their corresponding probabilities within the framework of Connectionist Temporal Classification (CTC). It illustrates that a single label sequence, such as "cat," can correspond to multiple paths of varying lengths, specifically highlighting paths of length 4 that aggregate to form the label sequence. The paths are represented graphically, demonstrating how they connect through time steps.

The authors introduce the notation B1(L) to denote the set of all paths that correspond to a given label sequence L, and they present an equation to compute the probability of L given an input sequence X. While the probability of individual paths can be calculated, determining the exact paths included in B1(L) poses challenges, making direct computation of p(L|X) impractical. Instead, the forward-backward algorithm is suggested as a more effective method for these calculations.

The paper emphasizes that CTC allows for a soft alignment between input sequences and label sequences, contrasting it with traditional Hidden Markov Models (HMMs) that require explicit alignment. This soft alignment capability simplifies the construction and training of Large Vocabulary Continuous Speech Recognition (LVCSR) models, removing the need for specialized dictionaries and data alignment, thus enabling a more flexible end-to-end model architecture that directly maps audio to text.
