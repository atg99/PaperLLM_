The paper titled "A Convergence of C-DQN in Stochastic Settings" discusses the limitations of C-DQN (Converging Deep Q-Network) in stochastic environments. It highlights that the minimum of the Loss Minimum Square Bellman Error (LMSBE) does not directly correspond to the solution of the Bellman equation in such contexts. This discrepancy arises because LMSBE incorporates a variance term, which means that while the first term reflects the Bellman equation, the added variance can lead to biases in convergence.

The authors argue that both the minimum of LMSBE and the solution to the Bellman equation are stationary points for C-DQN. They demonstrate that when adjusting parameters to minimize either loss function, C-DQN can oscillate between converging points of DQN and RG (Residual Gradient), or settle at a point in between. This behavior suggests that C-DQN attempts to minimize both loss functions simultaneously and will cease to do so if optimizing one leads to an increase in the other.

To illustrate the practical implications, the paper presents a case study using the "wet-chicken benchmark," a stochastic problem where a canoeist must navigate a river with random turbulence. The results from training a neural network on this task show that while C-DQN performs slightly worse than DQN, it outperforms RG significantly. This indicates that C-DQN can effectively handle high levels of stochasticity in tasks, maintaining performance close to that of DQN despite the inherent challenges.
