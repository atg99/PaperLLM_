The paper presents an analysis of C-DQN, a variant of the DQN algorithm, and its performance on the wet-chicken benchmark. The results indicate that while C-DQN may not achieve the optimal solutions like DQN, it demonstrates robustness and satisfactory performance, unlike the RG approach which fails significantly. The study estimates the distance between learned Q functions, revealing that C-DQN initially lags behind DQN but gradually reduces the distance, suggesting that it converges between the solutions of DQN and RG. 

The experimental setup included a neural network with four hidden layers and 128 units per layer, optimized using the Adam algorithm. The training process spanned 2000 epochs with specific adjustments to the learning rate and included normalization of position and rewards. Performance evaluations occurred every five epochs over multiple trials.

Additionally, the paper outlines further experimental results, showing C-DQN's consistent performance across different random seeds in the context of quantum quartic oscillators and its adaptability in Atari 2600 games. The findings suggest that C-DQN also accommodates flexible update periods for the target network, further enhancing its application in challenging gaming environments.
