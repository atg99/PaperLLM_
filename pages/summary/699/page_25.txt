The paper presents an algorithm for estimating the expected frequency of observing a next reward signal through a series of sample episodes. The algorithm consists of several steps, starting with the input of episodes and the computation of a weighted average of rewards. It involves decomposing reward sequences and iterating until all rewards are zero. The output is an estimate of the inverse number of time steps, denoted as f.

Key components of the algorithm include calculating the average reward and using root mean square (RMS) to emphasize episodes with larger estimates. The relationship between the Q function at initial states and the expected reward is established, leading to the estimation of the average reward and its variance. The standard deviation of rewards is computed to normalize the learned Q function, ensuring it adheres to the modified Bellman equation.

The modified Q function is defined to incorporate a normalized reward that adjusts for variance, allowing the agent to learn in a manner aligned with the expected dynamics of the environment. The paper highlights the importance of normalization in reinforcement learning to maintain stability and performance.
