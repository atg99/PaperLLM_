The paper discusses the game Skiing, where players must navigate downhill to reach a goal as quickly as possible, facing penalties for elapsed time and missed gates. The game mechanics include receiving a small minus reward for each time step, with a significant penalty added at the end for failing to pass through gates. The authors highlight the importance of a suitable discount factor, suggesting that it should be at least around 1/500 to facilitate effective learning. They tuned their hyperparameters to achieve a discount factor of 1/5000, which allowed their C-DQN agent to learn successfully and set a new record for the game.

The paper also addresses the fluctuations in training performance, attributing them to noise from the finite learning rate. While a smaller learning rate reduced noise, it led to the agent getting trapped in local optima, resulting in poorer final performance. The authors caution against directly comparing their results with those of Agent57, as their hyperparameter tuning was specifically tailored for this game, unlike Agent57's more generalized approach.

In the related works section, the authors summarize previous research on reinforcement learning (RL) methods, including the slow convergence of RG compared to TD methods, improvements in RG, and various attempts to stabilize DQN learning. They reference various studies that have proposed enhancements to DQN, including temporal consistency losses and methods to stabilize Q-learning, acknowledging that some methods may be computationally intensive.

Finally, the paper provides detailed calculations related to the condition number of their loss function, illustrating the mathematical foundations of their approach and performance metrics, including a noted performance ceiling for the game.
