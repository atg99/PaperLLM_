This paper presents a novel algorithm called C-DQN, designed to address inefficiencies and convergence issues associated with traditional DQN methods in reinforcement learning, particularly when applied to challenging Atari 2600 games. The authors observe that DQN tends to become unstable with high discount factors, which complicates learning long-term rewards. In contrast, C-DQN maintains stability and can function effectively with any discount factor, although the authors recommend a range between 0.99 and 0.9998 for optimal performance.

The study highlights that while larger discount factors can enhance performance, they also require the agent to predict distant rewards, which may be irrelevant and lead to stagnation. The authors propose a heuristic method to evaluate the frequency of reward signals to determine suitable discount factors for different tasks. They also introduce a technique for normalizing the Q values to improve learning efficiency.

Experimental results demonstrate that C-DQN outperforms standard DQN in several difficult Atari games, achieving state-of-the-art performance in Skiing, despite the algorithm's simplicity. The paper concludes by discussing the potential of C-DQN for tuning learning parameters without sacrificing stability and emphasizes the need for further research into its optimization dynamics, especially concerning stochastic transitions and potential integration with other advanced Q-learning strategies.
