The paper discusses the formulation of a loss function in a reinforcement learning context, specifically focusing on the addition of a term \(Q_0^2\) to the loss function \(L\). The modified loss function is expressed as a combination of squared differences between predicted and actual rewards, involving terms for each timestep \(t\).

To analyze the loss function, the authors derive the Hessian matrix of second-order derivatives of \(L\) with respect to the action-value function \(Q_t\). The Hessian has a specific structure, leading to the identification of its eigenvectors and eigenvalues. The eigenvalues are derived from the trigonometric properties of the eigenvectors, and the condition number of the Hessian matrix is calculated, revealing its dependence on the cosine function and the number of timesteps \(N\).

The paper highlights that for large \(N\), the condition number approaches a specific form, indicating the sensitivity of the optimization problem to parameter changes. Additionally, the authors explore the situation when the state transitions from \(s_{N-1}\) to \(s_0\) under a discount factor, leading to a revised loss function. The analysis of second-order derivatives is reiterated, confirming the non-zero derivatives in this scenario.

Overall, the paper provides a detailed mathematical exploration of the loss function's properties and the implications for reinforcement learning optimization.
