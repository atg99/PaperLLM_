The paper introduces a method for analyzing and optimizing reward structures in reinforcement learning, specifically in the context of Atari 2600 games. It defines the return \( R_i(E_k) \) as the cumulative rewards from a given time step, allowing for the calculation of the average number of steps until the next reward, denoted as \( \hat{l}(E_k) \). This approach distinguishes between clustered and evenly spaced rewards, disregarding time reversal symmetry.

To handle rewards of varying magnitudes, the authors suggest decomposing trajectories into sub-trajectories with uniform rewards, applying the average step calculation to each, and then combining the results using a weighted average. The proposed algorithm ensures that episodes with zero returns are excluded from the final computation and emphasizes episodes with frequent reward observations.

Additionally, the paper discusses the selection of a discount factor \( \gamma \), determined by a time horizon hyperparameter \( \tilde{c} \), which was initially set to 15 but later adjusted to between 5 and 15 for improved learning efficiency. The normalization of the Q function is also addressed, focusing on making it independent of the reward magnitude and the hyperparameter \( \tilde{c} \). The authors propose using the mean and standard deviation of the rewards under the assumption that they are independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.), thereby ensuring a consistent normalization approach.

Overall, the paper presents a comprehensive framework for managing rewards in reinforcement learning, with specific methodologies for calculating returns, normalizing Q values, and adapting to various reward structures.
