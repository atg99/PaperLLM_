This paper presents results comparing the stability and performance of C-DQN (Convergent Deep Q-Network) against DQN (Deep Q-Network) in the context of measurement feedback cooling of a quantum quartic oscillator, as well as in various Atari 2600 games. The authors replicate findings from Wang et al. (2020) to demonstrate that C-DQN offers significantly more stable and reproducible outcomes in the quantum cooling task, as illustrated by consistent learning curves and final performances across multiple trials, in contrast to the fluctuating and unstable results of DQN.

Additionally, in experiments involving six Atari 2600 games, the authors observe that C-DQN generally maintains lower loss values compared to DQN. While C-DQN performs comparably in simpler tasks, it excels in more complex tasks requiring precise control, such as Atlantis, and struggles in highly stochastic environments like Fishing Derby, likely due to limitations in accounting for stochastic transitions.

Furthermore, C-DQN's improved convergence properties allow for shorter update periods for the target network. Unlike DQN, which typically requires longer update periods to maintain stability, C-DQN can function effectively with significantly shorter periods, ranging from 20 to 200 iterations, without instability. This flexibility reduces the need for extensive fine-tuning, highlighting the practical advantages of C-DQN for reinforcement learning applications.
