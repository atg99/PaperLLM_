This paper discusses the properties of the Hessian matrix in relation to eigenvectors and eigenvalues, specifically in the context of an even integer N. It presents the eigenvectors as combinations of sine and cosine functions, with corresponding eigenvalues derived from these functions. The periodicity of sine and cosine is leveraged to confirm the eigenvalues and eigenvectors, leading to a limit analysis as N approaches infinity.

In the experimental section focusing on Atari 2600, the authors detail their preprocessing methods, which involve downscaling images to maintain object sharpness and translational invariance. The agent's state is formed by stacking frames from the recent four steps, and actions are executed repeatedly for four frames to accumulate rewards. A gradient descent iteration occurs every four steps, with the agent initially following a random policy to gather data. 

The paper describes the neural network architecture used, which includes specific adjustments such as zero padding. The update period for the target network is set, and various training parameters are established, including the discount factor and reward clipping. 

An important aspect of the training process is the handling of loss functions, where either mean squared error or Huber loss can be utilized. The paper also addresses the gradient computation during target network updates, proposing a method to maintain the effectiveness of gradient descent despite potential undefined gradients when the target network is updated. This ensures that the parameters remain distinct, facilitating a more accurate training process.
