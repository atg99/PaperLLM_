This paper evaluates the learning dynamics of Q-learning and RG learning in reinforcement learning tasks. It begins by initializing the Q-values for all state-action pairs to zero and compares the efficiency of Q-table learning with RG learning. The findings indicate that Q-table learning converges to the optimal solution more effectively than RG learning, which learns at a significantly slower rate. The experiments demonstrate that as the size of the problem increases, the sampling efficiency of the agent decreases, confirming the expected quadratic scaling of RG learning compared to the linear scaling of Q-learning.

The paper highlights a critical issue with RG learning: its tendency to maintain an average prediction, which can hinder performance. The authors illustrate that when the Q-values are updated in RG, changes to the predicted values can be minimal, especially when the average of Q(s,a) is initialized above the optimal Q-values, leading to inefficient learning dynamics. In scenarios where the Q-values are initialized below the optimal values, RG struggles to improve performance consistently, often requiring a non-zero exploration parameter to learn effectively.

The results emphasize that while Q-table learning adapts well across different scenarios, RG learning struggles with specific initialization conditions and state transitions, particularly in tasks involving loops or when the agent's expectations do not align with the actual rewards received. Overall, the paper suggests that Q-learning remains a more robust choice for reinforcement learning tasks compared to RG learning, especially in environments with complex state-action dynamics.
