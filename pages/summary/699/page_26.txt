The paper presents the performance evaluation of C-DQN (Categorical Deep Q-Network) on various Atari 2600 games, comparing its results with human performance and Double DQN. The experiments utilized a normalization technique for rewards, which involved modifying the reward signal and adding a terminal reward to enhance learning stability. The results indicated that while C-DQN performed well on many games, it sometimes became trapped in local optima, particularly in games like Hero. 

The authors noted the challenges of selecting an optimal learning rate due to the varying stochasticity and time horizons of different tasks. They acknowledged that their normalization strategy did not yield satisfactory results in some cases, particularly in games like Breakout, where the reward dynamics differed significantly between random and learned policies. 

In addition, the paper detailed experimental setups for cliff walking tasks, where state-action pairs were stored in a table, and evaluations were conducted on a logarithmic scale of iteration steps. The learning rate was fixed at 0.5, and the authors described the policy dynamics when multiple actions had equivalent Q-values. Overall, the findings suggest that while C-DQN shows promise, further refinement of strategies is necessary to improve performance across diverse scenarios.
