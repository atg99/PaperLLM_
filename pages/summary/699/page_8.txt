The paper presented at ICLR 2022 discusses the performance of three algorithms: C-DQN, DQN, and RG, focusing on their learning capabilities in reinforcement learning tasks, specifically in the games Pong and Space Invaders. The key findings are illustrated through various figures that depict training performance and loss.

C-DQN and DQN both demonstrate the ability to learn effectively from the provided data, while RG struggles to learn despite having a smaller loss, highlighting a significant difference in their practical applicability. The paper emphasizes C-DQN's convergence properties, showing that it performs comparably to DQN in standard tasks.

The authors introduce the concept of learning from incomplete trajectories, where DQN is prone to divergence when transitions are missing. They experimentally validate this by randomly discarding half of the transition data, revealing that C-DQN remains stable and convergent, while DQN shows a tendency to diverge, particularly in more challenging tasks.

Additionally, the paper explores the effects of different data replacement strategies in replay memory. It finds that using a random replacement strategy can benefit C-DQN when the replay memory size is reduced, while DQN's performance suffers. This highlights the importance of data diversity in replay memory and suggests that C-DQN is more robust in various data scenarios, making it potentially more suitable for offline learning and learning from observations compared to DQN. The findings underscore the significance of loss function choices and memory strategies in reinforcement learning algorithms.
