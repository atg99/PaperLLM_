This paper presents the performance comparison between C-DQN and DQN on various Atari 2600 games, emphasizing the impact of the hyperparameter N~ on their effectiveness. The training performance and loss metrics are analyzed across multiple games, revealing that C-DQN generally outperforms DQN, particularly when N~ is small. The paper also highlights the instability of DQN's performance, which is sensitive to the value of N~.

In further experiments on Space Invaders and Hero, the authors demonstrate that varying the update periods of the target network influences performance. Notably, in the game Hero, a local optimum is identified where the learning process can stagnate, indicating potential challenges in advancing beyond certain performance thresholds.

For evaluating the test performance on challenging Atari games, the authors adopt a strategy that focuses on the best-performing agents during training rather than the final model. They save checkpoints throughout training and select the top three agents based on their average performance over nearby episodes. A validation process is then conducted to determine the best agent among these, followed by a rigorous evaluation using additional episodes, employing an epsilon-greedy policy for decision-making during tests. This method aims to mitigate fluctuations in performance due to noise and local optima.
