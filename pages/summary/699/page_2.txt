In this paper, the authors address inherent inefficiencies in gradient-based methods used in reinforcement learning, specifically highlighting issues with the standard Deep Q-Network (DQN) algorithm. They introduce a modified approach called the Convergent DQN (C-DQN) algorithm, which alters the DQN loss function to ensure that the loss does not increase when updating the target network, a condition critical for preventing divergence. This modification leads to a monotonic decrease in loss, thereby ensuring convergence.

The paper is structured as follows: Section 2 provides background on reinforcement learning and Markov decision processes, explaining key concepts such as value functions and Q-learning. Section 3 discusses inefficiencies in traditional methods, illustrated through toy problem examples. Section 4 details the C-DQN algorithm and its convergence properties. Section 5 presents experimental results demonstrating the effectiveness of C-DQN on the Atari 2600 benchmark, indicating its scalability and efficiency in solving complex tasks. Finally, Section 6 concludes the paper and outlines future research directions.

The authors assert that C-DQN is the first convergent reinforcement learning method that successfully achieves strong performance on standard benchmarks, showcasing its potential for addressing challenging real-world problems.
