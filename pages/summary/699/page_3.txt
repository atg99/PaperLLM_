The paper discusses the limitations and challenges of the Deep Q-Network (DQN) algorithm, particularly regarding its stability and efficiency in learning. DQN employs a deep neural network for estimating Q-values and incorporates a target network to stabilize the learning process. However, the method of updating the target network is criticized for being poorly principled, leading to potential divergence and requiring extensive hyperparameter tuning, especially for new tasks.

To address these issues, the paper introduces the concept of minimizing the mean squared Bellman error (MSBE), originally proposed by Baird, as a more effective learning strategy. The MSBE is defined as the residual between the target value and the estimated Q-value, and minimizing it can lead to convergence of the Q-values to the optimal solution. This approach is straightforward to implement with neural networks and has seen various enhancements in the literature.

However, the authors highlight that minimizing MSBE can lead to inefficient learning due to the ill-conditioning of the loss function in deterministic tabular problems. The Hessian matrix associated with the loss exhibits a condition number that grows quadratically with the number of state-action pairs, making gradient descent optimization ineffective. This inefficiency underscores the need for improved methods to enhance the learning process in reinforcement learning applications, particularly in complex environments like Atari 2600 benchmarks.
