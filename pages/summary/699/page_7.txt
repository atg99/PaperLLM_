This paper presents a novel approach called Convergent DQN (C-DQN) to improve the learning stability of Deep Q-Networks (DQN). The authors analyze the behavior of DQN when it diverges, establishing that the loss function associated with DQN can increase over iterations. They introduce a non-increasing series of losses, defined as LCDQN, which incorporates a modified loss function that combines both the DQN loss and a mean squared Bellman error (LMSBE). The key findings include:

1. Theorem 1 establishes that the minimum of the DQN loss is upper bounded by LMSBE, indicating that as DQN diverges, LMSBE must also diverge.
2. Theorem 2 shows that C-DQN maintains a non-increasing property, ensuring that the iterative process converges, thus providing stability in training.
3. The loss function LCDQN is proposed to replace the traditional DQN loss, allowing for consistent optimization and the convergence property to hold.

The experiments conducted on the Atari 2600 benchmark demonstrate the effectiveness of C-DQN compared to standard DQN and a reference method called RG. The authors report that C-DQN can be further enhanced with existing DQN extensions and alternative loss formulations, such as the Huber loss. Overall, C-DQN is positioned as a robust alternative to traditional DQN, particularly in environments prone to instability during learning.
