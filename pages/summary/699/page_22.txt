The paper discusses the optimization techniques and hyperparameter settings used in training reinforcement learning algorithms, particularly focusing on the implementation of prioritized sampling. The learning rate is set to 6.25 x 10^-5 based on previous work, with specific adjustments made for different algorithms such as DQN, C-DQN, and RG due to observed differences in gradient sizes. Gradient clipping is employed with varying norms across sections.

A modified version of the prioritized sampling scheme from Schaul et al. (2015) is introduced, where the priority of a transition data sample is calculated based on the Bellman error, incorporating a small positive number and a sampling hyperparameter. The paper highlights the importance of correcting bias introduced by prioritized sampling through importance sampling weights, which are adjusted depending on the total data.

The authors argue that using a modified importance sampling weight can lead to excessive dependence on the hyperparameter, which could affect learning stability. They provide evidence indicating that the performance of the learning algorithms is significantly influenced by the choice of this hyperparameter, which may contribute to the challenges in reproducing results in previous studies.

To address this issue, the paper proposes a lower-bounded prioritization method that uses the average of priorities to prevent the priority from becoming negligible, thereby enhancing the robustness of the sampling process across different tasks. This adjustment aims to mitigate the challenges associated with hyperparameter tuning in reinforcement learning.
