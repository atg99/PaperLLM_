The paper presented at ICLR 2022 investigates the efficiency of two reinforcement learning algorithms, Q-learning and a method referred to as RG, through the lens of a cliff walking task. The cliff walking task requires an agent to navigate a grid while avoiding a cliff, with rewards structured to penalize undesirable moves. The study employs Mean Squared Bellman Error (MSBE) as a key metric to evaluate the performance of the Q-table and RG method.

The authors highlight that both algorithms were tested with varying grid widths and learning rates, yielding distinct performance outcomes. Notably, the Hessian matrix associated with the loss function exhibits cyclic properties, leading to eigenvalues that suggest the loss is ill-conditioned. This ill-conditioning results in two critical implications: first, the convergence rate of the RG algorithm is quadratic in relation to the problem size, making it computationally less efficient than Q-learning, which operates linearly. Second, a small MSBE does not reliably indicate proximity to the optimal Q-value, challenging its utility as a performance measure.

The results demonstrate that Q-learning's ability to propagate reward information efficiently allows it to outperform RG in terms of learning time and computational efficiency. The paper concludes that the ill-conditioned nature of the RG algorithm complicates its performance evaluation, indicating that traditional metrics like MSBE may not adequately reflect the algorithm's efficacy in practical scenarios.
