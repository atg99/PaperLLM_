The paper "Convergent and Efficient Deep Q Network Algorithm" by Zhikang T. Wang and Masahito Ueda addresses the limitations of the Deep Q Network (DQN) reinforcement learning algorithm, particularly its tendency to diverge and fail in realistic settings. Despite DQN's empirical success in various applications, including robotics and games, the authors highlight that it does not guarantee convergence due to the instability in its learning dynamics, which can lead to performance issues, especially with deeper neural networks and complex tasks.

The authors demonstrate that while gradient-based convergent methods exist, they face inherent challenges when applied to non-linear deep networks, often resulting in poorer performance compared to traditional temporal difference methods. To tackle these issues, they propose a new algorithm, the Convergent DQN (C-DQN), which is designed to ensure convergence even with large discount factors. C-DQN shows robust learning capabilities in difficult environments and successfully solves several challenging games in the Atari 2600 benchmark that DQN struggles with.

The authors provide empirical evidence of DQN's divergence through experiments and emphasize the necessity for a reliable learning framework in scientific applications. They have publicly released their code, enabling others to replicate their findings. Overall, the paper presents a significant advancement in reinforcement learning by offering a stable and efficient alternative to DQN.
