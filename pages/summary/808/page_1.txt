The paper "Importance Estimation for Neural Network Pruning" by Molchanov et al. presents a novel approach for structural pruning of neural networks, aiming to reduce computation, energy, and memory costs during inference. The authors introduce a method that estimates the contribution of individual neurons (or filters) to the final loss, allowing for the iterative removal of those with lower importance scores. Two variations of this method utilize first and second-order Taylor expansions to approximate a filter's contribution, enabling consistent scaling across any network layer without the need for per-layer sensitivity analysis.

The experimental results demonstrate a high correlation (over 93%) between the contribution scores calculated by their methods and actual importance estimates, leading to improved accuracy, FLOPs, and parameter reduction compared to state-of-the-art techniques. Specifically, on the ResNet-101 architecture, the authors achieved a 40% reduction in FLOPs by pruning 30% of the parameters, with only a minimal loss of 0.02% in top-1 accuracy on the ImageNet dataset.

The paper critiques traditional weight-based pruning methods, which often rely on the magnitude of weights as a proxy for importance, highlighting a significant gap between these decisions and empirically optimal results. The proposed method focuses on a new criterion based on the squared change in loss resulting from the removal of a filter, leveraging readily available parameter gradients during training for efficient computation.

In conclusion, the authors assert that their pruning method not only enhances network efficiency but is also straightforward to implement within existing frameworks, contributing to the development of more compact neural networks suitable for deployment on resource-constrained devices.
