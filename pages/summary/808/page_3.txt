This paper presents a method for pruning neural networks that aims to outperform existing techniques while minimizing computational overhead. Unlike traditional pruning methods that rely on sensitivity analysis to determine the appropriate pruning ratio for various layers, the proposed approach establishes a globally-comparable scaling criterion that can be applied to any layer, including those with skip connections, without requiring sensitivity analysis.

The authors critique existing methods, such as those by Molchanov et al., which assume equal layer importance and compute criteria that increase memory usage due to reliance on network activations. They note that while some prior works have successfully integrated pruning as a training regularizer, their focus remains on filter removal rather than extending the approach to regularization.

The methodology section outlines the objective of minimizing prediction error while incorporating a sparsification term in the cost function to reduce model size. The challenge of minimizing the ℓ0 norm due to its NP-hard nature is addressed through an incremental approach that removes parameters based on their importance, defined by the induced error from their removal. 

To efficiently estimate the importance of parameters without evaluating multiple network versions, the authors utilize a first-order Taylor expansion of the induced error. This leads to a simplified computation of importance, leveraging gradients from backpropagation. Additionally, the paper discusses methods for approximating the joint importance of sets of parameters, such as convolutional filters, through group contributions or summing individual parameter importances.

The introduction of gating layers simplifies the importance score computation by separating them from optimization and providing a constant value, thus streamlining the evaluation process. Overall, the proposed method promises an effective and efficient way to prune neural networks while maintaining performance.
