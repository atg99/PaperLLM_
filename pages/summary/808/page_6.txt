The paper investigates different criteria for pruning neurons in a ResNet-18 model trained on the CIFAR-10 dataset. It evaluates the Spearman correlation between various pruning methods and an oracle, which serves as the benchmark for effective pruning. The methods assessed include applying criteria directly to convolutional filter weights, using gates before and after batch normalization (BN2), and employing both Taylor first-order (FO) and second-order (SO) approximations.

Key findings include that placing gates after BN2 significantly enhances correlation with the oracle, particularly for the Taylor FO and SO methods. In contrast, using gates before BN2 leads to a notable drop in correlation values, indicating the influence of batch normalization on the pruning criteria. The study also reveals that while the Taylor SO method yields the highest correlation, the Taylor FO remains a viable alternative due to its computational efficiency.

The results emphasize the necessity of recalibrating pruning criteria after each iterative step. Fixed criteria methods performed poorly compared to dynamic approaches, highlighting the importance of adapting to changes in the network architecture during pruning. The OBD method, despite showing good correlation in preliminary assessments, underperformed in practice due to the negative impact of certain Hessian elements on network stability when not fine-tuned.

Overall, the paper concludes that the proposed Taylor-based pruning criteria exhibit strong correlation with oracle rankings, and both first- and second-order methods are effective, with Taylor FO being preferred for larger networks due to its lower computational demands. Further research will focus on applying these findings to larger datasets like ImageNet.
