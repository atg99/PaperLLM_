In this section, the authors investigate network pruning on the ImageNet dataset, specifically the ILSVRC2012 version, using PyTorch. The methodology begins with standard preprocessing techniques, including image resizing and normalization, followed by testing on central crops.

The authors conduct a neuron importance correlation study, comparing their pruning method against various heuristics like weight magnitude, batch-normalization (BN) scale, and output-based techniques. They assess the correlation between the estimated "real importance" of filters and these heuristics, which is determined by measuring the change in loss when individual filters are removed. Results show that their method achieves over 93% Spearman correlation across networks like ResNet-101, VGG11-BN, and DenseNet-201, indicating a strong predictive capability for neuron importance. In contrast, weight magnitude and BN scale exhibit low correlations, suggesting their ineffectiveness as importance measures.

Additionally, the study reveals that incorporating skip connections into the analysis further enhances correlation scores. The authors adopt this approach in their pruning strategy, termed Taylor-FO-BN, which simultaneously prunes channels from skip connections and bottleneck layers.

For pruning and fine-tuning, the authors outline their training settings, including GPU usage, batch size, and learning rate adjustments. They detail a process of iterative neuron removal until reaching a target pruning level, followed by fine-tuning to optimize model performance. Results from their pruning method are compared with other techniques, demonstrating that their approach yields lower error rates and reduced computational complexity (GFLOPs) for ResNet-101, with only a slight accuracy drop (0.02%) after significant pruning. Overall, the findings highlight the effectiveness of their pruning methodology in maintaining performance while reducing network size and complexity.
