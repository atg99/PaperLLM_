This paper presents a novel method for neuron pruning in neural networks, emphasizing efficiency and effectiveness. The main contributions of the study include:

1. **Neuron Contribution Estimation**: The proposed method estimates the contribution of neurons to the final loss using averaged gradients and weight values available during training, introducing minimal computational overhead.

2. **Comparison of Method Variants**: Two variants of the method, based on first and second-order Taylor expansions, are compared to a greedy search approach. The first-order variant is significantly faster and achieves comparable accuracy, while using squared loss for contribution measurement enhances correlation with the oracle method, yielding over 93% Spearman correlation for networks like ResNets and DenseNets trained on ImageNet.

3. **Pruning Performance**: The method demonstrates improved pruning results across various networks, including those with skip connections, outperforming existing state-of-the-art techniques on datasets like CIFAR-10 and ImageNet.

The paper also discusses related work in neural network optimization. It contrasts network distillation, which requires predefined architectures for smaller models, with pruning strategies that remove neurons with minimal output contribution. It highlights the limitations of traditional ℓ0 minimization in finding optimal pruning solutions and reviews various approaches that leverage regularization techniques.

Additionally, the paper critiques existing methods that rely on weight magnitude as a measure of neuron importance, citing historical skepticism about this assumption. Instead, it advocates for a more nuanced approach that does not presume all neuron contributions are positive, allowing for a more accurate approximation of loss changes when neurons are removed.

Overall, this work advances the field of neural network pruning by providing an efficient, scalable, and effective method applicable to a variety of network architectures.
