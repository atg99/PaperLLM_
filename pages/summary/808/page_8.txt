This paper presents a novel pruning method called Taylor-FO-BN, which utilizes Taylor expansion to estimate the contribution of neurons in deep neural networks. The method focuses on the squared change in loss resulting from the removal of neurons, showing that even a first-order approximation can effectively assess neuron importance.

The authors conduct a comprehensive evaluation of their approach on various architectures, including ResNet-101, ResNet-50, ResNet-34, VGG11-BN, and DenseNet-201. Results indicate that their pruning method leads to significant improvements in model performance compared to existing techniques. For instance, pruning ResNet-101 with Taylor-FO-BN-50% achieves a top-1 error of 24.62%, outperforming other methods and demonstrating reduced parameter counts and GFLOPs.

The analysis reveals that after pruning, neuron importance becomes more evenly distributed, and the method effectively handles skip connections, which are critical in architectures like ResNet. The study also highlights the benefits of iterative pruning over single-step neuron removal, as re-evaluating neuron importance between iterations leads to better accuracy retention.

In conclusion, the proposed Taylor-FO-BN method not only enhances pruning efficiency but also maintains or improves accuracy across various deep learning models, demonstrating its effectiveness under practical computational constraints.
