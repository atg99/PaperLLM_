In this study, we evaluate pruning methods on the CIFAR-10 dataset, focusing on smaller networks like LeNet3 and ResNet-18. The results reveal key insights into the effectiveness of various pruning strategies.

For LeNet3, which achieves a test accuracy of 73%, we first explore single layer pruning. We compare the Combinatorial oracle, which computes the optimal neuron removal through exhaustive evaluation, with a Greedy oracle that finds the best single neuron to prune at each step. Although the Combinatorial oracle shows slightly better performance, its exponential computational complexity makes it impractical for larger networks. Therefore, the Greedy oracle is adopted for further experiments, demonstrating a close approximation to the Combinatorial oracle for small pruning tasks.

When all layers of LeNet3 are pruned, we assess different criteria, including first-order and second-order Taylor expansion methods. Both Taylor methods perform similarly and significantly better than weight-based pruning, which shows poor results akin to random removal. The findings suggest that the Greedy oracle is computationally efficient while closely matching the performance of the Combinatorial oracle in small-scale pruning.

Next, we extend our analysis to ResNet-18, a more complex architecture that achieves a test accuracy of 94.79% on CIFAR-10. Although the specifics of the results for ResNet-18 are not detailed in the excerpt, the study indicates a systematic approach to comparing pruning criteria across different network architectures.

Overall, the experiments validate that the Greedy oracle serves as a practical and effective pruning strategy while highlighting the comparable performance of first and second-order methods in maintaining network accuracy post-pruning.
