The proposed method involves the optimization of channel-wise differentiable discrete gates that allow specific channels to be activated or deactivated during training. This approach effectively navigates the challenges associated with directly optimizing discrete variables, which are typically non-smooth and NP-hard. By employing a straight-through estimator, the method ensures that the identified sub-network accurately reflects the true sub-network, thus maintaining the model's discriminative capabilities.

Additionally, the method incorporates a stochastic version of the discrete gate to fully explore potential sub-networks, which enhances its robustness. Unlike prior methods that often require multiple hyper-parameters for each layer, this approach simplifies the process by using a single hyper-parameter to manage the computational budget in terms of floating-point operations (FLOPs). The globally discrimination-aware design of the method further contributes to its effectiveness.

Experimental evaluations on CIFAR-10 and ImageNet datasets demonstrate that the proposed method competes favorably with existing state-of-the-art techniques in terms of performance and efficiency. Overall, this research contributes to the ongoing efforts to make CNNs more suitable for deployment on mobile and resource-limited platforms by effectively reducing computational costs while preserving model accuracy.
