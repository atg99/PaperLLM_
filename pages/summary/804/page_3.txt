The paper discusses a novel method for channel pruning in neural networks, focusing on the use of discrete-valued gates to manage the activation of channels. The proposed approach incorporates a differentiable discrete gate mechanism that allows for the optimization of these gates using the straight-through estimator (STE) for backpropagation, enabling gradient calculations despite the binary nature of the gates.

Key concepts include the definition of the feature map dimensions and the implementation of a stochastic discrete gate that introduces randomness in the selection of channels. This randomness ensures that channels with lower activation probabilities still have the potential to be reactivated, addressing the limitation of deterministic pruning where certain channels may become permanently inactive.

The paper formulates the pruning objective as a constrained optimization problem, where the goal is to minimize a loss function while adhering to a predefined pruning rate. The method emphasizes that channel pruning is determined by the discriminative power of each channel independently of its magnitude, allowing for a more effective reduction in model size without sacrificing performance.

Overall, the proposed method enhances model compression by introducing a flexible and probabilistic approach to channel selection, which is validated through experiments and comparisons with existing pruning techniques.
