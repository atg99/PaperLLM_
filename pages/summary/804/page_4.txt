The paper discusses a novel approach to model pruning by focusing on a discrete gate mechanism where a global hyper-parameter controls the pruning rate. During the training of parameters for the gate function, model parameters remain fixed. This discrete approach allows for a more precise reflection of the impact of pruned channels in the loss function compared to continuous gate functions, which complicate the optimization process.

To simplify the optimization problem, the authors replace the equality constraint with a regularization term and redefine the objective function. They introduce a regularization function that can be a regression loss, although they propose a specific logarithmic regularization loss to maintain its value near zero during gate training. This helps the algorithm explore the best sub-architecture effectively.

Additionally, the paper highlights the growing interest in reducing floating-point operations (FLOPs) in model pruning. The authors derive a method to transform the pruning rate constraint into a FLOPs constraint, allowing the model to be pruned to any level of FLOPs by incorporating a FLOPs regularization term in their optimization.

The authors also propose a symmetric weight decay based on the subgradient of their regularization loss, which varies for each layer and training iteration. This approach aims to enhance the stochastic behavior of the gates, thereby expanding the search space for optimal network configurations.
