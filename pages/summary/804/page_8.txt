The paper presents a novel discrete model compression method aimed at pruning Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) under specific resource constraints. The approach utilizes both deterministic and stochastic discrete gates, allowing for a broader exploration of sub-network configurations. The experiments, particularly on CIFAR-10 using ResNet-56, illustrate the training process of these gates, revealing two distinct stages. Initially, the test accuracy of the sub-network declines while the regularization loss decreases. Subsequently, as the regularization loss approaches zero, test performance improves. 

Figures in the paper display various metrics, including test performance and pruning rates across layers, demonstrating that the method effectively navigates the search space rather than settling on a trivial solution. The evolution of gate randomness is also analyzed, showing a transition from high stochasticity to increased determinism as training progresses. This shift indicates that the model becomes more confident in its sub-network selections. 

The conclusion emphasizes the effectiveness of the proposed method, which leverages symmetric weight decay on gate parameters to enhance regularization and achieve superior performance compared to existing state-of-the-art techniques on datasets like ImageNet and CIFAR-10.
