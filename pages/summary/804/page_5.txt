The paper presents a discrete model compression (DMC) algorithm aimed at optimizing neural network architectures by reducing their computational complexity while maintaining performance. The key steps in the algorithm involve training gate parameters on a given dataset (Dgate) while keeping the model weights and batch normalization parameters frozen. The process includes a forward calculation to minimize a loss function that combines model predictions with a regularization term, followed by gradient computation and updates using an ADAM optimizer.

The DMC algorithm employs symmetric weight decay to control the gate parameters, promoting them towards deterministic values (0 or 1), which effectively enlarges the search space for potential configurations. This approach allows for the pruning of pre-trained models without altering their architecture, as both stochastic and deterministic gate calculations share the same parameters.

The experiments validate the effectiveness of the DMC method on datasets like CIFAR-10 and ImageNet, comparing its performance on various models, including ResNet and MobileNetV2. The methodology requires careful tuning of hyperparameters such as the remaining rate of FLOPs and regularization strength. The results indicate that DMC can effectively prune complex models while preserving their accuracy, demonstrating its applicability in resource-constrained environments. The implementation details, including training epochs, learning rates, and optimizer settings, are clearly outlined to facilitate reproducibility.
