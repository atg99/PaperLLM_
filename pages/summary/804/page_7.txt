The paper presents a comparison of various pruning methods applied to different architectures on the ImageNet dataset, specifically focusing on ResNet-34, ResNet-50, ResNet-101, and MobileNetV2. Key metrics evaluated include Top-1 and Top-5 accuracy before and after pruning, as well as the percentage of pruned FLOPs.

The proposed method, referred to as DMC, demonstrates competitive performance across all tested architectures. For ResNet-50, DMC achieves the best changes in accuracy (∆-Acc) compared to other methods, with a slight edge over the state-of-the-art CCP method in Top-1 accuracy while pruning a comparable amount of FLOPs. Notably, DMC prunes 56% of FLOPs in ResNet-101, resulting in a model with reduced FLOPs that still maintains a slight increase in accuracy.

When applied to MobileNetV2, DMC surpasses the results of DCP, showcasing significant improvements in accuracy despite similar levels of FLOP pruning. The paper highlights the advantages of global discrimination-aware pruning employed in DMC over local methods like DCP.

Additionally, the impact of gate placement on model performance is investigated. The results indicate that optimized gate placement leads to measurable improvements in model accuracy, emphasizing the importance of precise sub-network performance estimation.

Overall, the findings suggest that the DMC algorithm effectively balances model compression and accuracy retention, achieving superior results in pruning efficiency across multiple architectures.
