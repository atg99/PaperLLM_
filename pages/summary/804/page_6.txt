The paper presents a comparison of various channel pruning methods on the CIFAR-10 dataset using ResNet-56 and MobileNetV2 architectures, as well as results on ImageNet. The key findings indicate that the proposed method, referred to as DMC, outperforms existing methods in terms of accuracy retention after pruning while maintaining similar or improved computational efficiency.

In the CIFAR-10 results, DMC shows an improvement in accuracy over several established methods, including DCP and Soft Pruning, with a notable increase in performance compared to the baseline models. Specifically, DMC achieves a ∆-Acc of +0.07% for ResNet-56 and +0.26% for MobileNetV2, while efficiently pruning a significant amount of FLOPs. The results suggest that the DMC method effectively utilizes a global discrimination-aware approach, which is superior to the local discrimination-aware methods of some contemporaries.

On the ImageNet dataset, DMC demonstrates its capability to prune a substantial percentage of FLOPs with minimal accuracy drops, outperforming other methods like FPGM in terms of efficiency and performance preservation. For ResNet-34, DMC achieves 43.4% FLOPs pruning with only a small decrease in accuracy.

Overall, the findings underscore the effectiveness of the DMC method in channel pruning, highlighting its advantages in both accuracy retention and computational efficiency across different architectures and datasets.
