The section discusses learning and inference in deep latent variable models (DLVMs), focusing on the role of latent variables. Latent variables, denoted as z, are integral to the model but are unobserved in the dataset. The joint distribution of observed variables x and latent variables z is represented as pθ(x,z), while the marginal distribution over the observed variables is expressed as pθ(x) through integration over the latent variables.

The flexibility of this implicit distribution is highlighted, noting that if z is discrete and pθ(x|z) follows a Gaussian distribution, then pθ(x) results in a mixture-of-Gaussians distribution. In contrast, with continuous z, pθ(x) can represent an infinite mixture, enhancing its modeling capacity. These marginal distributions are also referred to as compound probability distributions.

DLVMs specifically utilize neural networks to parameterize the distributions within the latent variable model. They can be conditioned on additional context, such as pθ(x,z|y). A key advantage of DLVMs is their ability to yield complex marginal distributions pθ(x) even when the individual factors in the model are relatively simple, allowing for intricate dependencies to be captured in the overall model.
