The introduction outlines the framework of variational autoencoders (VAEs), emphasizing their role in jointly learning deep latent-variable models and inference models through stochastic gradient descent. VAEs have diverse applications, including generative modeling, semi-supervised learning, and representation learning. This paper serves as an expanded version of prior work, aiming to provide a detailed exploration of the topic and discuss significant follow-up research, while not attempting to comprehensively review all related studies.

The chapter begins by discussing essential background concepts such as probabilistic models, directed graphical models, and the integration of these models with neural networks. It also covers learning in fully observed models and deep latent-variable models (DLVMs). Subsequent chapters will delve into the fundamentals of VAEs, advanced inference techniques, and sophisticated generative models.

Probabilistic models are crucial in machine learning for understanding, predicting future unknowns, and aiding decision-making processes. They formalize knowledge and skill, capturing the inherent uncertainty in data and model unknowns through conditional probabilities.
