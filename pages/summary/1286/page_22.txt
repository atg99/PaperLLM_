The introduction discusses the challenges associated with differentiating and optimizing models that are not fully observed, specifically in the context of Deep Latent Variable Models (DLVMs). It highlights the relationship between the joint distribution pθ(x,z) and the posterior distribution pθ(z|x), emphasizing that while the former is computationally feasible, both the marginal likelihood pθ(x) and the posterior pθ(z|x) are intractable in DLVMs. The text notes that traditional inference methods can be costly and may lead to poor approximations, underscoring the need for more efficient approximate inference techniques. Additionally, it points out that the posterior over parameters in neural networks is also generally intractable, necessitating the use of these approximate methods.
