The section discusses deep latent-variable models (DLVMs), which are beneficial for approximating complex distributions. A common structure for DLVMs is given by the factorization pθ(x,z) = pθ(z)pθ(x|z), where pθ(z) represents the prior distribution over the latent variable z. An example of a DLVM for multivariate Bernoulli data is illustrated, utilizing a spherical Gaussian latent space and a factorized Bernoulli observation model. Here, the prior distribution is defined as a normal distribution, and the observation model is specified through a decoder neural network, with the log-likelihood expressed in terms of Bernoulli probabilities.

The section also highlights a significant challenge in DLVMs: the intractability of maximum likelihood learning. Specifically, the marginal probability of the data, pθ(x), involves an integral that does not yield an analytic solution or efficient estimator, complicating the computation of marginal likelihood or model evidence. This intractability presents a major obstacle in applying DLVMs effectively.
