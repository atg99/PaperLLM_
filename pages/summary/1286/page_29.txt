The section discusses alternative methods for learning in Deep Latent Variable Models (DLVMs), particularly focusing on the use of maximum likelihood estimation and variational EM (Expectation Maximization) with local variational parameters. 

The prior distribution p(θ) becomes less significant as the dataset size N increases, leading practitioners to often use maximum likelihood by omitting the prior, which is equivalent to treating p(θ) as a constant. Variational EM is introduced as a method for learning parameters in partially observed models, characterized by its iterative updates for local variational parameters φ(i) for each data point. This contrasts with Variational Autoencoders (VAEs), which utilize global variational parameters.

The EM algorithm begins with initial random values for θ and φ and alternates between the E-step and M-step until convergence. The E-step maximizes the expected log-likelihood, effectively minimizing the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence between the variational distribution qφ(z|x) and the true posterior pθ(z|x). The M-step then optimizes a bound on the marginal likelihood, where the quality of this bound is determined by the KL divergence. 

This iterative process, grounded in the principles of variational inference, ensures that the model parameters are learned in a way that approximates the true underlying distribution of the data.
