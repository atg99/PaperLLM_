The introduction discusses the concept of conditional modeling, where a model pθ(y|x) is designed to approximate an unknown underlying distribution p∗(y|x). A common application of this is image classification, where x represents an image and y denotes its class. The challenge increases with high-dimensional outputs, such as images or videos, which complicate the learning process. The paper notes that while it often assumes unconditional modeling for simplicity, the methods presented are applicable to both conditional and unconditional models.

Furthermore, the introduction highlights the use of neural networks as a powerful tool for parameterizing conditional distributions. Neural networks, particularly those with multiple hidden layers, are referred to as deep learning models. They are effective for probabilistic modeling, enabling the approximation of probability density functions (PDFs) and probability mass functions (PMFs). The scalability of these models is attributed to their compatibility with stochastic gradient-based optimization techniques.
