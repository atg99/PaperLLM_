In fully observed models utilizing neural networks, the objective is to maximize the log-probability of the dataset through maximum likelihood (ML) estimation. For an independent and identically distributed dataset of size ND, the goal is to compute the gradients of the log-probability with respect to the model parameters θ. This is typically achieved using batch gradient descent, which, while accurate, can be computationally intensive for large datasets as it scales linearly with ND.

To address this inefficiency, stochastic gradient descent (SGD) is introduced, which operates on randomly selected minibatches of data, denoted as M. This approach provides an unbiased estimator of the ML criterion, allowing for optimization to be done more efficiently. The unbiased stochastic gradients obtained from these minibatches can be utilized in gradient-based optimization algorithms, facilitating iterative improvements towards a local optimum.

Additionally, from a Bayesian viewpoint, maximum a posteriori (MAP) estimation offers a potential enhancement over traditional ML methods, allowing for the incorporation of prior information into the inference process. This approach can yield more robust estimates, especially in scenarios with limited data.
