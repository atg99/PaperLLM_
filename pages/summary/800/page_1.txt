The paper discusses a novel approach to network pruning, termed discrimination-aware channel pruning (DCP), which aims to enhance deep model compression by effectively selecting channels that contribute to the discriminative power of the network. Traditional pruning methods either train models from scratch with sparsity constraints or minimize reconstruction errors between pre-trained and compressed models. However, these methods face challenges such as high computational costs and a lack of focus on the discriminative ability of channels.

The authors propose integrating additional discrimination-aware losses into the network to strengthen the discriminative power of intermediate layers. This allows for the simultaneous selection of the most discriminative channels based on both the discrimination-aware loss and reconstruction error. The pruning process is framed as a sparsity-inducing optimization problem, which is solved using a greedy algorithm.

Additionally, the paper addresses kernel-level redundancy within channels by introducing a method for discrimination-aware kernel pruning (DKP). This further compresses deep networks by eliminating redundant kernels. To enhance efficiency, two adaptive stopping conditions are developed to automatically determine the number of selected channels and kernels, avoiding the need for manual pruning rates.

Extensive experiments validate the proposed methods, showing that a ResNet-50 model with a 30% reduction in channels outperforms the baseline by 0.36% in Top-1 accuracy on ILSVRC-12. The pruned MobileNetV1 and MobileNetV2 models demonstrate significant inference acceleration on mobile devices with minimal performance loss. The source code and pre-trained models are made available online. 

Overall, the paper contributes to the field of model compression by providing a method that effectively balances channel selection with maintaining discriminative capabilities, leading to enhanced performance and efficiency in deep learning applications.
