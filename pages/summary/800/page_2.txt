This paper addresses the limitations of existing channel and kernel pruning methods in deep neural networks (DNNs) by proposing a discrimination-aware approach. Traditional training-from-scratch methods struggle with training deep networks on large datasets due to the complexity of learning channel importance, while reconstruction-based methods face challenges related to the quality of pre-trained models and may retain redundant channels to minimize reconstruction error. 

To overcome these drawbacks, the authors introduce a Discrimination-Aware Channel Pruning (DCP) method that focuses on identifying channels with significant discriminative power. This method incorporates additional discrimination-aware losses into the training process to enhance the discriminative capacity of intermediate layers. Channel selection is then optimized by balancing the discrimination-aware loss with the reconstruction error of feature maps. Furthermore, to address kernel-level redundancy within selected channels, the authors propose a Discrimination-Aware Kernel Pruning (DKP) method.

The main contributions of the paper include:
1. The introduction of DCP/DKP to compress deep models while enhancing discriminative power.
2. Formulation of channel/kernel selection as a constrained optimization problem, solved using a greedy method to identify informative channels and kernels.
3. An adaptive stopping condition to limit the number of selected channels/kernels, ensuring efficient model compression.
4. Extensive experiments demonstrating the effectiveness of the proposed methods, including improvements in accuracy and inference speed when deploying pruned models on mobile devices.

The paper extends prior work by applying DCP to compact models like MobileNet and face recognition models, proposing new training techniques to reduce computational overhead, and conducting additional studies to validate the methods' effectiveness. Overall, the proposed methods show significant advantages in terms of performance and efficiency in deep model compression.
