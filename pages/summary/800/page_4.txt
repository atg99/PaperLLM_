The paper presents a method for discriminative channel pruning in convolutional neural networks (CNNs), focusing on retaining channels that enhance the network's discriminative power. The authors recognize the challenge of measuring the discriminative power of individual channels, especially in deep networks where shallow layers contribute less to the final output. To address this, they propose inserting additional discrimination-aware losses into the intermediate layers of the network. 

The method begins by defining a discrimination-aware loss that utilizes average pooled feature maps from specific layers, combined with batch normalization and ReLU activation to facilitate computation. The loss function is structured to enhance the discriminative capacity of the feature maps. The reconstruction error between the feature maps of a pre-trained model and the pruned model is minimized to ensure the essential information is preserved.

The optimization problem is formulated as a joint loss function that combines the discrimination-aware loss and reconstruction error, ensuring convexity with respect to model parameters. The pruning process involves a sparsity constraint on the channel weights, allowing for flexible selection of channels based on either a predefined rate or adaptive stopping conditions.

The proposed Discrimination-aware Channel Pruning (DCP) method is implemented in a multi-stage process, where additional losses are integrated into the model, parameters are learned and fine-tuned, and channel selection occurs iteratively. This approach effectively enhances the discriminative power of intermediate layers while performing channel pruning to create a more efficient model.
