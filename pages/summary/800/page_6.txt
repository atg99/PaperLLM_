The proposed Discrimination-Aware Channel Pruning (DCP) method enhances channel pruning efficiency by fine-tuning the network only once, allowing models with the same architecture but different pruning rates to skip repeated fine-tuning, thus reducing computational costs significantly. Additionally, the method employs feature reusing to avoid redundant calculations of input feature maps during channel selection.

DCP addresses the limitation of traditional channel pruning methods that treat all kernels in a channel as equally important, potentially degrading model performance. To mitigate this, Discrimination-Aware Kernel Pruning (DKP) is introduced to selectively prune kernels based on their contribution to the network's discriminative power. DKP incorporates additional losses to improve the discriminative capability of intermediate layers during the fine-tuning process, followed by a greedy kernel selection strategy.

Experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of DCP across various architectures, including ResNet and MobileNet, for tasks such as image classification and face recognition. The results are benchmarked against state-of-the-art pruning methods, showcasing DCP’s competitive performance in reducing computational costs while maintaining accuracy. The empirical evaluations reveal that DKP can be applied either independently or in conjunction with DCP, with similar training costs observed for both methods.
