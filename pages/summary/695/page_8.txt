The paper discusses the architecture and training process of a Deep Q-Network (DQN) agent designed for decision-making in a specific environment. It introduces two neural networks: the evaluation DNN, denoted as Q(s,a,q), which is responsible for estimating action values, and the target DNN, Q(s,a,q-), which stabilizes training by providing target values for updates. The target DNN is updated periodically with the weights from the evaluation DNN.

The experience dataset, D, is crucial for training, as it stores tuples of experiences that include the current state, action taken, reward received, and the subsequent state. The DQN agent utilizes an epsilon-greedy policy to balance exploration and exploitation during action selection. Initially, actions are chosen randomly to populate the experience dataset.

During training, the weights of the evaluation DNN are updated through a mini-batch gradient descent approach. The mean squared error (MSE) loss is computed based on experiences sampled from the dataset, and the weights are adjusted accordingly using a learning rate. After a specified number of updates, the target DNN's weights are synchronized with those of the evaluation DNN.

The training process is encapsulated in an algorithm that outlines the steps for data collection and weight updates. The overall training duration is determined by the MaxNumberOfTrainingSteps parameter, ensuring a structured approach to learning optimal actions for the DQN agent.
