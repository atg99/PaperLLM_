The paper discusses a multi-connectivity configuration for user equipment (UE) in radio access technologies (RATs), focusing on optimizing bandwidth allocation while meeting quality of service (QoS) requirements. It begins by defining the configuration matrix for the UE, which distributes traffic across multiple RATs and cells. The objective is to determine the optimal configuration that minimizes resource consumption while ensuring that the throughput meets the user's required bit rate.

The problem is formalized by maximizing the total throughput (Tu) while ensuring it exceeds the required bit rate (Ru) and that the total fraction of occupied physical resource blocks (PRBs) does not exceed a predefined threshold (rmax). The allocation of bandwidth is influenced by the UE's mobility, propagation conditions, and interference, which are captured in the bandwidth function g(bm,n).

To implement this multi-connectivity configuration, the paper proposes a system architecture based on the O-RAN Alliance framework, which emphasizes virtualized network elements and standardized interfaces. The O-RAN architecture enables the disaggregation of the 5G NR base station into several functional units, enhancing flexibility and adaptability in managing network resources. This approach aims to optimize the network's resource allocation strategies while adhering to the principles of openness and intelligence in network design.
