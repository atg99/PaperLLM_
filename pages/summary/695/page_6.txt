The paper discusses a DQN-based solution for addressing the multi-connectivity problem in radio access networks (RAN). The MAC scheduler, while relevant, is not the focus of this work. Instead, the authors highlight the complexity of the problem, which involves various factors such as propagation and interference conditions, existing load in cells, and QoS requirements. Given this complexity, deep reinforcement learning (DRL) techniques, specifically the DQN algorithm, are proposed as effective solutions. DQN is chosen for its model-free, value-based approach and its applicability to discrete action spaces, having been successfully utilized in similar contexts like capacity sharing and resource allocation in heterogeneous networks.

The paper outlines a dynamic learning process conducted by the DQN agent at the multi-connectivity (MC) controller. The agent makes decisions regarding MC configurations for different user equipment (UEs) at discrete time intervals. At each time step, the DQN selects an action based on the current state and a decision-making policy. A reward signal is provided at the end of each time window, which evaluates the performance of the chosen action based on optimization criteria, allowing the DQN to refine its decision-making over time.

The state vector for each UE comprises several components, including the UE's requirements, spectral efficiency per RAT/cell, current configuration, and fractions of occupied bandwidth resources. The action space consists of all possible MC configurations, represented as a matrix that specifies the configuration to be applied in the next time window. This structured approach aims to enhance the efficiency and effectiveness of resource allocation in multi-connectivity scenarios within RANs.
