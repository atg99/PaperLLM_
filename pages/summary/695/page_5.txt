This paper discusses an architecture based on O-RAN that incorporates LTE technology through O-eNB nodes, detailing the roles of various components in the radio interface protocol stack. The O-CU manages higher layers, including the PDCP layer for traffic splitting in multi-connectivity scenarios, while the O-DU handles lower layers such as RLC and MAC. The O-RU is responsible for the physical layer and RF functions.

To tackle the complexities of multi-connectivity (MC) configurations, the paper introduces a Deep Q-Network (DQN) based solution. It acknowledges the numerous variables involved, such as propagation conditions, load in cells, and QoS requirements, which complicate the task for the MC controller. As the controller lacks a precise model for resource allocation, the DQN algorithm is employed due to its effectiveness in similar radio access network (RAN) problems, including capacity sharing and resource allocation.

The DQN agent, situated at the MC controller, dynamically learns and makes decisions regarding MC configurations for different user equipment (UE). The agent operates in discrete time intervals, selecting actions based on the current state and optimizing its decision-making through a reward signal that reflects the effectiveness of each action. 

The paper also highlights the integration of the MC controller into the near-real-time RAN Intelligent Controller (near-RT RIC) of the O-RAN architecture, which facilitates the processing of input measurements and the configuration of traffic handling at the PDCP layer. This setup enables the effective management of dual connectivity processes, optimizing bandwidth allocation for UEs based on the established configurations.
