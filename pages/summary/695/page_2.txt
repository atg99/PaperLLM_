The paper discusses the concept of Multi-Connectivity (MC) in the context of advancing network technologies towards sixth generation (6G) systems. MC allows User Equipment (UE) to connect simultaneously to multiple nodes within the Radio Access Network (RAN), specifically eNodeBs (LTE) and gNodeBs (5G NR). This architecture enhances capacity, improving data rates and reliability but also introduces challenges in managing traffic effectively across nodes.

The Third Generation Partnership Project (3GPP) specifies MC through the Multi-Radio Dual Connectivity (MR-DC) feature, which accommodates different configurations based on the technology of the master node (MN) and secondary nodes (SN) as well as the core network (either 5G core or Evolved Packet Core). MR-DC operates using three types of radio bearers: Master Cell Group (MCG) bearers, Secondary Cell Group (SCG) bearers, and Split bearers, which facilitate various data transmission methods.

The interest in MC is underscored by ongoing 3GPP Release 17 studies that explore MR-DC with multiple frequency bands. Recent research has also examined MC applications in future networks, including communication over mmWave and THz bands, multi-connectivity involving relay UEs, and hybrid satellite-terrestrial networks.

Algorithmic challenges related to MC include resource allocation, cell and Radio Access Technology (RAT) selection, and traffic splitting. Various strategies and algorithms have been proposed to optimize these aspects, such as the Smart Aggregated RAT Access (SARA) strategy for joint RAT selection and resource allocation, heuristic approaches for resource allocation in 5G/LTE scenarios, and methods for secondary base station selection aimed at enhancing performance and energy efficiency.

Overall, the paper highlights the significance of MC in evolving network architectures and the ongoing research efforts to address the associated technical challenges.
