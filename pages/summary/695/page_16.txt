The paper evaluates the performance of a Deep Q-Network (DQN)-based strategy in comparison to a Signal-to-Interference-plus-Noise Ratio (SINR)-based strategy for optimizing user equipment (UE) connectivity in fixed positions. The study demonstrates that the DQN strategy significantly outperforms the SINR-based strategy, achieving up to a 65% higher reward in certain fixed positions and a 166% increase in average throughput, from 15.3 Mb/s to 40.72 Mb/s, over a 20-second analysis period.

Specifically, the analysis is centered on a fixed position where the UE experienced SINR values of 45.7 dB for the 5G NR cell and 33.8 dB for the LTE cell. While the SINR-based strategy consistently selected the 5G NR cell, the DQN strategy intelligently split traffic between both cells by considering the current cell loads. This approach mitigates the risk of overloading the higher-SINR cell, thereby enhancing throughput.

The results indicate that the DQN-based approach has a minimal average error of 3.76% relative to the optimum configuration, with a 5th percentile error of 0.08% and a 95th percentile error of 13.3%. The findings underscore the importance of optimizing multi-cell configurations for UEs to improve overall network performance.
