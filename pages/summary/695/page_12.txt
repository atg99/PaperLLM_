this study, the authors present a Deep Q-Network (DQN)-based method for managing multi-cell (MC) configurations for user equipment (UE) in a mobile network. The training process is performed until reaching a maximum of 106 training steps, with policies evaluated every 2500 steps. The results indicate that the average reward obtained by the trained policy increases significantly during training, stabilizing around 40 x 10^4 training steps, reflecting the algorithm's stability.

To benchmark the performance of the DQN-based strategy, two reference approaches are considered: an optimum strategy, which exhaustively searches all possible MC configurations to select the highest reward option, and a Signal to Interference plus Noise Ratio (SINR)-based strategy, where the UE connects to the cell offering the highest SINR. The optimum strategy serves as a theoretical upper bound for comparison due to its impractical implementation time.

The performance evaluation compares the DQN-based strategy against the two reference strategies across 100 different trajectories. The results show that the DQN-based method achieves performance very close to the optimum strategy, with differences of less than 1% in some cases, demonstrating its effectiveness in real-world applications. This research highlights the potential of DQN algorithms in optimizing resource allocation in mobile networks.
