The paper discusses a reinforcement learning framework for optimizing resource allocation in a cellular network environment, focusing on a user equipment (UE) connected to multiple cells. The action space for the optimization allows for a maximum of Nmax non-zero bandwidth allocations. The reward function measures the effectiveness of the action taken concerning the defined optimization targets and constraints.

The reward, r(t+1), is composed of three multiplicative components: the total bandwidth assigned to the UE, a penalty for failing to meet the minimum throughput requirement, and a penalty for overloading any cell from which the UE is receiving service. These components work together to guide the optimization process, ensuring that bandwidth is allocated efficiently while adhering to performance constraints.

The training process involves a Deep Q-Network (DQN) agent that learns a decision-making policy through reinforcement learning. The objective is to maximize the expected cumulative future reward by approximating the optimal action-value function Q*(s,a) using a deep neural network. This function quantifies the expected reward for taking a particular action in a given state, and the optimal policy is derived by selecting actions that maximize this value.

The DQN agent updates its approximation of Q*(s,a) iteratively, refining its decision-making capabilities based on the feedback received from the environment. The architecture includes an evaluation DNN that plays a crucial role in determining the actions to be taken, thereby facilitating the learning process in dynamic network conditions.
