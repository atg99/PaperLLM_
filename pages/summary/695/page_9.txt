The paper presents a Deep Q-Network (DQN) training algorithm for user equipment (UE) in a wireless communication scenario, specifically focusing on optimizing the performance of mobile communication networks. The training algorithm involves initializing a deep neural network (DNN) counter, collecting state information, generating random actions initially, and subsequently selecting actions based on learned policies. The algorithm includes mechanisms for storing experiences, sampling from a memory pool, and updating the DNN weights through gradient descent.

The evaluation of the proposed solution is conducted through system-level simulations, which are detailed across several sections. The scenario consists of a 500 m x 500 m area featuring four 5G New Radio (NR) cells and two Long-Term Evolution (LTE) cells. These cells are positioned to support a traffic hotspot, with 5G microcells deployed for dense coverage while LTE macrocells provide extended coverage.

The traffic distribution is non-homogeneous, with certain UEs capable of multi-connectivity (MC) moving at a speed of 1 m/s and requiring a bit rate of 50 Mb/s. In addition, background traffic is generated through Poisson session arrivals, leading to a variable number of background UEs, with an average of 72 present during simulations. The background UEs are distributed across the scenario, with a significant portion located within the hotspot.

Overall, the paper outlines the methodology for training a DQN to optimize UE performance in a specific wireless network scenario, detailing the setup, parameters, and expected outcomes of the simulations conducted.
