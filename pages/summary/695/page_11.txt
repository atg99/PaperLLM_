The paper details the configuration parameters for the Deep Q-Network (DQN) algorithm used in a training scenario involving a multi-connectivity (MC) capable user equipment (UE). Key parameters include an initial collection of 5000 steps, a maximum of 1 million training steps, an experience replay buffer of 100,000, a mini-batch size of 256, and a discount factor of 0.9. The DNN architecture consists of an input layer with 17 nodes, two hidden layers with 100 and 50 nodes, and an output layer with 58 nodes. 

The training process involves the UE moving according to a random walk model while maintaining a required bit rate of 50 Mb/s, with background UEs generating additional traffic. The DQN agent updates its decision-making policy based on rewards received during this process. Training continues until reaching the maximum number of training steps, with the policy evaluated every 2500 steps along a specified trajectory.

Results indicate that the average reward initially rises significantly, reflecting the algorithm's ability to learn improved policies. By approximately 400,000 training steps, the average reward stabilizes, suggesting that this is an adequate number of steps for effective policy learning, with subsequent fluctuations around 2%, indicating the algorithm's stability.
