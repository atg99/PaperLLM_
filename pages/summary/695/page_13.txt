The paper discusses the performance evaluation of a Deep Q-Network (DQN)-based Multi-Connectivity (MC) strategy for user equipment (UE) in 4G/5G networks. The DQN approach is compared against traditional strategies, specifically an optimum strategy and a Signal-to-Interference-plus-Noise Ratio (SINR)-based strategy. 

Results indicate that the DQN-based approach outperforms the SINR-based strategy by approximately 13.1% on average, with improvements of up to 50% in specific trajectories. This advantage stems from the DQN strategy's ability to intelligently distribute traffic across multiple cells, thereby preventing overload and enhancing bit rates. 

From an implementation standpoint, the DQN strategy necessitates the support of the MC feature and interconnection of base stations via the Xn interface for 5G NR or the X2 interface for LTE, which are standard in current networks. Performance metrics show that the DQN strategy closely approximates the optimum strategy, with an average error of 4.32% across all trajectories. 

Throughput analysis reveals that the DQN approach consistently yields higher throughput compared to the SINR-based strategy, achieving 90% of the time a throughput of at least 37 Mb/s, as opposed to only 18 Mb/s for the SINR approach. Additionally, the DQN strategy delivers a throughput of 50 Mb/s for about 50% of the time, while the SINR approach does so only 35% of the time.

A detailed examination of a specific trajectory further demonstrates the DQN strategy's effectiveness, showing a reward increase from 0.634 (SINR) to 0.835 (DQN), representing a 31% gain. Overall, the findings support the DQN-based MC strategy as a superior alternative to traditional methods in enhancing network performance.
