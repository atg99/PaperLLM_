This paper investigates the application of Deep Q-Network (DQN) for traffic splitting among cells in multi-connectivity scenarios, adjusting based on current traffic and radio conditions. The performance of the DQN-based strategy was evaluated against both an optimal case and a traditional Signal-to-Interference-plus-Noise Ratio (SINR)-based approach across various simulation scenarios with multiple Mobile Communication (MC)-capable User Equipments (UEs).

Five simulations were conducted with UEs ranging from 5 to 25, each generating traffic over 400 seconds while moving along distinct trajectories. Background traffic was simulated to average 24 UEs per session. Results indicated that the DQN-based strategy consistently outperformed the SINR-based approach, with performance gains increasing from 11.2% for 5 UEs to 31.7% for 25 UEs in terms of rewards. Additionally, throughput performance showcased that the DQN method achieved gains of approximately 33% compared to the SINR approach, maintaining throughput close to the required 25 Mb/s.

The DQN agent demonstrated the ability to learn a near-optimal policy, achieving an average reward only 3.9% lower than the optimal strategy. The analysis of fixed positions revealed potential throughput gains of up to 166% over the SINR strategy at certain times, emphasizing the DQN's effectiveness in optimizing resource allocation in multi-connectivity environments. The findings suggest that DQN can significantly enhance performance in traffic management for UEs in diverse operational conditions. Future work will likely explore further optimization techniques and broader deployment scenarios.
