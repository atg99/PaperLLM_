The paper discusses a simulation scenario designed to evaluate the performance of two radio access technologies (RATs), LTE and 5G NR, in a network environment characterized by varying traffic conditions. In this scenario, four 5G NR microcells are deployed in a high-traffic hotspot area, complemented by two LTE macrocells to ensure wider coverage. 

The simulation includes user equipment (UE) that support multi-connectivity (MC) and generates background traffic. UEs supporting MC move at 1 m/s and require a bit rate of 50 Mb/s, while background UEs, which arrive following a Poisson process, generate an average of 0.6 sessions per second with a session duration of 120 seconds. This results in an average of 72 background UEs at any time. Background UEs connect to the cell with the highest Signal to Interference and Noise Ratio (SINR), achieving bit rates of 2.5 Mb/s on LTE and 40 Mb/s on 5G NR.

The study employs a Deep Q-Network (DQN) model developed in Python using the TF-agents library to optimize the connection policy for UEs. The model's parameters were fine-tuned through various tests to ensure effective performance, stability, and convergence. The results presented reflect the performance of the DQN algorithm after one million policy updates.

Key parameters for the cell configurations are outlined in a table, detailing the differences in frequency, bandwidth, power, and path loss models between LTE and 5G NR. The findings highlight the potential of 5G NR to support higher bit rates compared to LTE, particularly in dense urban environments with significant traffic demand.
