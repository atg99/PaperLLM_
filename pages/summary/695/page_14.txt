The paper analyzes the performance of a Deep Q-Network (DQN)-based strategy for traffic splitting between LTE and 5G NR cells compared to a traditional Signal-to-Interference-plus-Noise Ratio (SINR)-based strategy. The DQN approach adapts traffic distribution based on real-time conditions, while the SINR strategy relies on the cell with the highest SINR. 

Key findings indicate that the DQN-based strategy significantly outperforms the SINR-based method, achieving an average reward of 0.62 compared to 0.27 for the SINR approach. This results in higher user equipment (UE) throughput, with 90% of the time achieving at least 37 Mb/s under the DQN method, while the SINR strategy only reaches 18 Mb/s. The DQN approach also maintains a throughput of 50 Mb/s for about 50% of the time, compared to 35% for the SINR strategy.

A detailed trajectory analysis of a specific UE shows a marked increase in average reward from 0.634 with the SINR strategy to 0.835 with the DQN approach, reflecting a 31% improvement. The DQN algorithm dynamically adjusts traffic configurations, such as using LTE and NR cells based on current conditions, thereby optimizing performance even when background UE traffic leads to high load scenarios.

Overall, the DQN-based strategy demonstrates greater adaptability and efficiency in managing traffic loads, leading to superior performance metrics in throughput and rewards.
