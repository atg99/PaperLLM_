The paper presents a comprehensive review of various deep learning models and techniques applied to brain imaging, specifically focusing on brain tumor segmentation and ischemic stroke lesion segmentation. It references significant studies that utilize fully convolutional neural networks (FCNNs), multi-scale 3D convolutional neural networks (CNNs), and other advanced algorithms for enhancing the accuracy of medical image analysis. 

Key contributions include the integration of deep learning with conditional random fields (CRFs) for improved segmentation results, as demonstrated in multiple studies cited, such as Zhao et al. and Kamnitsas et al. The paper emphasizes the evolution of methodologies, including the introduction of voxel-wise residual networks and boundary-aware networks, which have proven effective in delineating complex brain structures.

Moreover, the paper highlights the importance of benchmarks like the BRATS and ISLES challenges in standardizing evaluation metrics and fostering advancements in the field. The references provided illustrate a broad spectrum of approaches, from the foundational works on CNNs and residual learning to specific applications in clinical settings.

Overall, this review underscores the transformative impact of deep learning in medical imaging, particularly for challenging tasks such as tumor and lesion segmentation, while also acknowledging the ongoing need for robust evaluation frameworks and further research to enhance model performance and clinical applicability.
