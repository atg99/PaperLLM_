The paper discusses a novel architecture for dense 3D segmentation, emphasizing the design of convolutional blocks and a 3D refinement module. The architecture consists of four convolutional blocks, each maintaining identical 4D shapes for feature maps, with the number of channels increasing from 32 to 256. Unlike traditional approaches that utilize max pooling for down-sampling, this model employs convolution operations, which are more effective in preserving local details crucial for segmentation tasks.

The model includes two residual blocks in the second, third, and fourth convolutional blocks, totaling 17 3D convolutional layers. This compact design enhances running speed and reduces the amount of training data required while improving generalization compared to larger models.

A critical component of the model is the proposed 3D refinement module, which addresses the loss of fine structures during up-sampling. This module aggregates local details and high-level context information by reshaping the convolutional maps and recursively encoding features from higher to lower layers. It introduces a refine unit that employs both 1x1x1 and 3x3x3 convolutional layers to enhance the 3D features before and after combining them, leading to consistent 128-channel output maps that match the input volume's shape. This innovative approach distinguishes itself from previous methods by avoiding direct element-wise summation, thus improving feature refinement.
