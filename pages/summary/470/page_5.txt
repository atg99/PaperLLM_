The training strategy employs curriculum learning, focal loss, and data augmentation to maximize the potential of the 3D segmentation network, particularly when training data is limited. The curriculum learning approach introduces tasks of increasing complexity, enabling the model to learn effectively from simpler to more challenging data. 

Focal loss plays a crucial role in this strategy by focusing the training on difficult samples while down-weighting easier ones, ensuring that the model learns from a more informative set of training data. Data augmentation further enhances the diversity of training samples by applying various transformations to the 3D volumetric MRIs, such as voxel intensity adjustments, rotations, scalings, and cropping.

The combination of these techniques not only addresses issues associated with dense 3D training but also contributes to a more robust and capable segmentation model. The proposed method demonstrates the importance of both architectural design and innovative training strategies in achieving high performance in 3D medical image segmentation tasks.
