The paper presents a novel approach for brain segmentation using a three-stage curriculum training strategy in a 3D segmentation model known as Brain SegNet. The process begins with training the model on original data without data augmentation and focal loss, which is considered an easier task due to the limited training data predominantly consisting of simple samples. 

Next, data complexity is increased by applying data augmentation to 50% of the original 3D MRIs, generating diverse training samples that enhance the learning task's difficulty. In the final stage, focal loss is introduced to emphasize learning from harder samples, and data augmentation is applied to 75% of the training data, further improving the model's capacity to learn from meaningful samples.

This structured approach enables the model to learn more efficiently from a limited number of training samples, resulting in improved discriminative power and generalization capability, which translates to enhanced performance.

The Brain SegNet distinguishes itself from existing models like 3D U-Net and V-Net by being more compact, with fewer convolutional layers in the decoding branch, resulting in 12 million parameters compared to 19 million and 60 million in the other models. This compact design allows for faster training and inference, with a processing time of approximately 0.5 seconds per MRI, significantly faster than related state-of-the-art methods.

Additionally, the model maintains strong 3D detail by only implementing down-sampling in the spatial domain, preserving critical volumetric information for accurate brain lesion segmentation. The implementation was done using PyTorch, with specific training parameters outlined, including the generation of fixed-size 3D volumes from MRI modalities and the use of multiple GPUs for efficient training.

Overall, the proposed Brain SegNet utilizes a unique training strategy and model design to achieve state-of-the-art performance in brain lesion segmentation while optimizing efficiency and speed.
