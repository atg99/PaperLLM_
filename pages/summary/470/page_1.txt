The paper presents BrainSegNet, a novel 3D residual neural network designed for the automatic segmentation of brain lesions from MRI scans. Accurate segmentation is crucial for improving cancer diagnosis, surgical planning, and predicting patient outcomes, particularly in cases of brain tumors and ischemic strokes. Traditional manual segmentation methods are costly, time-consuming, and susceptible to biases, which underscores the need for automated solutions.

BrainSegNet offers a simple yet efficient approach, enabling rapid voxel-wise segmentation of brain tumors and stroke regions in 3D MRIs, achieving processing speeds of approximately 0.5 seconds per scan—about 50 times faster than previous methods. The model was evaluated using the BRATS 2015 benchmark for brain tumor segmentation, where it demonstrated state-of-the-art performance, surpassing results from recent studies.

Additionally, BrainSegNet was applied to predict outcomes for ischemic stroke lesions using the ISLES 2017 database, yielding impressive results. The study emphasizes the significance of utilizing multiple MRI modalities to enhance visual information and improve segmentation accuracy. Overall, this research highlights the potential of deep learning techniques, particularly CNNs, in advancing automatic brain lesion segmentation and outcome prediction, which can significantly assist clinicians in decision-making processes.
