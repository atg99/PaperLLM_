The paper discusses the development of a new 3D brain segmentation network, Brain SegNet, built on a deep residual architecture to improve brain tumor segmentation and ischemic stroke lesion outcome prediction from 3D MRI data. Traditional CNN approaches face challenges such as information loss due to pooling operations, data imbalance among classes, and difficulty in aggregating multi-modal MRI information. Brain SegNet addresses these limitations through several key innovations.

Firstly, it introduces a 3D refinement module that aggregates fine-scale 3D deep features from multi-modal brain MRIs across multiple convolutional layers, facilitating accurate voxel-level segmentation. Secondly, the model employs a novel training strategy that incorporates curriculum learning and Focal loss, enabling efficient learning and better handling of class imbalance during dense training. Lastly, these enhancements are integrated into a single model that allows for direct prediction of dense voxel-level segmentation in one pass, achieving a processing time of approximately 0.5 seconds per MRI.

The paper reports state-of-the-art results on benchmark datasets like BRATS2015 and ISLES2017 for brain tumor segmentation and stroke lesion prediction, demonstrating the effectiveness of the proposed approach.
