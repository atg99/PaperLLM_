In the paper by Hu et al., the authors present a method for brain MRI segmentation using a 3D Brain SegNet model, specifically targeting brain tumor segmentation and ischemic stroke lesion outcome prediction. The model processes MRI scans by dividing them into smaller sub-volumes to accommodate GPU memory limitations, allowing for efficient inference. Each scan is predicted individually, and the results are combined to yield the final segmentation output.

The authors evaluate their model on two databases: the BRATS 2015 for brain tumor segmentation and the ISLES 2017 for ischemic stroke lesions. The BRATS database comprises 220 cases of high-grade and 54 cases of low-grade gliomas, with the objective of segmenting four tumor tissue types: necrotic core, edema, non-enhancing, and enhancing core. Standard metrics such as the Dice score, sensitivity, and specificity are utilized for performance evaluation.

Results indicate that the proposed model, trained with a curriculum and Focal loss, achieves improved performance metrics compared to recent state-of-the-art methods, particularly in whole tumor and enhancing tumor segmentation. Notably, the model's approach negates the need for post-processing steps, which are commonly employed in other methods, leading to a more efficient single-stage segmentation process. The findings demonstrate significant improvements, contributing to the model's robust capability in accurately identifying tumor boundaries. Overall, the study highlights the effectiveness of the 3D Brain SegNet in medical imaging tasks, offering a promising avenue for future research and application in brain lesion segmentation.
