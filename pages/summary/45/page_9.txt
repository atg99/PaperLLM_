The paper discusses the Transformer model architecture, particularly highlighting its multi-headed attention mechanism and the use of skip connections and layer normalization in both the encoder and decoder. It notes that input and output sequences are embedded and positional encoding is applied to represent the proximity of sequence elements. The final output is generated through a linear layer and a softmax function, transforming the decoder's output vector into a probability distribution over the vocabulary, allowing for the selection of the most probable word.

Additionally, the paper introduces Pointer Networks (Ptr-Nets), which enhance the sequence-to-sequence model by allowing outputs to be pointers to elements within the input sequence rather than fixed categories. This adaptation enables Ptr-Nets to effectively solve combinatorial optimization problems, such as computing planar convex hulls and solving the symmetric planar Travelling Salesman Problem.
