The paper discusses the derivation of gradients for weight updates in RNNs, highlighting the complexity introduced by the dependencies across time steps. Specifically, it illustrates how the gradients with respect to weights Wxh and Whh can be expressed through a series of equations that account for the contributions of previous hidden states. However, this leads to challenges such as numerical instability when dealing with large sequences, as the gradients can either vanish or explode during backpropagation through time (BPTT).

To address these issues, the paper introduces Truncated BPTT, which limits the number of time steps considered in the gradient calculations, effectively operating within a moving window of past states. This method simplifies the computation and helps manage the problems associated with long sequences.

The paper emphasizes the significance of LSTMs as a solution to the vanishing gradient problem, allowing for effective learning over extended time periods. By incorporating gated mechanisms, LSTMs significantly enhance the capability of RNNs to learn from long sequences, outperforming traditional RNN architectures in various tasks.
