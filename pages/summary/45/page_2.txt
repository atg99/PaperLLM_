This paper discusses the fundamental differences between Feedforward Neural Networks (FNNs) and Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs), particularly focusing on their architectures and the implications for error propagation during training. The author presents equations that illustrate the computations for hidden and output variables in both network types, highlighting how RNNs incorporate previous hidden states recursively, which allows them to maintain contextual information over time.

The paper introduces Backpropagation Through Time (BPTT), a specialized adaptation of the backpropagation algorithm for RNNs. BPTT unfolds the RNN into a structure resembling a FNN to facilitate backpropagation. The process involves calculating the hidden and output states step-by-step and defining a loss function that quantifies the difference between predicted outputs and target values. The loss function aggregates contributions from all time steps, allowing for various definitions based on specific problems, such as Mean Squared Error or Cross Entropy Loss.

The author details how to compute the partial derivatives of the loss function with respect to the weight matrices used in the RNN, emphasizing the application of the chain rule in this context. The equations provided demonstrate the computation for the weight matrices associated with the hidden states and outputs, showcasing the complexity of training RNNs compared to FNNs.

Overall, the paper effectively outlines the theoretical framework and practical considerations for training RNNs using BPTT, offering insights into the unique characteristics and challenges of this neural network architecture.
