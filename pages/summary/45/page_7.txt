The paper discusses the implementation of an attention mechanism in sequence-to-sequence (seq2seq) models to enhance the handling of long sequences. It introduces the concept of self-attention, illustrated by an alignment matrix that shows correlations between different language translations, and emphasizes the importance of shortcuts between the context vector and the entire source input rather than relying solely on the last hidden state of the encoder.

The attention mechanism allows for a more dynamic construction of the context vector, represented mathematically through hidden states and alignment scores. Specifically, the context vector is computed as a weighted sum of the hidden states of the input sequence, where the weights are determined by alignment scores that reflect the relevance of each input state to the current output.

Different types of score functions for calculating these alignment scores are acknowledged, with a table summarizing their names, equations, and usage in various publications. The paper highlights the significance of these scores in determining how much influence each source hidden state has on the generated output, providing a foundational understanding of the attention mechanism in seq2seq architectures. 

For more detailed insights and visual representations, the paper refers readers to additional sections and appendices.
