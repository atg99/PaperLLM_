The paper discusses the performance of various deep learning architectures in terms of validation accuracy, trainable layers, and parameters. 

For VGG16, validation accuracy gradually increases after the fifth trainable layer, reaching a plateau of 92.5 at the tenth layer, with no further significant improvements noted. 

MobileNetV2 and NasnetMobile exhibit similar patterns to Resnet50, showing fluctuations in accuracy. They achieve slightly lower overall accuracies, with MobileNetV2 at 90% and NasnetMobile at 88%. However, NasnetMobile demonstrates better consistency in performance.

DenseNet121 stands out as the model with the highest accuracy of 95.5%, achieved with only ten trainable layers. Its accuracy remains consistently higher than other models, showing a plateau effect even with additional layers.

The analysis highlights that different architectures require varying numbers of trainable parameters to achieve peak accuracy, with DenseNet121 outperforming even advanced models like Xception and EfficientNetB2 in this regard. 

Training curves reveal that DenseNet121 maintains a near-perfect fit without signs of overfitting or underfitting, achieving maximum accuracy in fewer epochs. In contrast, newer architectures like Xception and EfficientNetB2 exhibit steeper and smoother curves, while mobile architectures such as MobileNet and NasNet converge more slowly, akin to Resnet50 and VGG16.
