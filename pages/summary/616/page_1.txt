Forest has shown promising results in classifying astronomical objects. The use of deep learning has gained traction due to its ability to handle vast datasets and complex patterns inherent in astronomical data. 

In this paper, the authors investigate the classification of astronomical bodies using the SDSS-IV dataset by leveraging deep neural networks and focusing on efficient layer-wise fine-tuning. They explore various architectures, including ResNet-50, DenseNet-121, VGG-16, Xception, EfficientNet B2, MobileNetV2, and NasNetMobile, to determine the effectiveness of different training strategies.

The findings indicate that simply freezing all layers and adding a trainable final layer does not yield optimal results. Instead, models with varying numbers of trainable layers show different levels of accuracy, suggesting that fine-tuning needs to be performed at different levels for each architecture. DenseNet-121, Xception, and EfficientNet B2 achieved high accuracy with stable training curves, while ResNet-50, VGG-16, MobileNetV2, and NasNetMobile exhibited delayed peak accuracies and less fit training curves.

Moreover, the study highlights that mobile architectures, despite their smaller sizes and fewer parameters, do not always perform well on low computational devices due to lower validation accuracies. The authors introduce customized evaluation metrics such as Tuning Parameter Ratio and Tuning Layer Ratio to assess model performance effectively.

In conclusion, this research contributes to the understanding of transfer learning in astronomical classification tasks, emphasizing the importance of layer-wise fine-tuning and the need for tailored training strategies across different neural network architectures.
