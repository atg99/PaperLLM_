The paper discusses advancements in the performance of classification models using various transfer learning techniques, specifically focusing on the application of these methods to astronomical datasets from the Sloan Digital Sky Survey (SDSS). It references previous work by Khramtsov et al. in 2019, which combined deep learning networks with support vector machines to enhance classification accuracy using photometric parameters.

The section on efficient transfer learning highlights the necessity of fine-tuning transfer learning models before application. It introduces the concept of adapter modules, which serve as additional networks in conjunction with the parent architecture to improve feature learning. The paper notes that integrating low-level and high-level features through adapters leads to better performance compared to traditional fine-tuning. Residual adapters are also discussed, allowing for shared parameters across multiple domains while retaining prior knowledge.

The methodology section outlines the dataset utilized, specifically the SDSS DR 16 release, which includes various types of data such as images and spectra. The research focuses on image data, employing transfer learning models like ResNet50, VGG16, and EfficientNetB2, among others. The images are resized to manage the number of training parameters, and a class imbalance is addressed by incorporating class weights during model training.

The study employs several transfer learning architectures, using pre-trained weights from ImageNet and replacing the original softmax layer with a customized version for three-class classification. The paper provides a comparison of the architectures in terms of layers and parameters, noting that ResNet50 has the highest number of parameters among traditional models, while mobile architectures like MobileNetV2 have significantly fewer parameters. Overall, the paper emphasizes the effectiveness of transfer learning in enhancing classification tasks within the context of astronomical data analysis.
