The paper presents an analysis of baseline models in deep learning, focusing on the performance of various architectures, including ResNet, MobileNetV2, NASNet, Xception, EfficientNetB2, and DenseNet121. The findings indicate that baseline models, which only have the last softmax layer trainable, perform poorly, with VGG16 achieving an accuracy as low as 44%. Newer architectures like Xception and EfficientNetB2 significantly outperform older models, and mobile architectures also show competitive accuracy.

The paper emphasizes the importance of fine-tuning the number of trainable layers to optimize model performance. As the number of trainable layers increases, accuracy improves until it plateaus or declines. Models are categorized based on parameters, trainable layers, and validation accuracies, highlighting that newer architectures consistently achieve higher accuracies compared to older ones, including mobile architectures, with DenseNet121 being identified as an ideal architecture.

Figures demonstrate that Xception and EfficientNetB2 maintain high accuracy even with minimal trainable layers. Specifically, EfficientNetB2 reaches 95.5% accuracy at 36 trainable layers, while Xception achieves 95% at 18 layers. In contrast, ResNet50 shows an increase in accuracy with more trainable layers but dips after 30 layers, peaking at 93.5%, which is lower than that of competing models. Overall, the study underscores the advantages of newer architectures and the significance of adjusting trainable layers for optimal accuracy.
