The paper discusses the development and implementation of BARTpho, a pre-trained model for Vietnamese text processing, focusing on two variants: BARTpho syllable and BARTpho word. Both models utilize a large architecture with 12 encoder and decoder layers, following the BART pre-training scheme. The pre-training involves corrupting input text through noise functions, such as text infilling and sentence permutation, and subsequently learning to reconstruct the original text.

BARTpho word is built on the PhoBERT pre-training corpus, which consists of 20GB of text, while BARTpho syllable uses a detokenized version of this corpus, amounting to approximately 4 billion syllable tokens. Both models employ advanced tokenization techniques and have significant parameter counts, with BARTpho word having around 420 million parameters and BARTpho syllable about 396 million.

The optimization process for both models involves using the Adam optimizer across multiple GPUs, with specific training configurations, including a peak learning rate and batch size. The paper highlights a practical application of BARTpho models for feature extraction, demonstrating their potential for various downstream tasks.

In experiments, BARTpho's performance is evaluated against mBART on the task of Vietnamese text summarization using the VNDS dataset. The dataset consists of over 150,000 news articles, which were filtered for duplicates to ensure quality in training and evaluation. The results of these experiments aim to establish BARTpho as a competitive model for text summarization in Vietnamese.
