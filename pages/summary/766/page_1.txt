This paper presents an end-to-end deep learning framework designed for digital holographic reconstruction, addressing the limitations of conventional methods. Digital holography captures the complete wavefront of an object, including both amplitude and phase. Traditional numerical reconstruction methods, such as the angular spectrum method and convolution method, require prior knowledge of various parameters like object distance, incident beam angle, and source wavelength. These methods often involve manual filtering to remove unwanted artifacts like zero-order and twin images, which is time-consuming. Additionally, phase imaging poses challenges such as phase aberration compensation and phase unwrapping, which can be sensitive to noise.

The proposed solution, termed the holographic reconstruction network, employs a data-driven approach to eliminate the need for prior knowledge in the reconstruction process. This framework not only reconstructs noise-free images but also efficiently handles phase imaging and depth map generation. The authors highlight the potential of deep learning to simplify and enhance the holographic reconstruction process, making it more accessible for various applications without the computational burdens of traditional techniques. The study underscores the significance of machine learning in advancing digital holography, particularly in biological imaging and environmental monitoring.
