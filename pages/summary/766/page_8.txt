The paper discusses the training of a deep learning model, specifically HRNet, for Enhanced Focusing Imaging (EFI) and Depth Mapping (DM) with considerations for training speed and memory efficiency. The training process is concluded after 25 epochs, after which the testing set is introduced to the network. For comparative analysis, traditional reconstruction methods based on self-entropy, variance, and Tenenbaum gradient are utilized. These methods require prior knowledge of the estimated range for reconstruction, which is set between 270 and 295 mm.

The reconstruction process involves performing 16 sequential numerical reconstructions within the specified range, and metrics are calculated for each pixel in an 11x11 window. The results indicate that HRNet significantly outperforms conventional methods in both visual and quantitative assessments. While traditional methods can reconstruct EFI and DM, they struggle with issues such as coarse distance sampling and noise, resulting in noticeable artifacts and indistinguishable focal distances in DM.

In contrast, HRNet facilitates fast feedforward predictions, completing the task in approximately 1 second, while conventional methods take longer due to their reliance on sequential numerical reconstruction. The paper includes visual comparisons and quantitative results, demonstrating the advantages of HRNet in the context of EFI and DM reconstruction.
