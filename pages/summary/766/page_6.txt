The paper presents a method for reconstructing quantitative phase images from holograms using a deep learning approach called HRNet. The study begins by calculating the true phase of a sample based on known parameters, including groove thickness, wavelength, and refractive indices. The ground-truth quantitative phase image is obtained through manual cleaning of initial phase images, followed by phase unwrapping and aberration compensation.

The authors compare the performance of HRNet against traditional phase aberration compensation methods, specifically Principal Component Analysis (PCA) and double exposure (DE). PCA relies on linear and spherical phase aberration assumptions, while DE requires an additional reference hologram captured immediately after the object hologram to mitigate noise and fluctuations. Both methods necessitate phase unwrapping post-compensation, which HRNet circumvents by integrating these processes into an end-to-end framework.

Experimental results indicate that HRNet outperforms PCA and DE, yielding phase images with fewer artifacts and higher quality, as evidenced by superior Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR) and Structural Similarity Index (SSIM) scores. HRNet achieves around 20 dB improvement in PSNR and 0.85 in SSIM compared to the conventional methods.

The paper underscores that HRNet simplifies the reconstruction process by eliminating the need for additional phase compensation and unwrapping algorithms, making it particularly useful for applications in biology and microelectronics where true phase information may not be readily available. The method's potential for practical applications is highlighted, particularly in detecting malaria-infected red blood cells and microelectronics surface defects. Overall, the proposed framework represents a significant advancement in quantitative phase imaging.
