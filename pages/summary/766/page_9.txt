The paper discusses the performance of HRNet in holographic reconstruction, highlighting its advantages over conventional metric-based methods. HRNet not only achieves high-quality estimations of the Electric Field Intensity (EFI) and Digital Microscopy (DM) but also significantly reduces computation time, often taking less than 1.5 seconds compared to over six minutes for traditional methods.

The authors explore the network's robustness under varying experimental conditions, particularly focusing on different incident angles and axial distances. It is noted that traditional holographic methods require manual adjustments when the incident angle changes, which can complicate the reconstruction process. In contrast, HRNet demonstrates the ability to effectively reconstruct images from holograms captured at different angles without prior exposure to those specific angles during training.

Additionally, the paper examines the network's performance with objects at various axial distances. The training data included holograms at discrete distances, but the authors emphasize the importance of the network's adaptability to distances not represented in the training set. Experimental results show that HRNet maintains high reconstruction quality across different angles and distances, indicating its practical applicability in real-world scenarios.

The performance metrics, including Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR) and Structural Similarity Index (SSIM), reveal that HRNet outperforms other methods such as SEN, VRA, and TEN in both validation and testing scenarios. The results underscore HRNet's effectiveness in achieving high-quality reconstructions while significantly reducing processing time.
