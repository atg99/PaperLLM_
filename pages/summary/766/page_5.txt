The paper discusses the application of deep learning, specifically HRNet, for the reconstruction of holograms. The training process involves 20,000 iterations across 25 epochs, allowing the network to learn representative features of the holograms, which reduces loss and updates model parameters. Once trained, the network predicts reconstructions from a test subset, yielding results visually indistinguishable from the ground-truth images, as shown in Figs. 4(e)–4(h). 

A comparison is made between HRNet and conventional methods like ASM and CONV, which rely on known optical setup parameters and require manual adjustments for band-pass filters. The conventional methods exhibit noticeable background noise and artifacts, while HRNet provides noise-free reconstructions, demonstrating superior performance in image quality. Quantitative metrics such as peak signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR) and structural similarity (SSIM) indicate that HRNet significantly outperforms ASM and CONV in reconstruction quality, although computational times are comparable.

The study further extends to the reconstruction of a phase object, leveraging known 3-D dimensions. Similar data collection methods resulted in 2500 holograms, with HRNet effectively reconstructing the phase object as well. Overall, the findings illustrate the efficacy of deep learning in enhancing holographic reconstruction over traditional techniques.
