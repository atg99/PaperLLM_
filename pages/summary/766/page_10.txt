This paper presents HRNet, an end-to-end learning architecture designed for numerical reconstruction in digital holography (DH). The network was trained using holograms recorded at a specific distance, yet it demonstrates the ability to reconstruct images effectively from holograms captured at different distances, indicating that it has learned the essential characteristics of holograms without requiring autofocusing. 

The authors showcase various reconstruction modalities, such as amplitude reconstruction and quantitative phase imaging, to highlight the network's efficacy in producing high-quality images. HRNet's design allows for multiple reconstruction methods to be executed with a single algorithm, simplifying the computational process and avoiding the need for extensive intermediate algorithm development. Additionally, the network can be easily retrained with new data, enhancing its performance and extending its application potential.

Despite its effectiveness, the authors acknowledge limitations in the current dataset, which consists of similar objects, and express interest in applying this method to microscopic samples in future work. This entails collecting data from microscopic specimens using DHM configurations, which will be a focal point of their ongoing research.

The paper also includes a detailed specification of the HRNet architecture, outlining its layers and parameters, ultimately totaling 2,857,248 parameters. The authors believe their framework has significant potential for applications in object detection, particle tracking, and super-resolution, making digital holography more accessible and paving the way for innovative applications.
