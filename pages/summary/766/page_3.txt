The paper discusses the architecture of a deep learning framework called HRNet, which utilizes three primary functional units: the ReLU unit, the residual unit, and the subpixel convolutional layer. The ReLU unit operates as a nonlinear activation function defined as max(0, x). The residual unit, labeled as ResUnit(N), is designed with a depth of N and includes a max-pooling layer, two identical convolutional layers with N feature maps of size 3x3, a batch normalization (BN) layer, and a ReLU layer. It incorporates skip connections by adding the input of each ResUnit to its output. The inclusion of the max-pooling layer is crucial for preventing overfitting but only appears in specific configurations due to its effect on image dimensions.

The subpixel convolutional layer enhances image resolution without the extensive trainable parameters typically associated with transposed convolution methods. This layer consists of a convolutional layer followed by a periodic shuffling operation, enabling the recovery of high-resolution images by rearranging tensor elements after downsampling.

The paper mathematically describes the functional layers of the HRNet, detailing how each layer processes input hologram data through learned functions. It emphasizes the reduction of computational load by minimizing the number of trainable parameters, particularly in the subpixel convolutional layer, where only the initial convolution step requires parameter updates.

To achieve effective learning, the network's parameters are estimated by minimizing a loss function, defined as the pixelwise mean squared error between the predicted images and their corresponding ground-truth images. The comprehensive structure and workflow of HRNet is illustrated, demonstrating its capability to reconstruct images from various types of input holograms.
