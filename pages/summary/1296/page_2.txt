The paper discusses the implementation of masked autoencoders (MAE) as spatiotemporal learners, particularly in the context of video processing. It highlights a strategy where a significant portion (e.g., 90%) of random patches in a video is masked, allowing an encoder to focus solely on the visible patches. A small decoder then reconstructs the entire input from the encoded patches and the mask tokens, without any spatiotemporal inductive bias in the encoder or decoder.

The authors note that in scenarios with identical static frames, a high masking ratio can lead to efficient computation, as most static frames can be inferred from a small sample of patches. They emphasize that this approach reduces the encoder's time and memory complexity significantly, achieving a theoretical computation reduction of 7.7 times compared to encoding all tokens. Practically, this results in a 4.1 times speedup in wall-clock time, addressing the challenges of large-scale video research.

Results from various video recognition datasets demonstrate that MAE pre-training enhances generalization performance significantly. For instance, it improves the accuracy of ViT-Large by 13% on the Kinetics-400 dataset compared to training from scratch, while also reducing overall training time. The MAE pre-training approach shows superior performance compared to traditional supervised pre-training methods and competes well with state-of-the-art techniques that leverage more domain-specific knowledge. Encouragingly, the authors report successful results from MAE pre-trained on uncurated Instagram videos, suggesting that self-supervised learning in video can align with approaches used in language and image processing.

The paper also reviews related work in denoising autoencoders, noting their evolution and application in learning representations through the reconstruction of clean signals from corrupted inputs. The paper traces the development of masked autoencoding methods, particularly in computer vision, and cites pivotal works that have contributed to its advancement, including the use of Transformer architectures and the exploration of masked prediction with patches. The study builds upon these foundations, particularly focusing on the efficiency and effectiveness of the MAE framework in video tasks.
