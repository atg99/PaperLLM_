This document provides a compilation of key research papers in the field of computer vision and machine learning, highlighting significant contributions to various aspects of these domains. 

The paper by Goyal et al. (2017) discusses advancements in training large-scale models efficiently using minibatch stochastic gradient descent. Goyal et al. (2017) also introduce the “something something” video dataset, which aids in learning visual common sense. Grill et al. (2020) propose a self-supervised learning approach termed "Bootstrap Your Own Latent," which enhances representation learning.

Gu et al. (2018) present a video dataset focused on localized atomic visual actions, while Han et al. (2019) explore video representation learning through dense predictive coding. He et al. (2021) introduce masked autoencoders as scalable vision learners, alongside their work on momentum contrast for unsupervised visual representation in 2020.

Hoffer et al. (2020) improve generalization through instance repetition, and Huang et al. (2016) discuss deep networks with stochastic depth. Kay et al. (2017) contribute the Kinetics human action video dataset for action recognition tasks. Kondratyuk et al. (2021) present MoviNets, emphasizing efficient video recognition.

The historical context is provided by LeCun et al. (1989) with their work on backpropagation in handwritten zip code recognition. Lee et al. (2017) propose an unsupervised representation learning method via sorting sequences. Liu et al. (2021) introduce the Swin Transformer v2 and the Video Swin Transformer, focusing on improved capacity and resolution.

Loshchilov and Hutter (2017, 2019) discuss stochastic gradient descent with warm restarts and decoupled weight decay regularization, respectively. Lotter et al. (2017) and Mathieu et al. (2016) explore deep predictive coding networks and multi-scale video prediction methods. Misra et al. (2016) introduce a method for unsupervised learning through temporal order verification, while Oord et al. (2017) focus on neural discrete representation learning. Lastly, Pan et al. (2021) present a network model for spatio-temporal action localization.

Overall, these papers collectively advance the understanding and capabilities in visual representation learning, video analysis, and efficient model training techniques.
