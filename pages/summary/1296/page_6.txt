The paper presents a comparative analysis of the performance of masked autoencoders (MAE) in video recognition tasks, specifically using the ViT-L architecture. It demonstrates that pre-training with MAE significantly enhances accuracy and reduces training time compared to training from scratch. 

Figure 5 illustrates that MAE pre-training for 800 epochs achieves an accuracy of 84.4%, a notable increase of 13.0% over the 71.4% accuracy achieved by training from scratch. The study indicates that MAE pre-training is particularly beneficial for video recognition, offering a larger accuracy improvement compared to similar tasks in image recognition.

The training time comparison in Table 1 shows that using a sparse encoder (without the mask token [M]) reduces floating-point operations (FLOPs) by 7.7 times and computation time by 5.8 times. Although data loading becomes a bottleneck, the overall speedup is still significant, achieving a 4.1 times faster training process.

Additionally, the paper explores the impact of different masking ratios on performance. A 90% masking ratio yields the best results, while a 95% ratio also performs well with extended training. In contrast, lower ratios (75% and 50%) are less effective for video data, suggesting that video data's inherent redundancy allows for higher masking ratios to be more effective.

Overall, the findings indicate that MAE pre-training is a practical and efficient approach for video recognition, balancing accuracy and computational efficiency.
