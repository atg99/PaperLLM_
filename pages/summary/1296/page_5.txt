The paper discusses a novel approach to design a decoder within a vision transformer (ViT) framework for video analysis, specifically focusing on a masked autoencoder (MAE) architecture. The decoder is a smaller version of the encoder, processing a combined set of encoded patches and mask tokens while maintaining lower complexity—approximately 1/20 per token compared to the encoder. This results in an overall complexity reduction of 7.7 times compared to full encoding. The decoder predicts pixel values or their normalized equivalents, utilizing a mean squared error loss function between predictions and targets.

Data preprocessing for MAE pre-training involves sampling 16 frames of size 224x224 from raw video with a temporal stride of 4, alongside random spatial augmentations. The authors note that the speed of their MAE pre-training is hindered by data loading times, which they address through repeated sampling to optimize efficiency without increasing the number of samples.

The architecture employs vanilla ViT designs with separable positional embeddings for spatial and temporal dimensions, ensuring manageable sizes. The MAE pre-training configuration is based on established practices, utilizing the AdamW optimizer and a batch size of 512. Evaluation is conducted through fine-tuning, as opposed to linear probing, to assess pre-training quality.

The experiments focus on ablation studies using the Kinetics-400 dataset, where the authors perform self-supervised pre-training followed by supervised fine-tuning, reporting top-1 classification accuracy. Further exploration of pre-training datasets and downstream tasks is also planned.
