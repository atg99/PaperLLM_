This paper presents a series of ablation experiments on a video processing model, specifically ViT-L, using the Kinetics-400 dataset. The focus is on understanding the impact of various parameters, including mask sampling strategies, reconstruction targets, data augmentation techniques, and model architecture on the overall accuracy of the system.

Key findings include:

1. **Mask Sampling Strategy**: The study employs a structure-agnostic random sampling method inspired by BERT and MAE. Results indicate that random sampling across space and time yields the best accuracy (84.4%) compared to space-only and time-only methods. Time-only sampling, especially with high masking ratios, significantly hampers performance.

2. **Reconstruction Target**: The model performs well when reconstructing original pixels, achieving 83.8% accuracy. Normalized pixel reconstruction improves results slightly. The study highlights the efficiency of pixel-based methods over more complex tokenizers, which can introduce additional computational burdens.

3. **Data Augmentation**: Natural temporal augmentations are shown to be effective, while additional spatial augmentations like random cropping yield mixed results. Stronger augmentations tend to decrease accuracy, indicating that simpler methods are often preferable.

4. **Model Architecture**: The experiments also examine decoder width and depth, revealing that overly narrow or shallow decoders lead to noticeable drops in accuracy, contrary to findings in image processing.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the effectiveness of simple, well-structured methods in video understanding tasks, advocating for a focus on fundamental principles rather than complex augmentations or architectures.
