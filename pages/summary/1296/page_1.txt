The paper "Masked Autoencoders As Spatiotemporal Learners" by Christoph Feichtenhofer et al. explores an extension of Masked Autoencoders (MAE) for spatiotemporal representation learning from videos. The authors propose a method where spacetime patches in videos are randomly masked, and an autoencoder is trained to reconstruct these masked patches in pixel form. The study reveals that the MAE approach can generate strong representations with minimal inductive bias, except for patch and positional embeddings. Notably, it is shown that a high masking ratio of 90% is optimal for videos, compared to 75% for images, highlighting the greater information redundancy present in video data. This high masking ratio enables significant computational speedups, exceeding four times in wall-clock time.

The results demonstrate that MAE outperforms traditional supervised pre-training methods across various challenging video datasets and shows promising outcomes when trained on real-world, uncurated Instagram data. The findings suggest that the masked autoencoding framework can serve as a unified methodology for representation learning with minimal reliance on domain-specific knowledge, aligning with a broader trend in deep learning towards reducing inductive biases across different domains.
