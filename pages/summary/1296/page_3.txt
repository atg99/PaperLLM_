The paper presents visualizations of a model's performance on the Kinetics-400 validation set with a focus on video reconstruction under varying masking ratios. Figure 2 illustrates the original video, masked video, and the model's output, demonstrating the model's ability to reconstruct original pixels from a 90% masking ratio. The video dimensions are specified as 16×224×224, and the spatiotemporal patch size is 2×16×16, resulting in a total of 1568 tokens, of which only 156 are visible. For clarity, the visualizations use known patches from the original input.

Figure 3 extends this analysis to a 95% masking ratio, highlighting a shift in focus from pixel prediction to tokenization of prediction targets. It references methodologies like BEiT, which utilizes pre-trained dV AE for reconstruction, and MaskFeat, which employs HoG for prediction targets. 

The discussion emphasizes the challenges and techniques of self-supervised learning in video contexts, particularly the importance of temporal coherence. The proposed method leverages this coherence implicitly, suggesting that a higher masking ratio capitalizes on the inherent information redundancy of video data compared to images.
