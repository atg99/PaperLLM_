This paper discusses advancements in masking-based methods for self-supervised learning applied to video data, emphasizing a novel autoencoding approach that operates directly on pixels rather than tokenized predictions. The method aims to simplify the process and enhance learning efficiency, which is crucial given the large scale and time-intensive nature of video research.

The proposed method extends the Masked Autoencoder (MAE) framework to spacetime data, maintaining a unified approach with minimal reliance on domain knowledge. It involves patch embedding, where video clips are divided into non-overlapping patches, which are then flattened and embedded through linear projection with positional embeddings added for spatial awareness. 

Masking is a key component, involving random sampling of embedded patches without replacement, which is agnostic to the spacetime structure. This strategy draws parallels with the BERT model for text and MAE for images. The authors hypothesize that the optimal masking ratio is linked to data redundancy, finding that a 90% masking ratio for videos is effective due to their temporal coherence. This ratio outperforms structure-aware sampling methods, such as space-only or time-only sampling, which may retain less information and complicate the pre-training task. 

The encoder utilizes a vanilla Vision Transformer (ViT) on visible patches, significantly reducing time and memory complexity, making the approach practical. The findings suggest that spacetime-agnostic sampling maximizes the utility of visible patches, allowing for higher masking ratios and more efficient learning in video representation tasks.
