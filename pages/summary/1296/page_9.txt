The paper investigates the performance of masked autoencoders (MAE) for video data pre-training, emphasizing the impact of dataset size and training epochs on model accuracy. Results show that pre-training on a 240k subset of IG-curated data yields lower performance on K400 classification due to potential domain shifts. However, increasing the dataset size to 512k and 1M significantly enhances performance, outperforming K400 pre-training even when evaluated on K400. Interestingly, IG-uncurated data, despite being randomly sampled, performs comparably to IG-curated data, highlighting the effectiveness of uncurated datasets in this context.

The authors provide system-level comparisons with existing methods on various datasets, noting that their results, based on vanilla ViT architectures, are competitive with leading entries that utilize more complex architectures. This suggests that learning from data with fewer inductive biases is a viable approach in self-supervised learning.

Additionally, the paper reports on the implications of video pre-training for image recognition, where models pre-trained on video data achieve better performance than those trained from scratch on image datasets, indicating potential benefits of video pre-training for broader visual representation tasks.

In conclusion, the study affirms that strong representations can be learned with minimal domain knowledge, and emphasizes the importance of the masking ratio in masked autoencoding methods. The findings encourage further exploration in utilizing real-world, uncurated data for self-supervised learning, though challenges remain in scaling to larger datasets. The authors hope their work will inspire future research in this area.
