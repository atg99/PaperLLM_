The paper investigates the influence of pre-training data on the performance of a model evaluated on various downstream tasks, specifically Kinetics-400 (K400), A V A, and SomethingSomething v2 (SSv2). The study compares different pre-training methods, including supervised and masked autoencoders (MAE), across various datasets such as ImageNet-1K (IN1K) and Kinetics datasets (K400, K600, K700, and IG-uncurated). 

Key findings indicate that MAE pre-training significantly enhances performance compared to supervised methods, particularly highlighting a 10.1% improvement in A V A when comparing K400 MAE to K400 supervised. Moreover, MAE pre-training on videos (K400) outperforms image-based MAE pre-training on IN1K, suggesting that video-specific training is crucial for video tasks. The study also notes that larger datasets, such as K600 and K700, further improve results, with K700 yielding additional gains on A V A and SSv2.

The paper emphasizes the practical implications of self-supervised learning methods, particularly in applications where data augmentation is challenging. Additionally, it discusses the efficiency of repeated sampling to optimize data loading and the necessity of adequate decoder capacity for effective learning from high-dimensional video data. Finally, the performance of models pre-trained on real-world Instagram videos (IG-uncurated) shows promising results, outperforming some traditional pre-training methods, underscoring the value of diverse and extensive datasets for enhancing model performance.
