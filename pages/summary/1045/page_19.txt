The paper discusses the use of Individual Conditional Expectation (ICE) plots to analyze how model predictions change when a specific feature varies. An example is provided with the feature "worst concave points" from a cancer dataset, showing that lower values correlate with higher occurrences of malignant cases, while values above 0.21 yield constant predictions for benign cases. 

ICE plots are highlighted for their intuitive understanding compared to Partial Dependence Plots (PDP), as they can reveal heterogeneous relationships. However, challenges include potential overcrowding of plots with multiple ICE curves, the risk of invalid data points due to feature correlation, and difficulty in determining average effects. 

A comparative analysis of PDP, ICE, and Feature Importance is presented, noting that while PDP shows global effects, it may obscure heterogeneous effects. In contrast, ICE reveals these effects but struggles with individual feature representation and average visibility. 

Accumulated Local Effects (ALE) plots are introduced as a solution to the limitations of PDP, particularly in cases of feature correlation. ALE plots are model-agnostic and provide clearer visualizations of feature effects on predictions, making them computationally efficient. An example using the Iris classification dataset illustrates how ALE plots can clarify feature impacts, demonstrating consistent effects of petal length across classes at a specific measurement. 

Overall, the paper emphasizes the strengths and weaknesses of different methods for interpreting model predictions, advocating for the use of ALE plots for more reliable insights in the presence of correlated features.
