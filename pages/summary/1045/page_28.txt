The paper discusses Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI), focusing on its core ideas, techniques, and solutions. It illustrates these concepts using examples from the MNIST and Fashion MNIST datasets, which contain images of various clothing items. 

In the first example, a shoe image demonstrates how positive attributions (indicated by green) significantly outweigh negative attributions (indicated by purple), highlighting the pixels that most influence the model's decision. The shoe's distinct features lead to a clear prediction. Conversely, the second example involves a shirt image where positive and negative attributions are balanced. Here, the collar and sleeves contribute positively, while ambiguity in the middle portion leads to negative attributions, affecting the prediction's accuracy.

The paper also highlights software libraries for implementing example-based XAI techniques, particularly the Diverse Counterfactual Explanations (DiCE) package. DiCE provides "what-if" explanations, allowing users to understand how feature modifications can change model predictions. It identifies influential features and suggests necessary changes to achieve desired outcomes, enhancing practical applicability in real-world scenarios. 

Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of understanding model predictions through XAI techniques and the role of software tools in facilitating this understanding.
