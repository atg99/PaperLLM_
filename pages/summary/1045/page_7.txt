The paper discusses the complexities of interpretability in machine learning models, emphasizing the challenges in understanding the significance of individual features and their interactions within prediction models. It differentiates between model-specific and model-agnostic techniques for interpretation. Model-specific techniques analyze the inner workings of a particular model to elucidate its results, while model-agnostic techniques can be applied across various models. 

The paper outlines various sections focused on data explainability, including exploratory analysis, visualization, and dimensionality reduction. It also categorizes model explainability into white box models, such as linear models and decision trees, and black box models, including tree ensembles and support vector machines. Furthermore, it introduces feature-based techniques that assess feature importance and utilizes various methods like LIME and Shapley for explanations. The taxonomy of explainable artificial intelligence (XAI) techniques is structured to cover both data and model explainability comprehensively.
