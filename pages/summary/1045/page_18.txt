The paper discusses Explainable AI (XAI) and focuses on two key techniques: Partial Dependence Plots (PDP) and Individual Conditional Expectation (ICE) plots.

Partial Dependence Plots (PDP) illustrate how a specific feature influences predictions by depicting the relationship between the feature and the predicted outcome. For instance, PDP can answer questions about how geographical factors like longitude and latitude affect house prices. The plotted graph shows variations in average predictions as the feature value changes, with a confidence interval represented by a shaded area. The findings suggest that certain features may have an initial negative impact on predictions before stabilizing.

The benefits of PDP include ease of implementation and intuitive computation, making it accessible for non-experts. However, PDP has limitations, such as the assumption of feature independence, which may not hold true in practice. Additionally, it can realistically only represent two features due to its two-dimensional nature, and it may overlook heterogeneous effects that could be revealed through individual analyses.

In contrast, Individual Conditional Expectation (ICE) plots provide a more granular view by displaying a separate line for each instance, enhancing intuitiveness compared to PDP. Each line indicates predictions for individual instances rather than averaging, allowing for a more detailed understanding of how specific features affect different cases.

Overall, both PDP and ICE are valuable tools in XAI, each with its strengths and weaknesses, contributing to a deeper understanding of model predictions.
