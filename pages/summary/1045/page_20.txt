The paper discusses Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI), focusing on core concepts, techniques, and solutions. One key technique highlighted is the use of global surrogate models, which are interpretable models designed to approximate the predictions of complex black box models. The process involves three steps: first, inputting data into the black box model to obtain predictions; second, selecting a suitable interpretable model type (e.g., linear regression or decision trees); and third, training the surrogate model using independent variables from the input data and dependent variables from the black box predictions. 

The effectiveness of the surrogate model is assessed by comparing its prediction error with that of the black box model; a smaller error indicates a better approximation. The benefits of this approach include the flexibility to interchange between interpretable models and the underlying black box model, allowing for multiple explanations. However, limitations exist, as conclusions drawn from the surrogate model may not universally apply to all subsets of the data, and it may not effectively explain individual predictions. Thus, while surrogate models provide valuable insights, they do not fully capture the complexities of the original model's decision-making process.
