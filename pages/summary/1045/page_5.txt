The paper discusses the various stakeholders involved in the explanation phase of AI systems, emphasizing the need for clear and understandable explanations for users, consumers, businesses, and regulators. Users require explanations to make informed decisions based on AI outputs, exemplified by an insurance company using AI to determine policy offerings. Consumers, who receive products and services, need simple explanations to build trust and transparency in AI systems. Business stakeholders, such as law enforcement and medical professionals, must understand AI decision-making to ensure fairness and protect against erroneous outcomes. Regulators monitor AI systems to ensure compliance and address any biases in decisions, which can arise from biased historical data.

The paper highlights the significance of explainable AI (XAI) in detecting biases, enhancing scientific understanding, and building robust models. It points out that XAI tools, such as feature importance analysis, can reveal biases in AI predictions. Furthermore, projects like the Automated Statistician help researchers interpret complex datasets, thereby improving their comprehension of data features. The paper also notes that robust models are less sensitive to input changes, and explainability contributes to this robustness.

The explaining phase is critical as it interprets AI predictions in real-world applications, providing human-readable explanations essential for mission-critical tasks. Understanding AI predictions aids organizations in making better decisions, such as identifying customers likely to churn. The overall message underscores the importance of explainability in AI to ensure ethical and effective deployment across various sectors.
