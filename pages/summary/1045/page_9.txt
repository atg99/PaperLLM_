The paper discusses two primary types of interpretation methods in machine learning: global and local interpretation. Global interpretation methods analyze the overall behavior of a model, focusing on variable definitions, dependencies, interactions, and their importance. Techniques such as feature importance and partial dependence plots are highlighted. In contrast, local interpretation examines individual predictions to elucidate the model's decision-making process, with techniques like LIME and Shapley values providing insights into specific data points.

The section on data explainability emphasizes the importance of data visualization as a foundational step for model validation and understanding. Exploratory data analysis is essential for interpreting machine learning models, while dimensionality reduction techniques such as PCA, t-SNE, and UMAP are introduced to enhance visualization and interpretation of complex datasets. These techniques simplify data representation by reducing dimensions while preserving significant structures and relationships.

Additionally, the paper outlines various software libraries available for implementing data explainability techniques, which facilitate the exploration and explanation of datasets. It notes that while traditional plots like histograms provide some insights, Kernel Density Estimates (KDEs) offer more flexibility and adaptability in visualizing data distributions.
