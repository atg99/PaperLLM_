The paper discusses the challenges and advancements in explainable artificial intelligence (XAI), particularly focusing on the contrast between black-box models and symbolic AI. Black-box models, such as deep learning systems, hinder explainability due to their opaque nature. In contrast, symbolic AI is inherently explainable as it uses meaningful symbols that are understandable to humans. However, symbolic systems struggle with scalability and handling noisy data.

Recent developments in neuro-symbolic systems, which combine symbolic reasoning with artificial neural networks, show promise in addressing these limitations. These systems leverage symbolic elements like knowledge representation and reasoning techniques, including ontologies and knowledge graphs. These tools provide a semantically rich context that enhances interpretability and allows for deductive reasoning, enabling users to understand the rationale behind conclusions.

The paper emphasizes that for a system to be comprehensible to both experts and non-experts, it must utilize symbols that facilitate understanding. Reasoning capabilities are essential for explaining the underlying causes of outcomes. For instance, ontologies can enable causal inference, allowing systems to deduce relationships and generate natural language explanations.

The authors highlight that linking outputs from subsymbolic approaches to contextual knowledge can yield user-specific explanations. This is illustrated through a pipeline that demonstrates how ontologies enhance the explainability of neural network classifications. Additionally, advancements in Natural Language Processing and Natural Language Generation are utilized to refine these explanations further, making them more accessible and understandable.
