Local Interpretable Model-agnostic Explanations (LIME) is a technique aimed at providing local interpretability of machine learning models. Unlike global surrogate methods that attempt to explain the entire model, LIME focuses on understanding individual predictions by perturbing input data samples and observing how the predictions change. This method aligns with human interests in model outputs by modifying feature values of a single data sample and analyzing the resulting impact on the prediction.

In the context of computer vision, LIME can be illustrated through an example involving image classification. The process involves acquiring an image, such as one of an Egyptian cat, and breaking it down into interpretable components. By generating a dataset of perturbed instances—where some components are turned "off"—the model's prediction probabilities for the presence of an Egyptian cat are computed. A locally weighted simple linear model is then applied to these perturbed instances, emphasizing those that closely resemble the original image. The super-pixels with the highest positive weights are highlighted as the explanation for the prediction.

Additionally, LIME can be employed in other domains, such as price prediction models. In this case, LIME produces explanations that detail how recommended prices are derived from various features, showcasing the contributions of each feature to the predicted price.

However, LIME has potential limitations. Trust is crucial for effective human-ML interaction, and providing explanations for individual predictions is key to building that trust. While LIME is noted for its ease of implementation and computational efficiency, it primarily relies on linear models to approximate local behavior. This linearity may hold true only for small data regions and could lead to inaccuracies when analyzing larger areas.
