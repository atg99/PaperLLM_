The paper by R. Dwivedi et al. addresses the critical importance of explainable artificial intelligence (XAI) in sectors like healthcare, finance, and military, where inaccurate AI predictions can lead to severe consequences. It highlights the challenge of understanding complex AI models, particularly deep neural networks (DNNs), due to their "black box" nature and the difficulty users face in interpreting vast amounts of data. The authors emphasize that high model accuracy alone is insufficient for trust and deployment in real-world applications, as biases can affect predictions and model performance metrics may not accurately reflect true decision-making.

The survey presents a comprehensive analysis of XAI approaches and tools, noting the growing attention XAI has received across various domains. It identifies the need for a comparative analysis to guide stakeholders in the development of XAI-enabled applications. The paper outlines its structure, comparing the stages of building traditional machine learning (ML) models with those of XAI models. It discusses the necessity of XAI in AI applications and provides a taxonomy of XAI techniques, focusing on data and model explainability.

Subsequent sections cover essential aspects of XAI, including data visualization, dimensionality techniques, and model explainability methods, along with relevant software libraries. It also explores feature-based and example-based XAI techniques, and reviews open-source and commercial toolkits for XAI model development. The paper concludes by analyzing implementation requirements for XAI, aiming to support fairness and accountability in AI systems.
