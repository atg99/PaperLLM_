The paper discusses the importance of Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI) in ensuring transparency and accountability in AI decision-making processes. It highlights that the quality of data used in AI systems is crucial for explainability, as biased data can lead to skewed predictions. The necessity for explainability varies depending on the context; for instance, consumers may not require explanations for shopping recommendations, whereas high-stakes decisions like credit risk profiling or medical diagnoses demand thorough explanations due to their significant consequences.

The survey reviews various XAI techniques, including inherently interpretable models like linear models and decision trees, as well as feature-based methods such as LIME and SHAP. It emphasizes the importance of these techniques in enhancing the understanding of AI systems, particularly in applications like healthcare and livestock management, while also addressing concerns about user privacy and fairness.

The paper aims to provide structured insights for researchers in the XAI field, suggesting that the future of XAI may involve distributed AI systems that prioritize data storage across multiple nodes, thus enhancing privacy and transparency. Overall, it underscores the critical role of explainability in the responsible deployment of AI technologies.
