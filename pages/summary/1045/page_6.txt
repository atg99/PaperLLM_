The paper discusses Explainable AI (XAI), emphasizing its core ideas, techniques, and solutions, particularly in the context of its application in different phases of model deployment. It identifies two main phases: the Explaining Phase, which occurs after model deployment, and the Understanding Phase, which takes place before deployment. Key use cases of XAI during these phases include detecting bias, building robust models, justifying decisions, and enhancing AI models.

The paper highlights the importance of explainability in decision-making processes, illustrating how XAI can elucidate the reasoning behind AI decisions, particularly in instances of discrimination within datasets. For example, it references a 2015 incident where Google Images returned a disproportionately low representation of women when searching for "CEO," showcasing how XAI can identify and address such biases.

Furthermore, the paper discusses the necessity of justifiability in AI systems, particularly for legal compliance, where users impacted by AI decisions, such as loan rejections, require explanations for those decisions.

The taxonomy of XAI techniques is also explored, differentiating between white-box and black-box models. White-box models are characterized by their transparency and simplicity, allowing for straightforward interpretation of their outcomes, while black-box models, such as neural networks, are more complex and less interpretable. The trade-off between model complexity and interpretability is noted, as more complex models may offer higher accuracy but at the cost of transparency.

In summary, the paper emphasizes the critical role of XAI in enhancing understanding, justifiability, and accountability in AI systems, while providing a structured analysis of various interpretability techniques.
