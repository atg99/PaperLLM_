The paper discusses the interaction between neural and symbolic approaches within a neuro-symbolic (NS) system, illustrated through a clinical prediction example for Streptococcus pyogenes infection. It describes how a neural network model, trained on diverse patient data, utilizes a symbolic knowledge base (KB) represented as a formal ontology to enhance predictions by including infection subtypes. The model's output refines the KB, allowing for causal analysis and the generation of human-understandable explanations for its decisions. The system supports further inquiries about patient risks and conditions related to S. pyogenes. However, many NS systems still rely on non-logical methods, highlighting the need for a stronger focus on logical aspects to improve explainability. The integration of neural and symbolic approaches combines learning from experience and reasoning, which is essential for developing explainable AI systems that align with human cognitive processes. Additionally, the paper emphasizes the importance of model performance evaluation metrics in the data science lifecycle, which aids in selecting optimal models and adjusting hyper-parameters.
