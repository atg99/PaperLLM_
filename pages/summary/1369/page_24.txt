The references listed provide a comprehensive overview of studies related to social anxiety, personality in robots, and the impact of personality on employee retention, among other topics. Kolbeck's dissertation explores the psychometric differentiation between social anxieties and deficits using both clinical and non-clinical samples. Krämer et al. developed a scale to measure the need to belong and its influence on media usage, presented at an international conference. Krohne et al. investigated a German version of the Positive and Negative Affect Schedule, providing insights into its diagnostic utility. 

Lee et al. examined personality recognition in human-robot interaction, highlighting social responses and presence. Leuwerink's work discusses the interaction of a robot with human groups, emphasizing the importance of personality in these exchanges. Li et al. focused on personality's role in employee retention, suggesting that certain traits are crucial for maintaining productivity. Lohse et al. conducted a video study evaluating extroverted and introverted behaviors in domestic robots, while Lombard et al. validated the Temple Presence Inventory in gaming contexts, contributing to the understanding of telepresence. These studies collectively advance the fields of psychology, robotics, and communication.
