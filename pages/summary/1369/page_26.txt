The references listed pertain to various studies and papers focused on human-robot interaction, perception of robots, and the psychological aspects influencing these interactions. Key studies include Mitsunaga et al.'s exploration of factors that lead to acceptance of robots in social settings, Mori et al.'s evaluation of impressions regarding hug dolls, and Moshkina and Arkin's longitudinal study on affective robotic behavior. 

Muir's dissertation examines operators' trust in automatic controllers, while Murray reviews research related to the Myers-Briggs Type Indicator. Nass and Lee investigate the perception of personality in computer-synthesized speech, and Neave et al. discuss anthropomorphic tendencies and hoarding behaviors. Neyer et al. contribute a validated scale for measuring technology commitment, and Niculescu et al. analyze how voice pitch, humor, and empathy can enhance the attractiveness of social robots. Lastly, Nomura et al. investigate the impact of negative attitudes towards robots on human-robot interactions. 

These works collectively contribute to understanding the nuances of human-robot relationships and the factors that affect user acceptance and interaction quality.
