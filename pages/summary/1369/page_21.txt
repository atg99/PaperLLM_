The references listed encompass a range of studies and theories related to human-robot interaction, personality perception, and technology acceptance. Key contributions include Hall and Lindzey's foundational work on personality theories, Haring et al.'s investigations into trust and perception towards lifelike robots, and Heerink et al.'s assessment of acceptance of social agent technology among older adults. The uncanny valley theory is revisited by Ho and MacDorman, offering alternative measures for evaluating human-robot interactions. Additionally, Hoffman et al. explore the impact of robotic responsiveness on social impressions and the effects of robotic companionship on music enjoyment. Collectively, these works highlight the complexities of human perceptions of robots and the factors influencing their acceptance and interaction with technology.
