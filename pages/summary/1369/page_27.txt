The references provided focus on various aspects of human-robot interaction, particularly exploring psychological factors such as anxiety and attitudes towards robots. Nomura et al. (2008) investigate the prediction of human behavior in this context by utilizing psychological scales. Their earlier work (2007) examines the relationship between anxiety, negative attitudes, and the acceptable distance from robots. Ogawa et al. (2009) discuss the persuasive capabilities of androids, while Park et al. (2012) introduce the concept of attraction in human-robot interactions. Further studies by Park et al. (2011) compare children's eye movements when observing robots versus humans. Paunonen and Ashton (2001) delve into personality traits and their predictive power regarding behavior, which is echoed by Peeters et al. (2006) in their meta-analysis on personality and team performance. Powers and Kiesler (2006) analyze how a robot's physical attributes influence people's mental models. Finally, Rammstedt and John (2005) present a short version of the Big Five Inventory, relevant for assessing personality traits in these studies. The text highlights the intersection of psychology and robotics, emphasizing how human perceptions can shape interactions with robotic entities.
