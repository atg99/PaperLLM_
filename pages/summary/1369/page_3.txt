The paper "A Review of Personality in Human–Robot Interactions" explores the role of personality traits in the dynamics of interactions between humans and robots. The authors, affiliated with the University of Michigan and HEC Paris, analyze how personality influences user perceptions, acceptance, and overall experiences with robots. They discuss various frameworks and models of personality, such as the Big Five personality traits, and how these can be applied to the design and programming of robots to enhance user engagement and satisfaction.

Key findings include the importance of aligning robot personalities with user expectations and the potential for personality-driven interactions to improve teamwork and collaboration in human-robot scenarios. The paper emphasizes that understanding personality can lead to more effective communication strategies and improve the social acceptance of robots in various settings, from personal assistance to industrial applications.

The authors argue for further research into personality dimensions in human-robot interaction, suggesting that tailored interactions could lead to more intuitive and beneficial relationships between humans and robots. Overall, the review highlights the significance of personality as a critical factor in the design and implementation of robotic systems.
