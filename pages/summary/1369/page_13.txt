This paper examines the critical role of personality in enhancing human-robot interaction (HRI), particularly focusing on embodied physical action (EPA) robots, which elicit strong emotional responses from users. As robots become more integrated into society, understanding the factors that influence HRI, such as personality, is increasingly important. The review synthesizes existing literature on personality in HRI, presenting an integrated conceptual model and identifying four key areas of focus: human personality in HRI, robot personality in HRI, the interplay between the two, and factors that impact robot personality.

The paper outlines the current state of research, highlights significant insights, and identifies gaps that future studies should address. It is structured into sections that cover the relevant literature and discuss each thrust area in detail. The final section emphasizes future research opportunities in personality and HRI, underscoring the importance of personality in facilitating effective interactions as robots continue to replace and complement human roles in various settings.
