The references listed provide a comprehensive overview of various studies and contributions related to human-robot interaction, personality factors, and the implications of these interactions in different contexts. Key studies include Chidambaram et al.'s exploration of persuasive robots utilizing vocal and nonverbal cues, Connell and Schau's symbiosis model of identity augmentation, and Conti et al.'s analysis of personality factors affecting the acceptability of socially assistive robotics among teachers. The NEO personality inventory by Costa and McCrae offers insights into normal personality assessment in clinical settings. Other notable works investigate the influence of user personality on task execution in robotic reminders, the teaching of nutrition through robotics, and the evolution of attitudes toward social robots among the elderly, emphasizing the interplay between personality traits and beliefs about robot functionality. Collectively, these references highlight the significance of personality in shaping interactions with robots and the potential for these technologies to affect various aspects of human life.
