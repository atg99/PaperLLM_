The introduction discusses the significance of personality traits as key predictors of individual behavior, emphasizing their role in understanding human interactions. Personality is recognized as a central construct influencing responses in social contexts, thereby affecting the quality of interpersonal interactions. The Big Five personality traits, often referred to by the acronym OCEAN—openness to experience, conscientiousness, extraversion, agreeableness, and neuroticism—are highlighted for their widespread acceptance in various disciplines, including sociology, psychology, and organizational behavior.

Each of the Big Five traits is defined: openness relates to imagination and curiosity; conscientiousness involves carefulness and self-awareness; extraversion pertains to sociability; agreeableness reflects cooperativeness; and neuroticism indicates emotional instability. The introduction notes that while the Big Five traits are extensively studied in social sciences, their application in human-robot interaction (HRI) research remains underdeveloped and fragmented. This lack of a coherent framework hampers the understanding of personality's role in HRI, highlighting the need for further research in this area.
