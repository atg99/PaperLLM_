The references provided encompass a range of studies focusing on socially interactive robots, human-robot interaction, and personality assessments. Fong et al. (2003) conducted a survey on socially interactive robots, highlighting their applications and implications. Gockley and Matarić (2006) explored the use of mobile robots to encourage compliance in physical therapy. Goetz and Kiesler (2002) examined cooperation dynamics when individuals interact with robotic assistants.

Goldberg's work (1990, 1992, 1999) contributed significantly to the understanding of personality through the development of the Big-Five factor structure and associated personality inventories. Gosling et al. (2003) provided a brief measure for the Big-Five personality domains, enhancing the assessment of personality traits in various contexts.

Groom et al. (2010, 2009) investigated human perceptions of robots in terms of blame attribution and the impact of robot form on operators’ identities. Gu et al. (2015) conducted an empirical study on the influence of a robot's personality in different contexts. Lastly, Guay et al. (2003) focused on the hierarchical structure of self-determined motivation, testing various effects within motivational frameworks. The full text of the overarching study is accessible online.
