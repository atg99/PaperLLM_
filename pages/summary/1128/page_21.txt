The paper presents an analysis of simulation environments and the application of swarm intelligence (SI) algorithms in wireless sensor networks (WSNs) and unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) from 2017 to 2019. It highlights several issues in the current literature, including the lack of clarity on simulation platforms, limited comparisons to existing methods, and the narrow scope of experimental scenarios. The majority of studies utilize Matlab or Castalia as simulation frameworks.

A statistical overview reveals that Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) is the most widely used SI algorithm in wireless networks, significantly outpacing Ant Colony Optimization (ACO) and Artificial Bee Colony (ABC) in application frequency. PSO's effectiveness stems from its simple parameters and rapid convergence, making it suitable for various optimization problems, including function optimization and neural network training. In UAV applications, PSO aids in path planning and positional design to avoid collisions.

The analysis indicates that SI algorithms are primarily employed in WSNs for cluster head (CH) selection (41%) and routing protocols (47%). PSO excels in CH selection and routing, while ACO is favored for routing due to its adaptive optimization capabilities. ABC is also extensively applied to CH selection and sensor deployment.

The paper identifies common challenges faced by traditional SI algorithms, such as PSO's tendency to converge prematurely and ACO's slow convergence rate. To address these issues, many studies propose improved parameter selection and combinations of SI algorithms with other techniques, including machine learning, to enhance performance.

Overall, the findings emphasize the need for more comprehensive experimental designs and comparisons in future research to advance the application of SI algorithms in WSNs and UAVs.
