The paper discusses various routing algorithms for Wireless Sensor Networks (WSNs), highlighting the use of different optimization techniques. Reference [27] introduces a multi-path routing method utilizing the FABC and EACO algorithms, where FABC identifies the Cluster Head (CH) and EACO facilitates multi-path discovery. This approach shows improved energy consumption and goodput, though its advantages diminish as node count increases. Reference [32] presents an enhanced energy-saving routing algorithm based on ACO that factors in location and search direction, proving effective in scenarios with greater distances between the sink and target area, with a slower rise in energy consumption over time.

Reference [33] outlines a secure routing protocol, SRPMA, which employs a multi-objective ACO strategy integrating Pareto optimization to enhance routing based on node energy and trust values, demonstrating better performance against black hole attacks compared to other ACO-based protocols. Reference [35] proposes the CB-RACO protocol for large-scale WSNs, combining ACO and Linear Programming (LP) to maintain high data transmission while minimizing energy use. This protocol ensures autonomous network operation during overloads and achieves high data delivery rates.

The paper emphasizes that WSN routing protocols must be simple, efficient, and energy-conserving, with the ability to adapt to node changes and ensure rapid convergence. Most existing algorithms are distributed, focusing on energy efficiency, while security and quality of service (QoS) considerations are less common. Popular optimization techniques like Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) and ACO are noted for their effectiveness in routing and multipath communication. Key factors for WSN routing protocols include energy conservation, average end-to-end delay, packet loss rate, fault tolerance, and security.
