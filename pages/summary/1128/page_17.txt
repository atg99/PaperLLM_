UAV-aided wireless networks. The paper emphasizes the need to optimize flight time, energy consumption, and operational risks of UAVs while adapting to dynamic environmental conditions. In contrast to static Wireless Sensor Networks (WSNs), mobile WSNs face challenges such as dynamic topologies and power limitations, with the mobile aggregation scheduling problem being particularly critical.

A BFO-based cooperative mobile sensing scheme is introduced, utilizing UAVs as mobile sinks. This method simulates the movement of Escherichia coli to enhance self-organization and adaptive movement of UAVs, resulting in reduced energy consumption, lower delays, and improved network throughput and coverage.

The paper also outlines key parameters for evaluating the performance of swarm intelligence applications in WSNs, including energy consumption, survival time, and throughput. A summary table categorizes various studies based on these parameters, highlighting that most literature focuses on energy consumption while often neglecting a comprehensive approach to multiple performance factors.

Finally, the application of swarm intelligence algorithms in UAV-aided wireless networks is categorized into three main scenarios: using UAVs as temporary base stations during network congestion, employing UAVs as relays for distant users, and utilizing UAVs for sensory data collection. These applications present specific challenges that swarm intelligence can address effectively.
