This paper surveys the state-of-the-art swarm intelligence (SI) algorithms used in Internet of Things (IoT) applications and outlines potential future directions for development. The organization of the paper is as follows: Section 2 introduces the principles of SI algorithms, while Section 3 analyzes and compares various applications of SI algorithms in wireless sensor networks (WSNs). Section 4 discusses the application of SI in UAV-aided wireless networks, and Section 5 summarizes the use of SI algorithms across other IoT domains. Section 6 focuses on the verification scenarios and platforms utilized, followed by Section 7, which examines the current state of SI algorithms in wireless networks and anticipates future developments. The conclusion is presented in Section 8.

The paper details the evolution of SI algorithms, which were designed to understand how simple individual behaviors can lead to complex swarm optimization through cooperation and information exchange. The historical development of SI algorithms began in the 1990s with notable examples such as Ant Colony Optimization (ACO) and Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO). Subsequent advancements from 2000 to 2010 included Bacterial Foraging Optimization (BFO), Artificial Fish-Swarm Algorithm, and others, with newer algorithms like Pigeon-Inspired Optimization, Grey Wolf Optimizer, and Butterfly Optimization Algorithm emerging after 2010.

In particular, the PSO algorithm, proposed by Kennedy and Eberhart, simulates swarm behavior during foraging, where particles representing potential solutions interact based on rules that promote cohesion and collision avoidance. Each particle's movement is influenced by its own experience and that of its neighbors, contributing to the collective search for optimal solutions.

The paper presents over 40 applications of SI in IoT, compares 11 SI-based cluster head selection algorithms and 13 routing protocols in WSNs, and discusses the validation platforms and parameter selections utilized in these methods, highlighting their advantages and limitations.
