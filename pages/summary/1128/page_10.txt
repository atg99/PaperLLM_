The paper discusses various algorithms for cluster head (CH) selection in wireless sensor networks (WSNs) with a focus on improving energy efficiency and network performance. The proposed EACO algorithm demonstrates superior performance in energy consumption and goodput during multi-path path discovery, particularly when adapted to the IEEE 802.15.4 protocol, although it reveals uneven CH distribution.

The LEACH-SF protocol, utilizing a Sugeno fuzzy reasoning system and adaptive fuzzy rules through the artificial bee colony (ABC) algorithm, outperforms existing fuzzy-based clustering methods by minimizing intra-cluster distances, maximizing network longevity, and increasing the number of packets received by the base station (BS). It is also suitable for mobile sensor nodes and multi-hop routing in heterogeneous networks.

An improved ABC, termed iABC, enhances initialization and solution searching using a Student’s t distribution and differential evolution, respectively. This method results in reduced energy consumption and improved packet delivery with minimal end-to-end delay, even with the BS positioned outside the network field.

The paper outlines two methods for CH selection: dividing clusters before selecting the CH or choosing the initial CH based on cluster center. It emphasizes the importance of CH rotation based on energy levels to prevent rapid depletion and suggests that the first method is more flexible. The discussion also highlights the need for energy balancing strategies, such as using mobile sinks, to address energy consumption disparities among member nodes close to the CH.

Furthermore, it notes that the performance of SI-based CH selection algorithms heavily relies on the chosen SI algorithm's effectiveness. Combining different algorithms, such as particle swarm optimization (PSO), can mitigate local optimum pitfalls and enhance overall analytic performance.
