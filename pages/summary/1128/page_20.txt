The paper discusses a novel Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO)-based shortcut addition algorithm for multi-sink Internet of Things (IoT) networks, referred to as SAPS. This approach treats each cluster as a single aggregate network, optimizing the average path length and load distribution to sink nodes through a fitness function. The algorithm incorporates Genetic Algorithms (GA) for updating particle positions via crossover and mutation, leading to improved network performance in terms of shorter average path lengths and stable load balancing among sink nodes.

The paper further analyzes verification methods used in Swarm Intelligence (SI)-based Wireless Sensor Networks (WSN) and identifies several key issues. A summary of simulation platforms reveals that a significant portion of studies (32.4%) do not specify their simulation environment, making experimental replication challenging. Most experiments rely on MATLAB, with limited comparisons to advanced existing methodologies, and often only simulate a single condition, complicating the assessment of algorithm efficacy across varied scenarios.

Lastly, the current state and development trends in SI algorithms such as PSO, Ant Colony Optimization (ACO), and Artificial Bee Colony (ABC) within wireless networks from 2017 to 2019 are examined. The analysis highlights the growing use of these algorithms in WSN applications, as evidenced by statistical data derived from Scopus, indicating a continued interest and potential for innovation in this area.
