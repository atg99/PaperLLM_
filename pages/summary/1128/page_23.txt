The paper discusses Quality of Service (QoS) in Wireless Sensor Networks (WSNs), emphasizing the need for improved frameworks tailored to the unique challenges of WSNs. Traditional QoS systems are inadequate due to the specific requirements of sensor technology and distributed processing. Key considerations for enhancing QoS include energy conservation, scalability at the physical layer, and network-wide energy balance at the network layer. The literature on QoS in WSNs is limited, indicating a research gap.

Furthermore, the paper explores Intelligent Mobile Edge Computing, which addresses the limitations of centralized cloud computing by enabling edge devices to perform data collection, analysis, and decision-making. This approach aims to reduce latency, bandwidth usage, and enhance security. Specific methods proposed to improve QoS include utilizing drones for real-time data analysis and optimizing data transfer by connecting only essential information to the cloud.

The emergence of 5G networks represents another significant development, introducing innovations such as micro-base stations and device-to-device communication. The paper highlights potential research directions involving swarm intelligence (SI) algorithms to tackle challenges in massive MIMO systems and network slicing technology. SI algorithms, particularly Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) and Artificial Bee Colony (ABC), are proposed as effective solutions for optimizing resource allocation and addressing migration issues in 5G networks, especially under conditions of high user demand and low latency requirements.
