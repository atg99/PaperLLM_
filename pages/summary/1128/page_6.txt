The paper discusses the calculation formulas and parameters used in the Ant Colony Optimization (ACO) algorithm and introduces the Artificial Bee Colony (ABC) algorithm. 

For the ACO algorithm, the transition probability Pk_ij(t) is calculated based on pheromone levels and heuristic factors, with specific formulas provided for each calculation. The pheromone on an edge is updated over time, considering evaporation and increments based on ant movements. Key parameters include the relative importance of the heuristic factor, the collection of cities available for selection by the ant, and the pheromone levels on edges between cities.

In contrast, the ABC algorithm mimics the foraging behavior of bees, categorizing them into employed bees, onlookers, and scouts. Each bee type plays a distinct role in exploring food sources, representing potential solutions. Employed and onlooker bees work together to optimize the search for solutions, while scouts are responsible for avoiding local optima by exploring new areas. The quality of each food source correlates with the solution's effectiveness.

Overall, both algorithms highlight the significance of collaborative search strategies in optimization problems.
