The paper discusses the role of swarm intelligence (SI) algorithms in solving complex nonlinear optimization problems, emphasizing their self-adaptability, robustness, and search capabilities. Various SI-inspired algorithms, including classical particle swarm optimization (PSO), ant colony optimization (ACO), artificial bee colony (ABC), bacterial foraging algorithm (BFO), and butterfly optimization algorithm (BOA), are highlighted for their heuristic approaches to optimization across dynamic and multi-objective problems, particularly within the context of the Internet of Things (IoT).

The IoT, powered by technologies like RFID, sensor networks, and cloud computing, facilitates connectivity between physical objects for data exchange and decision-making. However, challenges such as security, energy consumption, and system complexity hinder its development. SI algorithms can effectively model IoT systems by treating them as groups of simple devices, enabling global optimization and enhancing data analysis through cloud computing.

The paper also addresses wireless sensor networks (WSN) as a critical component of IoT, which faces limitations in battery capacity and sensing capabilities. SI algorithms, inspired by the foraging behavior of insects, are suitable for routing protocols in WSN, particularly in the context of cluster head (CH) selection, which is classified as an NP-hard problem. The adaptability and multi-objective optimization capabilities of SI make it a valuable tool for addressing these challenges in WSNs. The paper categorizes IoT applications into four key areas, reflecting current research trends and technological advancements.
