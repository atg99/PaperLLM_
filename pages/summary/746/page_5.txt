This paper presents an exact algorithm for the 1.5-entmax function, which lies between softmax and sparsemax. The algorithm (Algorithm 2) computes 1.5-entmax efficiently by sorting the input vector z and applying a series of mathematical operations to derive the optimal output distribution p⋆. The complexity of the algorithm is O(d log d) due to sorting, but practical implementations can leverage partial sorting to improve efficiency, particularly on GPUs.

The paper also discusses the gradient computation for the entmax mapping, which is crucial for using entmax in attention mechanisms. Proposition 1 outlines how to derive the gradient of the α-entmax function, highlighting that the Jacobians for softmax and sparsemax (when α is 1 and 2, respectively) can be recovered from this expression. The gradient for α-entmax with α not equal to 2 is shown to be more informative compared to sparsemax, as it depends on the values of p⋆.

The experimental section applies the proposed algorithm and gradient computation to two NLP tasks: morphological inflection and machine translation. These tasks are chosen to demonstrate the versatility of the entmax function in various contexts, with morphological inflection requiring exact sequence predictions and machine translation dealing with a larger vocabulary and ambiguity. The architecture and training procedures are kept consistent across experiments, using LSTM layers and standard optimization techniques.

Overall, the paper establishes the computational foundations for utilizing entmax in NLP applications and presents experimental evidence of its effectiveness across different tasks.
