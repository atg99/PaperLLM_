The paper introduces a novel approach to sequence-to-sequence (seq2seq) models by proposing an entmax sparse output layer and a corresponding natural loss function. This method enhances the accuracy of predictions in large-vocabulary settings by preventing the allocation of probability mass to unlikely outputs. The authors demonstrate that, for tasks with minimal output ambiguity, using entmax losses combined with beam search can yield exact finite sets of outputs.

Key contributions include the construction of entmax sparse attention, which improves the model's interpretability without sacrificing accuracy. The paper also presents a straightforward form for the entmax gradient, establishing a connection between softmax and sparsemax. Additionally, the authors develop an efficient algorithm for the 1.5-entmax case that approaches the processing speed of softmax on GPUs, even with large vocabularies.

The experiments are conducted on two tasks: one focusing on character-level morphological inflection generation with low ambiguity, and the other on word-level neural machine translation with higher ambiguity. Results indicate significant improvements in both accuracy and interpretability due to the proposed methods.

The underlying architecture utilized is an RNN-based seq2seq model with global attention and input feeding, which is briefly described in the paper. The encoder processes input tokens through layered bidirectional LSTMs, generating encoder states, while the decoder produces output tokens using an attention mechanism to compute probability distributions for the next word based on the current decoder state and the encoder outputs.
