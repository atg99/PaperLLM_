The paper discusses the architecture of a sequence-to-sequence model that uses an LSTM decoder to generate predictions based on previous outputs. The key components include the contextual output, which is a non-linear combination of various inputs, and the softmax transformation that converts a vector of scores into a probability distribution for predicting the next word. The model is trained to maximize the likelihood of target sentences, minimizing the negative log likelihood loss.

A significant aspect of the architecture is the softmax function, which serves dual roles in computing normalized attention weights and the predictive probability distribution. However, the authors note that softmax never assigns a zero probability to any word, which can lead to inefficiencies in resource allocation for less relevant words.

To address this, the authors introduce the sparsemax mapping, an alternative to softmax that yields sparse probability distributions by projecting onto the simplex. Sparsemax assigns zero probability to low-scoring options and is accompanied by a new loss function that is smooth and convex. The gradient of this loss function is necessary for training.

The paper also explores the relationship between softmax and sparsemax through entropy interpretations. It proposes a new family of mappings and losses, termed entmax, which generalizes the softmax and sparsemax functions using Tsallis entropies. This approach aims to create a flexible framework for generating sparse outputs and attention mechanisms in sequence-to-sequence models.
