The paper presents results from a study on morphological inflection and machine translation, utilizing data from the CoNLL–SIGMORPHON 2018 shared task. The research focuses on generating inflected word forms based on lemmas and morphological tags, utilizing multilingual training across various languages. Two data settings are employed: high, with approximately 10,000 samples per language across 86 languages, and medium, with around 1,000 samples across 102 languages.

Key findings include the implementation of a sequence-to-sequence model that replaces the traditional softmax with the entmax function, leading to improved accuracy. The results, shown in Table 1, indicate that the best models achieved higher word accuracy than all submissions in the 2018 shared task, apart from UZH, which used a more complex approach. Notably, the entmax models exhibited sparsity in predictions, concentrating probability mass into single predictions for a significant portion of validation samples. This property enhances interpretability and allows for optimal beam search decoding.

The paper also explores machine translation across three language pairs, highlighting the different challenges posed by larger vocabulary sizes and increased ambiguity. Overall, the study demonstrates the effectiveness of multilingual training and the advantages of the entmax function in enhancing model performance in both morphological inflection and machine translation tasks.
