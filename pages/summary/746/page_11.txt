This paper discusses Tsallis entropies and Fenchel-Young losses, focusing on their mathematical properties and applications in probabilistic prediction functions. 

The Tsallis family of entropies, defined for α≥1, is shown to be continuous in α, with the limit as α approaches 1 equating to the standard Shannon entropy. The paper provides a proof of this continuity by rewriting the Tsallis entropy in a separable form and applying l'Hôpital's rule to resolve indeterminate forms.

The section on Fenchel-Young losses introduces the concept of a probabilistic prediction function that is regularized by a strictly convex function, denoted as Ω. The prediction function aims to maximize a given objective, and the corresponding Fenchel-Young loss is defined based on ground-truth labels and prediction scores. Key properties of these losses include non-negativity, zero loss for correct predictions, convexity, differentiability, and temperature scaling.

The paper also characterizes the solution to the prediction function, providing the optimality conditions necessary for algorithm development and deriving the Jacobian of the mapping. The Lagrangian formulation and the associated subgradient conditions are detailed, highlighting the relationships between the variables involved in the maximization problem. 

Overall, the paper establishes foundational concepts that are essential for understanding the construction of α-entmax and its associated loss function, contributing to the broader field of probabilistic modeling.
