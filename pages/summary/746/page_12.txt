The paper explores the connections between softmax and sparsemax functions, particularly in the context of generalized sparse attention mappings within neural networks. It establishes that the softmax function arises from a specific form of the optimization problem, where the probability distribution \( p_j \) is derived from the expression involving the log-probabilities \( z_j \) and a normalization constant \( Z \). For sparsemax, the paper highlights that the entropy function \( \Omega(p) \) leads to a different optimization criterion, focusing on sparsity in the resulting probability distribution.

A significant contribution of the paper is the derivation of a new expression for the Jacobian of generalized sparse mappings \( \pi_{\Omega} \). This is crucial for propagating error information during the training of neural networks using gradient methods. The paper offers a two-step proof: first, it establishes that Jacobians are zero outside the support of the solution, and second, it characterizes the Jacobian within the support.

The lemma presented shows that for a strongly convex function \( \Omega \), the Jacobian \( \frac{\partial \pi_{\Omega}}{\partial z} \) is symmetric and zero when either entry of the mapping is zero. This is derived using Danskin’s theorem, which aids in expressing \( \pi_{\Omega}(z) \) in terms of the gradient of the conjugate function \( \Omega^* \). The paper concludes with a proposition that further characterizes the Jacobian based on the properties of the second derivatives of \( \Omega \).

Overall, the work provides insights into the mathematical foundations of sparse attention mechanisms and their implications for efficient computation in neural networks.
