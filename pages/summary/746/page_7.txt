The paper presents a comparative analysis of three models for machine translation: softmax, sparsemax, and the proposed 1.5-entmax, evaluating their performance as both attention mechanisms and loss functions. The results, summarized in Table 2, indicate that the 1.5-entmax configuration consistently yields the highest BLEU scores across various language pairs, suggesting it strikes an optimal balance between softmax and sparsemax.

Training utilized byte pair encoding with different segmentation strategies depending on the language pair, and validation steps were set according to dataset sizes. The findings show a small positive effect on accuracy when transitioning from softmax to sparser attention mechanisms, while the choice of loss function significantly impacts performance, with an optimal value around α=1.33 for the loss function. The paper suggests that interpolating between softmax and sparsemax can harness the advantages of both, confirming the efficacy of the 1.5-entmax approach.

Sparsity analysis, presented in Table 3, reveals that while 1.5-entmax is less sparse than sparsemax, it maintains a manageable level of interpretability. The average number of nonzero outputs indicates that 1.5-entmax retains a meaningful set of target types, facilitating more intuitive interpretations in predictive translation systems.

Notably, the computational efficiency of the proposed method is emphasized, with 1.5-entmax operating at speeds comparable to softmax on GPU, while the bisection method for other α values remains practical even for large vocabularies. The paper concludes that the 1.5-entmax method offers a promising direction for enhancing machine translation performance without incurring significant computational costs.
