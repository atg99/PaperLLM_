The authors introduce the α-entmax family of transformations, which generalizes softmax and sparsemax, allowing for sparse outputs for any α greater than one. They provide efficient algorithms for computing these transformations and their gradients, making them suitable for large vocabularies.

The experiments conducted on tasks such as morphological inflection and machine translation demonstrate that the proposed sparse sequence-to-sequence models consistently outperform their dense counterparts. The results indicate that these models can yield precise outputs and improve overall performance by focusing on a smaller set of plausible candidates during the decoding process.

In summary, the paper presents a significant advancement in sequence-to-sequence modeling by integrating sparsity into attention and output mechanisms, leading to enhanced interpretability and performance in various NLP applications.
