This paper presents an exact, sorting-based algorithm for computing the 1.5-entmax function, focusing on deriving an algorithm that efficiently handles the threshold determination based on the support size. The main insight is that the solution can be characterized by the size of its support, denoted as ρ. The authors propose to enumerate all possible values of ρ from 1 to d until all optimality conditions are satisfied.

The paper introduces Proposition 3, which outlines the exact computation of the 1.5-entmax function. It defines specific metrics such as the top-ρ mean, unnormalized variance, and the induced threshold as functions of ρ. The relationship between these metrics is crucial for determining the threshold τ, which is derived from the sorted coordinates of the input vector z. The proposition establishes that the 1.5-entmax for each component can be expressed in terms of τ and the sorted values, provided that τ lies within a specific range.

A key lemma demonstrates that the threshold τ is non-decreasing and finite for a maximum support size ρmax, beyond which it becomes infinite. The proof of Proposition 3 simplifies the problem of computing the 1.5-entmax to a maximization problem involving the input vector z. By applying stationarity conditions and dual feasibility, the authors derive a quadratic equation for τ, which includes a discriminant that indicates the existence of a valid solution based on the variance of the input.

The analysis concludes that if the variance exceeds a certain threshold, the corresponding values of τ can be determined, leading to unique solutions that satisfy the conditions laid out. The findings contribute to the understanding and computation of the 1.5-entmax function, establishing a framework for further exploration and application.
