The paper discusses the Generalized Scalable Optimal Sparse Decision Trees (GOSDT) method, which enhances the search process within the Decision Boundary Partitioning (DBP) algorithm. GOSDT utilizes a bound that effectively reduces the number of similar trees in the search space by focusing on a single representative tree. It considers weighted, additive, non-negative loss functions and defines the maximum weighted loss.

The Incremental Similar Support Bound is introduced, which allows for a comparison between two trees differing only by their root nodes. This bound shows that the differences in the objective values of child trees are constrained by the maximum weighted loss and a measure of uncertainty in support. This approach allows for efficient computation, especially with continuous variables, as previously computed splits can be reused.

Furthermore, GOSDT implements asynchronous bound updates, enabling the computation of hierarchical objectives without waiting for complete solutions to child problems. This non-blocking computation allows GOSDT to focus on promising branches more efficiently.

The paper also describes the Fast Selective Vector Sums technique, which uses precomputed prefix sums to speed up calculations of sums over subsets of elements in a vector. This optimization is particularly useful when assessing support sets for input features.

Finally, the GOSDT algorithm is presented, which constructs and optimizes a dependency graph, allowing for the extraction of the optimal decision tree through a greedy selection of the split with the lowest objective value. Overall, GOSDT offers significant improvements in efficiency and effectiveness for constructing sparse decision trees.
