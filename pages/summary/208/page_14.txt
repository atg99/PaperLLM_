The paper discusses the development of a UAV navigation model that incorporates deep reinforcement learning techniques for effective path planning while ensuring self-safety in the presence of enemy threats, such as radar detection and surface-to-air missiles. Utilizing the STAGE Scenario software tool, the model simulates an environment with enemy entities, allowing for detailed situation assessments based on the positions of UAVs and enemy units.

The research employs a Dueling Double Deep Q-Networks (D3QN) algorithm to train the UAV for optimal navigation. The training process involves initializing network parameters, generating situation maps, selecting and executing actions based on policy, and updating the situation maps with new data. This approach enables the UAV to learn from its interactions within the environment through a trial-and-error method.

The paper also categorizes reinforcement learning methods into discrete and continuous action spaces, highlighting the advantages and limitations of various approaches, including supervised and unsupervised learning. A new algorithm, Double Dueling DQN (D3QN), is introduced to address issues faced by traditional DQN methods, demonstrating improved performance in optimizing state quality. Simulations are conducted using a platform called Airsim, which, despite limitations in path complexity, allows for realistic obstacle environments. RGB and depth sensors are integrated with a CNN to enhance the RL network's inputs, facilitating the generation of optimal movement behaviors for the UAV.
