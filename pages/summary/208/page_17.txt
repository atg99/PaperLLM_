The paper discusses the application of Reinforcement Learning (RL) in Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) for Search and Rescue (SAR) missions. It highlights a significant improvement in the learning process, reducing the number of steps to reach a target from 100 to 8 steps in the final episode. The study details the implementation of a Q-learning algorithm, which helps the UAV navigate an unknown environment to locate a stationary human using onboard sensors and a quadcopter equipped with Ultra-Wide Band technology. 

The Q-learning algorithm operates by maximizing a reward signal, with the UAV's position represented in a discrete grid format. To manage the growing size of the Q-table and enhance practicality, a Fixed Sparse Representation (FSR) technique is employed. The simulation results indicate that the UAV trained with the original Q-learning required approximately 160 episodes to find the optimal route, while the approximated Q-learning achieved this in only 75 episodes. The research includes real-life testing using a Parrot AR Drone 2.0, which mirrored the simulation results in terms of efficiency in reaching the target.

Moreover, the paper addresses challenges posed by dynamic obstacles in realistic outdoor environments, noting that earlier models were limited to static or simple dynamic scenarios. It identifies significant research gaps, particularly regarding the difficulties faced due to frequent collisions and the need for enhanced exploration capabilities in the presence of dynamic obstacles.
