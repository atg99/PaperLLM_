The paper discusses the performance of various reinforcement learning (RL) algorithms in the context of drone flight control tasks. It differentiates between on-policy methods, such as SARSA, PPO, and TRPO, which learn from the policy currently being executed, and off-policy methods like Q-learning, DQN, and DDPG, which evaluate or improve a different policy. 

The study evaluates the step responses for attitude commands using a random simulation and presents results indicating that while all algorithms achieve a steady state, TRPO and DDPG exhibit significant oscillations in roll and yaw, potentially leading to instability during flight. In contrast, PPO emerges as a more accurate attitude controller, making it a preferred choice for training drones in competitive environments like AlphaPilot and Airsim.

Experiments were conducted using PPO, DDPG, and TRPO across various flight tasks, including hovering, landing, random waypoint navigation, and target following. Results show that PPO generally provided consistent performance, particularly in landing and waypoint navigation, while DDPG excelled in smooth hover performance and target following tasks. The paper includes a summary table of model-free RL algorithms, detailing their agent types, policy types, and characteristics regarding action and state spaces.

Overall, the findings highlight the strengths and weaknesses of different deep reinforcement learning algorithms for UAV tasks, emphasizing the effectiveness of PPO in various scenarios while recognizing the unique advantages of DDPG in specific tasks.
