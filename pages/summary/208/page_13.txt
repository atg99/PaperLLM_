The paper discusses the probabilistic nature of navigation in obstacle avoidance as a sequential decision-making problem for Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs). It introduces a model characterized by seven variables: states (S), actions (A), a probability function (P), a reward function (R), observations (W), a conditional probability distribution (O), and a discount factor (d). This model aims to maximize the expected sum of discounted rewards using a deep recurrent Q-network with temporal attention, facilitating effective object avoidance in unfamiliar environments.

The paper references previous research where reinforcement learning has been implemented in UAV position controllers, focusing on optimizing actions based on the vehicle's current position. Additionally, it highlights a study that combines object avoidance with the shortest path and minimum time interval challenges, emphasizing the computational requirements for real-time path planning while avoiding obstacles.

Another significant contribution is the introduction of an interference-aware path planning scheme that minimizes disruptions in wireless transmission while UAVs share mission-related data. This is framed as a dynamic non-cooperative game, employing deep reinforcement learning with echo state networks to achieve subgame perfect Nash equilibrium. The UAVs learn their trajectories in relation to ground networks and other UAVs, adapting their actions based on past rewards rather than continuous environmental observation.

The growing application of deep reinforcement learning in UAV path planning is noted, paving the way for more complex real-time solutions, particularly in dynamic settings. The paper cites a 2019 study that proposed a real-time path planning approach considering global situational awareness, including potential threats, thus enhancing navigation strategies in challenging environments.
