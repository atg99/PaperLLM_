The paper discusses various algorithms used in deep reinforcement learning, focusing on Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (DDPG), and Asynchronous Advantage Actor-Critic (A3C). 

PPO is introduced with a mathematical framework that includes the calculation of advantage estimates and an iterative optimization process. The algorithm involves running a policy for a specified number of time steps, estimating advantages, optimizing an objective function, and updating the policy iteratively. Variations of the PPO algorithm are noted, such as adaptive penalties for performance enhancement and the use of two policies for sample collection.

DDPG is highlighted as a significant algorithm that directly outputs actions rather than probabilities. It employs a target network to improve convergence and utilizes a replay buffer for parameter updates. The paper provides equations detailing the DDPG methodology, including action selection and loss calculations.

A3C is presented as a model-free, on-policy algorithm that supports continuous action and state spaces. It utilizes a global actor-critic network and multiple local networks, where local networks learn from the global one and provide updates based on their gradients.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the adaptability of these algorithms to different problems and their performance in deep reinforcement learning contexts.
