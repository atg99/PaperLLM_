In reinforcement learning, the agent interacts with the environment by taking actions based on its current state and prior experiences. The effectiveness of these actions is evaluated through a reward system, which influences the agent's future decisions. Deep reinforcement learning combines traditional reinforcement learning with deep learning techniques, allowing for more complex decision-making based on accumulated experiences.

There are three primary categories of approaches in deep reinforcement learning: value-based, policy-based, and model-based methods. Value-based methods focus on maximizing a value function over a sequence of actions, while policy-based methods aim to directly optimize the policy that yields the best outcomes. Policy-based methods can be deterministic, applying the same action in a given state, or stochastic, introducing variability based on probabilities. Model-based methods involve either providing the agent with a model of the environment or enabling the agent to learn this model, which can lead to greater efficiency in learning compared to model-free approaches.

Reinforcement learning algorithms are further categorized based on various criteria such as whether they are model-based or model-free, and whether they utilize value-based or policy-based strategies. Despite their differences, these methods share fundamental characteristics of deep reinforcement learning, such as operating within a closed-loop control system and relying on delayed rewards to inform decision-making.
