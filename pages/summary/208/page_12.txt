UAV must continuously analyze its environment and make navigation decisions based on real-time data. The paper discusses various approaches to UAV navigation and obstacle avoidance, highlighting the importance of using sensor data to build environmental maps and update path plans. One method utilizes a depth camera and a novel algorithm combining deep reinforcement learning (DRL) and imitation learning to enhance obstacle avoidance capabilities.

In urban scenarios, the optimization of UAV paths is complicated by numerous factors, including the dynamic nature of sensor networks. A learning algorithm based on Internet of Things (IoT) devices employs a Double Deep Q-Network (DDQN) to facilitate adaptive movement decisions for UAVs, accounting for constraints like flight time and sensor configurations.

The paper also addresses limitations in traditional methods, such as requiring a pre-defined path, which restricts real-time obstacle avoidance. Enhancements to SLAM techniques have been proposed, but they often fail to account for unpredictable, non-stationary obstacles or un-textured walls in indoor settings.

Furthermore, a self-trained UAV using Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (DDPG) is presented, aimed at avoiding both static and moving obstacles in a 3D environment through a reward-based learning system. This approach emphasizes minimizing collision risks while navigating.

Despite the progress made with deep reinforcement learning in indoor navigation, challenges remain, particularly in replicating human-like learning and memory usage for obstacle avoidance. The paper proposes a method that incorporates a monocular camera and conditional generative adversarial networks (cGAN) to predict depth information, enabling the UAV to navigate unstructured environments effectively. This method emphasizes the importance of real-time decision-making, where each action taken by the UAV influences future navigation outcomes.
