The paper discusses advancements in reinforcement learning (RL) applied to continuous action spaces, particularly for controlling unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) and optimizing drone delivery systems. It highlights the use of various algorithms, including Trust Region Policy Optimization (TRPO), Deep Deterministic Policy Gradients (DDPG), Actor-Critic using Kronecker-Factored Trust Region (ACKTR), and Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). The proposed model integrates U-Net-based segmentation with a policy gradient algorithm to automate the data labeling process necessary for RL training.

The authors conducted experiments comparing training results across continuous and discrete action spaces, including human piloting performance. The experiments spanned three environments: woodland, block world, and arena world. In discrete action spaces, D3QN outperformed other algorithms, while ACKTR demonstrated effective continuous trajectory control. The findings revealed that expert pilots excelled in certain environments, with ACKTR closely matching their performance.

Additionally, the paper explores RL's application to drone delivery, emphasizing the need for accurate navigation in dynamic outdoor settings. It discusses the implementation of deep reinforcement learning (DRL) algorithms that enable drones to navigate obstacles optimally. The study introduces new architectures and algorithms, including DQN and Double DQN, aimed at improving action-value estimation and reducing state correlation through techniques like experience replay.

Experiments were conducted in a virtual environment mimicking real-world conditions, employing different neural network architectures to enhance training efficiency. The use of checkpoints during training proved beneficial, increasing the rate of successful action episodes. Overall, the study underscores the potential of RL and DRL algorithms in enhancing UAV control and drone delivery systems through improved decision-making processes.
