The paper discusses deep reinforcement learning (DRL) as a solution to these challenges, emphasizing its sequential decision-making capability where long-term rewards depend on the sequence of actions taken. This is connected to the credit assignment problem, which highlights the temporal nature of actions and their consequences within the system.

The taxonomy of reinforcement learning algorithms is presented, including various techniques such as Dynamic Programming, Temporal Difference, and Deep Q-Network, among others. The paper classifies DRL techniques into positive and negative categories based on whether they use favorable or undesirable stimuli to influence agent behavior. 

In the context of unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs), the application of DRL is framed as a specialized representation of Markov decision processes (MDPs), requiring elements such as a policy, reward signal, utility function, and environment. The integration of deep neural networks with RL algorithms is emphasized as a means to overcome the limitations of traditional RL approaches, thereby enhancing UAV control capabilities.
