The paper discusses the complexities of controlling unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) and the application of deep reinforcement learning (DRL) in addressing these challenges. It highlights the difficulties in modeling UAVs due to system nonlinearities, the need for online learning without explicit training, and the operation in unknown environments. The presented taxonomy categorizes various UAV control tasks and suggests suitable algorithms for each.

Deep Q-Network (DQN) is emphasized as a prominent algorithm that has demonstrated exceptional performance in complex problem-solving, particularly in UAV control tasks such as path planning, navigation, and attitude control. The effectiveness of DQN relies on understanding key parameters: state space (S), reward value (R), transition probability (P), and utility function (V). The Q-function is defined mathematically, illustrating how it incorporates these parameters to inform decision-making.

The paper outlines a systematic approach to achieve the optimal Q-function and policy value through a series of steps, as described in Algorithm 1. Initial parameter setup is critical for stable learning, and the target DQN structure is designed to derive actions based on the UAV's current state. The paper concludes with equations that detail the calculation of the maximum Q-function and cumulative discount function, reinforcing the algorithm's foundational principles.
