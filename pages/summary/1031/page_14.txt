The paper discusses the need for a balanced approach to AI governance, advocating for a progressive level of oversight proportional to the risks associated with specific AI technologies and contexts. It emphasizes the importance of experimentation to determine the appropriate degree of oversight and calls for urgent research into evaluating AI ethics practices. The Digital Catapult is piloting a concept called "Ethics as a Service," which includes an AI ethics framework, consultations, and an independent ethics advisory board. The paper raises critical questions regarding the selection and compensation of board members, the independence of audits, and the responsibilities associated with halting harmful AI developments. It suggests that those exploring these questions should publicly share their findings to build a knowledge base on best ethical practices.

In conclusion, the paper asserts that AI ethics is complex and may require a customizable approach similar to that of research and medical ethics. By shifting focus from abstract principles to procedural regularity, AI ethics can become more relatable to practitioners. The authors advocate for a procedural approach to clarify the importance of ethical considerations in AI design, highlighting the parallels with quality assurance processes. The concept of "Ethics as a Service" is presented as a potential framework for improving ethical practices in AI development.
