The paper "Ethics as a Service: A Pragmatic Operationalisation of AI Ethics" addresses the growing ethical concerns associated with Artificial Intelligence (AI) and machine learning (ML). The authors highlight the inadequacy of current legislation and regulation in protecting individuals and society from potential harms caused by AI technologies. In response, there has been a surge in ethical codes and guidelines; however, a significant gap remains between the theoretical principles of AI ethics and their practical application in system design.

The authors previously analyzed the effectiveness of existing tools and methods aimed at bridging the gap between the 'what' and 'how' of AI ethics. They found that these translational tools are often either too flexible, allowing for ethical evasion, or too rigid, lacking responsiveness to specific contexts. This raises critical questions about the feasibility of embedding ethics into algorithmic design processes.

The paper seeks to explore the necessity of both ethical principles and technical tools, despite their limitations, and proposes a concept termed "Ethics as a Service." This approach aims to provide a theoretical foundation to help AI practitioners better utilize ethical guidelines in their work. The discussion emphasizes the importance of making AI ethics more applicable and effective in real-world scenarios.
