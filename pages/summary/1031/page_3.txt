The paper discusses the limitations of current translational tools and methods in operationalizing AI ethics, highlighting that they often fail to effectively address the governability of algorithmic systems. The authors note that while these tools have raised awareness and examined ethical interpretations within organizations, their external validity and real-world impact remain untested. They argue that existing tools tend to be either too flexible, which can lead to ethics shopping and washing, or too strict, failing to accommodate the diverse interpretations of ethics that arise from varying societal views.

The paper poses critical questions about the effectiveness of pro-ethical design in AI, particularly when even established guidelines, such as IEEE's Ethically Aligned Design, struggle to be integrated into algorithmic processes. It explores the necessity of principles and tools despite their limitations and introduces the concept of "Ethics as a Service." This concept is grounded in Habermas’s discourse ethics and Floridi's notion of distributed responsibility, providing a theoretical framework distinct from technocratic approaches by large tech firms.

The paper outlines the compromises necessary for practical AI ethics operationalization and concludes by suggesting areas for further research, emphasizing the need for a more effective integration of ethics in AI development.
