The perception of these tools as mere checklists can lead to superficial assessments rather than meaningful ethical reflection and action. 

In summary, while translational tools like the Digital Catapult AI Ethics Framework aim to bridge the gap between ethical principles and practical application, they face significant limitations. These include their extra-empirical nature, which can lead to misalignment with societal values; a diagnostic rather than prescriptive approach, which fails to provide actionable solutions; and the tendency to be viewed as one-time compliance checks rather than ongoing processes. Addressing these limitations is crucial for ensuring that AI systems meet societal ethical standards and for fostering a more responsible approach to AI development and deployment.
