The paper discusses the operationalization of AI ethics as a service, emphasizing the importance of context and procedural regularity over rigid standards. It highlights that understanding subjective biases can lead to better identification of unethical outcomes and appropriate mitigation strategies. The authors propose a framework for companies that involves three key commitments: justifying design decisions based on common principles developed inclusively, translating these principles into technical standards through a repeatable procedure, and ensuring oversight during validation and evaluation phases. This approach allows for adaptability to specific contexts while maintaining ethical justifiability and sustainability. The authors draw parallels with privacy engineering, advocating for a collaborative and iterative process in ethical governance. However, they caution that if the responsibility solely rests with AI practitioners, there is a risk of ethics washing, where the operationalization may be manipulated for superficial compliance rather than genuine ethical engagement.
