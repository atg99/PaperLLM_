The paper critiques the superficial approach to ethics in algorithmic systems, often reduced to compliance checklists that risk neglecting ethical considerations during deployment. It emphasizes that ethical evaluations should be an ongoing process encompassing three phases: validation, verification, and evaluation. Validation ensures the correct system is being developed, verification checks the development process, and evaluation assesses the system's ongoing operation and potential need for revisions. 

The authors argue that without integrating ethical evaluation into the operational framework, pro-ethical tools may inadvertently create a false sense of security. Despite skepticism about the feasibility of embedding ethics in AI design, the authors draw parallels to successful applications in fields like medical ethics, suggesting that it is indeed possible to operationalize ethical principles effectively.

To guide AI practitioners, the paper introduces the Digital Catapult AI Ethics Framework (DCEF), developed through expert consultation. This framework consists of four levels: 
1. Five high-level ethical principles: beneficence, non-maleficence, autonomy, justice, and explicability.
2. Seven contextual interpretations of these principles based on discussions with AI practitioners and stakeholders.
3. A discourse ethics approach encouraging ethical foresight analysis through targeted questions.
4. Practical tools, such as Python libraries, for identifying bias in data.

The framework aims to translate high-level ethical principles into actionable practices for AI startups, thereby fostering a more ethically responsible approach to AI development.
