This paper presents a novel approach for environmental sound classification using a deep convolutional neural network (DCNN) that incorporates multiple feature channels and an attention mechanism. The model is trained to minimize categorical cross-entropy loss with the Adam optimizer, utilizing techniques such as dropout and weight decay. The training process spans 500 epochs, with an initial learning rate of 0.01, decreasing every 150 epochs.

The authors emphasize the importance of mix-up data augmentation in enhancing performance, particularly with large models and limited data. The proposed model is evaluated against previous state-of-the-art models on three benchmark datasets: ESC-10, ESC-50, and UrbanSound8K. Results indicate that the proposed model outperforms existing models in terms of accuracy, achieving 94.75% on ESC-10, 87.45% on ESC-50, and 97.52% on UrbanSound8K.

Key findings demonstrate that using separable convolutions reduces the number of parameters while improving accuracy compared to standard convolutions. The attention mechanism, implemented with depthwise separable convolutions, further enhances performance with minimal increases in parameters. The analysis of varying feature coefficients shows that accuracy improves with more features, peaking at 128 features to balance complexity and performance.

In conclusion, the proposed model effectively combines multiple feature extraction methods and an attention-based architecture, achieving state-of-the-art results across all tested datasets.
