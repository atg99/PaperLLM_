The paper investigates the zero-shot learning capabilities of ChatGPT, a large language model developed by OpenAI, to determine if it can function as a general-purpose natural language processing (NLP) task solver. It highlights the growing attention ChatGPT has received for its ability to generate high-quality responses and self-correct during conversations. The authors conduct empirical evaluations across 20 popular NLP datasets spanning seven task categories, including reasoning, natural language inference, question answering, dialogue, summarization, named entity recognition, and sentiment analysis.

The findings reveal that while ChatGPT exhibits strong performance on tasks requiring reasoning, such as arithmetic reasoning, it encounters challenges with specific tasks like sequence tagging. The paper emphasizes that although large language models can perform various tasks without prior training, they still have limitations and are not fully generalized systems. The impact of prompt structure on performance is also noted, demonstrating that specific prompting strategies can significantly enhance results. Overall, the study provides a comprehensive analysis of ChatGPT's strengths and weaknesses in zero-shot learning, contributing to the understanding of its potential and limitations in the NLP domain.
