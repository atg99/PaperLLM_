This paper investigates the performance of ChatGPT (gpt-3.5-turbo) in comparison to previous models like GPT-3.5 (text-davinci-003) across a diverse range of natural language processing (NLP) tasks. The study addresses key questions regarding the general-purpose capabilities of ChatGPT and the reasons behind its performance discrepancies with other models.

Key findings include:

1. **Generalist Model Performance**: ChatGPT is recognized for its ability to handle multiple tasks but often underperforms compared to models specifically fine-tuned for particular tasks.

2. **Arithmetic Reasoning**: ChatGPT exhibits strong reasoning skills in arithmetic tasks, outperforming GPT-3.5.

3. **Commonsense and Symbolic Reasoning**: In areas of commonsense, symbolic, and logical reasoning, ChatGPT generally falls short compared to GPT-3.5.

4. **Natural Language Inference**: ChatGPT shows superior performance in natural language inference and question answering tasks, particularly in identifying logical relationships and classifying entailment.

5. **Dialogue Tasks**: The model excels in dialogue-related tasks compared to its predecessor.

6. **Summarization**: ChatGPT generates longer summaries but performs worse than GPT-3.5 in summarization tasks. Limiting summary length can negatively impact quality.

7. **Challenges**: Both ChatGPT and GPT-3.5 encounter difficulties in specific tasks, such as sequence tagging.

This study provides a comprehensive profile of ChatGPT's capabilities, highlighting its strengths in certain reasoning tasks while acknowledging its limitations in others.
