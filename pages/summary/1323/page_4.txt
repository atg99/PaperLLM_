The paper discusses various architectures developed for identifying COVID-19 from chest X-ray (CXR) images. Several models are reviewed, highlighting their accuracies and limitations. One model achieved a high accuracy of 99.27% for binary classification of COVID-19 images and 98.3% overall for both COVID-19 and normal images, though it was computationally inefficient. Other models, such as those by Panwar et al. and Pereira et al., demonstrated accuracies between 97.97% and 98.68% for COVID-19 detection, but some faced challenges like low F1-scores and reliance on small datasets. 

The authors propose a computer-aided diagnosis system designed to automatically detect COVID-19 using CXR radiographs. This system aims to assist medical professionals in the diagnostic process, potentially alleviating their workload. However, it is crucial to note that the proposed method has not undergone clinical validation and should not be used independently for diagnosis.

The proposed method includes a structured pipeline consisting of four key steps: preprocessing of CXR images, feature extraction, feature selection using the HSGO algorithm, and classification of the extracted features into healthy or infected categories. The preprocessing step involves resizing CXR images to enhance computational efficiency while reducing the feature pool, thus facilitating faster processing for accurate diagnosis.
