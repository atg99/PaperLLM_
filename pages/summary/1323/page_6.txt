The paper discusses the process of feature extraction and selection for lung chest X-rays (CXRs) using different image processing methods and the Hybrid Social Group Optimization (HSGO) algorithm. The study highlights that converting images to Bi-level mode resulted in a loss of important details, while Palette and RGB modes retained too many features. The Grayscale method was ultimately chosen, allowing for the extraction of 10,000 features from each image.

Feature selection followed, employing the HSGO algorithm, which is based on the Social Group Optimization (SGO) method. The SGO algorithm leverages population behavior, utilizing the collective problem-solving abilities of a group to enhance individual solutions. Each candidate solution within the population learns from the best-performing individual and randomly from others, improving their respective fitness.

The HSGO algorithm enhances the SGO by integrating a random factor and a Mutation phase, which aids in overcoming local optima. This improvement allows HSGO to more effectively reduce the feature set while maintaining higher accuracy. Experimental results indicate that HSGO outperforms SGO and other conventional methods, achieving a feature set of 116 with an accuracy of 99.65% using a Support Vector Classifier (SVC). In comparison, SGO selected 254 features with an accuracy of 99.31%, while other methods like Kernel Principal Component Analysis (KPCA) and Principal Component Analysis (PCA) selected even more features with similar accuracy.

Overall, the research demonstrates the efficacy of HSGO in optimizing feature selection for medical image classification, providing a more efficient and accurate approach compared to traditional methods.
