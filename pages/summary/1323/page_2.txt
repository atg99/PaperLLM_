The paper discusses the global impact of COVID-19, highlighting that by the end of January 2021, there were nearly 100 million confirmed cases and over 2 million deaths, with mortality rates varying by age and pre-existing health conditions. The typical onset of symptoms occurs 5-6 days after infection, with common symptoms including fever, cough, and fatigue, while severe cases can lead to pneumonia and serious organ damage. 

The pandemic has necessitated strict lockdowns and social distancing, straining healthcare systems, particularly in intensive care units and testing capacities. The primary testing method, RT-PCR, is effective in early infection but may require sputum samples as the disease progresses.

Recent advances in artificial intelligence (AI) and machine learning (ML) have spurred research into methods for early detection of COVID-19. Various AI and ML approaches have been developed to analyze lung images, identify symptoms, and support healthcare operations. However, traditional deep learning models demand large datasets and high computational resources, which are challenging to obtain during a pandemic.

To address these issues, the authors propose a novel pipeline that utilizes chest X-ray (CXR) images for COVID-19 detection. This pipeline leverages evolutionary algorithms, allowing for effective training on smaller datasets while maintaining accuracy comparable to more complex deep learning models. The proposed solution aims to enhance early detection and improve healthcare responses during the pandemic.
