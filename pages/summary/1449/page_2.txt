The paper presents wav2vec, an unsupervised pre-training approach for speech recognition that leverages a large corpus of unlabeled audio data. The model uses two stacked convolutional neural networks to encode raw audio signals into a lower temporal frequency feature representation. The primary objective during training is to predict future audio samples based on a given context, which helps avoid the challenges associated with accurately modeling data distributions.

Experimental results on the Wall Street Journal (WSJ) benchmark indicate that the pre-trained model, which was trained on approximately 1,000 hours of unlabeled speech, significantly enhances a character-based automatic speech recognition (ASR) system. Specifically, it improved the word error rate (WER) from 3.1% to 2.43%, surpassing previous results from the literature, such as Deep Speech 2. Additionally, on the TIMIT dataset, wav2vec matched the best reported results. In scenarios with limited labeled data, the model demonstrated a reduction in WER by up to 36% compared to traditional models reliant solely on labeled data.

The architecture consists of an encoder network that transforms raw audio into a latent space and a context network that aggregates multiple time steps to create contextualized representations. The encoder employs a five-layer convolutional structure, while the context network has nine layers, both utilizing causal convolutions, group normalization, and ReLU activations. The design choices in normalization and architecture contribute to the model's ability to generalize well across different datasets. Overall, wav2vec represents a significant advancement in the field of speech recognition, particularly in the context of unsupervised learning.
