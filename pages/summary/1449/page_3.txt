The paper presents wav2vec, an unsupervised pre-training approach for speech recognition, focusing on a variant called "wav2vec large." This model utilizes enhanced capacity with additional linear transformations in the encoder and a larger context network featuring twelve layers. Skip connections are introduced in the aggregator to aid convergence, resulting in a total receptive field of approximately 810 ms.

The primary objective of training is to differentiate future audio samples from distractor samples by minimizing a contrastive loss. This is achieved through a step-specific affine transformation applied to the context network's output, optimizing the loss across various step sizes. The model's representations replace traditional log-mel filterbank features in the acoustic model.

The experimental setup involves several corpora, including TIMIT for phoneme recognition, WSJ with 81 hours of transcribed audio, and Librispeech, which comprises 960 hours of speech data. Pre-training utilizes different subsets of these datasets. The acoustic models are trained using the wav2letter++ toolkit, with specific configurations for each dataset. Training employs SGD with momentum, and models are evaluated based on word error rate (WER) and letter error rate (LER). The paper also details the architecture of the acoustic models, emphasizing the use of convolutions, non-linearities, and dropout for effective training.
