The referenced works explore various advancements in the field of speech recognition, focusing on unsupervised learning and representation learning techniques. 

Ravanelli and Bengio discuss the development of speaker representations through mutual information, which enhances the understanding of speaker identity in speech recognition tasks. Their work highlights the importance of effective representation in improving model performance. 

The introduction of light gated recurrent units by Ravanelli et al. provides a novel approach to enhancing recurrent neural networks for speech recognition, focusing on efficiency and performance. 

Synnaeve and Dupoux propose a temporal coherence loss function aimed at learning unsupervised acoustic embeddings, which is crucial for capturing the temporal dynamics in speech data. Their research emphasizes the significance of temporal information in acoustic modeling.

Van den Oord et al. present contrastive predictive coding as a method for representation learning, which can potentially improve the quality of learned features for various tasks, including speech recognition.

Vinyals et al. reflect on lessons learned from the MS COCO image captioning challenge, which indirectly informs speech recognition by emphasizing the need for robust feature extraction and representation techniques.

Woodland et al. discuss the application of the HTK toolkit for large vocabulary continuous speech recognition, which remains a foundational framework in the field.

Wu and He introduce group normalization, a technique that enhances training stability and performance in deep learning models, including those used for speech tasks.

Zeghidour et al. investigate the learning of filterbanks directly from raw speech, which is a step towards more efficient acoustic modeling. They also explore fully convolutional architectures for speech recognition, showcasing their potential for robust performance in real-time applications.

Overall, these contributions collectively advance the understanding and capabilities of speech recognition systems, emphasizing unsupervised learning, representation efficiency, and the importance of temporal coherence in acoustic modeling.
