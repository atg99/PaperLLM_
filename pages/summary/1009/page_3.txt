The paper analyzes data mining tasks within the context of various analytic perspectives in the mining sector, focusing on the “Diagnostic and machinery,” “Geomechanics,” “Hazards,” and “Mine planning and safety” categories. A total of 15 papers were examined in the first two perspectives, while 16 papers were reviewed in the latter two. The majority of the publications emphasized predictive tasks, particularly classification, with regression and rule induction also noted.

The “Diagnostic and machinery” perspective primarily addressed diagnostic issues related to machinery, utilizing techniques such as artificial neural networks (ANN) and time series analysis. The “Geomechanics” perspective featured studies on rock mechanics and seismic events, with ANN and decision trees being popular data mining techniques.

The “Hazards” perspective included research on natural hazards such as methane and rock bursts, employing ANN, decision trees, and regression techniques. Finally, the “Mine planning and safety” perspective covered a broad range of topics related to mine planning, including equipment selection and production costs. The paper provides a comprehensive overview of the data mining techniques applied across these perspectives, highlighting the prevalence of ANN and decision trees across various studies.
