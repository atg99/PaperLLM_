The paper analyzes the applications of data mining (DM) and process mining in the underground mining sector, concluding that the use of DM techniques is prevalent, particularly for predictive tasks, with a significant focus on classification. Key DM techniques identified include artificial neural networks, tree models, rule induction, and regression. The study highlights a range of sophistication in the techniques used, from simpler models like CART trees and linear regression to more complex ones such as XGboost and Random Forest. While interest in these techniques is fairly uniform across various analytical perspectives, regression models dominate in economics-related studies concerning mine planning and safety.

The analysis also notes a limited application of process mining in underground mining, suggesting that it remains largely unexplored by the mining community. However, the potential for process mining to enhance analytics for process improvement and efficiency is acknowledged. The research was conducted at AGH University of Science and Technology under contract no 11.11.100.693.
