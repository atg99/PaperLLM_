The paper discusses the application of process mining (PM) techniques in the mining industry, highlighting the challenges and potential benefits of such methodologies. It notes that longwall monitoring systems operate at a low level of abstraction, making direct analysis with PM tools ineffective. Instead, preprocessing of data through supervised and unsupervised data mining techniques is necessary, as elaborated in previous works by Brzychczy and Trzcionkowska.

An example provided in the paper is the use of PM in analyzing emergency rescue processes following fatal gas explosions in China, where various techniques like Mine Petri Net with Inductive Miner and Social Networks Analysis were employed. The paper identifies two main barriers hindering the adoption of PM in mining: a lack of awareness of PM tools within the mining community and the absence of suitable event logs for effective process modeling and analysis.

Despite these challenges, the paper observes a growing interest in advanced analytics among mining companies, suggesting a potential for increased application of PM techniques in the sector.
