The paper discusses the main tasks of data mining, categorizing them into descriptive and predictive tasks. Descriptive tasks include summarization, clustering, and association rules, which focus on characterizing datasets and identifying patterns. Predictive tasks, such as regression, classification, and time series analysis, utilize models built from datasets for forecasting purposes.

The paper also distinguishes between supervised and unsupervised learning in machine learning, noting that unsupervised techniques are typically employed in descriptive tasks, while supervised techniques are used for predictive tasks. Additionally, it highlights the growth of process-oriented analytics, particularly in process mining, which has seen a significant increase in academic publications. Process mining serves as a bridge between data science and process science, focusing on knowledge discovery from event logs.

Key tasks in process mining include discovering process models, conformance checking, and enhancement. The foundational data structure for process mining is the event log, which can be enriched with contextual information. The resulting process models facilitate various types of analysis, leading to improved organizational knowledge for process optimization.

The paper provides an overview of data mining applications specifically within the context of underground mining processes, based on a review of 62 academic papers. It categorizes these applications into four perspectives: Diagnostic and Machinery, Geomechanics, Hazards, and Mine Planning and Safety. The analysis reveals a recent surge in publications related to these topics, particularly over the last five years.
