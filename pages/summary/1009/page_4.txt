The paper discusses the application of data mining (DM) techniques in the analysis of underground mining processes, focusing on occupational safety and price analysis. A total of 15 papers were reviewed, revealing that regression models and decision tree algorithms are the most commonly used DM techniques. The use of various techniques is attributed to the specific nature of the issues being analyzed, with some studies employing multiple methods.

The paper highlights a significant presence of "black-box" techniques in the diagnostic and machinery, as well as geomechanics perspectives, where complex phenomena are assessed without complete contextual information. Key DM techniques identified include artificial neural networks, regression, tree models, and rule induction. 

The analysis also explores the application of process mining in underground mining, noting its rarity in the literature. The search for relevant papers through databases such as Science Direct and IEEE Explore yielded limited results, with only a few studies focusing on this type of analytics. Notable works include studies by Brzychczy and Trzcionkowska, which analyze mechanized roof support operations and propose methods for creating event logs from sensor data for process mining purposes.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of advanced analytical techniques in improving safety and efficiency in underground mining operations.
