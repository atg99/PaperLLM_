The paper discusses the performance of a locality-aware (LA) trained ResNet-50 model in classifying images using a nearest-neighbor (KNN) classifier. The analysis is illustrated through validation images categorized into successful and failure cases. Successful classifications show that the model effectively groups images of the same category, demonstrating robustness against variations in backgrounds and viewpoints. In contrast, failure cases highlight the model's ability to recognize high-level visual context, often leading to misclassifications due to the ambiguous nature of ImageNet category labels.

The paper employs multi-dimensional scaling (MDS) to visualize the embedding space, revealing that the LA model clusters images of trombones effectively, irrespective of background or viewpoint, while distinguishing them from images of humans playing trombones.

Further, the paper investigates the design of the LA procedure through ablation studies, examining the impact of different configurations on nearest-neighbor performance. The results indicate that a dynamic locality approach for assessing background relevance significantly outperforms static methods. This finding aligns with the observation that feature density varies across the embedding space, emphasizing the importance of local rather than global background measurements.

Overall, the study provides insights into the strengths and limitations of the LA-trained model, illustrating its capacity for high-level visual recognition while addressing challenges posed by ambiguous image classifications.
