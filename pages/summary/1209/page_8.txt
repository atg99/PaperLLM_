The paper presents a local aggregation (LA) objective for learning feature embeddings in deep convolutional neural networks (DCNNs). The LA objective aims to strike a balance between clustering similar inputs together and distancing dissimilar inputs, integrating concepts from recent advancements in unsupervised learning. The authors demonstrate that this approach leads to effective representations for transfer learning across various visual tasks.

Table 5 outlines the nearest neighbor validation performances of ResNet-18, detailing the impact of different clustering choices on performance metrics. The experiments reveal that a cluster-based identification of close neighbors, as opposed to a simpler neighborhood-based method, significantly enhances representation quality. Although alternatives to k-means clustering exist, such as DBSCAN, they are less effective in high-dimensional spaces typical of the embedding context.

The paper discusses the influence of hyperparameters, including the number of clusters and the number of clusterings, on the clustering quality. It identifies two crucial factors: the skewness of clusters and their size. Lower skewness correlates with improved performance, and there exists an optimal cluster size that aligns with the network's representation capacity. The findings suggest that achieving isotropic distributions of clusters enhances the quality of embeddings.

The authors propose future work to enhance the LA objective by incorporating advanced methods for detecting similarity and dissimilarity, extending the application of LA to other domains like video and audio, and comparing their approach with biological vision systems.
