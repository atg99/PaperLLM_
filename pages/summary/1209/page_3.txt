This paper discusses a method for optimizing embeddings in a neural network by defining and utilizing two types of neighbors: close neighbors (Ci) and background neighbors (Bi). Close neighbors are those that should have similar embeddings to a given point, while background neighbors help establish a scale for measuring closeness. The paper outlines the mathematical definitions of these neighbor sets, which involve nearest-neighbor identification using cosine distance for background neighbors and a robust clustering algorithm for close neighbors.

The local aggregation metric, L(Ci,Bi|θ,xi), is formulated based on a non-parametric softmax operation, which calculates the probability of recognizing an embedding as belonging to a specific set of images. The metric is designed to maximize the likelihood of an embedding being recognized as a close neighbor while minimizing the likelihood of it being recognized as a non-close neighbor. The optimization process involves minimizing a loss function that includes this local aggregation metric and a regularization term.

The paper emphasizes the intuition behind the chosen definitions and metrics, illustrating how maximizing the probability of close neighbors while minimizing that of non-close neighbors leads to a more structured embedding space. It suggests that this results in a distribution of embedded points that form small clusters, enhancing the overall effectiveness of the embedding representation.
