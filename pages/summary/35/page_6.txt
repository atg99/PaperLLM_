The paper discusses the architecture of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), focusing on two significant models: LeNet-5 and AlexNet. 

LeNet-5 comprises multiple layers, starting with a second convolutional layer featuring 16 feature maps and a 5x5 filter, which reduces the image dimensions to 10x10x16. This is followed by a pooling layer that further downsizes the output to 5x5x16. The architecture includes fully connected layers, with the fifth layer containing 120 feature maps and the final layer employing a softmax activation function to classify the output.

AlexNet, introduced by Alex Krizhevsky in 2012, builds on the principles of CNNs with a straightforward yet effective design. It features five convolutional layers, a pooling layer, and three fully connected layers. AlexNet enhances training efficiency using a half-wave rectifier function for activation and incorporates dropout techniques to mitigate overfitting by randomly setting a portion of input neurons to zero during training.

Both architectures illustrate the evolution and effectiveness of CNNs in image recognition tasks.
