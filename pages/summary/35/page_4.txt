The paper "Understanding of Convolutional Neural Network (CNN): A Review" by Purwono provides an overview of the structure and functionality of CNNs, particularly focusing on the fully connected layer and activation functions. 

The fully connected layer, also known as the convolutional output layer, is positioned at the bottom of the network and receives input from the final pooling or convolutional layer. This layer transforms the output into a flattened vector, enabling the study of high-level non-linear combinations of features extracted by the previous layers.

The activation function is crucial in CNNs, determining the network's final output. The paper highlights the Rectified Linear Unit (ReLU) as the most prevalent activation function used in feature extraction. Activation functions are categorized into linear and non-linear types. Linear activation functions provide outputs proportional to the input, while non-linear activation functions facilitate complex mappings between inputs and outputs, essential for sophisticated learning and modeling.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the significance of these layers and functions in enhancing the performance and capabilities of CNNs in various applications.
