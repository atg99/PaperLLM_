The paper titled "Understanding of Convolutional Neural Network (CNN): A Review" by Purwono provides an overview of common activation functions used in CNNs and discusses popular CNN architectures. 

The paper begins by detailing various activation functions, which are crucial for introducing non-linearity into neural networks. The Sigmoid function outputs values between 0 and 1, represented mathematically as f(x) = 1 / (1 + e^(-x)). The Tanh function, similar to Sigmoid, outputs values between -1 and 1, expressed as f(x) = (e^x - e^(-x)) / (e^x + e^(-x)). The ReLU (Rectified Linear Unit) function is widely used in CNNs due to its efficiency, defined as f(x) = max(0, x). The Leaky ReLU addresses the dying ReLU problem by allowing a small gradient for negative inputs, mathematically represented as f(x) = x if x > 0, and mx if x ≤ 0. The Noisy ReLU introduces Gaussian noise into the ReLU function, while Parametric Linear Units modify Leaky ReLU by updating the leak factor during training.

The paper then transitions to discussing popular CNN architectures, emphasizing that their design is inspired by the visual cortex's organization. It highlights LeNet, particularly the LeNet-5 version, which is a gradient-based CNN structure initially developed for recognizing handwritten characters. LeNet-5 processes grayscale images of size 32×32×1 through six convolutional layers with 5×5 filters and a stride, ultimately producing a 14×14×6 image after two pooling layers. 

Overall, the review succinctly captures key concepts related to activation functions and the architecture of CNNs, illustrating their importance in the field of deep learning and computer vision.
