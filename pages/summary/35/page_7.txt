This review highlights the evolution of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), focusing on the VGGNet architecture, specifically VGGNet-16, which consists of 13 convolutional layers and 3 fully connected layers. VGGNet-16 uses a 3x3 convolutional layer with a stride of 1 and pooling layers of size 2x2 with a stride of 2, processing images at a resolution of 224x224. The feature map size reduces by 50% after each pooling operation, ultimately producing a 7x7 feature map with 512 channels.

The discussion compares various CNN architectures, including LeNet, AlexNet, and VGGNet, emphasizing their differences in depth, image resolution, and accuracy rates. LeNet operates on smaller images (32x32) and achieves over 90% accuracy on the MNIST database, while AlexNet and VGGNet, utilizing the ILSVRC database, report error rates of 15.3% and 6.8%, respectively. The paper summarizes these findings in a table that outlines the specifications and performance of each architecture.

In conclusion, the paper asserts that machine learning enables systems to learn independently, and deep learning further enhances this capability by mimicking human cognitive processes through advanced neural network designs.
