The paper discusses the critical role of mathematical optimization and hyper-parameter optimization in machine learning (ML), emphasizing their importance in model development. 

Mathematical optimization involves finding the optimal solution from a set of candidates to either maximize or minimize an objective function. Optimization problems are categorized into constrained and unconstrained types. In unconstrained optimization, decision variables can take any real values, represented mathematically as minimizing a function f(x) over all x in the real number space. Conversely, constrained optimization problems impose restrictions on decision variables through equality and inequality constraints. This is expressed as minimizing f(x) subject to specific constraints, where gi(x) represents inequality constraints and hj(x) denotes equality constraints, with x belonging to a defined domain X.

The section underscores that the optimization process is fundamental to refining both the weight parameters of ML models and their hyper-parameters, which dictate the model architecture, thus enhancing overall model performance.
