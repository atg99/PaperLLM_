Clustering algorithms are essential in data analysis, with key methods including k-means, Expectation-Maximization (EM), and hierarchical clustering. The primary hyper-parameter to tune in these algorithms is the number of clusters, denoted as 'nclusters'.

The k-means algorithm utilizes centroids to group data points, requiring the specification of 'nclusters' to minimize the sum of squared errors. The centroid initialization method, which can be 'k-means++', 'random', or predefined, as well as the number of initializations ('n init') and maximum iterations ('max iter'), can slightly influence performance.

The EM algorithm is an iterative approach for estimating parameters through maximum likelihood, commonly applied in Gaussian Mixture Models. The main hyper-parameter here is 'n components', indicating the number of clusters or Gaussian distributions. Additional parameters such as covariance constraints (e.g., 'full', 'tied', 'diagonal', 'spherical'), 'max iter', and 'tol' are also crucial for tuning.

Hierarchical clustering builds a tree-like structure of clusters through merging or splitting processes, with the root representing a single cluster and the leaves corresponding to individual samples. This method provides a different perspective on data grouping compared to k-means and EM algorithms.
