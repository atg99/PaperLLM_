3. Select a subset of chromosomes with high fitness scores to serve as parents for the next generation.
4. Perform crossover operations on selected parents to create new offspring by exchanging segments of their genes.
5. Apply mutation operations to introduce random changes in some genes of the offspring, promoting genetic diversity.
6. Replace the least fit chromosomes in the population with the new offspring to maintain a constant population size.
7. Repeat the evaluation, selection, crossover, and mutation processes for multiple generations until convergence is achieved or a stopping criterion is met.

Through this iterative process, the genetic algorithm aims to converge toward the global optimum hyper-parameter configuration for the given optimization problem. The strength of GA lies in its ability to explore a wide search space effectively while leveraging the principles of natural selection and genetics to enhance the performance of hyper-parameter optimization tasks.
