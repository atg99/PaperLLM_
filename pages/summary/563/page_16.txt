The paper discusses various classification algorithms, particularly focusing on Naive Bayes and tree-based models. Multinomial Naive Bayes (NB) is based on the Naive Bayes algorithm and is suited for multinomially-distributed data. It estimates the conditional probability P(xi|y) using relative frequency counting with smoothing to handle unseen features. The formula used for estimation incorporates smoothing priors, with Laplace and Lidstone smoothing as specific cases.

Complement Naive Bayes is an enhancement of the standard multinomial NB, particularly effective for imbalanced datasets. Bernoulli NB is applicable for binary-valued feature vectors, necessitating a multivariate Bernoulli distribution. Both variants utilize an additive smoothing parameter, which is a key hyper-parameter that may require tuning.

The paper also covers decision trees (DT), a widely used classification method characterized by a tree-like structure. A DT comprises a root node, decision nodes, and leaf nodes, with the algorithm recursively splitting the training data based on feature values to optimize decision-making. Pruning is employed to prevent overfitting, and the maximum tree depth is highlighted as a crucial hyper-parameter influencing model complexity. Additionally, the quality of splits within the tree can be adjusted through various hyper-parameters to enhance model effectiveness.
