The paper evaluates the performance of various hyperparameter optimization (HPO) methods applied to Random Forest (RF) classifiers on the MNIST dataset. The results are summarized in Table 4, which compares the accuracy and computational time of different optimization algorithms. The default hyperparameters yield an accuracy of 90.65%, while several HPO methods, including Grid Search (GS), Random Search (RS), and Bayesian Optimization (BO), show improved accuracies ranging from 93.32% to 93.88%. Notably, Hyperband and BOHB also demonstrate competitive performance with lower computational times. The findings highlight the significance of HPO methods in enhancing model performance over default configurations. The paper further discusses the limitations of GS and RS, indicating that although they serve as baseline methods, their higher computational demands and inability to consistently identify optimal hyperparameter settings can hinder effectiveness. Overall, the results underscore the importance of employing efficient HPO techniques for better machine learning outcomes.
