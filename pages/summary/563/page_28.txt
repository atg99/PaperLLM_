SMAC employs a set of regression trees, where B trees are built using samples from the training set. Each tree selects a split node from a set of hyper-parameters, and to optimize computational efficiency, thresholds are set for the minimum instances for further splitting and the number of trees to grow. SMAC's key advantage lies in its capability to handle various types of variables, including continuous, discrete, categorical, and conditional hyper-parameters. Its time complexities for fitting and predicting variances are O(n log n) and O(log n), respectively, which are significantly lower than those of BO-GP.

On the other hand, BO-TPE utilizes a different approach by creating two density functions to model observations based on their performance. Results are categorized into good and poor based on a predefined percentile, and these are modeled using Parzen windows. The expected improvement is determined by the ratio of these density functions, facilitating the evaluation of new configurations while inheriting conditional dependencies through a tree-structured organization. The time complexity for BO-TPE is also O(n log n), making it more efficient than BO-GP.

Both SMAC and BO-TPE are effective for hyperparameter optimization (HPO) problems, even in complex scenarios where the objective function is stochastic, non-convex, or non-continuous. However, a common limitation of BO methods is their potential to converge prematurely to suboptimal solutions if they do not properly balance exploration and exploitation.
