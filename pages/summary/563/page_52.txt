The paper discusses the challenges of hyper-parameter optimization (HPO) in the context of machine learning, particularly focusing on the ImageNet challenge. It highlights the complexity of the search space due to the presence of numerous hyper-parameters, some of which may have a minimal impact on performance while still requiring consideration. This complexity increases the dimensionality of the search space exponentially, complicating the optimization process and increasing the time required for objective function evaluations.

The paper emphasizes the need for efficient HPO methods that can mitigate the challenges posed by large search spaces, particularly in resource-intensive scenarios such as deep learning. It outlines the importance of strong anytime performance, which allows HPO algorithms to identify optimal or near-optimal configurations with limited resources and time. An effective HPO method should exhibit rapid convergence to minimize discrepancies between preliminary and final results, while avoiding randomness inherent in some methods like random search.

Additionally, the paper discusses the comparability of different optimization algorithms used in various machine learning frameworks, suggesting that the choice of algorithm can significantly affect the optimization process. Overall, the paper advocates for the development of advanced HPO techniques that can efficiently navigate complex search spaces and optimize model performance under resource constraints.
