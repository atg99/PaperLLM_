Gradient-based algorithms are not commonly used for hyper-parameter optimization (HPO) due to their limitations in handling only continuous hyper-parameters and their tendency to find local optima in non-convex problems. These algorithms, however, can be applied effectively to optimize specific hyper-parameters like learning rates in deep learning models. 

Bayesian optimization (BO) models, categorized into BO-GP, SMAC, and BO-TPE, utilize surrogate models to enhance efficiency by basing new hyper-parameter selections on previous evaluations. BO-GP is suitable for continuous and discrete hyper-parameters but does not accommodate conditional ones. In contrast, SMAC and BO-TPE can manage categorical, discrete, continuous, and conditional hyper-parameters, with SMAC excelling in scenarios with numerous categorical parameters and cross-validation, while BO-GP is advantageous for fewer continuous parameters. A key benefit of BO-TPE is its inherent support for specified conditional relationships.

Metaheuristic algorithms, such as Genetic Algorithms (GA) and Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO), are more complex but effective for intricate optimization tasks. They are versatile in handling all types of hyper-parameters and are particularly efficient in large configuration spaces. PSO benefits from large-scale parallelization and is well-suited for continuous and conditional HPO, often outperforming GA in speed. However, PSO's performance is highly dependent on proper population initialization, which is less critical for GA. GA, while sequential and less conducive to parallelization, introduces additional hyper-parameters like crossover and mutation rates, which can complicate its application.

The paper concludes with a summary of the strengths and weaknesses of the discussed HPO algorithms.
