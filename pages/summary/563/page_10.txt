the effectiveness of traditional optimization techniques in hyperparameter optimization (HPO) is limited due to several key challenges. 

The HPO process involves selecting an objective function and performance metrics, identifying hyper-parameters for tuning, training the machine learning model with a baseline configuration, initiating an optimization process within a defined search space, refining the search based on performance, and ultimately identifying the best-performing hyper-parameter configuration. 

However, traditional optimization methods face difficulties with HPO due to the nature of the optimization target, which is typically a non-convex and non-differentiable function. This complexity can lead to local optima instead of global optima, and traditional methods may perform poorly due to a lack of smoothness in the optimization target. 

Additionally, the variety of hyper-parameter types—continuous, discrete, categorical, and conditional—renders many traditional numerical optimization approaches inadequate, as they often focus solely on numerical variables. 

Finally, training machine learning models on large datasets can be computationally intensive, necessitating the use of data sampling for approximate objective function values. Traditional black-box optimization techniques often overlook function evaluation time and rely on exact values, which is not always feasible in the context of HPO.

In summary, the distinct characteristics of HPO necessitate specialized optimization techniques that can effectively address the challenges posed by non-convex objective functions, diverse hyper-parameter types, and computational constraints.
