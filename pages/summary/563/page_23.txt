The paper discusses methods for dimensionality reduction in machine learning, focusing on Principal Component Analysis (PCA) and Linear Discriminant Analysis (LDA). PCA involves computing the covariance matrix of a data matrix to extract eigenvectors corresponding to the largest eigenvalues, allowing for transformation into a lower-dimensional space. Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) is highlighted as a common technique for obtaining these eigenvalues and eigenvectors, with hyper-parameters such as the number of components and SVD solver type needing to be tuned.

In contrast, LDA aims to enhance classification by projecting features onto the most discriminative directions, minimizing variance within classes while maximizing variance between different classes. Similar to PCA, LDA requires tuning the number of components and offers different solver options such as SVD, least-squares, or eigenvalue decomposition. Additionally, LDA includes a shrinkage parameter for certain solvers.

The paper also introduces hyper-parameter optimization techniques, starting with a model-free approach called Babysitting. This method, also known as Trial and Error or grad student descent, involves manual tuning of hyper-parameters based on the experience and previous results, highlighting the importance of prior knowledge in achieving optimal results within time constraints.
