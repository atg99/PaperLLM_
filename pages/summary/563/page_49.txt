The paper presents a performance evaluation of various hyperparameter optimization (HPO) methods applied to Support Vector Machine (SVM) and K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN) regressors on the Boston housing dataset. 

For the SVM regressor, the mean squared error (MSE) results indicate that the best performance was achieved using the BO-TPE method with an MSE of 59.40, while the default hyperparameters resulted in an MSE of 77.43. Other notable methods include BO-GP and BOHB, with MSEs of 61.27 and 59.67 respectively. The computational time (CT) varied across methods, with BO-TPE being relatively efficient at 0.33 seconds.

In the case of the KNN regressor, the default hyperparameters yielded an MSE of 81.48, while the best MSE of 80.74 was achieved using the PSO method. The results show that the performance of the KNN regressor remained relatively close across all methods, with minimal differences in MSE values. The computational times for KNN methods were generally low, with the highest being 0.49 seconds for BO-GP.

Overall, the findings demonstrate the effectiveness of specific HPO methods in reducing MSE for both SVM and KNN regressors, highlighting the potential for improved model performance through optimization techniques.
