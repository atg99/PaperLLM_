In K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN), the choice of the number of nearest neighbors, k, is critical. A small k can lead to underfitting, while a large k may result in overfitting and increased computational time. Additionally, the prediction can utilize a weighted function, either 'uniform' or 'distance', depending on the problem context. Tuning the distance metric and the power parameter of the Minkowski metric can yield slight improvements. The algorithm for calculating nearest neighbors can be selected from options like ball tree, KD tree, or brute force, with sklearn allowing automatic selection by setting the algorithm to 'auto'.

Support Vector Machines (SVM) are supervised learning algorithms applicable for classification and regression. SVMs work by mapping data points into a higher-dimensional space to achieve linear separability, creating a hyperplane as the classification boundary. The objective function for SVM minimizes a combination of classification errors and a regularization term, with the penalty parameter C being a crucial hyper-parameter. The choice of kernel function, which measures similarity between data points, is also essential, with common types including linear, radial basis function (RBF), polynomial, and sigmoid kernels. Each kernel has its own mathematical representation, influencing the model's performance based on the specific data characteristics.
