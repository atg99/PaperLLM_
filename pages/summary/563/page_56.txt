The referenced works collectively explore the advancements and challenges in the field of automated machine learning (AutoML). Zöller and Huber provide a comprehensive benchmark and survey of various AutoML frameworks, highlighting their capabilities and limitations. Shawi et al. discuss the current state-of-the-art in AutoML while addressing open challenges that need to be tackled for further progress. Kuhn and Johnson’s book on applied predictive modeling serves as a foundational text for understanding practical applications in machine learning.

The effectiveness of hyperparameter optimization is examined through Diaz et al.'s algorithm, which enhances neural network performance, while Hutter et al. compile methods and systems in AutoML, emphasizing the need for robust solutions. Decastro-García et al. investigate the impact of dataset sampling on hyperparameter optimization efficiency, indicating that dataset characteristics can significantly influence algorithm performance.

Abreu focuses on automated architecture design for deep neural networks, reflecting the growing trend of automating not just model selection but also architecture configuration. Finally, Steinholtz's comparative study of black-box optimization algorithms highlights the importance of tuning hyperparameters in deep learning, while Luo reviews methods for automatic selection of machine learning algorithms and their hyperparameters, summarizing the evolving landscape of AutoML methodologies. Together, these works contribute to a deeper understanding of AutoML's potential and the ongoing research needed to address its challenges.
