This section discusses Bayesian Optimization (BO) and its common surrogate models, focusing on Gaussian Process (GP) and Random Forest (RF), also known as Sequential Model-based Algorithm Configuration (SMAC). 

Bayesian Optimization is effective for hyper-parameter tuning and typically finds near-optimal combinations within a few iterations. The three main types of BO algorithms based on surrogate models are BO-GP, BO-RF, and BO-TPE.

In BO-GP, the Gaussian Process is utilized to model the objective function. The predictions follow a normal distribution, and the model iteratively selects new points for evaluation based on confidence intervals derived from prior evaluations. However, BO-GP has a time complexity of O(n^3) and space complexity of O(n^2), which limits its scalability and parallelization capabilities, particularly for larger datasets.

SMAC employs Random Forest as its surrogate model, leveraging an ensemble of regression trees to estimate the objective function. The mean and variance of the regression function are calculated based on evaluations from the ensemble, allowing for effective modeling of the objective function in BO.

Overall, both BO-GP and SMAC are valuable methods for hyper-parameter optimization, each with its strengths and limitations in terms of complexity and parallelization.
