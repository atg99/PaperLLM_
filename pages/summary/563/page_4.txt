The paper discusses various strategies for hyper-parameter optimization (HPO), comparing traditional methods like grid search (GS) and random search (RS) with more advanced techniques such as Bayesian optimization (BO). GS exhaustively explores a fixed set of hyper-parameter values, while RS randomly samples from the search space, both treating configurations independently. In contrast, BO leverages past evaluation results to inform future selections, allowing it to converge on optimal configurations more efficiently. BO can utilize surrogate models like Gaussian processes, random forests, and tree-structured Parzen estimators, which can optimize conditional hyper-parameters effectively.

The document also highlights the challenges of training machine learning models due to time and resource constraints, leading to the development of multi-fidelity optimization algorithms such as Hyperband. Hyperband improves upon RS by using smaller datasets and allocating budgets to hyper-parameter combinations, eliminating underperformers iteratively.

Lastly, the paper discusses metaheuristic algorithms, particularly genetic algorithms (GA) and particle swarm optimization (PSO), which are commonly applied to HPO problems. GAs evolve hyper-parameter combinations through generations, while PSO relies on particle interactions to seek out global optima. These methods are particularly suited for navigating complex and non-convex search spaces in HPO.
