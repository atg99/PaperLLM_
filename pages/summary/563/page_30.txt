This section discusses the process of hyper-parameter optimization, specifically focusing on two methods: successive halving and Hyperband. 

Successive halving involves iteratively passing the better-performing half of hyper-parameter configurations to the next iteration with doubled budgets. This process continues until the optimal hyper-parameter combination is found. While more efficient than random search, successive halving faces a challenge in balancing the number of configurations against the budgets allocated to each, raising questions about whether to test fewer configurations with higher budgets or more configurations with lower budgets.

Hyperband addresses these concerns by dynamically selecting a reasonable number of configurations and balancing the trade-off between the number of configurations and their allocated budgets. It divides the total budget into pieces that are assigned to each configuration, and employs successive halving as a subroutine to eliminate poorly-performing configurations. The algorithm begins by determining budget constraints and calculating the number of configurations and budget size based on minimum and maximum budget limits. Configurations are sampled and processed through successive halving to refine the search for the best hyper-parameter combination. 

Overall, Hyperband enhances the efficiency of hyper-parameter optimization by systematically managing budget allocation and configuration selection.
