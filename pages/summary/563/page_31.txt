The successive halving algorithm, as outlined in steps 4-5, systematically eliminates poorly-performing hyper-parameter configurations while advancing well-performing ones through iterative evaluations. This method, employed within Hyperband, results in a computational complexity of O(n log n).

Bayesian Optimization HyperBand (BOHB) is a sophisticated hyper-parameter optimization (HPO) technique that merges Bayesian optimization with Hyperband, thus leveraging the strengths of both methods while mitigating their weaknesses. Unlike the original Hyperband, which relies on random search (a less efficient approach), BOHB utilizes Bayesian optimization to enhance performance and reduce execution time by effectively utilizing parallel resources. In BOHB, the Tree-structured Parzen Estimator (TPE) serves as the standard surrogate model, employing multidimensional kernel density estimators, maintaining a complexity of O(n log n). BOHB has demonstrated superior performance compared to other optimization techniques, particularly in tuning Support Vector Machines (SVM) and Deep Learning (DL) models. However, its effectiveness can be hindered if evaluations on small-budget subsets do not accurately reflect the entire training set, potentially resulting in slower convergence than standard Bayesian optimization models.

Metaheuristic algorithms, inspired by biological processes, are another category of optimization techniques adept at addressing complex problems that are non-convex, non-continuous, and non-smooth. A prominent subclass of these algorithms is population-based optimization algorithms (POAs), which include genetic algorithms, evolutionary algorithms, evolutionary strategies, and particle swarm optimization. POAs operate by initializing and iteratively updating a population of solutions, evaluating each individual until the global optimum is found. The variations among POAs largely stem from their distinct methods for generating and selecting populations, and they can be efficiently parallelized, allowing for simultaneous evaluations across multiple threads.
