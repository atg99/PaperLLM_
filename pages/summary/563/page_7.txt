The paper discusses the fundamental aspects of optimization problems, emphasizing the role of constraints in defining the feasible region where solutions can be found. An optimization problem is characterized by three main components: decision variables, an objective function to optimize, and constraints that restrict the variable values. The aim is to identify variable values that either minimize or maximize the objective function while adhering to the constraints.

In the context of machine learning, hyperparameter optimization (HPO) often involves constraints related to the feasible domain of parameters, such as the number of clusters in k-means, as well as operational constraints like time and space. Consequently, constrained optimization techniques are prevalent in HPO scenarios.

The paper highlights that many optimization problems yield only local optima rather than global optima. A local minimum is defined within a neighborhood of the feasible region, while a global minimum is the lowest point across the entire feasible region. Local optima are guaranteed to be global only in the case of convex functions, which possess a single optimum. The convexity of a function is defined using specific mathematical conditions, and a convex optimization problem requires both a convex objective function and a convex feasible region.
