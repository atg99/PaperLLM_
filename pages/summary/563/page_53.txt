This paper examines the various hyper-parameter optimization (HPO) techniques, highlighting their strengths and weaknesses in practical applications. It identifies the lack of a universally superior optimization approach across diverse datasets and metrics. The authors advocate for the establishment of standardized benchmarks within the community, similar to the COCO platform, to facilitate better comparisons among HPO algorithms. A unified metric is also suggested to enhance comparability, as current practices utilize varied metrics across different problems.

Furthermore, the paper discusses the potential of combining existing HPO models or developing new ones to leverage the strengths of multiple techniques, citing the BOHB method as a successful example that integrates Bayesian optimization and Hyperband. The authors emphasize the importance of considering both model performance and time constraints when developing HPO algorithms for real-world applications.

The issue of overfitting and generalization in HPO models is also addressed. The paper notes that optimal hyper-parameter values identified through finite evaluations may not generalize well to unseen data, akin to overfitting in machine learning models. This concern is particularly relevant for multi-fidelity algorithms like Hyperband and BOHB. To mitigate overfitting, the authors recommend using cross-validation to identify stable optimal hyper-parameter values that perform consistently across various data subsets.
