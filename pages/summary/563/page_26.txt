Gradient-based algorithms are limited in their application to hyper-parameter optimization (HPO), as they can only be used when gradients can be obtained, such as with learning rates in neural networks. However, these methods do not guarantee finding global optimums. 

Bayesian optimization (BO) is highlighted as an iterative and effective approach for HPO, distinguishing itself from grid search (GS) and random search (RS) by utilizing results from previous evaluations to inform future ones. BO relies on two critical components: a surrogate model and an acquisition function. The surrogate model approximates the objective function based on observed data, while the acquisition function balances exploration of untested areas and exploitation of promising regions based on the surrogate's predictive distribution.

The process of BO involves several steps: constructing the surrogate model, identifying optimal hyper-parameters from this model, evaluating these parameters on the actual objective function, and updating the surrogate with the new results. This cycle continues until a predefined maximum number of iterations is reached. 

BO's efficiency stems from its ability to analyze previously tested values, making it less computationally expensive than methods like GS and RS, which do not utilize prior information. However, its reliance on sequential testing means that it may not be as effective in scenarios where rapid evaluations are necessary.
