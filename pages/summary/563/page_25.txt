This paper discusses various optimization techniques used in hyper-parameter tuning, particularly focusing on Random Search (RS) and Gradient-based Optimization methods. 

RS allows for exploration of a larger search space with a limited budget compared to Grid Search (GS). Its key advantage lies in its parallelizability and resource allocation, as each evaluation is independent. RS samples a fixed number of parameter combinations from a predefined distribution, which enhances system efficiency by minimizing the time spent in poorly performing regions. The computational complexity of RS is O(n), and it can identify global or near-global optima if provided with sufficient budgets. However, a significant drawback of RS is that it does not leverage previously successful evaluations, leading to unnecessary function evaluations.

In contrast, Gradient-based Optimization, specifically gradient descent, calculates the gradient of variables to identify promising directions for optimization. This method is effective for continuous hyper-parameters and has a time complexity of O(nk) for k hyper-parameters. While gradient descent converges faster to local optima, it is limited to convex functions, as it may only find local optima in non-convex scenarios and cannot optimize categorical hyper-parameters due to the absence of gradient directions.

In conclusion, both RS and GS suffer from inefficiencies due to their independence from previous evaluations, which can lead to wasted time in suboptimal regions. This limitation may be addressed by alternative methods such as Bayesian optimization, which utilizes historical evaluation data to inform future evaluations.
