The paper discusses decision tree (DT) models and their associated hyper-parameters, which are critical for optimizing performance in machine learning tasks. It highlights two primary measuring functions: Gini impurity and information gain, used to evaluate splits. The split selection method, referred to as 'splitter', can either choose the best split or a random one. Additionally, hyper-parameters such as 'max features', 'min samples split', 'min samples leaf', 'max leaf nodes', and 'min weight fraction leaf' can be adjusted to enhance the model's effectiveness.

The paper further explores ensemble algorithms based on DT models, including random forest (RF), extra trees (ET), and extreme gradient boosting (XGBoost). RF employs the bagging method, building DTs on various randomly generated subsets and selecting the class with the majority vote. ET, while similar to RF, uses all samples to construct DTs and randomly selects feature sets, opting for random splits instead of optimizing them. XGBoost, designed for speed and performance, utilizes boosting and gradient descent methods, where each new DT is influenced by the results of previous models to minimize a specific objective function.

Overall, the paper emphasizes that while tree-based ensemble models maintain similar hyper-parameters to DT models, they also require tuning additional parameters, particularly the number of trees used in the ensemble.
