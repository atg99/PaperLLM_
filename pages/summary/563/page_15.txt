The paper discusses several kernel functions and their associated hyper-parameters, particularly in the context of Support Vector Regression (SVR) and Naïve Bayes (NB) algorithms. 

For kernel functions, it presents the Radial Basis Function (RBF) kernel and the Sigmoid kernel. The RBF kernel is defined as f(x) = exp(-||x - x0||^2), while the Sigmoid kernel is defined as f(x) = tanh(x^T * xj + r). After selecting a kernel type, hyper-parameters must be tuned. The 'gamma' parameter, which affects the RBF and polynomial kernels, and 'coef0', which influences the polynomial and sigmoid kernels, are highlighted. Additionally, the polynomial kernel has a degree hyper-parameter 'd', and the SVR model includes an 'epsilon' hyper-parameter that represents the distance error in its loss function.

The paper also explores Naïve Bayes classifiers, which are based on Bayes' theorem and assume independent features. The objective function for Naïve Bayes is expressed as maximizing the posterior probabilities of features given a target variable. Different types of Naïve Bayes models are identified, including Bernoulli, Gaussian, multinomial, and complement NB. For Gaussian Naïve Bayes, the features are assumed to follow a Gaussian distribution, and the model relies on maximum likelihood estimation to determine the mean and variance. Notably, Gaussian NB does not require tuning hyper-parameters, and its effectiveness depends on the dataset's adherence to Gaussian distribution characteristics.
