The paper discusses various clustering algorithms and their applications in clinical research, highlighting hierarchical clustering, density-based clustering, and grid-based clustering. Hierarchical clustering organizes data into a tree structure, making it effective for identifying relationships among clusters, though it has high time complexity. Density-based clustering, exemplified by DBSCAN, identifies clusters of arbitrary shapes but struggles with high-dimensional data and irregular densities. Grid-based clustering, such as STING, improves processing speed by converting data into a grid structure, though it may have limitations with non-convex shapes.

In clinical contexts, cluster analysis helps categorize heterogeneous patient groups into homogeneous subgroups, facilitating tailored medical management, as demonstrated in studies on fibromyalgia and hypertension. The paper also explores association rules, which uncover correlations in large datasets, originally applied in retail but increasingly relevant in medical research. The Apriori algorithm and its variant, the FP growth algorithm, are discussed for their efficiency in mining association rules, particularly in identifying risk factors for diseases like stroke and evaluating treatment effects in diabetes patients. As personalized treatment becomes a focus, cluster analysis and association rule mining are poised for significant future applications in clinical research.
