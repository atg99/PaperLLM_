Hierarchical clustering is another common technique that builds a hierarchy of clusters either through an agglomerative approach (merging smaller clusters into larger ones) or a divisive approach (splitting larger clusters into smaller ones). This method provides a dendrogram that visually represents the data structure.

Density-based clustering, such as DBSCAN, identifies clusters based on the density of data points. It can effectively find arbitrarily shaped clusters and is robust against noise.

Lastly, model-based clustering assumes that the data is generated from a mixture of probability distributions. The Expectation-Maximization algorithm is often used in this context to estimate the parameters of these distributions.

In summary, SVMs are effective for high-dimensional data classification but can be resource-intensive with large sample sizes. Competitive risk models provide accurate estimations in survival analysis with multiple endpoints, offering advantages over traditional methods. Unsupervised learning techniques, including various clustering methods, are essential for categorizing data when labeled information is sparse, each with unique strengths and applications.
