Wu et al. conducted a study to develop a competitive risk model for cecum cancer survival analysis using data from the SEER database. The methodology involved several key steps: extracting demographic and clinical characteristics, treatment modalities, and causes of death for patients; excluding incomplete data; applying Cox regression and two competitive risk models for analysis; and comparing results across the models. The findings indicated that the competitive risk model was more effective for survival data with multiple endpoints.

In a related study, Martínez Steele et al. employed Principal Component Analysis (PCA) to derive dietary patterns and assess the nutritional quality of the population. Their approach included extracting data from the NHANES database for 2009-2010, collecting demographic information and dietary recall data, applying data weighting and exclusion criteria, and utilizing PCA along with Gaussian regression and restricted cubic splines to analyze dietary associations in the U.S. population. 

These studies illustrate the use of advanced statistical models and data mining techniques to analyze health-related data and dietary patterns, providing valuable insights into cancer survival and nutrition.
