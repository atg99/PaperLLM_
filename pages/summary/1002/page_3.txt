The paper discusses data-mining methods relevant to clinical big data analysis, distinguishing between supervised and unsupervised learning approaches. Supervised learning involves the use of dependent variables or labels to generate predictions, employing techniques such as linear regression, decision trees, and support vector machines. It emphasizes the importance of dataset partitioning into training, validation, and test sets to prevent overfitting. Conversely, unsupervised learning does not utilize labels, focusing instead on uncovering internal data structures through methods like principal component analysis and clustering.

The authors highlight the significance of data-mining algorithms in deriving valuable insights from clinical big data, which is crucial for informed clinical decision-making and risk assessment. Additionally, the paper provides an overview of prominent medical public databases, detailing their range, location, founding year, cost, and URLs, which serve as resources for researchers in the field. These databases include SEER, MIMIC, NHANES, and others, offering a wealth of information for various health-related studies.
