The paper discusses the challenges and opportunities associated with the application of data mining in clinical big data, particularly in military medical research. It highlights the issues of dimensionality heterogeneity, timeliness, scarcity, and irregularity in existing clinical data, which hinder the effective use of such data. The authors emphasize the contributions of computer scientists in utilizing big data through data mining, defined as the extraction of useful information from large datasets that may be incomplete or noisy. 

The paper outlines the various components of data mining, including its distinction from traditional statistical methods, and identifies machine learning as a primary analytical method within this field. It notes the potential benefits of data mining in clinical research, especially with the availability of high-quality public medical databases that can facilitate broader participation in medical data mining.

Key sections of the paper provide an overview of common public medical databases, the multidisciplinary nature of data mining, and the types of models used in data mining, specifically descriptive and predictive models. Descriptive models focus on identifying patterns within data, while predictive models aim to forecast future values based on existing data attributes.

The authors also discuss the specific tasks associated with data mining models, including generalizing patterns and predicting variable values through classification and regression methods. Finally, the paper reviews the advantages and disadvantages of data mining in clinical analysis, offering insights into future applications in medical research.
