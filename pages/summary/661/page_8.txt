This paper presents an investigation into a multi-scale residual graph convolution network (MSR-GCN) designed to predict future human motion based on observed historical data. The study conducts ablation experiments to evaluate various model configurations, including the number of scale levels, the impact of intermediate losses, and the use of graph convolutional networks (GCNs) versus fully connected layers (FCLs). The results, derived from the CMU Mocap dataset, demonstrate that all model designs contribute positively to accuracy.

Key findings include that the MSR-GCN consistently outperforms its variants across different action types and forecasting times, as shown in the tables and figures. Specifically, the default grouping of human joints yields the best average errors in predictions compared to alternative grouping strategies. The model's architecture, which incorporates intermediate supervision across scales, is validated as essential for effective motion prediction.

In conclusion, the proposed MSR-GCN surpasses previous state-of-the-art methods in performance on benchmark datasets, with plans for further exploration of multi-scale grouping strategies in future research.
