This paper discusses the development and evaluation of a novel network architecture, the Multi-Scale Residual Graph Convolutional Network (MSR-GCN), for human motion prediction. The MSR-GCN decodes combined features at four different scales and employs intermediate supervisions through L2 distance computations between predicted poses and ground truth. This method enhances prediction accuracy by facilitating the learning of features from coarse to fine scales. The architecture incorporates residual connections, which allow the network to learn the residual vector between input and ground truth poses at all levels.

The implementation utilizes the Adam optimizer with a learning rate of 2e-4, trained on an NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPU. The effectiveness of MSR-GCN is validated through experiments on two benchmark datasets, Human3.6M and CMU Mocap, using the Mean Per Joint Position Error (MPJPE) as the evaluation metric. 

The Human3.6M dataset includes data from seven subjects performing 15 action categories, while the CMU Mocap dataset contains 8 action categories. The results indicate that GCN-based approaches, including MSR-GCN, significantly outperform RNN-based methods, with MSR-GCN achieving superior results compared to its GCN counterparts. Quantitative and qualitative analyses demonstrate that MSR-GCN excels in both short-term and long-term predictions, confirming its effectiveness in human motion prediction tasks.
