The paper presents a comparative analysis of various models for short-term and long-term action prediction using the CMU Mocap dataset. Key findings from the tables indicate the performance of models such as Residual Sup, DMGNN, Traj-GCN, and MSR-GCN across different action categories and time intervals. For short-term predictions, Residual Sup consistently achieves the best results across most scenarios, while for long-term predictions at 1000ms, DMGNN shows superior performance in certain categories.

Additionally, the paper discusses the effectiveness of the proposed multi-scale architecture of MSR-GCN through ablation studies. It explores the impact of various elements, including the number of scale levels and the presence of intermediate supervision losses. Results indicate that the MSR-GCN outperforms its variants, highlighting the advantages of a multi-scale approach and the necessity of intermediate supervision for improved accuracy.

The study also emphasizes the role of residual GCNs, demonstrating that they significantly enhance pose prediction quality compared to networks with only fully connected layers. Overall, the findings point to the effectiveness of the proposed methods in handling high-frequency motions and improving prediction accuracy across different actions and scenarios. Further analysis is available in the supplemental material.
