This paper introduces the Multi-Scale Residual Graph Convolution Network (MSR-GCN) for predicting 3D human motion. The authors build upon the idea that human poses can be represented as fully connected graphs of joints, utilizing graph convolution networks (GCNs) to learn relationships between joints without being limited by physical distances. While previous works have shown promising results, they often overlook the hierarchical structure of human poses, which can be leveraged to improve prediction accuracy.

The MSR-GCN adopts a multi-scale approach by organizing multiple GCNs into descending and ascending paths. The descending path extracts features from fine to coarse scales, while the ascending path does the opposite. This dual-path structure allows for a more comprehensive understanding of human motion, as it stabilizes the motion patterns by focusing on coarser representations before refining to finer details. The architecture also incorporates intermediate supervision at each scale to enhance the learning of representative features, ultimately improving the quality of future pose predictions.

The paper contrasts its approach with previous works, such as that by Li et al., which also recognized the hierarchical nature of human poses but used a different strategy for feature extraction and decoding. The MSR-GCN's unique U-Net-like organization and residual connections between input and output poses facilitate the learning of residuals instead of directly predicting target poses, further contributing to its effectiveness.

In summary, the key contributions of this paper include the introduction of a novel multi-scale architecture for human pose prediction, the innovative feature extraction mechanisms through descending and ascending GCN blocks, and the implementation of intermediate supervision to boost learning efficacy, addressing limitations seen in existing methods.
