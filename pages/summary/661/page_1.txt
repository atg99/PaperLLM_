In this paper, the authors introduce the Multi-Scale Residual Graph Convolution Network (MSR-GCN) designed specifically for human motion prediction. The approach leverages the strengths of graph convolutional networks to capture the dynamic relationships among pose joints while addressing the inherent challenges of predicting human motion, which can be unpredictable and non-periodic. 

The MSR-GCN operates on multiple scales, extracting features from both fine and coarse representations of human poses. By progressively abstracting poses, the model stabilizes the motion representation, which enhances the prediction accuracy. The architecture combines features at different scales and decodes them to compute the residuals between the observed and predicted poses. Additionally, the implementation of intermediate supervisions on all predicted poses ensures that the network learns more robust and representative features.

The effectiveness of the proposed method is validated through experiments on two benchmark datasets: Human3.6M and CMU Mocap. The results indicate that MSR-GCN outperforms existing state-of-the-art methods in human motion prediction, demonstrating its potential application in various domains such as human-computer interaction and autonomous systems. The authors provide access to their code and pre-trained models for further research and development.
