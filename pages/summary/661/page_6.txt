The paper presents a comparison of various methods for short-term and long-term action prediction using the H3.6M dataset and CMU Mocap dataset. Results are summarized in Tables 1 and 2, highlighting the performance of different models across multiple action categories and times. The MSR-GCN model consistently shows superior performance, achieving the best results for short-term predictions across all action categories and maintaining a strong showing in long-term predictions as well.

In short-term predictions, the residual supervised method outperforms others in most scenarios, while the MSR-GCN model closely follows, particularly excelling in average performance metrics. For long-term predictions, MSR-GCN again leads in several action categories, with results indicating that it consistently ranks second for other actions, demonstrating its robustness.

The analysis of performance gains reveals that predicting residuals between input and target poses is more effective than direct target pose prediction. This insight is supported by comparative results, showcasing that models utilizing global residuals, like MSR-GCN, significantly enhance prediction accuracy. Furthermore, the inclusion of Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) in the model design contributes positively to performance, illustrating the importance of the model's structure.

Overall, the findings underscore the effectiveness of the MSR-GCN model in action prediction tasks, with clear advantages in both short- and long-term forecasting, supported by empirical evidence and detailed performance analysis.
