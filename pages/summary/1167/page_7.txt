The paper discusses various clustering algorithms, focusing on hierarchical approaches. It outlines a general agglomerative hierarchical clustering method that merges clusters based on an optimal objective function, particularly suitable for quantitative data. The non-parametric algorithm developed by Gowda and Krishna in 1978 utilizes nearest neighbor techniques to identify mutual neighborhood values and is notable for its low storage requirements and ability to handle non-spherical clusters. 

The Clustering Using Representative (CURE) algorithm is highlighted for its robustness to outliers and capability to identify clusters of varying sizes and shapes, albeit with lower quality compared to BIRCH. CURE employs data sampling and partitioning to enhance scalability, representing clusters with scattered points that are adjusted to the cluster's centroid as the algorithm progresses. Input parameters significantly influence the clustering results, and CURE is designed for datasets with numerical attributes.

Additionally, the paper mentions divisive hierarchical clustering as a method that systematically divides clusters, starting with all objects in a single cluster, to refine the clustering process. Overall, the paper emphasizes the strengths and applications of these clustering techniques in handling complex data structures.
