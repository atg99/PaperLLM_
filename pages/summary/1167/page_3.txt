The paper by A.E. Ezugwu et al. presents a systematic review of clustering algorithms, emphasizing their various measures of similarity and evaluation criteria. It references prior studies that highlight the lack of a universally superior clustering algorithm for mixed or categorical datasets. Prior works, such as those by Oyelade et al. and Jain et al., have explored the suitability of clustering methods for specific applications, including gene expression data and non-numeric constraints.

Despite significant advancements in clustering analysis, the literature remains fragmented, making it difficult for researchers to navigate recent developments effectively. The authors propose a comprehensive survey to address this gap, posing the central research question regarding the state-of-the-art clustering methods and their application domains. Additional sub-research questions focus on identifying existing techniques, their applications, similarity measures used, differences between traditional and recent methods, and unexplored challenges.

The survey aims to provide a thorough review of clustering techniques across various data mining-related fields, highlighting novel applications and guiding new researchers in the domain. It also seeks to assist experts in developing new algorithms for emerging challenges. The contributions of the study include a systematic review of traditional and recent clustering techniques, a concise presentation of clustering concepts and taxonomy, discussions on current research issues, and future research directions.

The methodology section outlines the systematic literature review process adopted to ensure comprehensive coverage and minimize bias. Keywords relevant to the survey's goals were formulated and used to search academic databases for literature on clustering. The search was conducted in multiple rounds, targeting peer-reviewed journals and conference proceedings, with a focus on articles published since 1999 to capture the evolution of unsupervised classification techniques.
