The paper discusses search-based clustering algorithms, which are nature-inspired metaheuristic approaches designed to automate the clustering process without requiring prior information about dataset attributes. Traditional clustering methods often necessitate knowledge of the number of clusters, which can impose additional computational burdens. This issue, known as the automatic clustering problem, is particularly pronounced in real-world datasets characterized by high density and dimensionality.

Automatic clustering techniques address this challenge by autonomously determining the structure and number of clusters, thereby enhancing efficiency in identifying and classifying unlabeled data points. Unlike traditional algorithms that may only find local optima due to their reliance on initial conditions, automatic algorithms, through their design, are more likely to yield global optimal solutions. The paper emphasizes that these metaheuristic approaches treat clustering as an optimization problem, aiming to minimize dissimilarity within clusters while maximizing it between clusters.

The authors categorize various nature-inspired algorithms suitable for clustering, including Genetic Algorithm (GA), Differential Evolution (DE), Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO), and Ant Colony Optimization (ACO), among others. These algorithms fall into two main categories: Evolutionary algorithms (GA and DE) and Swarm intelligence algorithms (the rest). Both categories share similar design steps, starting with random initialization, evaluating candidate solutions, and iteratively refining the population to converge toward the best solution.

Overall, the paper highlights the advantages of search-based clustering algorithms over traditional methods, particularly in terms of their flexibility, convergence speed, and ability to produce high-quality solutions in complex, high-dimensional datasets. It sets the stage for further exploration of the application of these algorithms in clustering problems.
