The paper discusses various approaches to divisive clustering, contrasting it with agglomerative methods. Divisive clustering employs a top-down strategy, initially considering data objects as a single cluster and progressively splitting it until the desired number of clusters is achieved. While full enumeration of potential bipartitions yields optimal results, it is computationally expensive. Alternative methods, such as bisecting K-Means and reference-point-based dissimilarity measures, have been proposed to enhance efficiency and accuracy.

Divisive clustering can be categorized into monothetic and polythetic methods. Monothetic clustering focuses on single-variable splits, which simplifies computations and aids in interpreting cluster structures. Conversely, polythetic clustering analyzes all variables simultaneously, which can lead to scalability issues with large datasets. Recent advancements include hierarchical grid clustering, which efficiently manages large datasets, and model-based techniques that enhance clustering performance in high-dimensional contexts.

The paper emphasizes the growing interest in clustering categorical data and highlights various methods developed to address challenges in this area, such as the Maximum Dependency of Attributes and bi-clustering approaches. Additionally, it presents the scalable weighted iterative flow-clustering technique (SWIFT), which is designed for high-dimensional datasets and aims to identify rare populations while maintaining efficiency.

Overall, the paper illustrates the evolution of divisive clustering methodologies and their applications, particularly in handling complex and large datasets, while noting the significance of computational efficiency and clustering quality.
