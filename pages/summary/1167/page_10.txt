The paper discusses partitional clustering methods, which are widely used in engineering applications for their efficiency in representing and compressing large databases. However, these methods face challenges, such as the need for predefined parameters and the potential for suboptimal solutions due to their local search nature. The authors highlight that partitional clustering is an NP-hard optimization problem, making it computationally intensive to find optimal solutions.

The paper categorizes partitional clustering into various types, including Hard/Crisp Clustering, Fuzzy Clustering, and Mixture Resolving Clustering. Hard Clustering algorithms assign each data object to a single cluster and include techniques like Graph-theoretic clustering, Density-based clustering, and Model-based clustering.

Graph-theoretic clustering represents data objects as nodes connected by edges, reflecting their proximities. Clusters are formed based on edge density, aiming for high intra-cluster connectivity and low inter-cluster connectivity. The paper points out the advantages of representing clusters in graphs but also notes their limitations in handling outliers.

The authors provide examples of graph-based clustering methods, including the k-nearest-neighbor graph model and Delaunay triangulation graph. They emphasize the need for an understanding of cluster shapes to select appropriate heuristics for identifying edges, indicating that no definitive theory exists for choosing optimal graph properties for clustering applications.
