The paper by Ezugwu et al. presents a systematic review of clustering methods and algorithms, focusing on their applications and performance while excluding general data mining techniques and specific domain comparisons. The inclusion criteria emphasize articles that specifically examine clustering methods, their variants, and applications, while only English-language publications from reputable sources were considered.

The review summarizes past research on metaheuristic algorithms, highlighting key studies and their contributions to clustering. Notable works include Jain et al. (1999), who explored various clustering approaches, and Murtagh (1983), who provided an in-depth look at agglomerative hierarchical clustering algorithms. Other references outline advancements in clustering methodologies, including density-based algorithms and multi-view clustering.

The paper discusses the limitations of hierarchical clustering methods, such as high computational complexity and sensitivity to noise. Agglomerative hierarchical clustering is described as a process where clusters are formed by merging the closest objects iteratively, with Ward's method noted for its unique approach based on K-Means' objective function rather than traditional linkage metrics.

Overall, the study serves as a comprehensive survey of clustering techniques, their evolution, and practical applications, while also identifying challenges and future research directions in the field.
