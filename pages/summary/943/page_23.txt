The paper discusses the application of machine learning in various biological data types, highlighting specific challenges and appropriate models for each area. Key concepts include metabolite annotation, medical image recognition, RNA sequencing, molecular structure analysis, and protein-protein interaction networks. 

Metabolite annotation requires normalization to ensure comparability across datasets. Medical image recognition employs methods like 2D CNNs, ResNets, and autoencoders, but faces challenges due to systematic differences in data collection and the difficulty of obtaining large, consistent datasets. 

In molecular structure analysis, models such as Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs), multilayer perceptrons, and recurrent neural networks (RNNs) using text-based molecular representations like SMILES are utilized for tasks like predicting antibiotic activity and drug toxicity. However, there is a scarcity of experimental data for many small molecules.

For protein-protein interaction networks, GCNs and graph embedding techniques are used to understand polypharmacology and protein functions, although challenges arise from incomplete interaction networks and the complexity of cellular locations affecting interactions.

Overall, the paper emphasizes that while machine learning has proven effective across various biological data types, challenges like high dimensionality and systematic differences in data collection persist, necessitating normalization to ensure reliable comparisons and analyses. Normalization typically involves standardizing data by adjusting for means and standard deviations to mitigate systematic measurement differences.
