The paper provides a framework for evaluating articles that utilize machine learning on biological data, emphasizing critical considerations for readers and reviewers. Key questions include:

1. **Dataset Description**: The paper stresses the importance of a thorough description of the dataset, including its assembly process and availability. It warns against papers that focus heavily on machine learning methodology without adequately addressing the dataset used. Justification is required if existing datasets are employed.

2. **Test Set Validity**: It highlights the necessity for a valid test set, ensuring no data leakage between training and test sets. The test set must be sufficiently large and representative of the scenarios a typical user might encounter. Detailed discussion of the training and test set composition is mandated, alongside measures taken to prevent data leakage.

3. **Model Choice Justification**: Authors should provide reasoning for their choice of machine learning models, particularly for complex methods like neural networks. The paper encourages transparency about the trial-and-error process in model selection.

4. **Comparison with Other Methods**: Novel methods should be benchmarked against established methods that demonstrate strong performance. The paper advocates for comparisons across various model types, noting that simpler models can sometimes match the performance of complex ones.

5. **Skepticism Towards Results**: The paper cautions against claims of excessively high accuracy, suggesting that such claims often indicate issues with cross-validation rather than genuine breakthroughs. Both authors and reviewers are advised to remain vigilant regarding the plausibility of reported results.

Overall, the framework outlined aims to enhance the rigor and transparency of machine learning applications in biological research.
