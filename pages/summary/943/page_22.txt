The table presents recommendations for employing machine learning strategies across various biological data types, highlighting input data, example prediction tasks, recommended models, and associated challenges.

For gene sequence data, tasks include predicting genome accessibility, 3D genome organization, and enhancer-promoter interactions. Recommended models are 1D convolutional neural networks (CNNs), recurrent neural networks (RNNs), and transformers. Challenges involve repetitive regions, sparse areas of interest, and managing very long sequences.

In the context of protein sequence data, tasks encompass predicting protein structure, function, and protein-protein interactions. Suggested models include 2D CNNs, residual networks (ResNets) utilizing covariation data, multilayer perceptrons with windowing, and transformers. Challenges here include data accessibility due to the dispersed nature of metagenome data and validation difficulties resulting from data leakage.

For protein 3D structure, tasks such as protein model refinement, quality assessment, and mutation stability changes are noted. Recommended models include graph convolutional networks (GCNs), 3D CNNs using coordinates, and traditional methods based on structural features. Challenges consist of data scarcity, particularly regarding protein complexes and disordered proteins.

Gene expression tasks involve analyzing intergenic interactions and transcription machinery organization. Recommended approaches are clustering, CNNs, and autoencoders. Challenges include the unclear relationship between co-expression and function, along with high dimensionality and noise.

Lastly, for mass spectrometry, tasks like peak detection in spectra are addressed, with CNNs and traditional methods suggested. The main challenge is the lack of standardized benchmarks.
