The paper outlines a systematic approach to training machine learning models, emphasizing the importance of understanding the data and the prediction task before any coding begins. An essential first step is to gain a biological understanding of the data, including its origins, potential noise sources, and theoretical predictions based on biological principles. For instance, predicting secondary structures in proteins from amino acid frequencies illustrates the necessity of this understanding. 

Next, data should be split into training, validation, and test sets. The training set is used to update the model parameters, while the validation set, typically about 10% of the data, helps monitor training, select hyperparameters, and prevent overfitting. K-fold cross-validation is often employed to enhance hyperparameter selection by partitioning the training data into k subsets. The test set, also around 10%, is reserved for assessing the model's real-world performance and should be used minimally to avoid bias.

Model selection follows, guided by the data characteristics and prediction goals. The training set is utilized to train the model in alignment with the best practices of the chosen software framework, which often involves tuning hyperparameters through random or grid search methods combined with cross-validation. Additionally, model ensembling, where outputs from multiple models are averaged, can enhance accuracy. Finally, the model's performance is evaluated using the test set.
