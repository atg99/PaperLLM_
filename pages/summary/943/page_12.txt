The complexity of neural networks arises from their numerous parameters, layers, and non-linear activation functions, which can lead to overfitting if not properly managed. Techniques such as regularization, dropout, and early stopping are commonly employed to mitigate these issues. Additionally, the choice of optimization algorithms and learning rates can significantly impact training efficiency and model performance. 

The integration of advanced architectures like Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) and autoencoders enhances the ability of neural networks to process complex biological data. GCNs leverage graph structures to capture relationships between entities, allowing for more nuanced predictions in biological contexts, such as drug-gene interactions. Autoencoders facilitate dimensionality reduction and feature extraction, enabling the identification of latent representations that retain essential characteristics of the data while compressing it into a more manageable form. 

Overall, the application of these neural network architectures in biological research demonstrates their potential for addressing a variety of challenges, ranging from understanding molecular interactions to predicting biological outcomes. The ongoing development of training methodologies and software tools continues to enhance their effectiveness in computational biology.
