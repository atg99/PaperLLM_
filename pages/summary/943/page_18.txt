The paper discusses the current state and future potential of automated machine learning in biology, highlighting the need for reliable and flexible models that can be used by experimentalists without requiring extensive machine learning expertise. It emphasizes the importance of rigorous model validation and comparison to identify top-performing models and guide future research. The development of benchmark datasets and validation tasks, such as ProteinNet, ATOM3D, and TAPE, is crucial for the field's advancement. However, researchers must be cautious of over-optimizing for specific benchmarks and should rely on blind assessments, like CASP and CAFA, to evaluate model performance objectively.

The authors acknowledge the diversity of biological data, which complicates the establishment of general guidelines for machine learning applications in biology. They provide insights on effective machine learning practices while also cautioning that it may not be suitable for every scenario, particularly when data is insufficient or when understanding is prioritized over prediction. The evolving boundaries of machine learning utility in biology will continue to shift with advances in experimental data. Ultimately, the paper recognizes the significant impact of machine learning on biology and its potential for future contributions.
