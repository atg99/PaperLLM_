The paper discusses the concepts of bias and variance in machine learning models, highlighting that a model with high bias makes stronger assumptions and may underfit the data, while a model with low bias can adapt to various functions but may lead to overfitting due to high variance. The balance between bias and variance is crucial to avoid overfitting or underfitting.

It then transitions to traditional machine learning methods, emphasizing their strengths and weaknesses compared to deep learning. Traditional methods, such as support vector machines and random forests, are recommended as initial approaches for tasks involving biological data due to their faster development and testing times. The paper notes that while deep learning is powerful and trendy, it is best suited for large datasets with many structured features, which may not always be available in biological contexts. Additionally, deep learning poses challenges in estimating feature importance and model confidence, which are often critical in biological applications. Therefore, it is advisable to compare traditional methods with deep learning approaches when feasible.
