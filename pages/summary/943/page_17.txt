The paper discusses the growing integration of machine learning in biological research, emphasizing the significance of understanding the methodologies to prevent the dissemination of poor models and misleading results. It highlights that advancements in software, hardware, and methodology have facilitated this trend, with large technology companies aiding academic researchers in biological studies.

Most current successes involve applying algorithms from other fields, like convolutional neural networks and recurrent neural networks, directly to biological data. A promising future direction is the development of algorithms specifically designed for biological questions, which could lead to simpler, more interpretable models that leverage known biological structures. This shift may enhance applications in biological reaction systems and pharmacokinetics, moving towards generative models capable of designing novel proteins.

The emergence of differentiable programming, which utilizes automatic differentiation to optimize parameters in algorithms, is also discussed. This approach shows potential for improving models related to protein structure prediction and molecular dynamics. The availability of differentiable software packages will support this progress.

Furthermore, the paper notes that the public sharing of trained models has improved biological data analysis, allowing researchers to utilize existing models without training them from scratch. The rise of automated machine learning pipelines also aids non-experts in model training, although a solid understanding of the methods remains crucial for effective application and interpretation.
