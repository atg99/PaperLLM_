GCNs excel in situations where relationships between nodes are essential for understanding the dataset, such as social networks, molecular structures, or knowledge graphs. They operate by aggregating information from a node's neighbors, allowing the model to learn representations that take into account the local structure of the graph. This makes GCNs effective in applications like predicting molecular properties or classifying nodes in a graph.

In the context of biological data, GCNs can be leveraged for tasks such as predicting interactions between proteins or understanding the functional relationships within biological networks. Their ability to capture complex relationships in non-Euclidean spaces provides a powerful tool for biological investigations, complementing traditional methods like RNNs and transformers.

In summary, RNNs are well-suited for sequential data, particularly in natural language processing and biological sequence analysis. The introduction of attention mechanisms has led to the development of transformers, which have surpassed RNNs in performance for many tasks, including those in biology. GCNs add another dimension to data analysis by effectively handling and interpreting interconnected data structures. Together, these models represent significant advancements in machine learning applications across various domains, including biology.
