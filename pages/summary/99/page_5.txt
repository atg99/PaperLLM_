This paper discusses a deep neural network architecture, specifically a combination of Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) and Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU), designed to handle sequential data, particularly in the context of protein sequence analysis. The authors highlight the advantages of Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) over traditional neural networks, particularly their ability to utilize and memorize sequence information of arbitrary length for making predictions.

The implementation is carried out using the PyTorch library, with performance enhancements through GPU computing. The input consists of a product of the sequence length and the amino acid vocabulary size, allowing the CNN to extract relevant amino acid features using varying filter sizes. A 1D convolution is applied to the Position-Specific Scoring Matrix (PSSM) input, preserving spatial relationships and transforming the data into representative values.

The paper details the mathematical operations for convolution and pooling layers, emphasizing the role of pooling in reducing matrix sizes and computational load while maintaining key features. Zero-padding is utilized to adjust input sizes and apply filters effectively at the border positions of the matrices.

After feature extraction via CNN, a multi-layer GRU is employed to analyze the input sequence. The GRU's architecture, which includes update and reset gates, addresses the vanishing gradient problem typically encountered in RNNs, enabling the model to retain long-term dependencies. The update gate's role is explained mathematically, illustrating how it determines the amount of past information to be retained for future predictions.

Overall, the study provides a comprehensive approach to leveraging deep learning techniques for protein sequence classification, combining the strengths of CNNs and GRUs to enhance predictive accuracy.
