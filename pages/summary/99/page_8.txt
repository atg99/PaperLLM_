The paper discusses the comparative performance of a proposed method, specifically a Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) model, against previous state-of-the-art techniques for analyzing Position-Specific Scoring Matrix (PSSM) profiles. The previous techniques typically aggregated amino acid compositions into a vector or matrix, which preserved feature information but lost order information. The GRU method addresses this gap by incorporating order information, leading to improved classification performance.

Experiments were conducted using various classifiers, including k-nearest neighbors (kNN), Random Forest (RF), support vector machines (SVM), and Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN). Hyper-parameter tuning was performed for each classifier to optimize accuracy. The results, summarized in Table 3, indicate that the GRU outperformed other methods across most evaluation metrics, as confirmed by a two-proportion z-test at a 95% confidence level. The GRU achieved an Area Under the Curve (AUC) of 0.97 in cross-validation and 0.95 in independent tests, highlighting its effectiveness in classifying protein functions related to electron transport.

The paper also benchmarks the proposed method against different sensitive alignment methods, including PSI-BLAST and hidden Markov models (HMM). The methodology for generating these alignments aligns with previous bioinformatics research, which has shown the applicability of sequence alignments in protein classification tasks. Overall, the findings support the importance of order information in enhancing the predictive capabilities of models for protein function classification.
