The study investigates amino acid frequencies in two distinct datasets, focusing on differences between electron transport proteins and general transport proteins. Key findings indicate that specific amino acids, particularly E, I, L, F, R, D, Q, K, and S, play a significant role in classifying electron transport proteins. The analysis utilized standard error bars to demonstrate statistical significance, with non-overlapping bars indicating notable differences in amino acid distributions.

In terms of model selection, the research explored various hyper-parameters, including convolutional feature size and fully connected layer size. A filter size of 32 in the fully connected layer yielded the highest accuracy of 93.5% in cross-validation and 92.3% in independent datasets, suggesting that larger filter sizes did not enhance performance. The model exhibited consistency across datasets without overfitting. Further tests on GRU hidden layer sizes revealed that a size of 200 produced optimal results. The study concludes with a summary of the GRU model's layers and parameters, providing a foundation for subsequent experiments.
