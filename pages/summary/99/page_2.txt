This paper presents an innovative approach to address the identification of electron transport proteins through the integration of 1D convolutional neural networks (CNN) and gated recurrent units (GRU). The focus is on predicting the function of these proteins, which are crucial for cellular respiration and energy production in the form of adenosine triphosphate (ATP). Cellular respiration is described as a mechanism that enables cells to extract energy from food molecules, specifically sugars, by accumulating electrons from organic compounds.

The electron transport chain, a key component of cellular respiration, consists of five protein complexes (I, II, III, IV, and V) that facilitate the transfer and storage of electrons. The movement of electrons through these complexes leads to the pumping of protons across membranes, ultimately resulting in ATP synthesis. The paper emphasizes the significance of identifying electron transport proteins, as dysfunction in these complexes is linked to various diseases, highlighting the need for advanced computational techniques in this domain.

Several existing computational methods and databases, such as the Transporter Classification Database (TCDB), have been utilized for the identification of electron transport proteins. Previous studies have employed machine learning techniques and biochemical properties to differentiate these proteins, including the use of amino acid composition and PSSM profiles. The paper builds on this foundation by proposing a combined CNN and GRU model to improve prediction accuracy in identifying these essential proteins and their functions, thus contributing to a better understanding of cellular processes and potential therapeutic strategies for related diseases.
