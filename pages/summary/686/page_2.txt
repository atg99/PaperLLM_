This paper presents a novel method for predicting protein complex distances, named CDPred, which utilizes a deep learning architecture that integrates a deep residual network with channel-wise and spatial-wise attention mechanisms. Unlike existing methods that predict binary contacts, CDPred focuses on predicting inter-chain distance maps specifically for homodimers and heterodimers. It leverages multiple sequence alignments (MSAs) generated from the BFD and Uniclust databases, alongside predicted distance maps for monomers in dimers.

CDPred is rigorously tested against various methods including DNCON2_inter, DeepComplex, DeepHomo, and GLINTER on multiple homodimer and heterodimer datasets. The results demonstrate that CDPred significantly outperforms these methods in terms of contact prediction precision, accuracy rate, and AUC score. For instance, on the HomoTest1 dataset, CDPred's top L/5 contact prediction precision reached 60.94%, far exceeding the closest competitor, DeepHomo, by a margin of 23.54 percentage points.

Further analysis reveals that the depth of MSAs has a positive effect on prediction accuracy, with the combination of predictions from both BFD and Uniclust MSAs yielding the best results. CDPred's performance is consistently superior to that of CDPred_BFD and CDPred_Uniclust, indicating the effectiveness of averaging predictions from different MSAs.

In evaluating heterodimer predictions on datasets HeteroTest1 and HeteroTest2, CDPred also demonstrates superior performance compared to GLINTER, showcasing the robustness and versatility of this new approach for protein complex distance prediction. Overall, the findings suggest that CDPred provides a significant advancement in the field of protein structure prediction, particularly in accurately determining inter-chain distances.
