The paper discusses the optimization of maximizing direct co-evolutionary signals in protein complex predictions. It highlights that integrating two types of co-evolutionary features results in improved performance, as evidenced by data in Tables 1 to 4. Supplementary Figure 3 shows that CDPred_ESM outperforms CDPred_PLM in precision for 42 of 51 homodimers and 55 of 64 heterodimers across various test datasets, indicating a complementary relationship between the methods.

The study also examines the influence of the quality of predicted tertiary structures from AlphaFold on inter-chain distance predictions for dimers. It presents TM-scores for predicted structures, revealing high quality with averages of 0.95 and 0.90 for homodimers, and 0.90 and 0.89 for Chain A of heterodimers, while Chain B scores are 0.95 and 0.88. Despite this high quality, a Pearson correlation of 0.19 between TM-scores and contact prediction precision suggests that the generally high quality of structures may limit the correlation.

Figures illustrate the precision distribution of contact predictions for heterodimers and compare the performance of CDPred_PLM and CDPred_ESM across different datasets. Overall, the findings emphasize the significance of both co-evolutionary features and high-quality structural predictions in enhancing the accuracy of protein complex predictions.
