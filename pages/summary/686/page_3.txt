The study evaluates the performance of CDPred in predicting contacts in heterodimers using two datasets, HeteroTest1 and HeteroTest2. CDPred consistently outperforms GLINTER and DeepComplex across various metrics, with notable improvements in precision, accuracy order, and AUC. For instance, on HeteroTest1, CDPred achieves a top Ls/5 contact prediction precision of 47.59%, significantly higher than GLINTER's 23.24% and DeepComplex's 40.19%. The results on HeteroTest2 mirror these findings, demonstrating CDPred's superiority.

The analysis also includes a comparison of predictions based on different orders of monomers (CDPred(A_B) and CDPred(B_A)). While the precision varies by target and dataset, a two-sided pairwise t-test indicates no significant average difference between the two orders. Averaging predictions from both orders enhances stability, smoothing out variance.

Further, the study categorizes contact prediction precisions for heterodimers into intervals, revealing that many heterodimers have low precision, suggesting room for improvement, particularly due to shallow multiple sequence alignments (MSAs). The correlation between the number of effective sequences in MSAs and prediction precision is modest, underscoring the complexity of the task.

The paper also notes that inter-chain contact prediction accuracy for heterodimers is generally lower than for homodimers, attributed to the challenges in generating deep MSAs for heterodimers and the typically larger interaction interfaces of homodimers.

Lastly, a comparison between co-evolutionary features generated by statistical optimization (CCMPred) and deep learning (MSA transformer) reveals that the latter yields more informative features, as demonstrated by the better performance of models trained on these features across multiple test datasets. Overall, CDPred shows promise as a robust predictor for protein complex contacts, with significant implications for understanding protein interactions.
