The paper discusses the performance of CDPred in predicting inter-chain distances using both AlphaFold-predicted and true tertiary structures of monomers. While true structures generally yield slightly better performance across three out of four datasets, the differences are not statistically significant. The findings suggest that AlphaFold-predicted structures are sufficiently accurate for CDPred's predictions.

The study also highlights a strong correlation between the precision of inter-chain contact predictions and predicted probability scores, indicating that these probability scores can serve as confidence estimates for the predictions. 

In comparison with AlphaFold2-multimer, the state-of-the-art method for predicting quaternary structures, CDPred shows complementary capabilities. Although AlphaFold2-multimer generally achieves higher precision, CDPred outperforms it on a subset of challenging targets, particularly when AlphaFold2-multimer's confidence is low. The results demonstrate that CDPred can be effectively utilized to enhance the selection of diverse quaternary structural models from AlphaFold2-multimer predictions.

The paper also presents an interesting case where CDPred successfully predicted inter-chain distances for a homodimer with a shallow multiple sequence alignment (MSA) and a low-quality tertiary structure from AlphaFold2, showcasing its robustness in challenging scenarios. Overall, CDPred proves to be a valuable tool in the context of inter-chain distance prediction, particularly when combined with AlphaFold2-multimer.
