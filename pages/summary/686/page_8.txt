The paper describes a methodology for generating multiple sequence alignments (MSAs) and utilizing them in the prediction of inter-chain distances for protein dimers. During the training phase, MSAs for homodimers are generated using PSI-BLAST and Uniref90, while for heterodimers, HHblits and Uniclust30 are employed, with a focus on organism taxonomy to pair sequences. In the testing phase, HHblits and the Big Fantastic Database (BFD) are used for homodimers, while a jackhammer approach with Uniref90 is applied for heterodimers.

The generated MSAs are processed using statistical tools like CCMpred to create a co-evolutionary score matrix and a deep learning tool, MSA transformer, to derive attention matrices. The training procedure involves predicting heavy atom distance maps and computing a cross-entropy loss to optimize the model parameters via the Adam optimizer and stochastic gradient descent across defined epochs.

Datasets for training include 4,132 homodimers filtered for sequence identity, resulting in 4,129 usable proteins, and similar procedures were followed for heterodimers, yielding 3,955 proteins. Evaluation metrics for prediction accuracy include precision of contact predictions and various accuracy rates, assessing the predictive performance of the model against established methods like GLINTER and DeepHomo.

The paper also provides access to the datasets and code used for the research, promoting transparency and reproducibility in computational biology studies.
