This paper serves as a tutorial and survey on key methods in dimensionality reduction and generative modeling, specifically focusing on factor analysis, probabilistic Principal Component Analysis (PCA), variational inference, and Variational Autoencoder (VAE). The authors, affiliated with the University of Waterloo, emphasize that these methods assume data points are generated from low-dimensional latent factors, which facilitates dimensionality reduction through parameter learning in latent space. 

The paper begins with an overview of variational inference, detailing the derivation of the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) and the Expectation Maximization (EM) algorithm for parameter learning. It then discusses factor analysis, including its joint and marginal distributions, along with the corresponding EM steps. Probabilistic PCA is introduced as a specific instance of factor analysis, where closed-form solutions are derived. The discussion culminates with an explanation of VAE, outlining the roles of the encoder, decoder, and latent space sampling, as well as training methods utilizing both EM and backpropagation.

The introduction distinguishes between discriminative and generative models, noting that the methods discussed are generative in nature. Variational inference is highlighted for its ability to find a lower bound on log-likelihood, which is maximized instead of using traditional Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE). The paper also references various applications of these techniques in fields such as Bayesian analysis and human motion analysis, underscoring their practical relevance.
