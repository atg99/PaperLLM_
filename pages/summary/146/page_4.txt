The paper discusses variational inference and its relation to factor analysis, highlighting the use of the Expectation-Maximization (EM) algorithm in variational inference. The EM steps involve updating the variational distribution of latent variables based on observed data and maximizing the expected log-likelihood of the model parameters. It is noted that in variational inference, parameters can be absorbed into latent variables, simplifying the joint distribution of observed and latent variables.

In the context of factor analysis, the paper presents the background on the marginal multivariate Gaussian distribution. It defines the joint distribution of two random variables, with the mean and covariance structured in a way that reveals that both marginal distributions are Gaussian. The paper outlines the conditional distributions, further emphasizing the Gaussian nature of these relations.

The main idea of factor analysis is introduced as a generative model where data points are derived from latent variables. This process involves a linear projection of the latent variable into a lower-dimensional space, followed by a linear transformation and the addition of Gaussian noise. The model is illustrated through a graphical representation, emphasizing the relationship between visible data, latent variables, and noise.

Lastly, the factor analysis model assumes a prior distribution for the latent variable as a multivariate Gaussian, which facilitates the analysis of latent factors influencing observed data. This foundational concept in statistical modeling is crucial for understanding more complex generative models.
