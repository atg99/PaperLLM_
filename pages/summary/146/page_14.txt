This paper serves as a tutorial and survey on various dimensionality reduction and generative modeling techniques, specifically focusing on factor analysis, probabilistic PCA, variational inference, and variational autoencoders (VAE). It highlights that these methodologies operate under the assumption that data points are generated from latent variables or factors, incorporating a noise component in the data representation.

The paper also discusses hierarchical VAEs and their application to image data, mentioning a fusion approach with convolutional neural networks. Furthermore, it analyzes influential factors within VAEs, referencing relevant studies. 

The conclusion summarizes the interconnectedness of the discussed models, while the proof for Lemma 1 details the Kullback-Leibler divergence calculation between two probability distributions, illustrating the mathematical foundations underlying the methods explored.

Acknowledgments are given to the instructors from the deep learning course at Carnegie Mellon University, whose lectures informed parts of the tutorial. The references section lists key literature relevant to the topics covered, providing a resource for further exploration in the field.
