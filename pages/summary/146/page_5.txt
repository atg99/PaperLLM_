This paper discusses factor analysis and its relationship with probabilistic principal component analysis (PCA), variational inference, and variational autoencoders. It explains how data points are generated through a linear projection of latent variables, followed by a translation and the addition of Gaussian noise. The conditional likelihood of the data given the latent variables is defined as a multivariate Gaussian distribution.

The paper details the joint distribution of the observed data and latent variables, highlighting the relationships between expectations and variances. It derives the marginal distribution of the data and the posterior distribution of the latent variables given the data. Key equations demonstrate how the mean and covariance of the data are computed, emphasizing the independence of the noise component.

The authors note that centering the data, where the mean is set to zero, simplifies the marginal distribution and likelihood equations, a common preprocessing step in factor analysis. Overall, the paper provides a thorough overview of the mathematical foundations of factor analysis and its probabilistic interpretations, making it a valuable resource for understanding these statistical methods.
