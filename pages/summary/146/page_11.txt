This section of the paper discusses the training of Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) using Expectation Maximization (EM) and explores the simplifications of the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO). The training process involves optimizing the encoder and decoder weights iteratively through alternating optimization. 

In the E-step, the encoder weights are updated to maximize the ELBO based on the current decoder weights, while in the M-step, the decoder weights are updated. The optimization is performed using mini-batch stochastic gradient ascent, allowing for efficient computation over subsets of the data.

The ELBO is expressed in terms of the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence, which compares the approximated posterior distribution with the conditional distribution given the data. Two simplification methods for the ELBO are presented. The first method involves using Monte Carlo approximation to estimate expectations, allowing for the evaluation of the ELBO by drawing samples from the conditional distribution. The second method reformulates the ELBO using integrals over the posterior distribution.

Overall, the paper provides a comprehensive overview of the VAE training process and its mathematical foundations, emphasizing the role of EM and various approximation techniques in optimizing the model.
