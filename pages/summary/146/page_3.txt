The paper discusses the Expectation Maximization (EM) algorithm and its application in variational inference, particularly in maximizing the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) on log likelihood. EM is outlined through a conversational analogy, emphasizing its two iterative steps: the E-step and the M-step.

In the E-step, the expectation of the log-likelihood is computed with respect to missing data, allowing for mean estimation. The M-step then maximizes the expected log-likelihood derived from the E-step. The process involves adjusting the distribution of the missing data in the E-step to align with the posterior distribution, while the M-step focuses on maximizing the expected log-likelihood based on the updated distribution.

The interplay between the E-step and M-step is described as a game where the E-step aims to elevate the ELBO towards the true log-likelihood, while the M-step works to enhance the log-likelihood itself. This iterative process continues until convergence, ultimately improving the parameter estimates in the model. The paper highlights the significance of EM in variational inference as a means to effectively handle missing data and optimize model parameters.
