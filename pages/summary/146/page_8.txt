This paper discusses the application of Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE) in the context of factor analysis, probabilistic PCA, and variational inference, focusing on estimating the projection matrix and noise variance from data. The analysis begins with the log-likelihood of the observed data under a Gaussian distribution, leading to the formulation of the MLE objective. 

The covariance matrix of the data is defined, and the paper details the process of maximizing the likelihood with respect to the projection matrix and noise variance. It highlights the importance of the matrix inversion lemma, which simplifies the inversion of matrices involved in the optimization.

The derivation involves taking the derivative of the likelihood function and setting it to zero, leading to an eigenvalue problem for the sample covariance matrix. The singular value decomposition (SVD) is utilized to express the projection matrix, revealing a relationship between the eigenvalues of the covariance matrix and the singular values of the projection.

The paper concludes that the projection matrix can be expressed in terms of the orthogonal matrix and the derived eigenvalues and singular values, emphasizing that the choice of the orthogonal matrix does not affect the outcome of dimensionality reduction. The findings contribute to the understanding of probabilistic PCA and its estimation techniques within the broader context of variational inference and autoencoders.
