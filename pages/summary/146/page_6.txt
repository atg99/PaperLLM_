The section discusses the Expectation Maximization (EM) algorithm applied to Factor Analysis, focusing on the maximization of the joint likelihood to estimate the parameters involved. It begins by outlining the joint likelihood maximization process, which simplifies to the likelihood of the data over all data points. 

In the maximization step (M-step), two expectations are computed during the E-step: the expected value of the latent variables \( z_i \) and the expected value of their outer products. These expectations are derived from the posterior distribution \( q(z_i | x_i) \). The first expectation \( E[q(t)(z_i)][z_i] \) is computed using the formula based on the covariance and mean of \( z_i \), while the second expectation \( E[q(t)(z_i)][z_i z_i^T] \) is related to the covariance matrix.

The derivation to find the parameter \( \theta \) involves taking the derivative of the joint likelihood expression with respect to \( \theta \) and setting it to zero to find the optimal estimate. The expressions for these computations rely on properties of the trace and involve the sample covariance matrix \( S \). The optimization for the second parameter \( \Psi \) is presented as well, emphasizing the role of the sample covariance matrix in the estimation process.

Overall, the section provides a detailed mathematical framework for understanding how EM operates within the context of Factor Analysis, focusing on the computation of expectations and the resulting parameter updates.
