The paper provides a comprehensive overview of factor analysis, probabilistic PCA, variational inference, and variational autoencoders, focusing on their algorithms and mathematical formulations. 

In factor analysis, the Expectation-Maximization (EM) algorithm is employed to iteratively compute the mean of the data and update the covariance matrix. The noise covariance matrix is identified as diagonal due to the independence of noise dimensions. The algorithm simplifies when data is centered, leading to more straightforward calculations for the expected values of latent variables. Notably, factor analysis lacks a closed-form solution, necessitating iterative methods to determine the projection matrix and noise covariance until convergence.

Probabilistic PCA (PPCA) is introduced as a specific case of factor analysis with isotropic noise, which simplifies the calculations and allows for a closed-form solution to the EM algorithm. In PPCA, the noise covariance is uniform across dimensions, resulting in independent data points given the latent variables. The paper outlines the iterative process for solving PPCA and highlights its graphical model representation.

Overall, this survey emphasizes the relationships between these statistical methods and their iterative nature, particularly in the context of estimating parameters and understanding latent structures in data.
