The paper discusses the Channel Boosted CNN (CB-CNN) architecture proposed by Khan et al. in 2018, aimed at enhancing the representational capacity of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) by increasing the number of input channels. The CB-CNN utilizes auxiliary learners to artificially create additional channels, referred to as auxiliary channels, which are integrated into the original input channels to improve performance as a discriminator.

The architecture is mathematically represented in two equations. The first equation describes how the original input channels are combined with the auxiliary channels to form a boosted input for the discriminator. The second equation details the generation of feature maps through convolution of the boosted input with the respective kernels.

The paper emphasizes the importance of data representation in classifier performance, as highlighted by Bengio et al. in 2013. To enhance data representation, CB-CNN employs transfer learning (TL) alongside deep generative learners, specifically autoencoders (AEs), which capture the underlying data distribution during training. This innovative use of inductive TL allows for the augmentation of the input data distribution with the original channel space.

Overall, CB-CNN integrates generative learning models as auxiliary learners, significantly improving the representational capacity of deep CNNs during both generation and discrimination stages. This study underscores the effectiveness of multi-deep learners in enhancing CNN performance.
