• Another promising direction is the integration of unsupervised and semi-supervised learning techniques into CNNs. This could address the challenge of requiring large labeled datasets, as humans can learn effectively from fewer examples. By leveraging unlabeled data, models can learn richer representations and generalize better.

• The optimization of hyper-parameters remains a critical area for improving CNN performance. Implementing advanced optimization strategies can lead to more efficient training processes and better model accuracy.

• The deployment of CNNs on embedded systems is an ongoing challenge. Research into optimizing CNN architectures for lower computational power while maintaining performance will allow for broader applications in smart devices and real-time systems.

• Lastly, addressing the limitations of CNNs in estimating object pose, orientation, and location will be crucial. Further advancements in data augmentation techniques and architectural innovations may enhance CNN capabilities in these areas.

Overall, the future of CNNs in artificial intelligence research looks promising, with ongoing developments expected to yield significant advancements in image processing and computer vision applications.
