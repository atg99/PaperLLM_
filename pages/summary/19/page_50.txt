The paper discusses the application of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) in resource-limited systems and their effectiveness in various domains, including low-resolution thermal image recognition and 1D data analysis. It highlights the use of Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks for action recognition from thermal images, as noted by Kawashima et al. 

Despite the high computational demands of CNNs, they have been successfully implemented in embedded systems. Notably, Lee et al. developed a number plate recognition system using a simplified AlexNet architecture, demonstrating quick recognition capabilities. To enhance power efficiency and portability, Bettoni et al. implemented CNNs on FPGA platforms, while Xie et al. utilized FPGA for various CNN-based tasks. Furthermore, lightweight CNN architectures like MobileNet and ShuffleNet are highlighted for their suitability in mobile devices, as demonstrated by Shakeel et al., who created a real-time driver drowsiness detection application using MobileNet and SSD.

The paper also emphasizes the growing popularity of 1D-CNNs for analyzing 1D data due to their effective feature extraction capabilities. For instance, Vinayakumar et al. integrated 1D-CNN with RNN and LSTM for intrusion detection in network traffic, achieving superior performance compared to traditional machine learning models. Additionally, Abdeljaber et al. showcased the use of 1D-CNN for real-time structural damage detection, while Yildirim et al. demonstrated its effectiveness in analyzing biomedical datasets. Overall, the paper illustrates the versatility and efficiency of CNNs across various applications and data types.
