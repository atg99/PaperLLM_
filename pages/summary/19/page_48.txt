The paper discusses various applications of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) in both image classification and speech recognition. In the realm of image classification, CNNs have shown significant success, particularly in medical diagnostics such as cancer detection through histopathological images. Notable studies include Spanhol et al. who compared CNN performance in breast cancer diagnosis against traditional methods and Wahab et al. who implemented a two-phase approach to address class imbalance in breast cancer image datasets. Additionally, Cireşan et al. applied CNNs to traffic sign classification, achieving commendable recognition rates.

In the area of speech recognition, CNNs are emerging as effective tools, with research indicating a notable reduction in error rates compared to previous methods. Abdel-Hamid et al. demonstrated a CNN-based system that achieved a ten percent error reduction. Further studies explored various architectures and pre-training techniques, revealing strong performance in phoneme and vocabulary recognition tasks. The integration of CNNs with Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks for speech emotion recognition is also highlighted, showcasing the versatility and growing interest in CNN applications beyond image processing.
