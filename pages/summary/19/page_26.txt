The paper discusses the architecture of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), particularly focusing on the concept of depth in relation to network performance. It highlights the representation bottleneck in GoogleNet, which can lead to a loss of valuable information due to reduced feature space in subsequent layers. The authors emphasize that deeper CNN architectures improve the approximation of target functions through enhanced nonlinear mappings and richer feature hierarchies. Theoretical findings suggest that deep networks can represent certain functions more efficiently than shallow ones. While a single hidden layer can theoretically approximate any function, it requires an impractically large number of neurons. Consequently, deeper networks are posited to maintain expressive power while reducing computational costs. Empirical evidence from Bengio et al. supports the notion that deep networks are more efficient for complex tasks. The success of architectures like Inception and VGG in the 2014 ILSVRC competition further underscores the importance of depth in enhancing the learning capacity of neural networks.
