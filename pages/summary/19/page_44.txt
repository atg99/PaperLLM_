The paper discusses advancements in attention mechanisms within convolutional neural networks (CNNs), particularly focusing on the Recognition Attention Network (RAN) and the Convolutional Block Attention Module (CBAM). RAN enhances the recognition of cluttered and noisy images by stacking multiple attention modules and employing a hierarchical structure that adapts the weight assigned to each feature map based on relevance. This approach integrates three levels of attention: mixed, channel, and spatial, facilitating the capture of object-aware features.

CBAM, proposed by Woo et al., builds on the concept of attention by sequentially applying channel attention followed by spatial attention to refine feature maps. Unlike Squeeze-and-Excitation Networks (SE-Networks), which only consider the contribution of feature maps without spatial context, CBAM emphasizes the importance of spatial locality in object detection. It utilizes a combination of average and max pooling to generate effective spatial attention maps, enhancing the network's representational capability. The empirical results presented demonstrate that incorporating both pooling methods significantly improves the network's ability to focus on critical features while increasing overall feature representation.
