The paper discusses advancements in convolutional neural networks (CNNs), focusing on VGG and GoogleNet architectures. VGG, known for its simplicity and deep architecture, achieved notable performance in image classification and localization but suffered from high computational costs due to its 138 million parameters. In contrast, GoogleNet, or Inception-V1, won the 2014 ILSVRC competition by prioritizing high accuracy while significantly reducing computational demands to just 4 million parameters. It introduced the inception block, which utilizes multi-scale convolutional transformations and a bottleneck layer to optimize learning and reduce redundancy. GoogleNet also employed global average pooling and auxiliary learners to enhance convergence rates. However, its heterogeneous topology presents challenges in customization across modules.
