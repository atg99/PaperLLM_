The paper published in Artificial Intelligence Review discusses the taxonomy of deep convolutional neural network (CNN) architectures, categorizing them into seven distinct types. It highlights the complexity of CNNs, which include numerous parameters and hyper-parameters such as weights, biases, layer counts, neuron counts, filter sizes, strides, activation functions, and learning rates. The convolutional operation's focus on the locality of input pixels allows for the exploration of various levels of correlation through different filter sizes. Smaller filters capture fine-grained details, while larger filters focus on coarse-grained information. 

In the early 2000s, researchers began to leverage spatial filters to enhance performance, leading to insights on how filter adjustments could optimize CNN learning for both detailed and broader features. The paper also references LeNet, developed by LeCun in 1998, which is historically significant as the first CNN to achieve state-of-the-art results in hand digit recognition. LeNet's robustness to minor distortions in digit classification is noted as a key attribute.
