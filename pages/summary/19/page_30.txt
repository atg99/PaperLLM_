The paper discusses advancements in deep neural network architectures, particularly focusing on Highway Networks and ResNet, which address challenges in training very deep networks. Highway Networks utilize cross-layer connectivity to facilitate the flow of information, allowing for the effective training of networks with over 100 to 900 layers using stochastic gradient descent. This connectivity is governed by two gates: the transform gate (gt) and the carry gate (gc). When gt is active, the transformed input is forwarded to the next layer; when inactive, gc allows the original input to bypass transformation and proceed directly to the subsequent layer.

ResNet builds on the principles of Highway Networks by introducing bypass pathways to mitigate training difficulties in deep networks. The mathematical formulation of ResNet demonstrates how the original input is combined with transformed signals through a residual block, resulting in an aggregated output that is subsequently processed by the next layer. The architecture enhances training efficiency and performance in deep learning models, making it a significant contribution to the field.
