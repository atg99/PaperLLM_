The paper discusses significant advancements in Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), particularly focusing on AlexNet and ZfNet. AlexNet, notable for its innovative architecture, addressed the vanishing gradient problem and implemented techniques like overlapping subsampling and local response normalization to enhance generalization and reduce overfitting. The use of larger filters in the initial layers marked a departure from previous networks, establishing AlexNet as a pivotal model that spurred further research into CNN architecture.

ZfNet, introduced by Zeiler and Fergus in 2013, aimed to improve understanding of CNN performance through the visualization of network activity. This approach allowed for a quantitative assessment of how different layers of the network contributed to overall performance, building on earlier work that optimized Deep Belief Networks through similar visualization techniques. The paper emphasizes the importance of these developments in enhancing the understanding and capabilities of deep learning models in processing complex images.
