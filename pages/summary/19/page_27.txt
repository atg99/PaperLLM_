The paper discusses advancements in deep convolutional neural networks (CNNs) with a focus on Highway Networks and ResNet. Highway Networks, introduced by Srivastava et al. in 2015, aim to enhance learning capacity by increasing network depth while addressing issues of slow training and convergence speeds. They implement a cross-layer connectivity mechanism that allows for effective training even at depths of up to 900 layers, outperforming traditional architectures that struggle beyond 10 layers.

ResNet, proposed by He et al. in 2015, further advances deep learning by introducing residual learning, which facilitates the training of very deep networks. It achieved notable success with a 152-layer architecture that won the 2015 ILSVRC competition. ResNet demonstrated lower computational complexity compared to earlier models like AlexNet and VGG, while also achieving significant reductions in error rates on image classification tasks. Its performance improvements on benchmarks such as COCO underscore the importance of representational depth in visual recognition tasks. Both Highway Networks and ResNet exemplify the potential of multi-path based CNN architectures in enhancing deep learning capabilities.
