The paper discusses various challenges associated with the implementation of different architectures in Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), focusing on Multi-Path, Width-based, and Feature-Map exploitation architectures. 

**Multi-Path based CNN architectures** utilize shortcut paths to enhance layer connectivity. Key architectures include:

- **Highway Networks**: Introduce cross-layer connectivity, but can be parameter expensive due to data-dependent gates.
- **ResNet**: Employs identity-based skip connections, facilitating information flow without additional parameters; however, it may lead to redundant feature-map relearning.
- **DenseNet**: Maximizes data flow and minimizes redundancy, but increases parameters significantly due to the addition of feature-maps.

**Width-based CNN architectures** challenge the assumption that deeper networks yield better accuracy by focusing on layer width. Key architectures include:

- **Wide ResNet**: Enhances feature reuse by increasing width and reducing depth, but risks overfitting due to more parameters.
- **Pyramidal Net**: Gradually increases width to prevent information loss, though it incurs high complexity.
- **Xception**: Utilizes depth-wise separable convolutions for efficiency but has high computational costs.
- **Inception**: Employs varying filter sizes for diverse feature capture, increasing complexity.
- **ResNeXt**: Introduces cardinality for diverse transformations while being computationally intensive.

**Feature-Map exploitation based CNN architectures** address the challenge of selecting important feature-maps from the numerous generated during deep learning. Key architectures include:

- **Squeeze and Excitation Networks**: A simple block that enhances important features but relies solely on residual information in ResNet.
- **Competitive Squeeze and Excitation Networks**: Leverages statistics from both residual and identity mappings but lacks support for attention mechanisms.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the trade-offs between enhancing connectivity, managing complexity, and optimizing feature selection in CNN architectures.
