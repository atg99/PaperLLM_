The paper discusses the evolution of convolutional neural networks (CNNs), starting with LeNet, which laid the groundwork for image processing tasks by leveraging the correlation between neighboring pixels. LeNet's architecture consists of alternating convolutional and pooling layers followed by fully connected layers, which allowed it to automatically learn features from raw pixel data while significantly reducing the number of parameters.

However, LeNet's capabilities were limited to specific tasks, such as handwritten digit recognition. The introduction of AlexNet represented a major leap forward, expanding the application of CNNs to a broader range of image classification and recognition tasks. By increasing the network depth to eight layers and employing parallel training on multiple GPUs, AlexNet overcame hardware limitations and enhanced learning capacity.

To combat overfitting, AlexNet utilized dropout techniques and employed the ReLU activation function, which contributed to its robustness and improved performance. Overall, the advancements made by AlexNet set a new standard in deep learning and paved the way for subsequent developments in CNN architectures.
