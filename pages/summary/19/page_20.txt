The paper published in Artificial Intelligence Review provides a comprehensive comparison of various convolutional neural network (CNN) architectures, detailing their main contributions, parameters, depth, and error rates across different datasets. The architectures span from early models like LeNet in 1998 to more recent innovations such as Competitive Squeeze & Excitation Networks in 2018.

Key highlights include:

1. **LeNet (1998)**: Recognized as the first popular CNN, it had 0.060 million parameters and achieved a 0.8% error rate on MNIST.
2. **AlexNet (2012)**: Introduced deeper and wider networks with techniques like ReLU and dropout, achieving a 16.4% error rate on ImageNet with 60 million parameters.
3. **VGG (2014)**: Notable for its homogeneous architecture using small kernels, it reached a 7.3% error rate on ImageNet with 138 million parameters.
4. **GoogLeNet (2015)**: Introduced the inception module, achieving a 6.7% error rate with just 4 million parameters.
5. **ResNet (2016)**: Pioneered residual learning with skip connections, resulting in a 3.6% error rate on ImageNet using 25.6 million parameters.
6. **DenseNet (2017)**: Enhanced cross-layer information flow, achieving a 5.19% error rate on CIFAR-10 with 25.6 million parameters.

The table presents a clear performance comparison, highlighting the evolution of CNN architectures and their contributions to reducing error rates through innovative designs and techniques. Each architecture is categorized based on its unique approach, such as spatial exploitation, depth, width, and attention mechanisms, showcasing the diverse strategies in CNN development over the years.
