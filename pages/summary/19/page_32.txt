The paper discusses advancements in convolutional neural networks (CNNs), particularly focusing on the DenseNet architecture and the importance of network width in addition to depth. DenseNet is noted for its narrow layer structure, which can become computationally intensive as the number of feature maps increases. However, it enhances information flow by allowing each layer direct access to gradients from the loss function, thereby incorporating a regularizing effect that helps reduce overfitting, especially in scenarios with limited training data.

The research from 2012 to 2015 primarily emphasized deep architectures. However, recent studies, including those by Kawaguchi et al., highlight the significance of network width in learning complex functions. The multilayer perceptron benefits from parallel processing within layers, indicating that width is a critical parameter alongside depth. Studies by Lu et al. and Hanin and Sellke reveal that networks with ReLU activation must be sufficiently wide to achieve universal approximation properties, particularly as depth increases. They also note that a deep network with limited width may not effectively approximate continuous functions.

The paper suggests a shift in focus from deep and narrow architectures to wider designs to address issues like feature reuse in deep residual networks, as highlighted by the introduction of Wide ResNet. This architecture leverages the advantages of residual units while emphasizing width over depth, aiming to enhance learning potential by mitigating the limitations associated with deep structures.
