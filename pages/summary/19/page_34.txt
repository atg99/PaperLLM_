The paper discusses the Pyramidal Net and Xception architectures in the context of deep learning and convolutional neural networks. Pyramidal Net introduces a gradual increase in the width of the network per residual unit, allowing it to cover all possible locations without maintaining the same spatial dimension until down-sampling. The depth of the feature maps is regulated by a factor, calculated using a specific equation, which helps distribute the increase in depth across residual blocks. Zero-padded identity mapping is utilized for residual connections, offering a parameter-efficient alternative to projection-based shortcuts, potentially enhancing generalization.

Pyramidal Net employs two widening strategies: additive, which increases linearly, and multiplicative, which increases geometrically. However, a significant drawback is that increasing width leads to a quadratic increase in both space and time complexity.

Xception is described as an advanced version of the Inception architecture, utilizing depthwise separable convolution to enhance computational efficiency. It simplifies the original inception block by using a single spatial dimension followed by a 1x1 convolution, thereby decoupling spatial and feature-map correlations. This approach is mathematically represented in the paper, highlighting its effectiveness in managing computational complexity.
