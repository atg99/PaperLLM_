The paper discusses advancements in computer vision (CV), focusing on key areas such as face recognition, human pose estimation, and action recognition. 

Face recognition is highlighted as a challenging task due to variations in illumination, pose, and expressions. Notable contributions include Farfade et al.'s deep CNN approach for detecting faces across different poses and occlusions, and Zhang et al.'s multitasking cascaded CNN, which demonstrated superior performance compared to existing methods.

Human pose estimation is addressed through Li et al.'s heterogeneous deep CNN, which effectively learns localized body parts, and Bulat et al.'s cascade-based CNN that first detects heat maps before performing regression on them.

In the realm of action recognition, the paper notes the complexities arising from variations in motion patterns. Wang et al. proposed a three-dimensional CNN combined with LSTM for action recognition from video frames, achieving better results than previous techniques. Ji et al. also introduced a three-dimensional CNN that extracts features from multiple input channels, leading to a model capable of real-world activity recognition.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the evolution of CNN architectures in tackling the challenges associated with various CV tasks, showcasing significant improvements in performance across different approaches.
