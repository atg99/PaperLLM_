The paper discusses the Xception architecture, which utilizes depthwise separable convolutions to enhance computational efficiency in convolutional neural networks (CNNs). Xception employs a structure where each feature map is convolved separately across spatial axes, followed by pointwise convolutions using 1x1 kernels to capture cross-channel correlations. This approach contrasts with traditional convolutional layers, where the number of kernels is independent of the number of input feature maps. The architecture introduces a cardinality parameter, denoted as n, which defines the number of transformations applied to low-dimensional embeddings through 1x1 convolutions. The equations presented illustrate the process of spatial convolution and the relationship between kernels and feature maps across layers. Overall, Xception simplifies computations while maintaining performance by leveraging the unique properties of depthwise separable convolutions.
