The paper published in Artificial Intelligence Review discusses the advancements in Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), particularly in the context of high energy physics. It highlights the significant progress made in CNNs, especially in image processing and vision-related tasks, which has revitalized interest in Artificial Neural Networks (ANNs). 

The authors categorize advancements in CNNs based on various factors such as activation functions, loss functions, optimization techniques, regularization methods, learning algorithms, and architectural innovations. They propose a taxonomy for recent CNN architectures, providing a structured overview of the evolution of CNNs, their applications, challenges faced, and potential future directions.

A key observation is that the performance of CNNs has improved markedly through the use of deeper structures and other modifications. Recent literature indicates that replacing conventional layer structures with blocks has been instrumental in enhancing CNN performance. These blocks serve as auxiliary learners, utilizing spatial and feature-map information or augmenting input channels to facilitate problem-aware learning.

The block-based architecture promotes modular learning, simplifying the design and understanding of CNNs. The authors anticipate that the concept of blocks as structural units will continue to enhance CNN performance. Furthermore, they expect that the importance of attention mechanisms and the exploitation of channel information alongside spatial data will grow in significance.
