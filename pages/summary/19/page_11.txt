In a fully connected layer, every neuron is connected to every neuron in the previous layer, allowing for a comprehensive integration of features learned through earlier layers. This layer transforms the high-level features into the final output class probabilities, making it essential for tasks such as classification.

The paper discusses several key components in deep learning architectures, emphasizing the importance of activation functions, batch normalization, dropout, and fully connected layers. The proposed MISH activation function demonstrates superior performance compared to ReLU in recent deep networks. Batch normalization addresses internal covariance shift, enhancing convergence speed and stability by normalizing feature-map distributions. Dropout serves as a regularization technique to prevent overfitting by randomly omitting units during training, which fosters a variety of network architectures. Finally, the fully connected layer integrates features from previous layers to produce the final output, playing a crucial role in classification tasks. These components collectively contribute to the effectiveness and generalization capabilities of deep learning models.
