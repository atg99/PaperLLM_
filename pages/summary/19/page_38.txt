The paper discusses the significance of feature-map selection in enhancing the generalization capabilities of neural networks, particularly in the context of convolutional neural networks (CNNs). It introduces the Squeeze and Excitation Network (SE-Network), developed by Hu et al., which includes a novel processing unit called the SE-block. This block is designed to prioritize feature-maps that are crucial for object discrimination while suppressing less relevant ones, leading to improved performance on datasets like ImageNet.

The SE-block operates through two main processes: squeeze and excitation. The squeeze operation provides a global representation of feature-maps by generating statistics that summarize their information, effectively suppressing spatial details. This is achieved through global average pooling, which captures the essence of the target object. The output of this operation is then fed into the excitation operation, which assigns weights to the feature-maps using a two-layer feed-forward neural network. This process utilizes a gating mechanism to model interdependencies among feature-maps, enhancing the network's ability to focus on the most informative features.

The equations presented in the paper detail the mathematical formulations for the squeeze and excitation operations, illustrating how feature descriptors are generated and weighted. Overall, the SE-Network represents a significant advancement in CNN architecture, contributing to reduced error rates and improved object recognition capabilities.
