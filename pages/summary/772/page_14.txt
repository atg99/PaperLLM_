The study emphasizes the urgent need for an automatic identification tool for COVID-19 due to the slow identification procedures currently in place. The proposed classification model employs a deep convolutional neural network (CNN) to analyze chest X-ray images, effectively distinguishing COVID-19 cases. The CNN architecture includes multiple layers that perform convolutions and pooling to extract features, with max pooling layers reducing spatial dimensions and addressing overfitting. The ReLU activation function is utilized to map inputs to target variables, while dropout layers further mitigate overfitting. Fully connected layers serve as the final classifier.

A significant challenge in medical data analysis is the scarcity of labeled datasets, which limits the effectiveness of deep learning models. To overcome this, transfer learning is employed, allowing the model to leverage knowledge from pre-trained networks such as VGG, GoogleNet, and ResNet. The study specifically utilizes the ResNet-50 model, known for its skip connection architecture that alleviates the vanishing gradient problem and enhances learning efficiency. This approach not only reduces training time but also improves model performance, making it suitable for the limited dataset available in this context.
