The paper discusses a framework for monitoring COVID-19 patients, emphasizing the importance of timely data collection and analysis for effective patient management. It outlines the plasma test parameters for assessing patient health, including white blood cell count, lactate dehydrogenase, C-reactive protein, and ferritin levels. 

Patients are classified into two categories: severe cases requiring hospitalization for complications, and mild-to-moderate cases that can be managed at home. The monitoring approach for mild-to-moderate cases is central to the study, highlighting the need for continuous monitoring based on clinical examination results and risk factors.

To facilitate home monitoring, the paper suggests deploying wearable and environmental sensors, which connect to a mobile application used by patients and healthcare providers. This application allows patients to log their information and enables physicians to monitor their health remotely.

The paper also emphasizes the role of cloud computing for real-time data analysis, noting that rapid and quality care is crucial during the pandemic. It proposes the use of fog computing as a bridge between IoT devices and cloud systems, enabling quick data analysis without being hindered by internet connectivity issues. The use of LoRa network for data transmission is highlighted, as it allows for effective communication over long distances, suitable for home-care scenarios. 

Overall, the framework aims to enhance patient safety and limit the spread of infection through effective monitoring and data management.
