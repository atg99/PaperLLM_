This paper outlines the development of a COVID-19 classification model using the ResNet-50 architecture. The model construction involved several key steps: first, the fully connected layer of ResNet-50 was removed, and the remaining convolutional layers served as the base network. Next, batch normalization and dropout layers were integrated to enhance training efficiency and mitigate overfitting. The final layer employed a SoftMax activation function to classify images into two categories: COVID-19 and normal cases. The dataset consisted of 1824 X-ray images, with 122 representing COVID-19 cases and 500 normal cases, all resized to 192 by 192 pixels.

The experimental setup utilized Python libraries such as SKLearn, Keras, NumPy, and Pandas, running on a Windows 10 system with an Intel Core i7 processor, 4 GB graphics card, and 16 GB RAM. The dataset was split into training, validation, and testing sets in a 70-15-15 ratio. The Adam optimizer was selected for training, with a learning rate of 0.01 and a dropout rate of 0.30. Batch normalization parameters included an epsilon of 1e-5 and momentum of 0.1.

The evaluation of the model was based on five metrics: accuracy, precision, recall, specificity, and F1 score. True positives (TP) indicated correctly classified COVID-19 cases, while true negatives (TN) represented correctly classified normal cases. The paper discusses the limitations of the dataset, highlighting the scarcity of COVID-19 images from sources like chest X-rays and CT scans.

In conclusion, the model effectively utilized chest X-ray images to predict COVID-19 infections, leveraging the ResNet-50 pre-trained model with added dropout, batch normalization, and a SoftMax output layer. The model was trained over 25 epochs to prevent overfitting, achieving impressive metrics with an F1 score of 0.972, precision of 0.974, and recall of 0.975, as detailed in the results.
