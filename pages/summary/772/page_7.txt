The paper discusses the advantages of fog computing in remote patient monitoring systems (RPMS). It emphasizes improved user privacy through local data analysis, reduced bandwidth requirements for real-time data transmission to doctors, and lower power consumption compared to continuous cloud server connections. Fog IoT systems are structured in three layers: device, fog, and cloud. The authors reference works by Dastjerdi and Stojmenovic to clarify distinctions between edge, fog, and cloud computing, and provide a comparative analysis of their features in terms of latency, mobility, bandwidth cost, and security, among others. 

The paper also highlights the role of the backend layer, specifically the database layer, in managing the complex and often incomplete medical data. It notes the significance of clinical-decision-support systems (CDSS) in aiding healthcare professionals in their decision-making processes, portraying CDSS as essential to effective healthcare management. The findings underscore the importance of fog computing in enhancing real-time monitoring capabilities while ensuring privacy and efficient data handling.
