The paper presents the performance results of a classification model designed for detecting COVID-19 from chest X-rays. The results are summarized in two tables: Table 5 details the training and validation metrics over 25 epochs, showing a significant improvement in accuracy and a decrease in loss, with the final training accuracy reaching 100% and validation accuracy at 97.98%. Table 6 provides testing metrics, indicating high performance with an accuracy of 97.95%, recall of 97.59%, precision of 97.40%, specificity of 98.85%, F1-score of 97.29%, and AUC of 97.54%.

The paper highlights the challenges in developing detection models due to the limited availability of chest X-ray images for COVID-19. It compares the proposed model against several others, noting that it outperformed models like COVID-Net and ResNet-50 in accuracy, while achieving results similar to SqueezeNet despite the latter's inconsistencies. Although another study reported slightly higher accuracy, it was based on a very small dataset. The proposed model demonstrates robustness and reliability, showing consistent performance in both training and testing phases. It is suggested that this model could serve as a valuable tool for radiologists in confirming COVID-19 infections.
