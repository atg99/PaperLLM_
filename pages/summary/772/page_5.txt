The paper discusses the integration of mobile and cloud computing in remote patient monitoring (RPM) systems, specifically focusing on a system proposed by Pandey et al. that analyzes ECG data. This system collects health data and stores it in a cloud-based repository, enabling efficient analysis through cloud-hosted software services. However, it highlights several limitations of cloud computing in RPM, including issues related to power consumption, time efficiency, and the privacy and security of transmitted data. The study uses COVID-19 as a case study to address some of these RPM challenges.

The general architecture of RPM systems is outlined, consisting of three main layers: the data acquisition layer, storage layer, and backend layer. The data acquisition layer is crucial, as it employs various sensors to gather data on patients' health metrics, such as ECG and heart rate, as well as contextual information like room conditions. Actuators can also respond to this data, such as adjusting insulin doses based on sensor feedback. The architecture emphasizes the role of wireless body area networks (WBAN) as part of the Internet of Things (IoT) in facilitating this data collection process.
