This paper discusses the application of machine learning (ML) and deep learning models in predicting and classifying COVID-19 infections, alongside the development of healthcare monitoring systems. Various studies have utilized statistical models and ML techniques, such as logistic regression and the Gompertz model, to analyze infection data and predict future cases. Notably, Ozturk et al. employed a deep learning model based on Dark Net and convolutional neural networks (CNN) to classify chest X-rays, achieving high accuracy rates.

The paper emphasizes the necessity of a comprehensive tracking system to manage COVID-19 infections, as existing studies have not focused on deep-learning-aided end-to-end communication systems. Additionally, it reviews mobile health monitoring systems (MHMs) that enhance patient management through telemedicine and data acquisition methods. The development of these systems typically involves data collection, transmission, and backend processing.

Research by Pandey et al. and Sahoo et al. demonstrates the use of wearable sensors and non-contact methods for monitoring vital signs and ECG, respectively, using Bluetooth for data transmission. However, limitations in mobile-only systems for chronic disease patients are highlighted, necessitating more effective remote patient monitoring (RPM) solutions, especially during the COVID-19 pandemic. The integration of cloud computing with wireless sensor networks (WSN) is proposed to overcome these limitations, offering enhanced data access, processing capabilities, and cost-effective storage solutions.
