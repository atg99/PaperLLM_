The paper discusses the development of a COVID-19 classification model utilizing a pre-trained ResNet-50 architecture, aimed at predicting accuracy with a limited dataset of X-ray images. The model's output, H(X), is defined mathematically, emphasizing a residual learning approach where F(x) represents the residual mapping formed by stacked non-linear layers. The ResNet-50 architecture includes several key components: a 7x7 convolutional layer with 64 kernels, a 3x3 max pooling layer with 16 residual blocks, a 7x7 average pooling layer, a fully connected layer, and a SoftMax layer configured for 40 classes, aligning with the dataset's classification needs.

The model was adapted for COVID-19 detection by removing the fully connected layer from ResNet-50 and incorporating batch normalization and dropout layers to enhance training speed and mitigate overfitting. The final SoftMax layer classifies images into two categories: COVID-19 and normal cases. The experimental setup involved 1824 X-ray images, comprising 122 for COVID-19 and 500 for normal cases, with all images resized to 192x192 pixels.
