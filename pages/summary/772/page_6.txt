The paper discusses the critical components of Remote Patient Monitoring Systems (RPMS), highlighting the roles of data acquisition and storage layers. 

In the data-acquisition layer, sensors are essential as they bridge the physical world and the digital domain. RPMS utilize various sensors to gather health-related data (like ECG, SPO2, and heart rate) and contextual information (such as room temperature and oxygen levels). These sensors can be classified into implanted and external types, with both continuously measuring vital signs and transmitting data via wireless communication protocols. Wireless sensor nodes enable the aggregation of real-time patient data, facilitating monitoring of symptoms and therapy effectiveness, and supporting various healthcare systems like vital-sign monitoring and fall detection.

In the storage layer, cloud computing emerges as a beneficial paradigm for healthcare systems, enhancing speed and efficiency, providing substantial storage for large healthcare datasets, and supporting comprehensive data analysis for improved decision-making. It also offers more secure data storage compared to local servers. However, challenges such as power consumption, privacy, and security concerns persist, prompting the development of fog computing.

Fog computing extends cloud capabilities to edge networks, allowing for local data processing which reduces bandwidth usage and costs, and minimizes latency. This is particularly advantageous for time-sensitive applications, enhancing the overall functionality of RPMS by addressing issues related to reliability and location awareness.
