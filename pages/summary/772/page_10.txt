The paper discusses a healthcare monitoring framework utilizing a mobile app and fog computing to enhance patient care, particularly in the context of the COVID-19 pandemic. 

In the frontend, the Android application allows patients, homeowners, and physicians to interact through various profiles. Patients can register their basic information and collect data from wearable devices, while physicians can monitor this data and set personalized notifications.

The cloud side of the system emphasizes the necessity for real-time analysis of patient data, which is crucial for timely responses, especially during a health crisis. The paper advocates for fog computing as an efficient solution to bridge the gap between cloud systems and IoT devices, enabling rapid data analysis without relying on internet connectivity. Fog nodes receive data from sensors via the LoRa network, which is effective for long-range communication with low bandwidth.

The experimental setup utilized an Intel Core i5 PC and Network Simulation version-2 to evaluate the effectiveness of fog computing in improving the monitoring framework's performance.

Additionally, the paper presents an initial COVID score system based on various health factors, guiding patient testing recommendations. If a patient's score exceeds six, they are advised to undergo a plasma test. The paper cites WHO data correlating specific lab tests and blood markers with COVID-19 severity, emphasizing the importance of these tests in managing patient care.
