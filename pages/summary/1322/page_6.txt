This paper discusses the evolution and classification of bio-inspired optimization algorithms, highlighting the diversity and modifications of various solvers over time. It reviews classical algorithms such as Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) and Differential Evolution (DE), alongside more recent algorithms like the Ant Colony Optimization (ABC), Bacterial Foraging Optimization Algorithm (BFOA), and the Bat Algorithm. Existing literature primarily focuses on comparing the performance of these algorithms to determine their effectiveness for specific problems, with several studies documenting comparisons based on characteristics like convergence speed and parallel implementation.

The paper notes that while some classifications of algorithms have been proposed, they often lack a hierarchical structure or fail to adequately address algorithm behavior. For example, previous classifications categorize algorithms based on their biological inspiration, yet do not form a true taxonomy. Recent surveys have attempted to classify algorithms based on behavior, but these do not create comprehensive taxonomies either.

The authors argue that there is a pressing need for a coherent taxonomy that considers both the source of inspiration and the solution generation procedures of the algorithms. They propose that their study will fill this gap by unveiling structural similarities between classical and modern meta-heuristics, thereby contributing to a more organized understanding of bio-inspired algorithms.
