In summary, differential vector movement is a predominant approach in behavior-based algorithms, accounting for a significant portion of the analyzed algorithms. The movement is based on calculating a differential vector from existing solutions, which can incorporate attraction and repulsion dynamics. The methods of determining this vector can vary, leading to different subcategories based on the solutions considered. These subcategories include using the entire population, a select group of representative solutions, or a specific group of solutions, each influencing the movement in unique ways. Notable examples include Particle Swarm Optimization and Differential Evolution, which illustrate the diversity and complexity within this framework.
