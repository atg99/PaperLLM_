The paper critically examines various bio-inspired algorithms, highlighting issues such as redundancy in proposed methods, where the same researchers introduce multiple algorithms based on similar concepts. The authors argue for the introduction of new algorithms only when they provide genuine optimization benefits and adhere to established scientific standards. They call upon journal editors to enforce stricter publication criteria, ensuring that new methods are well-founded, novel, and thoroughly validated against existing techniques.

Several studies cited in the paper critique specific algorithms, such as Intelligent Water Drops and Cuckoo Search, arguing that they often rely on misleading metaphors and lack true innovation. These critiques point out that many algorithms contain unnecessary complexity or biases that hinder their effectiveness. The authors advocate for simplification and transparency in metaheuristics to improve understanding and performance.

The paper concludes that the current trend of publishing metaphor-based algorithms is driven by a culture of quantity over quality, which undermines the advancement of the field. Authors stress the importance of rigorous evaluation and meaningful contributions to ensure that bio-inspired algorithms remain valuable tools in optimization.
