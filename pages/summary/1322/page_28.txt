The paper discusses various nature- and bio-inspired meta-heuristics categorized under Differential Vector Movement, where the differential vector is influenced by the entire population. A list of algorithms includes the Artificial Electric Field Algorithm (AEFA), Artificial Plants Optimization Algorithm (APO.1), and others, highlighting their respective years and references. 

The text further distinguishes between two subcategories of differential vector computation: subpopulation-based and neighborhood-based differential vectors. In subpopulation-based algorithms, the population is divided into smaller groups, affecting movement only within those groups, exemplified by algorithms like the Monarch Butterfly Optimization (MBO). Conversely, neighborhood-based algorithms, such as the Bacterial Foraging Optimization Algorithm (BFOA), utilize local neighborhood solutions to influence movement vectors through attraction or repulsion based on fitness levels.

Additionally, the paper introduces a category focused on solution creation, comprising algorithms that generate new solutions rather than modifying existing ones. This category represents nearly 31% of all proposals and includes classical algorithms like Genetic Algorithms (GA). A key approach to solution generation is through combination, where existing solutions are selected and merged using crossover operators to create new solutions, allowing for adaptability across various problems.
