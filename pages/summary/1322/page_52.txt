The paper discusses the structured design and application of bio-inspired algorithms for real-world optimization problems. It emphasizes a systematic approach that involves several critical phases, aiming to enhance the quality of research and practical applicability of these algorithms. The authors critique existing methodologies, highlighting misconceptions and poor practices while providing guidelines for the community to foster the development of effective metaheuristics.

Key phases outlined include:

1. **Problem Modeling and Mathematical Formulation**: This initial phase involves a thorough conceptualization and formulation of the optimization problem.
   
2. **Algorithmic Design, Solution Encoding, and Search Operators**: Here, the focus is on creating the algorithm, ensuring it aligns with the specific constraints of the problem rather than relying solely on metaphors.

3. **Performance Assessment, Comparison, and Replicability**: This phase emphasizes the importance of evaluating the algorithm’s performance based on established best practices, ensuring reliability and consistency in research findings.

4. **Algorithmic Deployment for Real-World Applications**: The final phase examines the practical implementation of the algorithm in real-world settings.

The manuscript provides a comprehensive overview of these phases, supplemented by a figure that summarizes key recommendations for each stage, ultimately aiming to bridge the gap between theory and practice in bio-inspired optimization.
