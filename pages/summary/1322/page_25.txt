The paper presents a taxonomy for population-based metaheuristics, organizing various optimization algorithms based on their behavioral mechanisms rather than their inspirations. The primary criterion for classification is the method of generating new solutions or modifying existing ones, which fundamentally shapes the search process of each algorithm.

The authors categorize the reviewed algorithms into two main groups:

1. **Differential Vector Movement**: This category involves generating new solutions through shifts or mutations of previous solutions. These newly created solutions can compete with existing ones to secure their position in the search space during subsequent iterations. Notable algorithms in this category include Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO), which utilizes a velocity vector for exploration, and Differential Evolution (DE), where new solutions are formed by adding differential vectors to existing ones. This category is further divided based on the reference solutions used for movement: 
   - Utilizing the entire population,
   - Focusing on significant solutions (best or worst candidates), or 
   - Restricting to a small neighborhood or subpopulation.

2. **Solution Creation**: In this group, new solutions arise from the combination of multiple solutions rather than the mutation of a single reference solution. Two main approaches are identified:
   - **Combination or Crossover**: Illustrated by Genetic Algorithms (GA), where multiple solutions combine to create new ones.
   - **Stigmergy**: An indirect coordination mechanism where solutions are generated based on interactions mediated by an intermediate structure, exemplified by Ant Colony Optimization (ACO) through pheromone trails.

The analysis of 518 algorithms indicates that a significant majority (69%) employ differential vector movement for new solution generation, with a focus on representative solutions, particularly the current best solution, guiding the search process. This approach is prevalent in over half of the reviewed algorithms, showcasing the dominance of differential movement strategies in nature- and bio-inspired optimization techniques.
