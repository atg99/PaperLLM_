The paper discusses the relationship between the biological inspiration of algorithms and their actual behaviors, highlighting that the performance of these algorithms is often more informative than their natural inspirations. The authors conducted a thorough review of 518 proposals and noted a significant discrepancy between the diversity of inspirational sources and the limited number of algorithmic behaviors, leading to the conclusion that the novelty of biological inspiration does not necessarily equate to unique or effective algorithms.

The analysis revealed that over 60% of the proposals generate solutions based on differential vector forces, akin to classical algorithms like Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) and Differential Evolution (DE). Further categorization indicated that 23% of the reviewed algorithms were closely related to classical algorithms, suggesting that many are merely incremental variants. Conversely, about 77% of the algorithms exhibited sufficient differences to be considered new proposals, although many lacked originality or proper justification, particularly in relation to state-of-the-art comparisons.

The paper identifies PSO as the most influential algorithm, appearing in 11% of the reviewed literature, and emphasizes its historical significance in Swarm Intelligence. GA and DE follow as the second and third most influential algorithms, each utilized in around 5% of the proposals. The authors conclude that the simplicity and efficiency of PSO have inspired numerous new metaheuristics that often closely mimic its solution dynamics.
