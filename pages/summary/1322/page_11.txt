Bio-inspired meta-heuristics based on Swarm Intelligence (SI) draw inspiration from the collective behaviors of animal societies, such as those of insect colonies and bird flocks, allowing for efficient problem-solving in optimization tasks. The Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) algorithm was the first to utilize SI concepts, soon followed by Ant Colony Optimization (ACO). This initial exploration led to the development of various other algorithms, including Artificial Bee Colony (ABC), Firefly Algorithm (FA), and Grasshopper Optimization Algorithm (GOA).

The reviewed algorithms are categorized based on the type of animal that inspired them, leading to four main subcategories: flying animals, terrestrial animals, aquatic animals, and microorganisms. Each category reflects the environmental context of the animals, which influences their movement and foraging behaviors. 

1. **Flying Animals**: Algorithms like PSO and ABC are inspired by the flight patterns of birds and insects.
2. **Terrestrial Animals**: This category includes ACO, which mimics the foraging behavior of ants, along with algorithms like Grey Wolf Optimization (GWO), Lion Optimization Algorithm (LOA), and GOA, which are based on the hunting strategies of land animals.
3. **Aquatic Animals**: Algorithms such as Krill Herd (KH) and Whale Optimization Algorithm (WOA) draw from the exploration mechanisms of aquatic creatures, including echolocation used by dolphins.
4. **Microorganisms**: This category focuses on algorithms like Bacterial Foraging Optimization Algorithm (BFOA), inspired by the food-seeking behavior of bacteria and the replication processes of viruses.

Additionally, SI-based algorithms can also be classified according to the specific behaviors of the animals that inspired them, further enriching the taxonomy of these optimization techniques. Each classification provides insights into the underlying principles that guide the algorithms, demonstrating the diverse applications of SI in solving complex optimization challenges.
