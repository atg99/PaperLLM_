The paper discusses the importance of evaluating proposed algorithms across diverse problems to gauge community interest and potential applications. It emphasizes the value of making source code publicly available, as this enhances visibility and facilitates thorough performance comparisons among researchers. The authors highlight a variety of software frameworks for Evolutionary Computation and Swarm Intelligence, noting that a reference implementation could prevent discrepancies in results due to ambiguous algorithm descriptions.

The paper also addresses the role of bio-inspired algorithms in competitions, pointing out that many top-performing metaheuristic algorithms are not biologically inspired, which may discourage the community from utilizing nature-inspired algorithms for solver design. The authors argue that the proliferation of bio-inspired proposals may detract from efforts aimed at developing fewer, high-performing algorithms.

In the reflection section, the authors categorize the applications of bio-inspired optimization algorithms into three horizons. They highlight the successful application of these algorithms in real-world engineering problems, including structural design and logistics. The paper notes significant contributions from Evolutionary Algorithms in various fields, such as civil engineering, NASA space missions, and physics research, reinforcing the relevance and impact of bio-inspired techniques in solving complex optimization problems.
