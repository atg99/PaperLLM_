The paper "Comprehensive Taxonomies of Nature- and Bio-inspired Optimization: Inspiration versus Algorithmic Behavior, Critical Analysis and Recommendations" presents a thorough examination of bio-inspired optimization algorithms, which have gained significant traction in recent years. The authors, Daniel Molina and colleagues, assess the challenges posed by the rapid increase in the number of these algorithms, highlighting a trend where the focus on new biological inspirations often overshadows effective problem-solving.

In this fifth revision of their work, the authors introduce two comprehensive taxonomies: one categorizing algorithms based on biological similarity and another based on operational behavior related to population models. They classify 518 bio-inspired algorithms and critically analyze their design trends, revealing that many algorithms exhibit poor alignment between their biological inspirations and actual behavior. Notably, over 25% of the reviewed algorithms are found to be variants of classical algorithms.

The paper also provides a critical perspective on the field, discussing the strengths, weaknesses, and methodological shortcomings within metaheuristic optimization. The authors revisit evolutionary and bio-inspired algorithms by outlining current challenges, offering methodological guidelines for comparison, and providing tutorials for real-world applications.

Additionally, they review recent literature on best practices for designing metaheuristic algorithms and summarize the active research landscape. The study concludes by highlighting a dichotomy in the field—while there is a plethora of innovative proposals, there remains a significant gap in analyzing their practical effectiveness.
