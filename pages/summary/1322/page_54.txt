The paper discusses various advancements and evaluations in the field of metaheuristic algorithms, focusing on their design, performance, and classification. It highlights the development of several algorithms, including the Search, Backtracking Search Optimization Algorithm, Grey Prediction Evolution Algorithm, Grey Wolf Optimizer, and Gaining-sharing Knowledge-based algorithm, which draw from classical approaches and provide a research direction for transforming nature metaphors into innovative structures.

A key contribution is the introduction of a guided learning strategy, which serves as a novel update mechanism for enhancing metaheuristic algorithms. This mechanism allows for dynamic adjustments between exploration and exploitation based on the population's dispersion, validated through testing nearly 60 algorithms.

Furthermore, the paper presents a statistical test designed to identify bias in origin-biased metaheuristics. This test reveals that certain algorithms, such as Grey Wolf Optimizer and Whale Optimization, exhibit bias, while others like Differential Evolution (DE), Genetic Algorithm (GA), and Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) do not.

The section on recent taxonomies and overviews summarizes various contributions to the classification and understanding of optimization algorithms. Noteworthy works include a 2020 taxonomy that categorizes algorithms based on their components and operational strategies, a 2021 critique on the originality of new metaheuristics, and a 2023 comprehensive review questioning the novelty of algorithmic proposals. An additional overview distinguishes between trajectory and population-based metaheuristics, providing foundational insights for researchers in the field.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the need for rigorous evaluation and classification of metaheuristic algorithms to foster innovation and address existing challenges in optimization.
