Tzanetos explores whether nature-inspired computing can achieve lifelike features, indicating a potential intersection between artificial life and computational methods. Camacho-Villalón et al. critically assess six optimization techniques derived from animal metaphors, highlighting the misleading aspects of these approaches. Kudela warns against certain evolutionary computation methods, suggesting a need for caution in their application.

Hu et al. propose a framework for distinguishing novelty in new metaheuristic algorithms, while de Armas et al. advocate for a comparison methodology to address similarities across metaheuristics. Swan et al. discuss the implementation of metaheuristics on a larger scale, emphasizing practical applications.

Walden and Buzdalov present statistical tests to challenge origin-biased metaheuristics.

Brahim et al. offer an overview of various metaheuristic optimization algorithms, while Martí et al. reflect on 50 years of metaheuristics, contributing to the historical context of these computational methods. Lastly, Tang and Yao provide insights on learning to optimize, encapsulating the evolution and future trajectory of optimization techniques.
