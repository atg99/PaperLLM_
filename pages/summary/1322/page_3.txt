The paper discusses the increasing prevalence of bio-inspired and nature-inspired optimization algorithms, highlighting a significant rise in publications from 2005 to April 2024. Despite this growth, many publications lack a proper taxonomy or comprehensive analysis of how their proposed algorithms compare to existing ones. This issue is compounded by a focus on the biological or natural metaphors driving these algorithms, which has led to ongoing debates in the field.

To address these challenges, the authors propose a comprehensive taxonomy for classifying both existing and future algorithms. They present two distinct taxonomies: the first categorizes algorithms based on their biological or natural inspiration, facilitating easy identification of algorithm types. The second taxonomy classifies algorithms based on their operational behavior in generating candidate solutions, irrespective of their inspirational sources. This dual approach aims to provide clarity and organization in the rapidly evolving landscape of optimization algorithms, thereby aiding researchers in understanding and categorizing various contributions to the field.
