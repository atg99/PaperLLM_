This section introduces a novel taxonomy for classifying nature- and bio-inspired algorithms based on their sources of inspiration. The taxonomy was developed through a comprehensive review of 518 papers, encompassing foundational algorithms from the late 1980s to contemporary contributions up to 2024. The classification is hierarchical, with categories that are mutually exclusive, ensuring that each algorithm fits into one specific category based on its unique features.

The complexity of classifying these algorithms arises from diverse sources of inspiration, including biological, physical, and human behaviors. While some existing categorizations exist, they may not adequately reflect the growing diversity of algorithms. The proposed taxonomy prioritizes algorithms based on their significance in the field, measured by citation counts and the number of inspired variants.

To maintain a balance between simplicity and informativeness, the authors established criteria for creating and dividing categories. A category must contain at least four algorithms to warrant subdivision, ensuring coherence among subcategories. The classification reveals that the largest group consists of Swarm Intelligence algorithms, representing over 53% of the reviewed proposals, followed by Physics and Chemistry (15%), Social Human Behavior (11%), and Breeding-based Evolution (7%). A new category, Plants Based, was identified, while nearly 10% of proposals could not be classified into existing categories.

The section concludes with a commitment to clarity in classification, providing a description of each category, its characteristics, examples, and a table of associated algorithms. This comprehensive approach aims to facilitate understanding and analysis of the diverse landscape of nature- and bio-inspired algorithms.
