The paper discusses the performance of nature-inspired optimization algorithms, highlighting the prevalence of two main categories: Swarm Intelligence (SI) and Evolutionary Algorithms (EA), particularly Genetic Algorithms (GA). It notes that while numerous new algorithms have emerged, a significant portion are minor variations of classical algorithms such as Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO), Differential Evolution (DE), and GA. The authors argue that despite the variety of inspirations drawn from nature, many new algorithms do not exhibit significantly innovative behaviors.

The paper presents several key lessons and recommendations for future research in bio-inspired optimization:

1. **Focus on Behavior Over Inspiration**: The authors suggest that the emphasis on the natural sources of inspiration often overshadows the actual performance and behavior of the algorithms. There is a call for more research prioritizing the properties and effectiveness of algorithms rather than merely their inspirations.

2. **Clarity in Terminology**: The extensive use of nature-based terminology can hinder understanding and replicability of algorithms. The authors recommend that descriptions of algorithms be more accessible and defined using mathematical terms, with clear correspondences to biological terminology.

3. **Importance of Comparisons**: The paper emphasizes the necessity of rigorous comparisons in evaluating new algorithms. Many proposals only compare against basic algorithms, which can lead to misleading conclusions about their effectiveness. The authors encourage researchers to include a broader range of competitive algorithms in their evaluations.

4. **Limited Influence of Proposals**: The analysis reveals that many proposed algorithms have minimal follow-up in terms of improvements or applications in new contexts, indicating a need for more impactful research in the field.

Overall, the paper calls for a shift in focus towards algorithmic behavior, clarity in communication, comprehensive comparative studies, and the pursuit of influential contributions in the domain of bio-inspired optimization.
