The paper discusses the evolution and impact of evolutionary algorithms (EAs) in academic competitions and their applications in machine learning (ML) and deep learning (DL). It highlights the significant role of differential evolution (DE) as a leading algorithm in global optimization contests, particularly in renowned conferences like GECCO and CEC. Additionally, the paper emphasizes the rising importance of EAs in the design and optimization of ML models, including the innovative use of EAs to enhance deep neural networks and to contribute to Neural Architecture Search.

However, the paper also critiques the proliferation of novel bio-inspired optimization techniques, arguing that many of these new algorithms lack substantial mathematical differences from existing methods. This trend has led to confusion in the field, as many proposed algorithms do not provide practical advantages and may merely serve to gain academic recognition. The authors cite examples of algorithms that are essentially reiterations of established methods, raising concerns about the lack of originality in recent contributions to optimization techniques.

The discussion includes specific questions about the validity of the biological inspirations behind some algorithms, suggesting that many do not accurately represent their natural counterparts. Overall, the paper calls for a more discerning approach to algorithm development that prioritizes genuine innovation and practical applicability in solving optimization problems.
