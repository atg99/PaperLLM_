The collection of papers presented focuses on various nature-inspired optimization algorithms, showcasing the application of biological behaviors and systems to solve complex engineering and computational problems. 

Yang et al. (2006) explored Virtual Ant Algorithms for optimizing the shear strength of CFRP structures, highlighting the effectiveness of bio-inspired methods in structural engineering. Yang (2005) introduced the Virtual Bee Algorithm for engineering optimizations, demonstrating how natural processes can inform algorithmic strategies.

Li et al. (2016) proposed a Virus Colony Search algorithm, while Juarez et al. (2009) developed a Virus Optimization Algorithm, both contributing to the field of metaheuristic optimization. Cortés et al. (2008) discussed viral systems as a novel approach to optimization, indicating the versatility of biological concepts in algorithm design.

Theraulaz et al. (1991) modeled task differentiation in wasp colonies, providing insights into self-organizing systems that can be applied in robotics. Liu et al. (2011) presented the Wolf Colony Algorithm, illustrating its utility in optimization scenarios.

Arnaout (2014) introduced Worm Optimization based on C. Elegans behavior, while Mirjalili and Lewis (2016) described the Whale Optimization Algorithm, both contributing to the diverse array of bio-inspired techniques. Trojovský and Dehghani (2023) presented a walrus-based optimization algorithm, further expanding the scope of biological inspirations.

Additional algorithms include the Marriage in Honey Bees Optimization (Yang et al. 2007), Weightless Swarm Algorithm (Ting et al. 2012), and the Wolf Search Algorithm with ephemeral memory (Tang et al. 2012), each addressing different optimization challenges.

Pinto et al. (2005) explored Wasp Swarm Optimization for logistics, while Braik et al. (2022) introduced the White Shark Optimizer for global optimization problems. Zaldívar et al. (2018) developed a model based on Yellow Saddle Goatfish behavior, and Trojovská et al. (2022) proposed the Zebra Optimization Algorithm, showcasing the breadth of bio-inspired methods.

Nguyen and Bhanu (2012) presented the Zombie Survival Optimization algorithm, reflecting the influence of popular culture on algorithmic design. Finally, Yang et al. (2018) contributed the Multi-species Cuckoo Search Algorithm for global optimization, emphasizing the collaborative potential of different species in problem-solving.

Overall, these studies underscore the innovative application of biological principles to enhance optimization techniques across various domains, revealing the potential for interdisciplinary approaches to complex challenges.
