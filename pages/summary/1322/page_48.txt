The authors highlight significant issues in the field of bio-inspired optimization, particularly concerning the lack of a robust statistical tradition, which results in poor methodological practices. This deficiency not only hinders authors' ability to present credible research but also limits reviewers' effectiveness in identifying major methodological flaws in submitted papers.

The analysis presented in the referenced studies critiques several nature-inspired algorithms, including the Big Bang-Big Crunch and the Water Wave Optimization. The authors conclude that most of these algorithms do not effectively emulate lifelike features such as intelligence or resilience, and many recent algorithms fail to accurately reflect the behaviors of their biological or physical inspirations.

Further, the authors argue that certain widely cited algorithms, such as grey wolf and firefly algorithms, are not novel and are merely variants of established methods like Particle Swarm Optimization. They emphasize that many proposed algorithms exhibit a center-bias, which skews experimental results favorably towards them when tested against specific fitness functions.

A critical concern raised is the questionable reproducibility of results in published studies. Many research contributions lack sufficient detail for third-party verification, often due to missing algorithm implementations or inadequate reporting of experimental conditions. The authors advocate for empirical evidence demonstrating superior performance and innovative design in new algorithms, rather than relying solely on novel metaphors.

They call for adherence to rigorous methodological practices, suggesting that new proposals should be tested against modern techniques using standard benchmarks and appropriate statistical analyses. The authors lament that many recent contributions do not meet these standards, reflecting ongoing problems in the field of bio-inspired optimization.
