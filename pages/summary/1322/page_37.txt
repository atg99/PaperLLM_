The paper discusses methods for generating new solutions through combination and stigmergy. The combining method can be tailored to specific problems or generalized for various problem families. Genetic Algorithms (GA) are highlighted as a prominent example, alongside other bio-inspired algorithms like Cultural Optimization (CA), Light Algorithm (LA), Particle Collision Algorithm (PCA), and Light Ray Optimization (LRO), which all exhibit similar combinatorial behaviors.

Stigmergy is another effective solution creation method, characterized by indirect communication among agents through an intermediate structure that guides the search for new solutions. Ant Colony Optimization (ACO) exemplifies this approach, where ants leave pheromone trails that influence subsequent solution generation based on the fitness of previous trajectories. The paper also notes other algorithms inspired by natural phenomena, such as water flow dynamics.

In analyzing the taxonomies of these algorithms, the paper reveals that there is a weak correlation between the classifications. Algorithms inspired by different concepts can exhibit similar characteristics, and those with similar inspirations can have varied search procedures. For instance, both the Delphi Echolocation algorithm and Dolphin Partner Optimization are based on dolphins but employ different solution creation methods. The largest subcategory in the second taxonomy comprises a diverse range of algorithms, indicating that inspiration from nature does not strictly dictate algorithm behavior. Overall, the paper emphasizes the complexity and diversity in the design of bio-inspired algorithms.
