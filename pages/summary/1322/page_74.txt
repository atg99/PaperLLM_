The referenced papers discuss various nature-inspired optimization algorithms that are designed to solve global optimization problems. These algorithms draw inspiration from biological behaviors and systems, demonstrating the applicability of nature's strategies to computational challenges.

Fausto et al. introduce a global optimization algorithm based on the behavior of selfish herds, emphasizing the advantages of collective movement in optimization. Zhao et al. present the Sea-horse optimizer, a novel meta-heuristic approach that shows promise in addressing complex optimization tasks.

The swarm-inspired projection algorithm by Su et al. and the slime mold model for numerical optimization by Monismith and Mayfield highlight the effectiveness of swarm intelligence in achieving optimal solutions. Raouf and Hezam propose the sperm motility algorithm, which mimics biological processes for global optimization.

Chand Bansal et al. explore the Spider Monkey Optimization algorithm, while Zamani et al. introduce the Starling murmuration optimizer, both contributing to the field of numerical optimization. The Seeker Optimization Algorithm by Dai et al. and the Seagull optimization algorithm by Dhiman and Kumar showcase theoretical advancements and applications in industrial engineering.

Kaur et al. discuss the Sandpiper optimization algorithm, and Shadravan et al. present the Sailfish Optimizer, focusing on constrained optimization problems. The Serval Optimization Algorithm by Dehghani and Trojovský and the Symbiotic Organisms Search by Cheng and Prayogo further expand the range of bio-inspired methods available.

Additional algorithms, including the social spider algorithm, squirrel search algorithm, and Salp Swarm Algorithm, demonstrate the versatility and effectiveness of nature-inspired techniques in addressing engineering design challenges. The Sling-shot spider optimization algorithm and Shark Smell Optimization algorithm add to the rich diversity of approaches available for optimization in various fields.

Overall, these contributions reflect a growing interest in leveraging biological principles for the development of efficient and innovative optimization algorithms suitable for a wide range of applications.
