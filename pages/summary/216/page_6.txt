The paper discusses generalization bounds for deep neural networks with residual connections, focusing on the relationship between hypothesis complexity and generalization capability. It introduces Lemma 1, which uses Rademacher complexity to establish a generalization bound for a function set H. The lemma indicates that if an upper bound for empirical Rademacher complexity can be determined, it directly leads to a generalization error bound. 

The proof notably references Lemma 2, which connects empirical Rademacher complexity to covering numbers. Specifically, it states that the empirical Rademacher complexity of a hypothesis set can be bounded by the covering number, which quantifies how many small balls are needed to cover the hypothesis space. 

The paper aims to derive generalization bounds for deep neural networks by upper bounding these covering numbers. It introduces a notation system termed the "stem-vine framework," which reflects the topological structure of neural networks composed of interconnected weight matrices and nonlinear operators. The work intentionally omits technical complexities related to measurability and integrability, assuming Fubini’s theorem applies to the multiple integrations discussed.
