In this paper, the authors investigate the generalization capability of deep neural networks, particularly focusing on networks with residual connections, such as ResNet. They highlight the complexity introduced by residual connections, which create parallel structures within the network, complicating the expression of the computed mapping as simple compositions of transformations. This necessitates a novel proof framework discussed in Section 6.3.

The authors find that the hypothesis complexity influenced by weight matrices remains consistent, regardless of whether they are part of the main structure (stem) or the additional pathways (vines). Specifically, the introduction of an identity vine does not alter the hypothesis complexity of the network. 

Additionally, they establish that the covering number for a chain-like neural network, which includes both the stem and vines, can be upper bounded by the product of the covering numbers of individual layers. This relationship is articulated through equations that demonstrate how contributions from both the stem and vines can be decomposed similarly, suggesting that residual connections do not degrade the network's generalization ability.

The paper exemplifies these concepts through a detailed analysis of the 34-layer ResNet, where the structural components are clearly delineated. The analysis reveals that while identity mappings do not affect hypothesis complexity, the influence of vines on the stem's covering bound remains an open question, indicating a need for further exploration of the interactions within the network's architecture.
