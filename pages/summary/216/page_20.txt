This subsection addresses the upper bound for the covering number of the hypothesis space formed by the stem of a deep neural network. The complexity of the hypothesis space increases as one moves from the first vertex N(1) to the last vertex N(L), driven by the weight matrices and nonlinearities. To establish this upper bound, an induction method is employed, leading to the formulation of Lemma 3, which is referenced from the work by Bartlett et al.

The proof of Lemma 3 consists of two main parts. Initially, the covering number of the hypothesis space generated by the first weight matrix A1 is directly bounded using Lemma 5. Subsequently, for the vertex following the j-th nonlinearity, denoted as N(2j+1), an "-cover of the hypothesis space H2j+1 is assumed. The analysis continues by examining the contribution of the subsequent weight matrix Aj+1. 

Assuming there exists an upper bound sj+1 for the norm of Aj+1, it is shown that for any function F2j+1(X) in H2j+1, a corresponding W(X) exists in W2j+1 such that the distance between them is less than a specified error term. Lemma 5 ensures that for any W(X) in W2j+1, there is an "-cover W2j+2(W) for the function space formed by applying Aj+1 to W(X). 

The analysis reveals that the overall error can be bounded, leading to the conclusion that the covering number can be characterized by the relationship involving sj+1 and the error term, ultimately demonstrating that the complexity of the hypothesis space grows in a controlled manner as layers are added to the network.
