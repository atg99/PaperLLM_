The paper investigates various methods related to the generalization capabilities of deep neural networks, emphasizing the significance of scale normalization. It introduces a definition of sharpness and establishes a link between sharpness and PAC-Bayes theory, demonstrating how these concepts can elucidate experimental findings.

Lang et al. contribute by examining capacity measures of deep neural networks through a geometrical invariance lens, proposing the use of the Fisher-Rao norm for capacity measurement. They highlight the invariance property of this norm and present norm-comparison inequalities that position the Fisher-Rao norm as a comprehensive framework for existing norm-based complexity measures, supported by experimental results.

Novak et al. conduct comparative experiments revealing that the input-output Jacobian norm and linear region counting are critical for the generalization ability of networks. They note that the generalization bound is significantly influenced by the proximity of the output hypothesis to the data manifold.

Additionally, works by Bartlett et al. and Neyshabur et al. provide upper bounds for the generalization error of chain-like deep neural networks. Bartlett et al. propose a spectral-normalized margin-based generalization bound, while Neyshabur et al. achieve a similar outcome within the PAC-Bayesian framework, with the current study partially motivated by Bartlett's analysis.

The preliminary section outlines essential concepts for expressing the generalization capability of algorithms and presents a margin-based generalization bound for multi-class classification. It defines generalization error as the difference between expected and empirical risk, underlining the negative correlation between an algorithm's generalization capacity and the complexity of its hypothesis space, in line with Occam's razor. The paper aims to provide theoretical tools for analyzing multi-class classification algorithms effectively.
