The paper discusses the extraction of hierarchical features from input data by neural networks through transformations and nonlinear transformations. It highlights the use of spectrum norms of weight matrices and Lipschitz constants to quantify the intensity of affine and nonlinear transformations, respectively. A function is defined as Lipschitz continuous if it satisfies a specific inequality relating the outputs of the function for two inputs within its domain, a property that is commonly found in nonlinearities used in neural networks, such as ReLU and sigmoid.

The paper categorizes important tasks for deep neural networks as multi-class classification, where input examples are paired with corresponding labels. Through optimization methods like stochastic gradient descent (SGD), neural networks are trained to fit the training data and make predictions on test data. A trained neural network computes a hypothesis function, which can be converted into a multi-class classifier by selecting the output coordinate with the highest value, thereby defining a margin that quantifies the confidence in label assignment.

The framework introduced in the paper, termed the stem-vine framework, defines functions computed by both the stem and vines of the network. The functions produced by the vines and the stem are mathematically represented, with the output hypothesis function at a specific vertex being expressed in terms of the outputs from both components. This framework allows for a structured representation of the functions within the neural network architecture.
