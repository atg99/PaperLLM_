This paper discusses the generalization capability of neural networks, particularly focusing on the effects of residual connections as seen in ResNet. The findings are rooted in a theoretical framework, specifically highlighting two main insights: (1) the influence of weight matrices on generalization ability remains consistent regardless of their placement within the network structure, and (2) the addition of an identity vine does not alter generalization. These insights provide a theoretical basis for the comparable generalization performance of ResNet and traditional chain-like networks.

The paper also establishes a relationship between expected risk, empirical risk, and generalization error, affirming that residual connections help significantly reduce training error, contributing to lower test error in practical applications.

Furthermore, the authors explore practical implications of their findings, particularly the impact of weight norms on generalization bounds. They note that higher norms of weight matrices correlate with worse generalization ability, leading to the justification for weight decay as a regularization technique. Weight decay, which incorporates the L2 norm of weights into the objective function, effectively controls weight magnitudes and enhances generalization ability. The paper cites a systematic study by Li et al. that examines the effects of weight decay on the loss surface of deep neural networks, supporting the theoretical claims made regarding weight decay and generalization.
