The introduction discusses the significant advancements in deep neural networks, particularly the adoption of residual connections since the introduction of ResNet. These connections have facilitated training deep networks effectively while preserving generalization capabilities. Despite empirical successes, there is a lack of theoretical analysis regarding how residual connections affect generalization ability. The paper raises concerns about the potential for increased hypothesis complexity due to the introduction of loops in the network structure, which could negatively impact generalization, as suggested by Occam's razor.

To address this, the authors examine the hypothesis complexity linked to residual connections through the lens of the covering number of the hypothesis space. They propose an upper bound for this covering number, demonstrating that, with a fixed number of weight matrices, the complexity remains consistent regardless of whether these matrices are part of residual connections or the chain-like "stem" of the network. This finding implies that residual connections do not inherently increase hypothesis complexity when weight matrices and non-linearities are fixed.

Furthermore, the authors establish a generalization bound for ResNet, which is applicable to all neural networks with residual connections. This bound indicates an inverse relationship between generalization ability and the product of the norms of weight matrices, suggesting that regularization techniques should be employed to manage these norms for improved generalization performance.
