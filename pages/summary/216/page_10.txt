In this section, the authors investigate the generalization capability of deep neural networks with residual connections, specifically deriving a generalization bound for ResNet as a case study. They leverage the margin-based multi-class bound established in previous lemmas to formulate their approach. The authors first present a general covering bound applicable to any deep neural network under the stem-vine architecture, followed by a specific covering bound for ResNet.

The paper emphasizes the importance of preventing overfitting when introducing new structures to enhance training performance. Although empirical evidence suggests that residual connections in ResNet reduce training error and improve training speed while maintaining generalization capabilities, theoretical support for these findings has been lacking.

The results indicate that the complexity of the hypothesis spaces in deep neural networks remains constant regardless of the configuration of weight matrices, whether in the stem or the vines. This leads to the conclusion that the generalization capability of networks with residual connections can be as good as those without residual connections, at least in worst-case scenarios. This theoretical insight helps explain the competitive training performance and generalization capability of networks with residual connections.

The authors then introduce Theorem 1, which outlines a covering bound for deep neural networks with residual connections. This theorem considers the structure of the network, including the stem and the associated weight matrices, and establishes conditions under which the covering bound holds. The detailed proofs for these covering bounds will be provided in the subsequent section.
