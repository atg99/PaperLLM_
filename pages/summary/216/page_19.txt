The paper investigates the performance of stochastic gradient descent (SGD) on the CIFAR-10 dataset, utilizing batch sizes of 128 and 8192. The results indicate that employing weight decay enables SGD to locate flatter minima in the loss surface, which correlates with lower test errors. This is evidenced by the findings illustrated in Figure 3. The paper also references various technical advances and empirical analyses.

In the appendix, the authors present proofs that were omitted from earlier sections. The focus begins with a proof of the covering bound for a single weight matrix, which serves as a foundation for subsequent proofs. The covering bound for deep neural networks under the stem-vine framework is established, alongside a specific covering bound for ResNet. Additionally, a generalization bound for ResNet is provided.

The subsection detailing the covering bound for a single weight matrix introduces a lemma that connects the complexity of output values from a data matrix multiplied by the weight matrix to the covering number. This lemma, based on the Maurey sparsification lemma, helps quantify the hypothesis space complexity induced by the weight matrix. The paper emphasizes that the flatness of the loss surface around minima is crucial for assessing generalization ability, although the underlying mechanisms remain unclear.
