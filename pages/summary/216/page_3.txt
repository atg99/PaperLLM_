This paper justifies the use of weight decay in training deep neural networks by employing the L2 norm of the weights as a regularization term. The structure of the paper is organized into several sections: Section 2 reviews literature on the generalization ability of deep neural networks from both theoretical and empirical perspectives. Section 3 provides necessary preliminaries, while Section 4 outlines the notation for deep neural networks with residual connections using the stem-vine framework. Section 5 presents the main results, including covering bounds for deep neural networks and ResNet, a generalization bound for ResNet, and practical implementations based on theoretical findings. Section 6 contains all proofs, and Section 7 concludes the paper.

In the related works section, the paper emphasizes the importance of understanding the generalization ability of deep neural networks. It discusses findings from Zhang et al., who demonstrated that neural networks can fit training data even with random labels, prompting further theoretical exploration of neural network success. Kawaguchi et al. address open problems related to the generalization capabilities of deep networks amidst their complexity and potential instability. Harvey et al. establish bounds on the VC-dimension of ReLU-activated networks, leading to generalization bounds related to the network's VC-dimension and training sample size. Golowich et al. focus on the sample complexity and Rademacher complexity, presenting improved bounds that are less dependent on network size. Finally, Neyshabur et al. investigate various methods that contribute to explaining the generalization ability of deep neural networks, including norm-based control and robustness.
