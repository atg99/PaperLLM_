The paper "Why ResNet Works? Residuals Generalize" by Fengxiang He, Tongliang Liu, and Dacheng Tao investigates the theoretical implications of residual connections in deep neural networks, specifically focusing on hypothesis complexity and generalization ability. The authors demonstrate that the upper bound of the covering number of a neural network's hypothesis space remains unchanged when using residual connections compared to chain-like architectures, provided the number of weight matrices and nonlinearities is fixed. This finding suggests that residual connections do not increase the hypothesis complexity of the network.

Furthermore, the paper derives a margin-based multi-class generalization bound for ResNet, illustrating that similar bounds can be applied to other advanced architectures like DenseNet and ResNeXt. The authors conclude that to achieve good generalization, regularization techniques, such as weight decay, should be employed to control the norms of the weight matrices. This emphasizes the importance of managing model complexity to enhance generalization performance in deep learning models.
