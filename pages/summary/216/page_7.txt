The paper discusses the architecture of a neural network structured as a "chain-like" system, referred to as the stem, which is complemented by residual connections termed vines. The stem consists of a sequence of weight matrices and nonlinearities, denoted as \( A_i \) and \( \phi_j \) respectively, where \( i \) and \( j \) index the weight matrices and nonlinearities. The stem is expressed as a vector \( S \) consisting of alternating weight matrices and nonlinearities, with a specified example where the number of weight matrices equals the number of nonlinearities.

Vertices are indexed between each weight matrix and nonlinearity, allowing for the identification of connections within the network. Vines serve to connect different vertices in the stem and can have multiple connections between the same vertices, indexed by a triple vector \( (s, t, i) \). Each vine is also composed of weight matrices and nonlinearities, which may differ in number from those in the stem.

The paper highlights the transformations induced by both weight matrices and nonlinearities, noting that weight matrices perform affine transformations while nonlinearities introduce nonlinear transformations. It also mentions scenarios where the number of weight matrices does not match the number of nonlinearities, exemplified by the architecture of a 34-layer ResNet, where a vine connects the stem between two nonlinearities.
