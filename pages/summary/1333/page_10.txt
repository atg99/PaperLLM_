The paper "Robot as Legal Person" by Avila Negri explores the concept of legal personhood for robots, examining the implications of granting such status in legal and ethical contexts. It references various scholarly works discussing the intersection of technology, law, and ethics, particularly focusing on how robots, as non-human entities, might fit into existing legal frameworks. 

Key concepts include the evolving nature of techno-regulation, the ethical considerations surrounding robotic care for the elderly, and the potential for robots to hold legal rights similar to corporations or other entities. The author engages with debates on robot rights, political performances in social robotics, and the anthropomorphism of AI, emphasizing the necessity of adapting legal structures to accommodate advancements in technology.

The paper asserts that recognizing robots as legal persons could lead to significant changes in liability, accountability, and social interactions. It argues for a re-evaluation of legal definitions and the implications of integrating intelligent machines into society. The author maintains that this exploration is crucial for understanding the future landscape of law and technology.

The paper is published under an open-access license, allowing for unrestricted distribution and use, provided proper credit is given to the original authors. The author declares no conflicts of interest, ensuring the integrity of the research presented.
