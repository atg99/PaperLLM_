The paper discusses the implications of integrating robotic artifacts into environments shared with humans, particularly in medical and caregiving contexts. It critiques the anthropomorphic perception of robots, which can lead to misconceptions about their capabilities, potentially attributing human-like autonomy and consciousness to them. This metaphorical thinking can blur the distinctions between humans and robots, complicating discussions around legal personhood.

The author argues that the legal framework may grant personhood to entities like corporations, suggesting that robots with artificial intelligence could similarly acquire rights and duties. This pragmatic approach to electronic personhood shifts focus from philosophical debates to functional aspects of legal personality. However, it raises concerns about the implications of equating robots with humans, especially regarding the risks associated with Socially Assistive Robotics.

Drawing on Borges' metaphor of a sphere, the paper reflects on the ethical considerations of robotic integration into daily life without waiting for definitive ontological classifications. It questions the societal impacts of social robots and emphasizes the need to critically assess the relationship between anthropomorphism and the imitation of human traits in robots. The author concludes that while the notion of electronic personhood may not resolve the ethical dilemmas posed by robots, it reinforces dangerous assumptions about their human-like qualities.
