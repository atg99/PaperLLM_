The concept of "legal person" refers to an autonomous center of legal relations, distinguishing between natural persons (human beings) and artificial legal persons (such as corporations). The paper discusses the ongoing debate surrounding the legal personhood of robots, emphasizing the need to differentiate between the questions of whether AI can and should be considered persons. It suggests that while it is possible for AI to achieve legal personhood, it may not be advisable.

The paper also highlights the distinction between moral personhood and legal personality, noting that legal rights can be granted without implying moral personhood. This differentiation is crucial as it relates to how courts may recognize rights without acknowledging broader moral considerations.

Furthermore, the paper explores the role of legal entities as decision structures that facilitate the identification of problems and normative solutions in legal practice. It critiques the oversimplified analogy between corporations and robots regarding legal personhood, emphasizing the complexities involved in attributing legal status to robots, particularly in light of the European Parliament's recommendations on Civil Law and Robotics.

The influence of digital platforms and technology on society is also discussed, with the assertion that programmers and engineers face challenges in translating ethical values into impactful regulations. The paper references the growing intersection of law and technology, suggesting that technological rules increasingly shape behaviors in cyberspace rather than traditional legal frameworks.

Robotics, while not new, is becoming more intertwined with AI, particularly as advancements in data processing and computational power enhance their capabilities. The European Parliament's 2017 resolution outlines characteristics of intelligent robots, proposing a potential legal status for more complex artifacts endowed with legal (electronic) personhood.

Overall, the paper critically examines the implications of granting legal personhood to robots, considering the risks and challenges associated with expanding legal subjectivity in contemporary legal frameworks.
