This paper explores the field of socially assistive robotics, focusing on the development of care robots for a children's hospital in Barcelona. The authors, as social scientists collaborating with an interdisciplinary team, investigate the ethical and social implications of integrating these robots into healthcare. They conducted three empirical research projects aimed at understanding the fears and hopes of various stakeholders involved in the care process, including children, nurses, hospital volunteers, relatives of hospitalized children, and roboticists.

Key aspects of their research include:

1. A participatory design process with 60 six-year-old children to create a care robot, utilizing design-thinking techniques over 12 sessions.
2. A Vision-Assessment process that involved focus groups and interviews with nurses, hospital volunteers, relatives, and physicians to identify perceived risks and benefits of care robots.
3. Interviews with roboticists to gather their concerns regarding the implementation of care robots.

The framework for their research was based on existing robot implementations in the hospital, such as pet robots for anxiety reduction, therapy robots for children with autism, and telecommunication robots for facilitating communication. The authors employed qualitative analysis methods, including thematic analysis and thick description, to interpret the data collected.

The paper also discusses the broader social and ethical debates surrounding AI and robotics, highlighting the polarization between utopian and dystopian views. The authors reference sociologist de Sousa Santos, who critiques the collapse of fear and hope in technological discourse, leading to a state of uncertainty. This uncertainty is not uniformly distributed; rather, it varies based on social groups' access to scientific knowledge and technology, creating disparities in experiences of fear and hope. The authors aim to build a responsible approach to AI and robotics that acknowledges these diverse perspectives and the associated uncertainties.
