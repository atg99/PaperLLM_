The paper discusses the complexities of care robots through the lens of Science and Technology Studies (STS), particularly emphasizing Actor Network Theory and social constructivism. It argues that technological innovations, including care robots, cannot be understood in isolation but rather as part of heterogeneous networks that encompass social, technical, and ethical dimensions. This perspective suggests that care robots are not merely tools but integral components of a wider relational network that includes various actors and processes.

The concept of interpretive flexibility is used to illustrate how the classification of robots varies depending on their context of use and the functions they serve. For instance, a robot may be labeled a care robot in one scenario, such as assisting in a hospital, while in another, it may be considered an entertainment robot. The authors highlight that technologies shape human actions and perceptions, a process termed "technological mediation," which underscores the moral implications of how technologies influence care practices and relationships.

The paper also explores children's perceptions of care robots, revealing that their sense of well-being in healthcare settings is closely tied to the presence of family and healthcare personnel. This finding suggests that the integration of robots into care networks must consider the social dynamics at play. The Robot Embedded in a Network approach (REN) is proposed to address the complexities and uncertainties inherent in daily care practices, such as the delegation of tasks between nurses and robots and the involvement of psychological staff in implementing robotic therapies for children.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the need to understand care robots as entities that mediate and transform care practices, highlighting the intricate relationships that define their role in healthcare settings.
