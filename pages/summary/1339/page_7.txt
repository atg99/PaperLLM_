The paper argues that ethical discussions surrounding AI and robotics in healthcare should extend beyond theoretical debates to include practical implications and the lived experiences of those involved in care. It highlights the need to investigate the interactions and relationships formed between patients, healthcare providers, and robots, rather than merely assessing the pros and cons of robotic technologies. By focusing on the social context, the research aims to uncover the nuances of care practices and the ethical dilemmas that emerge from the integration of robots.

The authors propose a framework based on three key assumptions: first, ethical reflection should start by identifying the actors and processes involved in the care network; second, ethical implications are context-specific and influenced by how robots alter existing care relationships; and third, a responsible approach to introducing robots in healthcare must align with the specific ethical principles and values of that context.

In conclusion, the paper emphasizes that understanding the role of robots in healthcare requires a relational approach that considers the complexities of care dynamics, advocating for a more nuanced exploration of ethical and social issues in the deployment of care robots.
