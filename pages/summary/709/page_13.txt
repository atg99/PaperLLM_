The experimental environment was constructed using real marine data, which was rasterized into a grid format with a size of 0.1 nautical miles. This created a two-dimensional simulation space measuring 400 by 350, where each coordinate represents either a navigable area (0) or an obstacle (1). The simulation environment was designed in Python, displaying navigable regions as white and non-navigable areas as black.

The model's goal is twofold: to orient towards the target and make actionable decisions. In scenarios with minimal obstacles, the model directs the ship swiftly to the target. Conversely, when obstacles are present, it enables the ship to navigate around them effectively.

The model employs ReLu as the activation function within its neural network, promoting sparse and efficient learning. This choice aids in overcoming gradient issues, enhances autonomous learning capabilities, and improves training speed, ultimately ensuring reliable path planning for coastal ships. The experimental setup and parameter definitions are detailed in the accompanying tables and figures.
