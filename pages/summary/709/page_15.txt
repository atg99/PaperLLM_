The paper presents an experimental verification of a ship path planning model designed for complex marine environments. The model's effectiveness is demonstrated through a series of iterations, showcasing improvements in path planning as the iterations increase. Initially, at the 500th iteration, the path exhibits numerous backtracking behaviors due to the model's trial-and-error learning process. As the iterations progress to 1000, 2000, and finally 3000, the path becomes more refined, successfully avoiding obstacles and stabilizing in trajectory.

Figures included in the paper illustrate the changes in path planning results over these iterations. By the 3000th iteration, the model consistently achieves safe navigation to target points without colliding with obstacles. Two specific marine environment cases are analyzed, confirming that the model adheres to navigation specifications and effectively reaches designated target coordinates. The results indicate that the model is capable of adapting to various marine scenarios while ensuring safety and compliance with navigation standards. Overall, the experimental outcomes validate the model’s credibility and effectiveness in path planning for maritime applications.
