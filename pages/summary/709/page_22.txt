The paper presents a global path planning model for coastal ships utilizing an optimized Deep Q-Network (DQN) algorithm. It identifies limitations in traditional path planning methods, such as slow convergence and non-optimized paths due to a lack of autonomous learning and data recycling. The proposed model divides the path planning process into three components: environment status processing, path searching, and path smoothing. 

A key innovation is the optimization of the reward function, which incorporates potential rewards for target points and penalties for obstacles, addressing convergence issues seen in traditional DQN approaches. The model is trained using actual marine environment data through a grid method, aligning its structure with ship navigation rules. 

Experimental results indicate that the optimized DQN algorithm outperforms traditional methods in terms of convergence speed, accuracy, navigation error, and planning time, thus confirming its effectiveness. However, the study acknowledges the limitation of not addressing scenarios with both static and dynamic obstacles, suggesting that future research will focus on more complex marine environments for verification.
