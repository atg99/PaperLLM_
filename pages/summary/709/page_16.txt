The paper discusses a model for ship path planning in various marine environments, demonstrating its effectiveness through experimental verification. The model employs a series of iterations to refine the planned path, as evidenced by results shown in figures depicting the trajectory at different iteration stages. Specifically, in marine environment case 1 and case 2, the model successfully navigates around obstacles while adhering to navigation specifications, ensuring safe arrival at target points.

The research also incorporates an obstacle potential map, where different colors indicate potential energy levels associated with navigational hazards. The model's predictions consistently avoid high potential energy areas, indicating effective obstacle avoidance and safe navigation.

Additionally, the paper touches on trajectory data compression algorithms used for path smoothing, categorizing them into linear and nonlinear approaches. While linear methods are simpler and computationally efficient, nonlinear fitting provides a more accurate representation of the real track but requires more complex calculations. Overall, the study validates the model's capability to plan safe and efficient paths in complex marine environments.
