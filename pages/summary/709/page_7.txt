The paper discusses the navigation of ships in accordance with the International Regulations for Preventing Collisions at Sea (COLREGS), emphasizing the importance of these regulations in path planning models for marine navigation. The concept of "self-ship" is introduced, referring to a ship's decision-making based on its environmental observations, while surrounding vessels and obstacles are termed "other ships". 

When navigating a clear area, the self-ship should optimize its path considering economic factors. In contrast, when encountering obstacles, it must prioritize safe avoidance while adhering to COLREGS. The paper categorizes ship encounters into three types: head-on, crossing, and overtaking. The head-on situation requires both ships to turn right; in crossing scenarios, the give-way ship must maneuver, while the stand-on ship maintains its course; overtaking involves the stand-on ship continuing without action.

In situations of restricted visibility, the responsibilities of ships become less clear, and specific turning protocols are outlined depending on the direction of approaching vessels. The study primarily focuses on scenarios with good visibility, highlighting the navigation strategies necessary for safe maritime operations.
