The paper emphasizes that the process of ship path planning can be viewed as a reinforcement learning task, where the ship selects actions based on its current state and receives feedback from the environment in the form of rewards or punishments. This feedback is crucial for learning optimal actions that maximize positive outcomes.

Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) is highlighted as a powerful approach that combines deep learning and reinforcement learning, allowing for end-to-end learning from input to output. The DQN algorithm, a prominent DRL method, is discussed in detail, noting its innovations such as experience replay, the use of a target value network to stabilize learning, and constraints on reward and error values to maintain reasonable ranges during training. These features contribute to DQN's adaptability and stability across various tasks.

The application of DRL in transportation, particularly in unmanned driving and path planning, is noted as an area of growing interest, with successes in marine contexts such as surface unmanned vehicles. However, the paper identifies a gap in research specifically focused on DRL-based path planning for coastal ships. It critiques existing methodologies that combine DRL with traditional algorithms, arguing that they dilute the importance of the reward function, potentially hindering the learning of optimal strategies.

To address issues of slow convergence and training inefficiencies associated with traditional reward functions, the paper proposes an enhanced reward structure based on the COLREGS navigation rules. This new approach incorporates potential energy rewards related to target points, reward areas near targets, and danger zones near obstacles, aiming to improve the learning efficiency of the DRL model.
