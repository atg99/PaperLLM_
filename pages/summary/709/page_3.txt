The paper discusses the evolution of path planning algorithms for coastal ships, categorizing them into traditional non-intelligent algorithms, bionic intelligent algorithms, and machine learning algorithms. 

Traditional non-intelligent algorithms include the velocity obstacles (VO) algorithm, A* algorithm, and artificial potential field (APF) algorithm. These methods, while straightforward and computationally efficient, often struggle with multiple local minima, making it difficult to guarantee optimal solutions. For instance, Kuwata et al. employed the VO algorithm to enhance navigation safety for unmanned surface vehicles (USVs), but encountered challenges in achieving optimal paths. Similarly, Petres et al. utilized the APF to create a virtual gravitational field for obstacle avoidance, but faced issues with local minima and oscillation around target points. Campbell et al. developed a real-time path planning method based on an enhanced A* algorithm, but its reliance on grid design affected accuracy and speed. Xue et al. combined APF with maritime collision avoidance rules, showing effectiveness in path search but difficulty in unknown environments.

Bionic intelligent algorithms, including ant colony optimization (ACO) and genetic algorithms (GA), aim to improve path planning efficiency. For example, Xin et al. enhanced the particle swarm optimization (PSO) algorithm to address premature convergence, while Ding et al. applied PSO with path distance constraints but faced issues with diversity loss. Lazarowska et al. reformulated path planning as a dynamic optimization problem using ACO, yielding optimal paths and collision avoidance strategies, though these methods generally require significant prior knowledge and computational resources.

Overall, the paper highlights the strengths and weaknesses of various path planning algorithms in the context of coastal ship navigation, emphasizing the ongoing need for advancements in computational efficiency and optimality in complex environments.
