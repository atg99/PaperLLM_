The paper presents a comparative analysis of various path planning algorithms applied to ship navigation, highlighting their effectiveness in terms of path length, planning time, and maneuverability. The algorithms evaluated include traditional Deep Q-Network (DQN), Q-learning, Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (DDPG), A*, BUG2, and Artificial Potential Field (APF). 

The results indicate that the path generated by the proposed optimized DQN algorithm is smoother, with fewer corners and a safer distance from obstacles, aligning better with actual navigation requirements. In contrast, the A* algorithm produced paths that were closer to obstacles and had an excessive number of corners, increasing driving risks. The BUG2 algorithm required circular navigation around obstacles, resulting in less efficient paths. 

Quantitatively, the optimized DQN achieved a path length of 137.612 meters in 0.6105 seconds with six corners, outperforming other methods in both efficiency and adherence to navigation standards. The comparative data demonstrated that the proposed algorithm not only provided a shorter path but also reduced time consumption and improved overall path planning effectiveness. 

In conclusion, the optimized DQN algorithm offers a significant advancement in ship path planning, ensuring safer and more efficient navigation while meeting essential operational criteria.
