The paper titled "Path Planning of Coastal Ships Based on Optimized DQN Reward Function" by Siyu Guo et al. addresses the critical issue of path planning in coastal navigation, which is essential for the intelligent development of ships. The authors propose a model that utilizes an optimized deep Q network (DQN) algorithm, incorporating environment status information to enhance training effectiveness based on real navigation conditions and international collision avoidance regulations.

The DQN algorithm in the model consists of four key components: ship state space, action space, action exploration strategy, and a reward function. The traditional reward function, which can hinder learning efficiency and convergence speed, is improved through three main enhancements: the introduction of potential energy rewards related to the target point, the establishment of reward areas near the target, and the addition of danger zones near obstacles. These optimizations enable the ship to navigate obstacles more effectively and reach the target point more quickly, thereby accelerating the model's convergence.

The authors conducted experimental comparisons between the optimized DQN algorithm and traditional algorithms such as A*, BUG2, and artificial potential field (APF). The results demonstrated that the optimized DQN algorithm offers superior stability, faster convergence, and significantly reduced calculation time, ultimately allowing for optimal path planning that adheres to actual navigation rules. This advancement contributes to enhancing the safety, economic efficiency, and autonomous decision-making capabilities of coastal ship navigation. 

In summary, the paper highlights the importance of intelligent path planning for maritime safety and presents an innovative approach that leverages deep reinforcement learning to improve navigation outcomes.
