The paper discusses the development of a coastal ship path planning model using an optimized Deep Q-Network (DQN). The model enhances action decision-making quality by integrating ship-environment interactions and navigation constraints. Key parameters for the neural network training include an action space size of 8, a learning rate of 0.01, and the use of the ReLu activation function, which promotes efficient training and feature learning.

The effectiveness of the model is validated through simulations at different training iterations. Initially, at 500 iterations, the path reached the target but exhibited many backtrack behaviors due to the exploratory nature of the DQN algorithm. After 1000 iterations, the model began to plan paths with fewer redundancies, although it still encountered obstacles. By 2000 iterations, the path showed improvements in safety with reduced collisions, but still had significant fluctuations. After 3000 iterations, the model successfully navigated without hitting obstacles, demonstrating a stable path while maintaining slight fluctuations due to the exploration rate.

Overall, the study highlights the model's progression and effectiveness in autonomous ship path planning, emphasizing the balance between exploration and safety in navigation.
