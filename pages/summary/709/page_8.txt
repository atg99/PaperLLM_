The paper discusses the design of a coastal ship path planning model, focusing on the optimization of the reward function, which is crucial for the effectiveness and efficiency of neural network training. The reward function provides feedback based on the ship's actions, indicating performance through positive or negative values.

Traditional reward functions assign fixed rewards based on the proximity of the ship to a target point, leading to inefficiencies in learning and slow convergence. To address these issues, the paper proposes three optimizations:

1. **Potential Energy Reward**: A dynamic reward based on the distance from the ship to the target point is introduced. The reward increases exponentially when the ship moves closer to the target, encouraging faster approach.

2. **Reward Area Near Target**: A designated reward area around the target point is established, providing varying rewards based on the ship's distance from the target. This approach aims to expedite model convergence while preventing local optima by maintaining appropriate gaps between rewards.

3. **Danger Area Near Obstacles**: Although not explicitly detailed in the provided text, this optimization likely involves penalizing the ship for approaching obstacles, thereby enhancing safety.

These optimizations collectively aim to improve the ship's ability to navigate towards the target efficiently while avoiding obstacles, ultimately accelerating the convergence of the path planning model.
