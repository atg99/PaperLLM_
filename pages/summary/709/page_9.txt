The paper discusses the design of a dangerous area surrounding obstacles in maritime navigation to prevent ship collisions. It defines the dangerous area as a zone where a ship is close to an obstacle but has not yet collided. To facilitate the ship's safe navigation away from obstacles, the punishment for being in this area is increased, while ensuring that the punishment is not overly dense to avoid local minima situations. The design is mathematically represented by a formula that categorizes the distance from the obstacle into different thresholds with corresponding penalties.

The reward function for the Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) algorithm is also formulated as a nonlinear piecewise function, assigning different rewards based on the ship's state. It distinguishes between navigable and non-navigable areas, incorporating factors such as distance to the target point and obstacles.

The paper further describes the state space and action space for the DRL model. The state space includes real-time positional and azimuth information from ship sensors, capturing the ship's relationship to its target and nearby obstacles. The action space defines the possible movements of the ship, balancing the need for a comprehensive range of actions with the efficiency of training the model to avoid overly complex navigation paths.
