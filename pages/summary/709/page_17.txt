The study presents a comprehensive analysis of ship path planning in a marine environment, utilizing an obstacle potential map to visualize potential energy levels related to obstacles. The colors in the potential map indicate varying levels of influence from obstacles, with red representing high potential energy areas (obstacles) and blue indicating navigable waters. The interaction between the predicted path and the marine environment is illustrated, demonstrating that the ship successfully avoids high potential energy areas, thereby preventing collisions.

Figures included in the paper depict the initial marine environment, the ship's starting and target positions, and the model's predicted path. The results confirm that the ship's trajectory, guided by the DP algorithm, effectively navigates around obstacles, ensuring a safe arrival at the target location.

The DP algorithm operates by connecting the start and end points of a curve with a straight line, measuring distances from intermediate points, and selectively retaining points based on a defined threshold. This method is efficient, requiring predefined start and end points, and is particularly suited for offline compression in ship path planning.

In conclusion, the research underscores the effectiveness of the DP algorithm in enhancing ship navigation by simplifying paths while ensuring safety in obstacle-rich marine environments.
