The paper emphasizes the importance of defining an appropriate action space for ship path planning in a dynamic maritime environment. It identifies the challenges associated with having either too many or too few actions, explaining that a well-structured action space significantly affects the efficiency of the training process and the quality of the resulting navigation paths. 

The proposed method generalizes the ship's action space into eight discrete actions, including cardinal and diagonal movements, to enhance the exploration of the environment without overly complicating the training process. 

Additionally, the exploration strategy is designed to gradually shift focus from exploring new actions to exploiting known successful actions as the training progresses. This adaptive approach is crucial for developing an effective path planning model that seeks to avoid local optima while maximizing future rewards. 

Overall, the paper provides a comprehensive framework for improving ship navigation through careful consideration of state and action spaces, as well as a strategic exploration method in deep reinforcement learning.
