This method utilizes a series of grids to represent the marine environment, allowing for systematic modeling. The grid size affects the resolution of environmental representation, with larger grids leading to reduced detail and potential loss of optimal paths in densely packed obstacle areas, while smaller grids enhance detail but require longer computation times.

The authors highlight the importance of applying the COLREGS (International Regulations for Preventing Collisions at Sea) in path planning to maintain safety and practicality during navigation. The paper proposes enhancements to traditional reward functions used in algorithms by integrating potential energy rewards and danger zones near obstacles, aimed at improving convergence speed and stability. The grid method transforms the marine environment into a two-dimensional simulation, categorizing areas as navigable or non-navigable, which is crucial for effective path planning. The findings suggest that this approach can optimize coastal ship navigation processes while ensuring safety and economic efficiency.
