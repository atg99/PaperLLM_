The paper presents a comparative analysis of various path planning algorithms, including traditional DQN, Q-learning, DDPG, A*, BUG2, and APF, in a marine environment. The proposed algorithm demonstrates superior performance in terms of path length, planning time, and the number of corners in the path, making it more suitable for actual navigation requirements.

In the results, the traditional DQN algorithm generated a path with a length of 154.241 m and 16 corners, while the Q-learning algorithm produced a 146.617 m path with 6 corners. The traditional DDPG algorithm yielded a longer path of 172.315 m but did not calculate corners due to its arc-like path. The A* algorithm, although shorter at 116.421 m, had 28 corners, which increased navigational risk. The BUG2 algorithm offered a 92.728 m path with 6 corners but took the longest time at 2.3268 s. The APF algorithm's path was 135.513 m long, but like DDPG, it did not account for corners.

In contrast, the proposed algorithm achieved a path length of 137.612 m in 0.6105 s with only 6 corners, indicating a smoother trajectory that maintains a safe distance from obstacles and aligns better with navigational standards. Overall, the findings suggest that the proposed algorithm is more efficient and effective for marine path planning compared to the traditional methods evaluated.
