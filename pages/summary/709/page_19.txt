This paper presents a comparative analysis of path planning algorithms, focusing on an optimized Deep Q-Network (DQN) algorithm. The results indicate significant improvements in path length and efficiency after optimization. Specifically, the number of path corners was reduced from 6 to 1, and the path length decreased from 137.612 meters to 111.318 meters.

The study compares the proposed algorithm with traditional DQN and other algorithms, including Q-learning, DDPG, A*, BUG2, and APF, across three criteria: path length, number of corners, and planning time. The proposed algorithm demonstrated a faster convergence rate, achieving a minimum step count of 103 steps compared to the traditional DQN's 138 steps. The convergence trend for the optimized algorithm began around the 200th training round, while the traditional DQN only began to converge around the 450th round, indicating better stability and decision-making capabilities of the proposed method.

Figures illustrate the performance of various algorithms in a marine environment, highlighting the proposed algorithm's ability to generate safer and more economical paths with fewer redundant corners. Overall, the findings suggest that the optimized DQN algorithm offers superior efficiency in path planning, making it a valuable contribution to the field of marine navigation.
