This paper discusses optimizing ship navigation trajectories using the Douglas-Peucker (DP) algorithm to enhance safety and economic efficiency. The initial path planning results include random explorations which lead to redundant corners, necessitating a smoothing process to eliminate unnecessary turns. The integration of the DP algorithm achieves a more streamlined path, as illustrated in the figures provided. 

Table 2 quantifies the improvements before and after optimization, demonstrating a reduction in path corners from six to one and a decrease in path length from 137.612 meters to 111.318 meters, thus indicating enhanced navigation efficiency. 

Additionally, a comparative analysis is presented between the traditional Deep Q-Network (DQN) algorithm and the proposed algorithm. The results, shown in a graph, depict the convergence speed and training effectiveness, revealing that the proposed algorithm outperforms the traditional DQN in terms of training iterations and steps from the start to the target point. This analysis reinforces the advantages of the proposed method in optimizing ship navigation paths.
