The paper discusses various machine learning approaches for autonomous path planning in maritime contexts, particularly using reinforcement learning techniques. Zhou et al. proposed a deep reinforcement learning method for unmanned surface vehicles (USVs) that incorporates a motion model and collision risk assessment, validated through simulations and real-world tests. Chen et al. introduced Q-learning for unmanned cargo ships, which, despite its effectiveness in training optimal paths, struggles with high-dimensional spaces and slow convergence due to large Q-tables. Zhang et al. developed a hierarchical DRL model aimed at port navigation, demonstrating improved safety and obstacle avoidance capabilities. Yu et al. applied an enhanced DRL approach for trajectory tracking of underwater vehicles, achieving significant results in complex environments. Wang et al. presented a decision-making model for maritime surface ships (MASS) that utilizes an improved DRL structure, showing enhanced navigation and obstacle avoidance in uncertain conditions.

While these DRL approaches offer strong learning capabilities and stability, challenges such as extended training times and slow convergence persist. The paper also highlights the Markov Decision Process (MDP) as a foundational model for decision-making in reinforcement learning, emphasizing its components: state information, action space, transition probabilities, reward functions, and discount factors, all crucial for developing effective path planning strategies for coastal ships.
