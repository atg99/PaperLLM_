The paper discusses the implementation of a new basic block in deep neural networks that incorporates a dropout layer to mitigate overfitting. It highlights the use of ReLU activation functions across all neurons. A specific ResNet model designed for image classification tasks achieved a Top-1 classification accuracy of 49% on the tiny ImageNet validation set.

To address the issue of limited images—500 per class in the tiny ImageNet dataset—data augmentation techniques were employed. The authors opted for an online augmentation method due to memory constraints, where images are randomly transformed during batch processing. This involves horizontal flipping with a 50% probability and random cropping to a 56x56 size before rescaling back to 64x64, with a cropping probability of 0.7.

The experimental results reveal insights into the vanishing gradient problem in deep neural networks. The authors compared shallow networks with 7 and 9 layers, finding that while ResNet and ConvNet performed similarly for the 9-layer network, the 7-layer ResNet underperformed compared to ConvNets. This suggests that the addition of convolutional layer outputs to their inputs can hinder training unless other benefits are present in deeper networks, which can lead to improved accuracy.

Further experiments involved training various deep ResNets ranging from 12 to 21 layers on the tiny ImageNet dataset. The best-performing network, referred to as Net 1, achieved a validation accuracy of 49%. An equivalent plain ConvNet (Net 3) was also trained for comparison.
