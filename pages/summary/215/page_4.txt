The paper discusses the performance of ResNet compared to a conventional ConvNet on the CIFAR-10 dataset. It highlights that ResNet achieves significantly higher training and validation accuracies, with a difference of 30% in training accuracy and 13% in validation accuracy compared to the ConvNet. However, it also notes that ResNets are more susceptible to overfitting, as evidenced by a 23% difference in accuracy between training and validation, compared to 7% for the ConvNet.

To mitigate overfitting, the authors employed dropout and stochastic augmentation techniques, which reduced the overfitting gap by 6%. They also explored reducing the model's complexity by using fewer convolutional layers in a smaller network (Net 3), which resulted in a 16% difference in accuracy but a 3% decrease in overall validation accuracy.

In conclusion, while adding shortcut connections in ResNet enhances accuracy and training speed in image classification tasks, it increases the risk of overfitting. The study demonstrates that applying strategies like dropout and stochastic augmentation can help manage this issue, and careful design can allow for fewer parameters without significantly sacrificing performance.
