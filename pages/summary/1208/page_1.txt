This paper presents a novel approach to passive beamforming in reconﬁgurable intelligent surface (RIS)-assisted wireless communication systems using unsupervised learning. The authors highlight the challenges posed by the unit modulus constraint on the phase shifts of reflecting elements, which complicates the derivation of optimal solutions. Traditional methods, such as semi-definite relaxation (SDR), provide suboptimal solutions but suffer from high computational complexity, making them impractical for real-time applications.

The proposed method leverages a customized deep neural network (DNN) trained offline through unsupervised learning, eliminating the need for labeled data and significantly reducing computational time. Simulation results demonstrate that this approach maintains comparable performance to the SDR-based methods while offering lower complexity.

The introduction of RIS technology is framed within the context of reducing deployment costs and energy consumption in fifth-generation wireless systems. The paper distinguishes RIS from other technologies like amplify-and-forward relaying and backscatter communications, emphasizing its low power consumption and passive nature. The study also reviews existing literature on passive beamforming and the application of deep learning in wireless communications.

The paper is structured as follows: Section II outlines the RIS-assisted communication model and problem formulation; Section III discusses conventional passive beamforming approaches; Section IV details the proposed unsupervised learning method; Section V presents simulation results; and Section VI concludes the findings. Overall, this research contributes to enhancing the efficiency of wireless communication systems through innovative machine learning techniques.
