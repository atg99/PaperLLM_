The paper presents a study on optimizing the phase shifts of a Reconfigurable Intelligent Surface (RIS) in a wireless communication system comprising an access point (AP), a RIS with multiple reflecting elements, and a single-antenna user. The system model illustrates that the received signal at the user is a combination of direct signals from the AP and reflected signals from the RIS, with the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) expressed in a specific mathematical form.

The optimization problem P1 aims to maximize the transmission rate by selecting the optimal phase shifts while adhering to certain constraints. However, it is identified as a non-convex quadratically constrained quadratic program (QCQP), making it challenging to derive an optimal solution, especially for multi-antenna scenarios. The paper discusses conventional approaches, highlighting that for a single antenna, the optimal phase shifts can be achieved through phase alignment. In the multi-antenna case, the problem is reformulated into a homogeneous QCQP, allowing for a relaxation that leads to a convex problem, which can be solved to yield a suboptimal solution.

To address the limitations of traditional optimization methods, which may not guarantee performance and often involve high complexity, the authors propose a deep learning-based framework. This approach aims to enhance the performance and efficiency of phase shift optimization in RIS-assisted communication systems.
