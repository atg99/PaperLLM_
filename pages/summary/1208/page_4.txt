The paper presents simulation results for a proposed unsupervised learning approach, specifically the RISBFNN, in an indoor communication scenario characterized by independent Rayleigh fading. The authors evaluate the performance based on the number of training samples, reflecting elements, and antennas.

Key findings indicate that increasing the number of reflecting elements (N) enhances performance for both RISBFNN and the conventional SDR approach. For the single antenna case, the performance of RISBFNN closely matches that of the optimal solution, while in multi-antenna scenarios, the gap between RISBFNN and SDR widens as N increases.

The impact of the number of antennas (M) also shows improved performance for both methods as M increases. The RISBFNN significantly outperforms random phase methods and achieves results comparable to SDR across various configurations.

In terms of computational complexity, the RISBFNN exhibits a much lower complexity of O((64M + 804)N^2) compared to the SDR's O(N^6.5). This difference translates into a considerable reduction in running time, making RISBFNN substantially faster and more practical for real-time applications, particularly given the constraints of channel coherence time in dynamic environments.

Overall, the RISBFNN emerges as a promising alternative to traditional methods, providing competitive performance with significantly reduced computation time, making it suitable for practical implementation in wireless communication systems.
