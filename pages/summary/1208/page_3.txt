This paper discusses a neural network architecture termed "RISBFNN" designed for efficient training and improved performance in an unsupervised learning context. 

In the feature design section, it critiques the initial use of channels G and hr as inputs, proposing instead the use of their product structure, which enhances training efficiency by reducing input dimensions while capturing structural information. The resulting feature vector is denoted as F and consists of real and imaginary parts treated as separate features.

The loss function is defined as the negative of the objective function, differing from traditional mean square error approaches. This reflects the unsupervised nature of the model. The paper employs a Lambda layer to convert the predicted phase shift vector into a complex form for loss computation.

The network architecture comprises five fully-connected layers with neuron counts proportional to N, utilizing ReLU activation for the first four layers and a linear activation for the output layer. The training process employs the Adam optimizer, with a learning rate of 0.001, maximum epochs of 1000, and early stopping to enhance efficiency. The learning rate is also decayed based on validation loss performance.

Key hyperparameters include the BatchNormalization layer and batch size, which significantly influence the effectiveness of RISBFNN. The paper highlights the importance of a sufficiently large batch size (5000) and the incorporation of BatchNormalization after each layer to prevent training and validation loss stagnation.

Furthermore, the study emphasizes the impact of the number of training samples on performance, noting that overfitting occurs with insufficient samples, and the minimum sample requirement varies based on configuration. The authors generated a total of 800,000 samples for training, 200,000 for validation, and 10,000 for testing, with preprocessing standardization applied to each feature dimension prior to training.
