The paper investigates the robustness of image classifiers to perturbations such as Gaussian blurring and JPEG compression. Results indicate that classifiers trained with data augmentation, specifically with both blurring and JPEG compression, generally perform best across various datasets. Notably, classifiers trained without augmentation experience significant performance degradation when subjected to perturbations. 

The study also explores the impact of data diversity on classifier performance. It finds that increasing the number of classes in the training dataset enhances generalization, although improvements diminish after a certain point, suggesting a threshold for optimal diversity.

Furthermore, the paper compares the proposed method to existing forensic techniques, highlighting its effectiveness against artifacts generated by common CNN architectures. The findings demonstrate that well-implemented data augmentation and diversity in training data are crucial for improving robustness in real-world image processing scenarios.
