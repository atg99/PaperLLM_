The paper investigates the impact of data augmentation methods on the performance of detectors trained on ProGAN and tested on various generative adversarial networks (GANs). The findings indicate that training with augmentation generally enhances performance across different models, with notable exceptions observed in super-resolution (SAN) and DeepFake, where augmentation negatively affects performance.

The analysis reveals that the generalization ability of the models improves significantly when diverse datasets and aggressive augmentation techniques, such as simulated post-processing, are employed. For instance, the performance on BigGAN increased from 72.2 to 88.2 after applying augmentations, and substantial improvements were also noted for conditional models like CycleGAN and GauGAN.

However, the paper highlights that SAN and DeepFake models, which perform well without augmentation, experience a drop in performance when augmentation is applied. This is attributed to the nature of SAN, which relies on high-frequency components for distinguishing real from fake images, and the unique characteristics of DeepFake images, which do not originate solely from CNN architectures.

The study concludes that while augmentation typically enhances generalization, careful consideration is required for certain models where it may hinder performance. The optimal balance appears to be achieved with moderate augmentation, such as Blur+JPEG at a lower rate, which maintains strong detection capabilities across various datasets while preserving performance on challenging cases like DeepFake.
