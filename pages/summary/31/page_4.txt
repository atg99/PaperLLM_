The paper investigates the generalization capabilities of classifiers trained on images synthesized by various Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), particularly focusing on the ProGAN model. The study aims to determine whether classifiers can effectively identify CNN-generated images across different generators by training on a single model, which simulates real-world scenarios where the diversity of models is unknown during training.

The authors present a comprehensive table summarizing the average precision (AP) of different classifiers across 11 test generators, comparing their performance with baseline results from previous work. The table highlights the impact of varying training settings, including the number of classes and data augmentation techniques such as Gaussian blur and JPEG compression.

For the classification task, a "real-or-fake" classifier is trained using a large dataset composed of images generated by ProGAN and real images, totaling 720K training images and 4K validation images. The classifier utilizes a ResNet-50 architecture pre-trained on ImageNet, and various data augmentation strategies are employed to enhance model robustness.

Evaluation metrics include average precision, which provides a threshold-less performance measure, allowing for a nuanced understanding of classifier effectiveness across different datasets. The results indicate that classifiers trained on ProGAN images exhibit varying degrees of generalization to other CNN-generated images, with the study providing insights into the common features and artifacts present across diverse GAN models. This work contributes to the field of image forensics by exploring the challenges and potential of detecting synthesized images in a cross-generator context.
