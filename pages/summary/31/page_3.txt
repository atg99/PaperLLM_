The paper discusses the limitations of strided convolutions in reducing translation invariance in CNNs, referencing Zhang's work on improving this aspect by minimizing aliasing. It also highlights Bau et al.'s recent findings on the restricted generative capabilities of GANs, particularly their inability to produce certain image structures.

To investigate the transferability of classifiers trained to detect CNN-generated images, the authors compiled a dataset featuring images from various CNN-based generation models. The dataset includes 11 synthesis models that encompass a range of architectures, datasets, and loss functions, all utilizing an upsampling-convolutional structure, which is prevalent in generative CNNs.

The dataset comprises three state-of-the-art unconditional GANs: ProGAN, StyleGAN, and BigGAN, which are trained on LSUN and ImageNet datasets. Additionally, three conditional GANs are included: GauGAN, CycleGAN, and StarGAN. The paper also considers models optimized for perceptual loss, such as Cascaded Refinement Networks and the Implicit Maximum Likelihood Estimation model. Furthermore, it incorporates low-level vision models like Seeing In The Dark and the Second Order Attention Network.

The authors evaluate their model on face replacement images from the FaceForensics++ benchmark, referring to this specific method as DeepFake. This model employs an autoencoder for face generation and undergoes extensive post-processing, serving as an out-of-distribution test case.

The image generation process ensures that pre-processing operations align with each model's requirements, maintaining an equal number of real images from each training set. The dataset standardizes to a resolution of 256x256 pixels, with adjustments made for models producing images at different resolutions. The authors report that their model effectively detects fake images across these categories, despite variations in the training scheme.
