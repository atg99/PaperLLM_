The paper investigates the correlation between the confidence of various generative models, specifically BigGAN and StarGAN, and the visual quality of the images they produce. The findings suggest that images deemed more realistic by the models tend to exhibit higher visual quality, although this trend does not apply universally across all models. The authors present frequency analysis results, indicating that most synthetic images exhibit periodic patterns, while BigGAN and ProGAN show fewer artifacts.

The study also compares the generalization capabilities of their models against others, demonstrating significantly better performance with notable exceptions like CycleGAN and StarGAN, which achieved near-perfect accuracy. The authors express hope that their detection system will adapt to new deep synthesis models, illustrated by testing on the recently released StyleGAN2, which maintained a high accuracy rate of 99.1%. 

Lastly, the qualitative analysis explores the cues the classifier utilizes to assess "fakeness" and examines frequency responses of real versus synthetic images across different datasets, contributing to a deeper understanding of how these models operate. The results indicate that training on current generators can effectively generalize to future models, given shared underlying components.
