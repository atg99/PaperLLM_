The paper evaluates the effectiveness of a model designed to detect images generated by various Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), specifically ProGAN, StyleGAN, and StyleGAN2. The model demonstrates high accuracy in detecting StyleGAN2 images, with scores of 100 for ProGAN, 99.6 for StyleGAN, and 99.1 for StyleGAN2. It learns to identify both low-level features associated with CNN architectures and high-level features related to visual quality.

The study ranks synthesized images based on "fakeness" scores and visualizes examples across different percentiles. While a weak correlation between model predictions and visual quality is noted in some datasets, such as BigGAN and StarGAN, it is primarily suggested that the model captures low-level CNN artifacts rather than perceptual realism.

The paper further investigates CNN image synthesis artifacts through frequency spectrum analysis, revealing distinct patterns among images generated by different models. Notably, effective GANs like BigGAN and ProGAN exhibit fewer artifacts, while DeepFake images, which undergo extensive processing, show no obvious artifacts.

In the discussion, the authors express concern over the potential for future GANs to produce indistinguishable images from real ones, although current models retain detectable features. They highlight practical challenges in forensic detection, including the trade-off between true detection and false positives, the impact of aggressive transformations on social media, and the differing behaviors of shallow versus deep methods.

The authors conclude that addressing visual misinformation requires a multifaceted approach, integrating technical, social, and legal strategies.
