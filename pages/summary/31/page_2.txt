This paper addresses the effectiveness of image forensics approaches, particularly in detecting images generated by convolutional neural networks (CNNs). Contrary to previous findings that classifiers trained on one GAN architecture struggle to generalize to others, this study demonstrates that classifiers trained to detect CNN-generated images can generalize well across various datasets, architectures, and tasks. The authors utilize ProGAN to generate a large dataset of fake images and train a binary classifier using real images as negative examples.

A significant contribution of this work is the creation of the ForenSynths dataset, which includes synthesized images from multiple models, spanning unconditional generation methods like StyleGAN to super-resolution and deep-fake techniques. The study reveals that data augmentation through common image post-processing operations is crucial for the generalization of classifiers, even when the target images are unaltered. Additionally, the diversity of training images plays a vital role, with larger datasets yielding better performance up to a certain point. The authors also investigate the impact of post-processing on model generalization, showing that classifiers remain robust to common operations such as JPEG compression and resizing when appropriately trained.

The paper's main contributions are threefold: it highlights the generalization capabilities of forensics models across different CNN synthesis methods, introduces a new dataset and evaluation metric for detecting CNN-generated images, and experimentally analyzes the factors contributing to cross-model generalization.

In the related work section, the authors review previous research on detecting CNN-based manipulations, noting that while some studies have focused on specific manipulation techniques, they often did not explore generalization between models or datasets. The paper contrasts its empirical approach with other methods that aim to enhance transfer learning through new representation learning techniques. Overall, this research offers valuable insights into the robustness of image forensics models in the context of CNN-generated images.
