This paper investigates the feasibility of creating a universal detector for distinguishing real images from those generated by various convolutional neural networks (CNNs). The authors collected a dataset of fake images from 11 different CNN-based generators, including ProGAN, StyleGAN, and BigGAN, among others. They found that a classifier trained specifically on images from one generator (ProGAN) could generalize effectively to detect images produced by other architectures and datasets, including the newly released StyleGAN2.

The research highlights a potential commonality among CNN-generated images, suggesting they may share systematic flaws that hinder their realism. This finding is particularly relevant given the rising concerns about the implications of deep image synthesis in areas such as politics and misinformation. The authors emphasize that while detecting images from a specific generator is straightforward, developing a robust method that generalizes across various techniques poses significant challenges due to dataset bias and evolving generation methods.

Overall, the study provides insights into the current state of CNN-generated imagery and the underlying artifacts that may assist in its detection, indicating that, for now, distinguishing these images from real ones remains a feasible task.
