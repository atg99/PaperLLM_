The paper presents an ablation study on a large-scale dataset pre-trained with DAMO-YOLO-S, evaluating its performance on downstream tasks such as COCO and VisDrone. The results indicate an improvement in average precision (AP) when using additional datasets like Obj365 and OpenImage. The study emphasizes the importance of balancing distillation and task loss, noting that excessive focus on distillation can hinder classification convergence. It also highlights the benefit of reducing the head depth of the detector to enhance feature distillation.

The General Class DAMO-YOLO model is introduced, trained on multiple datasets, which addresses category overlap by creating a unified label space, thereby reducing the total categories from 945 to 701. A polynomial smoothing technique and class-aware sampling are employed to mitigate dataset size imbalances and long-tail effects. The training of DAMO-YOLO-S on these large-scale datasets resulted in a 1.1% improvement in mean Average Precision (mAP) on COCO and a mAP of 26.6% on the VisDrone dataset after fine-tuning.

The implementation details include training with a specific set of hyperparameters for 300 epochs and utilizing advanced data augmentation techniques. The performance of DAMO-YOLO models is compared to state-of-the-art models, demonstrating superior accuracy and speed. The lightweight models also show significant advantages in both speed and accuracy when evaluated on different hardware setups, confirming the effectiveness of the proposed methods and improvements in the model.
