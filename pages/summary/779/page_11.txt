The paper by Zebari et al. discusses various feature selection and extraction methods aimed at improving classification accuracy and reducing computational time in high-dimensional datasets. It highlights the effectiveness of clustering techniques, specifically K-means, which were employed in several studies to eliminate non-relevant features and group important ones. The authors noted that these methods significantly decreased computational time, achieving reductions of over 50% compared to traditional methods like ReliefF and Information Gain (IG).

The paper identifies that approximately half of the reviewed feature selection methods utilized optimization approaches, employing algorithms such as Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO), Genetic Algorithms (GA), and Ant Colony Optimization (ACO). These methods were shown to effectively streamline the feature selection process, often resulting in high dimensionality reduction and improved accuracy, with PSO achieving notable results in selecting relevant features.

Additionally, traditional methods like ReliefF and IG were integrated with other techniques, enhancing their effectiveness. The authors found that optimization-based methods generally outperformed traditional methods, with Support Vector Machines (SVM) and K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN) being the most frequently used classifiers, where SVM tended to yield the highest accuracy.

The paper also reviewed 21 feature extraction methods, noting the increasing reliance on deep learning techniques, particularly Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN), which demonstrated superior accuracy in various applications. Overall, the study emphasizes the critical role of clustering and optimization in reducing computational complexity while enhancing classification performance across diverse datasets.
