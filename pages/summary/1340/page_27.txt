The paper "Security Modeling of Autonomous Systems: A Survey" discusses the critical need for security in autonomous systems, highlighting various approaches and frameworks that address vulnerabilities and threats facing these technologies. It emphasizes the importance of understanding the complex interactions between autonomous agents and their environments, particularly in scenarios involving cyber threats and physical operations.

Key themes include the development of multi-layer hierarchical models for addressing jamming in mobile agent teams, the integration of autonomy indices for improved capability assessment, and the importance of cybersecurity in specific applications such as teleoperated surgical robots. The paper also explores the role of artificial intelligence in enhancing security measures for autonomous vehicles and the implications of automation in human-agent interactions.

Significant contributions from various studies are referenced, including the need for dynamic autonomy in urban search and rescue operations and the exploration of policy-based frameworks for adjustable autonomy. The survey identifies challenges in defining and measuring autonomy and security metrics, providing a comprehensive overview of existing literature while suggesting areas for future research.

Overall, the paper serves as a foundational resource for understanding the security landscape of autonomous systems, drawing attention to the necessity for robust security frameworks that can evolve alongside technological advancements in autonomy.
