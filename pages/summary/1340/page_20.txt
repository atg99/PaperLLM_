The paper discusses various vulnerabilities in autonomous systems, particularly focusing on autopilot systems in UAVs and mobile service robots. It highlights key threats identified by multiple researchers, including Gage, Cornelius, Clark, Lera, and Maggi, who categorize threats based on their nature, target, and impact. 

Gage outlines direct threats to sensors, actuators, communication networks, and processing elements. Cornelius identifies specific threat vectors such as attacks on sensor data and hardware. Clark elaborates on cyber threats at different levels, while Lera models cybersecurity threats and classifies them by origin and risk. Maggi discusses threat scenarios involving potential alterations to production outcomes and data breaches.

The paper also delves into vulnerability modeling, referencing Kim et al.'s identification of two primary vulnerabilities in UAV autopilot systems: control system security and application logic security. Control system vulnerabilities can be exploited through hardware or software attacks, while application logic vulnerabilities can be targeted via methods such as GPS spoofing. Behzadan emphasizes the vulnerabilities present in various layers of UAV communication networks, and Krishna and Murphy review recent cyber vulnerabilities based on attacks.

Additionally, the paper notes that modern vehicles contain numerous Electronic Control Units (ECUs) that regulate various functions, further highlighting the complexity and potential risks associated with these technologies.
