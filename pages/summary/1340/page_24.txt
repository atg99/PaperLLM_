This paper discusses the current state and challenges of cybersecurity and attack modeling in autonomous systems, particularly focusing on Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) and autonomous vehicles. The authors highlight that research in these areas is still in its infancy, with UAVs being the most active field. They note that while significant work has been done on vehicle-to-vehicle and vehicle-to-infrastructure networks, there is a pressing need for more in-depth modeling of threats and attack vectors specific to autonomous vehicles.

The paper identifies several key research challenges and future directions for ensuring the safety and security of autonomous systems. These include the need for algorithmic solutions to address uncertainties in modeling, the protection of autonomous systems from malicious attacks, and the establishment of cooperation and collaboration among systems to achieve higher goals. The dynamic nature of autonomy requires systems to adapt to unknown situations, necessitating a resilient design that performs reliably throughout its operational life.

The authors emphasize the importance of building human trust in autonomous systems, especially in light of incidents like the 2018 Uber self-driving car crash, which significantly decreased public confidence. They argue that extensive testing and robust security measures are essential before these systems can be integrated into society. Additionally, they highlight the necessity for diverse and comprehensive training datasets for machine learning applications, as simulated datasets often fail to represent real-world complexities.

Overall, the paper advocates for rigorous mathematical modeling and the development of an autonomy assessment tool that considers security factors, underscoring the vast potential for research and improvement in the cybersecurity of autonomous systems.
