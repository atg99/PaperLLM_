The paper provides a survey on security modeling for autonomous systems, focusing on various approaches to enhance collaboration between human operators and robotic systems. It discusses several methodologies, including:

1. **Mixed-Initiative/Teamwork-Centered Approach**: This method emphasizes seamless control exchange between humans and robots during missions, enhancing situational awareness and productivity. The NASA Astronaut-Rover project exemplifies this by allowing human-robot collaboration with adjustable levels of autonomy, addressing challenges like user trust and operational stability.

2. **Sliding Scale Approach**: This approach facilitates a continuous mode of autonomy where the system's autonomy increases as the user's control decreases. It allows for flexible user guidance and management of autonomous systems, incorporating trust scales to adjust levels of autonomy.

3. **Hierarchical Approach**: As the number of systems increases, coordination challenges arise. This method structures systems hierarchically to solve global problems based on lower-level system knowledge, allowing for localized task management and defining autonomy levels. Research has shown that effective automation in control and navigation can enable operators to manage multiple UAVs successfully.

The paper highlights the importance of these approaches in improving human-robot collaboration, ensuring that systems can operate efficiently and safely while adapting to varying levels of human intervention.
