The paper provides a survey on security modeling of autonomous systems, focusing on various frameworks that categorize levels of automation (LoA). It summarizes different LoA frameworks, including those by Sheridan and Verplank, Endsley and Kaber, Clough, and Huang et al., delineating ten levels ranging from low automation with remote control to fully autonomous systems that operate independently of human intervention.

Key concepts include the distinction between supervisory control and system-initiative approaches. In supervisory control, the human acts as a supervisor, dynamically modifying system behavior without fully taking control, which is crucial for complex missions like UAV operations. The system manages navigation while humans apply judgment and experience. Conversely, the system-initiative approach entails autonomous systems requesting human intervention when they encounter challenges, highlighting the importance of human responsiveness in maintaining efficiency.

The paper emphasizes the need for a balanced interaction between human operators and autonomous systems to optimize performance and ensure mission success, while also addressing security concerns inherent in these technologies.
