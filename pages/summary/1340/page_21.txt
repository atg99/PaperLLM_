The paper surveys the security modeling of autonomous systems, particularly focusing on vulnerabilities within autonomous vehicles and robots. It highlights that these systems, interconnected through in-vehicle networks, possess various vulnerabilities, including those related to sensors, control modules, and human behavioral aspects. A review by Parkinson et al. (2017) identified significant vulnerabilities across these domains, while Cerrudo and Apa (2017) reported nearly 50 cybersecurity vulnerabilities in commercial robots. Maggi et al. (2017) further noted weaknesses in industrial robots, such as lack of user authentication and insecure network protocols.

The paper emphasizes the importance of attack modeling to prepare organizations for potential cyberattacks. Various techniques, including attack graphs and the diamond model, are utilized to analyze these threats. Guo et al. (2017) introduced a theoretical model for mobile robots that allows for actuator and sensor attacks. Petit and Shladover (2015) discussed potential cyberattacks on automated vehicles, identifying attack surfaces and executing real-world tests on sensor vulnerabilities.

The taxonomy of attacks on autonomous vehicles has been explored, categorizing them by attacker type, attack vector, and potential consequences. The paper also acknowledges the integration of robots in various sectors, from healthcare to manufacturing, highlighting the risks posed by cyberattacks on these systems. Studies, such as Bonaci et al. (2015), have categorized potential attacks on teleoperated surgical robots, while Maggi et al. (2017) provided a profile of industrial robot attackers based on their capabilities and access levels.

Lastly, Guo et al. (2017) presented an analytical model illustrating how sensor attacks can lead to erroneous sensor readings, further emphasizing the need for robust cybersecurity measures in autonomous systems.
