The paper provides a comprehensive survey on security modeling for autonomous systems, with a focus on the vulnerabilities these systems face from cyberattacks. It outlines various attack types and their effects on autonomous systems, particularly Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs). Key attack types include:

1. **Jamming**: Intentional interference, such as GPS jamming, leads to the loss or corruption of communication packets.
2. **Spoofing**: Masquerading as a legitimate source allows attackers to gain unauthorized access to systems and information.
3. **Flooding**: Overloading the host with packets, as seen in DoS and DDoS attacks, results in network congestion and communication loss.
4. **Side-channel Attacks**: Exploiting physical analysis to leak sensitive information without targeting flaws in the system components.
5. **Stealthy Deception Attacks**: Tampering with system components or data can mislead the system into taking undesirable actions.
6. **Sensor Input Spoofing**: Manipulating environmental inputs creates an implicit control channel, allowing direct control over system actions.

The paper highlights the increasing use of UAVs across industries, such as for door-to-door delivery and cargo transport, and discusses the growing risk of these systems being targeted by cyberattacks. Early research identified vulnerabilities in UAV autopilot systems and focused on developing safety assessment algorithms to enhance system performance against stealthy deception attacks. The prevalence of GPS jamming and spoofing as common threats to UAV navigation is emphasized, referencing significant works in the field.
