The paper provides a comprehensive survey on security modeling of autonomous systems, particularly focusing on robots. It begins by referencing early work by Gage in 1985, which introduced a generic model for autonomous robots concerning security. More recent contributions, such as those by Maggi et al. in 2017, describe an architecture for industrial robots that emphasizes communication between the programmer and the robot controller for effective security and threat modeling.

The analytical model section highlights the unique dynamics of various autonomous systems, noting that generalized studies may be limited to standard components. Goppert et al. (2012) modeled UAV dynamics using simulation tools to assess vulnerabilities to cyberattacks, while other works described UAVs as linear time-invariant systems subject to noise. Game theory has also been employed to analyze UAV kinematics.

Threat modeling is defined as the process of identifying potential threats and establishing countermeasures. This approach aids in visualizing system vulnerabilities from an adversarial perspective and evaluating security risks. The paper discusses modeling strategies like STRIDE, which categorizes threats, and Persona Non-Grata, which focuses on attacker motivations. The CIA model—confidentiality, integrity, and availability—serves as a framework for identifying attacks across system components.

The paper notes advancements in technology and the corresponding evolution of attack strategies. It references works that analyze UAV autopilot vulnerabilities and the development of threat models for smart device ground control stations used in military contexts. These models address attack motivations, system vulnerabilities, and mitigation strategies. Additionally, it mentions automated threat modeling tools like Microsoft Threat Modeling Tool and Threat-Modeler.

Overall, the study emphasizes the growing importance of cybersecurity in robotics, with preliminary efforts identifying various threats to robotic systems.
