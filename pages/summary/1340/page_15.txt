The paper surveys the security modeling of autonomous systems, focusing on the advancements in driverless cars and robotic technologies. By the end of 2018, companies like Waymo and Cruise had significantly tested their autonomous vehicles, while others such as Drive.ai began offering services in geo-fenced areas. The integration of advanced driver-assistance features in new cars is highlighted, alongside the necessity for these vehicles to connect through vehicular networks like VANET, which facilitates the exchange of critical traffic information. However, this connectivity also increases vulnerability to cyberattacks, as illustrated by a 2015 hacking incident involving a Jeep Cherokee where the hacker gained control of the vehicle.

The paper emphasizes the importance of secure communication within VANET to protect confidential data and mitigate risks of eavesdropping and ransomware attacks. Various researchers have explored vulnerabilities in VANETs, advocating for robust authentication methods as a primary defense. Individual attack detection methods, such as denial of service and jamming, have been researched to enhance security.

In the robotics sector, significant investments and technological advancements are driving the evolution of robots across various environments, from manufacturing to emergency response. The paper underscores the need for high precision in surgical robots and efficiency in rescue robots, reflecting the diverse applications and critical role of robotics in modern society. Overall, the paper highlights the necessity for comprehensive security measures in the development and deployment of autonomous systems.
