The paper by F. Jahan et al. focuses on the cybersecurity of autonomous systems, highlighting key concepts and methodologies relevant to this emerging field. The authors categorize their research using primary and secondary keywords, which include terms related to autonomy, artificial intelligence, human-machine teaming, and security threats. They refined their literature review to identify relevant articles, ultimately selecting 88 that address the cybersecurity of autonomous systems and 26 that focus specifically on security modeling.

One significant limitation noted in the study is the scarcity of literature addressing the security of both individual and generalized autonomous systems, as cybersecurity is a relatively new area within this domain. The paper emphasizes the need for more research, particularly on the security of unmanned aerial vehicles, robots, and driverless cars, which are highlighted due to their prominence. Additionally, the authors mention the concept of Autonomous Internet of Things as a new area to explore.

The historical evolution section outlines how the concepts of autonomy have developed over the decades, tracing their roots back to mythology, literature, and early technological inventions. The paper suggests that advancements in robotics, sensors, and communication technologies have significantly influenced the current definitions and levels of autonomy. Overall, the authors believe they have identified the most relevant works in the field, despite the existing limitations in literature.
