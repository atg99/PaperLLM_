The paper discusses the vulnerabilities of cyber-physical systems, particularly focusing on attacks targeting control commands and sensors in unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs). Kwon et al. (2013) explored stealthy deception attacks that can result in unbounded estimation errors without detection, using a steady-state Kalman Filter. They later expanded their research to include direct control acquisition and navigation attacks, proposing a real-time safety assessment algorithm based on reachability analysis (Kwon et al. 2016). Su et al. (2016) examined GPS spoofing attacks, detailing how falsified data could mislead navigation systems and proposing a real-time manipulation method for UAVs under such attacks.

The paper also highlights the application of decision-making theories in modeling attack strategies as games, with examples including coordination among UAVs during jamming attacks (Bhattacharya and Basar 2010) and a zero-sum network interdiction game regarding delivery drones (Sanjab et al. 2017).

In the discussion section, the authors emphasize the rapid advancement in technology towards autonomous systems, noting the increase in attack rates exploiting vulnerabilities. They stress the importance of addressing security challenges during the development of autonomous systems. The historical evolution of autonomy is presented, providing insight into various approaches such as supervisory control, goal-driven autonomy, and mixed-initiative systems. The paper underscores the challenges of control transfer between drivers and autonomous systems, especially in critical situations like adverse weather or driver impairment, advocating for further research in this area.
