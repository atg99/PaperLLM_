The paper provides a comprehensive survey on the security modeling of autonomous systems, tracing the historical evolution of robotics and automation. It highlights the origins of the terms "robotics" and "automation," linking them to early developments in the field, including remote-controlled vehicles and industrial robots in the 1950s. Key contributions, such as the 10-level scale of autonomy (LoA) introduced by Sheridan and Verplank in 1978, are discussed as foundational for understanding autonomy in systems.

The paper addresses the challenges of human operators in supervisory roles, particularly issues related to feedback, communication, and situational awareness that arose with increased autonomy. It emphasizes the need for adaptive automation as a solution to these problems, advocating for designs that incorporate situational awareness and allow for dynamic task allocation between operators and systems.

Research by Endsley and others on improving situational awareness and adaptive autonomy is highlighted, including the evolution of adaptive systems that adjust LoA based on environmental factors and operator performance. The paper also discusses the Department of Defense's use of UAVs for various missions, underscoring the ongoing advancements in technology and the importance of reliability and security in autonomous systems.

Overall, the survey presents a historical context and outlines key research directions and challenges in the field of autonomous system security, advocating for the integration of adaptive automation and enhanced situational awareness to improve operator performance and system reliability.
