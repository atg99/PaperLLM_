The paper discusses the evolving landscape of security modeling for autonomous systems, particularly within the context of the Internet of Things (IoT) and Artificial Intelligence (AI). As technologies such as big data, machine learning, and Blockchain advance, IoT devices are anticipated to gain autonomy comparable to human actors. Future IoT infrastructures need to support diverse platforms and environments, emphasizing scalability through decentralized management and adaptability to environmental changes. Ensuring data confidentiality and preventing personal information breaches are critical, allowing users to retain control over their data.

The paper also highlights swarm robotics, an area inspired by natural systems where groups of robots collaborate to perform complex tasks. Applications include disaster response, industrial inspections, agricultural mapping, and military operations. Swarm robotics is characterized by its redundancy and self-organization, enabling faster task completion through parallel operations. Current research primarily focuses on modeling methods and algorithms related to swarm behaviors such as flocking, foraging, navigation, and search tasks. Overall, the paper underscores the necessity for enhanced cybersecurity and privacy measures in the design of autonomous networks, as the threat landscape includes various cyberattack vulnerabilities applicable to IoT and AI systems.
