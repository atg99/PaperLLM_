The paper discusses the integration of autonomous systems into everyday life, highlighting their potential roles as home assistants, companions, and in high-stakes environments such as healthcare and space exploration. Despite the promising applications, the authors caution against the risks associated with improper implementation or malicious use, citing incidents involving autonomous robots and self-driving vehicles that have resulted in harm. The growing fear of autonomous weapons, fueled by media representations, raises concerns about the ethical implications and safety of fully autonomous systems. 

The primary objective of the study is to explore the cybersecurity aspects of various autonomous systems, an area that remains under-researched in the existing literature. The authors aim to provide a comprehensive understanding of autonomy, its historical evolution, and the factors influencing its levels, from manual to fully autonomous systems. Key contributions include an examination of current trends, cybersecurity vulnerabilities, and threat modeling relevant to autonomous systems.

The paper is structured into several sections: it begins with a historical overview of autonomy, followed by a discussion on cybersecurity issues, system modeling, and threat analysis. It concludes with an exploration of research challenges and future directions in the field. Overall, the work aims to advance the understanding of security modeling for autonomous systems, essential for their safe integration into society.
