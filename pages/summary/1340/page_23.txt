The paper titled "Security Modeling of Autonomous Systems: A Survey" provides a comprehensive overview of various studies related to the modeling of systems, vulnerabilities, threats, and attacks in autonomous systems, particularly focusing on UAVs, autonomous vehicles, and robots.

The survey highlights key works in the field, detailing approaches to modeling and analyzing security aspects. For instance, Vachtsevanos and Reimann introduced a hierarchical control model for multiple UAVs utilizing game theory for collision avoidance. Bhattacharya and Basar presented a kinematic model of UAVs framed as a zero-sum game, while Kim et al. discussed the vulnerabilities of UAV autopilot systems. The paper includes a detailed examination of threat assessments in UAV communication networks, dynamics of UAV systems, and the impact of cyber threats such as GPS spoofing.

In the context of autonomous vehicles, the work of Urmson et al. is noted for its design based on the "Sense-Plan-Act" paradigm, while Petit and Shladover explored potential cyberattacks. The discussion extends to robotic systems, where various attack models and vulnerabilities are identified, including those in teleoperated surgical robots and industrial robots.

The paper emphasizes the importance of understanding the interplay between system architecture, potential vulnerabilities, and attack strategies to enhance the security of autonomous systems. The findings underscore the need for robust cybersecurity measures as these systems become increasingly integrated into various sectors. Overall, the survey serves as a foundational resource for researchers and practitioners aiming to address security challenges in autonomous systems.
