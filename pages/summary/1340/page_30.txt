This paper references a range of studies and articles focused on the autonomy and cybersecurity of unmanned systems, including aerial vehicles and autonomous cars. Key contributions include the Autonomy Levels for Unmanned Systems (ALFUS) framework, which provides a structured approach to assessing autonomy levels in various systems. The research highlights advancements in GPS technology and its vulnerabilities, particularly in the context of UAVs, showcasing the potential for GPS spoofing and the need for robust detection algorithms.

Several studies emphasize the importance of cybersecurity in unmanned vehicles, discussing vulnerabilities and proposing security models to protect against cyberattacks. The paper also explores the integration of blockchain technology for enhancing the autonomy of IoT devices.

The implications of autonomous vehicles in urban settings are examined, underscoring the need for effective perception systems. Overall, the references collectively contribute to the understanding of the technological, security, and operational challenges faced by autonomous systems and the ongoing efforts to address these issues.
