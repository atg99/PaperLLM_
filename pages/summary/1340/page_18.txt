The paper by F. Jahan et al. highlights the security challenges in swarm robotics, focusing on potential threats to these systems and comparing their characteristics with other systems like multi-robot and mobile sensor networks. The authors emphasize the need for a comprehensive understanding of autonomous systems, particularly regarding their response to alarms, control transfers, and attack scenarios. 

They discuss the importance of system modeling to identify vulnerabilities and threats, which can inform the development of attack models. Various theoretical and analytical models are explored, particularly concerning unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs), robots, and autonomous vehicles, which are increasingly relevant in real-world applications.

In the section on system modeling, the authors present an abstract view of autonomous systems, detailing their functions and interactions. For UAVs, they reference works that describe communication networks and the architecture of guidance, navigation, and control systems. Notably, a hierarchical model for coordinating multiple UAVs is discussed, utilizing differential game theory for collision avoidance.

For autonomous vehicles, the paper references the DARPA Urban Challenge, which spurred advancements in vehicle autonomy. The architecture of an autonomous vehicle capable of navigating in GPS-denied environments is outlined, along with a model addressing sensor and actuator attacks. Overall, the paper underscores the ongoing research and the necessity of addressing security in autonomous systems.
