The paper "Security Modeling of Autonomous Systems: A Survey" addresses the challenges in developing fully driverless autonomous systems, emphasizing the need for effective automated path and motion planning. It highlights the importance of human action models, user state monitoring, and intent recognition techniques to ensure collaboration between users and systems. Advancements in AI and human-machine interaction (HMT) fields, particularly in facial and emotion recognition, are crucial for enhancing user monitoring and communication through interfaces like augmented reality and voice commands.

The paper also discusses the necessity for autonomous systems to make timely and informed decisions in complex environments, which is facilitated by the integration of multiple sensors for accurate perception. Data fusion techniques are essential for obstacle detection and mapping. It underscores the importance of benchmarking and metric design in evaluating the autonomy levels of these systems, drawing from previous works that aimed to establish common metrics across various categories such as navigation, perception, and social interaction.

Cybersecurity is a significant concern for autonomous systems, which are categorized as cyber-physical systems that combine embedded computing with physical components. The paper notes the vulnerability of these systems to cyberattacks, particularly in critical applications like nuclear power and aviation, where failures can have severe consequences. It references existing research on threats and vulnerabilities in cyber-physical systems, providing a taxonomy of common attacks on autonomous systems and discussing their implications for cybersecurity. The paper concludes by acknowledging the need for further exploration of cyber threats in the context of autonomous systems.
