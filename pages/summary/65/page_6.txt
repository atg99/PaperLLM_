This paper presents advancements in the training of Recurrent Neural Network Transducer (RNN-T) models for end-to-end automatic speech recognition (ASR). The authors focus on two main improvements: reducing memory consumption during training and enhancing the model architecture.

The study compares hybrid models and RNN-T models on various test sets, including Cortana, Conversation, and DMA. The results indicate that RNN-T models utilizing an ecltGRU encoder outperform baseline models with LSTM units, achieving relative Word Error Rate (WER) reductions of 6.6%, 7.5%, and 11.8% across the test sets, while maintaining a smaller model size of 216 MB. In contrast, hybrid models with similar sizes yielded worse WERs, demonstrating the effectiveness of the RNN-T model for device deployment.

The paper also highlights the latency differences between the two model types, revealing that the ecltGRU model exhibits a reduced alignment discrepancy compared to the baseline LSTM model, albeit at a higher average latency. The authors attribute the performance gains to the use of future context lookahead in the ecltGRU encoder, which facilitates earlier decision-making in the decoding process.

In conclusion, the proposed RNN-T model achieves significant improvements in recognition accuracy while minimizing model size, making it particularly suitable for deployment in resource-constrained environments. The results underscore the potential of innovative architectures in enhancing ASR technology.
