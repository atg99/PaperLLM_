This paper presents a detailed evaluation of various RNN-T (Recurrent Neural Network Transducer) models on multiple test sets, including Cortana, Conversation, and DMA, which contain both close-talk and far-field recordings. The models are assessed based on their size and word error rates (WERs), with all encoder networks comprising six hidden layers and prediction networks consisting of two hidden layers. The study employs greedy search for decoding results.

Key findings include the performance metrics of different RNN-T architectures. The base model, which uses LSTM for both encoder and prediction networks, demonstrates a size of 255 megabytes and yields specific WERs across the test sets. Notably, using ltLSTM in the encoder significantly reduces WERs while increasing the model size to 422 Mb. However, further application of ltLSTM to the prediction network does not produce substantial WER improvements.

The introduction of cltLSTM with future context frames results in notable WER reductions of up to 17.5% on the Cortana test set, although the model size rises to 469 Mb, presenting deployment challenges. Conversely, switching to GRU networks reduces the model size significantly to 139 Mb, albeit with slightly worse WERs. The ltGRU variant improves performance while maintaining a manageable size.

The ecltGRU model, which also utilizes future context, achieves competitive WER reductions while remaining smaller than the baseline model. The analysis indicates that larger values of future context frames consistently lower WERs, although increasing the context size may also impact model latency.

In addition to RNN-T models, the paper includes a comparison with hybrid models trained with a cross-entropy criterion. It emphasizes that both model types can benefit from sequence discriminative training, highlighting the potential for further performance enhancement through advanced training techniques. Overall, the results underscore the effectiveness of future context in improving model performance while balancing the trade-offs in model size and deployment feasibility.
