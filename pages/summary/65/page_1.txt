The paper "Improving RNN Transducer Modeling for End-to-End Speech Recognition" by Jinyu Li et al. discusses advancements in end-to-end (E2E) automatic speech recognition (ASR) systems, focusing on the RNN Transducer (RNN-T) model. The authors highlight the advantages of RNN-T over other methods like Connectionist Temporal Classification (CTC) and Attention Encoder-Decoder (AED), particularly its capability for online streaming and its lack of the frame-independence assumption inherent in CTC.

The study aims to enhance RNN-T training in two main ways: optimizing the training algorithm to reduce memory consumption and proposing improved model structures. By lowering memory requirements, larger training minibatches can be utilized, resulting in faster training speeds. The authors introduce model structures that yield better accuracy while maintaining a smaller model footprint.

The best RNN-T model developed, trained on 30,000 hours of anonymized and transcribed Microsoft production data, achieves an 11.8% relative word error rate (WER) reduction compared to the baseline model. This model, at 216 Megabytes, outperforms a device hybrid model of similar size by a 15.0% relative WER reduction and matches the performance of a much larger server hybrid model.

The paper is organized into sections detailing the RNN-T method, memory consumption optimization strategies, proposed model structures, evaluation results, and conclusions. Overall, the work contributes to the ongoing development of efficient and effective E2E ASR systems, demonstrating the potential of RNN-T modeling in practical applications.
