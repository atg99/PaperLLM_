This paper discusses methods to enhance the efficiency and accuracy of training Recurrent Neural Network Transducer (RNN-T) models. The authors propose two main strategies for memory optimization: sorting training sequences and merging functions within the model architecture. 

Firstly, sorting sequences by acoustic features and label lengths reduces memory waste during training. However, the authors found that randomizing utterances during training yields better accuracy compared to sorted sequences. They reference the Deep Speech 2 project, which employed a method called SortaGrad that initially used sorted data for better model initialization before reverting to randomized data, highlighting the importance of data shuffling in training.

Secondly, the paper addresses the inefficiencies arising from modular design in neural network training frameworks like PyTorch. The authors explain that traditional methods require storing multiple large tensors when calculating gradients, leading to significant memory usage. They propose a more efficient approach by merging softmax and loss computation, allowing the gradient calculations to utilize existing memory, thus reducing memory overhead and enabling larger mini-batch sizes during training. This optimization allows for substantial increases in mini-batch sizes, particularly for RNN-T models with larger output labels.

The paper also explores various model structures, starting with a baseline model that utilizes layer-normalized Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks. The authors introduce the Layer Trajectory LSTM (ltLSTM), which separates temporal modeling and senone classification tasks, thereby improving accuracy by allowing each LSTM type to focus on its specific task. The paper details the formulation of these LSTMs, emphasizing the roles of different components such as input, output, and forget gates.

In summary, the authors provide insights into optimizing RNN-T training through memory-efficient techniques and innovative model architectures that enhance performance while managing computational resources effectively.
