The referenced papers collectively explore advancements in end-to-end speech recognition using neural network architectures. Key contributions include the development of neural transducers, recurrent neural aligners, and attention-based models, which enhance the mapping of acoustic signals to text. 

Battenberg et al. (2017) investigate neural transducers for end-to-end speech recognition, while Sak et al. (2017) propose a recurrent neural aligner for sequence mapping. Hadian et al. (2018) focus on discriminatively trained HMM-based models, and Chiu et al. (2018) present state-of-the-art results using sequence-to-sequence models.

Sainath et al. (2018) improve online neural transducer performance, and Li et al. (2018) advance acoustic-to-word CTC models. Notably, Graves et al. (2006) introduce Connectionist Temporal Classification, which is foundational for labeling unsegmented sequences.

Bahdanau et al. (2015) and Chorowski et al. (2015) contribute to attention mechanisms in speech recognition, enhancing model performance. Other significant studies focus on LSTM architectures and their efficiency in recognizing distant speech, as seen in works by Zhao et al. (2016) and Pundak & Sainath (2017).

Overall, these studies highlight a trend towards integrating various neural network strategies, including attention mechanisms and recurrent architectures, to improve the accuracy and efficiency of speech recognition systems.
