The paper presents evaluation results of various neural network models, focusing on the Fast Learning Network (FLN) and several extreme learning machines (GA-ELM, Bat-ELM, Bee-ELM) with varying hidden neuron counts. The FLN results indicate that the model's performance fluctuates with hidden neuron numbers, achieving a maximum accuracy of 92.50% at 725 neurons. In comparison, the GA-ELM demonstrates superior performance across all metrics, with a peak accuracy of 97.50% at 725 and 775 neurons. 

The findings highlight that the GABONST-ELM model outperforms all tested models, including basic ELM and FLN, achieving an accuracy of 99.38%. This success is attributed to effective bias and weight generation, which minimizes classification errors and prevents the model from getting trapped in local maxima. Overall, the results emphasize the importance of optimizing hidden neuron configurations and the advantages of the GABONST-ELM in classification tasks.
