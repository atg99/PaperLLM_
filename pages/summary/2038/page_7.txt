GABONST algorithm. The algorithm operates through iterative processes to evolve a population of potential solutions, termed chromosomes. The process begins by initializing a population of random real values and evaluating their fitness using a defined function. The algorithm runs through a set number of iterations or until convergence is achieved.

In each iteration, the algorithm compares the fitness of each chromosome against the mean fitness of the population. If a chromosome's fitness is below the mean, it undergoes mutation to create a new generation. If it exceeds the mean, a crossover operation with a randomly selected chromosome from the top five is performed. The new offspring is then evaluated; if it meets the fitness criteria, it is retained; otherwise, mutation is applied again. If this also fails, the chromosome is discarded in favor of a new random one.

The effectiveness of GABONST is evaluated through experimental tests against other algorithms like EATLBO, conventional GA, Bat, and Bee algorithms. The tests involved fifteen different objective functions and were conducted over 100 iterations with a population size of 50. This comprehensive evaluation aims to benchmark GABONST's optimization performance.
