The paper discusses a genetic algorithm process for selecting chromosomes to survive into the next generation based on their fitness in the current environment. The first method of selection involves a crossover operation between a weak chromosome (s i) and a well-qualified chromosome (RS). If the resulting new chromosome (s i,new)C meets the survival criteria (g(s i,new)C less than or equal to the mean), it advances to the next generation. If not, a second chance is provided through genetic mutation, where a mutation operation is applied to the weak chromosome. If the mutated chromosome (s i,new)M qualifies for survival, it also moves to the next generation. If both attempts fail, the weak chromosome is discarded and replaced with a randomly generated chromosome. The paper includes a diagram illustrating the arithmetic crossover and uniform mutation operations used in the process.
