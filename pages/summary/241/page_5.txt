The paper discusses the proposed Dense Shortcut Network (DSNet) which incorporates a dense (weighted normalized) shortcut mechanism. The authors compare this approach with the traditional ResNet50 architecture, revealing that simply adding a dense identity shortcut does not enhance performance. The motivation behind using normalization is to standardize preceding features to prevent any single feature from dominating the output, while weighted summation allows the network to assign significance to each feature dynamically.

An extensive ablation study is conducted using the ResNet50 backbone on CIFAR100, highlighting two main observations: normalization techniques significantly improve performance, with group normalization outperforming batch normalization, and the importance of learnable channel-wise weights for performance enhancement. The study finds that the proposed DS2Net architecture yields the best results, outperforming both DSNet and the original ResNet.

The experiments indicate that DSNet and DS2Net achieve lower classification errors compared to ResNet across various configurations in both CIFAR-100 and CIFAR-10 datasets. The authors conclude that the integration of weighted normalized shortcuts leads to notable performance improvements over traditional architectures, validating their proposed design approach.
