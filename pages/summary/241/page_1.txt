The paper "ResNet or DenseNet? Introducing Dense Shortcuts to ResNet" by Chaoning Zhang et al. addresses the trade-offs between two popular deep learning architectures: ResNet and DenseNet. While both networks exhibit strong performance in various computer vision tasks, they each have inherent limitations. ResNet employs an identity shortcut that stabilizes training but may restrict its representation capacity. Conversely, DenseNet enhances feature reuse through dense concatenation, which improves efficiency but leads to higher GPU memory requirements and longer training times due to its reliance on small convolutions.

To reconcile these issues, the authors introduce a novel approach called dense weighted normalized shortcuts. This method merges the strengths of both ResNet's simplicity and DenseNet's feature utilization while addressing their drawbacks. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed DSNet outperforms ResNet significantly and achieves performance comparable to DenseNet, all while using fewer computational resources. The paper ultimately provides a unified perspective on dense summation, facilitating a deeper understanding of the differences between ResNet and DenseNet and offering a practical solution for their integration.
