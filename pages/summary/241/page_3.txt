The paper discusses advancements in deep neural networks, specifically focusing on architectural differences and innovations among ResNet, DenseNet, and their variants. ResNet introduced the concept of identity shortcuts, while DenseNet utilized concatenation techniques instead. CondenseNet further developed the idea of dense connections by integrating layers with varying resolution feature maps. The Dual Path Network and Mixed Link Network combined elements of both ResNet and DenseNet. However, the proposed approach in this paper distinguishes itself by neither employing identity shortcuts nor dense concatenation.

The authors clarify the differences between ResNet and DenseNet, noting that while ResNet connects to only the preceding feature map, DenseNet connects to all preceding convolutional block outputs. They demonstrate that the dense concatenation used in DenseNet can be reformulated to show equivalence with dense summation used in ResNet, suggesting a unified perspective on dense connections in deep learning architectures. This insight reinforces the idea that both architectures, despite their different methodologies, share a fundamental principle of leveraging information from previous layers to enhance performance.
