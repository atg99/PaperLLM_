The paper introduces a novel network structure called Dense Summation Network (DSNet), which incorporates a dense normalized shortcut to enhance performance without the drawbacks of traditional identity shortcuts or dense concatenation used in ResNet and DenseNet. This approach allows DSNet to significantly outperform ResNet with minimal parameter overhead while matching or slightly exceeding DenseNet's performance with reduced computational requirements.

The authors provide a unified perspective on dense summation that clarifies the fundamental differences between ResNet and DenseNet. They argue that existing techniques, such as FractalNet, which also avoids identity shortcuts, show inferior performance, particularly in very deep networks. The proposed dense weighted normalized shortcuts aim to address these limitations effectively.

The paper also reviews related work in deep convolutional network (CNN) design, categorizing contributions into micro-module and macro-architecture designs. Micro-modules, particularly normalization techniques like batch normalization, are highlighted for their widespread use in enhancing performance and convergence speed. However, issues such as adversarial vulnerability are noted. Alternative normalization methods, including weight normalization and group normalization, are discussed for their potential benefits, especially in memory-intensive applications.

On the macro-architecture side, the paper mentions prominent networks like ResNet and DenseNet, emphasizing their popularity due to their competitive performance and straightforward designs. While ResNet's identity shortcut stabilizes training, its effectiveness decreases in extremely deep networks, a challenge addressed by preactivation techniques. Overall, this research contributes to the understanding and development of more effective deep learning architectures.
