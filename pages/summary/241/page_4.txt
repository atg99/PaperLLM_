The paper discusses the equivalence of dense concatenation in DenseNet and dense summation in ResNet, highlighting their similarities and core differences. Both architectures utilize dense summation of convolution weights, but the key distinction lies in the shared convolution weights in ResNet versus the unique weights for each preceding layer output in DenseNet. This difference results in DenseNet generally requiring more training time and GPU memory due to the growing input channels with more layers, despite its flexibility in utilizing previous feature maps.

The authors propose a new dense connection method, termed DSNet, to mitigate the drawbacks of both architectures. This method maintains the flexibility of DenseNet while employing shared convolution weights akin to ResNet. The proposed DSNet uses a dense weighted normalized shortcut, which combines normalization and channel-wise weighting, enhancing performance. The paper presents two variants of the dense shortcut, with the second variant suggesting that the aggregated output contains more useful features than the single convolutional output. Experimental results support this conjecture, leading to the adoption of the second variant in their study.

The work emphasizes that DSNet retains the ResNet architecture while replacing the identity shortcut with the dense shortcut, aiming to improve performance without redesigning the backbone structure. The authors also provide a naive approach to introduce dense shortcuts, illustrating the potential recursive connections among feature maps.
