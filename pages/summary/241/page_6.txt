The paper evaluates the robustness of the proposed DSNet architecture on the CIFAR-100 dataset by conducting experiments that analyze the effects of varying network depths and widths. The results, presented in Tables 3 and 4, demonstrate that DSNet consistently outperforms ResNet across different configurations. For depth exploration, DSNet shows lower classification errors at various depths when compared to ResNet, especially at 50 layers, where DSNet achieves a significant improvement. 

The width exploration also indicates that increasing width is more effective than increasing depth for performance enhancement. The DSNet architecture is further tested against the ResNeXt variant, yielding similar performance trends, with DS2NeXt outperforming ResNeXt despite having significantly fewer parameters and computational requirements. 

Comparative analysis with previous works, shown in Table 6, highlights that networks employing dense connections, including DSNet, perform better than those without. DSNet not only surpasses traditional models like ResNet and DenseNet but also demonstrates that dense weight normalized shortcuts are effective as a dense connection technique.

The paper also includes experiments on the ImageNet dataset, where DS2Net50 achieves competitive classification errors, affirming its effectiveness in practical applications. Overall, the findings suggest that the proposed DSNet architecture offers a robust alternative to existing models, particularly in terms of efficiency and performance.
