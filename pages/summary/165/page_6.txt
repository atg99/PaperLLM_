The paper discusses the application of conditional Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) in the field of ophthalmology, specifically focusing on the challenges presented by the variability in vessel characteristics such as width, color, tortuosity, and branching. Following the initial implementation of conditional GANs, various architectural modifications have been proposed to enhance their performance. Notably, Son et al. improved the conditional GAN by incorporating a U-Net based generator, akin to the Pix2pix architecture, which has shown promise in segmentation tasks. Additional enhancements have been made through methods such as patch-based GANs, multi-kernel pooling layers, and topological structure-constrained models.

The paper also provides examples of GAN applications in ophthalmological imaging, including post-intervention predictions, denoising of fundus photography, super-resolution imaging, domain transfer, data augmentation, and segmentation of corneal sub-basal nerves. These advancements largely utilize publicly available datasets, with some studies incorporating proprietary datasets. The review categorizes the studies based on their objectives and the specific imaging domains within ophthalmology, indicating that some studies address multiple issues, leading to a double-counting in the analysis.
