This section of the paper discusses advancements in using Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) for various ophthalmology imaging tasks, particularly in enhancing data augmentation and improving diagnostic accuracy. Yoo et al. introduced a postoperative appearance prediction model for orbital decompression surgery utilizing a conditional GAN, highlighting its potential as a decision support tool in oculoplastic and cosmetic surgeries. Other studies explored the application of conditional GANs to predict optical coherence tomography (OCT) images following anti-vascular endothelial growth factor (anti-VEGF) injections, showcasing models like Pix2pix and multi-channel GANs that effectively generated synthetic post-injection OCT images.

The paper summarizes several studies employing different GAN techniques across various ophthalmological imaging domains, as illustrated in Table 3. Notable findings include:

- Diaz-Pinto et al. demonstrated that DCGAN could generate high-quality synthetic optic disc images.
- Burlina et al. used PGGAN to synthesize realistic fundus images for deep learning applications.
- Zheng et al. found that synthetic OCT images generated were comparable in quality to real images and useful for training datasets.
- Zhou et al. and Wang et al. focused on generating balanced training data for diabetic retinopathy and utilizing both labeled and unlabeled data, respectively.
- Yoo et al. showed that CycleGAN could enhance diagnostic accuracy for rare retinal diseases.
- Abdelmotaal et al. highlighted that synthesized corneal topography images improved classification performance for keratoconus detection.

Overall, the studies emphasize the effectiveness of GANs in generating synthetic medical images to augment training data, thus enhancing the performance of deep learning models in ophthalmology.
