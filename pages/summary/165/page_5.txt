This paper discusses the application of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) in medical imaging, particularly in ophthalmology. It highlights the advancements in synthetic image generation through various GAN architectures, especially conditional GANs, which utilize additional conditioning variables for improved performance. The paper outlines the functionality of different GAN types, such as Pix2pix for image-to-image translation and CycleGAN for unpaired image generation, emphasizing their ability to learn characteristics of image domains without requiring paired datasets.

In ophthalmology, the paper surveys the literature on GAN applications across various tasks, including segmentation, data augmentation, denoising, domain transfer, super-resolution, post-intervention prediction, and feature extraction. It notes that segmentation is the most prevalent task, focusing on identifying structures such as retinal vessels and layers, which is crucial for accurate diagnosis.

The paper also details the distribution of studies across different imaging modalities, with fundus photography being the most analyzed. While conditional GANs are frequently referenced, the paper suggests that their widespread use is complex due to the diversity of deep learning structures involved. A literature summary is provided for each application area, with specific attention to segmentation tasks, where GAN techniques have been particularly effective in retinal vessel segmentation, a historically challenging problem in ophthalmology.
