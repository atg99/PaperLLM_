The paper discusses various applications of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) in the field of ophthalmology, particularly focusing on feature extraction and anomaly detection. Feature extraction is highlighted as a crucial task, with studies utilizing GAN architectures for detecting pathologies in ophthalmic images. For instance, Schlegl et al. introduced a GAN-based anomaly detection method for retinal Optical Coherence Tomography (OCT), which estimates latent space through inverse mapping and calculates anomaly scores. This model has been successfully applied beyond ophthalmology to fields like industrial anomaly detection.

Another study by Xie et al. developed a modified conditional GAN for ultra-widefield fundus photography, enhancing retinal disease detection through an attention encoder and a multi-branch discriminator structure. Furthermore, the paper notes other significant applications that do not fit the primary search criteria, such as retinal pathology localization using CycleGAN, where the pathological region is identified by image subtraction, and image registration for aligning multimodal images, essential for effective GAN training.

The paper also summarizes various studies on domain transfer tasks using GANs in ophthalmology, detailing the techniques employed and the outcomes achieved in synthesizing retinal images from different modalities. These studies demonstrate the versatility and effectiveness of GANs in enhancing ophthalmic imaging and improving disease detection and analysis.
