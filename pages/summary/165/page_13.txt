The paper discusses the advancements and limitations of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) in the context of ophthalmic imaging. Mahapatra et al. demonstrated that a GAN-based autoencoder outperformed traditional methods for registering fundus photography and retinal angiography images. However, several limitations of GANs are highlighted, including mode collapse, spatial deformities due to misaligned training images, unintended changes during image translation, and the presence of high-frequency noise and artifacts. The complexity of GAN architectures, which often involve multiple deep learning modules, can lead to instability during training and challenges such as vanishing gradients.

To mitigate these issues, increasing the diversity and volume of clinical data is suggested as a solution. Despite the widespread use of GANs for data augmentation, the lack of standard evaluation metrics for assessing the realism of synthesized images remains a challenge, often relying on subjective assessments from researchers. The paper summarizes findings from various studies that applied GANs in ophthalmology, illustrating their potential for predicting postoperative outcomes and treatment responses in retinal conditions. Overall, while GANs present promising applications in ophthalmic imaging, ongoing research aims to refine their effectiveness and reliability.
