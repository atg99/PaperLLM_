The paper discusses the potential impact of data augmentation on the storage and development of deep learning models, suggesting a shift towards using labeled datasets primarily for evaluation rather than for representation learning. It draws comparisons with a similar survey by Feng et al., highlighting both surveys' efforts to define data augmentation and emphasize key themes, while noting that Feng et al. delve deeper into task-specific augmentation.

The authors acknowledge the challenges in creating large labeled datasets, such as ImageNet, which drives the exploration of alternatives like self-supervised language modeling exemplified by models like GPT-3 and BERT. Data augmentation aligns with this motivation by enabling learning with limited labeled data. The paper also highlights the effectiveness of transfer learning, which utilizes weights from previous tasks to improve learning with smaller datasets.

Additionally, self-supervised learning is explored, describing methods that learn from unlabeled data by algorithmically generating labels. Key techniques include generation, contrastive learning, and pretext tasks, which leverage augmentations to train models.

The section on text data augmentation introduces key motifs such as Strengthening Decision Boundaries, Brute Force Training, Causality and Counterfactual Examples, and the distinction between Meaning versus Form, which provide a framework for understanding the various augmentations discussed in the survey.
