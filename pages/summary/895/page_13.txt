The paper discusses various techniques for data augmentation in feature space to strengthen decision boundaries in classification tasks. It outlines methods such as hard example interpolation, which creates new examples by adjusting existing embeddings near the decision boundary, and hard example extrapolation, which moves samples along the angle from the class mean vector. Gaussian noise addition and difference transform are also introduced as methods to enhance the dataset. The authors highlight the MODALS algorithm's aim to improve decision boundaries and suggest that incorporating Supervised Contrastive Learning may enhance its effectiveness.

Differentiable Data Augmentation techniques are presented as a means of augmenting data while allowing for gradient backpropagation through the augmentation process. This approach can optimize transformations without significantly altering the data distribution. The paper also explores neural augmentation, which uses auxiliary neural networks to generate new training data, including methods such as neural machine translation and neural style transfer.

Back-translation is another key technique discussed, where texts are translated into another language and then back to the original language to create new instances. This method has garnered significant interest in the field of machine translation, demonstrating its potential for effective data augmentation.
