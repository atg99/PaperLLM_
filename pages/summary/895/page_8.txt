The paper discusses the distinction between understanding and meaning in language modeling, emphasizing that meaning cannot be derived solely from form. It references benchmarks like GLUE and SuperGLUE, which focus on form, versus KILT, which probes for meaning. The authors advocate for a deeper exploration of these definitions as a valuable area in language processing research.

Additionally, the paper addresses Data Augmentation as a method to prevent overfitting through regularization. It highlights the importance of understanding dataset characteristics to enhance systems, such as the need for consistent responses to comparison questions.

The authors classify augmentations into two categories: Symbolic and Neural. Symbolic Augmentations utilize human-designed rules, offering interpretability and effectiveness for short transformations, while Neural Augmentations leverage auxiliary models for data generation but may struggle with longer inputs. The results indicate that the effectiveness of augmentations is more pronounced with smaller datasets, such as 500 labeled examples, compared to larger sets.
