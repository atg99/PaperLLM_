The paper discusses the complexities of tokenization and data augmentation within the preprocessing pipeline for machine learning models. Tokenization, the conversion of word tokens to numeric indices, complicates the application of data augmentations, as it necessitates additional engineering efforts, such as creating synonym dictionaries for replacements. Researchers are investigating tokenizer-free models like byT5 and CANINE, which operate on byte-level sequences and require specialized processing for augmentations.

Position embeddings, integral to Transformer models, utilize sine and cosine functions for positional encoding, and the paper suggests exploring perturbations of these parameters as a form of data augmentation.

The paper also contrasts data augmentation practices on CPUs versus GPUs. Traditionally, augmentations are applied on CPUs before data is fed to GPUs. However, recent practices advocate for GPU-based augmentations, where techniques like Data Echoing can minimize idle time between data loading and model training.

Furthermore, the distinction between offline and online augmentation is emphasized. Offline augmentation involves pre-processing and storing augmented data, while online augmentation occurs dynamically during training, enhancing stochasticity and diversity. Although offline methods may speed up loading times, they lack the benefits of online approaches. The concept of augmentation multiplicity, which refers to generating multiple augmented samples from a single original instance, is also discussed. While it can enhance performance, it may lead to memory overhead if not managed within an online augmentation framework.
