The paper discusses advancements in transformer models that can handle longer input sequences, particularly emphasizing the potential of in-context learning models to process more task demonstrations. However, it notes that methods involving gradient updates are more practically advantageous due to scale limitations.

Prompt tuning is introduced as a method where prompts are embedded into a continuous space and optimized using gradient descent while keeping the rest of the network unchanged. Research by Lester et al. indicates that prompt tuning, similar to GPT-3, improves performance significantly over in-context learning, with ensembling of optimized prompts boosting results on SuperGLUE from 88.5 to 90.5. The analysis of optimized prompt embeddings can inform task complexity and similarity metrics.

The paper highlights the importance of domain and task alignment in fine-tuning, supported by studies showing that aligned data improves model performance. Techniques such as pattern exploiting training (PET) are discussed, where pre-trained language models label task-specific data using templates. PET has been shown to enable smaller models to outperform GPT-3 with limited labeled examples. The evolution to ADAPET further enhances performance by applying dense supervision without a verbalizer.

Despite these advancements, a noted limitation of these methods is their reliance on maintaining large language models for downstream tasks.
