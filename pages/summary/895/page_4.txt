The paper by Shorten et al. discusses the emerging solutions of self-supervised and transfer learning in the context of limited labeled data, highlighting the role of Data Augmentation in self-supervised learning and recent advancements in transfer and multi-task learning. It introduces AI-generating Algorithms (AI-GAs), referencing works such as POET, Generative Teaching Networks, and the Synthetic Petri Dish, which focus on algorithms that learn from their environment. This section differentiates AI-GAs from traditional augmentation controllers and emphasizes the potential for skill acquisition from artificial data in natural language processing (NLP).

The paper outlines the significance of Data Augmentation in NLP, noting its ability to prevent overfitting, integrate prior knowledge, and enhance model generalization. The structure of the survey includes key motifs of Data Augmentation, a categorization of text data augmentations into symbolic and neural methods, an examination of generalization testing, a comparison between image and text augmentation, practical implementation considerations, and a discussion of research questions. The conclusion summarizes the motivations and findings of the survey.

The background section emphasizes the importance of Data Augmentation in machine learning, noting its evolution alongside advances in generative modeling such as StyleGAN, GPT-3, and DALL-E. The paper posits that these advancements have opened new avenues for adapting language models for various tasks, making the survey timely and relevant.
