Adversarial augmentation involves using adversarially optimized inputs to enhance model robustness against misclassifications, contrasting with traditional controllers that aim to prevent such errors. Adversarial attacks in text data can include introducing typos or removing words, but many perturbations may be filtered out by preprocessing techniques like spell checkers. 

TextAttack is an open-source library for implementing adversarial text attacks and data augmentation, featuring four main components: a goal function that defines the desired output, constraints that limit input changes, transformations that detail methods for modifying the input, and a search method for finding attacks. Efficient construction of adversarial examples is crucial, as traditional techniques can slow down deep learning training. The DEAT algorithm by Wang et al. improves efficiency by reducing time consumption by up to 60% through batch replay.

Stacking augmentations has shown success in vision models, although it is more complex for text data. The CoDA strategy introduces local consistency loss to ensure that stacking does not excessively distort samples, alongside a global loss to maintain the integrity of neighborhoods around original instances.
