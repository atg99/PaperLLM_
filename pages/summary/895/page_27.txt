The paper discusses various strategies for enhancing data augmentation in natural language processing (NLP), focusing on curriculum learning, class imbalance, and task-specific augmentation techniques. It highlights the use of two fine-tuning heads to train on both unaugmented and highly augmented data, emphasizing the importance of exploring detailed augmentation pipelines.

Curriculum learning is explored as an effective method to structure data batches by varying data augmentation strength throughout training. Research by Kucnik and Smith suggests that selectively augmenting a subset of the dataset is more efficient than augmenting the entire dataset. Wei et al. demonstrate the benefits of gradually introducing augmented examples, aligning with the concept of optimizing augmentation parameters. Thakur et al. stress the importance of carefully selecting sentence pairs for successful augmentation.

The issue of class imbalance in classification models is addressed, with solutions including customized loss functions and sampling techniques like random oversampling and the synthetic minority oversampling technique (SMOTE). The paper notes that MixUp shares similarities with SMOTE and can be explored for text data.

Task-specific augmentation is crucial in NLP due to the diversity of tasks such as text classification, paraphrase identification, and question answering. Variations in input length necessitate tailored augmentation strategies; for instance, care must be taken to avoid altering answers in question-answering datasets. The authors refer to Feng et al. for a comprehensive overview of how data augmentation applies across various NLP tasks, including summarization and dialogue generation.
