In this paper, the authors discuss the relationship between generalization in deep learning models and data distributions, emphasizing the role of Data Augmentation as a tool for assessing this relationship. They critique traditional methods of measuring generalization, such as comparing training and test set accuracy, noting that overfitting in large-scale deep neural networks is often misunderstood. Instead, they advocate for examining generalization through the lens of data space and distribution shifts, particularly highlighting the limitations of models when exposed to adversarial examples that introduce noise or altered contexts.

The authors reference various studies and tools that illustrate these concepts. For instance, they mention CheckList, which tests linguistic capabilities in NLP models by introducing distribution shifts, and WILDS, a collection of real-world distribution shifts that can also be linked to Data Augmentations. Furthermore, they describe a method by Kaushik et al. that utilizes human annotators to create counterfactual examples in movie reviews and natural language inference, showcasing how minimal edits can shift model outputs. Overall, the paper presents Data Augmentation as a valuable framework for understanding and testing model generalization amidst varying data distributions.
