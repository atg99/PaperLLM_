Self-supervised learning and Data Augmentation both aim to incorporate prior knowledge into data domains, addressing challenges related to changing data distributions and tasks. Self-supervised learning focuses on creating tasks and loss functions for representation learning, while Data Augmentation simplifies the integration of multiple priors through random sampling, making it easier than self-supervised methods that often rely on unstable or costly multi-task learning.

Data Augmentation has significantly contributed to the success of self-supervised learning techniques, such as contrastive learning, which depends on generating multiple views of data. Additionally, it enhances the data efficiency of GAN frameworks and has been applied in large-scale models like ImageGPT.

Transfer learning has proven effective in training deep neural networks, particularly with the rise of unified dataset hubs like HuggingFace. Multi-task learning, exemplified by the T5 model, optimizes several tasks simultaneously and presents opportunities for Data Augmentation, such as MixUp and Back-Translation. Wei et al. introduced Multi-Task View (MTV), which utilizes augmented subsets and shares information across distributions by training separate heads and ensembling predictions. Geva et al. also demonstrated the benefits of using a shared feature extractor for different tasks.

AI-generating algorithms represent a novel concept in AI research, built on meta-learning architectures and algorithms. Data Augmentation is poised to play a crucial role in developing effective learning environments within this framework.
