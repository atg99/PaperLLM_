The paper by Shorten et al. discusses the distinctions between task-specific and task-agnostic augmentations, as well as form versus meaning augmentations, in the context of Deep Learning. It highlights generalization as a primary challenge, focusing on how well models can extrapolate from training data to unseen test sets. The authors propose that simulating distribution shifts through data augmentations, such as modifying tokens in datasets, can help address this challenge and enable deeper linguistic understanding.

The survey builds on previous work in image data augmentation, noting that while transformations in Computer Vision, like rotation and brightness adjustment, are easier to implement and stack, similar advancements in Natural Language Processing (NLP) remain limited. The authors emphasize the need for further exploration of successful image augmentation techniques in the text domain.

Practical implementation strategies for text data augmentation are discussed, including the use of consistency regularization and contrastive learning. The paper examines augmentation controllers that manage hyperparameters and transformation magnitudes, as well as strategies like AutoAugment and RandAugment aimed at improving performance. The distinction between augmentation controllers and curriculum learning is also addressed, along with considerations for CPU to GPU data transfer and the offline versus online augmentation debate.

The discussion section outlines opportunities for exploring text data augmentation, particularly noting the importance of task-specific strategies in various NLP applications, such as question answering and natural language inference, which require different input lengths and knowledge-intensive approaches.
