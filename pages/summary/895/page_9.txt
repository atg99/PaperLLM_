Rule-based augmentation involves creating rules to generate augmented examples through if-else programs and symbolic templates. Easy Data Augmentation (EDA) by Wei et al. introduces four primary techniques: random swapping, random deletion, random insertion, and random synonym replacement, which significantly enhance performance, particularly with smaller datasets. The semantic relationships between words, as illustrated by the similarity between "I am jogging" and "I am swimming," can inform the design of token vocabularies to improve augmentation. 

Additionally, rule-based programs are linked to adversarial attacks in NLP, where the intent differs but the methodology is similar. For instance, TextFooler assesses word importance by gauging output changes upon word deletion, selecting impactful words for synonym replacement. 

Regular Expression Augmentation is another method, commonly used for data cleaning, which can also identify language patterns and generate extensions aligned with a graph-structured grammar. For example, it can expand phrases like "This object is adjective" to include variations like "and adjective." 

Moreover, Min et al. suggest syntactic heuristics for augmentation, such as Inversion and Passivization, to alter sentence structure effectively.
