In this section, the authors discuss the implications of sending either classifiers or generative models to local databases to avoid centralized data storage. While generative models can capture a broader data distribution and provide insights into general data manifolds, they also pose risks of exposing sensitive information. 

The authors then shift focus to label augmentation in supervised learning, which involves enhancing class labels to improve model performance. A prominent method highlighted is Knowledge Distillation, which converts traditional one-hot encoded labels into a soft distribution based on another neural network's predictions. This technique has significantly impacted various fields, including natural language processing and computer vision.

Several other strategies for label augmentation are also discussed. Label smoothing adjusts the density of negative classes, benefiting classifier training, while approaches like Meta Pseudo Labels and Teaching with Commentaries utilize a meta-controller to learn from the student’s loss gradients to enhance label augmentation. Generative Teaching Networks have been proposed to apply similar concepts in the data space, although their application has been limited to image data thus far.

Additionally, Thakur et al. introduce Augmented SBERT, which uses a cross-encoder for label augmentation that outperforms bi-encoders in certain classification tasks, especially with less training data. This approach highlights the strategic selection of data for soft labeling.

Finally, the authors emphasize the challenge of achieving out-of-distribution generalization, which differs from in-distribution generalization. Measuring this requires assumptions about potential shifts in data distribution, as noted by Arjovsky, stressing the complexity of generalization when test data is unrelated to training data.
