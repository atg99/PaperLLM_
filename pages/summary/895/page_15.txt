The paper discusses the applications of generative models in representation learning and Data Augmentation, emphasizing their value beyond artistic applications. A key point is the distinction in using these models for Data Augmentation, particularly through Transfer Learning. While pre-trained language models are often fine-tuned with supervised tasks, fine-tuning with additional language modeling can enhance performance, particularly on limited datasets.

The authors highlight the importance of language modeling as a pre-training phase, noting that it can leverage more abundant data compared to specific downstream tasks, such as question-answering. They reference The Pile, an extensive dataset, as an example of the rich data available for training. Gururangan et al. argue that pre-training on relevant datasets significantly improves downstream performance, contrasting this with models like GPT-3.

A notable method for Generative Data Augmentation is Conditional BERT (C-BERT), which augments data by replacing masked tokens while preserving semantic labels. The paper also introduces the emerging strategy of prompting, which involves guiding language model outputs with text templates to generate or label new data. Research by Scao and Rush demonstrates that prompting can substantially enhance learning from limited data compared to traditional Transfer Learning.

The paper further explores in-context learning, particularly as exemplified by GPT-3, where task descriptions and examples are prepended to inputs without requiring model updates. The authors point out the significance of model scale in this technique, noting performance drops with fewer parameters, and suggest that this approach may still have untapped potential.
