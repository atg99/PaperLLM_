This section discusses various data augmentation techniques, specifically MixUp augmentation and Feature Space augmentation, as methods to enhance model performance and reduce overfitting.

MixUp augmentation involves creating new examples by blending existing ones, often merging their labels as well. For instance, it can concatenate halves of two text sequences to generate a new example. Variations of MixUp occur at different processing layers, such as word and sentence levels. Guo et al. explore these approaches, with wordMixup averaging embedding vectors at the input layer and sentMixup averaging sentence embeddings through siamese encoders. Their findings indicate that MixUp significantly reduces overfitting compared to traditional methods like dropout.

Feature Space augmentation, on the other hand, takes place in the intermediate representation space of Deep Neural Networks. This technique manipulates intermediate features by adding noise, which can originate from various distributions, including uniform or Gaussian. MODALS outlines several strategies for performing feature space augmentations, illustrating how to navigate class boundaries to create new data instances.
