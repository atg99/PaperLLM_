The paper discusses the use of Counterfactual Examples in text data augmentation, highlighting their reliance on human expertise for construction rather than algorithmic methods. These examples involve interventions like negations or numeric changes that can help establish causal links between semantic concepts and labels. Liu et al. propose a formal causal framework for data augmentation using structured causal models, which shows promise in improving translation systems and could potentially be adapted to other sequence-to-sequence tasks.

The distinction between meaning and form in language processing is explored through Bender and Koller’s octopus example, illustrating how mere mimicry of language does not equate to understanding its meaning. The paper suggests that augmentations aimed at enhancing form, such as synonym swapping and syntactic tree rotations, can improve models' grasp of language structure. However, achieving a true understanding of meaning may require grounding language in other modalities or abstract concepts, as well as incorporating embodiment through learning agents interacting with their environments.
