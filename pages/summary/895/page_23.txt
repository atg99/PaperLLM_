Shorten et al. (2021) discuss advancements in data augmentation techniques, particularly consistency regularization and contrastive learning, in the context of NLP. Xie et al. demonstrate that consistency regularization can outperform traditional supervised learning methods, achieving a notable reduction in error rates, such as a decrease from 6.5% to 4.2% in IMDB review classification with the fine-tuned BERT model. 

Contrastive learning is highlighted as a distinct approach that employs negative samples to enhance the learning signal. The authors suggest that leveraging successful data augmentation strategies from Computer Vision could benefit Text Data Augmentation. They reference frameworks like SimCLR and c-REALM, the latter using contrastive loss to align question embeddings with supervised answers. 

Gunel et al. show that incorporating supervised contrastive loss alongside cross-entropy loss leads to significant improvements on GLUE benchmark tasks, particularly with limited labeled examples. The results indicate a more distributed class embedding space, suggesting enhanced learning outcomes. The paper emphasizes the potential of these methodologies for advancing information retrieval in NLP.
