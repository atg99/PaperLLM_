The paper discusses text data augmentation techniques in Natural-Language Inference (NLI) tasks, particularly focusing on transformations like Inversion and Passivization. Inversion changes sentence perspectives, while Passivization alters the sentence structure to passive voice. The authors demonstrate that these augmentations improve performance on the HANS challenge set for NLI.

Additionally, the paper explores graph-structured augmentation, which utilizes structured representations of text, such as knowledge graphs and syntax trees. These structures provide explicit information about relationships and entities, enhancing the capabilities of Deep Learning models. The authors highlight the distinction between structured and unstructured data, noting that structured data can facilitate label-preserving transformations and incorporate prior knowledge.

A key concept introduced is the Knowledge Graph, which consists of entity-relation-entity tuples. The authors propose using Knowledge Graphs for synonym swaps, allowing for more precise replacements that maintain class labels. This method is more efficient than manual synonym dictionaries, especially given advancements in automated knowledge graph construction. The paper emphasizes the potential of structured language graphs, like WordNet, to improve augmentation strategies significantly.
