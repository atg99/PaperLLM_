The paper discusses the challenges and advancements in text data augmentation for deep learning, particularly in the context of Natural Language Processing (NLP). It highlights that the success of deep learning relies heavily on supervised learning, which requires large labeled datasets that are difficult to collect. The authors provide an example of the COVID-QA dataset, illustrating the labor-intensive nature of producing annotated data.

The paper outlines major motifs of data augmentation, such as enhancing local decision boundaries, brute force training, causality, counterfactual examples, and differentiating meaning from form. It presents various augmentation frameworks developed for text data and emphasizes the need for better generalization and overfitting measurement in deep learning.

Furthermore, the authors note that NLP lags behind computer vision in applying data augmentation techniques and discuss practical implementation tools like consistency regularization and augmentation pipelines. They also explore topics such as task-specific augmentations and the relationship between self-supervised learning and data augmentation.

The authors aim to inspire further research in text data augmentation, emphasizing its potential to improve deep learning models in NLP.
