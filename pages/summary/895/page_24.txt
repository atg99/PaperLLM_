The paper discusses contrastive learning and consistency regularization as methods to enhance model performance, particularly in representation learning. Contrastive learning focuses on making the representation of an instance similar to its transformed pair while distancing it from other instances within a mini-batch. This approach has shown significant advancements in computer vision with models like SimCLR and MoCo. Recent research has explored its application in natural language processing (NLP), particularly through information retrieval models like REALM.

The paper highlights that both methods aim to address performance issues in models, such as those identified by Thorne et al., where fact verification models perform better when ignoring evidence. Contrastive learning would enhance the model's ability to associate supporting evidence with claims by contrasting it against refuting evidence. In contrast, consistency regularization focuses on ensuring similar predictions despite slight perturbations in the evidence.

Negative Data Augmentation extends the idea of negative examples in contrastive learning by applying aggressive augmentations that may alter the data beyond its natural distribution. This method can reinforce the structural aspects of language, as demonstrated by Sinha et al.

Furthermore, the paper discusses augmentation controllers, which optimize the strength and combination of data augmentations in computer vision. Successful implementations like AutoAugment and RandAugment have not been widely adopted in NLP yet. The paper emphasizes the importance of hyperparameter optimization in data augmentation, noting that techniques like SpanBERT improve performance by masking multiple tokens instead of single ones, indicating a potential area for further research.
