The paper presents PP-LiteSeg, a real-time semantic segmentation model characterized by its efficient architecture, which comprises three novel modules: the Flexible and Lightweight Decoder (FLD), the Unified Attention Fusion Module (UAFM), and the Simple Pyramid Pooling Module (SPPM). 

The encoder extracts hierarchical features, where the channel count increases and spatial size decreases from low to high levels. In contrast, the decoder utilizes FLD to gradually enhance spatial size while reducing channels, balancing computational complexity between the encoder and decoder. This design improves efficiency.

To enhance feature representations, UAFM employs both spatial and channel attention modules, allowing for effective fusion of low-level and high-level features while minimizing computational costs. Additionally, SPPM optimizes contextual aggregation by reducing channel counts, eliminating shortcuts, and favoring addition over concatenation, thus achieving improved segmentation accuracy without significant extra computational load.

Experimental results on the Cityscapes and CamVid datasets demonstrate that PP-LiteSeg achieves a favorable balance between segmentation accuracy and inference speed, recording 72.0% mIoU at 273.6 FPS and 77.5% mIoU at 102.6 FPS on the Cityscapes test set. The contributions of the paper include the introduction of FLD, UAFM, and SPPM, culminating in the development of PP-LiteSeg, which showcases state-of-the-art performance in both accuracy and speed.
