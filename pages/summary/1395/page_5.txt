The paper presents the Simple Pyramid Pooling Module (SPPM) and its integration into a lightweight segmentation network called PP-LiteSeg. SPPM employs global-average-pooling with bin sizes of 1x1, 2x2, and 4x4, followed by convolution and upsampling operations. It enhances efficiency by reducing intermediate and output channels, removing shortcuts, and utilizing addition instead of concatenation. 

PP-LiteSeg consists of three main modules: an encoder, an aggregation module, and a decoder. The encoder utilizes the STDCNet architecture to extract hierarchical features, with two versions (PP-LiteSeg-T and PP-LiteSeg-B) offering trade-offs between segmentation accuracy and inference speed. The SPPM module captures long-range dependencies and global context from the encoder's output. The final output is generated through a Feature Level Decoder (FLD), which merges multi-level features using spatial attention modules.

The experimental section evaluates PP-LiteSeg on the Cityscapes and CamVid datasets, highlighting its performance in accuracy and inference speed compared to state-of-the-art methods. The training employs stochastic gradient descent with specific settings for each dataset, incorporating data augmentation techniques to improve model robustness. The results affirm the effectiveness of the proposed modules, supported by ablation studies.
