The paper discusses two primary types of explanations for machine learning models: saliency and global explanations. 

Saliency explanations focus on individual pixels and their contributions to classification decisions. Notable algorithms include Class Activation Maps (CAM) and its enhancement, GRAD-CAM, which generalizes CAM for any convolutional network. Additional local explanations like Individual Conditional Expectation (ICE) provide insights into how changes in specific features affect predictions.

Global explanations, on the other hand, provide insights into the entire model. Partial Dependence Plots (PDPs) illustrate how predicted responses change with varying feature values, helping to identify linear or complex relationships. TCA V (Testing with Concept Activation Vectors) represents neural network states using Concept Activation Vectors, making explanations more user-friendly by associating them with human-friendly concepts.

Both PDPs and ICE curves serve as visualization tools for global explanations. Prototypes, which are representative samples of each class, can also offer global insights. Additionally, methods like SP-LIME summarize local explanations to provide a broader understanding of model behavior. Overall, these techniques aim to enhance interpretability and understanding of machine learning models.
