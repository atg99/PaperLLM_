The paper discusses various methods for evaluating the accuracy and quality of explanations generated by AI systems, particularly deep neural networks. Adebayo et al. assessed explanation accuracy by measuring changes in saliency pixel explanations relative to differences in trained models. Sixt et al. randomized middle convolutional layers to compare saliency pixels based on actual versus random labels. Qi et al. evaluated explanation accuracy by modifying image pixels deemed relevant and assessing the impact on system scores. Bhatt et al. examined "feature importance" explanations through sensitivity and faithfulness checks, ensuring similar inputs yield similar explanations and that explanation changes correlate with input changes.

Additionally, Wachter et al. tested the quality of counterfactual explanations, determining the minimal input change required for a system to alter its decision. The paper also explores the role of human performance as a benchmark for AI systems, highlighting differing opinions on whether machines should exceed human performance or simply match it. It emphasizes the necessity of explainable decisions in high-stakes human contexts, such as in fields like medicine and law, and critiques the reliability of human-generated explanations for their own judgments. Understanding human explanations can guide the development of metrics for explainable AI and enhance human-machine collaboration.
