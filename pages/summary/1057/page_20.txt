Post-hoc explanations are categorized into local and global explanations. Local explanations focus on specific decisions or subsets of inputs, while global explanations approximate the overall model behavior. For instance, logistic regression can serve both as a self-explanatory model and as a global approximation for more complex models, offering insights into all inputs through its regression coefficients.

Local explanations, detailed in Section 6.2.1, typically provide insights for individual decisions. A notable algorithm is LIME (Local Interpretable Model-agnostic Explanations), which generates an interpretable model based on nearby data points to explain local decisions. It commonly employs logistic regression and utilizes superpixels for image data. Another important local explanation method is SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations), which applies game theory concepts to derive per-feature importance through Shapley values, treating features as players in a coalitional game.

Counterfactual explanations also fall under local explanations, suggesting alternative inputs that would lead to different decisions from the system. These explanations typically present a single instance that closely resembles the original input but results in a different outcome. Some methodologies even allow for multiple counterfactual instances. Ustun et al. have explored counterfactuals in the context of logistic regression, detailing the necessary feature changes.

Furthermore, saliency maps are commonly used in image data to provide local explanations by highlighting areas of importance in an image that influence the model's decision.
