The paper discusses the concept of human simulatability in the context of self-interpretable models, emphasizing its importance in assessing model complexity. It outlines various studies that have tested human simulatability through different methodologies. For instance, Lage et al. measured human accuracy and response times when simulating models, while Hase and Bansal categorized simulatability into forward and counterfactual simulations. Poursabzi-Sangdeh et al. focused on logistic regression models related to housing prices, and Slack et al. conducted evaluations based on minor perturbations of input data.

The paper also highlights alternative strategies for evaluating the meaningfulness of model outputs by having humans perform tasks based on these outputs. Poursabzi-Sangdeh et al. asked participants to predict house prices, revealing potential discrepancies with model predictions. Other studies, such as those by Kim et al. and Lai and Tan, examined human classification abilities and deception detection accuracy, respectively, in relation to machine-generated explanations.

Additionally, the paper notes that subjective ratings can also assess the meaningfulness of explanations, as discussed by Hoffman et al., who proposed criteria for effective explanations. Overall, the studies indicate that explanations can significantly enhance human understanding and decision-making accuracy when interacting with machine learning models.
