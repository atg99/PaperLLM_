The paper discusses the implications of model risk in the context of explainable AI (XAI), emphasizing the importance of accurate explanations to avoid miscarriages of justice and promote a just society. It highlights that incorrect use of models or operating beyond their knowledge limits can lead to significant risks. The paper identifies four principles, including the need to describe a model's knowledge limits to build trust and ensure that the model operates within its capabilities.

The risks associated with software exposure vary based on the end users, whether internal or external, necessitating different management strategies. The paper also notes that XAI presents both threats and opportunities, with risk management focusing on trade-offs and the assessment of likelihood and impact.

The literature review section summarizes various perspectives on explainable AI, categorizing techniques into transparent and post-hoc interpretability. Transparent explanations reflect how a system arrives at its output, whereas post-hoc explanations provide useful insights without clarifying the model's inner workings. The paper references key contributions, such as Lipton's distinction between explanation types and Rudin's advocacy for maintaining interpretability in high-stakes decisions without sacrificing accuracy.

Additionally, it mentions the development of a risk management framework for AI, indicated by a request for information from NIST, and reviews user-centered approaches to explainable AI systems, including the Self-Explanation Scorecard. The paper emphasizes the necessity of interpreting AI outcomes responsibly to foster trust and understanding among users.
