The paper discusses the risks associated with explainable artificial intelligence (AI), highlighting the balance between potential threats and opportunities. It emphasizes that any explainable AI system carries inherent risks, which necessitate a risk management strategy that incorporates four key principles: the necessity of explanations, the requirement for meaningfulness, the need for accuracy, and the management of stakeholder risk appetite.

The first principle, explanations, is crucial for understanding AI systems but may expose proprietary information. The number of explanations provided can influence the understanding of the system, with multiple explanations possibly revealing intellectual property if interconnected.

The second principle focuses on meaningful explanations, which can enhance user insight but may also risk exposing vulnerabilities. Conversely, explanations that lack meaning may be disregarded.

The third principle addresses the importance of accuracy in explanations. Model risk arises from errors in AI models, leading to potential misinterpretations. Inaccuracies in explanations can mislead users and result in poor decision-making, particularly in sensitive applications such as face recognition and judicial systems.

Overall, the paper underscores the need for careful consideration of these principles in the development and implementation of explainable AI to mitigate risks while maximizing benefits.
