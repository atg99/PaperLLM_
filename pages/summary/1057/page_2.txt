The document NISTIR 8312 outlines four key principles of explainable artificial intelligence (AI) aimed at enhancing the transparency, accountability, and trustworthiness of AI systems. The authors, part of the Information Access Division and Information Technology Laboratory, emphasize that these principles are essential for fostering public confidence in AI technologies.

The four principles are:

1. **Transparency**: AI systems should provide clear information about their operations, decision-making processes, and underlying data. This helps users understand how outcomes are determined.

2. **Interpretability**: The results produced by AI systems should be understandable to users. This involves offering explanations that are accessible and coherent, ensuring that users can grasp the rationale behind AI decisions.

3. **Reliability**: AI systems must perform consistently and predictably across various scenarios. This principle underscores the importance of robust design and testing to ensure that AI behaves as expected.

4. **Accountability**: There should be mechanisms in place to hold AI systems and their developers accountable for their actions and decisions. This includes establishing clear lines of responsibility and processes for addressing errors or biases.

By adhering to these principles, stakeholders can improve the design and implementation of AI systems, promoting ethical practices and enhancing user trust. The publication serves as a guideline for organizations seeking to develop explainable AI technologies.
