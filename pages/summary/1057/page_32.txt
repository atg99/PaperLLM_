The referenced works focus on various aspects of explainability and interpretability in artificial intelligence and related fields. Broniatowski's 2021 paper discusses the psychological foundations underlying the need for explainability in AI systems, emphasizing how human cognitive processes influence the perception of AI explanations. The 2018 collaboration between Broniatowski and Reyna presents a formal model of fuzzy-trace theory, addressing framing effects and the Allais paradox, which highlight decision-making biases. Caruana et al. (2020) provide best practices and challenges for creating intelligible machine learning models, while Chen et al. (2018) propose an explainable model specifically for credit risk assessment.

Additional contributions include Chi's examination of expert characteristics and a technical report by the Chief Financial Officers Council on enterprise risk management. The foundational ImageNet database by Deng et al. (2009) is also noted for its role in advancing computer vision. Dimanov et al. (2020) address the issue of model fairness and transparency, while Doleac and Stein (2013) explore race-related outcomes in online markets. Doshi-Velez and Kim (2017) advocate for a rigorous approach to interpretable machine learning, and Došilović et al. (2018) provide a comprehensive survey on explainable AI. Lastly, Edmond et al. (2017) discuss the application of cognitive science principles in forensic practices, illustrating the broader implications of cognitive insights in various domains.
