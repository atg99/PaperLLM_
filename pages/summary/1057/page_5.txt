The paper discusses the evolving landscape of artificial intelligence (AI) and the importance of explainability as part of building trustworthy AI systems. The National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) has collaborated with the AI community to identify key characteristics that contribute to trust in AI, including accuracy, privacy, reliability, robustness, safety, security, mitigation of harmful bias, transparency, fairness, and accountability. 

Focusing specifically on explainable AI, the paper introduces four fundamental principles essential for AI systems that require explanations:

1. **Explanation**: The system provides evidence or reasons for its outputs or processes.
2. **Meaningful**: The explanations are understandable to the intended users.
3. **Explanation Accuracy**: The explanations accurately reflect the reasoning behind the output and the system's processes.
4. **Knowledge Limits**: The system operates only within its designed parameters and when it has sufficient confidence in its outputs.

The paper emphasizes that both process-based and outcome-based explanations are crucial, with varying needs depending on the audience, such as developers, policymakers, or end-users. It highlights that the context of the explanation, including regulatory requirements and user interactions, influences its appropriateness and quality. The proposed principles aim to address a wide range of motivations and perspectives, ultimately guiding the measurement of explanation quality in AI systems.
