The paper discusses the vulnerabilities of explainable AI systems to adversarial attacks, particularly focusing on explanation accuracy as a critical component of effective explanations. It highlights that explanations with less than 100 percent accuracy can be exploited by adversaries to manipulate classifier outputs through minor input perturbations, thereby obscuring system biases. Lakkaraju and Bastani note that while some explanations may mimic predictions, this does not guarantee accuracy, potentially misleading users. Slack et al. demonstrate how a scaffolding approach can create misleading explanations by matching classifications across inputs but altering outputs for small perturbations, thus concealing the broader behavior of the system. Aivodji et al. illustrate another tactic, "fairwashing," where an ensemble of fairer models is used to mask the unfairness of a closed-box model. Dimanov et al. further show how slight model perturbations can manipulate explanations. The paper also references vulnerabilities identified by Hall et al., including a lack of input invariance in saliency pixel explanations, as discussed by Kindermans et al.

The subsequent section evaluates explainable AI algorithms, categorizing evaluations based on the principles outlined in previous sections. While various methods for assessing explanation meaningfulness and accuracy are reviewed, the paper notes a scarcity of research on evaluating knowledge limits, leading to its exclusion from the discussion. One method for measuring meaningfulness involves assessing human simulatability, where a person's ability to understand the model and produce predictions from input data is evaluated.
