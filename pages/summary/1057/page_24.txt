The paper discusses various methods and metrics for evaluating the interpretability and accuracy of model explanations in machine learning. Holzinger et al. introduced the System Causability Scale (SCS) for comparing explanations, while Lage et al. investigated human simulatability by asking users to rate simulation difficulty. Rajagopal et al. conducted experiments to evaluate different properties of explanations, and Lakkaraju et al. assessed interpretability by gauging whether users found provided information sufficient for making conclusions.

Poursabzi-Sangdeh et al. found that less transparent models could sometimes facilitate better simulation due to potential information overload. The complexity of models was further explored by Lage et al., who noted that various complexity types impact transparency differently. Lakkaraju et al. also measured decision-making speed and accuracy with provided explanations, while Narayanan et al. evaluated the influence of output complexity on human performance. Bhatt et al. developed a metric to quantify feature importance in explanations.

Regarding explanation accuracy, the concept of fidelity is highlighted, with several studies evaluating how well explanations reflect model behavior. Methods include simulating models with system outputs as ground truth and assessing post-hoc explanations using machine learning metrics. Mohseni et al. proposed human evaluations alongside "sanity checks" for accuracy. Other approaches involved assessing how changes in input affected output based on explanations, with Samek et al. measuring the impact of removing important pixels on classification scores. Additionally, Yeh et al. introduced an infidelity measure, while Alvarez Melis and Jaakkola evaluated explanation accuracy by analyzing the effects of feature manipulation on classification probabilities. Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of understanding model interpretability and accuracy in developing effective machine learning systems.
