The paper discusses the significance of explainable AI and its impact on user acceptance of system outcomes. It presents a multidisciplinary framework based on four principles that explore human-machine interaction, emphasizing that practical needs will shape how these principles are applied across various applications. The authors argue that while the focus has been on enhancing AI systems' ability to generate quality explanations, human capabilities to meet these principles are limited, providing a benchmark for comparison.

The paper highlights recent regulations that demand accountability in AI through explainability, suggesting that advancements in explainable AI may enable certain AI components to better meet societal expectations than humans can. Understanding the explainability of both AI systems and humans can lead to improved outcomes in human-machine collaboration.

Additionally, the authors note that while they focused on specific human factors related to explainable decisions, there remains much to explore regarding human reasoning and decision-making principles. They point out that humans generally prefer simpler explanations, yet individual differences and contextual factors heavily influence perceived explanation quality.

The authors conclude that studying the interface between humans and AI is essential for advancing explainable AI, as successful human-machine collaborations could lead to breakthroughs in AI explainability. Their principles aim to guide the development of explainable AI, ultimately fostering a safer and more effective interaction between users and AI systems.
