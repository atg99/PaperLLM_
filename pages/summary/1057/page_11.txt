The paper discusses the principles of meaningful explanations and explanation accuracy in the context of AI systems. It emphasizes that explanations should be understandable and relevant to the intended audience, which can vary based on individual experiences, prior knowledge, and the specific context of use. Developers must tailor explanations to meet the diverse needs of different user groups, such as system developers versus end-users.

The meaningful principle highlights that what constitutes a good explanation can change over time as users gain more experience with a system. To achieve meaningful explanations, it is crucial to understand the audience's needs and level of expertise.

The paper also introduces the explanation accuracy principle, which requires that explanations truthfully reflect the processes behind a system's outputs. Explanation accuracy is distinct from decision accuracy, which concerns the correctness of a system's judgment. While existing metrics assess decision accuracy, researchers are still developing methods to measure explanation accuracy.

Moreover, the level of detail in explanations is important; simpler explanations may suffice for some audiences, while others may need more nuanced information to fully understand the system's reasoning. The paper indicates ongoing research in both meaningfulness and accuracy, aiming to establish effective measurement protocols tailored to different audiences and contexts.
