The principles discussed in the paper serve as a framework for addressing the needs of a system, guiding responses to new challenges and inquiries. The authors categorize explanations based on two key properties: purpose and style. The purpose refers to the reason behind requesting an explanation, which varies according to the audience and their roles within the system. For instance, system builders may seek clarifications related to debugging AI models, while regulators might focus on compliance with regulatory requirements.

The style of an explanation is influenced by its purpose and encompasses three main elements: level of detail, degree of interaction, and format. The level of detail can range from sparse, providing minimal information, to extensive, offering comprehensive insights. For example, a sparse explanation might simply state that a system slowed due to overheating, while an extensive explanation would include a detailed report on system performance.

Interaction between humans and machines is categorized into three types: declarative explanations, one-way interactions, and two-way interactions. Declarative explanations involve the system providing a fixed explanation without further interaction, typical of many current explainable AI methods. One-way interactions allow for user queries to influence the explanation provided, enabling more tailored responses. The deepest level of interaction is two-way interaction, resembling a conversation where both the user and the machine can ask and respond to questions, facilitating a more dynamic exploration of explanations.

Overall, these elements are essential for developing effective explanations that meet the outlined principles, laying the groundwork for more informed system interactions.
