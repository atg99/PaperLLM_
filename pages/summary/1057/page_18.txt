The paper presents a comprehensive examination of user-centered design principles, particularly in the context of explainable AI (XAI). Key perspectives highlight the distinction between interpretability and explainability, with Broniatowski emphasizing the need for tailored system outputs for different user types. Wachter et al. argue for the necessity of explanation accuracy, advocating for counterfactual explanations that outline minimal changes needed to alter predictions while safeguarding intellectual property.

Gilpin et al. propose a balance between interpretability and completeness in explanations, stressing that trade-offs should not obscure system limitations. Doshi-Velez and Kim introduce a framework for assessing the meaningfulness of explanations, identifying the need for metrics and evaluation methods to bridge the gap between conceptual principles and practical applications.

The Information Commissioner’s Office and The Alan Turing Institute outline essential principles for XAI, including transparency, accountability, contextual consideration, and reflection on societal impact. They differentiate between process-based and outcome-based explanations, emphasizing fairness and safety.

Barredo Arrieta et al. explore various terminologies related to explainability, highlighting their interconnectedness. Weller addresses types of transparency, introducing the notion of faithfulness in explanations, which should convey a complete and accurate understanding.

Overall, the paper identifies both commonalities and divergences across the discussed viewpoints, underscoring the complexity of creating effective user-centered design principles for explainable AI systems.
