The paper presents four fundamental principles for explainable artificial intelligence (AI) systems, emphasizing the need for these systems to provide evidence or reasons for their outcomes, deliver understandable explanations tailored to individual users, accurately reflect the processes behind their outputs, and operate within their designed conditions with sufficient confidence in their results. These principles are termed explanation, meaningful, explanation accuracy, and knowledge limits. Developed through extensive stakeholder engagement, they address the multidisciplinary nature of explainable AI, incorporating insights from computer science, engineering, and psychology. The paper highlights the necessity for tailored explanations, acknowledging that different users require different types of information. It categorizes explanations into five types, summarizes relevant theories, and reviews major classes of explainable algorithms. Additionally, it assesses how human-generated explanations align with the proposed principles, shedding light on the challenges in designing effective explainable AI systems.
