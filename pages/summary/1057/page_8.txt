The paper discusses the importance of explainable artificial intelligence (AI) in fostering trust and understanding in automated decision-making processes, using the experience of an author's father with his oncologist as a foundational example. It highlights how the oncologist's effective communication and bedside manner contributed to the father's trust in his treatment, suggesting that AI systems could similarly enhance trust by providing clear explanations of their recommendations.

The demand for explainable AI is driven by legal requirements and societal expectations for transparency in decisions made by automated systems, such as those used in medical diagnoses, loan evaluations, and sentencing recommendations. The paper emphasizes that trust in AI systems hinges on several properties, including explainability, resiliency, reliability, bias, and accountability. A lack of clear rationale for AI decisions can lead to distrust and concerns about fairness, potentially hindering the technology's acceptance.

To address these challenges, the authors propose four human-centered principles for explainable AI. These principles prioritize the need for explanations to be understandable, accurate, and reflective of the AI system's decision-making process. Additionally, they advocate for systems to signal when they operate outside their intended conditions. The focus of these principles is on the quality and limitations of explanations rather than the underlying algorithms or their deployment, aiming to establish a framework for measuring the effectiveness of AI explanations in a structured manner.
