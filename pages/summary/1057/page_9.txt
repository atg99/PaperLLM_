The paper emphasizes the necessity for concrete definitions to measure explanation quality in explainable AI (XAI). It outlines four fundamental principles that serve as a foundation for assessing XAI, guiding future research, and addressing policy, safety, and societal acceptance of AI technologies. These principles are influenced by the interaction between AI systems and human users, highlighting the importance of context in determining the appropriateness of explanations.

In Section 2, the four principles are introduced, which apply to various AI techniques beyond just machine learning. Key terms such as explanation, output, and process are operationally defined to clarify the discussion. An explanation is characterized as the evidence or reasoning related to a system's output or process, while output refers to the results of a system’s actions, which can vary depending on the task at hand. The process encompasses the design and workflow underlying the system.

The paper also addresses the risks associated with explainable AI, particularly if the proposed principles are ignored. It reviews existing methods and metrics for evaluating XAI and compares human and machine performance expectations. Overall, the principles aim to establish a framework for enhancing understanding and trust in AI systems through effective explanations.
