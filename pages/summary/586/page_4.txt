The paper discusses various approaches to plankton classification, highlighting both traditional and modern techniques. Early methods employed texture descriptors and machine learning classifiers such as Support Vector Machines (SVM) and ensemble techniques. Li et al. utilized shape descriptors reduced by Principal Component Analysis (PCA) for classification. Ellen et al. combined gradient boosted random forest classifiers with SVM, while Chang et al. applied SURF descriptors with PCA but were limited by a small dataset.

Recent advancements have shifted towards deep learning, particularly Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN), which offer significant advantages over handcrafted methods by reducing the need for extensive preprocessing. Notably, the 2015 National Data Science Bowl showcased an ensemble of CNNs that excelled in classifying plankton images. Innovations included robust layers designed by Dieleman’s team to tackle cyclic variations.

Various studies have proposed CNN architectures, such as Py et al.'s GoogleNet-inspired model and Lee et al.'s approach addressing class imbalance through transfer learning on a large dataset. Dai et al. introduced ZooplanktoNet, leveraging data augmentation to combat overfitting. Hybrid CNNs and other models have explored preprocessing techniques and feature fusion, although some methods yielded lower performance compared to score-level fusion.

The paper emphasizes the exploration of deep learning and transfer learning to develop an ensemble of classifiers for plankton classification. Key objectives include fine-tuning pre-trained CNNs, utilizing them for transfer learning, and integrating preprocessing to enhance feature extraction. The proposed heterogeneous ensemble demonstrates substantial performance improvements across well-known plankton datasets, achieving state-of-the-art results with minimal parameter tuning required.
