The paper presents experimental results on the classification performance of various deep learning models and transfer learning techniques, as shown in Tables 4 and 5. 

Table 4 details the F-measures obtained from different preprocessing techniques (Gradient and Orientation) across three datasets: WHOI, ZooScan, and Kaggle. The F-measure values indicate that models such as InceptionV3 and GoogleNet performed notably well, with InceptionV3 achieving the highest F-measure of 0.943 for the WHOI dataset using Gradient preprocessing. The ensemble methods, particularly the combination of multiple feature extraction techniques, also yielded high performance, with the Fusion method achieving an F-measure of 0.953.

Table 5 focuses on transfer learning outcomes using various selection strategies (1FC, aFC, Man, SFFS) and highlights that the manual selection approach (Man) consistently outperformed others in terms of F-measure. Notably, some results were marked as "---" where the SVM classifier failed to converge, particularly for the 1FC method in the ZooScan dataset, indicating ineffective feature extraction from the last fully connected layer.

The paper emphasizes the significance of the transfer learning results, which surpassed traditional SVM classification based on handcrafted features. Furthermore, it sets the stage for comparing the proposed ensemble method to existing literature, including various handcrafted approaches and classifiers, indicating potential improvements over previous methods.

Overall, the findings suggest that both preprocessing techniques and transfer learning strategies can significantly enhance classification performance in deep learning applications.
