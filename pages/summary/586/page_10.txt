The paper discusses challenges in plankton classification, specifically addressing the issue of dataset drift, where the distribution of classes changes between training and testing sets due to varying conditions over time. The authors conducted experiments without specific parameter optimization or preprocessing, unlike some previous studies.

For evaluation, the paper utilizes common performance indicators in multi-label classification, including F-measure, accuracy, and area under the ROC curve (AUC). These metrics are derived from confusion matrices for each class, allowing calculation of recall, precision, and F-measure as averages across classes. 

In the results section, the authors present findings from experiments involving the fine-tuning of several convolutional neural network (CNN) models on three datasets. They explore two image resizing strategies: SqR, which pads images to a square size, and Pad, which only pads smaller images. The results indicate that the SqR strategy generally yields better performance across two out of three datasets, while Pad performs better in one specific dataset. Among the CNN models tested, DenseNet emerged as the top performer across all datasets. The study also includes ensemble methods that combine the outputs of the various models, with the best strategies identified for plankton classification.
