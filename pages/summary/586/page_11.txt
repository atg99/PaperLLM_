The paper presents a series of experiments evaluating the classification performance of various convolutional neural network (CNN) architectures using two training approaches: one round (1R) and two rounds (2R) of tuning. The F-measure results for different models across three datasets—WHOI, ZooScan, and Kaggle—are detailed in Tables 2 and 3.

In the first experiment with 1R training, the results indicate that DenseNet achieved the highest F-measure of 0.949 on the WHOI dataset, while the ensemble methods (Fus_RS and Fus_1R) yielded the best overall performance with F-measures of 0.952. The data suggests that the sample sizes in the datasets are adequate, rendering preliminary training on external datasets unnecessary.

The second experiment, which involved 2R tuning, produced results comparable to the 1R training. DenseNet again performed well, with an F-measure of 0.947 for WHOI. The ensemble method (Fus_2R) reached an F-measure of 0.951, indicating that combining classifiers from both 1R and 2R can enhance performance.

Additionally, the third experiment explored pre-processing strategies before classification. Two notable strategies, Gradient and Orientation, were assessed, with the results indicating that their combination in an ensemble provided superior performance compared to other preprocessing methods.

Overall, the findings highlight the effectiveness of both the 1R and 2R tuning approaches, as well as the benefits of using specific pre-processing techniques to optimize CNN classification performance.
