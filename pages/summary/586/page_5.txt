The CNN architectures discussed in this paper include several well-known models that are fine-tuned to enhance their performance for specific classification tasks. The preprocessing approaches serve as filters that prepare the data prior to training, potentially improving the model's ability to learn relevant features. Transfer learning is employed to leverage knowledge from pre-trained models, allowing for efficient feature extraction and classification.

Section 3 elaborates on the datasets utilized for the experiments, detailing the testing protocols and presenting the results obtained from the various configurations tested. The performance of the models is evaluated based on their ability to classify plankton into different categories, demonstrating how the proposed methods yield improvements over traditional approaches.

The paper concludes in Section 4, summarizing the findings and implications of the research, emphasizing the effectiveness of deep learning and transfer learning in the context of image classification tasks. The overall aim is to demonstrate the viability of using fine-tuned CNNs and transfer learning techniques to address the challenges presented in image recognition, particularly in the classification of plankton types.
