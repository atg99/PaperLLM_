The paper discusses various Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) architectures available in the MATLAB Deep Learning Toolbox, all pre-trained on the ImageNet database and fine-tuned for specific tasks. 

1. **AlexNet**: Winner of the 2012 ILSVRC challenge, it has five convolutional layers, three fully connected layers, and utilizes ReLU activation for faster training. The input size is 227x227 pixels.

2. **GoogleNet**: Winner of the 2014 challenge, it introduces the Inception module, allowing for a deeper network with fewer parameters. It consists of 22 layers (27 with pooling) and accepts 224x224 pixel images.

3. **InceptionV3**: A variant of GoogleNet, it factors 7x7 convolutions into smaller 3x3 convolutions, accepting images of 299x299 pixels.

4. **VGGNet**: Placed second in 2014, it features 16 or more layers with 3x3 convolution filters and pooling layers between groups. The models VGG-16 and VGG-19 accept 224x224 pixel images.

5. **ResNet**: Winner of the 2015 challenge, it is significantly deeper than previous models, introducing residual layers and global average pooling. The input size is 224x224 pixels, with variants like ResNet50 and ResNet101 used in this work.

6. **DenseNet**: An evolution of ResNet that connects each layer to every other layer, resulting in improved performance at increased computational cost, accepting 224x224 pixel images.

7. **SqueezeNet**: A compact model with only 18 layers, it achieves performance similar to AlexNet with significantly fewer parameters and accepts 227x227 pixel images.

The paper also evaluates three fine-tuning approaches for the pretrained models:

- **One Round Tuning (1R)**: Standard fine-tuning starting from pretrained weights, using a uniform learning rate across layers.
  
- **Two Rounds Tuning (2R)**: The first round involves tuning on an external dataset of plankton images, followed by standard fine-tuning to adjust classification weights.

- **Pre-processing Tuning (PR)**: This method utilizes preprocessed images, employing various strategies to enhance feature discernibility for plankton classification, leveraging insights from handcrafted techniques.

The experimental section highlights the most significant results, emphasizing the effectiveness of different fine-tuning strategies to adapt CNNs for specific applications.
