The paper discusses the effectiveness of various data augmentation techniques for Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) using synthesized utterances based on different speaker representation methods. The experiments utilize the L IBRISPEECH corpus, which includes both clean and noisy test partitions. Results indicate that the choice of speaker representation significantly impacts ASR performance, as shown in Table 1. Specifically, using random d-vectors yields less effective speaker diversity due to inconsistencies between training and inference d-vectors. In contrast, sampling from actual speaker representations leads to a relative improvement of approximately 4% in Word Error Rate (WER) for both clean and noisy tests.

Further analysis reveals that while high-quality single-speaker TTS has superior synthesis quality, it does not improve ASR performance, underscoring the necessity of multi-speaker TTS for effective data augmentation. The study also explores the impact of varying amounts of source training data on ASR performance, as detailed in Table 2. The findings suggest that while data augmentation remains beneficial with reduced source data, performance declines as the amount of source material decreases, particularly for noisy test data.

Additionally, the paper examines lexical diversity, highlighting the potential of speech synthesis to generate unseen utterances to enhance training material. Topline experiments demonstrate that knowledge of test utterances can lead to significant ASR gains, emphasizing that access to lexical content—rather than audio—is sufficient for improvement. This insight is particularly relevant for domain-specific applications where prior knowledge of utterances can be leveraged.
