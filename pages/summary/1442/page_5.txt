The paper investigates the impact of data augmentation techniques on automatic speech recognition (ASR) performance, specifically focusing on the use of synthesized utterances and domain adaptation. The results are detailed through various experiments that highlight the effectiveness of augmenting training data with both test utterances and synthesized data from different language models.

Initially, the paper presents findings on ASR performance, measured by word error rate (WER), when augmenting training data with test utterances. The experiments show a slight improvement in WER with the inclusion of synthesized utterances generated from a MaxEnt language model trained on the L IBRISPEECH dataset. Notably, a significant reduction in error is observed when the number of synthesized utterances is increased up to 600,000, indicating a 5% relative reduction in WER. However, beyond this point, the benefits plateau, suggesting diminishing returns with further augmentation.

The study also explores the effects of domain adaptation through the use of text-to-speech (TTS) data from different sources, specifically contrasting ISOLATED-SENTENCES and L IBRISPEECH data. While training solely on ISOLATED-SENTENCES yields lower synthesis quality, combining datasets enhances ASR performance. The results indicate that while domain differences exist, the additional training data from L IBRISPEECH contributes positively to overall performance.

Furthermore, the paper examines lexical diversity by generating utterances using a language model trained on ISOLATED-SENTENCES data. The findings reveal that while significant gains are achievable from training on ISOLATED-SENTENCES alone, these improvements diminish when L IBRISPEECH data is included, likely due to the inherent differences in the datasets.

In summary, the research underscores the value of data augmentation and domain adaptation in improving ASR performance, while also highlighting the complexities associated with synthesizing diverse utterances and the influence of training data sources on recognition outcomes.
