The paper discusses the potential of using synthesized speech as a means to enhance speech recognition performance, particularly in scenarios where human speech data is limited or expensive to obtain. Recent advancements in the Tacotron speech synthesis architecture, which can produce natural-sounding multi-speaker synthesized speech, have prompted the exploration of this approach.

The authors conduct experiments using two distinct corpora: L IBRISPEECH, a large collection of audiobooks, and I SOLATED -SENTENCES, a dataset of shorter utterances. They investigate the impact of augmenting training data with synthesized speech on automatic speech recognition (ASR) systems. The hypothesis is that incorporating synthesized speech, which is designed to mimic human speech, could improve ASR performance.

The study employs a Tacotron 2-based multi-speaker synthesis model and an end-to-end encoder-decoder with attention for recognition. The results show that while adding synthesized material can enhance recognition performance, there remains a significant performance gap between recognizers trained solely on human speech versus those trained on synthesized speech. The paper highlights the importance of acoustic and lexical diversity in training datasets and suggests that synthesized speech can serve as a valuable augmentation strategy, particularly for domain transfer. However, it also acknowledges the limitations and challenges of fully replacing human speech with synthesized alternatives in training ASR systems.
