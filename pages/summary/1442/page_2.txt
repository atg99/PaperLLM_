This paper investigates the effectiveness of using speech synthesis models for data augmentation in automatic speech recognition (ASR). It emphasizes that the performance of ASR systems improves when both training and testing data come from the same population. The authors explore whether a synthesizer trained on one domain can effectively generate data for another domain, potentially allowing for broader applications of a general-purpose synthesizer. However, if the performance does not transfer well, it would necessitate a domain-specific synthesizer, which poses challenges.

Key findings include that synthesized speech can enhance ASR performance through increased acoustic and lexical diversity in training data. Despite significant progress in the quality of synthesized speech, the paper notes that further advancements are needed for synthesized speech to match the effectiveness of real speech in ASR training.

The contributions of this study are summarized as follows:
1. Demonstrating the benefits of data augmentation with text-to-speech (TTS) utterances for ASR.
2. Highlighting the importance of effective and diverse speaker representations in TTS for ASR data augmentation.
3. Establishing the impact of using lexically diverse TTS utterances for ASR data augmentation.
4. Introducing a novel TTS technique, hierarchical variational autoencoder (VAE), which improves TTS training with limited multi-speaker data.

The paper also relates its findings to prior work, particularly by Li et al., who similarly explored speech synthesis for data augmentation and achieved improvements through expanding acoustic diversity. The authors build on this by investigating lexical diversity and report superior performance on challenging test sets.

Additionally, the paper discusses a joint ASR and TTS training approach known as the "speech chain," which aims to leverage the interdependence of speech production and perception, further enhancing ASR performance.

The TTS model is based on Tacotron 2, which processes input text sequences to generate mel spectrogram frames, serving as the foundation for the experiments conducted in this research.
