This paper investigates the effects of data augmentation through speech synthesis on automatic speech recognition (ASR) performance. The study reveals that synthesized utterances generated from a language model (LM) can enhance ASR outcomes, particularly when the synthesized data is lexically and acoustically diverse. The experiment utilized isolated sentences as the training material, with a test dataset significantly smaller in size. 

The results, summarized in Table 7, show that various configurations of text-to-speech (TTS) utterances lead to different word error rates (WER). Notably, the introduction of synthesized data improves performance, with in-domain TTS synthesis yielding greater benefits than out-of-domain synthesis. Specifically, performance improved from a WER of 32.9 to 30.74 with in-domain augmentation, which is comparable to adding 960 hours of out-of-domain human speech.

The paper concludes that while synthesized speech can improve ASR performance, its effectiveness is still considerably lower than that of actual human speech. The findings underscore the potential of TTS-driven data augmentation in enhancing ASR systems, particularly through increased acoustic and lexical diversity.
