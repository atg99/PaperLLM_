The referenced papers focus on advancements in neural network applications, particularly in the fields of speech synthesis and recognition. The work on transfer learning from speaker verification to multi-speaker text-to-speech synthesis highlights the potential of leveraging existing models to enhance new tasks. Graves' research on generating sequences with recurrent neural networks outlines foundational techniques in sequence modeling. Hsu et al. introduce hierarchical generative modeling, which allows for greater control in speech synthesis. Shen et al. present Lingvo, a flexible framework for sequence-to-sequence modeling, facilitating various applications. Chiu et al. discuss state-of-the-art speech recognition achieved through sequence-to-sequence models, demonstrating significant progress in the field. Chan et al.'s exploration of latent sequence decompositions contributes to understanding complex data structures. Kingma and Ba's Adam optimization method is pivotal for efficient training in deep learning. Goyal et al. address the challenges of large minibatch stochastic gradient descent, achieving rapid training times on extensive datasets. Finally, Biadsy et al. discuss methods for building large-scale language models that integrate non-linguistic signals, enhancing language processing capabilities. Collectively, these works represent significant strides in neural information processing and its applications in speech technology.
