The Information Bottleneck (IB) method seeks to find a compact representation of the input that retains relevant information for a particular task while discarding irrelevant information. In this paper, we build on these principles to optimize the mutual information between inputs and learned representations, specifically focusing on disentangling common knowledge from task-specific knowledge.

The paper introduces Knowledge Factorization (KF), a new knowledge-transfer task designed to create modular and interpretable factor networks from pretrained teacher models. This approach allows for the integration of these factor networks into multi-task networks without requiring retraining, enhancing the overall modularity and transferability of learned representations.

Key contributions include:
1. The introduction of KF as a novel task that emphasizes learning factor networks with disentangled representations.
2. A proposed solution that decomposes a pretrained teacher model into task-wise factor networks.
3. The design of an InfoMax Bottleneck objective that effectively disentangles common and task-specific representations through mutual information control.
4. Empirical validation showing strong performance and enhanced disentanglement capability across various benchmarks.

The paper situates its work within the broader context of knowledge distillation and disentangled representation learning, highlighting its unique contributions to the field.
