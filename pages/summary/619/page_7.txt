The section discusses representation factorization in neural networks, focusing on the goal of achieving statistical independence among learned representations in factor networks. This independence allows each sub-network to master task-specific knowledge while ensuring that the common representation retains as much information as possible.

To achieve this, the Infomax Bottleneck (IMB) objective is introduced, which aims to optimize mutual information (MI) between input features and their representations. The mutual information I(X;Y) quantifies the information one variable provides about another, using Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence. The IMB seeks to maximize I(X;Z), ensuring that the common knowledge feature retains comprehensive information from the input, while minimizing I(X;Tj), so that task representations contain only relevant task-specific information.

The optimization problem is formulated with constraints on the mutual information, introducing Lagrange multipliers to balance the objectives. The goal is to ensure that task representations are effective for their respective tasks while maintaining the fidelity of the common knowledge representation.

Additionally, due to the challenges in estimating mutual information for continuous variables, a variational lower bound is derived to approximate the IMB objective. This bound provides a practical way to evaluate the IMB, suggesting that the derived lower bound is an effective approximation of the true objective, achieving equality under specific conditions.
