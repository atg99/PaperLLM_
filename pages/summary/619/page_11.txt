The paper discusses the challenges of knowledge distillation (KD) in neural networks, particularly the balance problem in conventional KD methods. It introduces a novel approach called task factorization (KF), which enhances the performance of ResNet18-KD on ImageNet by 1.08% and 1.31% when learning from ResNet-50 and ResNet-34, respectively. The findings indicate that separating task-specific and task-agnostic knowledge improves performance.

The paper further evaluates the effectiveness of KF using two multi-task dense prediction datasets: NYU Depth Dataset V2 and PASCAL Context. NYUDv2 includes tasks for segmentation and monocular depth estimation, while PASCAL Context encompasses tasks such as semantic segmentation and saliency detection. The evaluation metrics used are mean intersection over union, angle mean error, and root mean square error.

Results show that multi-task baselines generally outperform single-task models on NYUDv2, but the performance degrades significantly in the PASCAL experiments due to negative transfer issues in multi-task learning (MTL). The joint optimization of multiple objectives can create conflicts between tasks, leading to reduced performance. In contrast, KF addresses this issue by maintaining structural and representational independence among different task-specific networks, resulting in superior student performance compared to other baselines.

The performance comparison on the NYUDv2 dataset highlights that the proposed method outperforms traditional single-task and multi-task models, demonstrating its efficacy in knowledge factorization for improved neural network performance.
