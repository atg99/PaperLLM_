The section presents experiments aimed at evaluating the effectiveness of factorization in neural networks, focusing on performance, modularity, and transferability. The authors set specific hyperparameters for their experiments and provide additional details in supplementary materials. 

In the synthetic evaluation, the authors test their factorized network (KF) on two datasets, dSprites and Shape3D, which consist of independent latent factors treated as multi-label classification tasks. They compare KF against four baseline methods, including single-task and multi-task models, as well as knowledge distillation approaches. The results indicate that KF achieves superior ROC-AUC scores compared to the multi-task baseline and knowledge distillation methods, while all methods performed well overall, achieving scores above 0.92. This suggests that the factorized model benefits from learning across multiple labels.

In the real image classification segment, the authors extend their evaluation to CIFAR-10 and ImageNet1K datasets, constructing Pseudo-Multi-task Datasets based on category hierarchies. The findings reinforce the advantages of factorization in enhancing predictive performance across various tasks. Overall, the experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of factor networks in maintaining competitive predictive performance while leveraging multi-task learning.
