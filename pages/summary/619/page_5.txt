The architecture consists of a common knowledge network (CKN) and multiple task-specific networks (TSNs). The CKN is responsible for learning shared representations across tasks, while the TSNs focus on capturing unique features relevant to individual tasks. This dual approach allows for effective knowledge transfer and representation learning, aligning with principles from self-supervised and contrastive learning.

Knowledge Factorization (KF) is defined as the division of a multi-task teacher network into several factorized networks, each tailored for specific tasks. By adopting this method, the model can better exploit commonalities among tasks while maintaining the distinctiveness of task-specific information. The overall goal of this work is to enhance multi-task learning by providing a structured way to manage information flow between shared and individual task representations, thereby improving performance on diverse tasks while leveraging shared knowledge.
