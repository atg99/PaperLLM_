In this paper, the authors introduce a novel knowledge-transfer approach called Knowledge Factorization (KF). This method involves decomposing a pretrained teacher network into task-disentangled factor networks that capture both task-specific and common knowledge. The factor networks can function independently or be combined to create multi-task networks, enhancing scalability. To achieve this, the authors propose an InfoMax Bottleneck objective that optimizes the mutual information between inputs and their representations, effectively disentangling common and task-specific knowledge.

Results demonstrate that using the ImageNet-CKN pretrained weights significantly improves transfer learning performance and distillation accuracy across various datasets, as shown in Table 5. The experiments indicate that the CKN factorization from the teacher network serves as an effective model for training student networks, leading to better performance compared to traditional pretrained weights.

Overall, the proposed method exhibits strong performance, robust disentanglement capabilities, and improved modularity and transferability across benchmarks.
