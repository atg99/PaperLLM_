In this paper, the authors introduce a novel knowledge-transfer task called Knowledge Factorization (KF). The main objective of KF is to modularize and assemble knowledge from a pretrained network model into several factor networks, each dedicated to a specific task while retaining task-specific knowledge. These factor networks are designed to be disentangled and can be combined without fine-tuning, akin to Lego bricks, enabling users to construct customized networks easily.

Each factor network consists of two components: a common-knowledge module, which is task-agnostic and shared among all factor networks, and a task-specific module tailored to the individual factor network. To achieve KF, the authors propose an information-theoretic objective named InfoMax-Bottleneck (IMB), which optimizes the mutual information between the learned representations and the input data.

Experimental results demonstrate that the factor networks not only perform well on dedicated tasks but also excel in disentanglement, interpretability, and modularity. Furthermore, the common-knowledge representations learned through KF show strong potential for transfer learning applications. The authors have made their code available for further research and implementation.
