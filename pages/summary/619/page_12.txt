The paper presents a performance comparison of various methods on the PASCAL dataset, focusing on single-task and multi-task learning using HRNet and ResNet architectures. The results indicate that the multi-task learning approach generally yields improved performance metrics across segmentation and normalization tasks compared to single-task methods. Notably, the proposed method demonstrates competitive results, particularly with the HRNet48 and ResNet-50 architectures, outperforming baselines in several metrics.

In the subsequent section, the authors explore the concept of knowledge factorization and its effect on disentanglement through a series of evaluations using datasets like dSprites and Shape3D. Four disentanglement metrics—DCI, MIG, FactorVAE, and SAP—are employed to assess the effectiveness of their knowledge factorization (KF) method against three baseline approaches. The results reveal that multi-task learning inherently promotes disentangled representations, with KF providing significant improvements in disentanglement metrics.

Additionally, the paper investigates representation similarity through centered kernel alignment (CKA), comparing teacher models to distilled and factorized models across multiple datasets. The analysis aims to quantify feature similarity, reinforcing the claim that knowledge decomposition enhances the model's ability to identify and represent independent factors across tasks. Overall, the findings emphasize the advantages of knowledge factorization in achieving disentangled representations and improved performance in multi-task learning settings.
