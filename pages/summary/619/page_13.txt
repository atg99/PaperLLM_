This paper discusses the factorization of knowledge in neural networks, focusing on the comparison of various disentanglement metrics and representation similarities across different model architectures. The experiments were conducted on datasets such as dSprites and Shape3D, where the effectiveness of different approaches—Single-Task Baseline, Multi-Task Baseline, Knowledge Distillation (KD), and the proposed Knowledge Factorization (KF)—was evaluated.

Key findings include that models trained on the same subtask exhibit high feature similarity, indicating that they share significant knowledge. The factorized Task-Specific Networks (TSN) demonstrated a greater ability to capture "pure" knowledge compared to Multi-Task Learning to Single-Task Learning (MTL2STL) students. The results showed that conventional distilled models retained high similarity with their peers, while factorized TSNs exhibited smaller similarities, evidencing their capability to disentangle factors across tasks.

Additionally, the study explored the benefits of common knowledge in transferring learned information to new tasks. Fine-tuning of the factorized Common Knowledge Network (CKN) on new domains, such as Caltech-UCSD Birds (CUB), was conducted to assess the effectiveness of this approach in facilitating transfer learning. The results suggest that the factorization of knowledge enhances the model's ability to generalize and transfer learning to unseen domains.
