The paper presents a comparative analysis of knowledge distillation (KD) and knowledge factorization (KF) methodologies on CIFAR-10 and ImageNet datasets. The authors evaluate the performance of various teacher-student network architectures, including ResNet and WideResNet models, by measuring the top-1 accuracy of the student networks under different task settings.

In Table 1, results indicate that while both KD and KF enhance the baseline accuracy in single-task scenarios, KD does not yield improvements in pseudo-multi-task evaluations. The paper highlights the challenges faced during pseudo-multi-task training, particularly due to imbalanced labeling that leads to low-confidence predictions when a network specializes in a single super-class. Conversely, KF employs a shared classifier knowledge network (CKN) across tasks, which mitigates this issue and results in better performance.

Table 2 further illustrates the accuracy comparisons on ImageNet, showing that KF outperforms KD in several instances, particularly in multi-task settings. The authors construct CIFAR-10 and ImageNet datasets with defined super-classes to facilitate their experiments, utilizing various ResNet architectures as teachers and MobileNetv2 as a student backbone.

Overall, the findings suggest that KF provides a more robust framework for knowledge transfer in multi-task learning scenarios compared to traditional KD methods, especially when dealing with imbalanced class distributions.
