The paper discusses a novel approach to multi-task learning through the development of a framework called KF, which constructs K factor networks, each dedicated to a specific task while leveraging shared knowledge from a multi-task teacher model T. The primary aim is to decompose the teacher's knowledge into distinct task-specific and common representations. 

To achieve this, the authors introduce two key mechanisms: structural factorization and representation factorization. Structural factorization focuses on creating functional distinctions among the sub-networks, where each factor network consists of a Common Knowledge Network (CKN) that is shared across all tasks and a Task-specific Network (TSN) that is exclusive to each task. For each input, the CKN extracts task-agnostic features, while the TSN learns task-related knowledge. This dual structure allows each factor network to utilize shared knowledge while specializing in its respective task.

The authors define a structural factorization objective that combines a supervised loss and a knowledge transfer loss to ensure that each factor network closely mimics the teacher's predictions while minimizing errors. This approach not only facilitates the creation of new combined-task models without the need for retraining but also enhances the scalability of the overall model. For instance, integrating a CKN with multiple TSNs allows for the efficient assembly of models for various classification tasks. 

Overall, the proposed framework emphasizes the importance of both common and task-specific features in multi-task learning, offering a scalable solution for developing specialized models based on a shared knowledge base.
