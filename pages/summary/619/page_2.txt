The paper discusses various approaches to Knowledge Distillation (KD), a process where knowledge from a larger, more complex model (teacher) is transferred to a smaller model (student) to enhance performance while reducing size for deployment on edge devices. The authors categorize KD into three types: 

1. Single-Task Learning to Single-Task Learning (STL2STL), which involves distilling a single-task student from a single-task teacher.
2. Multi-Task Learning to Multi-Task Learning (MTL2MTL), where a multi-task student is distilled from a multi-task teacher.
3. Sub-Knowledge Distillation, which allows for the distillation of a subset of the teacher's knowledge to the student.

While KD has shown promising results, the authors highlight significant limitations. The process is often treated as a black box, lacking transparency in how knowledge is transferred, which can lead to a lack of interpretability in the student model. Additionally, traditional KD approaches are constrained to homogeneous tasks, which restricts their applicability. 

The authors also point out the scalability issue. For instance, if a teacher model pre-trained on a large dataset like ImageNet is used to create multiple student models for different classification tasks, the process can become computationally prohibitive, as it would require numerous distillation processes for each task.

To address these challenges, the authors propose a new framework called Knowledge Factorization, aiming to improve efficiency and interpretability in the distillation process. This framework seeks to minimize the complexity and enhance the scalability of knowledge transfer, making it more feasible for practical applications.
