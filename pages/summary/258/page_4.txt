The paper presents a novel method for detecting face swapping and facial reenactment in images and videos, leveraging capsule networks. The method was evaluated on two datasets: the deepfake dataset by Afchar et al. and the FaceForensics dataset. Results indicate that the proposed Capsule-Forensics-Noise method achieved the highest accuracy in face swapping detection at both frame and video levels, outperforming existing state-of-the-art techniques. Specifically, it recorded accuracies of 95.93% and 99.23% at frame and video levels, respectively.

In facial reenactment detection, the proposed method exhibited performance comparable to leading methods and surpassed Afchar et al.’s MesoNet in video-level detection using only the first ten frames. On the FaceForensics dataset, Capsule-Forensics and its noise-augmented variant demonstrated strong results under varying compression levels.

Additionally, the method was tested against computer-generated images (CGIs) and photographic images (PIs), achieving perfect accuracy on full-size test images. The findings support the effectiveness of capsule networks in detecting a broad range of image and video manipulations, highlighting the beneficial impact of incorporating random noise during training.

The paper concludes with future work aimed at enhancing the method's robustness against adversarial attacks and mixed forms of manipulation, emphasizing the importance of addressing these challenges within the research community. The research was funded by several grants from JSPS and JST in Japan.
