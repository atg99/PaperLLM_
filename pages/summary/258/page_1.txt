The paper "CAPSULE-FORENSICS: USING CAPSULE NETWORKS TO DETECT FORGED IMAGES AND VIDEOS" by Huy H. Nguyen, Junichi Yamagishi, and Isao Echizen addresses the growing threat of forged images and videos, particularly in the context of facial authentication and misinformation. The authors highlight that advancements in media generation techniques have simplified the creation of high-quality forgeries, making it easier for attackers to develop convincing fake content using minimal resources, such as a short video or an ID photo.

The paper critiques existing detection methods, noting that many are narrowly focused on specific types of attacks and quickly become ineffective as new forgery techniques emerge. For instance, methods targeting replay attacks may not perform well against deepfake forgeries, and while some methods can detect individual forged images, others require video input for analysis.

The authors propose a novel approach utilizing capsule networks, which are typically employed for computer vision tasks, to enhance detection capabilities across various forgery scenarios, including replay attacks and both fully and partially computer-generated content. The research includes a comprehensive survey of existing techniques and extensive evaluations using four major datasets, demonstrating that the proposed capsule network method outperforms traditional approaches in detecting a wider range of image and video forgeries.

This work represents a pioneering application of capsule networks in digital forensics, aiming to provide a more robust and adaptable solution to the challenges posed by evolving forgery techniques.
