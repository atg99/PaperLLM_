Wang et al. provide a comprehensive survey of reinforcement learning (RL), focusing on deep RL algorithms and their advancements. Unlike previous surveys that concentrated on specific branches, this paper systematically categorizes various deep RL methods and highlights their relationships and development trends. Key contributions of the paper include:

1. A detailed review of state-of-the-art RL methods, categorized into model-free, model-based, and advanced methods.
2. A systematic summary of advanced RL developments, divided into exploration, inverse RL, and transfer RL.
3. An analysis of RL applications across fields such as robotics, natural language processing, and computer systems.
4. Identification of open research problems, such as the data hunger issue and the quest for artificial general intelligence (AGI), along with proposed directions for future studies.

The paper emphasizes that deep RL is still in its infancy, primarily focusing on deterministic and static environments. Model-free RL is highlighted as a common approach due to its simplicity and availability of resources. It is divided into two scenarios: value function-based RL and policy gradient-based RL.

The section on value function-based RL discusses the Deep Q-Network (DQN), which utilizes convolutional neural networks and a variant of Q-learning. DQN addresses challenges in high-dimensional state representations and incorporates techniques like experience replay and target networks to enhance stability and performance. Experimental results indicate that DQN outperforms previous algorithms in various tasks, particularly in Atari 2600 games, showcasing its adaptability.

The paper also mentions several improvements to DQN, such as the Double Deep Q-Network (DDQN), which mitigates overestimation bias in Q-learning. Overall, the authors aim to provide a thorough understanding of RL's current state and future directions.
