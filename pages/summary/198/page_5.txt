Model-based reinforcement learning (RL) involves learning transition dynamics to predict the next state after performing an action in a given state. This approach allows for more efficient learning compared to model-free methods, as it can derive value functions or policies without continuous interaction with the environment. However, it faces challenges related to model identification and accuracy.

The paper categorizes model-based RL into three scenarios based on the availability of simulators and the cost of simulation versus training. If simulation costs are low, online-policy or policy-gradient algorithms like TRPO, PPO, and A3C are preferred due to their ease of tuning and reliability. Conversely, if simulation is expensive, off-policy algorithms such as DDPG and SQL might be more suitable. In the absence of a simulator, the choice hinges on the acceptable waiting period for training, with impatience favoring model-based methods like guided policy search.

The section also discusses the choice between global and local dynamics models for model-based algorithms. Global models can be computationally inexpensive but often struggle with numerical stability, while local models may offer more accuracy but at a higher runtime cost. A comparative table illustrates the sample requirements and computation time for various RL algorithms, providing a starting point for selecting appropriate methods based on specific scenarios.
