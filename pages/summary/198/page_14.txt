The paper by Wang et al. discusses advancements in low-resource neural machine translation (NMT) and reinforcement learning (RL) applications across various domains, including dialogue systems and computer systems. The authors highlight how sharing lexical and sentence representations from high-resource languages can benefit low-resource languages, enabling effective NMT training with minimal data. They reference Gu et al.'s extension of a deep meta-RL algorithm, MAML, which allows adaptation to low-resource languages through multilingual tasks, demonstrating superior performance compared to previous methods.

In the context of dialogue systems, the paper notes the use of modified RL algorithms to enhance chatbots' ability to simulate human-like conversations. Additionally, challenges in text-to-speech (TTS) systems are addressed, particularly the difficulty of generalizing to new speakers with limited audio samples. Adaptive TTS methods utilizing MAML are presented as potential solutions.

The discussion then shifts to RL in computer systems, emphasizing its applicability to complex problems such as dynamic job scheduling and data center traffic optimization. Various RL methods, including policy gradient algorithms and deep deterministic policy gradient (DDPG), are highlighted for their effectiveness in optimizing performance in these environments.

Despite the promising developments in RL, the authors acknowledge significant challenges, particularly the inefficiency of sample usage. They note that training models often requires an extensive number of samples, leading to long training times, especially in environments like Atari games. This inefficiency is compounded in model-based RL, which struggles with sample requirements and planning challenges. The paper concludes by emphasizing the need for continued research to enhance sample efficiency and address these fundamental difficulties in the field.
