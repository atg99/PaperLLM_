The paper by Wang et al. discusses various exploration strategies in reinforcement learning, particularly in complex environments where traditional methods may falter. 

One notable approach is the optimistic initial value method, which enhances exploration by increasing the initial values of the value function rather than counting state frequencies. The gradient bandit algorithm utilizes entropy as a measure of uncertainty, adjusting action selection based on received rewards, which is particularly useful in high-dimensional spaces where states are rarely repeated. 

The concept of pseudo-counts, introduced by Bellemare et al., generalizes exploration bonuses for non-tabular reinforcement learning agents. Subsequent advancements in this area include improvements from Ostrovski et al. using neural density models, hash-based counting by Tang et al., and exemplar model exploration by Fu et al., which compares new states to previously visited ones to enhance exploration.

Additionally, the paper outlines posterior sampling exploration methods, such as Thompson sampling and Bootstrapped DQN. Thompson sampling utilizes a Beta distribution for reward probabilities, while Bootstrapped DQN employs multiple networks to randomize value functions, allowing for a balanced approach to learning and exploration in complex state/action spaces.

Lastly, the paper highlights information gain exploration, which leverages the mutual information between actions and observations to guide decision-making. Information-directed sampling selects actions that maximize learning, while variational information maximizing exploration (VIME) reduces reliance on sparse rewards by approximating the dynamic model through variational inference in Bayesian neural networks. This approach encourages agents to explore more effectively in challenging environments.
