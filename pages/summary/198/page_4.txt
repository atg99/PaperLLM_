The paper discusses the limitations of traditional deterministic policy gradient methods like DDPG in complex environments with noise, highlighting the need for randomness in policy execution. It critiques model-free reinforcement learning (RL) algorithms such as TRPO and PPO for their high sample requirements, making them expensive for learning effective policies, particularly as task complexity increases.

The authors present Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) as a promising solution, which leverages off-policy updates and a maximum entropy framework to enhance performance and sample efficiency. Key advantages of SAC include its ability to provide effective initializations for complex tasks, improved exploration under multimodal rewards, and increased robustness and generalization capabilities.

Furthermore, the paper addresses improvements in policy gradient methods through trust region approaches, which stabilize policy updates amidst unstable data. TRPO is noted for its performance gains through the use of total variation divergence, though it suffers from sample inefficiency. The introduction of Actor-Critic with Experience Replay (ACER) offers a solution by maintaining a sliding average of past policies, while methods like Kronecker-factored trust region and constrained policy optimization also aim to enhance performance.

The authors also discuss the simplicity and practicality of Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) despite its potential for sample inefficiency. Distributed PPO is highlighted for its ability to leverage multiple workers for data collection and gradient calculation, enhancing performance in rich environments. Lastly, Path Consistency Learning (PCL) and its improved variant Trust-PCL are presented as methods that combine on-policy and off-policy benefits, enhancing exploration and sample efficiency through constrained optimization.
