The paper by Wang et al. discusses various advancements in deep meta-reinforcement learning (meta-RL) methodologies, highlighting the interaction process between an agent and its environment. The authors detail how fresh initial states are drawn for each episode in a Markov Decision Process (MDP) and emphasize the role of recurrent neural networks (RNNs) in training agents with historical trajectories. 

Key contributions include SNAIL, which modifies RNN architecture with LSTM and integrates one-dimensional convolutions and soft attention. E-RL2 introduces an indicator to account for the impact of initial sampling distributions on meta-rewards, promoting broader sampling for better performance. ReBAL shifts the focus from action policies to learning transition models, although recurrent models can struggle with complex tasks due to their data demands.

The paper also highlights model-agnostic meta-learning (MAML) as a significant approach, enabling adaptation across learning tasks using a fixed optimizer. Variants like E-MAML and MAESN aim to enhance exploration by differentiating through sampling distributions and incorporating structured stochasticity. Other methods include CAVIA, which learns embeddings during training, and SMILe, which integrates inverse reinforcement learning efficiently.

Additionally, the meta-MDP framework proposes a structured approach to exploration by separating exploration and exploitation policies, contributing to lifelong learning. The authors conclude that deep meta-RL is a promising area of research, with diverse strategies being explored to improve training and adaptation efficiency. The relationships between various algorithms in this domain are visually represented, illustrating the evolution and interconnections among these approaches.
