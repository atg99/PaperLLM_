The paper by Wang et al. provides a comprehensive review of deep reinforcement learning (RL), highlighting the challenges and advancements in the field. It emphasizes the critical role of a well-defined reward function, noting the complexities involved in designing such functions, including the need for suitable priors, perfect state knowledge, and clear problem definitions. The authors point out that excessive focus on reward function design can introduce biases and that even effective rewards may not prevent agents from getting stuck in local optima. 

The paper further discusses issues of overfitting and instability in RL, noting that agents often struggle to generalize across different environments. It highlights the sensitivity of RL to initial conditions and training dynamics, with specific challenges associated with various algorithm categories, such as fitted Q methods and policy gradient methods. The authors argue for the necessity of algorithms that guarantee improvement and convergence, the introduction of supervision signals to enhance exploration, and the potential of inverse RL and imitation learning to mitigate reward function limitations.

Additionally, the authors propose that deep meta-RL could be a promising approach towards achieving artificial general intelligence (AGI), advocating for increased exploration capabilities and the integration of meta-RL with inverse RL. The paper concludes with a summary of recent deep RL advancements categorized into exploration methods, inverse RL, and transfer RL, while also discussing applications in fields like robotics, natural language processing, and computer systems. Finally, the authors identify open challenges that present future research directions in deep RL.
