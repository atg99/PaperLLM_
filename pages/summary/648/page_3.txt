The paper presents a framework for graph representation and processing through two main techniques: graph coarsening and EigenPooling. The framework begins with a single node representation that evolves through fully connected layers to generate predictions. 

In the graph coarsening section, the authors describe a method for partitioning a graph into non-overlapping subgraphs using spectral clustering, allowing for control over the number of subgraphs and thus the pooling ratio. Each subgraph is treated as a supernode, and the relationships between them are established. The authors define mathematical operators to relate the original graph's nodes to those in the subgraphs, enabling the extraction of signals specific to each subgraph and the construction of adjacency matrices that represent connections both within and between subgraphs.

The coarsened graph, denoted as Gcoar, is characterized by an adjacency matrix that encodes its network structure. The subsequent section introduces EigenPooling, which aims to derive node features that encapsulate information from both the network structure and the node features of the original graph. Unlike traditional pooling methods that overlook graph structure, EigenPooling leverages graph spectral theory and the eigenvectors of the Laplacian matrix of subgraphs to summarize the features of nodes within each subgraph. 

The paper emphasizes the importance of preserving crucial information from the original graph signal during the pooling process, setting the stage for enhanced learning and classification capabilities through the stacked layers of GCN, graph coarsening, and EigenPooling. Overall, the proposed methods aim to improve graph representation learning by considering both structural and feature-based information.
