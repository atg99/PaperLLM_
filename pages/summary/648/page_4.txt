The paper discusses the representation of graph signals using a spatial view where each node in a graph is associated with a scalar or vector value. It introduces the concept of the graph Fourier transform, which leverages the Laplacian matrix to analyze the smoothness of graph signals. The smoothness is quantified using a specific formula, indicating that a lower value corresponds to a smoother signal. The Laplacian matrix, being real symmetric and semi-positive definite, has orthonormal eigenvectors known as graph Fourier modes, which relate to real non-negative eigenvalues. The graph Fourier transform enables the transformation of a graph signal into the spectral domain, and the inverse transform restores it back to the spatial representation.

The paper further explores the design of pooling operators based on graph Fourier transforms, which allows for the aggregation of graph signals from a graph to its coarsened version. This involves up-sampling eigenvectors from subgraphs and organizing them into pooling operators. The pooling process is mathematically described, and the final pooled results can be efficiently computed by selecting a subset of pooling operators.

In the theoretical analysis section, the paper examines EigenPooling from both local and global perspectives. It demonstrates that the pooling operation retains useful information for subsequent graph convolutional network (GCN) layers and confirms that EigenGCN is permutation invariant, which is crucial for graph classification tasks. The analysis emphasizes the local effectiveness of pooling operators on specific subgraphs while ensuring that the overall representation remains informative.
