The paper discusses the properties and effectiveness of the EigenGCN framework for graph classification, focusing on its energy preservation and permutation invariance. 

Lemma 3.4 establishes that the ℓ2 norm of a graph signal is equal to the summation of the ℓ2 norms of pooled signals, indicating energy preservation when using Nmax filters. Theorem 3.5 further asserts that even with a reduced number of filters (H), the majority of energy and information can still be retained, as the ratio of the norms is preserved.

The paper then examines the permutation invariance of EigenGCN, demonstrating that the framework produces consistent representations regardless of input permutations of the adjacency matrix and node feature matrix. This is shown through the invariance of key components: GCN, graph coarsening, and EigenPooling, thus ensuring the capability of EigenGCN for graph classification.

In the experimental section, the authors aim to evaluate EigenGCN's performance against state-of-the-art methods across six benchmark datasets, including protein and chemical compound datasets. The experiments are designed to assess the effectiveness of EigenPooling and the reliability of using a limited number of filters.

Overall, the findings underscore EigenGCN’s potential in efficiently learning graph representations while maintaining critical characteristics like energy preservation and permutation invariance.
