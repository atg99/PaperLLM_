The paper introduces EigenPooling, a novel pooling operator designed to effectively summarize information from subgraphs in graph neural networks (GNNs). The challenges addressed include the variability in the number of nodes across subgraphs and their differing structures, which complicate the design of a generalized pooling operator. EigenPooling utilizes the eigenvectors of subgraphs to maintain consistent sizes and capture local structures, thus enhancing supernode representations.

The authors propose a framework called EigenGCN, which integrates EigenPooling with graph convolutional layers for graph classification tasks. Key contributions include the introduction of EigenPooling, theoretical insights on its operation from local and global perspectives, and its incorporation into existing GNN architectures. Extensive experiments validate the effectiveness of EigenPooling on various real-world graph classification benchmarks.

The framework aims to learn graph representations by combining convolutional and pooling layers, where graphs are represented as G={E,V}, with an adjacency matrix A and node feature matrix X. The pooling process involves coarsening the graph into subgraphs treated as supernodes and summarizing node information through EigenPooling. This hierarchical approach allows for the extraction of meaningful graph-level representations while preserving structural information, ultimately facilitating accurate predictions in graph classification tasks.
