This paper introduces EigenPooling, a novel pooling operator based on local graph Fourier transform, designed to enhance the extraction of subgraph information by leveraging both node features and subgraph structure. The authors argue that traditional graph neural network models often struggle with the hierarchical summarization of node representations for graph classification tasks, as these models typically output flat representations for all nodes. Existing global pooling methods are criticized for neglecting critical structural information within the graph.

The proposed EigenPooling operator aims to address this issue by allowing for hierarchical learning of graph representations through a two-step process: coarsening the graph by grouping nodes into supernodes and learning supernode representations iteratively. This method retains essential structural information, which is often lost in mean or max pooling approaches.

The paper provides a theoretical analysis of EigenPooling from both local and global perspectives and integrates this pooling operator into a new graph neural network framework called EigenGCN. The effectiveness of EigenGCN is demonstrated through comprehensive graph classification experiments conducted on six standard benchmarks, where it achieves state-of-the-art performance in most cases. Overall, the research highlights the potential of combining spectral techniques with graph neural networks to improve graph representation learning.
