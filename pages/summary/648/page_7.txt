The paper presents a comprehensive evaluation of various graph neural network models for graph classification, focusing on the efficacy of hierarchical pooling methods. The datasets analyzed include ENZYMES, PROTEINS, D&D, NCI1, NCI109, and MUTAG, with varying characteristics such as the number of graphs and classes.

Several baseline models are discussed, including GCN, GraphSage, SET2SET, DGCNN, and Diff-pool, alongside the proposed EigenGCN framework, which incorporates hierarchical pooling through EigenPooling. Experimental settings involve splitting datasets into training, validation, and testing sets, with performance evaluated using accuracy as the metric.

Key findings indicate that models employing hierarchical pooling, specifically Diff-pool and EigenGCN, generally outperform those without such mechanisms. EigenGCN demonstrates superior performance across most datasets, emphasizing the importance of hierarchical pooling in enhancing graph representation learning. The paper also notes that while incorporating more eigenvectors in EigenPooling often leads to better performance, it can sometimes result in diminished effectiveness due to the potential inclusion of noise.

Additionally, the paper investigates the distribution of Fourier coefficients in graph signals, revealing that a significant amount of information is concentrated in the initial coefficients. This observation supports the use of a limited number of filters in EigenPooling, suggesting that the proposed framework can effectively capture essential graph features while maintaining performance.

In conclusion, EigenPooling is shown to significantly improve graph classification performance, positioning EigenGCN as a state-of-the-art approach in this domain.
