The paper discusses the pooling operation on a graph signal defined on a subgraph, represented through a graph Fourier transform. It establishes that the Fourier coefficient of a Fourier mode characterizes the smoothness of the graph signal. By focusing on the first H coefficients, which capture the smoothest parts of the signal, the paper highlights the preservation of information during reconstruction. Theorem 3.1 quantifies this preservation by comparing the norms of the coefficients before and after reconstruction, indicating that using only the first H coefficients retains most of the signal's information.

The paper then shifts to a global perspective of eigenpooling, where pooling operators function as a filterbank. It presents two key properties: perfect reconstruction and energy/information preservation. Lemma 3.2 confirms that the original graph signal can be perfectly reconstructed from the filtered signals when using the maximum number of filters. Lemma 3.3 asserts the orthogonality of the pooling operator columns, which leads to the conclusion that the ℓ2 norm of the original graph signal equals the sum of the ℓ2 norms of the pooled signals, thus ensuring energy preservation.

Overall, the study emphasizes the effectiveness of graph Fourier transforms and pooling operations in maintaining the integrity of graph signals, enabling efficient processing for applications like graph classification.
