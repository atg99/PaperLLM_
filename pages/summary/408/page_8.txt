The paper discusses advancements in the use of YOLO-based technologies for Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs), particularly focusing on object detection and localization in complex environments. Ruan et al. explored a deep learning method for detecting and localizing fog droplets during autonomous aerial refueling, employing the YOLO algorithm for cone detection and utilizing a monocular camera for precise localization. Their results demonstrated effective cone identification and localization within a range of 2.5 to 45 meters.

Yang et al. investigated real-time pedestrian detection and tracking using a UAV, which transmitted video streams to a ground station equipped with YOLOv2. Their experiments highlighted the robustness of the method under varying brightness and interference, indicating its stability for pedestrian tracking.

The paper notes that early studies primarily focused on basic applications due to the nascent state of technology fusion, but these foundational works provide valuable insights for future research in YOLO-based UAV technologies (YBUT). The evolution of YBUT has led to the development of UAVs equipped with high-performance processors capable of real-time object detection and classification.

Further advancements include Zhang et al.'s embedding of the YOLOv3 algorithm into the NVIDIA Jetson TX1 platform, demonstrating real-time pedestrian detection feasibility on resource-limited UAVs. Alam et al. proposed a cost-effective aerial surveillance system that offloads heavy computational tasks to the cloud, achieving significantly faster target detection and reduced latency compared to traditional methods. This ongoing research emphasizes the innovative integration of YOLO algorithms into UAV systems, enhancing their operational capabilities in various applications.
