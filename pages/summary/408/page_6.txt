The paper discusses the evolution and application of the YOLO algorithm in conjunction with Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs), referred to as YBUT. Initially proposed in 2016, the YOLO algorithm was first implemented with UAVs in 2017 by Jiang et al. This integration has led to significant advancements and a growing body of research, transforming what began as exploratory experiments into a prominent academic focus. 

The paper presents survey results indicating that popular research topics in YBUT are predominantly in technical studies, engineering, and transportation, with both English and Chinese journals showing a strong interest in these sectors. The development of YBUT has progressed alongside improvements in UAV technology, moving from manual remote control to automated computer control, and advancing image recognition capabilities from traditional systems to real-time onboard processing.

The early development of YBUT was characterized by cross-disciplinary technological fusion, with roots tracing back to UAV research initiated in the 1920s. This technology has since found successful applications across various fields, including agriculture and surveillance, demonstrating its versatility and potential for future growth. The paper highlights the increasing practical applications of YBUT and the ongoing interest from researchers in further exploring its capabilities.
