The paper discusses advancements in the integration of the YOLO (You Only Look Once) algorithm with Unmanned Aerial Vehicle (UAV) technology, particularly through the development of the new generation of YBUT (Yet Another UAV Technology). Despite the limited computational resources of the onboard processors, practical experiments have demonstrated the potential of this integration, as highlighted by Zhang et al. The feasibility of implementing real-time pedestrian detection using the YOLOv3 algorithm on resource-constrained platforms was successfully tested using the NVIDIA Jetson TX1.

To address computational limitations, Alam et al. proposed a cost-effective aerial surveillance system that offloads intensive Tiny-YOLO tasks to the cloud, achieving significant improvements in processing speed—up to six times faster than other methods—while also reducing latency and resource consumption. Similar efforts were noted in studies by Dimithe et al.

Further advancements were made by Cao et al., who introduced a target detection and tracking method utilizing both the YOLO algorithm and a PID controller with the NVIDIA Jetson TX2. This method effectively identified and tracked objects in real-time, translating pixel coordinates from the camera image to actual spatial coordinates.

Additionally, Doukhi et al. demonstrated a visual SLAM (Simultaneous Localization and Mapping) approach using a UAV equipped with a high-performance embedded processor and the YOLOv3 algorithm for target detection, achieving successful tracking without relying on external positioning sensors. Afi fi et al. presented a robust framework for multi-scene pedestrian detection, leveraging YOLOv3 on the NVIDIA Jetson TX2.

Overall, the research indicates a strong future direction for the integration of YOLO algorithms with UAV technology, aiming for enhanced performance and practical applications in real-time target detection and tracking.
