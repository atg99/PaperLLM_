The paper examines advancements in UAV (Unmanned Aerial Vehicle) technology, particularly focusing on object detection frameworks utilizing the YOLO (You Only Look Once) algorithm. Experimental results indicate that improvements to the YOLOv3 algorithm, particularly through enhancements like YOLOv3-tiny, have led to significant performance increases, including an 86.1% reduction in model size and a 19.2% rise in average precision. This makes the improved algorithm more suitable for low-end embedded processors in UAV applications.

The integration of flight control systems, such as the Pixhawk4 and PID algorithms, with object detection capabilities allows for effective real-time tracking of targets. Studies show that using high-performance processors, such as the Nvidia Jetson TX2, enables the UAV to successfully perform tasks like visual SLAM (Simultaneous Localization and Mapping) without relying on external positioning systems.

Despite these advances, challenges remain regarding the computational limitations of current embedded processors, which hinder performance in complex applications. The paper notes a trend where researchers often utilize UAVs for data collection while employing high-performance computers for processing, thereby driving forward the research and application of UAV technology in various fields. The ongoing evolution of UAV capabilities, particularly in load capacity, further supports the development of specialized applications.
