The paper discusses advancements in the application of YOLO (You Only Look Once) models for various tasks involving drones, specifically focusing on object classification and damage assessment. Arnold et al. conducted research on a dispersed autonomous swarm of UAVs using YOLO, showing effective target identification even at a distance of 25 meters from buildings. Jing et al. introduced a neural network based on YOLOv5s-ViT-BiFPN to evaluate damage to rural houses after natural disasters using drone imagery.

A comprehensive overview of various studies utilizing YOLO technologies is provided, highlighting their applications across different fields such as urban search and rescue, wildlife monitoring, and construction safety. The table summarizes key references, types of objects targeted, metrics used, and purposes of the papers, indicating that YOLO models have been successfully employed to enhance detection and tracking capabilities in diverse scenarios.

The paper concludes that there is substantial potential for further development and optimization of these technologies, suggesting that expanding successful applications could lead to significant advancements in the industry and societal benefits.
