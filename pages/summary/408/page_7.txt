The paper discusses the evolution and application of the YOLO (You Only Look Once) algorithm in conjunction with UAV (Unmanned Aerial Vehicle) technology, specifically focusing on its use in vehicle detection and monitoring. Initially proposed in 2016, YOLO has undergone several enhancements, establishing itself as a leader in object recognition due to its speed and accuracy.

The application of YOLO-based UAV technology (YBUT) began in 2017, where UAVs were utilized for data acquisition, followed by processing through YOLO algorithms for object detection and classification. Early research, particularly by Jiang et al., integrated thermal infrared and visible-light imaging sensors on UAVs, leading to improved vehicle detection accuracy through data fusion and deep learning techniques.

Subsequent studies, such as those by Xu et al., introduced modifications to the YOLOv2 model to enhance small vehicle detection capabilities, demonstrating significant improvements in localization accuracy. Further advancements were made by Ruan et al. and Yang et al., who applied YOLO for drogue detection in UAV refueling and real-time pedestrian tracking, showcasing the algorithm's robustness in complex environments.

Overall, the paper underscores the progress in YBUT, highlighting the transition from traditional computer systems to onboard embedded systems, which enable real-time detection and classification of objects. The ongoing research continues to drive innovation in this field, leveraging the increasing computational power of UAVs and improving the efficacy of YOLO-based applications.
