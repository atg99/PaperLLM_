The paper discusses the integration of Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) with the YOLO (You Only Look Once) algorithm to enhance object detection capabilities in various applications. UAVs, defined as unmanned flying devices that can operate autonomously or be manually controlled, are increasingly replacing manned aircraft across numerous industries. The paper highlights the challenges faced by UAVs in complex scenarios, particularly in maintaining effective communication with ground control, which may hinder their technological advancement.

The YOLO algorithm, known for its classification and regression-based object detection, is noted for its simple structure, small model size, and rapid computational speed. The paper emphasizes the potential of combining UAV technology with the YOLO algorithm, creating a new technology termed YOLO-based UAV technology (YBUT). This combination allows UAVs to undertake novel tasks and increases the utility of YOLO in diverse scenarios, potentially enhancing productivity in various sectors.

As UAV technology matures, it is projected that by 2025, there will be approximately 2.68 million UAVs globally, with a market size of about USD 5.3 billion. The paper suggests that deploying YOLO algorithms on these UAVs could significantly enhance their value and effectiveness in real-world applications, further facilitating advancements in daily life and industry productivity. The ongoing evolution of the YOLO algorithm, with multiple versions released since its inception, underscores its adaptability and relevance in the field of drone technology.
