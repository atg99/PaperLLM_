The paper discusses the integration of YOLO algorithms in unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) for enhanced object identification, highlighting the growing popularity of this technology in various fields. As UAV technology and algorithm performance improve, the applications of YOLO-based UAV technology (YBUT) are expected to expand significantly in areas like engineering, transportation, agriculture, and automation.

In engineering, the paper emphasizes the transition from manual operations to machine-assisted processes, which can alleviate labor pressures and reduce human error. YBUT has been effectively implemented in tasks such as transmission line detection, building surface inspection, moving target tracking, gauge display reading, photovoltaic module detection, and building classification. Notably, research indicates that transmission line detection is a favored area for YBUT applications, where various components like power line poles and insulators can be identified and classified accurately in complex environments.

For instance, a study by Bao et al. introduced the PMA-YOLO network, achieving a 93.8% accuracy in detecting abnormal vibration dampers using UAVs. This advancement lays the groundwork for developing intelligent power systems. Additionally, Alsanad et al. proposed an improved YOLOv3 algorithm, which showed efficacy in detecting small UAVs in low-altitude airspace, contributing to anti-drone efforts.

Overall, the paper illustrates the transformative potential of YBUT in engineering applications, showcasing its ability to enhance operational efficiency and safety.
