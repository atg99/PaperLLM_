The paper discusses various algorithms and techniques employed in multi-objective optimization for Efficient Neural Architecture Search (ENAS). Prominent algorithms like NSGA-II and MOEA/D are highlighted for their role in finding Pareto-optimal solutions, with adaptations made for ENAS applications. Notable contributions include Baldeon et al.'s use of a penalty-based boundary intersection in MOEA/D, and LEMONADE's division of objectives into expensive and cheap evaluations to enhance efficiency. 

The paper also explores Swarm Intelligence (SI) techniques, particularly Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) and Ant Colony Optimization (ACO), noting advancements such as gradient-priority PSO and the introduction of role-specific ant agents to improve architecture generation. Research in multi-objective SI for ENAS is still emerging, with studies demonstrating the application of multi-objective PSO and other methods for neural network pruning.

Additionally, the paper discusses other evolutionary computation techniques for ENAS, including Differential Evolution (DE) and hybrid models combining PSO and Genetic Algorithms (GA), emphasizing their effectiveness in architecture optimization. 

The final section addresses the critical issue of evaluation efficiency in ENAS, which is often resource-intensive. The paper presents strategies to mitigate evaluation costs, such as weight inheritance, early stopping, and reduced training sets. It emphasizes the necessity of developing innovative methods to decrease evaluation time and reliance on extensive computational resources, ultimately leading to more accessible and efficient ENAS methodologies.
