The block-based encoding space, on the other hand, groups layers into blocks, where each block may consist of multiple layers functioning together. This approach allows for more complex architectures by treating groups of layers as single units, which can simplify the search for optimal configurations.

The cell-based encoding space further abstracts the architecture by focusing on smaller units, or cells, which can represent various operations and connections within the network. This method offers flexibility in designing architectures while reducing the complexity of the search space.

Topology-based encoding spaces, unlike the previous categories, emphasize the connections between units rather than the internal configurations of the units themselves. This approach can lead to innovative architectures by allowing the exploration of different connectivity patterns.

Constraints play a crucial role in shaping the encoding space. They limit the search space, guiding the evolutionary algorithms toward promising architectures while preventing the exploration of unfeasible configurations. A well-defined set of constraints can lead to efficient searches for effective architectures, while a lack of constraints may result in an overwhelming number of mediocre designs.
