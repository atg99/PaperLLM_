This paper provides a comprehensive review of Evolutionary Neural Architecture Search (EC-based NAS) methods, highlighting their significance in automating the design of Deep Neural Networks (DNNs). The authors emphasize that while DNNs have achieved remarkable success across various applications, their architectures are often manually crafted, a process that is labor-intensive and requires specialized expertise. 

Neural Architecture Search (NAS) emerges as a solution to automate this design process, but existing literature lacks a thorough summary of EC-based NAS algorithms. The authors analyze over 200 recent papers, discussing core components, design principles, and justifications for these algorithms. They also address current challenges in the field, such as complex constraints and computational expenses, to pave the way for future research.

The paper outlines that NAS can be framed as an optimization problem where the goal is to minimize a performance measure of potential architectures. Traditional weight optimization techniques are effective, but finding optimal architectures remains a complex challenge due to the lack of continuous functions to guide the process. The authors categorize existing NAS algorithms based on the optimizers used, marking the evolution of research in this area since the initial work by Google in 2016.

Overall, the paper serves as a critical resource for understanding the landscape of EC-based NAS methods, their design considerations, and the challenges that lie ahead for researchers in this rapidly evolving domain.
