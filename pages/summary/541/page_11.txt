The paper discusses various methods to optimize Neural Architecture Search (NAS) by reducing evaluation time. Key techniques include weight inheritance, early stopping policies, reduced training sets, reduced populations, and population memory.

Weight inheritance allows new neural network individuals to retain certain weights from their parents, minimizing the need for training from scratch. This concept enhances efficiency by enabling knowledge transfer, particularly in modified architectures. Early stopping policies, widely used in NAS, help conserve resources by halting training for underperforming architectures. However, caution is advised as premature stopping can lead to inaccurate performance assessments.

Using a reduced training set, which mirrors the properties of a larger dataset, can also expedite evaluations. Researchers have successfully trained promising architectures on smaller datasets before applying them to larger ones. The reduced population method is unique to Evolutionary NAS (ENAS), as it strategically decreases the population size during evolution to maintain a balance between exploration and efficiency. Dynamic population reduction has been employed to enhance global search capabilities while shortening evolution time.

Population memory allows for the reuse of architectural information from previously evaluated individuals, thus avoiding redundant evaluations. This method can improve efficiency but may also risk excluding well-performing architectures from future generations.

The paper also highlights other effective strategies, such as utilizing one-shot models that train a single SuperNet to represent multiple architectures, thereby significantly reducing computational demands. Overall, these methods collectively aim to streamline the evaluation process in NAS, enhancing both efficiency and effectiveness.
