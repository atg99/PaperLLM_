The crossover operator is crucial in evolutionary algorithms for neural architecture search, as it allows for the generation of diverse offspring, enhancing the search for promising architectures. In contrast, relying solely on mutation leads to a gradual exploration of the search space, which may not yield effective results without extensive computation. The evolutionary computation (EC) approach is noted for its effectiveness in practical applications, particularly in neural architecture search (ENAS), by embedding domain knowledge into the search process.

Scalability is a significant concern for ENAS methods, as performance verification on larger datasets like ImageNet is often limited due to the high computational demands. Researchers often use smaller datasets, such as CIFAR-10, for initial testing, which may lead to overfitting. To address this, proxy methods or down-scaled versions of large datasets are employed.

Efficient evaluation methods are essential to reduce computational costs while balancing evaluation accuracy. Current ENAS methods strive to shorten fitness evaluation time, but many still require substantial computational resources, making them less accessible. The lack of standardized assessment criteria for search acceleration methods poses a challenge for further advancements.

Interpretability remains a critical issue in deep learning, particularly for convolutional neural networks (CNNs). While some research has aimed to enhance interpretability through genetic programming (GP) for automatically designing neural networks, most work has focused on shallow architectures. There is a need for methods that can evolve deeper networks while maintaining interpretability, potentially through the use of deep-GP techniques.
