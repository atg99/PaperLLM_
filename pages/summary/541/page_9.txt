The paper discusses advancements in evolutionary algorithms (EAs) for neural architecture search (NAS), particularly focusing on mutation and crossover techniques. Lorenzo et al. proposed a Gaussian mutation method that uses Gaussian regression to direct mutations towards high-fitness regions in the search space. Maziarz et al. utilized recurrent neural networks (RNNs) to guide mutation operations, sampling from distributions inferred by the RNN rather than randomly. Various strategies to maintain population diversity post-mutation are also explored, including variable mutation probabilities and force mutation.

To address the computational expense of evolutionary neural architecture search (ENAS), Zhang et al. introduced exchange mutation, which swaps gene positions without introducing new layers, preserving existing weights. Chen et al. presented network morphisms, which modify deep neural networks (DNNs) while maintaining their functional performance, allowing for the creation of wider or deeper networks without performance loss. However, these morphisms can only increase network capacity, which may not be suitable for devices with limited resources. To tackle this, approximate network morphisms were proposed to allow for reduced architectures.

For crossover operations, single-point crossover remains popular but is limited to equal-length individuals. New crossover methods for variable-length individuals have been developed, including a disruptive crossover that swaps entire clusters of layers and Simulated Binary Crossover (SBX), which combines encoded parameters from matched layers.

The paper also highlights the growing interest in multi-objective ENAS, which simultaneously optimizes for classification accuracy and parameter count, addressing the conflict between complex architectures requiring significant resources and the constraints of devices like mobile phones. The weighted summation method is a common approach to convert multi-objective problems into single-objective ones, though it may introduce bias due to manually defined weights. Overall, the paper provides insights into the evolution of mutation and crossover strategies in ENAS, emphasizing the importance of balancing performance with computational efficiency.
