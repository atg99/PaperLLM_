The paper discusses the challenges and methodologies in the design of evolutionary operators for deep neural network (DNN) architectures, particularly focusing on the encoding strategies used in evolutionary neural architecture search (ENAS). One significant issue highlighted is the potential for over-depth architectures due to the flexibility of variable-length encoding, which can complicate fitness evaluations. The paper categorizes methods that utilize a maximal length for variable-length encoding as fixed-length strategies.

It distinguishes between two key aspects of encoding: configurations of basic units (such as layers and blocks) and the connections between these units. Different basic units have varying configurations, with CNNs serving as an example where multiple parameters are involved. The flexibility of cell configurations compared to block configurations is emphasized, noting that cells can be derived from combinations of primitive layers.

The paper also details the encoding of connections within neural network architectures, differentiating between linear and non-linear architectures. Linear architectures consist of sequential basic units, while non-linear architectures incorporate skip or loop connections. The encoding approaches for non-linear architectures are outlined, including the use of adjacency matrices and directed graphs to represent connections.

The population updating process in ENAS is also addressed, noting that different evolutionary computation (EC) methods lead to varying population updating strategies. The paper identifies that evolutionary algorithm (EA)-based ENAS methods are prevalent, with genetic algorithms (GA) being particularly favored due to their effective architecture representation. The paper concludes by discussing the selection strategies in EA-based ENAS and provides a summary of the state of the field.
