The paper discusses the categorization of evolutionary algorithms (EAs) and selection strategies in the context of evolutionary neural architecture search (ENAS). It presents a detailed classification of different types of neural networks such as Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN), Deep Belief Networks (DBN), Recurrent Neural Networks (RNN), and Autoencoders (AE), along with their corresponding references.

In terms of selection strategies for population updating in EA-based ENAS, the paper identifies five main categories: elitism, discarding the worst, roulette wheel selection, tournament selection, and others. Elitism retains high-fitness individuals but may reduce diversity, potentially leading to local optima. Discarding the worst individuals helps maintain diversity, with variations like aging evolution that can discard the oldest members. Roulette wheel selection provides survival probabilities based on fitness, while tournament selection involves choosing the best from a random sample of individuals. The paper also highlights strategies aimed at preserving diversity, such as selecting individuals inversely proportional to their density or based on genetic distance.

Evolutionary operations, specifically mutation and crossover, are discussed as key components of EA-based ENAS. Mutation alters a single individual to explore the search space, while crossover combines two individuals to create offspring. The paper emphasizes the importance of these strategies and operations in optimizing neural architectures effectively.
