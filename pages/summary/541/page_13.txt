B. Computational Efficiency
Another significant challenge is the computational efficiency of ENAS methods. The search for optimal architectures often requires substantial computational resources and time, which can be a barrier to their practical application. The concept of "GPU Days" highlights the extensive search time needed for ENAS, which can be a disadvantage compared to simpler methods like random search. Researchers are exploring ways to reduce the search time while maintaining or improving performance.

C. Generalization
The generalization ability of architectures discovered through ENAS is also a point of concern. While ENAS may yield architectures that perform well on specific datasets, there is skepticism about their performance on unseen data or different tasks. This raises questions about the robustness and transferability of ENAS-derived architectures across various applications.

In conclusion, while existing ENAS algorithms have shown promising applications across diverse fields, including image classification, medical imaging, and time series forecasting, challenges related to effectiveness, computational efficiency, and generalization remain. Addressing these issues is crucial for advancing the reliability and applicability of ENAS methods in real-world scenarios.
