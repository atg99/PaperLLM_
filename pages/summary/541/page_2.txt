this paper, the authors explore various Neural Architecture Search (NAS) algorithms, focusing on Reinforcement Learning (RL), gradient-based methods, and Evolutionary Computation (EC) techniques. RL-based NAS algorithms are noted for their high computational demands, often requiring extensive GPU resources over several days, particularly on datasets like CIFAR-10. Although gradient-based algorithms are more efficient, they tend to yield poorly conditioned architectures due to an unproven relationship with gradient optimization and necessitate the prior construction of a supernet, which demands significant expertise.

In contrast, ENAS leverages EC techniques, which simulate natural selection processes to address complex optimization problems without requiring gradient information. Key EC methods include Genetic Algorithms, Genetic Programming, and Particle Swarm Optimization, which have been effectively used for over two decades to optimize both neural architectures and weights—a process known as neuroevolution. The distinction between ENAS and neuroevolution lies in their focus; ENAS primarily targets architecture optimization, while neuroevolution seeks to optimize both architectures and weights simultaneously. ENAS is generally applied to deep neural networks, whereas neuroevolution targets smaller networks.

The survey highlights the evolution of ENAS algorithms, starting with Google's LargeEvo algorithm in 2017, which demonstrated effectiveness on CIFAR datasets. Despite the proliferation of ENAS-related submissions from 2017 to 2020, the authors note a lack of comprehensive surveys specifically addressing ENAS methodologies. Existing reviews tend to concentrate on broader NAS methods or pre-2019 literature, thus this paper aims to fill that gap by categorizing ENAS algorithms according to a three-stage framework: search space, search strategy, and performance estimation strategy, tailored to the nuances of ENAS.

The paper is structured as follows: it begins with a background on ENAS, followed by sections discussing encoding and search spaces, architecture representation, population updating processes, evolution acceleration techniques, applications of ENAS, challenges and future prospects, concluding with a summary of findings. This organized approach seeks to inspire new developments in the ENAS field.
