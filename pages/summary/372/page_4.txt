The paper discusses a taxonomy for Natural Language Inference (NLI) that categorizes inference processes based on their internal or external dependencies. The taxonomy includes three main categories: TIC (text-internal categories), which consists of lexical, syntactic, and factivity types; LOGICAL, which incorporates external inference processes categorized into Connectives and Deduction; and KNOWLEDGE, which requires external or commonsense knowledge for inference.

The authors extend the existing TaxiNLI taxonomy by reintroducing a pruned Numerical category and adding a new high-level category, PRAGMATIC, which consists of pre-supposition and implicatures. They emphasize the importance of manual template creation for testing specific reasoning types, as public datasets often encompass multiple capabilities.

The paper outlines various challenges in template generation, especially regarding linguistic and logical reasoning. It details the creation of templates for different reasoning types, including ellipsis for syntactic understanding, Boolean operations, quantifiers, coreference resolution, spatial and temporal reasoning, as well as causal reasoning. The authors highlight the need for careful construction of templates to accurately test these reasoning types and suggest the potential use of knowledge graphs for generating causal pairs.

In summary, the paper presents a structured approach to enhancing NLI taxonomy through detailed reasoning categories and the development of targeted templates for testing those categories, addressing the complexities involved in both linguistic and logical inference.
