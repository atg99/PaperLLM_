This paper presents significant contributions to the Natural Language Inference (NLI) task. Firstly, a template-based test suite comprising 194 templates and 184,000 examples is created by extending a reasoning taxonomy, facilitating the benchmarking of state-of-the-art NLI systems. This benchmarking uncovers notable insights into model performance. Secondly, the study identifies inconsistencies in model performance both within individual templates and across similar templates, indicating potential biases in BERT. Thirdly, a user study through simulation experiments suggests that RoBERTa exhibits more robustness and predictability compared to BERT.

The paper discusses related work that highlights the conflicting performance of Transformer-based pre-trained language models (PTLMs) in various benchmarks and targeted tests. Previous probing tasks aimed at understanding linguistic information encoding within models have raised concerns regarding their effectiveness. Additionally, while several works have curated inference datasets to evaluate reasoning capabilities, they often rely on aggregate statistics, complicating the identification of specific model failures.

The CHECKLIST methodology, which creates templates for testing NLP models, is referenced as a tool to assess minimal functionality. However, the paper argues that NLI tasks require a deeper understanding of reasoning capabilities, necessitating a more comprehensive evaluation framework. The authors aim to develop a testing approach that addresses these challenges by generating a templated test suite that aligns with the inherent complexities of reasoning in NLI tasks.
