The paper investigates the predictability of natural language understanding models, specifically BERT and RoBERTa, through a study involving human participants. It employs a two-stage approach using LIME output to analyze the models' predictions based on their nearest neighbors from different datasets (MultiNLI and CheckListNLI). The study focuses on simulation questions where participants predict the model's output given an explanation and input.

Key metrics tracked include prediction accuracy and mutual agreement among participants. A total of 10 participants, trained in analytical reasoning, were chosen to evaluate the models. Results indicate an increase in average prediction accuracy from 58.7 to 61.4 in Stage 1 to Stage 2 for BERT, and a 12.5% improvement in prediction accuracy for RoBERTa. Participants found the nearest neighbor examples from CheckListNLI more relevant and helpful for understanding model behavior.

The paper concludes by emphasizing the importance of behavioral analysis in assessing the predictability of language models, noting that RoBERTa demonstrates greater predictability than BERT despite inconsistencies. The findings contribute to the ongoing discourse on explainable AI and the need for models that can be trusted in their decision-making processes.
