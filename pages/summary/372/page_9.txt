The paper discusses an ethical framework for conducting human studies involving a blackbox Natural Language Inference (NLI) system. The authors obtained Internal Review Board (IRB) approval and ensured informed consent from all participants, who electronically signed a consent form. They assert that no personally identifiable information was collected and that participants were not exposed to harmful content. The acknowledgment section credits contributors to the study and insights gained during pilot testing. A comprehensive list of references is provided, highlighting significant works related to NLI, interpretability in machine learning, and language processing techniques, underscoring the academic rigor and context of the research.
