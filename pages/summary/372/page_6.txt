This paper analyzes the performance of three language models—BERT, DistilBERT, and RoBERTa—focusing on their capabilities and intra-template performance. 

In terms of capability-wise performance, all models perform well on Lexical, Syntactic, and Presupposition tasks. However, they show poor and inconsistent results in Logical, Knowledge, and Implicature categories. The authors classify template performance into three bins: passed (accuracy above 80%), unsure (20-80%), and failed (below 20%). Notably, all models fail on templates involving negation with "but not," and they show uncertainty in ordered resolution for boolean tasks. RoBERTa exhibits a bias towards entailment, especially when the hypothesis contains "not," leading to incorrect labels.

In comparative reasoning, all models fail on templates lacking sufficient information, while BERT and DistilBERT struggle with superlative adjectives. Quantifier templates reveal that BERT and DistilBERT fail when "all" is replaced by "some." RoBERTa outperforms in numerical counting but still struggles with incorrect counts.

Under spatial reasoning, all models have difficulty with cardinal directions and spatial relationships. For temporal reasoning, they cannot accurately compare events based on birth years or times of day, with BERT showing sensitivity to lexical variations of temporal terms. RoBERTa excels in resolving coreferences between male and female names and performs better than the others in causal templates. Overall, all models show weaknesses in knowledge templates.

In the intra-template performance analysis, BERT and DistilBERT exhibit uncertainty across a significant number of templates, compared to RoBERTa. The accuracy of templates varies widely based on placeholders, suggesting that certain professions and adjectives significantly impact model performance. A linear regression analysis indicates that placeholders such as COLOR and ACTION positively influence accuracy, while others have a negative impact.

The study highlights the varying strengths and weaknesses of the models across different reasoning tasks and emphasizes the importance of template design in evaluating model performance.
