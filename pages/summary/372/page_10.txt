The referenced works encompass a range of contributions to the field of natural language processing (NLP) and machine learning, focusing on understanding and interpreting models, as well as probing their capabilities. 

Key themes include the exploration of function word comprehension and its implications across various NLP tasks, as examined by Kim et al. in their 2019 study. The evaluation of model interpretability is highlighted by Lage et al. and Lipton, emphasizing the need for transparency in AI systems. The Winograd Schema Challenge, presented by Levesque et al., serves as a benchmark for assessing machine reasoning capabilities.

Further discussions on contextual representations and their linguistic knowledge transferability are presented by Liu et al. Meanwhile, McCoy et al. investigate the reliance of models on syntactic heuristics in natural language inference, with implications for understanding model behavior.

Ribeiro et al. contribute to the discourse on model explanations, while Richardson et al. probe the semantic aspects of natural language inference. The ATOMIC project by Sap et al. introduces a commonsense reasoning framework, enhancing the understanding of implicit knowledge in AI.

Overall, these works collectively advance the understanding of model interpretability, reasoning, and the complexities of language comprehension in AI, underscoring the importance of systematic evaluation in NLP.
