The paper discusses the regression coefficients for various placeholders across three models: BERT, DistilBERT, and RoBERTa. It highlights that the model's inability to account for the year of birth affects the YEAR variable. Notably, the placeholders MALE NAME and FEMALE NAME exhibit negative coefficients in BERT and DistilBERT, while RoBERTa shows a near-zero coefficient, indicating its superior capability in resolving gendered coreferences. The NAME placeholder has a more negative impact in BERT, suggesting that BERT is more affected by variations in names compared to RoBERTa. The models perform adequately with comparative adjectives but struggle with superlatives, implying that RoBERTa may be more robust but still presents inter-template inconsistencies that need further investigation.

To quantify these inconsistencies, the authors propose a human study inspired by explainable AI literature. Participants will be shown various behavioral information types and asked to predict model behavior on new examples. The study considers multiple factors that may influence outcomes, such as the abstraction level of behavioral information and interface design.

The authors emphasize the need for local example-based explanations over global summaries due to the cognitive load associated with understanding template-wise accuracies. They adopt an interface design from previous research that improves participant comprehension. For testing, they select 25 templates from CheckListNLI, ensuring a balance of accuracy representation and capability diversity, and utilize example-based LIME explanations as a baseline for comparison.
