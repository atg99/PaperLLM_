The paper presents findings from a pilot study that evaluates the effectiveness of template-based versus example-based explanations in a web interface. The study measured average accuracy and agreement scores for local question-wise explanations, with results indicating that example-based explanations outperformed template-based ones. Specifically, accuracy scores were 14.5 for template-based, 17.0 for another template-based approach, and 22.0 for example-based explanations. Agreement scores similarly showed improvements, with 15 for template-based and 20 for example-based.

The methodology involved selecting 25 test templates and formulating questions where participants predicted accuracy bins for System X using either template-based or example-based explanations. For template-based explanations, three templates and five examples highlighted with LIME output were used. In contrast, example-based explanations provided five nearest neighbor examples from the CheckListNLI dataset without any template information, focusing instead on predicting entailment labels for random examples.

The results clearly indicate a trend of increasing accuracy as the study shifted from template-based to example-based questions and explanations, highlighting the effectiveness of examples in improving understanding and prediction accuracy.
