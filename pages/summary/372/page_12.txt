The paper presents an analysis of model performance across various reasoning capabilities, highlighting the impact of lexicon variations on accuracy. It includes two case studies focusing on specific templates: 

1. **Case Study 1 (PROFESSION)** investigates a template where the expected label is "contradiction." By manipulating the gender of NAME2 and the profession lexicon, the study finds significant variations in BERT's accuracy, ranging from 0% for "engineer" and "poet" to 0.82% for "doctor" and 0.92% for "professor." This indicates a strong bias based on the profession's lexicon.

2. **Case Study 2 (Adjectives)** examines a template with the expected label of "entailment." Here, the accuracy remains relatively stable across different genders, suggesting that BERT exhibits less bias concerning adjectives compared to professions. However, accuracy still varies significantly with the adjectives used, from 0% for "bigger" to 1.00% for "creepier."

The paper also includes a benchmarking section that details various templates and model behaviors. It notes that all models struggle with certain templates, such as simple boolean tests and those requiring comparative reasoning. Notably, BERT and DistilBERT show bias towards "entailment" while failing to comprehend gendered names, whereas RoBERTa performs more accurately overall. The findings underscore the influence of lexical choices on model performance and highlight areas for further investigation in natural language processing.
