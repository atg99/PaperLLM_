The paper presents an analysis of various reasoning capabilities in natural language inference (NLI) systems using different models, including BERT, DistilBERT, and RoBERTa. It introduces a dataset called CheckListNLI, consisting of 194 templates that span 17 reasoning capabilities, and discusses the methodology for generating these templates, which include testing basic knowledge in areas such as geography, personalities, and concepts. 

The paper also examines the performance of these models on the MNLI test set and the CheckListNLI dataset, revealing that RoBERTa consistently outperforms both BERT and DistilBERT. The use of adversarial training with RoBERTa-ANLI and the larger DeBERTa model is mentioned, providing additional insights into model performance.

For the CheckListNLI dataset, templates were created with a significant number of examples generated for each, leading to a high inter-annotator agreement score, indicating the reliability of the annotations. Overall, the findings highlight the effectiveness of the models in handling various reasoning tasks and the comprehensive nature of the CheckListNLI dataset.
