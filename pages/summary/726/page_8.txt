The paper discusses the global convergence rates of Softmax Policy Gradient methods, particularly focusing on entropy-regularized objectives in Markov Decision Processes (MDPs). The authors present several key results, beginning with Lemma 16, which establishes a condition for the convergence rate of the entropy-regularized policy gradient, showing it to be O(e^(-t)). Theorem 6 formalizes this by asserting that, under specific conditions, the difference between the value function estimates converges at a rate dependent on the logarithm of the action space size.

The paper also addresses bias in the policy gradient estimation, proposing a two-stage approach to mitigate this issue. The first stage involves using entropy-regularized policy gradients to ensure a certain condition on the policy estimates, followed by a second stage without regularization. Theorem 7 provides a bound on the bias reduction achieved through this method.

Another proposed strategy is to decrease the strength of the regularization over time, which is explored in Update 3. The authors note that while this approach shows promise, challenges in proving convergence rates persist. Theorem 8 outlines the outcomes of this method, suggesting that the convergence rates could improve, particularly under certain conditions, although this remains an open question for further research.

The paper also examines whether entropy regularization genuinely aids convergence, presenting empirical evidence supporting its effectiveness. The authors propose a new perspective on entropy regularization, framing it as an optimization tool that enhances the Łojasiewicz degree, thereby leading to faster convergence rates. They clarify that while entropy regularization promotes exploration, it does not necessarily address exploration in the context of epistemic uncertainty.

Finally, the authors establish lower bounds that affirm the tightness of the O(1/t) convergence rates for unpenalized policy gradients, emphasizing that progress in policy improvement cannot be excessively large in each iteration, as shown through reversed Łojasiewicz inequalities.
