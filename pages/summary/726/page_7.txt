The paper discusses the global convergence rates of softmax policy gradient methods in the context of Markov Decision Processes (MDPs). It introduces the "soft" advantage function and the softmax optimal policy, emphasizing the role of entropy regularization in improving convergence rates. 

In the one-state case with no regularization, the objective simplifies to maximizing an entropy-regularized reward, which leads to a non-concave optimization problem. The paper presents a gradient update rule that incorporates this regularization, demonstrating that the optimal solution becomes biased, though this bias diminishes as the regularization parameter approaches zero.

The authors derive a non-uniform contraction property of the update rule, showing that the distance to the optimal policy decreases at a rate dependent on the action probabilities. This leads to a linear convergence rate for the entropy-regularized softmax policy gradient, contrasting with the slower convergence in the absence of regularization.

For general MDPs, the paper establishes that the softmax optimal policy satisfies specific consistency conditions and presents a smoothness result for the augmented value function. The authors also introduce a non-uniform Łojasiewicz inequality, indicating that the gradient signal becomes stronger as the sub-optimality gap decreases, enhancing convergence behavior. Throughout, the paper emphasizes the importance of regularization for achieving faster convergence rates in policy gradient methods.
