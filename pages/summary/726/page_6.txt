This paper discusses the global convergence rates of softmax policy gradient methods, addressing the smoothness of the objective function and the establishment of a non-uniform Łojasiewicz inequality for softmax parametrization. The authors build upon prior work by Agarwal et al. (2019) and demonstrate that the results from bandit problems can extend to multi-state Markov Decision Processes (MDPs).

Key findings include Lemma 7, which confirms the smoothness of the value function, and Lemma 8, which establishes a non-uniform Łojasiewicz inequality. The paper also introduces Lemma 9, showing that the minimum action probability is uniformly bounded away from zero under specific assumptions. These results culminate in Theorem 4, which asserts that softmax policy gradient converges to an optimal policy at a rate of O(1/t), marking a significant advancement in understanding convergence rates in MDPs.

Additionally, the paper compares the iteration complexity of their algorithm with previous methods, indicating improvements in dependence on sub-optimality levels. The authors also explore entropy regularized policy gradients, demonstrating that this approach yields a superior convergence rate of O(e^(-t)), likening the effects of entropy regularization to those of strongly convex regularizers in convex optimization.

The paper provides a comprehensive analysis of convergence properties and the implications of regularization techniques in reinforcement learning, contributing to the understanding of policy gradient methods in complex decision-making environments.
