The paper investigates the global convergence rates of softmax policy gradient methods, particularly focusing on the impact of entropy regularization on these rates. It establishes lower bounds for convergence in both general and specific Markov Decision Processes (MDPs), demonstrating that with an appropriate learning rate, the difference in value functions can be bounded in terms of the optimal value gap.

Theorem 9 provides a lower bound on convergence for large enough iterations, while Theorem 10 extends this to any MDP. The authors note that their convergence rates align with these lower bounds, although there are significant gaps attributed to the reversed Łojasiewicz inequality. This gap presents an open challenge for future research.

The paper introduces the concept of non-uniform Łojasiewicz degree to better understand how entropy regularization accelerates convergence. It highlights that the expected reward objective without entropy regularization exhibits a non-positive Łojasiewicz degree, indicating limitations in achieving faster convergence rates. In contrast, the entropy-regularized objective demonstrates a positive degree of at least 1/2, suggesting improved convergence properties.

In conclusion, the study finds that entropy regularization significantly enhances the convergence rate of softmax policy gradient methods, transforming it from sub-linear to linear. The introduction of non-uniform Łojasiewicz inequalities is a key innovation, and the authors propose further exploration of the gaps in convergence bounds and broader applications of their findings in non-convex optimization contexts.
