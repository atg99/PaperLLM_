This paper examines the global convergence rates of softmax policy gradient methods, particularly in the context of expected reward updates. The update mechanism is described in Algorithm 1, which employs a specific form of gradient update based on expected rewards.

The key findings include:

1. **Smoothness of the Objective**: The expected reward objective is shown to be 5/2-smooth, which implies that small gradient updates can improve the objective value. However, smoothness alone does not guarantee convergence to a global optimum, especially for non-concave objectives.

2. **Łojasiewicz Inequality**: To ensure convergence to global maxima, the paper establishes a non-uniform Łojasiewicz inequality, which indicates that the gradient of the objective at any parameter dominates the sub-optimality of that parameter. This inequality is weaker as it scales with the probability of the optimal action, which may become small during updates.

3. **Intermediate Results**: Lemmas presented in the paper combine the concepts of smoothness and the Łojasiewicz inequality to derive a pseudo-rate of convergence. However, this rate is limited by the behavior of the probability of the optimal action during updates.

4. **Asymptotic Behavior**: The paper references previous work to show that the probability of selecting the optimal action remains bounded away from zero, leading to a more favorable convergence rate.

5. **Convergence Rate**: The main results include Theorem 2, which establishes an O(1/t) convergence rate for arbitrary initializations, and Theorem 3, which strengthens this result under uniform initialization conditions. These results indicate that the convergence improves when the initial action probabilities are uniform.

In summary, the paper provides a thorough analysis of softmax policy gradient methods, illustrating the conditions under which these methods can achieve global convergence, supported by both theoretical insights and inequalities related to the behavior of the objective function.
