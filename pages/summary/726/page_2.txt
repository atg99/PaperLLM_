The paper investigates the global convergence rates of softmax policy gradient methods, addressing gaps in existing literature regarding convergence rates, particularly for entropy-regularized methods. The authors first establish that softmax policy gradient methods converge to the optimal policy at a rate of O(1/t) when using the true gradient, thus enhancing previous asymptotic results. This conclusion is supported by two key findings: the softmax policy gradient satisfies a non-uniform Łojasiewicz-type inequality, and the minimum probability of optimal actions during optimization has a bound related to its initial value.

Subsequently, the paper analyzes entropy-regularized policy gradients, demonstrating a linear convergence rate of O(e^(-t)), which is significantly faster than the unregularized version. This finding resolves an open question posed by Agarwal et al. (2019) regarding the effectiveness of entropy regularization. The authors provide a theoretical framework that explains how entropy regularization accelerates convergence, showing that even with the true gradient, entropy enables faster convergence than unregularized methods.

The paper also explores the concept of non-uniform Łojasiewicz degree, revealing that without regularization, the degree of expected reward cannot be positive, allowing for O(1/t) rates. With entropy regularization, the degree improves to 1/2, facilitating the linear convergence rate. This shift in the relationship between gradient norm and suboptimality offers insights into improved convergence rates and aligns with empirical findings on the benefits of entropy in policy optimization.

The paper is structured as follows: Section 2 introduces notation and settings, while Sections 3 to 5 present the main contributions. Section 6 concludes the study. The findings provide a comprehensive theoretical understanding of the dynamics of softmax policy gradient methods and the role of entropy regularization in enhancing optimization performance.
