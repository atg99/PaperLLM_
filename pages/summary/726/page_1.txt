The paper makes significant contributions to understanding the global convergence rates of softmax policy gradient methods in a tabular setting. The authors establish that policy gradient with a softmax parametrization converges at a rate of O(1/t), where the constants are problem-dependent and influenced by initialization. This result builds on the finding that the softmax policy gradient satisfies a Łojasiewicz inequality, allowing the authors to bound the minimum probability of selecting an optimal action.

Additionally, the analysis shows that entropy-regularized policy gradient methods achieve a faster linear convergence rate of O(e^(-t)) towards the optimal policy, resolving an open question in the literature. The paper further combines these findings with new lower bound results to illustrate how entropy regularization enhances policy optimization, even when using the true gradient, by examining the convergence rates through the lens of non-uniform Łojasiewicz degree.

Overall, this work provides a deeper theoretical understanding of the effects of entropy regularization on policy gradient methods, corroborating empirical findings and advancing the field of reinforcement learning.
