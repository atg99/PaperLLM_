The paper discusses the global convergence rates of softmax policy gradient methods, focusing on the softmax function as it relates to policy gradient methods in reinforcement learning. The softmax function is defined using logit values, which originate from logistic regression. It establishes a mapping from the parameter space to the action distribution over a set of actions, denoted as .

A key component of the analysis is the H matrix, which serves as the Jacobian of the softmax mapping. The paper also introduces the concept of smoothness in the context of convex analysis, defining a function as smooth if it satisfies specific inequality conditions related to its derivatives.

The paper highlights the policy gradient theorem, which allows for the reexpression of the gradient of the value function concerning policy parameters. The authors specifically analyze the vanilla softmax policy gradient method, presenting an algorithm that updates logit values iteratively based on the calculated gradients.

A significant contribution of the paper is the strengthening of existing results regarding the convergence of softmax policy gradient methods. While previous work established that the method asymptotically converges to an optimal policy, this paper demonstrates that the rate of convergence is O(1/t), where t represents the number of iterations. 

The authors begin their analysis with a simplified "bandit case," where the environment has a single state and a zero discount factor. They show that even in this straightforward scenario, the objective function can be non-concave, complicating the optimization process.

Overall, the paper provides a rigorous examination of softmax policy gradient methods, detailing their convergence properties and addressing the challenges posed by non-concave objectives in reinforcement learning settings.
