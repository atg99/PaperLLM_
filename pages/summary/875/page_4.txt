This paper provides a detailed analysis of the computational costs associated with different modules in a ResNet50 architecture, focusing on convolution and self-attention mechanisms. The study is divided into stages, examining the floating-point operations (FLOPs) and parameters for each module.

Key findings include:

1. **Module Performance**: The analysis presents a table (Table 1) that quantifies FLOPs and parameters for various modules across two stages. The first stage is primarily dominated by 1x1 convolutions, which account for the majority of computational costs, while the second stage involves lighter operations like shifting and aggregation.

2. **Self-Attention Mechanism**: The paper explains how self-attention allows models to prioritize significant regions within large contexts. It details the two-stage process of self-attention, where initial projections are made through 1x1 convolutions, followed by the computation of attention weights and aggregation of values.

3. **Computational Cost Analysis**: The authors highlight that both convolution and self-attention exhibit quadratic complexity in FLOPs relative to channel size in Stage I, while Stage II shows linear complexity with no additional parameters. This trend is consistent across both modules, with empirical evidence demonstrating that a significant portion of computational load occurs in Stage I.

4. **Relationship Between Self-Attention and Convolution**: The paper concludes by establishing a connection between self-attention and convolution modules, suggesting that understanding their decomposed stages can enhance model efficiency.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of analyzing computational costs in deep learning architectures, particularly in the context of ResNet50, to optimize performance and resource utilization.
