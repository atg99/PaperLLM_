This paper explores the integration of convolution and self-attention mechanisms in neural networks, emphasizing their shared operations and computational efficiencies. The authors highlight two main stages in their proposed architecture, ACmix. In Stage I, both convolution and self-attention utilize 1x1 convolutions to project input features into deeper spaces, which results in a quadratic complexity in terms of FLOPs and parameters relative to the channel size. Stage II focuses on feature aggregation, where both modules operate effectively with minimal computational cost.

The integration allows for a shared projection of features, enhancing efficiency by reusing intermediate feature maps for different aggregation strategies. Specifically, in the self-attention path, these features are grouped to serve as queries, keys, and values, while the convolution path generates additional feature maps through a light fully connected layer. The outputs from both paths are combined using learnable scalars to adjust their contributions.

The authors also address the inefficiencies of traditional tensor shifts in convolution operations, proposing the use of depthwise convolutions with fixed kernels as a more effective alternative. This modification aims to maintain data locality and improve vectorized implementations, ultimately enhancing overall module efficiency during inference.
