The paper presents a novel hybrid module called ACmix, which integrates convolution and self-attention mechanisms for improved feature extraction in neural networks. The proposed architecture consists of two stages. In Stage I, the input feature map undergoes three 1x1 convolutions to project it into different feature representations. Stage II employs these intermediate features through two distinct paradigms, where the results from both paths are concatenated and summed to produce the final output.

The authors revisit the standard convolution operation, reformulating it to highlight its relationship with self-attention. They explain that a standard 3x3 convolution can be decomposed into a summation of shifted feature maps, where each map is derived from applying a 1x1 convolution based on the kernel weights from specific positions. This reformulation emphasizes the underlying mechanics of convolution and introduces a Shift operation to facilitate the manipulation of feature maps.

In the self-attention mechanism, the input feature map is projected into queries, keys, and values using 1x1 convolutions. The attention weights generated from queries and keys are then used to aggregate the values, thereby capturing relationships within the feature map.

Overall, the ACmix module effectively combines the strengths of convolution and self-attention, offering a promising approach to enhance performance in downstream tasks while maintaining computational efficiency.
