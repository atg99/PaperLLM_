This paper investigates the integration of convolution and self-attention mechanisms in vision transformers, proposing a hybrid operator that combines both techniques while maintaining computational efficiency. The results demonstrate that both methods share similar computational overheads, leading to the development of a new operator that effectively integrates them.

The study includes extensive evaluations on various transformer-based models for object detection and image classification, showcasing performance metrics such as mean Average Precision (mAP) and Frames Per Second (FPS). Key findings indicate that the ACmix architecture yields significant improvements in object detection performance, with models like Mask R-CNN ACmix achieving a mAP of 51.1 at 754G FLOPs, outperforming other configurations.

An ablation study reveals the influence of different path combinations and the efficacy of various shift modules on inference speed and accuracy. Results indicate that using group convolutions can significantly enhance inference speed, while learnable convolution kernels improve model flexibility.

The paper also highlights the learned parameters α and β, which represent the model's bias toward convolution or self-attention at different depths. Analysis shows that convolution is more effective in early layers, while self-attention becomes dominant in later stages, aligning with previous research on transformer design patterns.

In conclusion, the findings underscore the potential of hybrid operators in vision transformers, affirming their effectiveness and efficiency in processing visual data.
