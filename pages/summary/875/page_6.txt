The paper presents a novel convolutional module named ACmix, which enhances computational efficiency while maintaining the performance of various tasks, including image classification, semantic segmentation, and object detection. It introduces a specific convolution kernel design that allows for simple tensor shifts, facilitating effective feature extraction from multiple directions. This approach is implemented as a single group convolution, which optimizes computational resources.

ACmix features learnable weights initialized from shift kernels, improving model capacity without losing the original shift operation's benefits. Additionally, the architecture employs multiple groups of convolution kernels to align the output channel dimensions with those of self-attention paths. The computational costs of ACmix are analyzed, revealing that it performs comparably to traditional convolution methods while being more efficient than self-attention mechanisms.

The paper also highlights ACmix's adaptability to various attention mechanisms, such as patchwise, window, and global attention, allowing for broader applicability across different model architectures. Empirical evaluations demonstrate that ACmix consistently outperforms several state-of-the-art models across multiple tasks, achieving higher accuracy with reduced computational costs. For instance, the ResNet-ACmix model exhibits superior performance compared to SASA-ResNet, while maintaining significantly lower FLOPs.

In summary, ACmix stands out for its efficient design and adaptability, achieving state-of-the-art results in image classification and downstream tasks, showcasing its potential as a versatile component in modern neural networks.
