The paper discusses the integration of self-attention and convolutional modules in neural networks, aiming to leverage the strengths of both paradigms. Previous research has identified self-attention as an augmentation for convolutional modules, while more recent studies have proposed self-attention as a replacement for convolutions in convolutional neural network (CNN) models. However, existing methods often treat these two mechanisms as separate components, limiting their potential synergy.

The authors propose a mixed model called ACmix, which integrates self-attention and convolution by recognizing their reliance on 1x1 convolution operations. By first projecting input feature maps using 1x1 convolutions, ACmix generates intermediate features that can be reused and aggregated in both self-attention and convolutional manners. This approach minimizes computational overhead while benefiting from both mechanisms.

The contributions of the paper include revealing the strong underlying relationship between self-attention and convolution and presenting an elegant integration of the two, which consistently outperforms models that rely solely on either self-attention or convolution.

The related work section highlights the evolution of convolutional neural networks and self-attention mechanisms, noting that self-attention has shown promise in vision tasks. It reviews two main approaches: using self-attention as standalone building blocks in vision models and integrating self-attention with convolution to enhance performance. Additionally, it discusses various models that have explored these hybrid strategies, reinforcing the need for further investigation into the interplay between self-attention and convolution.
