In the paper "On the Integration of Self-Attention and Convolution," the authors Xuran Pan, Chunjiang Ge, Rui Lu, Shiji Song, Guanfu Chen, Zeyi Huang, and Gao Huang explore the underlying relationship between convolution and self-attention in representation learning. The authors argue that both techniques, while often viewed as distinct, share a common computational foundation, specifically in their initial operations.

The paper demonstrates that a traditional k×k convolution can be decomposed into k² individual 1×1 convolutions, followed by shift and summation operations. Similarly, the self-attention mechanism can be interpreted as multiple 1×1 convolutions that compute attention weights and aggregate values. This similarity leads to the conclusion that both methods share similar computational complexity, particularly in the first stage, which is significantly more demanding than the subsequent stages.

To leverage the strengths of both approaches, the authors propose a mixed model named ACmix, which integrates self-attention and convolution while minimizing computational overhead. Extensive experiments show that ACmix consistently outperforms competitive baselines in image recognition and other downstream tasks.

The paper highlights the complementary nature of convolution and self-attention, emphasizing that their integration can lead to enhanced performance in various computer vision applications. The authors plan to release the code and pre-trained models to facilitate further research in this area.
