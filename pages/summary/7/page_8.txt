The paper investigates the effects of importance weighting in deep learning, particularly focusing on its influence on decision boundaries in modern deep neural networks. The authors highlight a gap in existing literature, noting that previous studies have not explicitly examined how importance weighting impacts these decision boundaries. They draw theoretical inspiration from earlier works that established the weight-agnostic nature of deep linear networks and the efficacy of importance weighting in addressing model misspecification.

The authors summarize various applications of importance weighting in deep learning, including its use in contextual bandit feedback, imitation learning, domain adaptation, and causal inference. Despite the diverse applications, the authors find that the effects of importance weighting are often negligible, particularly when combined with early stopping, suggesting that the learned solutions may depend more on the training examples' locations rather than their density.

Their experiments reveal that importance weighting does not significantly correct for class imbalance in training sets, although sub-sampling does influence predictions. They observe that while importance weighting has been shown to be useful in some contexts, its standard application alongside deep learning warrants reevaluation. The authors suggest that practitioners should be cautious in employing importance weighting and raise questions regarding the optimal use of early stopping and weight decay when importance weighting is desired. Overall, the findings challenge the conventional wisdom surrounding importance weighting in deep learning, advocating for a more nuanced understanding of its implications.
