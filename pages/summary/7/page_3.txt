The paper investigates the effects of importance weighting in deep learning, particularly in the context of neural networks and their training dynamics. Soudry et al. (2017) highlight that neural networks often overfit significantly, continuing to reduce generalization error even after achieving zero training error. They analyze linear networks, finding that the learned decision boundary converges in direction to that of a hard-margin support vector machine, despite the weights themselves not converging in magnitude. This convergence occurs because the norm of the weight vector increases, leading support vectors to dominate the loss function under specific conditions.

Building on these findings, the authors hypothesize that similar weight-invariance properties might apply to modern deep, nonlinear networks, particularly since many practical datasets are separable. They also explore the implications of regularization methods like L2 regularization and dropout on the solutions found by stochastic gradient descent (SGD) when combined with importance weighting.

The experimental section of the paper encompasses various datasets, including two-dimensional toy datasets, CIFAR-10, and the Microsoft Research Paraphrase Corpus (MRPC). The authors focus on label shift scenarios, down-weighting loss contributions from specific classes, and assess the effects of regularization in conjunction with importance weighting. They conduct experiments using logistic regression and multi-layer perceptrons (MLPs) on synthetic datasets, as well as binary classification tasks on CIFAR-10 with a convolutional network architecture.

Overall, the study aims to empirically confirm the theoretical insights regarding the implications of importance weighting and regularization on the training dynamics and performance of deep learning models.
