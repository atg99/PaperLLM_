Importance-weighted risk minimization plays a crucial role in various machine learning applications, including causal inference, domain adaptation, class imbalance, and off-policy reinforcement learning. While its effects are well understood in low-capacity models, the impact on over-parameterized deep neural networks remains less clear. This paper investigates the role of importance weighting in realistic deep networks, particularly in contexts where training data is separable.

The authors find that importance weighting significantly influences deep networks during the early stages of training. However, as training progresses and the networks successfully separate the training data, the effect of importance weighting diminishes. The study also highlights that certain techniques, such as L2 regularization and batch normalization, can restore some influence of importance weighting, although they do so in ways that may not align with practitioners' expectations. Specifically, the paper questions how practitioners should adjust L2 regularization to achieve the desired weighting effects.

The findings are supported by experiments across various architectures and datasets, confirming that while importance weighting is beneficial initially, its relevance decreases as the model learns to separate the data effectively. This research contributes to a deeper understanding of the dynamics of importance weighting within deep learning frameworks.
