The paper investigates the effects of importance weighting in deep learning, particularly in the context of classification tasks such as paraphrase identification using the MRPC dataset. The authors fine-tune the BERT BASE model without weight decay, employing stochastic gradient descent (SGD) with a learning rate of 0.01. Various importance weights, specifically inverse powers of 4, are applied during training. Results indicate that while importance weighting influences model decision boundaries early in training, its effects diminish over time, leading to convergence among models with different weights.

The experiments reveal that after extensive training, there is no significant correlation between class-based importance weights and classification ratios, regardless of the dataset or model architecture. This trend is consistent across both convolutional networks and transformer-based models. Notably, sub-sampling the training set yields more pronounced effects on classification ratios compared to down-weighting the loss function.

The study also explores the impact of label noise on the effectiveness of importance weighting. Even under label noise, importance weighting does not significantly alter classification outcomes. Furthermore, models with extreme importance weighting exhibit slower convergence in decision boundaries, with stabilization of classification ratios taking much longer than achieving perfect training accuracy.

The presence of L2 regularization is shown to influence importance-weighted classifiers, affecting their convergence rates in classification ratios. Overall, the findings suggest that while importance weighting may provide initial benefits in training, its long-term impact on model performance is limited.
