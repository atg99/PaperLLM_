The paper investigates the effects of importance weighting in deep learning, highlighting its applications in various contexts such as treatment effect estimation, crowdsourcing, and reinforcement learning. Despite the technique's prevalence, the authors point out that the effectiveness of importance weighting in deep neural networks is not well understood, particularly given the networks' ability to overfit training data.

Key findings reveal that the impact of importance weighting is significant early in training but diminishes as training progresses. The authors conducted experiments on classification tasks, including CIFAR-10 image classification and paraphrase detection, observing that the effects of importance weighting become indistinguishable from unweighted risk minimization after sufficient training epochs. They also found that L2 regularization and batch normalization interact with importance weights, affecting the learned models, while dropout regularization does not exhibit this interaction.

The contributions of the paper include demonstrating the diminishing impact of importance weighting in unregularized networks, exploring the interactions of L2 regularization and batch normalization with importance weights, replicating results across various tasks and datasets, and questioning the conventional applications of importance weighting in deep learning. This research has practical implications for fields such as causal inference, domain adaptation, and off-policy reinforcement learning. The theoretical motivation stems from recent developments in understanding decision boundaries in neural networks, informing the empirical findings of the study.
