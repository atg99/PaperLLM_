The paper investigates the impact of importance weighting in deep learning, particularly in scenarios involving class imbalance. The study utilizes the CIFAR-10 dataset to simulate imbalanced training conditions by sub-sampling images from the cat or dog classes. The loss function is adjusted by downweighting the contribution of the class that is not sub-sampled, aligning the weighting factor with the degree of sub-sampling.

In addition to binary classification, the research extends to multiclass classification, applying varying weights to the loss function for different classes to address label shifts during testing. The experiments involve a two-layer MLP with 256 hidden units trained using weighted cross-entropy loss.

The findings reveal that importance weighting significantly influences classification outcomes, as illustrated through various plots showing the fraction of images classified as dogs across different epochs and weighting ratios. The results indicate that early stopping and regularization techniques, such as L2 regularization and dropout, also play a role in the effectiveness of importance weighting.

Furthermore, the study validates its conclusions by applying similar methodologies to the Microsoft Research Paraphrase Corpus, confirming the robustness of the findings across different domains. Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of incorporating class weighting strategies to enhance model performance in imbalanced datasets.
