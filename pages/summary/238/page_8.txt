The paper discusses the effectiveness of various representations in reinforcement learning (RL), specifically focusing on the proposed method, RRL, and its performance compared to existing techniques. Key findings indicate that RRL, utilizing Resnet34 features, outperforms other representation methods, including RRL using VAE, demonstrating a balance between representation capacity and computational efficiency.

The experiments reveal that RRL maintains robust performance even in the presence of noisy proprioceptive signals and when relying solely on visual inputs. This resilience showcases the method's adaptability to real-world conditions where sensory noise or failures may occur. 

Additionally, the paper evaluates design decisions impacting RRL's performance, establishing that sparse rewards can be as effective as dense rewards in complex tasks, thus reducing the need for extensive domain expertise. The results also highlight that while increasing policy network capacity can enhance performance, it can also lead to diminishing returns if excessive or irrelevant features are introduced.

Finally, the paper critiques existing benchmarks in visual RL, suggesting that traditional methods may not adequately address the complexities encountered in real-world tasks, as illustrated by the challenges faced by state-of-the-art methods in more representative environments. Overall, the findings advocate for a rethinking of benchmarking strategies for RL to better align with practical applications.
