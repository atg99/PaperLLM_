The paper presents RRL: Resnet as representation for Reinforcement Learning, a novel approach aimed at enabling robots to learn complex behaviors autonomously in uninstrumented environments. These environments require the robot to operate solely on proprioceptive sensors like cameras and joint encoders, which presents challenges due to high dimensionality and partial observability. RRL integrates features from a pre-trained Resnet into the reinforcement learning pipeline, achieving performance comparable to learning directly from state information.

The authors highlight the limitations of existing reinforcement learning methods, which often succeed in controlled simulations but struggle in real-world applications due to the lack of direct access to environmental states. RRL addresses these challenges by utilizing a pre-trained encoder that has been exposed to diverse image distributions, allowing it to remain effective across varying environments without needing additional adaptations.

The approach demonstrates significant success in a simulated dexterous manipulation task, where it outperforms state-of-the-art methods by delivering contact-rich behaviors despite the complexities of the visual input. RRL serves as a bridge between representation learning, imitation learning, and reinforcement learning, showcasing its capability to learn directly from visual inputs with efficiency akin to state-based learning, even in challenging high-dimensional scenarios.
