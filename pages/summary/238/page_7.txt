The paper discusses advancements in reinforcement learning (RL) through the use of representations, particularly focusing on the performance of various feature extractors. It presents experiments conducted on the ADROIT dexterous manipulation suite, highlighting that the proposed method, RRL, demonstrates stable performance and approaches the upper bound set by DAPG using privileged state information. In contrast, the FERM baseline shows good initial progress but becomes unstable, often reaching performance saturation before the allocated computational budget is exhausted.

The paper further explores the computational cost of RRL compared to other architectures, including Resnet variants and MobileNet. It reveals that RRL consistently outperforms FERM across various environmental distractions, such as lighting conditions and object color.

An essential investigation in the paper addresses whether the success of Resnet is due to its architecture or the dataset used for pre-training. The results indicate that RRL maintains performance with various feature extractors pre-trained on ImageNet, suggesting that the dataset contributes more significantly to the effectiveness of features than the specific architecture.

Moreover, the paper contrasts task-specific representations, such as those learned via variational inference techniques, with task-agnostic representations derived from a broader dataset like Resnet. The findings suggest that the pre-trained Resnet provides superior generic features compared to methods that rely on learning brittle, task-specific representations, thereby emphasizing the advantages of using robust, pre-trained models in RL applications.
