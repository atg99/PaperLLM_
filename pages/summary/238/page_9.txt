The paper investigates the effectiveness of different representations in reinforcement learning, particularly comparing RAD and RRL methods across various benchmarks. The results indicate that RAD consistently outperforms other baselines in the DMControl Benchmark, while RRL performs better on the Adroit Manipulation benchmark despite struggling with DMControl. This suggests that RAD is more effective for simpler tasks, whereas RRL benefits from pretrained Resnet features, showcasing its capability to generalize across more complex environments. 

The analysis reveals that representations learned by RAD are highly task-specific and do not generalize well to other tasks, particularly as task complexity increases. This highlights the limitations of current representation learning methods, which often fail to adapt to diverse environments. The paper emphasizes the need for benchmarks that reflect real-world tasks to address these challenges.

The strengths of the proposed RRL method lie in its simplicity and broad applicability to reinforcement and imitation learning algorithms, allowing for effective learning directly from high-dimensional proprioceptive signals. The use of pretrained Resnet features enhances robustness and stability in learning, avoiding issues related to non-stationarity found in methods that optimize representation and policy simultaneously.

However, the paper acknowledges limitations in addressing data mismatch between pretrained features and the operational environment of robots, as well as the static nature of the datasets used for training. There is an opportunity for RRL to utilize a shared representational space, potentially facilitating data sharing across various robotics applications and improving efficiency in training and execution. Overall, this work contributes to understanding how representation learning can be effectively integrated into reinforcement learning frameworks.
