The paper discusses various representations used in reinforcement learning (RL) and highlights the importance of representation learning in improving RL performance. It references key contributions in the field, such as the work by Ahn et al. on robotics benchmarks, and the foundational research by Bengio et al. on representation learning. 

Several methodologies are explored, including deep generative models like variational autoencoders, which enhance the understanding of disentangled representations, as evidenced by Burgess et al. and Higgins et al. The paper also emphasizes the significance of model-based approaches, like those by Hafner et al., which utilize latent dynamics for planning.

Moreover, it addresses challenges in real-world applications of RL, as discussed by Dulac-Arnold et al. The advancement of scalable distributed deep RL frameworks, such as IMPALA, is highlighted through the work of Espeholt et al. 

The interplay between visual feature learning and robotic manipulation is examined, with Gupta et al. demonstrating the effectiveness of learning from human demonstrations. The paper concludes with a discussion on the role of image augmentation in regularizing deep RL, supported by recent findings from Kostrikov et al. and Laskin et al.

Overall, the paper presents a comprehensive overview of the evolving landscape of representations in reinforcement learning, underscoring their critical role in enhancing learning efficiency and effectiveness across various tasks.
