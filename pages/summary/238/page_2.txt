The paper discusses the challenges of high dimensionality and multi-modality in reinforcement learning (RL) and the necessity of learning effective representations that can simplify input data into low-dimensional features. Traditional supervised methods for representation learning require deep domain knowledge, while unsupervised methods, though autonomous, often yield brittle task-specific representations that do not generalize well across different tasks.

The authors propose a method called RRL that leverages features from pre-trained image classification models, specifically ResNet34, as representations in standard RL pipelines. The key insight is that representations trained on a wide distribution of real-world scenarios can be effective across various task distributions, which contrasts with traditional approaches that rely on narrow task-specific distributions.

The main contributions of the paper include:
1. Introducing a simple method that integrates representation learning with imitation and reinforcement learning, facilitating the use of features from image classification models.
2. Demonstrating that these features are robust to visual distractors and can efficiently learn policies from proprioceptive inputs, overcoming the limitations of brittle task-specific representations.
3. Showing that RRL performs well on high-dimensional dexterous manipulation tasks, achieving performance and sample efficiency comparable to state-based methods, while traditional methods often fail in such scenarios.
4. Highlighting the performance gap between RRL and state-of-the-art approaches in both low-dimensional and complex high-dimensional tasks, and arguing that current environments for testing image-based control methods do not accurately reflect real-world challenges.

The paper positions RRL as a promising advancement in the intersection of representation learning, imitation learning, and reinforcement learning, emphasizing its general applicability and efficiency in learning from visual inputs.
