The paper discusses representations for reinforcement learning, particularly focusing on the visual tasks of Hammer and Door. The images presented illustrate the effectiveness of latent features in encoding essential information for reconstructing original input images. The comparison of various methods—RRL (our approach), FERM, RRL with Resnet-50, and RRL with Resnet-18—highlights compute costs associated with each method over a consistent sample complexity of 4 million steps. The findings indicate that FERM is significantly more compute-intensive, primarily due to data augmentation and frequent updates to Actor and Critic parameters. In contrast, RRL using Resnet-34 achieves a balance between computational efficiency and performance, with the cost of VAE pretraining factored into the overall computation. The analysis does not account for simulation costs, emphasizing real-world applicability.
