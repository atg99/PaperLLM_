This paper discusses various approaches to reinforcement learning (RL) focusing on representation learning. 

In section 2.1, the authors describe learning without explicit representation, highlighting methods that operate end to end from raw pixels to actions. Notable examples include Mnih et al.'s work on Atari games and Haarnoja et al.'s advancements in dexterous manipulation and locomotion. While successful, these methods require large networks and extensive training data, limiting their applicability. The paper introduces RRL, which employs an explicit representation network to improve efficiency by decoupling representation from policy learning.

Section 2.2 covers learning with supervised representations, where expert supervision is used to acquire features from input images. Techniques such as learning keypoints have shown effective task manipulation in complex environments. However, these methods face challenges in generalization and scalability to diverse tasks, in contrast to RRL's task-agnostic representations.

In section 2.3, the authors explore unsupervised representation learning, which aims for scalability without expert guidance. Methods like contrastive learning and variational inference have been utilized to derive compressed representations and dynamics models. Despite their potential, unsupervised methods often produce brittle representations sensitive to changes in the environment. RRL addresses these issues by using pre-trained representations that are robust to noise and irrelevant information.

Finally, section 2.4 examines learning with representations and demonstrations, focusing on DAPG, a method that combines imitation and reinforcement learning. RRL extends DAPG to operate with proprioceptive signals, achieving comparable performance while demonstrating greater stability and robustness against distractions. The paper concludes that RRL's fixed feature extractor, trained on a diverse range of real-world images, enhances its performance over methods like FERM, which struggle with task-specific representations.
