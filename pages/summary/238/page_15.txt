The paper discusses the implementation of reinforcement learning (RL) techniques with a focus on hyperparameter settings and evaluation methods for various environments. Key hyperparameters for the Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) algorithm are outlined, including settings for frame stacking, replay buffer capacity, batch size, learning rates for the actor and critic, and various update frequencies. The training process incorporates a variational auto-encoder (VAE) trained on a reconstruction objective, where the encoder's weights are frozen to function as feature extractors, enhancing the RL agent's performance by integrating proprioceptive sensor inputs.

Additionally, the paper evaluates the robustness of the reinforcement learning algorithms, specifically RRL and FERM, against visual distractions during inference. Various distractors such as changes in light position and direction, alterations in object color, and the introduction of new objects in different sizes and geometries are employed to assess the generalization performance of the trained policies. The results are presented as average performance over multiple rollouts, demonstrating the effectiveness of the approaches in maintaining performance despite environmental changes.
