The paper discusses Reinforcement Learning (RL) representations, focusing on the performance of the proposed method, RRL, in various tasks within the MJRL (Multi-Joint Reinforcement Learning) environment. The experiments involve collecting different numbers of trajectories for tasks like Hammer-v0, Door-v0, Relocate-v0, and Pen-v0, with 200 and 400 trajectories collected per iteration for certain tasks.

Results indicate that RRL outperforms FERM and matches the performance of DAPG(State) in the Peg Insertion task. However, in the Reacher task, RRL initially struggles, while DAPG(State) and FERM perform well. This suggests that task-specific representations can be beneficial in simpler, low-dimensional environments, as RRL's generic Resnet features may lead to overfitting.

The paper also explores variations of RRL using different pretrained encoders like MobileNet and ShuffleNet, and a hierarchical VAE, all utilizing frozen parameters during training. Additional experiments with DMControl employ various state-of-the-art methods including RAD and SAC, with specific hyperparameters detailed.

For RRL(VAE), a comprehensive dataset of 1 million images is collected, ensuring a diverse representation of actions, including optimal actions, actions with noise, and random actions. This dataset is intended to enhance the robustness of the policy learned by the RL agent. Overall, the findings underscore the importance of representation learning in RL and the potential advantages of tailored representations for specific tasks.
