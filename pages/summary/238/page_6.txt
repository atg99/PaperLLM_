The experimental evaluations in this paper focus on several key questions regarding the effectiveness of Reinforcement Learning (RRL) with pre-trained representations. The study examines whether these representations, derived from large real-world image datasets, enable RRL to successfully learn complex tasks using proprioceptive signals, and how RRL's performance and efficiency compare with state-of-the-art methods. Additionally, it investigates the influence of various representational choices on behavior generality and versatility, the impact of design decisions on RRL, and the effectiveness of commonly used benchmarks for image-based continuous control methods.

The tasks selected for evaluation extend beyond simple low-dimensional challenges to include the Adroit manipulation suite, which comprises high-dimensional dexterous manipulation tasks that require complex depth information. The study utilizes a Resnet-34 model pre-trained on ImageNet, which is modified to produce a 512-dimensional feature space by removing the last layer, with parameters frozen during the training of the RL agent.

Results indicate that RRL outperforms several baselines, including NPG, which struggles even with full state information, and FERM, which exhibits instability across tasks. RRL shows strong performance across all tasks, particularly in the challenging relocate task, often approaching the performance of the best-case oracle, DAPG(State). Furthermore, RRL demonstrates significant computational efficiency, being approximately five times more compute-efficient than FERM.

The robustness of RRL's policies is assessed by introducing visual distractors during inference. The use of pre-trained Resnet features contributes to the resilience and versatility of RRL's policies, allowing them to maintain performance despite environmental clutter, in contrast to the task-specific features learned by FERM, which perform poorly under similar conditions. Overall, the findings underscore the advantages of using pre-trained representations in reinforcement learning for complex tasks.
