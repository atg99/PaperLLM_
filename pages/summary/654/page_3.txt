The paper discusses training methodologies for Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs), comparing conventional training methods with layer-wise training approaches. In conventional training, the optimizer simultaneously adjusts the weight matrices and the linear classifier, leading to a joint optimization output. In contrast, layer-wise training involves a sequential approach where each layer is optimized in stages, allowing for the saving of weights at each step while dropping intermediate classifiers.

The paper further analyzes the time and memory complexities associated with mini-batch training, noting that the complexities are influenced by factors such as neighborhood size and batch size. Several variants of the standard mini-batch algorithm are introduced, including GraphSAGE, FastGCN, VRGCN, and Cluster-GCN, each offering different strategies for reducing computational burdens.

GraphSAGE utilizes fixed-size sampling but faces challenges with neighborhood expansion, while FastGCN employs global importance sampling to mitigate complexity growth. VRGCN aims for variance reduction but requires significant memory for storing intermediate embeddings. Cluster-GCN takes a different approach by clustering graphs before applying SGD, though its performance is contingent on the clustering methods used.

The proposed algorithm emphasizes the importance of feature aggregation and transformation in the learning process, detailing how these processes impact the efficiency of node representation learning within GCNs. Overall, the paper highlights the complexities and inefficiencies in current GCN training strategies and proposes a structured approach to improve training performance.
