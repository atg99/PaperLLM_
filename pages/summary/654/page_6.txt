The paper discusses a layer-wise training algorithm for a two-layer Graph Convolutional Network (GCN) that utilizes an RNN controller to optimize training efficiency. The training process is divided into two stages, where each stage generates training loss data used as input for the RNN controller. This controller determines whether to continue training or proceed to the next layer based on a stopping probability. If this probability exceeds a defined threshold, training for that layer concludes; otherwise, it continues for another epoch.

The authors highlight the challenges of applying this layer-wise training approach to GCNs, particularly the need for manual adjustments to training epochs for each layer. They propose L2-GCN, which employs a learned RNN controller to automate the stopping decision using a reinforcement learning technique called REINFORCE. This approach models the training as a Markov Decision Process (MDP), where actions, states, and rewards are defined to optimize training efficiency and performance.

The paper emphasizes the necessity for further development in loss functions for classification tasks, as the current reliance on cross-entropy may not capture the network's capacity analytically. The authors conducted experiments on multiple datasets to evaluate the predictive performance, training time, and GPU memory usage of their proposed methods, demonstrating their effectiveness in various classification scenarios.
