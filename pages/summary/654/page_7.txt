The paper discusses the implementation and performance evaluation of two proposed algorithms, L-GCN and L2-GCN, which leverage a recurrent neural network (RNN) controller for layer-wise training of graph convolutional networks (GCNs). The RNN controller processes previous hidden states and generated embeddings, which include action embeddings, loss metrics, and layer indices. This allows for the selection of actions based on a probability vector generated through multinomial sampling, ultimately leading to weight updates based on a final reward calculated from the selected actions.

The experimental setup employs the Adam optimizer with specific learning rates for different datasets, and results are obtained using a high-performance machine. The comparison with state-of-the-art GCN mini-batch training algorithms like GraphSAGE, FastGCN, and VRGCN reveals that L-GCN is significantly faster while maintaining comparable prediction performance across multiple datasets, including Cora, PubMed, PPI, and Reddit. Notably, L2-GCN further reduces training time by half with minimal performance loss by utilizing a learned controller for stopping decisions.

The paper also highlights issues faced by existing methods on larger datasets, where GraphSAGE and FastGCN fail to converge, while L-GCN and L2-GCN consistently achieve good performance. It emphasizes that the time required for hyper-parameter tuning was not included in the comparisons, as this data was unavailable for pre-trained methods. The transferability of the learned controller is examined, demonstrating its reusability across datasets with similar loss functions, thus saving search time.

Additionally, the paper explores the impact of different epoch configurations on performance, indicating that varying the number of epochs for each layer can influence results. The RNN controller in L2-GCN learns to optimize these configurations, achieving performance improvements with fewer epochs. The study also evaluates deeper networks, suggesting the necessity of further investigation into their training efficacy. Overall, the findings underscore the efficiency and adaptability of the proposed algorithms in graph-based learning tasks.
