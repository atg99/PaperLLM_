This paper presents a novel training framework called L-GCN for graph convolutional networks (GCNs), addressing the challenges of high computational costs and memory usage when training on large graph datasets. Traditional GCNs aggregate and transform node embeddings recursively, which can lead to exponential growth in complexity with the number of layers. L-GCN separates feature aggregation from feature transformation, significantly reducing both time and memory requirements while maintaining the effectiveness of GCNs under certain conditions.

The authors further introduce L2-GCN, which incorporates a learned controller for each layer to optimize the number of training epochs, effectively halving the training time. Experimental results indicate that L-GCN outperforms existing state-of-the-art mini-batch training algorithms by at least an order of magnitude in terms of speed, with consistent memory usage regardless of dataset size, while achieving comparable prediction performance. The paper emphasizes the practical implications of their approach, providing a pathway for scalable GCN training in various applications such as node classification, link prediction, and graph classification. The source code is made available for further research and implementation.
