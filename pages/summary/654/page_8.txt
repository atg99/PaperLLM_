The paper introduces novel layer-wise training algorithms for Graph Convolutional Networks (GCN), referred to as L-GCN, which effectively separate feature aggregation and transformation during training, significantly reducing computational complexity. The authors provide theoretical foundations that demonstrate L-GCN's capacity to achieve performance comparable to traditional training methods while also proving its increased efficacy with deeper network architectures.

Key experimental results highlight L-GCN's superior performance in terms of training speed and GPU memory usage compared to state-of-the-art methods across various datasets, including Cora, PubMed, PPI, Reddit, Amazon-670K, and Amazon-3M. The results indicate that deeper networks yield improved prediction performance, with L-GCN outperforming two-layer networks by notable margins on larger datasets.

Additionally, the paper explores the transferability of learned controllers, showcasing the effectiveness of a recurrent neural network (RNN) controller in optimizing training epochs. This leads to the development of L2-GCN, which utilizes the learned controller to further reduce training time by approximately 50% with minimal performance trade-offs.

In conclusion, the proposed L-GCN and L2-GCN algorithms demonstrate significant advancements in GCN training efficiency and performance, supported by both theoretical analysis and empirical evidence.
