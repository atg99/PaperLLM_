The paper presents a novel layer-wise training algorithm for Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs), referred to as L-GCN, aimed at reducing the time and memory complexities associated with GCN training while maintaining competitive performance. The authors differentiate between two key operations in GCNs: feature aggregation (FA) and feature transformation (FT). By decoupling these operations, they propose a greedy algorithm that, surprisingly, does not compromise the network's representation capability, as supported by theoretical analysis based on graph isomorphism.

Additionally, the paper introduces L2-GCN, which employs learned controllers to automatically adjust the training epochs for each layer, eliminating the need for manual hyper-parameter tuning. The authors provide a comparative analysis of time and memory complexities between their methods and existing algorithms, demonstrating significant advantages in both aspects. The experiments indicate that L-GCN and L2-GCN are not only faster than state-of-the-art methods but also maintain consistent GPU memory usage regardless of dataset size.

Key contributions include:
- A layer-wise training algorithm for GCNs with reduced complexities.
- Theoretical justification that the greedy approach preserves graph-representative power under certain conditions.
- Automated layer-wise epoch configuration via learned controllers.
- Achieving state-of-the-art performance while being lightweight across various applications.

The paper also categorizes existing GCN training algorithms into full-batch and mini-batch methods, highlighting their respective advantages and disadvantages. The original GCN uses full-batch gradient descent, which leads to high time and memory complexities that do not scale well with large graphs. In contrast, mini-batch stochastic gradient descent algorithms provide a more scalable solution by propagating vertex representations in smaller batches.
