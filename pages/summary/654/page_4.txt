The paper discusses the limitations of conventional Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) in terms of time and memory complexity during training, particularly when using mini-batch Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD). It identifies the coupling of feature aggregation (FA) and feature transformation (FT) as a key issue, where FA requires representations from neighboring nodes, leading to a large sampling requirement. The conventional approach necessitates multiple rounds of both FA and FT for each layer, which is inefficient.

To address these challenges, the authors propose a layer-wise GCN training approach (L-GCN) that separates FA and FT processes. In L-GCN, FA is performed once per layer to gather structural information, followed by a single-layer perceptron for FT, thus streamlining the training process. This method significantly reduces both time and memory complexity compared to traditional methods, as L-GCN only requires FA for each layer once throughout training, while FT is conducted once per batch.

The theoretical justification for L-GCN is also provided, showing that under certain conditions, the performance of layer-wise trained GCNs can match that of conventionally trained GCNs. The paper builds on previous work that evaluates the representation power of aggregation-based graph neural networks and establishes conditions under which L-GCN can be as effective as conventional methods. The findings suggest that L-GCN can maintain the capacity for robust representation learning while improving efficiency in training GCNs.
