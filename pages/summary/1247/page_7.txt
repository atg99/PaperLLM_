The paper presents NGBoost, a method for probabilistic prediction that utilizes natural gradient boosting. The performance of NGBoost is evaluated against various probabilistic regression approaches on UCI benchmark datasets, measured using average negative log-likelihood (NLL). The results indicate that NGBoost demonstrates competitive performance, particularly on smaller datasets, with several results outperforming or closely matching other methods like MC dropout, Deep Ensembles, and Concrete Dropout.

Tables included in the paper detail the NLL scores for different datasets, showcasing NGBoost's effectiveness across various contexts. For instance, in the Boston dataset, NGBoost achieved an NLL of 2.43, which is comparable to other methods. The paper also explores the impact of key components of NGBoost, demonstrating that using multiparameter boosting alongside the natural gradient is crucial for performance enhancement.

The experiments were conducted with a consistent setup, employing a decision tree base learner and specific learning rates tailored to the dataset sizes. The methodology involved selecting the optimal number of boosting stages based on log-likelihood and retraining models for validation against a held-out test set. Results highlight the importance of NGBoost in providing accurate predictive uncertainty, establishing it as a strong candidate in the field of probabilistic regression.
