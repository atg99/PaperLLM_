The paper presents NGBoost, an algorithm for probabilistic prediction that extends traditional gradient boosting methods. NGBoost consists of three modular components: a base learner, a parametric probability distribution, and a proper scoring rule. The algorithm operates through a series of boosting iterations, initializing with a common parameter that fits the marginal distribution of the response variable.

In each iteration, the algorithm calculates natural gradients of the scoring rule concerning predicted parameters and fits base learners to these gradients. The outputs of the base learners are scaled by a factor determined through a line search to minimize the scoring rule loss. This sequential learning process allows for the prediction of a conditional distribution, with parameters obtained from an additive combination of base learner outputs.

NGBoost generalizes gradient boosting to predict conditional distributions, allowing users to specify various distribution families and enabling diverse applications such as negative binomial boosting for counts and Gamma or Weibull boosting for survival predictions. The approach maintains flexibility by permitting the parameters of the distributions to vary with covariates, thereby enhancing its applicability across different prediction tasks.
