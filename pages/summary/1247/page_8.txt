The paper "NGBoost: Natural Gradient Boosting for Probabilistic Prediction" presents NGBoost, a model designed for probabilistic predictions using natural gradient boosting techniques. The authors evaluate NGBoost's performance on various regression benchmark datasets from UCI, focusing on Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) as a metric for point-estimation performance. Despite NGBoost not being optimized for point estimation, it demonstrates competitive results compared to other methods such as Elastic Net, Random Forest, Gradient Boosting, GAMLSS, and Distributional Forests across several datasets, including Boston, Concrete, and Energy.

The paper details the methodologies used in conjunction with NGBoost, including an automatic relevance detection kernel and variational inference for datasets with large sample sizes. Preprocessing steps involved standardizing features and labels to ensure consistency across the datasets. The authors also compare NGBoost against various ablation studies to assess the impact of using natural gradients versus standard methods and the benefits of allowing multiple distribution parameters to vary.

Results indicate that NGBoost maintains a strong performance in terms of NLL (Negative Log-Likelihood) and RMSE, showcasing its versatility in probabilistic modeling. The findings suggest that while NGBoost is primarily intended for probabilistic predictions, it can effectively generate point estimates that perform well against established algorithms in the field.
