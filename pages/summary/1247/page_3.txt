The paper discusses NGBoost, a method for probabilistic prediction using natural gradient boosting. It begins by defining proper scoring rules, which are essential for evaluating probabilistic forecasts. The logarithmic score is highlighted as the most common proper scoring rule, leading to maximum likelihood estimation (MLE). The Continuous Ranked Probability Score (CRPS) is presented as a robust alternative to MLE.

The paper then introduces the concept of the generalized natural gradient. Traditional gradient descent methods are sensitive to parameterization, which can affect training dynamics. This motivates the use of the natural gradient, which is invariant to parameterization and provides a more reliable direction for optimization in Riemannian space.

Divergences induced by proper scoring rules serve as local distance metrics in the distribution space, with MLE inducing Kullback-Leibler divergence and CRPS inducing L2 divergence. These divergences are almost symmetric for small parameter changes and facilitate the creation of a statistical manifold.

The natural gradient is defined as the direction of steepest ascent that respects the Riemannian metric induced by the scoring rule. The paper formulates the natural gradient for any proper scoring rule and illustrates it with both MLE and CRPS, showing how they relate to Fisher Information and the corresponding Riemannian metrics.

This comprehensive approach allows for effective optimization in probabilistic prediction, ensuring robustness and adaptability in various contexts.
