The referenced papers focus on various methodologies and models for predicting bank failures and financial crises. Notable works include Chen and Benesty's introduction of XGBoost, a scalable tree boosting system, which enhances predictive accuracy in various applications. Chiaramonte et al. investigate the predictive power of Z-scores in anticipating bank failures, while Cleary and Hebb propose a functional model for predicting bank distress with evidence from both in-sample and out-of-sample. 

Cortés et al. compare linear discriminant analysis and AdaBoost for forecasting failures, highlighting the effectiveness of ensemble methods. Dell'Ariccia et al. analyze the relationship between credit booms and lending standards in the subprime mortgage market, providing insights into risk factors leading to bank failures. Demyanyk and Hasan review various prediction methods for financial crises and bank failures, and DeYoung and Torna examine the impact of nontraditional banking activities during the financial crisis on bank stability.

Ecer evaluates the predictive performance of neural networks versus support vector machines in forecasting bank failures in Turkey. Ekinci and Erdal explore the effectiveness of base learners, ensembles, and hybrid ensembles in forecasting bank failure. Elith et al. provide guidance on boosted regression trees, showcasing their utility in ecological studies. Lastly, Eygi applies support vector machines to predict bankruptcy, specifically within the banking sector. Collectively, these works contribute to the understanding and advancement of predictive analytics in finance.
