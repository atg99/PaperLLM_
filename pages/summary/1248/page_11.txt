The studies mentioned highlight various methodologies and findings related to predicting bank failures during the financial crisis. Serrano-Cinca and Gutiérrez-Nieto employed partial least squares discriminant analysis to assess the 2008 U.S. banking crisis, finding that their results were comparable to other predictive algorithms like linear discriminant analysis and support vector machines. Lu and Whidbee focused on the characteristics of U.S. commercial banks that received bailouts versus those that failed, revealing similarities between the two groups. Cleary and Hebb achieved a high success rate of 92% in distinguishing between failed and non-failed banks using discriminant analysis. Lastly, Chiaramonte et al. examined the predictive capability of Z-scores, concluding that they could predict 76% of bank failures without the need for additional variables. Overall, these studies contribute to understanding the risk factors and predictive methodologies relevant to bank stability during financial crises.
