In this study, we utilized 75% of the observations to train an XGBoost model, reserving the remaining 25% as a holdout sample for validation. The training and test samples comprised paired banks, where failed banks were matched with similar-sized nonfailed banks based on total assets. We initiated the model training with a high learning rate of 0.1, a maximum tree depth of 5, and other initial parameters including a gamma of 0 and a subsample ratio of 0.8. Following the recommendations of Jain (2016), we set a minimum child weight of 1 and a minimum sum of instance weight in a child to 1. 

Through cross-validation, we aimed to identify the optimal combination of parameters to maximize the area under the curve (AUC), which was our chosen performance metric. This led to the development of Model 1, which achieved an optimal configuration of 149 trees, resulting in a maximum AUC mean of 0.9805. The AUC mean for the excluded folds of cross-validation is illustrated in Figure 2, where solid black curves represent the AUC mean while dotted curves indicate the one standard error interval. The analysis revealed that exceeding the optimal number of trees could lead to overfitting, emphasizing the importance of appropriately tuning the size of decision trees in XGBoost. Shallow trees were noted to perform poorly, while deeper trees tended to capture excessive details, often referred to as weak learners.
