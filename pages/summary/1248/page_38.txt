The paper presents the performance of an extreme gradient boosting (XGBoost) model on a test dataset, highlighting its effectiveness with an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.9280 and an accuracy of 94.74%. The confusion matrix indicates that the model accurately predicted 100% of the non-distress cases and achieved an 89.47% accuracy in predicting distress cases. 

In the section on model interpretability, the authors emphasize the importance of understanding how the XGBoost model classifies observations. They discuss the use of the R XGBoost Explainer package, which enables the decomposition of predictions to reveal the influence of individual variables, thereby enhancing the transparency of the model. This approach allows for an understanding of the model's decision-making process, making it comparable to simpler models like linear regression or single decision trees.
