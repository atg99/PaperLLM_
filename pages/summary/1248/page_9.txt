The paper discusses the application of AdaBoost and other predictive techniques in forecasting business failure across various European firms. It highlights the use of standard predicting variables, such as financial ratios, alongside qualitative factors like firm size and legal structure. The findings show that AdaBoost significantly reduces generalization error by approximately 30% compared to neural networks. Cortés et al. (2008) further demonstrate that AdaBoost outperforms discriminant analysis in predicting business failure, based on a sample of 1,180 Spanish firms. 

Momparler et al. (2016) applied boosted classification trees to predict failures in the EU banking sector, identifying four critical variables for anticipating financial distress. Amendola et al. (2015) explored the effects of microeconomic indicators and firm-specific factors on financial distress, concluding that influences on firm exit depend on the chosen exit routes, thereby advocating a multiple-state approach. 

Lastly, Volkov et al. (2017) introduced a dynamic approach to bankruptcy prediction using financial ratios over several periods and Markov-based variables. Their analysis of Belgian bankruptcy data indicates that using multi-period data is superior to snapshot data, and incorporating Markov variables enhances the performance of non-ensemble bankruptcy prediction models.
