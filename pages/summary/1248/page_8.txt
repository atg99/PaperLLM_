Tian and Yu (2017) conducted a study on bankruptcy prediction in the international market utilizing the Compustat Global database. They employed the adaptive least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) for variable selection, identifying key predictor variables for different markets. For Japan, they pinpointed three significant predictors: retained earnings to total assets, total debt to total assets, and current liabilities to sales. In the European context, particularly for the UK, Germany, and France, the equity ratio (equity to total liabilities) emerged as a consistent predictor across various time horizons, alongside other fluctuating variables.

Ekinci and Erdal (2017) focused on predicting bank failures in Turkey from 1997 to 2001, applying three machine learning models: Logistic regression, J48, and voted perceptron. Their results demonstrated that hybrid ensemble machine learning models significantly outperformed traditional base and ensemble models in prediction accuracy.

In the European Union context, Betz et al. (2014) developed an early-warning model leveraging both bank-level and country-level data to predict vulnerabilities leading to bank distress. They found that integrating bank-specific vulnerabilities with macro-financial indicators enhanced model performance, yielding valuable out-of-sample predictions during the recent financial crisis. Additionally, research by Alfaro et al. (2008) compared the prediction capabilities of artificial neural networks and support vector machines, concluding that artificial neural networks had greater accuracy in forecasting financial distress.
