The paper discusses the role of regularization in XGBoost, highlighting its importance in preventing overfitting by controlling the complexity of the model. Regularization applies a penalty to the weights of variables, effectively shrinking them towards zero. When the regularization parameter is zero, there is no penalty, and the model behaves like traditional gradient boosting models (GBM). As the regularization term increases, the model's variance decreases, which can result in increased bias, reflecting the bias-variance trade-off.

The authors emphasize that regularization helps maintain predictive power while simplifying the model. They suggest that the optimal parameters for XGBoost can be determined through cross-validation methods, specifically k-fold cross-validation. This technique involves partitioning data into k sets, training the model on subsets, and evaluating its performance by averaging the prediction errors across the holdout folds. The paper underscores that regularization is a key differentiator for XGBoost compared to other boosting models.
