The paper investigates the prediction of bank failure in the U.S. banking sector using extreme gradient boosting, a machine learning technique. It highlights the critical role banks play in developed economies and the destabilizing effects of systemic banking crises on financial markets and global economic growth. The study analyzes data from 2001 to 2015, encompassing 30 financial ratios from 156 U.S. national commercial banks. Identifying leading indicators of bank failure is essential for enabling timely interventions by regulators and bank managers. The findings reveal that lower values of retained earnings to average equity, pretax return on assets, and total risk-based capital ratio correlate with increased bank failure risk. Additionally, a very high yield on earning assets is linked to a greater likelihood of financial distress. The research underscores the importance of these indicators in anticipating and preventing bank defaults.
