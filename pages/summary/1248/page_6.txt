The paper investigates bank failures by pairing failed banks with solvent banks of similar size based on total assets. It is structured into several sections: a literature review, data description, methodology, results, and conclusions with managerial implications.

In the literature review, early work by Meyer and Pifer in 1970 established a model for analyzing bank failure, identifying key financial variables indicative of insolvency, such as fraud and financial irregularities. Boyd and Runkle in 1993 examined the relationship between bank size and failure indicators, concluding that failure probability was largely independent of bank size, despite size potentially correlating with market power. Lane et al. in 1986 compared different prediction techniques for bank failures, demonstrating that the Cox proportional hazards model, rooted in survival analysis, outperformed discriminant analysis in predicting failures two years prior. 

Overall, the literature highlights various factors and methodologies related to bank failure prediction, emphasizing the complexity and multifaceted nature of the issue.
