The paper discusses the importance of using independent datasets to evaluate the predictive accuracy of models, specifically in the context of the XGBoost model. It emphasizes that using the same observations for both model training and testing can lead to misleading results. To address this, the authors divided a balanced sample of paired banks into two sets: 75% for training the model and 25% for testing its generalizability.

After tuning the model's parameters, the authors identified key features influencing predictions. The test dataset was then used to calculate the model's accuracy, reinforcing the necessity of independent validation for assessing predictive performance. The paper highlights the use of the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve as a valuable tool for evaluating the XGBoost classifier's performance on the test dataset. The ROC curve visualizes both the true positive rate and true negative rate across various thresholds, providing a comprehensive view of the model's effectiveness without reliance on specific class probabilities. This method facilitates the interpretation and comparison of classifier performance.
