The paper discusses the variability in bank performance ratios, specifically focusing on ratios P14, P15, and P16. It notes that a significant spread exists between the minimum and maximum values of these ratios, indicating variability among the 22 banks analyzed. Lower values for ratio P15, which measures efficiency, correlate with higher failure rates, while ratio P16 shows that non-failed banks manage more assets per employee, linking this to human resource efficiency. The ratio logTA does not reveal meaningful differences in total assets between failed and non-failed banks due to the balanced sample used.

In the section on XGBoost parameters, the paper outlines the tuning process for the XGBoost model to enhance prediction accuracy for bank failures. It emphasizes the importance of selecting the right parameter combinations to prevent overfitting, given the model's sequential nature of tree fitting. The area under the curve (AUC) is used as the optimization metric, with a maximum value of 1 indicating a good fit. AUC is preferred over accuracy for evaluating binary classification models, as it provides a more robust measure of performance.
