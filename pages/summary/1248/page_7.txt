Kumar and Ravi (2007) categorized failure prediction methods into statistical and intelligent techniques, noting that each standard algorithm has its advantages and disadvantages. Statistical techniques encompass logistic regression and factor analysis, while intelligent techniques include neural networks, nearest neighbor classifiers, operations research methods, and decision tree induction.

Demyanyk and Hasan (2010) conducted a review of empirical findings in economics and operations research related to bank failures, detailing methods from prior studies and emphasizing intelligent techniques in operational literature for predicting bank failure.

Cao et al. (2011) developed a model combining rough set theory and support vector machines to predict financial distress in Chinese listed companies, aiming to enhance early warning methods and prediction accuracy. Their findings indicated that support vector machines outperformed the rough set theory model.

Olson et al. (2012) utilized various data mining tools on bankruptcy data, discovering that decision trees provided greater accuracy compared to neural networks and support vector machines, despite generating a higher number of rule nodes.

Ecer (2013) assessed the predictive capabilities of artificial neural networks versus support vector machines in forecasting failures of Turkish commercial banks, concluding that neural networks demonstrated slightly superior predictive ability.

Eygi (2013) applied support vector machines to analyze bank distress, focusing on 42 Turkish banks that failed or did not fail between 1997 and 2003. The results highlighted the effectiveness of support vector machines in extracting valuable insights from financial data, suggesting their utility in early warning systems.
