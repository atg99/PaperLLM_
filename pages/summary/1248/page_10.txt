This section discusses various studies that analyze the factors contributing to bank failures in the United States. Trussel and Johnson (2012) utilized logistic regression to identify six financial indicators, concluding that an increase in Tier 1 capital relative to total assets and return on assets significantly reduces the likelihood of bank failure. Lu and Whidbee (2013) focused on the influence of charter type, holding company structure, and bank fragility during the late 2000s financial crisis. Their findings revealed that factors such as receiving bailout funds, low capital and liquidity ratios, reliance on brokered deposits, and a high proportion of nonperforming loans were critical in determining the failure of established banks. Serrano-Cinca et al. (2014) investigated bank bankruptcies post-2009, proposing hypotheses related to loan growth, specialization in real estate, and neglect of profit margins. Their structural equation modeling indicated that five years before the crisis, failed banks exhibited higher loan growth, a greater focus on real estate, higher risk ratios, increased turnover, and lower margins compared to solvent institutions.
