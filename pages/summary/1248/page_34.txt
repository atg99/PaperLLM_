The analysis highlights the significance of various financial ratios in predicting failure, with the ratio of total assets emerging as the fourth most critical variable in one-year and two-year pre-failure models. The cost of funding earning assets was identified as the second most important variable in both two-year and three-year pre-failure models. 

The performance evaluation of the extreme gradient boosting model for predicting failure two years prior yielded an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.8753 and an overall accuracy of 92.11%. The confusion matrix indicates a high accuracy in predicting distress, with a true positive rate of 89.47% for actual distress cases and a true negative rate of 94.74% for non-distress cases. The results were based on a threshold value of 0.5 with a sample size of 38.
