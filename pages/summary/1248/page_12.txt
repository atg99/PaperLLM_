The study investigates factors contributing to bank defaults, focusing on the stability of Z-scores over a three-year period. DeYoung and Torna (2013) analyzed the impact of nontraditional banking activities on the failure rates of U.S. commercial banks during the financial crisis. Their findings revealed that income from fee-based activities, such as securities brokerage and insurance sales, reduced the likelihood of bank failures, while asset-based activities like venture capital and investment banking increased this probability. Berger and Bouwman (2013) explored the relationship between bank capital and performance, concluding that capital consistently bolsters the survival and market share of small banks across various economic conditions. For medium and large banks, capital primarily enhances performance during banking crises.

The data utilized in this research comprises 30 annual financial ratio series from 2001 to 2015, covering 156 U.S. national commercial banks. This sample includes 78 failed banks matched with 78 non-failed banks of similar asset size, with data sourced from the Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation database.
