Boosting is an effective technique for addressing classification problems by reducing overall errors through the incorporation of supplementary models that focus on the errors made in previous iterations. This method combines multiple individual models to create a stronger predictive model, outperforming the individual models when used alone. The tree ensemble model, consisting of several classification or regression trees, is utilized as a single tree is often insufficient for practical applications. 

Early boosting algorithms, developed in the 1990s, aimed to combine weak classifiers to form a more robust ensemble classifier, effectively lowering the generalized misclassification error rate. Friedman's boosting machine enhances model accuracy by utilizing past fitted trees, maintaining minimal depth, and contributing variably to the final model. His 2002 update introduced stochastic gradient boosting, which employs random sampling to improve predictive performance and reduce variance by fitting each tree to a random subset of data.

Boosting models are adept at managing extreme values, nonlinearity, and missing data, and they can accommodate various predictor types, including categorical variables. By fitting multiple trees, boosting mitigates the limitations of single-tree models, enhancing predictive performance. The boosting process involves sequentially fitting small decision trees, with each new tree addressing the residuals from the previous model, thereby refining the overall fit.
