The paper outlines a set of financial condition ratios used to assess the health of financial institutions. Each ratio provides a specific metric that reflects various aspects of asset management, loan performance, and capital structure. 

1. **C1** measures the proportion of earning assets to total assets, indicating how effectively a bank utilizes its assets to generate income.
2. **C2** assesses the allowance for loan and lease losses relative to total loans and leases, highlighting the institution's preparedness for potential defaults.
3. **C3** focuses on the allowance for losses in relation to noncurrent loans, providing insight into the bank's risk management regarding overdue loans.
4. **C4** evaluates noncurrent assets, which include overdue loans and real estate owned, as a percentage of total assets, indicating asset quality.
5. **C5** looks at the ratio of noncurrent loans to total loans, giving a view of the bank's loan performance and credit risk.
6. **C6** examines net loans and leases as a percentage of total assets, reflecting the institution's lending efficiency.
7. **C7** analyzes net loans and leases against total deposits, indicating how well the bank is leveraging its deposit base for lending.
8. **C8** assesses net loans and leases in relation to core deposits, providing insight into funding stability.
9. **C9** measures domestic deposits as a percentage of total assets, reflecting the bank's reliance on local funding sources.
10. **C10** evaluates equity capital as a percentage of total assets, indicating the financial cushion available to absorb losses.
11. **C11** presents the core capital leverage ratio, which assesses the strength of a bank’s capital relative to its total assets, excluding certain intangible assets.
12. **C12** highlights the Tier 1 risk-based capital ratio, a crucial measure of a bank's financial stability based on risk-weighted assets.

These ratios collectively provide a comprehensive framework for analyzing the financial condition and risk profile of banking institutions.
