This paper discusses the development of a classification model aimed at predicting bank failures between 2001 and 2015, utilizing the XGBoost algorithm. The model's performance is enhanced through 10-fold cross-validation, which estimates test error and aids in parameter tuning. The algorithm iteratively optimizes model parameters by learning the relationship between the qualitative response variable (bank failures) and its predictors. The analysis was conducted using R version 3.3.0 with the XGBoost and caret packages.

Prior to analysis, the study addressed multicollinearity by examining Pearson's correlation coefficients, leading to the removal of several highly correlated ratios (P6, P8, P10, C4, C7, C11, and C12) from the analysis. The use of cross-validation is emphasized as a crucial method for model selection and tuning.
