This paper presents a method for classifying univariate time series data, utilizing a training procedure that enhances the separability of representations derived from different time series. The efficacy of these representations is evaluated through the classification performance of a Support Vector Machine (SVM), demonstrating efficient training in terms of time and space.

The authors introduce a combined version of their method that leverages representations computed with varying parameters (K) to enhance classification performance by reducing noise. The study employs the new iteration of the UCR archive, which includes 128 diverse univariate datasets, to compare their method against contemporary techniques such as TimeNet and RWS, both of which are unsupervised methods. The results are benchmarked against top-performing supervised classifiers like HIVE-COTE, ST, BOSS, EE, and a DTW baseline.

The findings reveal that the proposed method consistently matches or surpasses the performance of the unsupervised methods across multiple UCR datasets. Specifically, it outperforms TimeNet and RWS on 11 out of 12 and 10 out of 11 datasets, respectively. Overall, the results indicate that the authors' approach is competitive with leading methods in the field, suggesting its potential as a robust solution for time series classification tasks.
