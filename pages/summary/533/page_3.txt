In this paper, the authors propose a novel unsupervised training approach for an encoder-only architecture, avoiding the computational costs associated with joint training of decoders in autoencoder-based methods. They introduce a triplet loss specifically designed for time series data, drawing inspiration from the word2vec model. This triplet loss leverages time-based sampling strategies to effectively learn representations from unlabeled data, marking a first in the time series literature.

The core idea is to ensure that similar time series yield similar representations without supervision. The authors outline a framework where a random subseries (xref) is selected from a time series (yi). The representation of this subseries is encouraged to be close to that of a positive example (xpos) from the same series, while being distant from a negative example (xneg), which can either come from another time series or a different subseries of the same series. This mirrors the context-word relationship in word2vec, where the context is related to a word and unrelated to a random word.

The loss function derived from this setup is designed to differentiate between xref and xneg while associating xref with xpos. The training process involves iterating through the dataset, randomly selecting tuples of (xref, xpos, xneg) and minimizing the loss. The computational cost of this approach scales with the number of negative samples and the complexity of the encoder.

The authors also discuss the flexibility in selecting the lengths of negative examples, noting that while random lengths can be used for diverse datasets, fixed lengths can enhance computational efficiency when time series are uniform. Overall, the proposed method aims to improve the stability and convergence of the training process, ultimately leading to effective representation learning for time series data.
