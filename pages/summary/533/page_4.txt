The paper presents an innovative approach to unsupervised time series representation learning through a triplet loss mechanism and a specific encoder architecture. The algorithm outlined in the paper focuses on selecting reference, positive, and negative samples from time series data for training, enabling the encoder to learn meaningful representations irrespective of varying input lengths. This is achieved through a time-based triplet loss that capitalizes on the encoder's capacity to process time series of different lengths, as detailed in Section 5.

The encoder architecture is designed to meet three critical requirements: effective information extraction from time series, time and memory efficiency for both training and testing, and support for variable-length inputs. The authors chose deep neural networks with exponentially dilated causal convolutions for this purpose. Unlike recurrent neural networks, which are sequential and may suffer from issues like exploding and vanishing gradients, the proposed architecture allows for efficient parallelization on modern hardware and better captures long-range dependencies.

The model employs stacks of dilated causal convolutions, ensuring that the output at any time step is computed solely from past values, thus maintaining causality and allowing for efficient online processing during testing. Each layer combines causal convolutions with weight normalization, leaky ReLU activations, and residual connections, utilizing exponentially increasing dilation to enhance performance. This architecture not only supports the scalability needed for long time series but also optimizes memory usage during training and evaluation. Overall, the proposed method demonstrates significant potential for effective time series classification and forecasting.
