In this paper, the authors, Jean-Yves Franceschi, Aymeric Dieuleveut, and Martin Jaggi, address the challenges associated with multivariate time series data in the context of unsupervised representation learning. They propose a novel unsupervised method that aims to learn universal embeddings for time series, which are often of variable lengths and sparsely labeled in real-world scenarios.

The authors emphasize the need for scalable and efficient techniques that can handle time series of unequal lengths during both training and inference. To achieve this, they introduce an encoder designed as a deep convolutional neural network utilizing causal dilated convolutions. This architecture allows for the generation of fixed-length vector representations regardless of the input time series length.

A key contribution of the paper is the development of a triplet loss function that incorporates time-based negative sampling, facilitating the learning of general-purpose representations. The authors provide extensive experimental results demonstrating the quality, transferability, and practicality of their learned representations, showcasing their method's effectiveness in addressing the unique challenges posed by time series data.
