The paper discusses the optimization process for a multi-view representation learning framework known as AE2-Nets. It presents the gradient calculations for updating weights and biases in relation to different views, using specific equations to derive the necessary gradients. The parameter updates are governed by a learning rate, and the latent representation is optimized through a similar process.

The connection to Canonical Correlation Analysis (CCA) and matrix factorization is established, suggesting that the AE2-Nets model can be interpreted as a generative model. It highlights how fixing features instead of learning them through autoencoders leads to a formulation akin to CCA, emphasizing the learning of a common representation.

The experimental section evaluates AE2-Nets against other state-of-the-art multi-view representation methods using various real-world datasets, including handwritten digits, Caltech101, ORL, COIL-20, and Caltech-UCSD Birds. The evaluation focuses on clustering and classification tasks, measuring performance with standard metrics.

The optimization algorithm for AE2-Nets is summarized in Algorithm 1, detailing the iterative process of updating view-specific parameters and the latent representation until convergence is achieved. The experiments showcase the effectiveness of AE2-Nets in handling multi-view data, reinforcing its practical application in representation learning.
