The paper presents I-BERT, an approach for integer-only quantization of BERT models, specifically RoBERTa-Base and RoBERTa-Large, evaluated on the GLUE benchmark. The quantization process involves fine-tuning the models to maintain high accuracy while reducing the computational load. 

Table 2 shows the performance comparison between the baseline models (trained using full-precision FP32) and the I-BERT models (quantized to INT8). For RoBERTa-Base, the average accuracy of the baseline is 86.0, while I-BERT achieves 86.3, with minor differences in task-specific accuracies. For RoBERTa-Large, I-BERT outperforms the baseline with an average accuracy of 89.5 compared to 89.0. 

Table 3 details the inference latency speedup achieved with INT8 models versus FP32 models, indicating that I-BERT inference is approximately 3.08 times faster for BERT-Base and 3.56 times faster for BERT-Large, with maximum speedups reaching 4.00. This speed improvement is particularly beneficial when deployed on hardware optimized for integer computations. 

Additionally, the paper includes ablation studies that assess the impact of different GELU approximation methods on model accuracy. The i-GELU method, an integer-only approximation, is compared against the standard GELU and h-GELU. Results in Table 4 indicate that i-GELU consistently yields better accuracy across multiple tasks compared to h-GELU, demonstrating that h-GELU is not a sufficiently precise approximation. Notably, i-GELU even surpasses the accuracy of the full-precision GELU in certain cases, although the authors do not provide a definitive explanation for this phenomenon.

Overall, the findings underscore the effectiveness of I-BERT for quantization without significant loss in performance, while also achieving notable improvements in inference speed.
