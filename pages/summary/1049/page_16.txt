This paper summarizes various gradient-based methods for explainable artificial intelligence (XAI), highlighting their interpretations, applications, and inherent challenges. Key methods include Saliency Maps, which visualize gradients and neuron activations but suffer from significant limitations; Grad-CAM, which localizes neuronal activities for class-specific queries; and Axiomatic Attribution Maps, which introduce axioms to enhance the reliability of saliency maps. PatternNet and PatternAttribution offer cleaner attributions but may be sensitive to irrelevant features.

The discussion extends to model intrinsic explainability, emphasizing that models like decision trees and Bayesian Rule Lists (BRL) are inherently interpretable due to their structure. While BRL improves interpretability by generating a posterior distribution over decision rules, its effectiveness diminishes with complexity. Additionally, Generalized Additive Models (GAMs) and their extensions, such as GA2Ms, strive to balance accuracy and interpretability, though they may require extensive decision trees and face challenges with over-regularization.

The paper underscores the importance of these methods in mission-critical applications while acknowledging their practical disadvantages and the need for improved techniques to enhance explainability and reliability in AI systems.
