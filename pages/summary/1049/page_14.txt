This paper summarizes various perturbation-based methods used in explainable artificial intelligence (XAI), focusing on their interpretations and applications in neural networks. The methods discussed include:

1. **DeConv Nets**: Developed by Zeiler et al., this method visualizes neural activations by occluding input instances. The authors trained an AlexNet model on the ImageNet dataset, revealing insights into feature generalization and dataset biases.

2. **LIME**: Introduced by Ribeiro et al., LIME generates local explanations through iterative perturbation of input data. A user study indicated that LIME can enhance the interpretability of untrustworthy classifiers.

3. **SHAP**: Lundberg et al. created SHAP, which analyzes feature correlations via a game theoretic approach. SHAP consistently outperformed LIME and aligned well with human reasoning, although some studies question its impact on decision-making.

4. **Prediction Difference Analysis**: Zintgraf et al. examined the effects of removing individual features to understand their positive and negative correlations with model outputs. This study used various models trained on the ImageNet dataset.

5. **Randomized Input Sampling**: Petsiuk et al. explored saliency maps through randomized input masking techniques.

6. **Randomization and Feature Testing**: Burns et al.'s method used counterfactual feature replacements to assess feature importance.

The paper also discusses gradient-based methods for generating saliency maps, such as the work by Simonyan et al. and the improvements made in DeConvNets. It highlights the significance of gradient values in backpropagation for accurate visualizations.

Furthermore, the paper covers Gradient Class Activation Mapping (CAM) and its extensions, Grad-CAM and Grad-CAM++, which enhance class-specific localization in images. The Salient Relevance (SR) maps proposed by Li et al. provide context-aware saliency values for pixels, differentiating between significant and irrelevant features in an image.

Overall, the methods discussed contribute to enhancing the interpretability of deep learning models, providing insights into their decision-making processes.
