The paper presents a comprehensive survey on explainable artificial intelligence (XAI), focusing on the methods of explainability in deep learning algorithms. It categorizes XAI approaches into three main areas: scope, methodology, and usage. 

**Scope** distinguishes between local and global explanations. Local methods focus on individual data instances, generating specific explanation maps for each input, while global methods aim to understand the model as a whole, often providing insights based on a group of data instances.

**Methodology** outlines the core algorithmic approaches, which include:
- **BackPropagation**: Relies on gradients back-propagated from the output layer to the input layer.
- **Perturbation**: Involves random or systematic changes to the input features to assess their impact on the model's predictions.
- **Intrinsic**: Explainability is integrated into the model architecture, making it less transferable.
- **Post-Hoc**: These algorithms can be applied to pre-trained models, independent of their architecture.

**Usage** examines how XAI methods are developed and whether they can be integrated into existing models or are applicable across different architectures.

The paper highlights the challenges posed by the "black-box" nature of modern AI models, particularly in understanding deep learning systems where the end-user has limited access to model internals. It also emphasizes the importance of a systematic taxonomy for XAI methods to enhance clarity and accessibility.

The contributions of the survey include:
1. A systematic analysis and taxonomization of explainable algorithms to improve understanding.
2. A summary and classification of the mathematical models and algorithms in recent XAI research.
3. A comparative generation of explanation maps for various XAI algorithms, discussing limitations and future directions for enhancing trust and fairness in AI.

The survey is grounded in a review of literature from 2007 to 2020, utilizing a range of academic databases and employing specific keywords related to explainability in AI. The paper aims to provide a detailed understanding of XAI techniques, their methodologies, and their evaluation strategies, alongside popular open-source implementations.
