The paper provides a comprehensive summary of published research on explainability and interpretability of deep learning algorithms, highlighting various methodologies and their applications. It categorizes methods based on their scope, such as global or local explanations, and whether they are model-specific or model-agnostic. Key methodologies include Bayesian averaging over decision trees, activation maximization, gradient-based saliency maps, and LIME, among others, spanning from 2007 to 2020.

The authors discuss metrics for evaluating feature importance, such as model contrast score (MCS), input dependence rate (IDR), and input independence rate (IIR). They introduce concepts of faithfulness and monotonicity to assess the correlation between feature importance and model performance. A human-grounded evaluation benchmark is presented to compare machine-generated explanations against human annotations, acknowledging potential bias but suggesting that large populations can mitigate this.

Additionally, the paper notes the significance of open-source software packages in enhancing reproducibility in research. It highlights the InterpretML package, which supports various explainability methods for different model types. Overall, the paper underscores the ongoing advancements in XAI, emphasizing the importance of robust evaluation and practical tools for researchers.
