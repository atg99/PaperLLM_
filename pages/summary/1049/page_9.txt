The paper discusses the application of Shapley values in the SHAP method for fair payout distribution among data features in machine learning models. SHAP interprets features as players in a coalition game, where explanations are derived as linear functions of these features. Various enhancements to the baseline SHAP method are introduced, including KernelSHAP for efficient evaluation on large inputs, LinearSHAP for linear models, Low-Order SHAP for small coalition sizes, and DeepSHAP for deep neural networks.

The paper highlights several studies utilizing SHAP in the medical domain, such as Antwarg et al.'s use of SHAP to explain autoencoder anomaly detection, and Sundararajan et al.'s critique of SHAP's limitations, which led to the development of the Baseline Shapley method. Aas et al. explored dependencies among features in SHAP values, while Lundberg et al. proposed TreeExplainer for tree-based models, and VegaGarcia et al. applied DeepSHAP to explain LSTM network predictions.

Additionally, the paper emphasizes the importance of global explanations for understanding AI model behavior across a suite of input data points. Globally explainable methods aim to summarize model behavior, enhancing transparency by interpreting complex models in simpler terms. Rule-based and tree-based models are noted for their inherent interpretability, while globally explainable methods help elucidate the overall behavior of machine learning models on diverse datasets.
