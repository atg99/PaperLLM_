The paper discusses the SpRAy technique for analyzing local relevance maps generated by machine learning models using Layer-wise Relevance Propagation (LRP). The method involves resizing the relevance maps for computational efficiency and applying spectral clustering to identify significant clusters in a high-dimensional space. Eigenmap analysis is employed to determine relevant clusters based on eigengaps, and optional visualizations using t-SNE are provided.

Further, the paper introduces Global Attribution Mapping, which utilizes well-defined feature semantics to create a pair-wise rank distance matrix for clustering local attributions. This approach allows for the identification of differences in explanations across subpopulations.

Neural Additive Models (NAMs) are also presented as a novel method that combines deep learning with additive modeling. NAMs allow individual neural networks to focus on single input features, overcoming limitations of traditional Generalized Additive Models (GAMs). The architecture incorporates exp-centered hidden units to better fit complex, jagged functions in real-world data. The model outputs provide interpretable contributions of each feature, indicating their positive or negative impact on predictions.

The paper categorizes Explainable AI (XAI) methodologies into two main types: perturbation-based methods, which involve modifying input data to generate explanations, and backpropagation-based methods, which focus on model architecture and parameters. This classification highlights the different approaches to achieving interpretability in machine learning models.
