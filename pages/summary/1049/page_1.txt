This paper by Arun Das and Paul Rad explores the field of Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI), emphasizing its significance in enhancing the interpretability of deep neural networks (DNNs) used in critical applications such as healthcare and autonomous vehicles. The authors highlight the challenges posed by the black-box nature of DNNs, which raises ethical and trust issues due to their complexity and lack of transparency.

The paper begins by providing a comprehensive overview of the current XAI landscape, proposing a taxonomy that categorizes XAI techniques based on their explanation scope, underlying methodologies, and usage levels. It presents key principles in XAI research and outlines a historical timeline of significant studies from 2007 to 2020. 

The authors delve into various algorithms and approaches in XAI, evaluating the effectiveness of eight XAI algorithms on image data and discussing their limitations. They argue that reliable explanations are essential for building trust in AI systems, as they help users understand AI decisions and promote ethical standards. The paper concludes by suggesting future directions for improving XAI evaluation and advancing the field.

In summary, the paper underscores the necessity of XAI in fostering transparency, trust, and fairness in AI systems, particularly as these technologies increasingly impact human lives.
