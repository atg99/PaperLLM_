Sparse LDA and Sparse Penalized Discriminant Analysis (SPDA) are highlighted as advancements in interpretability within machine learning. Graph-Sparse LDA is a Bayesian non-parametric model designed for hierarchical datasets, while SPDA enhances the interpretability and accuracy of algorithms applied to FMRI data. However, the integration of these model-intrinsic methods into existing high-accuracy models poses challenges, requiring meticulous algorithm development.

The paper also distinguishes between post-hoc explainability methods, which analyze pre-trained classifiers as either black or white boxes, depending on their access to internal model structures. Post-hoc methods are advantageous as they add interpretability to already accurate models without compromising their performance. Various techniques like deconvolution networks, saliency maps, and Shapley sampling methods exemplify post-hoc approaches that can be applied across different network architectures.

The evaluation of explainability methods remains a significant challenge, with a focus on human-in-the-loop evaluations rather than quantitative metrics. The paper outlines essential constraints for effective explanations, such as identity, stability, consistency, separability, and similarity. It also emphasizes the need for minimal implementation costs and the ability to detect bias within data.

Emerging evaluation schemes like the System Causability Scale (SCS) and Benchmarking Attribution Methods (BAM) are discussed as frameworks to enhance the understanding and effectiveness of explainability in AI systems. The SCS evaluates human-AI interfaces in specific domains, while BAM provides a dataset and methodology for assessing feature attribution correctness, aiming to ensure that important features consistently influence model decisions.
