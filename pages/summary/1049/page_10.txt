The paper discusses various techniques for providing global explanations of machine learning models, particularly focusing on deep learning. 

1. **Global Surrogate Models**: These models approximate the predictions of complex AI models using simpler, interpretable models like linear models or decision trees. They help in understanding model generalization and variations. Techniques such as SHAP and LIME serve as surrogate models to analyze local correlations.

2. **Class Model Visualization**: This method, based on activation maximization, aims to generate images that represent a specific class. By maximizing the class probability score, it provides insights into what the model has learned. The generated images, often referred to as 'deep dream' visuals, illustrate the characteristics of classes such as goose, ostrich, and limousine.

3. **LIME Algorithm for Global Explanations**: The LIME model has been enhanced with a submodular pick algorithm (SP-LIME) to derive global insights from individual data instances. This method generates a global decision boundary by selecting non-redundant features, allowing for a comprehensive understanding of the model's behavior based on input samples.

4. **Concept Activation Vectors (CAVs)**: Introduced by Kim et al., CAVs serve as a global explainability method that interprets the internal states of neural networks using human-friendly concepts. By mapping model features to human-understandable concepts, CAVs facilitate the interpretation of model predictions. The paper also describes a method called Testing with CAVs (TCA V), which evaluates the sensitivity of class predictions to changes in inputs in relation to specific concepts.

Overall, these methods aim to enhance the interpretability of complex AI models, providing insights into their behavior and decision-making processes.
