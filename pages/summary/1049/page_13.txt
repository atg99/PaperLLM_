This section discusses various perturbation-based methods and gradient-based methods for explainable artificial intelligence (XAI). Perturbation-based methods involve manipulating input features to analyze their impact on model predictions. Techniques like LIME and SHAP are highlighted for their use of superpixels and feature removal to understand feature correlations. DeConvolution networks, as introduced by Zeiler et al., visualize neural activations by occluding input segments, allowing for deeper insights into a convolutional network's learning process.

Additionally, Prediction Difference Analysis by Zintgraf et al. assigns relevance values to input features, capturing their positive and negative correlations with model decisions. The RISE method uses randomized masks to perturb images and generate saliency maps, while the Interpretability Randomization Test and One-Shot Feature Test focus on identifying important features through hypothesis testing.

In contrast, gradient-based methods leverage the backward flow of information in a neural network to assess the influence of neurons on output classes. This approach enables a different understanding of feature relevance and neuronal activity, complementing the insights gained from perturbation-based techniques. Overall, these methods collectively enhance our understanding of model behavior and feature significance in deep learning frameworks.
