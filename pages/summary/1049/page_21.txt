The paper discusses the limitations and advancements in explainable artificial intelligence (XAI) methods, particularly focusing on the effectiveness of various techniques in generating explanations for machine learning models. It highlights that traditional methods like Guided Backprop and Guided GradCAM can produce explanations even when models are not properly trained. Newer approaches, such as explaining with Concepts, represent a meta-explanation class that enhances both perturbation- and gradient-based XAI methods by providing additional insights into class predictions.

The introduction of Interpretable Basis Decomposition (IBD) allows for the decomposition of individual explanations based on distinct objects or scenes, enabling evaluation of each concept's importance in decision-making. Moreover, PatternNet and PatternAttribution are proposed as improvements to gradient-based methods, offering a neuron-wise attribution of input signals to output classes.

The conclusion emphasizes the need for caution in trusting highly predictive classifiers due to issues like data bias and adversarial examples. The paper categorizes various XAI methods based on their scope and methodology, indicating a significant focus on model-agnostic post-hoc explainability. It notes a trend toward using concepts as explanations while acknowledging the challenges in evaluating these methods. The research landscape in XAI is evolving, with user studies revealing that typical explanation maps might not effectively aid decision-making due to human biases. Recent efforts show promise in enhancing XAI evaluations through human-grounded assessments.
