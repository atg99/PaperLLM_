In this section, the authors evaluate various gradient-based and perturbation-based explainable artificial intelligence (XAI) techniques using an InceptionV3 model pre-trained on the ImageNet dataset. Figure 19 presents explanation maps for four different images: a koala, a sandbar, an arabian camel, and a leaf beetle, with respective prediction accuracies of 94.5%, 38.0%, 17.4%, and 95.5%. 

The techniques compared include gradient-based methods like saliency maps, gradient times input, integrated gradients, Layer-wise Relevance Propagation (LRP), DeepLIFT, and Grad-CAM, alongside perturbation-based methods such as LIME and SHAP. Each method generates attribution maps that visualize the importance of different pixels in the model's decision-making process. For instance, Grad-CAM produces a heatmap indicating pixel influence, while SHAP values range from -0.3 to +0.3, showing how features impact class probabilities.

The authors highlight the effectiveness of integrated gradients, particularly in images with lower class probabilities, as seen in the sandbar image where high attributions are clearly identified around relevant features like the sandy beach and plastic chairs. This comparison of explanation maps underscores the strengths and weaknesses of the different XAI techniques in interpreting model predictions. The visualizations were created using the DeepExplain package for gradient methods and respective individual implementations for Grad-CAM, LIME, and SHAP.
