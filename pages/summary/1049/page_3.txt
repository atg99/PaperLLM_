The paper discusses two main categories of explainable artificial intelligence (XAI) methods: backpropagation-based and perturbation-based techniques. Backpropagation-based methods, such as saliency maps and class activation maps, generate attributions during the backpropagation stage by utilizing partial derivatives of activations after performing one or more forward passes through the neural network. In contrast, perturbation-based methods focus on altering the feature set of an input instance through techniques like occlusion, feature substitution, and masking, typically requiring only a forward pass to produce attribution representations.

The usage of explainable methods can be categorized into model-intrinsic and model-agnostic approaches. Model-intrinsic methods are often specific to the architecture of the neural network, necessitating significant adjustments with any architectural changes. Conversely, model-agnostic post-hoc explanations allow for the interpretation of predictions from existing models without altering their structure, applicable across various input types, including images and text.

Additionally, the paper outlines evaluation strategies for XAI algorithms, emphasizing the need for improved methodologies to assess their performance in terms of transparency, trust, and bias understanding. It highlights the immaturity of current evaluation methods and the potential for further research. The authors also provide a list of popular open-source software packages compatible with Scikit-Learn, TensorFlow, and PyTorch, which facilitate the application of XAI methods.

Mathematical notations used throughout the paper are standardized to enhance clarity and comprehension for readers. The authors define explainability as a suite of techniques aimed at improving the transparency and trustworthiness of AI systems, noting that explanations serve as metadata providing insights into AI decisions and internal functionalities. The paper concludes by summarizing the objectives of explainable algorithms in elucidating predictions made by deep learning models.
