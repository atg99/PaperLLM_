The paper presents a comprehensive overview of various feature selection methods categorized by their filtering and search strategies. It distinguishes between univariate and multivariate approaches, highlighting the specific techniques employed in each case.

For univariate methods, key techniques include NDM, SOP Filter-RB, and several others such as GI, MI, and IG, which are suitable for both textual and numeric data. These methods demonstrate varying degrees of effectiveness based on the data type and the specific attributes they aim to analyze.

In contrast, multivariate methods, including RRFS, mRMR, RSM, and GUFS, utilize greedy search strategies but face challenges such as higher computational complexity and potential local optima trapping. Additionally, methods like MECY_FS apply genetic algorithms (GA) and are limited to numeric datasets, showcasing a trade-off between speed and performance in text datasets.

The paper also discusses advanced techniques such as ACO-based methods (RRFSACO, GCACO, MGCACO) and graph-based approaches (FASTFS, GCNC), which involve more complex search strategies but can capture feature dependencies effectively.

Finally, the paper highlights the role of sequential methods such as MICGSOFS and MBFFS, as well as wrapper techniques like BT-SFS, emphasizing their reliance on initial solutions and the risks of local optima entrapment while maintaining low computational complexity.

Overall, the document underscores the diversity of feature selection techniques and their applicability depending on the data type and the specific requirements of the analysis.
