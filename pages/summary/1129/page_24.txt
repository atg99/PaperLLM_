This collection of papers presents various methodologies and algorithms for feature selection, particularly in the context of high-dimensional data and applications such as cancer classification and stock market analysis. 

Al-Ani et al. (2013) introduce a differential evolution approach combined with a wheel-based search strategy for effective feature subset selection. Al-Betar et al. (2020) propose a TRIZ-inspired bat algorithm aimed at gene selection to enhance cancer classification accuracy. Al-Zoubi et al. (2020) focus on salp chain-based optimization techniques to improve support vector machines in medical diagnostics.

Alshamlan et al. (2015) present the Genetic Bee Colony algorithm as a novel method for gene selection in microarray cancer data. Jain et al. (2000) review statistical pattern recognition, providing a foundational understanding relevant to the feature selection process. 

Arslan and Ozturk (2019) apply a multi-hive artificial bee colony programming technique for symbolic regression, emphasizing feature selection in high-dimensional spaces. Bandyopadhyay et al. (2014) integrate dense subgraph finding with feature clustering for unsupervised feature selection, while Banka and Dara (2015) develop a Hamming distance-based binary particle swarm optimization algorithm for high-dimensional feature selection.

Barak et al. (2015) utilize a wrapper ANFIS-ICA method for stock market timing and feature selection based on Japanese candlestick patterns. Basir and Ahmad (2014) compare various swarm algorithms for reducing feature sets. Battiti (1994) discusses the utilization of mutual information for feature selection in supervised neural networks.

Bi et al. (2019) introduce a multi-feature selection approach for gender identification from handwriting using kernel mutual information. Brezočnik et al. (2018) provide a comprehensive review of swarm intelligence algorithms applied to feature selection. Lastly, Cadenas et al. (2013) explore a filter-wrapper based feature subset selection method tailored for low-quality data, and Carmen Lai et al. (2006) discuss the random subspace method for multivariate feature selection.

Overall, these studies highlight the significance of advanced algorithms and techniques in optimizing feature selection processes across various domains, particularly in enhancing classification performance and accuracy.
