The paper presents a comparative analysis of various feature selection methods, specifically focusing on the performance of ACO-based, PSO-based, DE-based, and WOA methods across different classifiers, including SVM, Naive Bayes, and AdaBoost. The results indicate that the ACO-based method consistently achieved the highest average classification accuracy across all classifiers. For instance, it attained an average accuracy of 79.52% with the SVM classifier, surpassing the PSO-based method by 0.70%. Additionally, the ACO-based method also led in accuracy with the AdaBoost classifier, recording 79.26%, while PSO and DE methods followed with 78.76% and 77.72%, respectively.

Execution times for each feature selection method were reported, revealing that the DE-based method had the longest execution time, while the PSO-based method exhibited the shortest average execution time across all datasets. The ACO-based method was ranked second in terms of execution time following the PSO method. Overall, the findings highlight the effectiveness of the ACO-based feature selection method in achieving high classification accuracy while maintaining reasonable execution times compared to other methods.
