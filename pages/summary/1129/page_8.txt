The Frog Leaping Algorithm has been developed to enhance the accuracy of acoustic defect detection by reducing the dimensionality of original features through a single-objective function defined using the Minimum Redundancy Maximum Relevance (MRMR) criterion, which is optimized using Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO). Following feature selection, a neural network classifier is employed for final acoustic defect detection. 

Various studies have explored PSO-based methods for dimensionality reduction and feature selection across different domains. For instance, Qasim and Algamal proposed a PSO-based regression model that leverages logistic regression and the Bayesian information criterion as a fitness function for medical datasets. Prasad et al. introduced a recursive PSO scheme for feature selection in DNA microarray datasets, integrating multiple filter-based ranking methods. Pashaei et al. presented a hybrid feature selection method for cancer classification, combining the binary black hole algorithm with PSO to enhance exploration and exploitation capabilities. Gunasundari et al. applied a multi-swarm binary PSO algorithm for feature selection in liver and kidney cancer data. Yan et al. developed a hybrid feature selection method using PSO to improve the accuracy of Laser-induced breakdown spectroscopy analysis, while Xue et al. proposed a PSO-based feature selection approach with self-adaptive parameters to tackle high-dimensional datasets, significantly enhancing the search capabilities of particle optimization algorithms.

Ant Colony Optimization (ACO), introduced by Dorigo in the early 1990s, serves as a multi-agent approach for solving optimization problems, inspired by the behavior of ants in finding optimal paths. ACO has been effectively utilized for feature selection. Kabir et al. integrated ACO with neural networks and information gain to select salient features from datasets. Li et al. developed an ACO framework for gene selection in DNA microarray data, employing a two-stage process for pivotal gene selection. Chen et al. represented the feature space as a directed graph in their ACO-based feature selection method. Forsati et al. introduced a novel ACO variant that combines local search strategies to avoid local optima entrapment. Other studies, such as those by Ke et al. and Tabakhi et al., explored ACO in the context of rough set theory and unsupervised feature selection, respectively, utilizing clustering techniques to address feature selection without relying on learning algorithms.
