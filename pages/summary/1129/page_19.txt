The paper presents a comparative analysis of various swarm intelligence (SI) based feature selection methods applied to different datasets, including Arrhythmia, Madelon, Isolet, and Colon. Each method's performance is evaluated based on two metrics: accuracy and execution time. 

For the Arrhythmia dataset, methods like FA and GWO achieved the highest accuracy, while PSO showed the lowest execution time. In the Madelon dataset, BA emerged as the most accurate, while PSO again had the fastest execution time. The Isolet dataset showed a similar trend, with ABC achieving the highest accuracy and PSO maintaining the lowest execution time. In the Colon dataset, ACO had the highest accuracy, but PSO consistently outperformed in terms of execution speed.

The results indicate that single objective SI-based feature selection methods, particularly PSO, are generally faster than multi-objective methods like ACO and ABC. This is attributed to the computational complexity of multi-objective methods, which evaluate multiple criteria for fitness calculation. Overall, PSO was identified as the most efficient method across all datasets, followed by WOA and GSA.
