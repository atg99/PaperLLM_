The paper discusses various machine learning algorithms, focusing on Support Vector Machine (SVM), Naïve Bayes (NB), and AdaBoost (AB). SVM aims to maximize the margin between data samples and has demonstrated effectiveness in classification and regression tasks. Naïve Bayes classifies data based on the independence of random variables, utilizing Bayes' theorem. AdaBoost, a meta-algorithm, sequentially fits classifiers to the same dataset, adjusting weights for misclassified instances to improve focus on difficult cases.

The experimental framework employed is Weka, which implements SVM, NB, and AB through methods like SMO, Naïve Bayes, and AdaBoostM1. The study evaluates feature selection methods categorized into wrapper-based and filter-based approaches. Wrapper-based methods, including PSO, ACO, and GWO, are contrasted with filter-based methods like PSO and ACO to ensure fair comparisons due to their differing computational complexities and accuracies.

Results indicate that classification accuracy and feature subset size are key performance metrics. The experiments reveal that the PSO-based feature selection method generally outperforms other methods, achieving a classification accuracy of 92.84% on the SpamBase dataset with the SVM classifier. Other methods showed lower accuracies, highlighting the effectiveness of PSO-based techniques in feature selection. Overall, the paper presents a systematic evaluation of various feature selection methods within the context of different classifiers, emphasizing the superior performance of PSO-based methods.
