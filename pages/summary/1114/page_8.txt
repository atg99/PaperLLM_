The paper discusses the application of a Tree Augmented Naive Bayes (TAN) classifier to analyze Twitter data in the context of significant events, specifically the 2010 Chilean earthquake and the 2017 Catalan independence referendum. The TAN model offers a nuanced understanding of public sentiment and communication patterns surrounding these events, contrasting with traditional qualitative research methods like interviews and focus groups.

In the case of the Chilean earthquake, the TAN model illustrates the public's call for assistance and recovery efforts, highlighting the controversial decision to involve military forces in urban areas. For the Catalan referendum, the model reveals the complexities and controversies regarding its democratic legitimacy, showcasing various perspectives, including state repression against independence supporters and the Spanish government's refusal to acknowledge Catalan demands.

The paper emphasizes that while TAN can provide a detailed qualitative view of Twitter communications, challenges remain in accurately coding and classifying data, especially as the volume of tweets grows during critical events. This dynamic nature of social media can lead to the dilution of meaningful communication patterns, complicating the identification of significant information. The authors call for strategies to enhance the accuracy of data interpretation, increase user participation, and improve geo-location techniques in future analyses. Overall, the TAN model is presented as a valuable tool for unpacking the complexities of social media discourse in relation to critical events.
