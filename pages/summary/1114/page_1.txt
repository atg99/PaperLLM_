The paper discusses sentiment analysis of Twitter data during critical events using Bayesian network classifiers. It focuses on two significant datasets: the 2010 Chilean earthquake and the 2017 Catalan independence referendum. The authors utilize a Bayesian factor approach to manage the number of edges in the Bayesian networks, resulting in more realistic models. The findings indicate that Bayesian network classifiers are effective and competitive compared to support vector machines and random forests, particularly when there is a sufficient amount of training data. Additionally, the resulting networks provide insights into the relationships among words, contributing qualitative information that enhances the understanding of event dynamics. The paper highlights the growing importance of social media, especially Twitter, in shaping public opinion and influencing traditional media during critical events. The authors argue for the utility of Bayesian networks in sentiment classification, emphasizing their ability to reveal underlying probabilistic relationships among features, which is often obscured in traditional black-box models.
