The paper focuses on analyzing tweets related to two significant events: the 2010 Chilean earthquake and the 2017 Catalan independence referendum. The authors utilized the twitteR package in R to extract a dataset of 2,187 tweets for the Chilean earthquake and 60,000 tweets for the Catalan referendum, with specific keywords guiding the collection process. The tweets were gathered over defined time periods, with the Chilean earthquake data spanning from February 27 to March 2, 2010, and the Catalan referendum data collected from September 22 to October 30, 2017.

A crucial step in the analysis was the pre-processing of tweets to eliminate inconsistencies and redundancies. The authors removed URLs, hashtags, user mentions, punctuation, numbers, and non-Spanish tweets. They also standardized the text by converting it to lowercase and addressing repeated characters in words.

For feature representation, the bag-of-words (BOW) technique was employed to convert the pre-processed tweets into a numeric format, resulting in a term-document matrix. Each tweet was represented as a vector indicating the presence of words from a predefined vocabulary.

Sentiment analysis was performed by labeling each tweet as either negative or positive. The authors adapted a sentiment scoring measure based on counts of positive and negative words, which were translated into Spanish from an English list of around 7,000 sentiment words. The sentiment polarity was quantified, allowing the classification of tweets into discrete sentiment classes.

The study highlights the potential of social media data for understanding public sentiment during critical events while emphasizing the need for careful data processing and sentiment analysis methodologies.
