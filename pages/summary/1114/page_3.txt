The paper discusses the use of sentiment analysis on Twitter data to understand emotional dynamics during critical events, such as natural disasters and crises. It identifies three key phases of such events: the incubation phase, the critical event itself, and the post-crisis restructuring phase. The authors argue that combining qualitative and quantitative analyses can enhance the understanding of these phases and improve disaster management strategies. They highlight the importance of Twitter for real-time monitoring and public awareness regarding risks, as well as for facilitating responses during and after crises.

The research aims to evaluate whether established sentiment analysis methods for English texts, specifically Support Vector Machines (SVM) and Random Forests (RF), are effective for Spanish tweets. Additionally, it seeks to explore the relationships among words for sentiment classification using Bayesian networks, focusing on two datasets: the 2010 Chilean earthquake and the 2017 Catalan independence referendum.

The paper is structured into several sections, beginning with a background on classifiers used for sentiment analysis, followed by an introduction to Bayesian network classifiers, a detailed methodology, and results analysis. The authors reference various studies that demonstrate the effectiveness of different classifiers and preprocessing techniques for sentiment analysis on Twitter, including deep learning methods and the integration of multimodal data.

In summary, the research contributes to the understanding of sentiment dynamics on social media during critical events and assesses the applicability of existing sentiment analysis techniques to Spanish-language data.
