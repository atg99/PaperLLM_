This paper presents a comparison of phoneme error rates (PER) and word error rates (WER) across various models and training setups on the TIMIT and Multilingual Librispeech datasets. Table 2 summarizes the PER results for both matched and unmatched training data. Notably, the wav2vec-U model demonstrates competitive performance, achieving a PER of 11.3 on core-dev for the matched setup and 13.8 for the unmatched setup with the inclusion of self-training (ST). The results indicate a significant improvement over earlier models such as GAN and EODM in both setups.

Table 3 details the WER results on the MLS dataset for multiple languages, showcasing the wav2vec-U + ST model's effectiveness with no labeled data, achieving an average WER of 18.6 across languages compared to the supervised model by Pratap et al. which had a WER of 10.0 with full labeled data. The paper also highlights the performance of wav2vec-U on low-resource languages like Tatar and Kyrgyz, where it outperforms previous work despite using significantly less audio data.

The related work section acknowledges the foundational contributions of self-training and self-supervised learning in speech recognition, referencing significant studies that have shaped the field. The authors emphasize the advancements made in leveraging unlabeled data to enhance speech recognition accuracy, particularly in low-resource scenarios.
