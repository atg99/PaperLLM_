The paper discusses a generative adversarial network (GAN) framework for phonemized speech transcription, employing a discriminator that processes both real and generated phonemized text sequences. The discriminator, designed as a convolutional neural network (CNN), outputs a probability indicating the authenticity of the input sample. The training objective combines the original GAN objective with additional penalties: a gradient penalty for stability, a segment smoothness penalty to enforce consistency in adjacent segment outputs, and a phoneme diversity penalty to ensure a broad usage of the phoneme vocabulary.

The gradient penalty is calculated based on the norm of the discriminator's gradient concerning the input, stabilizing the training process. The segment smoothness penalty encourages the generator to produce similar outputs for closely related segments, addressing the granularity of speech audio segmentation. The phoneme diversity loss maximizes the entropy of the generator's phoneme output, promoting the use of a diverse phoneme set.

To evaluate the model's performance without labeled data, the authors introduce an unsupervised cross-validation metric that incorporates language model negative log-likelihood (NLL) and vocabulary usage. LM-NLL indicates transcription fluency, while vocabulary usage assesses the breadth of phonemes produced by the model. This metric aids in tasks such as early stopping and hyper-parameter selection, ultimately contributing to the development of effective speech recognition models without supervision.
