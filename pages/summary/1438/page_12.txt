The provided references encompass a range of studies and methodologies related to Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR), machine learning, and natural language processing. Key contributions include:

2. Jang et al. (2016) introduce categorical reparameterization using Gumbel-Softmax, facilitating gradient-based optimization in discrete variables.
3. Jegou et al. (2011) discuss product quantization techniques for efficient nearest neighbor search in large datasets.
4. Johnson and Jusczyk (2001) explore how infants utilize speech cues over statistical patterns for word segmentation.
5. Kahn et al. (2020) focus on self-training methods for end-to-end speech recognition, while their subsequent work presents the Libri-light benchmark for ASR with minimal supervision.
6. Kamper et al. (2017) propose a segmental framework for unsupervised speech recognition, along with a model for unsupervised segmentation and clustering of speech.
7. Kawakami et al. (2020) emphasize the development of robust multilingual speech representations.
8. Kingma and Ba (2015) present the Adam optimization algorithm, widely used in training machine learning models.
9. Kreuk et al. (2020) apply self-supervised contrastive learning to phoneme segmentation without supervision.
10. Lample et al. (2018) demonstrate unsupervised machine translation using only monolingual data.
11. Lee and Glass (2012) introduce a nonparametric Bayesian approach for acoustic model discovery.
12. Liu et al. (2019) and Liu et al. (2018) focus on unsupervised speech recognition and phoneme recognition through adversarial learning techniques.

These studies collectively advance the understanding of ASR, contributing novel methodologies for unsupervised learning, segmentation, and multilingual representation, while addressing challenges in the field.
