The paper presents a detailed analysis of word error rates (WER) on the Librispeech dev/test sets using both 960 hours of unlabeled audio from Librispeech and 53.2k hours from Libri-Light, employing representations from the wav2vec 2.0 LARGE model. It outlines results for various models under supervised, self-supervised, and unsupervised learning paradigms.

In the supervised learning section, models like DeepSpeech 2, Fully Conv, and TDNN+Kaldi are evaluated, showing varying WERs across clean and other data sets. The self-supervised learning results highlight improvements with models such as wav2vec 2.0 and its variants, including wav2vec 2.0 + NST, which achieve lower WERs compared to prior models.

The paper also provides a comparison to prior unsupervised work on the TIMIT benchmark, demonstrating that wav2vec-U outperforms previous methods in both matched and unmatched settings, significantly reducing phoneme error rates (PER) compared to existing benchmarks.

Additionally, the performance of the method on non-English languages is explored using the multilingual Librispeech corpus. Although performance is lower than supervised systems, wav2vec-U shows promise for generalization across multiple languages. The study further examines three low-resource languages, Swahili, Kyrgyz, and Tatar, with limited labeled data, indicating potential effectiveness in low-resource scenarios.

Overall, the findings underscore the efficacy of wav2vec-U and its enhancements through self-training, showcasing its competitive performance in both English and multilingual applications while paving the way for future research in unsupervised speech recognition.
