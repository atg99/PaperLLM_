This paper discusses a method for generating phoneme transcriptions using a language model (pLM) and evaluates its performance against supervised speech recognition systems, particularly on the Librispeech benchmark. The authors introduce a vocabulary usage metric, U(P), to optimize the model configurations by minimizing the vocabulary-usage adjusted negative log-likelihood (NLL). The process involves selecting configurations that maintain a balance between NLL and vocabulary usage, with specific constraints that allow for flexible inclusion based on performance metrics.

In the results section, the authors compare their unsupervised approach to state-of-the-art supervised systems. Using the frozen representations from a wav2vec 2.0 model trained on Libri-Light, they achieve a word error rate (WER) of 5.9 on the noisy test set, indicating that unsupervised methods can perform competitively. The paper highlights the effectiveness of self-training, showing that training on just the Librispeech dataset leads to a WER of 6.4, which is close to the performance achieved using a larger dataset. The authors conclude that their method demonstrates significant promise in unsupervised speech recognition, especially with minimal parameters involved in adversarial training.
