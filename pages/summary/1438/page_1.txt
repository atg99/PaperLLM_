The paper introduces wav2vec-U, a novel method for unsupervised speech recognition that eliminates the need for labeled training data, which has traditionally limited the technology's application to a small number of languages. The authors, Alexei Baevski, Wei-Ning Hsu, Alexis Conneau, and Michael Auli, present a framework that utilizes self-supervised speech representations to segment unlabeled audio and establish a mapping to phonemes through adversarial training.

The significance of the right representations is emphasized, as wav2vec-U achieves a substantial reduction in phone error rate on the TIMIT benchmark, decreasing it from 26.1 to 11.3. Additionally, on the larger English Librispeech benchmark, it reaches a word error rate of 5.9 on test-other, which is competitive with systems trained on extensive labeled datasets. The authors also explore applications of wav2vec-U on nine other languages, including low-resource ones like Kyrgyz, Swahili, and Tatar.

The paper highlights the limitations of current speech recognition systems, which rely on transcribed data and are thus restricted to about 125 languages out of nearly 7,000 globally. The success of unsupervised learning in other domains, such as machine translation, inspires this approach, aiming to achieve similar advancements in speech recognition without the constraints of labeled data. The framework is built upon the self-supervised representations from wav2vec 2.0, demonstrating promising results in the unsupervised learning of speech recognition models.
