The paper discusses a method for unsupervised speech recognition using a combination of audio and text data processing techniques. Initially, the speech audio representations are segmented, and a 512-dimensional Principal Component Analysis (PCA) is applied to extract the most significant features from the wav2vec 2.0 outputs. Mean-pooling is utilized to create average representations for each segment, and adjacent segments are pooled together to enhance robustness against noise in segment boundaries.

For the text data, two pre-processing steps are employed: phonemization and silence token insertion. Phonemization converts words into phonemes, allowing for a more effective mapping between audio and phonetic sounds. Silence tokens are also introduced, addressing the limitations of unsupervised silence removal in audio data. Silence markers are added to the phonemized text to facilitate adversarial learning.

The unsupervised learning approach utilizes adversarial training, leveraging a Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) architecture. The generator network maps the segment representations to phonemes, including a silence label to accurately identify silences in the audio. This design prevents the model from mislabeling phonemes as silences, which would degrade performance. The generator is implemented as a single-layer convolutional neural network, while the segment representations remain unmodified during training.

Overall, the methodology integrates sophisticated audio and text processing techniques to enhance the performance of unsupervised speech recognition models.
