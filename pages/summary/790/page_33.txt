The paper presents a comprehensive evaluation of various mismatch removal methods in image matching, utilizing the VLFeat toolbox for feature detection and description via SIFT. It details several datasets used for experiments, including DAISY, DTU, Retina, RemoteSensing, and VGG, each with distinct characteristics and challenges. The datasets are assessed based on ground truth geometrical transformation matrices and manual labeling of matches.

The DAISY dataset features wide baseline image pairs with high match counts, while DTU is designed for multiple-view stereo evaluation with accurate camera parameters. Retina involves retinal images with non-rigid transformations, presenting difficulties in matching due to modality differences. RemoteSensing encompasses diverse image types for positioning and change detection tasks, and VGG consists of planar scenes with defined homographies.

For evaluation, the study employs metrics such as precision, recall, and F-score, calculated based on true positives, true negatives, false positives, and false negatives. Various mismatch removal methods are examined, including RANSAC, SM, ICF, GS, LO-RANSAC, VFC, LPM, GMS, and LFGC. The results indicate that while traditional methods like RANSAC and LO-RANSAC perform well under certain assumptions, they can be time-consuming. In contrast, LPM shows a balance of efficiency and performance, outperforming other methods with minimal computational cost. The recent deep learning approach, LFGC, is highlighted as significantly more effective than traditional methods.

Overall, the findings underscore the importance of selecting appropriate mismatch removal techniques based on dataset characteristics and performance requirements.
