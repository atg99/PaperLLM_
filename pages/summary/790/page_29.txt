The paper discusses various methods and advancements in visual homing, image registration, stitching, and fusion, emphasizing their reliance on feature matching and correspondence techniques.

In visual homing research, methods like those by Ramisa et al. (2011) leverage landmark vectors and invariant feature points from panoramic images. However, these approaches face challenges due to mismatches, which can degrade performance, as noted by Schroeter and Newman (2008). To mitigate this, Liu et al. (2013) employed a RANSAC-like method for mismatch removal, while Zhao and Ma (2017) introduced a method that simultaneously addresses mismatches and interpolates sparse motion flows. Ma et al. (2018b) further improved robustness against outliers through a guided locality preserving matching technique.

The paper also details image registration, which aligns images from various viewpoints or sensors. Feature-based methods have gained traction due to their efficiency. After establishing correspondences, transformation models are estimated for registration. Notable contributions include Ma et al. (2015b) who proposed a Bayesian approach, and Pilet et al. (2008) who focused on non-rigid registration with outlier insensitivity. Multi-modal registration presents additional challenges, tackled by methods such as Chen et al. (2010) and Liu et al. (2018a) which develop invariant descriptors tailored for specific image types.

Image stitching, which combines overlapping images to create a wider field of view, relies on precise pixel-level alignment. Techniques like those from Brown and Lowe (2007) and Lin et al. (2011) utilize SIFT feature matching and RANSAC for accurate homography estimation.

Lastly, image fusion integrates information from different sources to enhance image quality. This process begins with accurate registration through feature matching. Recent innovations include CNN-based approaches for multi-focus image fusion and generative adversarial network frameworks for infrared and visible image fusion by Ma et al. (2019c), which improve the resultant image's detail and intensity.

Overall, the paper highlights the critical role of feature matching in enhancing visual homing, registration, stitching, and fusion across various applications.
