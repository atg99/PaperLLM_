This paper discusses advancements in deep learning techniques for image registration and stereo matching, highlighting various approaches and their contributions to the field. 

In image registration, Wang and Zhang introduced DeepFLASH, which utilizes a low-dimensional bandlimited space for efficient deformable medical image registration, significantly reducing computational and memory demands. Mok and Chung proposed an unsupervised symmetric image registration method that enhances topology preservation and smoothness by maximizing image similarity through diffeomorphic maps. Truong et al. introduced a universal network for geometric matching, optical flow estimation, and semantic correspondence, achieving high accuracy and robustness by leveraging both global and local correlation layers.

In stereo matching, numerous studies have employed deep convolutional techniques to improve matching cost computation and refine disparity maps. Key contributions include Zbontar and LeCun's deep Siamese network for similarity prediction between image patches and Luo et al.'s efficient Siamese network for fast stereo matching. Mayer et al. developed DispNet, an end-to-end CNN for fine disparity mapping, which was later enhanced by Pang et al. with a two-stage CNN. Recent advancements, such as Chang and Chen's spatial pyramid pooling and Liu et al.'s framework for translating synthetic images to realistic ones, further illustrate the evolving landscape of stereo matching techniques.

The paper also addresses the challenges of point-based learning for feature extraction and similarity measurements, noting that deep learning applications in this area are still emerging. Techniques include parameter fitting inspired by RANSAC and point classification/segmentation methods. Brachmann et al. introduced DSAC, a differentiable RANSAC approach using reinforcement learning, while Ranftl and Koltun proposed a trainable method for fundamental matrix estimation.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the growing integration of deep learning in both image registration and stereo matching, while also acknowledging the limitations and challenges posed in point-based learning.
