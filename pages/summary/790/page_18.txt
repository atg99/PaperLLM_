The paper discusses various methods for solving the Graph Matching (GM) problem, which is framed as a Quadratic Assignment Problem (QAP) that is known to be NP-hard. The GM problem seeks to find optimal one-to-one correspondences between nodes in two graphs to maximize an overall affinity score. The authors outline different relaxation strategies that have emerged to provide approximate solutions.

1. **Spectral Relaxations**: Initial methods, such as those proposed by Leordeanu and Hebert, involve replacing strict mapping constraints with spectral constraints, allowing solutions to be derived from eigenvector problems. Subsequent improvements maintained the one-to-one mapping constraint for tighter relaxations. Other studies introduced l1-norm constraints and probabilistic interpretations, further diversifying the spectral approach.

2. **Convex Relaxations**: This section highlights the use of lifting methods to achieve strong convex relaxations of the non-convex QAP. Semi-definite programming (SDP) has been applied to GM, offering tight approximations despite high computational costs. Linear programming (LP) relaxations and their dual problems have also been explored for GM solutions, linking them to MAP inference algorithms.

3. **Convex-to-Concave Relaxations**: The path-following technique is introduced as a method to transition from convex to concave formulations, yielding effective solutions with lower computational complexity. This approach has been extended to directed graphs and partial matching scenarios. A unified framework based on the factorization of the affinity matrix helps reduce complexity and connects the Koopmans–Beckmann and Lawler QAPs.

The paper emphasizes the evolution of GM solvers through these relaxation strategies, noting their respective efficiencies and limitations in accuracy and computational feasibility.
