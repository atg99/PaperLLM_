This paper reviews image matching techniques, classifying them into area-based and feature-based methods. Area-based methods facilitate dense matching without identifying salient features, making them suitable for applications like medical image registration and narrow-baseline stereo matching. The paper highlights the growing interest in deep learning approaches within this domain.

Feature-based image matching addresses challenges such as large viewpoint differences and non-rigid transformations. This process involves salient feature detection, discriminative description, and reliable matching, often incorporating transformation model estimation. The paper outlines various matching forms including graph matching and descriptor matching, emphasizing the flexibility and applicability of feature-based methods compared to area-based ones. A comprehensive review of traditional to deep learning techniques is provided, along with applications and comparative analyses based on extensive experiments.

Despite advancements, the paper identifies ongoing challenges in image matching. Issues include limited recall in two-stage feature matching strategies, the need for new paradigms in matching semantic analogs, and the complexity of joint matching across multiple images. Additionally, while deep learning has improved feature detection and description, its potential for enhancing matching accuracy remains underexplored. The paper also notes unresolved challenges in multi-modal image matching and suggests customizing feature matching techniques for specific vision tasks like Structure from Motion (SfM) and Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) as promising research directions.
