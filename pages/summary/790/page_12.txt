This paper reviews advancements in image descriptor learning, focusing on both classical and deep learning methods. It highlights key contributions in dimensionality reduction and the development of binary descriptors. Cai et al. (2010) explored linear discriminant projections for enhancing local descriptor discriminability, while Brown et al. (2010) introduced a framework for constructing descriptors using optimization techniques. Simonyan et al. (2014) further advanced descriptor learning by framing spatial pooling and dimensionality reduction as convex optimization problems.

The paper emphasizes the significance of binary descriptors, which offer advantages like low storage requirements and fast matching speeds. These are typically derived from float-valued descriptors using hashing methods, notably locality sensitive hashing (LSH) and its variants. Supervised hashing methods, which tailor feature spaces to specific tasks, are also discussed, with various machine learning strategies proposed for effective image matching.

Trzcinski and colleagues (2012, 2017) advocated for directly learning representations from image patches, emphasizing the effectiveness of non-linear filters over linear ones. They introduced methods for generating binary descriptors through discriminative projections and online optimization techniques.

The paper also delves into deep learning-based descriptors, characterizing them as supervised learning problems. These methods seek to minimize the distance between matched features while maximizing the distance between unmatched ones. It distinguishes between metric learning, which focuses on similarity measurement, and descriptor learning, which generates descriptor representations. Deep learning's flexibility and efficiency in extracting information and generating descriptors are underscored, marking a significant evolution from classical approaches.
