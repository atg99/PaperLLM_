The paper discusses two primary approaches for blob feature detection in computer vision: second-order partial derivative-based methods and segmentation-based detectors. 

Second-order partial derivative-based methods include techniques such as the Laplacian of Gaussian (LoG) and the Difference of Gaussians (DoG), which utilize scale-space theory for detecting blob features through edge detection and noise reduction. LoG identifies local extrema in multi-scale spaces, while DoG serves as a computationally efficient approximation. The determinant of Hessian (DoH) is also highlighted for its affine invariance, allowing for effective feature detection by estimating and correcting affine regions. The paper reviews various advancements based on these methods, including the SIFT and SURF detectors, which have been enhanced for better performance in visual applications.

Segmentation-based detectors, such as the Maximally Stable Extremal Region (MSER), extract regions that maintain stability across varying intensity thresholds, thus eliminating the need for additional scale estimation. This method, along with other segmentation techniques that consider color and shape structure, offers robustness against viewpoint changes but is less effective for feature matching compared to second-order methods.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of blob features in visual applications due to their accuracy and robustness, while also noting the ongoing developments in both detection methods to improve efficiency and effectiveness.
