The paper discusses the importance of feature description and matching in computer vision, emphasizing the need for accurate identification of patch size and orientation to achieve robustness against global and local deformations like rotation and scaling. The primary goal of feature description is to enhance discrimination compared to raw image similarity measurements. Various descriptors, both handcrafted and data-driven, are reviewed for their effectiveness in image matching, coding, retrieval, and face recognition.

Handcrafted feature descriptors are based on expert knowledge and typically involve extracting low-level image information, such as gradients and intensity, followed by pooling and normalization to create discriminative vectors. Gradient statistic-based descriptors, including the histogram of oriented gradients (HOG) and SIFT, are highlighted for their scale and rotation invariance through techniques like Difference of Gaussian (DoG) computation and histogram orientation analysis.

The paper also reviews improvements to SIFT, such as SURF, which enhances computational efficiency by approximating gradient calculations using Haar wavelets, and other variants that address specific image characteristics or tasks. Notable enhancements include CSIFT, which incorporates color information, and ASIFT, which achieves affine invariance through simulating different camera orientations. Additionally, methods like RootSIFT and DSP-SIFT modify SIFT to improve performance without increasing computational costs. Overall, the paper provides a comprehensive overview of feature description methods, aiming to guide future research in developing advanced techniques, particularly leveraging deep learning and convolutional neural networks.
