The paper discusses advancements in graph matching (GM) and point set registration (PSR) methods, highlighting their mathematical formulations and applications in computer vision. The extension of GM to a high-order formulation, known as hypergraph matching, is introduced through a tensor-based objective. This method emphasizes the use of geometrical cues to enhance robustness, with references to key studies in the field.

PSR focuses on estimating spatial transformations to align two point sets, contrasting with GM by emphasizing global transformations. The conventional PSR objective includes minimizing a distance metric between transformed point sets while incorporating regularization to prevent trivial solutions. PSR assumes a global transformation exists between point sets, leading to lower computational complexity.

The paper elaborates on the iterative closest point (ICP) algorithm as a foundational PSR method, noting its simplicity and computational efficiency, albeit with sensitivity to initialization. Variants of ICP, such as EM-ICP and RPM, aim to address its limitations, with RPM incorporating soft assignments and extending to non-rigid transformations. EM-based methods are also discussed as crucial in PSR, leveraging expectation-maximization frameworks to refine correspondences and transformations based on probabilistic models.

Furthermore, density-based methods represent point sets through generative models, facilitating registration without explicit correspondences. The paper underscores the evolution of PSR methods, addressing challenges like noise and outliers while enhancing generalization capabilities.
