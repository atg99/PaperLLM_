The paper discusses various approaches to solve the Quadratic Assignment Problem (QAP) in Graph Matching (GM), particularly focusing on continuous relaxations and multi-graph matching techniques. Continuous relaxation methods aim to approximate the QAP without guaranteeing global optimality, with several algorithms proposed over the years. Gold and Rangarajan's graduated assignment algorithm, and advancements by Tian et al. and van Wyk et al. emphasize gradient ascent and projection methods for enforcing constraints. Recent contributions include techniques for concave relaxation and determinant regularization combined with gradient optimization.

Multi-graph matching, which involves aligning multiple graphs while ensuring cycle-consistency, is highlighted as a growing area of interest. The paper categorizes existing methods into three groups: iterative two-graph matching, recovery of globally consistent pairwise matchings, and techniques utilizing clustering or low-rank recovery. Recent optimization frameworks propose convex relaxations and projected power iterations for feasible solutions.

Beyond the QAP formulation, alternative paradigms are also explored. Cho et al. introduced a random walk perspective, while Monte Carlo methods by Lee et al. enhanced matching robustness. Progressive matching methods and functional representations of graphs were also discussed, along with the challenges of outlier suppression in matching. The significance of the affinity matrix in GM is noted, with methods for its learning proposed, including unsupervised and supervised approaches. Recent advancements also include a fully trainable framework that integrates graph networks for learning node and edge affinities alongside solving combinatorial optimization.
