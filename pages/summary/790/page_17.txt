This paper presents a comprehensive review of various image registration techniques, highlighting their strengths and limitations. It begins by discussing area-based methods, which are effective for medical and remote sensing images but struggle with significant geometric transformations and local deformations. The authors note that mutual information (MI) is a robust statistical measure for multi-modal image registration, although it faces challenges in finding the global maximum, impacting its reliability. Recent advancements include structure consistency boosting transforms to enhance multi-spectral registration.

The paper also explores graph matching (GM) methods, which utilize feature points from images to establish node-to-node correspondences. GM is categorized into exact and inexact matching, with a focus on inexact matching due to its practical applicability in real-world scenarios. The authors describe GM's formulation as a Quadratic Assignment Problem (QAP), detailing Lawler's QAP and the Koopmans-Beckmann formulation, which incorporate geometrical and high-level information to improve matching accuracy. Overall, the review underscores the evolution and ongoing challenges in image registration and graph matching, providing a foundation for future research in these fields.
