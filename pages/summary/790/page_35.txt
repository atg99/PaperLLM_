The paper presents a comprehensive evaluation of point set registration methods, focusing on both non-rigid registration of 2-D shape contours and rigid registration of 3-D point clouds. The study identifies limitations in existing learning methods, particularly noting a low recall and high accuracy in RemoteSensing applications, underscoring their data-dependent nature.

For non-rigid 2-D registration, six methods (TPS-RPM, GMM, CPD, L2E, PR-GLS, and APM) are assessed using a set of 20 instances derived from four patterns, with additional noise, outlier, and rotation experiments. The results indicate varying performances: TPS-RPM shows robustness to outliers but struggles with noise, while CPD and GMM are sensitive to outliers. L2E and PR-GLS demonstrate unstable results, and APM, effective only for affine deformations, exhibits limitations in handling severe transformations.

In the rigid 3-D registration scenario, methods such as GMM, CPD, and ICP are found to underperform in the presence of random rotations, whereas GoICP, a globally optimal method, significantly surpasses them.

Overall, the findings highlight the strengths and weaknesses of various point set registration techniques, contributing valuable insights for future research and applications in this domain.
