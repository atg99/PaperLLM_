The paper discusses advancements in robust model estimation and feature matching techniques in computer vision, particularly focusing on variations of RANSAC and non-parametric methods. LO-RANSAC addresses computational costs with improvements suggested by Lebeda et al. and further developed with graph-cut techniques by Barath and Matas. MAGSAC employs σ-consensus to eliminate user-defined thresholds, while MAGSAC++ introduces a new scoring function and a novel approach to inlier-outlier decisions through an iteratively re-weighted least squares procedure.

Despite the effectiveness of resampling methods, they face limitations, especially in runtime as outlier rates increase and in handling complex transformations. This has led to the exploration of non-parametric model-based methods that utilize more generalized priors for matching, such as motion coherence. Various robust estimators have been applied to these methods, including triangulated 2-D meshes and Support Vector Regression for correspondence functions.

The paper also highlights the trend towards relaxed methods for matching, which allow for less strict geometric constraints to accommodate complex scenarios like motion discontinuities. Techniques such as quadratic models and piecewise affine deformations have been employed to improve the accuracy of correspondences. Learning-based matching methods are noted as a contemporary alternative to traditional approaches, categorized into image-based and point-based learning, enhancing information extraction and representation in feature matching tasks.
