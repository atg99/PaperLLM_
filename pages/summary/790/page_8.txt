This paper reviews various advancements in feature detection methods, emphasizing the transition from traditional handcrafted techniques to modern CNN-based approaches. Key differences in training methodologies are highlighted, specifically the need for differentiability across the entire process.

Notable methods include LIFT, which integrates detector, orientation estimator, and descriptor training through supervision from a Structure from Motion (SfM) system. SuperPoint employs a fully convolutional model to compute interest point locations and descriptors in a single pass, utilizing synthetic datasets for pre-training and homography adaptation for self-supervised learning. LF-Net and RF-Net focus on optimizing the detection process in a differentiable manner, enhancing keypoint accuracy through unique feature extraction techniques.

The paper also discusses the evolution of 3-D feature detectors, categorizing them into fixed-scale and adaptive-scale detectors. Fixed-scale detectors, such as the Local Surface Patch (LSP) and Intrinsic Shape Signature (ISS) methods, select keypoints based on predefined saliency measures. Conversely, adaptive-scale detectors like the Laplace-Beltrami scale space and MeshDoG adaptively determine scale characteristics to improve detection accuracy.

Overall, the review underscores the significance of integrating learned parameters within complete vision pipelines and the ongoing need for innovation in both local and salient feature detection methodologies.
