The paper discusses the evaluation of various methods for camera pose estimation and the efficacy of higher-order information in geometric matching (GM) through experiments on a Chinese character dataset and two distinct datasets for pose estimation. 

In the Chinese character dataset, which consists of handwritten characters with 10 samples each, the results indicate that while the overall accuracy is relatively low due to the challenging nature of the scene and the use of simple edge distances for constructing the affinity matrix, superior performance is noted in methods like FGM and TM, with TM being more efficient.

For camera pose estimation, the paper highlights the importance of determining camera position and orientation in 3-D computer vision tasks. Traditional methods rely on 2-D to 3-D matches, which can lead to inaccuracies. The paper proposes using 2-D to 2-D correspondences instead, leveraging feature matching techniques such as SIFT combined with RANSAC for geometric model estimation.

Two datasets are employed: the YFCC100M dataset for outdoor scenes and the SUN3D dataset for indoor scenes. The YFCC100M dataset consists of 100 million images curated into sequences, while the SUN3D dataset presents challenges due to repetitive elements and self-occlusions. Both datasets are split into training, validation, and testing subsets.

Evaluation metrics focus on the angular difference between estimated and ground-truth pose vectors, with performance assessed using the area under the curve (AUC) of precision curves at various angle thresholds.

The results show that traditional mismatch removal methods struggle against high outlier ratios, while deep-learning-based methods demonstrate significantly better performance, indicating their robustness in challenging scenarios.
