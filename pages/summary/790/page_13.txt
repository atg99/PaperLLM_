The paper reviews various deep learning methods for image patch similarity and descriptor learning, highlighting the evolution and application of different loss functions such as hinge, Siamese, triplet, and contrastive losses. Key contributions include Zagoruyko and Komodakis' DeepCompare, which utilizes Siamese networks to learn from raw image pixels, and MatchNet, which combines descriptor learning with metric learning through a cross-entropy classification framework. The authors discuss several architectures and techniques, such as DeepDesc, which employs a pairwise hinge loss for training Siamese networks, and Wang et al.'s ranking model that captures fine-grained image similarity using a triplet-based hinge loss.

Additionally, the paper mentions advances like TFeat and L2Net, which utilize triplet sampling and progressive sampling strategies, respectively, to enhance descriptor performance. HardNet improves upon L2Net with a simple hinge triplet loss and hard negative mining, while PN-Net integrates distance metric learning with positive and negative constraints for faster convergence.

The discussion extends to descriptor learning methods that incorporate attention mechanisms and generative adversarial networks (e.g., BinGAN), as well as recent approaches like ContextDesc, which combines local patch similarity with spatial constraints. The paper also emphasizes the trend of end-to-end learning methods that unify feature description with detection processes, exemplified by LIFT and SuperPoint, which aim to create differentiable and trainable pipelines for keypoint detection and descriptor extraction.

Overall, the review underscores the importance of loss function design, network architecture, and training strategies in advancing the field of image patch matching and descriptor learning.
