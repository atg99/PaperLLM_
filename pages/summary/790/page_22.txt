The paper discusses methods for constructing putative match sets and removing false matches in the context of feature matching between two images. It outlines strategies for initial correspondence establishment, including fixed threshold, nearest neighbor, mutual nearest neighbor, and nearest neighbor distance ratio. Each method has its advantages and drawbacks, particularly regarding sensitivity to one-to-many matchings and the preservation of true correspondences.

The authors emphasize the need for robust mismatch elimination techniques due to the limitations of local appearance information and the challenges posed by non-rigid deformations, viewpoint changes, and low-quality images. They categorize mismatch removal methods into resampling-based, non-parametric model-based, and relaxed methods.

Resampling-based methods, particularly the RANSAC algorithm, are highlighted for their effectiveness. RANSAC employs a hypothesize-and-verify strategy to identify inliers based on a geometric model. Variants and improvements to RANSAC, such as MLESAC and PROSAC, enhance performance by modifying verification strategies and incorporating prior information about inliers. Additionally, locally optimized RANSAC is introduced as a method to mitigate noise effects by refining the model through local optimization techniques.

The paper underscores the importance of selecting the appropriate matching strategy based on descriptor properties and application context to optimize performance in feature matching tasks.
