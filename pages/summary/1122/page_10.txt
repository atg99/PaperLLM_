The paper by W. Deng, J. Xu, Y. Song et al. published in the Applied Soft Computing Journal discusses the performance comparison of various optimization algorithms across different functions and dimensions. The results are presented in a detailed table highlighting the best, mean, and standard deviation values for each algorithm under 30 and 50 dimensions.

The study emphasizes the complexity of gate assignment in airports, which is framed as a multi-objective optimization problem. Key optimization objectives include minimizing idle time for gates, reducing the total walking distance for passengers, optimizing the use of large gates, and maximizing occupancy efficiency. Each objective function is mathematically formulated, reflecting the need to balance various stakeholder interests such as passengers, airlines, and airport management.

The specific objective functions outlined include the balanced idle time for gates, shortest walking distance for passengers, effective use of large gates, and highest occupancy efficiency based on gate assignments. These formulations aim to enhance operational efficiency and service quality in airport gate management.
