This paper discusses the challenges of solving NP-Hard problems using traditional methods and highlights the emergence of intelligent optimization techniques, such as genetic algorithms and various forms of differential evolution (DE). While these methods have shown improved performance over traditional techniques, issues such as lower accuracy and increased computation time persist.

The DE algorithm, introduced by Storn and Price, is noted for its simplicity and robustness but is prone to premature convergence and localized optimality. Numerous improvements to DE have been proposed over the years, focusing on enhancing its optimization capabilities through new mutation strategies and adaptive parameter control.

To address the limitations of existing DE algorithms, the authors present a novel approach named WMSDE, which integrates wavelet basis functions and an optimal mutation strategy derived from the strengths of five different mutation methods. This new algorithm aims to improve convergence and avoid local optima by dividing the initial population into subpopulations, thereby fostering information exchange and enhancing diversity.

The effectiveness of WMSDE is validated through experiments on 11 benchmark functions and a real-world airport gate assignment problem, achieving a gate assignment rate of 97.6%. The paper is structured to first introduce the DE algorithm, followed by a detailed description of WMSDE, experimental results, and a conclusion that outlines potential future research directions.
