The paper discusses the WMSDE algorithm, detailing its operational steps and optimization performance through numerical experiments. The algorithm involves a sequence of steps including the selection operation, fitness value calculation, and individual selection for the next generation. It concludes when either the maximum number of iterations is reached or the error requirement is satisfied, outputting the optimal solution for complex optimization problems.

To assess the algorithm's performance, 11 benchmark functions are utilized, categorized into single-peak functions (f1 to f4, f6, f7) for accuracy and convergence speed evaluation, and multimodal functions (f8 to f11) for global search stability assessment. The paper emphasizes the careful selection of initial parameters, which were optimized through simulation experiments to ensure efficient problem-solving and reasonable running times. The results of these experiments, along with the selected parameters and mutation strategies, are summarized in tables, illustrating the effectiveness of the WMSDE algorithm across different functions.
