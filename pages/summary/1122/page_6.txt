The paper presents a comprehensive evaluation of the WMSDE algorithm, a novel mutation strategy within the Differential Evolution (DE) framework, applied to various benchmark functions commonly used in optimization. The study includes a detailed comparison of the WMSDE algorithm against five traditional DE mutation strategies across multiple dimensions and iterations.

Key findings from the experiments indicate that the WMSDE algorithm consistently achieves optimal values, mean performance, and standard deviation across many benchmark functions, particularly f1, f2, f4, and f6, where it reaches the ideal value of zero. While performance on function f5 is generally poor across all algorithms due to its unimodal nature, the WMSDE outperforms other DE strategies. Additionally, for multi-peak functions, the WMSDE demonstrates superior adaptability and convergence, effectively navigating local optima to approach global optima.

The comparative analysis against existing algorithms (DE2/F, MEDE, pADE, and RMDE) highlights that the WMSDE algorithm achieves the best overall optimization performance across several metrics, particularly in dimensions of 30 and 50. The results suggest that WMSDE offers enhanced stability, convergence, and diversity in solving complex optimization problems.

Overall, the findings underscore the effectiveness of the WMSDE algorithm in optimization tasks, demonstrating its potential for broader applications in the field.
