The paper presents experimental results of various optimization algorithms applied to 11 benchmark functions, analyzed over 30 and 50 dimensions. The algorithms tested include different configurations of Differential Evolution (DE), such as DE/rand/1, DE/best/1, DE/rand-to-best/1, DE/rand/2, and DE/best/2. Additionally, WMSDE is introduced as a new method.

Key findings indicate that for many functions, particularly f1 through f11, the performance metrics are reported as best, mean, and standard deviation (Std) values. For example, in the case of function f1, the best result achieved with WMSDE in 30 dimensions is 6.35E-032, while the mean is 0.00E-000, showcasing its effectiveness. Other functions similarly exhibit varying degrees of success across the different algorithms, with some configurations consistently yielding lower error values.

The results highlight the strengths and weaknesses of each algorithm across different benchmarks, suggesting that WMSDE often outperforms traditional DE variants. The comprehensive data allows for a thorough comparison of algorithm efficiency in solving complex optimization problems. Overall, the paper contributes valuable insights into the performance of evolutionary algorithms in high-dimensional spaces.
