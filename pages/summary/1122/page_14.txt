The paper presents a study on airport gate assignment using an improved differential evolution algorithm known as WMSDE. The authors address issues of solution accuracy and convergence associated with traditional differential evolution (DE) algorithms by introducing a new optimal mutation strategy that leverages the strengths of five existing mutation strategies. 

The results are summarized in tables showcasing gate assignments for various flights and comparisons of algorithm performance. The WMSDE algorithm demonstrates improved solution quality, achieving a lower minimum value and higher assignment rates compared to other algorithms, although with a longer average runtime. The study emphasizes the significance of optimizing gate assignments to reduce passenger walking distances, balance gate idle times, and enhance overall service levels at airports.

The conclusion highlights the effectiveness of the WMSDE algorithm in providing quick convergence to optimal solutions for both single-peak and multimodal functions, suggesting its potential as a valuable tool for gate assignment challenges in the aviation industry. Future work may focus on further enhancing the algorithm's efficiency and exploring additional applications in airport operations.
