The paper discusses the Differential Evolution (DE) algorithm, focusing on its mutation, crossover, and selection operations, which are integral to its optimization performance. The mutation operation generates a mutation vector for each individual in the population using various strategies, including DE/rand/1, DE/best/1, DE/rand-to-best/1, DE/best/2, and DE/rand/2. Each strategy employs a scaling factor (F) that affects the search step size based on the population's proximity to the global best solution.

The crossover operation combines target and mutation vectors to create test vectors, employing a binomial crossover defined by a crossover rate (CR). The selection operation then determines whether to replace the target vector with the test vector based on their objective function values, ensuring the population evolves towards better solutions.

The authors propose an improved version of the DE algorithm called the WMSDE, which aims to address the limitations of traditional DE regarding parameter settings and mutation strategies. WMSDE introduces a new optimal mutation strategy that combines the advantages of the existing five strategies to enhance local search ability while maintaining global search characteristics. It also incorporates wavelet basis functions to control the scaling factor and normal distribution to manage the crossover rate, ultimately improving solution diversity, convergence speed, and optimization performance.

Furthermore, the WMSDE employs a multi-population strategy, dividing the initial population into subpopulations to facilitate information exchange and enhance search efficiency. This approach helps to prevent premature convergence and balances global and local search capabilities, making WMSDE more effective in solving complex optimization problems.
