The paper presents FoveaBox, an object detection model that surpasses traditional anchor-based methods, achieving a remarkable average recall (AR) of 52.9, which is an improvement of 8.4 points over the Region Proposal Network (RPN). The authors demonstrate that FoveaBox consistently outperforms baseline models by 0.9 to 1.4 average precision (AP) across various backbone networks and input resolutions. Notably, FoveaBox models are reported to be 1.1 to 1.3 times faster than RetinaNet counterparts.

Key hyper-parameters such as η and σ are analyzed, with η influencing the scale assignment for the object detection pyramid and σ affecting the classification of positive and negative samples. The paper shows that a shrunk version of the model performs better than an IoU-based assignment method, yielding an additional 0.4 AP.

Furthermore, FoveaBox incorporates advanced techniques such as deformable convolution for feature alignment, enhancing the model's performance significantly. The integration of a heavier classification head alongside these techniques resulted in an AP of 40.1 when using a ResNet-50 backbone.

The study also addresses per-class performance differences, indicating that FoveaBox shows improvements across most classes compared to RetinaNet. In terms of speed, the inference time for FoveaBox was evaluated on a single Nvidia V100 GPU, showcasing its efficiency in real-time applications.

Overall, the findings affirm the effectiveness of FoveaBox in generating high-quality region proposals and highlight its potential for advancing object detection frameworks.
