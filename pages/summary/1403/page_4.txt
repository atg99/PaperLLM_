The paper discusses FoveaBox, an object detection framework that employs feature pyramid networks (FPN) to enhance detection accuracy. FoveaBox attaches two subnetworks to each FPN feature level: one for classifying cells and another for predicting the coordinates of ground-truth object boxes. The score output map illustrates predicted boxes, with color density indicating score probability before applying non-maximum suppression (NMS).

FoveaBox demonstrates its efficacy on the COCO minival set, achieving a single model box average precision (AP) of 38.6 with a ResNet-101 backbone. A notable aspect of FoveaBox is its strategy of assigning objects to multiple adjacent pyramid levels rather than a single level. This approach has two main benefits: it allows for simultaneous optimization of pyramid features with similar semantic capacities and significantly increases the training sample size for each pyramid, leading to a more stable training process. The results indicate an improvement of 3.1 AP compared to traditional single-level training.

In terms of box prediction, FoveaBox computes normalized offsets for each ground-truth bounding box based on a specified format. The method involves mapping coordinates to the input image and calculating offsets between projected coordinates and ground-truth values, regularized with a log-space function. This systematic approach underlines FoveaBox's contribution to the field of object detection, emphasizing enhanced performance through innovative training strategies and prediction mechanisms.
