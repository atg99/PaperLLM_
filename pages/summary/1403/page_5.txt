The paper presents FoveaBox, a novel approach for anchor-based object detection that aims to enhance the performance of existing detection models. FoveaBox utilizes a simplified training framework with Smooth L1 loss for box predictions and generates bounding box boundaries from optimized targets. The network architecture is designed to demonstrate versatility by employing various convolutional backbones for feature extraction and different head architectures for result computation, while focusing primarily on performance improvements.

The implementation details reveal the use of Feature Pyramid Networks (FPN) for a fair comparison with existing models, constructing a pyramid of levels with shared parameters and employing stochastic gradient descent for training. The training process involves a well-defined schedule and data augmentation through horizontal flipping. During inference, predictions are filtered based on confidence scores, followed by non-maximum suppression to refine the output.

Experimental results on MS COCO and Pascal VOC benchmarks indicate that FoveaBox outperforms previous state-of-the-art models across various configurations. The study includes comparisons between two-stage and one-stage detectors, illustrating the potential of FoveaBox to enhance performance in both categories. Overall, FoveaBox demonstrates significant advancements in the field of object detection, validating its effectiveness through rigorous experimentation.
