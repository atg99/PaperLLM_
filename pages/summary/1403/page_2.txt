The paper presents FoveaBox, an object detection framework that predicts both object category scores and bounding boxes directly at each spatial position without the need for anchors or IoU matching during training. This approach allows FoveaBox to effectively handle generic object detection tasks, outperforming previous anchor-based methods, including RetinaNet, by achieving a 2.2 AP gain on the COCO dataset.

FoveaBox integrates recent advancements in feature pyramid networks with a streamlined detection head, resulting in state-of-the-art performance in object detection. The authors highlight the limitations of existing methods, such as DenseBox, which requires image pyramids and fixed scaling, making it less efficient. In contrast, FoveaBox can process over 10 images per second, demonstrating its superior flexibility and accuracy.

The paper also reviews related work in object detection, categorizing methods into anchor-based and anchor-free frameworks. It discusses the evolution of two-stage and one-stage detectors, emphasizing the reliance on anchors in traditional approaches. Additionally, it critiques earlier anchor-free methods like YOLO, which struggled with low recall, while positioning FoveaBox as a more capable solution for multi-scale object detection.

Overall, FoveaBox's direct prediction mechanism and efficient processing make it a significant contribution to the field of object detection, paving the way for future research and applications.
