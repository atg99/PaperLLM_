The paper discusses FoveaBox, a novel object detection method that demonstrates superior performance compared to existing single-stage and many two-stage detectors, including CornerNet and Mask R-CNN, particularly when using a ResNet-101 backbone. 

The ablation study reveals several key findings:

1. **Qualitative Results**: FoveaBox's detection outputs show accurate box predictions closely aligned with ground truth, achieved without the need for candidate anchors.

2. **Anchor Density Analysis**: The study evaluates various anchor densities and confirms that increasing anchor density beyond 6-9 anchors does not yield performance improvements. Excessive anchors complicate optimization and create ambiguity in position definitions. FoveaBox mitigates these issues by predicting a single target per position, simplifying the output space and reducing the number of hyper-parameters.

3. **Robustness to Box Distribution**: FoveaBox exhibits greater robustness in predicting bounding boxes across varying aspect ratios, outperforming RetinaNet especially as aspect ratios increase.

4. **High-Quality Region Proposals**: The method effectively generates region proposals with a class-agnostic head, significantly surpassing the performance of the FPN-based Region Proposal Network across multiple criteria.

Overall, FoveaBox's design enhances detection accuracy, simplifies the model, and improves robustness, making it a compelling alternative to traditional anchor-based methods.
