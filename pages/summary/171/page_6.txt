The paper discusses the exploration of the vast configurational phase space for new inorganic materials, highlighting the limitations of current materials databases like ICSD and Materials Project, which represent only a fraction of the possible combinations of elements. To address this, the authors propose a Generative Adversarial Network (GAN)-based model that efficiently samples the extensive chemical design space of inorganic materials, achieving high uniqueness, validity, and diversity in generated compounds.

The authors acknowledge that while charge neutrality and electronegativity balance principles help filter plausible compositions, they are insufficient for effective sampling in the expansive chemical design space. The GAN model can produce a wide range of compositions, but it faces limitations related to polymorphism, where a single composition may correspond to multiple structures and properties. Therefore, structural predictions are necessary for a comprehensive inverse design process.

The study also identifies areas for improvement, such as integrating explicit chemical rule filters to eliminate invalid candidates and allowing for fractional ratios in compound representations to accommodate doped materials. The GAN model could be enhanced by combining it with other sampling techniques like genetic algorithms and Bayesian optimization approaches.

The methods section describes the datasets used for training the GAN and Autoencoder (AE) models, primarily sourced from the OQMD database, which includes 606,115 compounds. After applying screening criteria, the final dataset consists of 291,884 compounds. The training process involves optimizing hyperparameters for the GANs, with a specific focus on the OQMD, MP, and ICSD datasets, as well as training a bandgap prediction model using a subset of the MP dataset.

Data and code availability is mentioned, indicating that the supporting data can be requested from the authors, ensuring transparency and accessibility of the research findings.
