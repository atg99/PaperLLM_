This paper presents a novel generative adversarial network (GAN) model designed for efficient sampling of the inorganic materials design space by generating hypothetical inorganic materials. The model, termed MatGAN, is trained using data from various inorganic materials databases, including OQMD, Materials Project, and ICSD. It successfully learns the implicit chemical compositional rules, enabling it to generate chemically valid compounds with a high degree of accuracy, specifically achieving 84.5% in reproducing charge neutrality and balanced electronegativity without explicit rule specification.

The authors utilize a sparse matrix representation for the materials, where each of the 85 elements is encoded in a binary one-hot format. This encoding facilitates the application of convolutional neural networks within the GAN framework. The GAN consists of a generator and a discriminator, both modeled as deep neural networks, with the generator employing a combination of fully connected and deconvolution layers, while the discriminator uses convolution layers.

To enhance performance and address potential issues such as gradient vanishing, the authors implement a Wasserstein GAN approach, which minimizes generator and discriminator losses based on the Wasserstein distance. Additionally, to evaluate GAN performance and address challenges in generating specific material categories, the authors construct a variational autoencoder (AE) model.

Overall, this research highlights the effectiveness of GANs in exploring the vast chemical composition space of inorganic materials, offering a more efficient alternative to exhaustive enumeration methods.
