This paper discusses the development and validation of a machine learning model, specifically a Gradient-Boosted Decision Tree (GBDT), for predicting the bandgap values of high-bandgap materials generated by Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs). The study demonstrates that the bandgap distribution of generated samples closely resembles that of the training set, confirming the model's ability to efficiently generate hypothetical high-bandgap materials.

To assess the potential of these GAN models in discovering new materials, a cross-validation approach was employed. The results revealed that from 2 million generated materials by the GAN-ICSD model, 13,126 were confirmed by the Materials Project (MP) dataset and 2,349 by the Open Quantum Materials Database (OQMD). Similarly, the GAN-MP model produced 6,880 and 3,601 confirmed materials from the ICSD and OQMD datasets, respectively. Notably, the study also highlights the successful prediction of two new materials, AlS2 and AlS, along with an additional undiscovered material, AlS3.

The limitations of the GAN models were examined using an autoencoder (AE) approach. The AE model demonstrated a high decoding success rate of 96.31% for the training set and 95.50% for the test set, indicating that decodable samples share common chemical composition rules. A T-SNE dimension reduction technique was utilized to visualize the differences between decodable and non-decodable samples, revealing distinct distributions that suggest non-decodable materials possess unique composition rules. The GAN-OQMD model generated fewer non-decodable materials, with only 5% of them appearing in the generated samples, while 60.26% of the decodable training samples were rediscovered.

Overall, the findings underscore the effectiveness of GANs in generating new materials while also identifying the challenges in producing non-decodable materials, which may require more data or advanced models to improve generation capabilities.
