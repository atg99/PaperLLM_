The paper investigates the formation energies and uniqueness of hypothetical materials generated using three different Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) trained on various datasets: OQMD, Materials Project (MP), and ICSD. It finds that materials generated by GAN-ICSD and GAN-MP predominantly have formation energies less than zero, indicating thermodynamic stability. In contrast, GAN-OQMD produces a higher percentage of materials with positive formation energies due to its training set being largely comprised of unstable ternary compounds.

To assess the uniqueness of generated samples, the authors calculate the percentage of unique materials as the number of generated samples increases. Despite a decline in uniqueness with more samples, GAN-MP consistently yields the highest uniqueness, followed by GAN-ICSD and GAN-OQMD. The differences in uniqueness are attributed to the distribution of training samples; GAN-OQMD is biased towards ternary compounds, leading to many duplicates, while GAN-MP's more balanced training set allows for greater diversity.

The study also conducts a novelty check by leaving out a portion of the training samples to determine if the GANs can generate new materials. Results show that GANs can recover a significant percentage of both training and hold-out samples, while also producing a large number of novel candidates that adhere to basic chemical principles. For instance, GAN-ICSD generates a high percentage of new binary, ternary, and quaternary materials, affirming the model's capability to discover new materials beyond the training data.

Additionally, the paper explores the conditional generation of materials with desired properties, specifically high-bandgap materials, demonstrating the potential for targeted material design using GAN models. Overall, the findings underscore the effectiveness of GANs in generating stable and unique hypothetical materials, while also facilitating the discovery of novel materials.
