The paper discusses the development and training of an autoencoder (AE) and Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) to explore inorganic materials using data from the Open Quantum Materials Database (OQMD). The AE is trained on 291,840 inorganic materials, employing the negative dice coefficient as a loss function to ensure effective reconstruction of input data. The architecture of the decoder in the AE mirrors that of the GAN's generator, allowing for the identification of materials that cannot be decoded, thereby informing the limitations of the GAN models.

The GANs, including GAN-OQMD, GAN-MP, and GAN-ICSD, generated 2 million hypothetical materials, which were filtered for charge neutrality and balanced electronegativity, leading to 1.69 million valid samples. The distribution of these generated materials compared to existing datasets was visualized using t-SNE dimensionality reduction, revealing that the GAN-ICSD model can produce materials that significantly expand the design space beyond the training dataset.

A validity check showed that the generated samples adhered closely to fundamental chemical rules, with percentages of charge-neutral samples being comparable to those in the training sets. Specifically, the GAN models produced a much higher percentage of chemically valid candidates compared to traditional exhaustive enumeration methods, indicating their ability to implicitly learn chemical rules.

Finally, the stability of the generated materials was assessed through formation energy calculations, using a machine learning model to predict energies for lithium-containing compounds. This comprehensive approach highlights the effectiveness of GANs in generating valid and potentially stable inorganic materials, contributing to advancements in materials discovery.
