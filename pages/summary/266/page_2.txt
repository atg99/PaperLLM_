This paper explores the application of capsule networks in hierarchical multi-label classification (HMC), a domain where traditional neural networks have been the norm. HMC involves assigning one or more labels to samples organized in a structured hierarchy. Previous work predominantly utilized Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) and Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs), such as LSTMs, for text classification tasks. The authors highlight that capsule networks, which group features into capsules that capture relationships more effectively, have not been tested in HMC until now.

Capsule networks have shown promise in various classification tasks, outperforming traditional CNN architectures in some instances. They encapsulate features into vectors that represent latent category information, thus providing a more informative structure compared to scalar outputs from standard neural networks. The paper emphasizes that the length of these vectors indicates the probability of activation while their orientation denotes the existence of different cases within a category.

The research aims to leverage the unique properties of capsule networks to enhance performance in HMC. The first layer of the capsule network consists of primary capsules, which can take input from convolutional layers or RNNs, and output vectors that represent latent information. Each subsequent layer, termed classification capsules, processes these vectors to improve classification accuracy in hierarchical structures.

In summary, the paper fills a gap in the application of capsule networks to HMC by providing a thorough analysis of their properties and potential advantages over existing methods.
