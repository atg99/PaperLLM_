The paper investigates label correction methods in multi-label classification, specifically focusing on three approaches: correction by extension, removal, and threshold. The first two methods modify labels to ensure consistency, with extension adding parent labels and removal eliminating parentless labels. Correction by threshold assesses the average confidence of ancestor labels and includes them if they exceed a set threshold.

The experiments utilize two distinct datasets, the Blurb Genre Collection (BGC) and the WOS-11967 dataset from the Web of Science. The BGC consists of book blurbs categorized hierarchically, with 1,342 different label co-occurrences across 146 labels. The WOS dataset contains abstracts of published papers with a shallower hierarchy and requires exactly two labels for each example, resulting in a more balanced label distribution.

Feature selection for the models is constrained to the first 100 tokens of text, and preprocessing includes the removal of stop-words and low-frequency words, while retaining certain special characters relevant to the target audience. The study employs a variety of baseline models, including SVM, CNN, and LSTM, with specific initialization strategies and label correction methods for training.

Key quantitative characteristics of both datasets are detailed, highlighting differences in text count, average tokens, class distribution, and label co-occurrences. The findings contribute to understanding the effectiveness of different label correction methods in enhancing classification performance across diverse datasets.
