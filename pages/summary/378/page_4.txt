The paper discusses the performance of various baseline models, specifically RoBERTa, BERT, and DistilBERT, on question-answering tasks within the CoQA dataset. The models were trained for four epochs using the AdamW optimizer and a learning rate of 3e-5. The results show that RoBERTa achieved the highest F1 score of 81.2, followed by BERT at 76.9 and DistilBERT at 66.6, establishing a baseline for future research.

The analysis identifies specific linguistic inference challenges faced by these models, particularly in counting listed phrases and handling negation. For instance, extractive models struggle to count characters in a phrase due to their inability to chunk text, leading to consistent failures in numerical questions with answers between 1 and 5. Additionally, the models often neglect negation cues, resulting in incorrect answers that either do not recognize negation or fail to provide informative responses.

To address these issues, the paper suggests using a BERT-based model trained on the Sherlock dataset to detect negation spans, thereby improving performance on the NEG QA class. The findings indicate that all models perform poorly in recognizing negated answers, particularly DistilBERT, which highlights the need for further refinement in handling linguistic nuances in question-answering tasks.
