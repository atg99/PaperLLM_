The paper discusses enhancements made to models in the context of the Conversational Question Answering (CoQA) task, particularly focusing on the integration of surprising word substitution and an ensemble approach. The surprising word substitution technique involves augmenting the training data by replacing surprising entities with more predictable ones, as identified by BERT. This method creates additional training samples while preserving sentence structure and aims to provide rare entities with better contextual representation, addressing social biases in NLP models.

The results from enhanced models on various question-answering classes indicate that BERT and RoBERTa show significant improvements, particularly on counting questions, while DistilBERT requires more auxiliary resources to achieve comparable performance. BERT demonstrates a capacity to learn formal semantic aspects, improving specifically in tasks involving negation and semantic role labeling (SRL). Notably, BERT outperforms RoBERTa on negation tasks when enhanced with explicit negation information.

The findings highlight that while RoBERTa relies heavily on lexical representations, it benefits from combined linguistic features in an ensemble setting, especially for pragmatic aspects such as antonymy and dialogue context. Furthermore, the models trained using surprising word substitution show improved performance on questions involving surprising entities, suggesting better generalization capabilities. Overall, the enhanced models leverage various strategies to improve performance across different QA classes, showcasing the potential of innovative data augmentation techniques in natural language processing.
