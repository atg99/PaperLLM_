The paper presents results from a study evaluating various language models, including RoBERTa, BERT, and DistilBERT, on the CoQA development set, using F1 scores to measure performance across different QA classes. The results highlight that RoBERTa generally outperforms BERT and DistilBERT, but its improvements are primarily due to enhanced lexical representations rather than better compositional semantic understanding. 

The analysis shows that while RoBERTa excels in certain areas, it struggles with biases related to lexical information. In contrast, BERT shows significant performance boosts in the SURP class when utilizing models focused on negation and semantic roles, indicating that these linguistic elements help reduce biases associated with prominent entities. DistilBERT, however, relies heavily on lexical information and demonstrates the least capacity for compositional representation learning among the models evaluated.

The study also emphasizes the benefits of integrating linguistic knowledge through a multitask approach, revealing that even minimal training on specific linguistic features, such as negation, can substantially enhance model performance in relevant QA classes. The ensemble models, which combine the strengths of the individual models, outperform all others, showcasing the importance of diverse error characteristics and specializations in improving overall accuracy. 

In conclusion, the findings suggest that while modern language models have made significant advances, they still exhibit limitations in linguistic abstraction capabilities, often depending on superficial features. The paper underscores the potential for enhancing model performance through targeted linguistic training.
