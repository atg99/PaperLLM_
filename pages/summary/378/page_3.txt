The paper highlights significant findings regarding the limitations of language models like BERT and RoBERTa in terms of their inference and reasoning capabilities. While RoBERTa outperforms BERT due to its larger training dataset, it still struggles with generalization, particularly for ages outside a specified range. This indicates a gap in abstract reasoning abilities that is not bridged merely by increasing data size. Furthermore, the models, including RoBERTa, exhibit a notable deficit in handling numerical questions, which leads to a higher error rate in such cases.

The CoQA dataset serves as a robust case study, comprising over 127,000 samples that include diverse domains and conversational elements. This dataset is particularly useful for analyzing linguistic relations and reasoning capabilities due to its inclusion of dialogue history and various question types.

To evaluate performance, baseline models such as RoBERTa, BERT, and DistilBERT are developed as extractive models that select answer spans from the text. Training involves identifying the most relevant substrings from the rationales that correspond to the correct answers. The implementation of linear classifiers allows the models to classify tokens for answer span identification, rationale inclusion, and answer type classification.

Overall, the paper seeks to investigate the less explored aspects of BERT's linguistic capabilities, particularly in complex reasoning and inference, and emphasizes the need for further probing tasks in conversational question contexts to better understand these models' limitations.
