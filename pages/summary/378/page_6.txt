The paper discusses the limitations of existing models, particularly BERT and DistilBERT, in answering questions that require reasoning over semantic roles. It highlights issues where models tend to select prominent entities based on their lexical semantics rather than their contextual relevance. For example, in a children's story where "cereal" is anthropomorphized, the baseline BERT model fails to identify "cereal" as a good swimmer, instead opting for a human entity mentioned earlier. This indicates a reliance on lexical semantics and biases toward certain entity types.

The study introduces a QAclass that identifies entities with high entropy, suggesting they are surprising within their context. By masking proper nouns and evaluating their likelihood as answers using BERT, the study identifies surprising entities, with all models performing worse on these questions, particularly DistilBERT.

Additionally, the paper categorizes entities into human, location, and general types, revealing that RoBERTa and BERT perform better on these classifications than overall, while DistilBERT struggles with human and location questions, often defaulting to entity types rather than context.

The findings corroborate previous research on BERT's limitations, with DistilBERT showing even greater biases and a loss of representation of formal language aspects. To address these shortcomings, the authors propose enhancements through auxiliary tasks that incorporate linguistic knowledge into the models. They define additional classifiers for integer responses and create enhanced multitask models that incorporate various linguistic annotations. Despite exploring alternative architectures, the multitask approach was ultimately selected as the most effective for improving model performance.
