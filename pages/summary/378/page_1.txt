This paper investigates the types of linguistic knowledge represented by language models such as RoBERTa, BERT, and DistilBERT in the context of Conversational Question Answering (CoQA). The authors conduct a systematic error analysis to identify shortcomings in these models, particularly in handling basic arithmetic, compositional semantics, and lexical semantics. They find that enhancing these models with relevant linguistic knowledge through multitask learning leads to improved performance, with ensemble methods yielding an increase of 2.2 to 2.7 points in F1 score overall, and up to 42.1 points on more challenging question classes.

The paper highlights that while contextualized word embeddings are beneficial for many NLP tasks, they often fail to exploit the compositional nature of language, relying instead on superficial features. The study contrasts the performance of larger models like RoBERTa with smaller counterparts like DistilBERT, noting that despite the advantages of larger models, issues with generalization and computational expense remain. The authors argue that the tasks of machine reading comprehension and dialogue are particularly suited for examining the linguistic capabilities of these models, as they require contextual interpretation and semantic inference. The findings underscore the differences in how well each model represents compositional and lexical information.
