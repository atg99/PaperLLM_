The paper discusses several innovative strategies. First, DropBlock is exclusively applied to the Feature Pyramid Network (FPN), as its inclusion in the backbone negatively impacts performance. The use of IoU loss for bounding box regression is highlighted, with an additional branch for IoU loss calculation rather than a direct replacement of L1 loss. This approach retains the benefits of IoU metrics while maintaining the original loss function.

An IoU aware branch is introduced to enhance the detection confidence by incorporating localization accuracy during inference, despite the minimal additional computational cost. The Grid Sensitive technique improves bounding box center predictions by adjusting the coordinate decoding process, also with negligible added complexity.

Matrix NMS is proposed as a parallel implementation of Soft-NMS, enhancing efficiency without sacrificing performance. CoordConv is selectively applied to certain convolution layers to allow the network to utilize input coordinates, balancing efficiency and added parameters.

The Spatial Pyramid Pooling (SPP) method increases the receptive field of the backbone features without introducing additional parameters, although it slightly increases FLOPs. Lastly, the use of a pre-trained model with higher classification accuracy aims to enhance detection performance without affecting the overall efficiency of the detector.

The experimental section validates these techniques on the COCO dataset, using a specific training and validation split for robust evaluation. The implementation details confirm the use of ResNet50-vd-dcn as the backbone, aligning with the architecture established in YOLOv3. Overall, the paper demonstrates a systematic approach to improving object detection through carefully selected enhancements.
