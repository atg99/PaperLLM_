This paper presents a comprehensive ablation study of various modifications to the PP-YOLO model on the MS-COCO minival split, highlighting incremental improvements in mean Average Precision (mAP) through a series of methodological enhancements. The baseline models, utilizing Darknet53 and ResNet50-vd backbones, showed initial mAP scores of 38.9% and 39.1%, respectively. The authors employed various strategies such as exponential moving average (EMA), DropBlock, and modifications to the loss function, which collectively enhanced the mAP to 44.6% while maintaining efficient computational metrics, including parameters and GFLOPs.

Key findings include:

1. **Model Initialization and Training**: The backbone networks were initialized with ImageNet weights, and training followed established YOLOv3 schedules. A batch size of 192 images was utilized across 8 GPUs for 250K iterations.

2. **Incremental Improvements**: Each modification was introduced stepwise, with careful consideration of their interactions and cumulative effects. The introduction of IoU Loss and subsequent modules like IoU Aware and Grid Sensitive incrementally increased mAP while managing infer time effectively.

3. **Post-processing Enhancements**: The use of Matrix NMS showed improvements in mAP without increasing infer time, showcasing the potential for efficiency in post-processing techniques.

4. **CoordConv and SPP**: These two methods provided further enhancements to mAP with minimal increases in parameters and GFLOPs, demonstrating the effectiveness of targeted architectural changes.

Overall, the systematic exploration of these tricks underscores the importance of both training strategies and architectural innovations in enhancing the performance of object detection models like PP-YOLO. The final model achieved a notable mAP of 44.6%, indicating significant advancements in the field of object detection.
