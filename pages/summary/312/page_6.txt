This paper investigates the impact of fine-tuning BERT versus training from scratch on the optimization landscape and generalization performance. The authors compare the flatness of the loss surfaces, finding that fine-tuning leads to flatter optima, which are associated with better generalization on unseen data. Specifically, the endpoints of fine-tuned BERT are located in wider areas of the loss surface, corresponding to lower generalization errors, indicating a strong correlation between training loss surfaces and generalization error surfaces.

Furthermore, the study reveals that fine-tuned models maintain optimal performance even under small perturbations, while models trained from scratch show sharper optima and poorer generalization, particularly on smaller datasets. Larger datasets exhibit more consistent generalization error surfaces when trained from scratch, suggesting that such approaches require more data for effective generalization compared to fine-tuning.

Additionally, the research highlights the varying roles of BERT's layers during fine-tuning. Lower layers are found to be more invariant and transferable across tasks, as they retain performance after rollback to their pre-trained states. In contrast, rollback of higher layers results in significant performance degradation, indicating their critical role in the fine-tuning process. Overall, the findings underscore the advantages of fine-tuning BERT and the importance of layer-specific optimization strategies for enhancing model generalization.
