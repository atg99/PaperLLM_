The paper investigates the effects of pre-training and fine-tuning on the BERT model across four datasets: MNLI, RTE, SST-2, and MRPC. It compares training from scratch with fine-tuning BERT, highlighting that pre-training leads to better optimization outcomes. Key findings include:

1. **Optimization Dynamics**: Training from scratch requires more iterations to converge and often results in higher final loss compared to fine-tuning. The trajectories during training from scratch exhibit divergence initially, with a rough loss landscape, making convergence more challenging. In contrast, fine-tuning BERT shows a more direct and smoother optimization path.

2. **Convergence Speed**: Fine-tuning BERT converges faster in terms of training loss, indicating that pre-training provides a beneficial initialization that eases the optimization process. For instance, on the RTE dataset, models trained from scratch struggle to reduce loss in the early epochs.

3. **Robustness to Overfitting**: Despite the large number of parameters in the BERT-large model, fine-tuning demonstrates robustness against overfitting. Generalization errors do not significantly increase with additional training epochs, suggesting that the model can effectively leverage its capacity without compromising performance.

Overall, the findings underscore the advantages of a pre-training followed by fine-tuning approach for optimizing BERT, both in terms of convergence efficiency and robustness to overfitting.
