The paper investigates the layer-wise training loss surfaces of BERT during fine-tuning on the MNLI and MRPC datasets. It highlights that different layers of BERT exhibit varying levels of transferability, with low layers (0-7) being more invariant and transferable across datasets. Previous research indicates that intermediate and high layers contribute more to classification tasks, while low layers capture local syntactic information and higher layers encapsulate complex semantics.

The authors visualize the loss landscapes and optimization trajectories to assess the effectiveness of language model pre-training. They find that pre-training results in broader optima on the loss landscape, facilitating optimization and reducing the risk of overfitting. Additionally, there is a correlation between the training loss surfaces and generalization error surfaces, supporting the claim that pre-training enhances generalization capabilities.

The paper concludes with suggestions for future research, including exploring the effects of multi-task training on loss surface geometry and developing fine-tuning algorithms that seek wider, flatter optima for improved performance.
