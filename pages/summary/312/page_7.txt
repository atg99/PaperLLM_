BERT contribute differently to performance on various natural language processing tasks. The paper investigates the impact of training BERT from scratch versus fine-tuning it, specifically focusing on generalization errors and layer rollback techniques. 

Figure 6 illustrates the two-dimensional generalization error surfaces for both training methods, showing initial and final error points. Table 1 presents accuracy results for different datasets (MNLI, RTE, SST-2, MRPC) when using fine-tuned BERT and the effects of rolling back layers. The findings indicate that rolling back higher layers tends to degrade performance, while lower layers, which capture more transferable language representations, enhance generalization capabilities.

The paper also contextualizes its findings within the broader landscape of pre-trained contextualized word representations, referencing previous works that highlight the importance of pre-training for encoding syntactic and semantic information. By visualizing loss landscapes and optimization trajectories, the authors provide insights into why fine-tuning BERT yields superior generalization compared to training from scratch, emphasizing the distinct roles of various layers in learning task-specific information versus transferable features.
