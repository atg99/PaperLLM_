The paper investigates the optimization dynamics of fine-tuning the BERT model on various datasets, highlighting the advantages of pre-trained models over training from scratch. It defines the optimization direction and computes the deviation between current and target optimization trajectories, utilizing cosine similarity and Euclidean norms to visualize these projections.

Experiments were conducted on four datasets: MNLI, RTE, SST-2, and MRPC, adhering to previously established data splits and evaluation metrics. The BERT-large model was fine-tuned using a cased tokenizer, with specific hyperparameters for different datasets, including batch sizes and learning rates.

The findings reveal that fine-tuning BERT significantly outperforms training models with random initialization, particularly with limited data. Pre-training yields a better starting point, leading to wider optima in the loss landscape. This is evidenced by smoother training paths and a more stable performance under parameter perturbations compared to models trained from scratch. The paper emphasizes that these characteristics enhance the model's generalization capability.
