The solid lines representing fine-tuning show a smoother and less sharp loss curve compared to the dashed lines for training from scratch, indicating that the optima achieved through fine-tuning are flatter.

This study focuses on the MRPC dataset, which is smaller and more prone to overfitting when trained from scratch. The findings reveal that fine-tuning BERT for five epochs initially yields substantial generalization capabilities, and extending the fine-tuning to an additional twenty epochs does not significantly improve performance. This suggests that the model remains within a region of good generalization, highlighting the robustness of the pre-training-then-fine-tuning approach against overfitting.

The paper emphasizes that while training from scratch can reach comparable training losses, it often results in poorer performance on unseen data due to sharper optima. The research reinforces the idea that flatter optima are associated with better generalization, supported by previous literature. By analyzing the loss landscapes, the study concludes that the wide and flat nature of the optima obtained through fine-tuning contributes to enhanced generalization capabilities, making the pre-training approach more effective than training from scratch.
