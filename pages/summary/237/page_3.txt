The paper addresses the challenge of identifying masked faces in computer vision, emphasizing the difficulty of extracting identity features when only limited facial areas, such as the eyes and forehead, are visible. The study utilizes the pre-trained ResNet-50 architecture, known for its efficiency in image recognition tasks, to tackle this issue.

The methodology involves transfer learning, where the ResNet-50 model, initially trained on unmasked faces, is fine-tuned with masked face data. This approach was chosen due to ResNet-50's superior performance in terms of time and memory compared to other models like VGGNet19 and DenseNet121. The authors also experimented with cropping occluded areas of the face and considered alternative methods such as segmentation and supervised domain adaptation. However, due to data scarcity and time constraints, these alternatives were not pursued.

Transfer learning is highlighted as a key technique, allowing the adaptation of a model trained on a similar task to enhance performance on the masked face identification task. ResNet-50's architecture, which includes 50 layers and employs residual blocks, enables effective gradient flow and learning of complex features, making it suitable for this application.

Overall, the paper outlines the technical challenges and strategic choices made to enhance the identification of individuals wearing masks, leveraging advanced neural network techniques.
