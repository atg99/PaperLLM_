The paper discusses advancements in face recognition technology, particularly through deep learning techniques. It highlights the significance of dropout in architecture and its impact on developing enhanced algorithms in computer vision, specifically for face recognition. Notable achievements include FaceNet's accuracy of 99.63% using the GoogleNet-24 framework and VGGFace's 98.95% accuracy with VGGNet-16 on the LFW database. The challenges of recognizing faces with occlusions, such as masks and hats, are acknowledged, noting that traditional algorithms often rely on full-face images, which may not yield effective features for identification under occlusion.

The paper categorizes existing approaches to occlusion handling into restoration and discard methods. Restoration techniques aim to reconstruct occluded facial parts, while discard methods eliminate occluded sections to focus on remaining identifiable features. The discussion includes various studies that have explored these methods, particularly in the context of masked face recognition, which has gained attention during the COVID-19 pandemic. The authors argue that their work extends existing research by focusing on identifying masked individuals, a problem less explored compared to mask detection.

The study employs a ResNet architecture, chosen for its robustness against variations in illumination and facial expressions, to develop a deep CNN-based model for masked face detection. The structure of the paper is outlined, indicating sections dedicated to problem statements, technical approaches, experimental methods, preliminary results, and future research directions.
