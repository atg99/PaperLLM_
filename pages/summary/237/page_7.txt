The analysis of the training and validation loss for the masked model indicates that while the validation curve stabilizes quickly, the high validation loss suggests inadequate learning from the data. In contrast, the unmasked dataset shows a favorable training and validation accuracy curve, but further enhancements are needed for validation accuracy. When comparing the masked dataset, the continuous increase in training and validation accuracy without reaching stability highlights the challenges posed by occlusion on feature learning. The performance metrics reveal that the model excels in recognizing unmasked faces, with F1-scores of 0.897 for unmasked and 0.4473 for masked faces, indicating a need for improvement in masked face recognition.

The conclusion asserts that while convolutional networks are effective for face recognition, their performance diminishes with occluded faces. To address this, the paper suggests exploring innovative techniques, including cropping masked areas, employing 3D learning methods, and augmenting the dataset by simulating masks on unmasked images to balance the data. Additionally, domain adaptation methods and combining different CNN architectures with machine learning techniques are proposed as potential strategies to enhance model performance.
