This study investigates the impact of parameter tuning on the performance of XGBoost, particularly focusing on subsampling rates and feature selection. By fixing the subsampling rate at 0.75 without replacement and using the square root of the number of features, the researchers were able to reduce the parameter grid search size and improve average XGBoost performance. The findings indicate that the tuning phase accounts for over 99.9% of the computational effort required for training XGBoost models, but this effort can be significantly reduced by employing a smaller grid for the search.

The results suggest that while XGBoost achieves top performances in Kaggle competitions, this does not contradict the efficiency gains observed in this study. The authors highlight that XGBoost allows for precise parameter tuning through a computationally efficient algorithm, which is not as effective with random forests or traditional gradient boosting methods that demand longer computation times for minimal gains.
