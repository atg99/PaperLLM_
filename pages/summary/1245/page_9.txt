The results section presents a comparison of various machine learning methods, specifically tuned and default versions of XGBoost, random forest, and gradient boosting, across multiple datasets. Table 3 highlights the average accuracy and standard deviation of each method, revealing that tuned gradient boosting achieves the highest performance across 10 out of 28 datasets, followed by tuned XGBoost in 8 datasets. Default random forest shows consistent performance relative to its tuned version, with minimal differences in accuracy across most datasets. In contrast, default XGBoost and gradient boosting typically underperform compared to their tuned counterparts, although there are exceptions where default XGBoost outperforms its tuned version due to factors like dataset noise and effective default parameter settings.

The methodology for summarizing the results employs a ranking system illustrated in Figure 1, which indicates that tuned XGBoost has the highest average rank, followed by gradient boosting and default random forest. Statistical analysis via a Nemenyi test shows no significant differences in average ranks among the methods, emphasizing the competitive nature of the algorithms.

Additionally, Table 4 provides average training execution times for the methods under analysis, underscoring the efficiency of the different models tested. Overall, the findings suggest that while tuning can enhance performance, the effectiveness of default settings should not be overlooked, especially in certain datasets.
