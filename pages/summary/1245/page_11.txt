The paper discusses the training times associated with different machine learning methods, particularly focusing on XGBoost, Random Forest (RF), and Gradient Boosting (GB). The training times are reported in a table where methods tuned using grid search are shown with two numbers: the time for the grid search and the training time after parameter optimization. All experiments were conducted on an eight-core Intel i7 processor, and the reported times reflect sequential execution to accurately measure computational resources, despite grid search being performed in parallel.

The analysis reveals that the grid search process significantly influences the overall training time, contributing over 99.9% to the total time for tuned models. The grid sizes vary across classifiers, making direct comparisons of grid search times challenging. However, when excluding grid search time, XGBoost demonstrates superior efficiency, being approximately 3.5 times faster than RF and 2.4 to 4.3 times faster than GB when training with a fixed set of parameters. Notably, XGBoost's grid search for optimal parameters is quicker than that of GB, despite XGBoost having a larger grid size.

The paper also addresses the need for improved parameterization of XGBoost through further experiments aimed at identifying a better default configuration and exploring alternative parameter grids. The authors computed average ranks in test error for various parameter configurations to assess their effectiveness across datasets.
