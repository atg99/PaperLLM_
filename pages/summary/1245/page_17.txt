This paper presents an empirical analysis of XGBoost, a gradient boosting method, evaluating its performance in training speed and accuracy against gradient boosting and random forest across various classification tasks. The analysis highlights that while gradient boosting emerged as the most accurate classifier, the differences in performance between XGBoost and random forest using default parameters were not statistically significant. 

The study emphasizes the necessity of meticulous parameter tuning for gradient boosting models, as both XGBoost and gradient boosting with default parameters were less successful. In contrast, random forest performed better on average with its default settings. Notably, the research found that tuning the randomization parameters in XGBoost, specifically the subsampling rate and the number of features selected at each split, was unnecessary as long as some level of randomization was applied. Overall, the findings suggest that optimizing parameters is crucial for enhancing the accuracy of gradient boosting models, while random forest may require less fine-tuning.
