The evaluation metrics for anomaly detection included precision, recall, AUC, F1 score, and variations of these metrics when trained on limited data. The results indicated that TranAD consistently outperformed baseline models in terms of F1 and AUC scores across most datasets, achieving notable improvements over state-of-the-art models. However, it faced challenges with the WADI dataset, attributed to its complexity and size.

For anomaly diagnosis, metrics like HitRate@P% and Normalized Discounted Cumulative Gain (NDCG) were employed. The results demonstrated that TranAD maintained a robust diagnostic performance, effectively identifying ground truth dimensions among top predicted candidates.

Overall, the analysis reveals the strengths and weaknesses of various models in handling anomaly detection and diagnosis in complex datasets, with TranAD emerging as a leading approach due to its innovative use of sequence encoding and adversarial training. The study underscores the importance of tailoring methods to the specific characteristics of the data to enhance detection accuracy and reliability.
