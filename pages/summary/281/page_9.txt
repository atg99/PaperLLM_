The paper presents TranAD, a deep transformer network designed for anomaly detection in multivariate time series data. It compares the performance of TranAD with various baseline methods across multiple datasets, including NAB, UCR, MBA, SMAP, MSL, SWaT, WADI, SMD, and MSDS. Key performance metrics include Precision, Recall, Area Under the Curve (AUC), and F1 score, with the best scores highlighted in bold.

TranAD outperforms other methods, achieving the highest scores in several categories. For instance, in the NAB dataset, it achieved Precision of 0.8889, Recall of 0.9892, AUC of 0.9541, and F1 score of 0.9364. Similarly, it maintained strong performance across other datasets, notably achieving an F1 score of 0.9605 in WADI and 0.9262 in MSDS.

The paper notes that while GDN exhibited slightly higher scores in MSL and MSDS datasets due to its scalable graph-based inference, TranAD's use of self-attention allows it to capture long-term dependencies and contextual information effectively. This capability is particularly advantageous for identifying long-term anomalies, a limitation observed in methods like USAD and MTAD-GAT, which focus on local contextual windows. The transformer architecture in TranAD enhances its performance by leveraging temporal attention and complete trace embeddings with positional encoding.

Overall, TranAD demonstrates superior anomaly detection capabilities in multivariate time series data, highlighting the advantages of transformer networks in this domain.
