The paper presents TranAD, a deep learning model utilizing Transformer networks for anomaly detection in multivariate time series data. The training algorithm is structured in two phases as described in Algorithm 1.

In the first phase, known as Input Reconstruction, the model employs an encoder-decoder architecture to predict the reconstruction of each time-series window. Traditional models often fail to capture short-term trends, which can lead to missed anomalies. To address this, TranAD introduces an auto-regressive inference style that generates an approximate reconstruction of the input window. The deviations identified during this phase serve as focus scores, allowing the Transformer Encoder to concentrate on subsequences with significant deviations, thereby enhancing the model's ability to detect anomalies.

The second phase, Focused Input Reconstruction, uses the reconstruction loss from the first decoder as a focus score to inform the second decoder's output. This self-conditioning approach enables the model to amplify deviations and improves its ability to capture short-term temporal trends, ultimately leading to a more reliable anomaly score and reducing false positives.

The paper also discusses the evolving training objective, which addresses the challenges associated with adversarial training stability. The model employs two decoders that independently reconstruct the input time-series window, with the second decoder tasked with distinguishing between the actual input and the first decoder's output. The training objective is structured to minimize reconstruction errors for the first decoder while maximizing the difference for the second decoder.

The cumulative loss function combines reconstruction and adversarial losses, adjusting the weight of the adversarial component throughout the training process. Initially, the focus is on reconstruction loss to stabilize training when outputs are poor, gradually increasing the weight of the adversarial loss as the model improves.

Overall, TranAD's two-phase adversarial training and self-conditioning mechanism contribute to its effectiveness in detecting anomalies in multivariate time series data, enhancing generalizability and robustness against diverse input sequences.
