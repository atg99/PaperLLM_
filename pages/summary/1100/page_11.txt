The references listed encompass a range of significant contributions to the field of protein structure and function analysis, as well as advancements in computational methods for protein sequence alignment and prediction. 

Key works include Thomas Creighton's exploration of protein structures and molecular properties, and Burkhard Rost's discussion of the challenges in protein sequence alignments, termed the "twilight zone." Brenner et al. focus on evaluating sequence comparison methods against reliably identified evolutionary relationships, while Altschul et al. introduce the Basic Local Alignment Search Tool (BLAST), a fundamental tool for sequence alignment.

Durbin et al. provide insights into probabilistic models for biological sequence analysis. The development of semi-supervised learning techniques, highlighted by Chapelle et al. and Weston et al., showcases innovative approaches to protein classification and function prediction from networks.

Recent advancements in deep learning applied to protein sequences are represented by works from Heinzinger et al., Rives et al., and Yang et al., illustrating the emergence of biological structure and function from large-scale unsupervised learning. The Pfam database update by El-Gebali et al. and the improved structural feature prediction by Klausen et al. reflect ongoing efforts to enhance protein analysis tools. JPred4, a server for secondary structure prediction, rounds out this collection of resources pivotal for researchers in bioinformatics and computational biology.
