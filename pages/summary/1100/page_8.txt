The paper presents results on various downstream supervised tasks related to protein engineering, comparing different machine learning architectures, including Transformers, LSTMs, and ResNets. Table 2 summarizes the performance of these models on tasks such as contact prediction, homology, fluorescence, and stability, using metrics like accuracy, precision, and Spearman's ρ, where higher values indicate better performance.

The findings indicate that self-supervised pretraining generally enhances model performance across all tasks. Notably, the pretrained Transformer models achieved the highest accuracy in most tasks, particularly in fluorescence and stability prediction. The fluorescence task revealed a bimodal distribution of protein brightness, suggesting challenges in distinguishing beneficial from deleterious mutations. The best pretrained model demonstrated a 70% accuracy in predicting beneficial mutations compared to 68% for the non-pretrained model, emphasizing the potential for improvement in practical applications.

In the contact prediction task, pretraining allowed models to better capture long-range interactions between amino acids, improving the resolution of predictions. Overall, the results suggest that pretraining contributes significantly to the effectiveness of machine learning models in protein engineering, particularly in identifying beneficial mutations and understanding protein interactions. Further detailed results and metrics are provided in the appendix.
