The paper addresses the potential of adapting natural language processing (NLP) techniques for extracting valuable biological insights from extensive protein sequence datasets. It introduces the Tasks Assessing Protein Embeddings (TAPE), marking the first systematic evaluation of semi-supervised learning applied to protein sequences. TAPE comprises five biologically relevant supervised tasks aimed at assessing learned protein embeddings in crucial areas such as structure prediction, remote homolog detection, and protein engineering.

To simulate biologically relevant generalization, the authors constructed data splits that challenge models to generalize to unseen sequence portions and resolve small sequence segments. Applications of improvements in these tasks include antibody design, cancer diagnosis enhancement, and discovery of antimicrobial genes from the "Dark Proteome," which consists of unlabeled sequences that current similarity methods struggle with.

The study evaluates the performance of three model architectures—recurrent, convolutional, and attention-based—previously successful in other sequence modeling contexts, along with two recent semi-supervised models. The findings reveal that self-supervised pretraining generally enhances performance across models for downstream tasks, though the effectiveness varies by architecture and task, underscoring the necessity for a multi-task benchmark.

Notably, non-deep alignment-based features excel in secondary structure and contact prediction, while learned features outperform in remote homology detection. While self-supervision shows promise for protein learning, significant advancements are still required for breakthrough performance. The TAPE code and data are publicly available to encourage collaboration from the machine learning community.

The paper also provides foundational knowledge on protein terminology, explaining the structure of proteins as sequences of amino acids and their evolutionary relationships. Understanding these relationships is vital for ensuring data integrity in model training and evaluation. The authors emphasize the use of sequence identity to quantify evolutionary relationships, which is critical for preventing information leakage between training and testing datasets.
