The paper explores advanced self-supervised learning techniques for protein sequence modeling, specifically focusing on masked-token prediction and the effectiveness of protein-specific loss functions. It builds on the work of Bepler et al., who advocate for supervised pretraining on tasks like contact prediction to enhance model performance in secondary structure prediction.

The authors implement three main architectures: LSTM, Transformer, and dilated residual network (ResNet), with the Transformer featuring 12 layers and 38 million parameters. Hyperparameters are aligned across models to maintain comparable sizes, and the training involves both masked-token and next-token prediction strategies. Additionally, the paper benchmarks two other architectures proposed by Bepler et al. and Alley et al., highlighting differences in their design and training methodologies.

To evaluate the effectiveness of the learned features, the authors establish baselines using one-hot encoding and alignment-based inputs, noting the limitations of alignment-based methods in protein engineering tasks. The experimental setup aims to provide a systematic comparison of various featurizations across different tasks, ensuring consistent fine-tuning procedures. Results indicate a notable drop in model accuracy when tested on heldout families compared to random splits, suggesting challenges in generalization to new data.
