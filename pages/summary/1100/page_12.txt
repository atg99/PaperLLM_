The referenced papers cover a range of topics in protein structure prediction, machine learning in protein engineering, and advancements in neural network architectures. 

Cuff and Barton (1999) evaluate multiple sequence methods for predicting protein secondary structures, aiming to improve prediction accuracy. Kim et al. (2014) discuss a method where one contact per twelve residues enables effective topology-level protein structure modeling. Hou et al. (2017) introduce DeepSF, a deep convolutional neural network designed for mapping protein sequences to their corresponding folds.

Tavares et al. (2013) focus on strategies to combat antimicrobial resistance, highlighting the importance of resistomes and antimicrobial peptides. Liu et al. (2019) identify CasX enzymes as a new family of RNA-guided genome editors, expanding the toolkit for genome editing. Sarkisyan et al. (2016) explore the local fitness landscape of green fluorescent protein, providing insights into protein stability and functionality.

Yang et al. (2018) discuss the role of machine learning in protein engineering, while Rocklin et al. (2017) present a comprehensive analysis of protein folding through parallel design and testing methods. 

The papers also delve into advancements in neural network techniques, such as Mikolov et al. (2010) who propose a recurrent neural network-based language model, and Vaswani et al. (2017) introduce the attention mechanism, which has transformed natural language processing. Other contributions include Donahue et al. (2013) with their deep convolutional activation feature for visual recognition, and Yu et al. (2017) presenting dilated residual networks for improved feature extraction.

Lastly, Schaarschmidt et al. (2018) assess contact predictions in the CASP12 competition, highlighting the maturation of co-evolution and deep learning methods in protein contact prediction. Overall, these studies contribute significantly to the fields of bioinformatics, protein engineering, and machine learning.
