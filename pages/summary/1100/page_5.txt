The paper discusses various protein engineering and prediction tasks aimed at enhancing our understanding of protein structures and functions. 

1. **Fluorescence Task**: This task involves mutating a parent green fluorescent protein (GFP) to explore a local landscape of mutations. The goal is to train models on a small neighborhood of the parent protein and test them on more distant variants. The performance is evaluated based on accuracy per amino acid using the CB513 dataset.

2. **Stability Task**: In this task, models are trained on a broad range of proteins and tested on one-mutation neighborhoods of the most promising variants. This approach helps in assessing the stability of proteins after mutations.

3. **Contact Prediction**: This task focuses on predicting pairwise contacts between amino acids based on sequence data. Each pair is labeled to indicate whether the amino acids are in contact or not. Accurate predictions can significantly aid in modeling the full three-dimensional structure of proteins, with a particular focus on medium- and long-range contacts.

4. **Remote Homology Detection**: This sequence classification task identifies proteins based on their structural similarities, even across distant evolutionary relationships. It holds significance in fields like microbiology for detecting antibiotic resistance genes. The models are evaluated on their ability to generalize across large evolutionary gaps, with metrics reporting overall classification accuracy and top-k accuracy.

5. **Fluorescence Landscape Prediction**: This regression task involves predicting the log-fluorescence intensity of proteins based on their mutations. 

Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of model generalization in protein engineering tasks, using various metrics to assess the effectiveness of predictions and classifications across different protein datasets.
