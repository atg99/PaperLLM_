The paper discusses advancements in protein representation learning, emphasizing the effectiveness of alignment-based inputs over current self-supervised models in predicting secondary structures, contact predictions, and remote homology classification. The results demonstrate that models utilizing alignment features yield sharper and more accurate predictions, highlighting a challenge in pretraining methods.

In the discussion, the authors compare their model's performance against state-of-the-art methods, revealing that while their best model performs well in some areas, it falls short in others, underscoring the necessity for multiple benchmark tasks to evaluate model performance comprehensively. The authors note that their Transformer model excels in fluorescence and stability tasks, while the ResNet model performs comparably in secondary structure prediction but poorly in fluorescence.

Looking ahead, the authors identify the need for further exploration in self-supervised learning and protein-specific tasks to enhance model performance. They advocate for a systematic evaluation framework, such as TAPE, to facilitate contributions from the machine learning community to this evolving field.
