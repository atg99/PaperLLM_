This paper discusses the increasing relevance of machine learning in the field of protein sequences, particularly focusing on semi-supervised learning due to the high costs associated with obtaining supervised protein labels. The authors introduce the Tasks Assessing Protein Embeddings (TAPE), a set of five semi-supervised learning tasks that are biologically relevant and designed to evaluate protein representation learning effectively. They emphasize the importance of curated training, validation, and test splits to ensure generalization to real-world scenarios.

The paper benchmarks various approaches to semi-supervised protein representation learning, noting that self-supervised pretraining significantly enhances model performance, often more than doubling it. However, it also highlights that even with this improvement, self-supervised features may still underperform compared to state-of-the-art non-neural techniques, indicating a substantial opportunity for advancements in model architecture and learning paradigms to better capture biological signals.

The authors make their data and code publicly available to encourage further research in this domain, aiming to bridge the gap between the vast amount of unlabeled protein sequences and the limited annotated subsets. The paper underscores the potential of self-supervised learning in leveraging the rich biological information contained in large protein databases, which have expanded rapidly due to advancements in sequencing technologies.
