The proof of Lemma 2 establishes a relationship between the Jensen-Shannon (JS) divergence of two mixtures of distributions and their individual components, demonstrating that the JS divergence can be bounded by the weighted sum of the JS divergences of the individual distributions. 

The implementation section discusses how the DAG model is applied to GANs, specifically mentioning the averaging of K branches for stability and the tuning of parameters within the model. It details the objectives for both the overall GAN and CycleGAN frameworks, emphasizing the use of multiple discriminators (Dk) that share weights while maintaining distinct last layers. The CycleGAN objectives incorporate adversarial losses from transformed samples, integrating the DAG structure to enhance performance.

Appendix B includes model diagrams that illustrate the application of DAG for DistGAN and SSGAN baselines, showing that the DAG paths align with those of traditional GANs. The diagrams reinforce the concept that the DAG framework effectively integrates into existing GAN architectures without altering their fundamental components.
