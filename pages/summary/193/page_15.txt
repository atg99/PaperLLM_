The paper discusses the application of a Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) framework to various Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), specifically the SSGAN and DistGAN models. The DAG design incorporates multiple real/fake discriminators for transformed samples, demonstrating its versatility across different GAN architectures. 

In the context of the SSGAN model, the framework utilizes rotation techniques (0, 90, 180, 270 degrees) and a classifier for self-supervised tasks. The augmentation techniques applied in the DAG are noted to be compatible with vanilla GAN structures. 

Generated examples from the MNIST dataset highlight the effectiveness of data augmentation (DA) methods, such as flipping and cropping, in enhancing the quality of generated samples. The paper also provides visual examples of transformed real samples from the CIFAR-10 dataset, showcasing various augmentation techniques including rotation, flipping, translation, and cropping.

Additionally, the paper includes details about the architectures used, specifically the small DCGAN backbone for CIFAR-10 and the Residual Networks for both CIFAR-10 and STL-10 datasets, indicating the configurations of the encoder, generator, and discriminator components. 

Overall, the research emphasizes the applicability of the DAG framework in improving GAN performance through effective data augmentation strategies.
