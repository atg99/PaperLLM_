The paper discusses various data augmentation (DA) techniques used in Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) and their impact on the quality of generated images. A distinction is made between invertible and non-invertible transformations, with invertible transformations allowing the original image to be exactly reverted. The authors present a list of techniques employed in their experiments, including rotation, flipping, translation, cropping, and a combination technique called FlipRot.

The experiments reveal that applying classical DA methods can misguide the GAN generator, leading to generated samples that differ significantly from the original data distribution. This is evidenced by the Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) scores, which indicate that DA methods, particularly flipping and cropping, yield higher FID scores compared to the baseline, suggesting a divergence from the original data distribution.

The authors argue that classical DA methods may result in the generation of infrequent samples due to alterations in the data distribution. They propose a new DA framework aimed at preserving the learning of the original data distribution. This framework emphasizes the importance of using invertible transformations to minimize the Jensen-Shannon divergence between the generator distribution and the original data distribution.

The paper concludes by outlining a method to modify the vanilla GAN architecture to effectively learn from the augmented dataset while ensuring the maintenance of the original data distribution. This addresses the identified challenges associated with classical DA methods in GAN training.
