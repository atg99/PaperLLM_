The paper presents a mathematical proof concerning the representation of a distribution as a combination of its subsets. It begins with the definition of Jensen-Shannon divergence, represented as JS(px || qx), and proceeds to derive it through a series of equations that manipulate probability distributions. The proof ultimately demonstrates that JS(px || qx) is valid.

Following this, Lemma 1 is introduced, stating that a distribution p can be expressed as a linear combination of K distributions pm, given that the weights wm sum to 1. The proof is structured inductively. 

For K=1, the distribution is trivially p=p1. For K=2, two cases are considered: when the subsets are disjoint and when they intersect. In the disjoint case, p is represented as a weighted sum of the two distributions. In the intersecting case, the proof uses additional notation and breaks down the subsets further, ultimately simplifying to a similar representation. 

The inductive hypothesis is applied to extend the proof to K=k+1, showing that the distribution can still be represented in the desired form. The overall argument establishes the validity of the initial lemma, confirming that any distribution can be expressed as a weighted sum of its component distributions.
