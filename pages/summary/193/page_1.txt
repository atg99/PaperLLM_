The paper discusses the significance of data augmentation (DA) in training Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), particularly in data-limited domains like medical applications. It argues that traditional DA methods can mislead the GAN generator by training it on augmented data that may not represent the original data distribution. This can result in the generator producing samples that are not relevant or appropriate for the target distribution.

To address this issue, the authors propose a new framework called Data Augmentation Optimized for GAN (DAG). This framework aims to improve the learning of the original data distribution by effectively leveraging augmented data. The paper includes a theoretical analysis demonstrating that DAG aligns with the original GAN objective of minimizing the Jensen-Shannon divergence between the original and model distributions.

The authors conduct experiments across various GAN models, including unconditional GAN, conditional GAN, self-supervised GAN, and CycleGAN, using both natural and medical image datasets. The results indicate that DAG consistently enhances performance, achieving state-of-the-art Fréchet Inception Distance scores in some cases. The framework is grounded in principles such as invertible transformations and discriminator regularization, ensuring its effectiveness in overcoming the limitations of classical DA methods.

Overall, the paper highlights the potential of DAG to improve GAN training outcomes by aligning augmented data with the original data distribution, thus addressing the challenges posed by traditional data augmentation techniques.
