The paper presents an ablation study on the effects of discriminator regularization through the number of shared layers in a Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) model. Results indicate that sharing layers enhances performance, with the best outcome achieved when all convolutional layers are shared, leading to a FID score of 23.7. The study also emphasizes the importance of preserving Jensen-Shannon (JS) divergence during generator learning, revealing that models like IDA that do not maintain this preservation result in degraded performance. 

Data augmentation emerges as a critical factor in improving GAN performance, as shown by comparisons with multiple discriminator (MD) variants. The experiments demonstrate that while MD provides slight improvements, incorporating augmentation techniques through DAG significantly enhances the results across different datasets.

Further analysis on the number of branches in DAG indicates that a larger K allows for more augmentations, improving FID scores, although this comes with increased processing time. The experiments primarily focus on the CIFAR-10 dataset using a small DC-GAN architecture, comparing the proposed DAG approach against traditional data augmentation (DA) and baseline models. Results reveal that DA generally fails to outperform the baseline, while DAG consistently provides substantial improvements, particularly with augmentations like Fliprot.

Overall, the findings advocate for the effectiveness of the DAG framework in leveraging data augmentation, demonstrating enhanced stability and performance of GAN models, especially when dealing with smaller datasets.
