The paper presents experimental results comparing the performance of BigGAN and BigGAN with Directed Augmentation Generator (DAG) across various datasets, including CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet, under limited data conditions. The results indicate that the integration of DAG significantly improves Inception Scores (IS) and Fréchet Inception Distances (FID) across different data percentages, showcasing its effectiveness in enhancing generative performance.

In further evaluations on Cityscapes, the addition of DAG to CycleGAN resulted in improved per-pixel accuracy, per-class accuracy, and class Intersection over Union (IOU). The paper also compares its approach against state-of-the-art methods on Stacked MNIST, demonstrating competitive results in terms of modes and Kullback-Leibler divergence.

Additionally, experiments on medical images using DIST-GAN as a baseline indicated that the use of DAG led to substantial reductions in FID scores, affirming its utility in scenarios with limited data samples. Overall, the findings suggest that DAG serves as a valuable augmentation technique, enhancing the performance of generative models across various applications and datasets.
