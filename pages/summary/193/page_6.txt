This paper proposes a novel approach to regularizing discriminators in Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) through weight sharing, enhancing the learning process and representation capabilities of discriminators. The approach, referred to as the Discriminator Augmentation Graph (DAG), leverages data augmentation while preserving the Jensen-Shannon divergence (JS) objective, ensuring convergence akin to traditional GANs. The authors assert that the number of shared layers does not affect the JS-preserving property and that empirical results demonstrate significant performance improvements with this regularization method.

The study focuses on invertible transformations within the image domain, such as rotation and flipping, while also showing effectiveness with non-invertible transformations like cropping and translation. The DAG framework can be integrated with various GAN models, including unconditional and conditional GANs, allowing for augmented data training and multiple discriminator utilization without limitations on augmentation techniques.

The experimental section details an ablation study to evaluate the significance of discriminator regularization and JS preservation, particularly using the Dist-GAN and SS-GAN as baselines. The authors outline their training methodology, including the use of specific parameters and batch sizes, while also describing their evaluation metrics, notably the Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) for assessing the quality of generated samples.

Results indicate that weight sharing among discriminators enhances performance, with the study examining different configurations of shared layers. The findings suggest that sharing all layers except the last (heads) is both efficient and effective. Overall, the DAG framework demonstrates superior performance over baseline GAN models, contributing to advancements in the field of GAN research.
