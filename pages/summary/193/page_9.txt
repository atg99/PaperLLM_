The paper presents an ablation study on the number of branches in a Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) model, using Dist-GAN combined with DAG as a baseline. The experiments reveal that increasing the number of branches (K) improves the Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) score, with K=10 yielding the best performance at 22.4. The authors also demonstrate the effectiveness of their best DAG version (K=10) on limited datasets, achieving an FID of 30.5, significantly better than the baseline of 54.6.

Further comparisons with state-of-the-art Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) were conducted. When applying their DAG to a self-supervised GAN (SS-DistGAN), the combination showed competitive results in natural image datasets like CIFAR-10 and STL-10, outperforming other unconditional GANs and approaching the performance of conditional GANs like SAGAN and BigGAN, despite not using labeled data.

The DAG was also tested with conditional GANs, notably BigGAN, where it significantly improved performance on limited datasets. In image-image translation tasks using CycleGAN, the integration of DAG led to enhanced accuracy metrics.

The paper also investigates mode collapse in Stacked MNIST, finding that their system outperformed previous methods in diversity and stability. Additionally, experiments on medical imaging using the IXI dataset indicated that DAG substantially improved FID scores on limited data.

Lastly, the authors evaluated the training time of their models, comparing those with and without DAG. Overall, the results illustrate the effectiveness and versatility of the proposed DAG augmentation approach across various GAN frameworks and datasets.
