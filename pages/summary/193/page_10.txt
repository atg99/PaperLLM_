The paper presents a Data Augmentation optimized GAN (DAG) framework designed to enhance the learning capabilities of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) by effectively incorporating various data augmentation techniques. This approach aims to improve the stability of both the discriminator and generator, thereby better capturing the distribution of the original dataset. The authors provide both theoretical and empirical analyses, demonstrating that DAG maintains the Jensen-Shannon divergence of original GANs through invertible transformations, leading to improved convergence.

The experimental results indicate that the DAG framework significantly enhances the performance of baseline GAN models across multiple datasets, including CIFAR-10, STL-10, and Stacked-MNIST, achieving state-of-the-art Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) scores. The method is particularly beneficial in scenarios with limited data, making it applicable in various fields, including medical applications.

The paper highlights the computational efficiency of the proposed method, using K=4 for data augmentation, which balances FID scores with processing time. The results suggest that even with a reduced dataset size, the incorporation of DAG can yield performance comparable to training on a full dataset. Overall, the findings underscore the potential of DAG in advancing GAN performance and stability.
