The paper presents a comparative analysis of different Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) methods, specifically focusing on the FID (Fréchet Inception Distance) scores achieved by DistGAN and SS-GAN with and without data augmentation techniques (DAG and DA). The results indicate that using the proposed DAG method significantly reduces FID scores across various transformations such as rotation, flipping, cropping, and translation, indicating improved image quality.

Table IV shows the FID scores for DistGAN and SS-GAN, both with and without augmented samples during generator learning. Notably, DistGAN + DAG achieves the lowest FID score of 23.7, while its baseline counterpart, DistGAN + DAG (-G), scores 30.1. For SS-GAN, the FID scores are 25.2 and 31.5, respectively. This trend is consistent across different data augmentation strategies.

Figures 5 and 6 further illustrate the performance of the models over training iterations, demonstrating that both DistGAN and SS-GAN benefit from the DAG approach, especially with limited dataset sizes, where DistGAN + DAG maintains competitive FID scores compared to the baseline trained on the full dataset.

Table V compares FID scores for DistGAN with and without the DAG method, showing improvements across various transformations, with the best performance in rotation (23.7) and flipping+rotation (23.3). Similarly, Table VI highlights SS-GAN's performance, where the addition of DAG consistently leads to lower FID scores compared to the baseline.

Overall, the findings support the argument that the integration of DAG into GAN architectures enhances performance, particularly in scenarios with limited training data, thereby addressing issues like mode collapse and divergence effectively. The results underline the importance of data augmentation in improving the quality of generated samples in GANs.
