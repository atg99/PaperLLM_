The paper presents a modified Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) model that utilizes an augmented dataset to enhance the learning of the target distribution. The authors introduce a theorem demonstrating that the Jensen-Shannon (JS) divergence between two distributions remains invariant under differentiable and invertible transformations. This property allows the GAN to effectively train using transformed samples while maintaining the learning of the original data distribution.

The training mechanism involves transforming both real and fake samples before they are fed into the discriminator. The generator is designed to produce samples that mimic these transformed real samples, ensuring that it learns the original data distribution. The mini-max objective remains the same as the original GAN, but the discriminator now operates on transformed samples, leading to an equivalent minimization of the JS divergence between the transformed real and fake distributions.

Building on this framework, the authors propose an approach called Improved Data Augmentation (IDA), which incorporates augmented samples into GAN training. This technique aims to enhance the generalization capabilities of the discriminator by increasing feature invariance to specific transformations. However, while IDA leverages the benefits of invertible transformations, it does not preserve the JS divergence of the original GAN, which may affect convergence. The authors outline the limitations of IDA in a theorem, indicating that the JS divergence of the original distributions may be greater than or equal to that of the transformed mixtures.

Overall, the paper explores innovative methods to improve GAN training through transformations and data augmentation while addressing the implications for the convergence of the model.
