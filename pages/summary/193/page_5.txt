This paper presents an enhancement to the Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) framework, specifically through the introduction of Data Augmentation optimized for GAN (DAG). The authors compare the conventional GAN model with their proposed method, which incorporates transformed data to improve the learning process.

In the original GAN setup, a single discriminator assesses the authenticity of real versus fake samples. However, the authors propose a model with multiple discriminators, each trained on different transformed samples. This approach aims to leverage the diversity of transformed data to better capture the distribution of the original dataset.

The paper discusses the limitations of existing methods like the Invertible Data Augmentation (IDA), which, despite using augmented samples, does not significantly outperform baseline models due to its lower-bound minimization of Jensen-Shannon (JS) divergence. The DAG framework addresses this issue by training discriminators on various transformations while maintaining the generator's objective to produce original images, not their transformed versions.

The discriminator's objective is augmented with contributions from multiple transformed discriminators, allowing the generator to receive multiple feedback signals that enhance its learning process. The authors argue that this method ensures optimal convergence of the discriminators and improves the generator's performance in creating realistic samples.

The analysis indicates that if the transformations are invertible, the convergence of the GAN is guaranteed, and the performance of the proposed model shows a slight improvement in FID scores compared to the baseline. The authors also suggest that weight sharing among discriminators could further enhance feature representation learning, potentially leading to better overall performance.
