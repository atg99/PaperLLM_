The paper presents a comparison of various methods for the ST-VQA (Scene Text Visual Question Answering) dataset, focusing on their performance across three tasks. The metrics analyzed include Average Normalized Levenshtein Similarity (ANLS) and Accuracy (Acc) for methods that utilize Optical Character Recognition (OCR), question (Q), and visual (V) information.

Key findings include:

1. Baseline Comparison: Traditional Visual Question Answering (VQA) models that do not incorporate textual information tend to perform worse than heuristic methods like STR (retrieval) and Scene Image OCR. This indicates the importance of integrating textual data to enhance VQA performance.

2. Method Performance: The SAAA and SAN models showed slight improvements when combined with visual features and PHOC-based textual features, as seen in the SAAA+STR and SAN+STR configurations.

3. Question Type Analysis: A bar chart analysis illustrates that the STR model generally outperforms the Scene Image OCR in terms of ANLS across various question types. However, none of the models effectively address questions related to license plates or specific numerical queries, which highlights a limitation in treating VQA as a classification issue.

4. PHOC Features: The use of PHOC (Projection-based Heterogeneous Output Codes) embeddings is emphasized for their ability to capture word morphology, making them more effective for answering questions that may not be represented in standard semantic models.

Overall, the results underscore the necessity of incorporating textual information in VQA systems to improve accuracy and address challenges posed by out-of-vocabulary responses.
