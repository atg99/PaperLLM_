The paper discusses the ST-VQA dataset, which consists of 23,038 images and 31,791 question-answer pairs. The dataset was created by combining images from six public datasets, including ICDAR, ImageNet, and COCO-Text, to reduce biases and enhance variability in questions. An end-to-end text retrieval architecture was employed to select images that contain at least two text instances, ensuring questions could have multiple answer options.

To gather questions and answers, the authors utilized Amazon Mechanical Turk, instructing workers to formulate closed-ended questions based on text in images. The collection process involved two steps: generating question-answer pairs and verifying them through a second task to ensure clarity and consistency in answers. The final dataset includes a training set of 19,027 images with 26,308 questions and a test set of 2,993 images with 4,163 questions.

The paper also compares the ST-VQA dataset with the TextVQA dataset, noting that the length distributions of questions and answers are similar. Most questions begin with words like "What" or "Where," indicating that they often require background knowledge beyond just the visible text in the images. This suggests that the questions are designed to engage with broader contextual understanding rather than solely relying on the text present in the images.
