The paper discusses an open-lexicon task within the Visual Question Answering (VQA) framework, emphasizing its potential to enhance research in processing and generating prior information for model design and performance. The authors propose a novel evaluation metric known as Average Normalized Levenshtein Similarity (ANLS) to assess model outputs in relation to ground truth answers, addressing limitations of traditional accuracy measures that do not account for OCR errors. ANLS provides a score that reflects both OCR accuracy and model reasoning by penalizing significant discrepancies in answer recognition.

The paper outlines the evaluation methodology, defining ANLS mathematically and establishing a threshold to differentiate between correct and incorrect outputs based on the normalized Levenshtein distance. Furthermore, the authors present an online service for researchers to benchmark their methods against a public validation/test dataset.

In the results section, various baselines are described, including random selection and scene text retrieval methods that utilize visual information, scene text, and questions. These baselines help illustrate the dataset's complexity and the performance bounds of different approaches, highlighting the challenges inherent in the proposed tasks.
