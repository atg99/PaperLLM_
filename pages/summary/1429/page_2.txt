The paper discusses the challenges of integrating textual information into Visual Question Answering (VQA) systems, highlighting the complexities of recognizing and interpreting text within visual contexts. Current VQA models suffer from biases and limitations in tasks such as counting and attribute identification, making them inadequate for incorporating scene text, which often does not correlate with visual statistics.

To address these issues, the authors propose a new dataset named Scene Text Visual Question Answering (ST-VQA). In this dataset, questions are designed to be answered solely based on text present in images, with 85.5% of the images sourced from existing datasets that contain generic question-answer pairs. This integration aims to create a more comprehensive VQA task. The ST-VQA includes three progressively challenging tasks that simulate varying levels of contextual information availability. A novel evaluation metric is introduced, utilizing Levenshtein distance to assess models' reasoning and text recognition capabilities.

The paper also reviews related works in text detection and recognition, noting that while several approaches exist, few datasets like TextVQA incorporate textual content in VQA tasks. The ST-VQA dataset distinguishes itself from TextVQA by its diverse image sources and specific focus on questions that require direct answers from image text. Overall, the proposed dataset and evaluation framework aim to enhance VQA systems by effectively leveraging scene text for improved understanding and reasoning.
