The paper discusses a framework for visual question answering (VQA) that incorporates scene text retrieval (STR) to enhance model performance. The approach begins with the STR bounding box method, which focuses on the largest text instance in an image, extracting text representation from the biggest bounding box and identifying the nearest word from corresponding dictionaries.

For processing images, the paper utilizes a state-of-the-art text recognition model to rank detected text based on confidence scores. The most confident detections are matched to provided vocabularies, with direct adoption of the highest confidence detection for certain tasks.

Two standard VQA models are evaluated: "Show, Ask, Attend and Answer" (SAAA) and "Stacked Attention Networks" (SAN). SAAA employs a CNN-LSTM architecture to extract image features and generate attention maps, while SAN utilizes a VGGN CNN and offers two question encoding methods (LSTM and CNN). Both models are optimized with specific parameters over multiple epochs.

The paper outlines three experiments that vary the size of the output classification vector, ranging from the most common answers in the training set to a comprehensive list of all answers found. The goal is to assess the impact of vocabulary size on model performance.

A significant contribution of this work is the fusion of modalities, combining textual features from the STR model with the VQA models. This involves selecting confident predictions and concatenating them with image features before calculating attention maps.

Results are presented in a table comparing the performance of different baselines using the Average Normalized Levenshtein Similarity (ANLS) metric and standard accuracy measures. The findings indicate that while standard VQA models achieve similar scores, the SAAA model benefits from a larger classification vector, although performance declines with excessively large vocabularies. The ANLS metric is shown to correlate well with accuracy while also addressing cases where text recognition errors occur, demonstrating its utility in evaluating VQA performance.
