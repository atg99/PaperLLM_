This paper presents the Scene Text Visual Question Answering (ST-VQA) dataset, which emphasizes the significance of utilizing semantic information from scene text in images to enhance the VQA process. The ST-VQA dataset includes a wide variety of questions and answers, posing significant challenges for existing VQA methods. The authors conducted extensive experiments with baseline models, which highlighted the limitations of current approaches and established performance benchmarks.

The study reveals that integrating textual information into standard VQA models yields improvements; however, it also demonstrates that specialized methods, such as those based on Optical Character Recognition (OCR), can surpass generic models. This underscores the necessity for diverse approaches in the field. Traditional VQA models often treat the task as classification, but the complexity of scene text responses—where potential answers may include multiple words or out-of-dictionary terms—necessitates a generative framework similar to image captioning techniques.

The authors propose the Average Normalized Levenshtein Similarity metric, arguing it is more suitable for evaluating generative models rather than classification, as it provides a more responsive assessment of text recognition accuracy.

In conclusion, the ST-VQA dataset introduces a new dimension to visual question answering, highlighting the importance of effectively leveraging scene text for improved understanding and response accuracy in VQA systems. Future work will likely focus on refining these methods and exploring additional applications within the domain. The research received support from various projects and institutions, indicating a collaborative effort in advancing this area of study.
