The paper discusses the significance of language priors in visual question answering (VQA) datasets and how these priors can lead to uneven answer distributions. Specifically, it highlights examples from the VQA v1 dataset, where certain answers dominate, such as "tennis" and "baseball" for sports questions and "yes" for yes/no questions. In contrast, the ST-VQA dataset aims for a more uniform answer distribution, minimizing the influence of language priors while maintaining a diverse vocabulary.

The paper contrasts ST-VQA with other datasets, noting that VQA 2.0 contains a vast number of questions, but only a small fraction require reading text from images. The TextVQA dataset includes more images and questions but suffers from a significant portion of answers not being directly tied to the text present in the images. ST-VQA addresses this by ensuring that each answer is explicitly grounded in the scene text, allowing for the collection of a single, unambiguous answer per question. The authors introduce a soft metric for evaluation that accounts for the text recognition performance, demonstrating that ST-VQA's approach results in similarly low ambiguity compared to TextVQA.

Furthermore, the paper presents three novel tasks designed for the ST-VQA dataset: "strongly contextualised," "weakly contextualised," and "open vocabulary." These tasks are differentiated based on the degree of prior knowledge provided, with the strongly contextualised task employing a unique dictionary for each image, while the weakly contextualised task uses a universal dictionary. The open dictionary task does not provide any prior information, emphasizing a blank-slate approach. The paper outlines the methodology for generating dictionaries for these tasks, incorporating both recognized scene text and semantically relevant distractors. Overall, the proposed tasks reflect an increasing level of difficulty and aim to enhance the understanding of scene text in VQA contexts.
