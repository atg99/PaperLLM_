The paper by Yu-Feng Li et al. introduces the concept of Safe Semi-Supervised Learning (SSL), focusing on the importance of graph quality in enhancing the safety and performance of SSL methods. The authors highlight that poor graph quality can lead to suboptimal results in Graph-based Semi-Supervised Learning (GSSL). They propose a large margin-based method for assessing graph quality, arguing that graphs with a high quality will yield predictive results with significant margin separation. This approach encourages the use of high-margin graphs to mitigate performance degradation.

Li et al. suggest a stacking method that regenerates a new SSL dataset based on predictive results from candidate graphs, framing safe GSSL as a classical Semi-Supervised SVM optimization on this dataset. Experimental results support their assertion that the large margin principle effectively judges graph quality and enhances GSSL safety. 

Subsequent work by Liang and Li formulates safe graph construction as a minimax optimization problem, optimizing the worst-case nearest-neighbor error on candidate graphs. Guo et al. further develop a graph construction method based on the large margin principle by optimizing a margin-type loss.

The paper also addresses data selection strategies for safe GSSL. Wang et al. introduce GsslIs, which focuses on identifying unlabeled instances that are likely to improve performance while avoiding high-risk samples. Li and Zhou propose S3VM, which utilizes hierarchical clustering to assess the reliability of unlabeled instances, retaining only those with high confidence for prediction.

Additionally, the paper discusses model uncertainty in SSL, noting that instability in model performance can stem from incorrect selections during trials. Issues in generative methods and disagreement-based methods are identified as contributing factors to performance degradation. The authors develop the safe S3VM (S4VM) method, which generates a pool of diverse large margin low-density separators, aiming to optimize label selection and enhance safety in SSL.
