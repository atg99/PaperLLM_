This paper highlights the necessity of ensuring the quality of data, managing model uncertainty, and maintaining performance diversity when incorporating unlabeled examples.

The authors begin by acknowledging the success of supervised learning, particularly deep learning, which relies heavily on large datasets with labeled instances. However, in many practical scenarios, acquiring sufficient labeled data is challenging due to various constraints. Semi-supervised learning (SSL) emerges as a solution, leveraging both a limited number of labeled instances and a vast amount of unlabeled data to optimize model performance.

Despite the potential benefits of SSL, the paper points out empirical evidence indicating that the inclusion of unlabeled data can sometimes lead to degraded performance. This risk underscores the importance of developing safe SSL approaches that ensure the generalization performance remains statistically comparable to methods using only labeled data. 

The review focuses on three critical aspects of safeness in semi-supervised learning: 
1. Data quality, emphasizing the need for high-quality training data to avoid risks associated with low-quality or noisy data.
2. Model uncertainty, addressing the challenges faced by learning algorithms in effectively managing uncertainty during the training process.
3. Measure diversity, which involves adapting safe performance evaluations to various metrics.

In conclusion, the authors advocate for the advancement of safe semi-supervised learning techniques that can harness the advantages of unlabeled data while mitigating the risks of performance degradation, thereby making SSL more viable for real-world applications.
