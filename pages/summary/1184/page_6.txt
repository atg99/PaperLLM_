This paper discusses weakly supervised learning, which is closely related to semi-supervised learning. It defines weakly supervised learning as a machine learning approach that operates under incomplete supervision, where only a subset of training data is labeled. It highlights different types of weak supervision, including inexact supervision with coarse labels and inaccurate supervision with potentially incorrect labels. The prevalence of weakly supervised data in real-world applications, such as label noise learning and domain adaptation, is noted, along with the associated risks of performance degradation.

The paper reviews recent research aimed at enhancing the safety of weakly supervised learning, emphasizing the integration of base learners to improve outcomes. Specific studies are mentioned, including one by Guo and Li that proposes a method to aggregate predictions from multiple weak learners, ensuring safety under certain loss functions. Wei et al. explore safe multi-label learning, optimizing metrics based on a combination of base learners. However, the paper acknowledges uncertainties regarding the impact of different labels on safety when using weakly supervised data.

The role of clustering analysis in unsupervised learning is also examined, highlighting its potential to enhance learning with limited labels. The challenges in evaluating clustering methods and improving performance for categorical data are noted.

Furthermore, the paper addresses automated machine learning (AutoML) and its limited applicability to semi-supervised learning (SSL), outlining the challenges of automated SSL, including the absence of certain meta-features and the potential drawbacks of using unlabeled data. Li et al. are mentioned for their work on an automated learning system for SSL that incorporates meta-learning to improve performance.

The discussion extends to the difficulties of learning in dynamic environments, such as distribution drift and the emergence of new classes, which complicate the application of SSL. The paper concludes by emphasizing the importance of safely leveraging unlabeled data in SSL, identifying three key areas of focus: data quality, model uncertainty, and measure diversity. It acknowledges the limitations of the review and encourages further exploration of the boundary conditions for safe SSL in future research.
