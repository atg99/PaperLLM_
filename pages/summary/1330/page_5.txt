The paper discusses the importance of ethical standards in the development and deployment of autonomous systems, particularly through the introduction of the P7000 series of standards, which include P7001. These standards aim to enhance transparency and explainability for system designers, ensuring that the quality management processes of developers and manufacturers are adequately documented. 

The outlook section addresses the voluntary nature of these standards and proposes several pathways for their adoption. Firstly, safety-related standards can be mandated by licensing authorities, making compliance essential for operational licensing. Secondly, adherence to ethical standards can serve as a competitive advantage in the marketplace, especially among consumers who prioritize ethical considerations. Thirdly, professional bodies are increasingly advocating for ethical behavior among their members, leading to internal pressure on organizations to adopt these standards. Lastly, soft governance, such as making compliance a requirement for procurement contracts, can influence widespread adoption of standards across supply chains.

The paper acknowledges the ongoing debate regarding the impact of robotics and AI on society but emphasizes the ethical challenges posed by advancing intelligent systems. The establishment of ethical standards is presented as a crucial step in building trust in robots and AI, which is necessary for their acceptance and realization of their potential benefits.

Alan Winfield, a notable figure in robot ethics, emphasizes that without ethical standards, the trustworthiness and societal acceptance of these technologies remain in jeopardy. The views expressed in the paper are solely those of the author.
