Alan Winfield discusses the essential role of standards in modern society, comparing them to fundamental infrastructures like roads and airports. He notes that standards, such as those published by the International Organization for Standardization (ISO), encompass a wide range of everyday activities, underscoring their significance for health and safety.

Winfield highlights that all standards inherently embody ethical principles, such as safety, quality, and interoperability. He distinguishes between implicit ethical standards, which are inherent in all standards, and explicit ethical standards, which directly address articulated ethical concerns. These explicit standards aim to mitigate or highlight potential unethical impacts of technology.

The paper references an informal survey from December 2017, identifying ten sets of ethical principles for robotics and AI, with many published recently. The foundational principles suggest that robots and AIs should prioritize safety, avoid bias and deception, respect human rights and dignity, and maintain transparency and accountability. The increasing frequency of these publications indicates a growing commitment to establishing ethical frameworks in the development and deployment of robotics and AI technologies.
