The paper presents a comparison between the Performance of PiT (Pooling-based Vision Transformer) and ViT (Vision Transformer) architectures on the ImageNet dataset. The analysis includes various training techniques and architectures, focusing on efficiency and performance.

The results, summarized in Table 2, indicate that PiT consistently outperforms ViT across different configurations while requiring fewer FLOPs and exhibiting faster throughput. Specifically, PiT models demonstrate superior performance even in simpler training settings like vanilla and CutMix, and maintain comparable or improved results in more complex settings such as DeiT and distillation.

The experiments also extend the comparison to ResNet architectures, assessing whether PiT can surpass their performance. Additionally, PiT's effectiveness is evaluated as a backbone for object detection using deformable DETR, alongside robustness benchmarks to analyze performance from multiple perspectives.

The paper highlights that while ViT models often show stagnant performance with increased size, PiT's performance improves consistently with model size due to its pooling layers, addressing ViT's generalization issues. Furthermore, comparisons with convolutional networks are made, emphasizing the need for distinct training settings for different architectures. Overall, the findings suggest that PiT is a more efficient and effective architecture than ViT for image classification tasks.
