This paper presents a comparative analysis of different neural network architectures, specifically focusing on ResNet, Vision Transformer (ViT), and the proposed Pooling-based Vision Transformer (PiT). The authors illustrate the dimensional configurations of these networks, highlighting how ResNet reduces spatial dimensions progressively, while ViT maintains spatial dimensions throughout. PiT integrates a ResNet-like spatial dimension reduction into the ViT framework.

The study delves into the attention mechanisms within the transformer blocks of ViT and PiT, employing entropy and average distance measures to analyze attention patterns. The findings indicate that PiT outperforms ViT across various tasks, including ImageNet classification and object detection on the COCO 2017 dataset. The paper also discusses the optimal scaling of transformer architectures compared to convolutional neural networks (CNNs).

In the context of related works, the authors review the evolution of dimension configurations in CNNs, tracing back to early networks like AlexNet and discussing various approaches to spatial and channel size adjustments in subsequent architectures. They also explore the self-attention mechanism and its application to vision tasks, noting how previous research has integrated attention layers into CNN backbones.

Overall, this research contributes to the understanding of network architectures in computer vision and proposes a new direction for the design of effective models, emphasizing the advantages of combining pooling strategies with transformer frameworks.
