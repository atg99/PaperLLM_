The paper investigates the impact of spatial dimensions on model capability, generalization performance, and overall model performance, focusing on ResNet50 and the proposed Pooling-based Vision Transformer (PiT). The authors emphasize that while dimension conversion is common in convolutional architectures, its effects are underexplored. They demonstrate that ResNet-style dimensions outperform ViT-style configurations in terms of model capability and generalization performance.

The study details how ResNet50 reduces spatial dimensions while increasing channel dimensions through various layers. Experiments reveal that ResNet with ResNet-style dimensions achieves lower training loss and higher validation accuracy compared to configurations using ViT-style dimensions. This indicates that ResNet-style dimensions enhance architectural capability and generalization.

To leverage these benefits in Vision Transformers, the authors introduce PiT, which incorporates a new pooling layer designed to adapt the spatial dimensions akin to ResNet. This pooling layer is crucial for enhancing the self-attention mechanism in ViT, as it allows for effective spatial interaction by adjusting the configuration to mirror that of ResNet.

Overall, the findings suggest that adopting ResNet-style dimensions in ViT can lead to significant improvements in model performance, and the proposed PiT architecture is a step towards integrating these advantages into transformer networks.
