The paper presents a comparative analysis of various neural network architectures on the ImageNet dataset, focusing on their parameters, throughput, and accuracy. It highlights the performance of PiT models (Progressive Image Transformers) against established architectures such as ResNet and EfficientNet.

Key findings indicate that while PiT models exhibit superior performance at larger scales (ResNet50 and above), they lag behind convolutional architectures at smaller scales, such as PiT-Ti compared to ResNet34 and MobileNetV3. This suggests that the transformer architecture is more effective in handling complex tasks when scaled up, but there is a need for lightweight transformers for smaller models.

Extended training settings were also explored, revealing that PiT models maintain competitive performance with ViT models when subjected to long training periods (1000 epochs) and fine-tuning at larger resolutions (384x384). However, they show a performance drop in throughput at larger resolutions, indicating a design limitation for such settings.

Additionally, the paper evaluates the object detection capabilities of PiT models using the COCO dataset, demonstrating that while they can serve as effective backbones for detection tasks, performance varies based on image resolution and architecture.

Overall, the research underlines the potential of PiT architectures, particularly at larger scales, while identifying areas for improvement in lightweight designs and performance consistency across different training settings.
