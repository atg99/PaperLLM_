The paper analyzes the attention mechanisms in Vision Transformers (ViT) and proposes a new architecture called PiT. The focus is on spatial tokens, excluding the class token, and the last transformer block's attention is disregarded due to its independence from network outputs. The findings indicate that in ViT, as layers deepen, entropy and interaction distance increase, suggesting that shallow layers concentrate on nearby tokens while deeper layers interact across a wider range. In contrast, PiT exhibits varying patterns based on spatial dimensions, with shallow layers showing increased entropy and distance due to larger spatial sizes, while deep layers demonstrate decreased values due to smaller sizes. This behavior suggests that PiT spreads interactions in shallow layers and concentrates them in deeper layers, making it more suitable for image recognition tasks compared to ViT.

The architecture of PiT is designed to be comparable to smaller ViT models, specifically targeting scales similar to ViT-Tiny, ViT-Small, and ViT-Base. PiT incorporates two pooling layers and employs a reduced stride size in the embedding layer to accommodate larger spatial sizes. The proposed configurations aim for reduced FLOPs, parameter size, and GPU latency compared to ViT, while maintaining similar depth and channel adjustments. The paper concludes that PiT's design can be further refined through network architecture search, indicating its potential for improved performance in image recognition tasks.
