The paper "Rethinking Spatial Dimensions of Vision Transformers" by Byeongho Heo et al. explores the adaptation of design principles from convolutional neural networks (CNNs) to Vision Transformers (ViTs) in computer vision tasks. The authors highlight that while ViTs have shown promising results in image recognition, they lack the spatial dimension reduction mechanisms that are integral to CNNs. 

The study investigates the importance of spatial dimension conversion, which in CNNs involves increasing channel dimensions while decreasing spatial dimensions as the network depth increases. The authors empirically demonstrate that applying similar spatial dimension reductions in ViTs enhances model capability and generalization performance.

To implement this, the authors propose a novel architecture called Pooling-based Vision Transformer (PiT), which incorporates a pooling layer into the ViT framework. Through extensive experiments, they show that PiT outperforms the baseline ViT across various tasks, including image classification and object detection, establishing that the incorporation of CNN-inspired design principles can significantly benefit transformer architectures in computer vision. 

The findings suggest that adapting established CNN design conventions can improve the performance of ViTs, paving the way for more effective transformer-based models in the field. The source codes and models are made available for further research.
