The paper presents a comparison between Pooling-based Vision Transformer (PiT) and the original Vision Transformer (ViT), focusing on model capability, generalization performance, and overall model performance. The authors demonstrate that PiT outperforms ViT in these aspects.

In the PiT architecture, a pooling layer based on depth-wise convolution is introduced to enhance channel multiplication and spatial reduction while maintaining a small number of parameters. This pooling layer reshapes spatial tokens into a 3D tensor, facilitating spatial size reduction and channel increase. The authors argue that this approach enables better alignment with the spatial structure compared to ViT, which lacks this correspondence in certain components like the class token.

Experimental results indicate that PiT achieves lower training loss at the same computational cost compared to ViT, suggesting improved model capability. Additionally, while ViT shows limited improvement in validation accuracy despite increased training accuracy, PiT demonstrates a positive correlation between training and validation accuracy, indicating superior generalization performance. The authors attribute the performance gap to the necessity of spatial reduction layers for enhancing ViT's generalization.

Attention analysis is also conducted, focusing on the attention matrix's entropy and distance metrics. The entropy reflects the degree of attention spread or concentration, while attention distance offers insights into the relative spatial locations of tokens. The findings from these metrics are averaged across validation images and transformer heads, providing a comprehensive understanding of the attention dynamics in the models.

Overall, the paper underscores the advantages of the PiT architecture over the original ViT, emphasizing its potential for improved generalization and performance in visual tasks.
