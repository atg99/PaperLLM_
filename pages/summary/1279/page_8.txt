The paper presents a comparative analysis of the robustness of various image classification architectures on ImageNet benchmarks, specifically focusing on PiT (Pooling in Transformers), ViT (Vision Transformer), and ResNet50. The study evaluates their performance under different robustness benchmarks including occlusion, ImageNet-A (IN-A), background challenge (BGC), and adversarial attacks using the fast gradient sign method (FGSM).

The results indicate that PiT outperforms ViT in all robustness benchmarks despite similar performances on the standard ImageNet classification task. This suggests that the pooling mechanism in PiT enhances the model's resilience to background changes and local feature sensitivity. For instance, PiT demonstrates only a 5% performance drop with occluded samples, while ResNet50 experiences a 15% drop, indicating a higher sensitivity to local features typical of convolutional operations.

Interestingly, while ResNet50 shows superior results in the BGC benchmark compared to ViT, the findings highlight a potential drawback of the self-attention mechanism in Vision Transformers, which may inadvertently focus more on background information. The paper concludes that incorporating spatial dimensional transformations, such as pooling, into transformer architectures is crucial for improving computational efficiency and generalization ability. This work advocates for further exploration into enhancing the robustness of vision transformers by addressing these limitations.
