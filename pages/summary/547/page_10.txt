The referenced papers contribute to the field of neural architecture search (NAS), focusing on improving reproducibility and efficiency in finding optimal neural network architectures. 

In "Nas-bench-101," Ying et al. introduce a benchmark for NAS that aims to standardize the evaluation of different architectures, facilitating reproducibility in research. They provide a comprehensive dataset that allows for systematic comparisons across various NAS methods.

Zhou et al. present "Bayesnas," which employs a Bayesian approach to NAS, enhancing the search process by incorporating uncertainty estimation. This method aims to improve the selection of architectures by considering the probabilistic nature of model performance.

Zoph et al. explore the concept of transferable architectures in their work on scalable image recognition. They demonstrate how learned architectures can be effectively transferred across different datasets, emphasizing the potential for generalization in NAS.

Together, these works highlight ongoing efforts to make neural architecture search more systematic, reliable, and applicable across various tasks in computer vision.
