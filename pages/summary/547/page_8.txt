The paper discusses the implementation and performance of a Neural Predictor designed to identify high-quality mobile-sized models with optimal trade-offs between accuracy and inference time. The study involves a comparison of the Neural Predictor against random search and ProxylessNAS, showcasing its ability to generalize well to unseen data with validation accuracy exceeding 76%.

The methodology includes sampling 112,000 models with specific inference time constraints and predicting their accuracies. The results validate the effectiveness of the Neural Predictor, which outperformed the random search baseline and showed comparable performance to ProxylessNAS. To ensure robust model selection, a soft version of Pareto optimality is introduced, allowing for a more nuanced selection process that accommodates potential inaccuracies in predictions.

The discussion highlights the efficiency of the Neural Predictor relative to traditional methods like Regularized Evolution, indicating that it is significantly more computationally efficient. While both the Neural Predictor and ProxylessNAS yield comparable model quality, the former offers advantages in targeting various latency requirements and can be completed more quickly when parallelized.

In conclusion, the paper posits that the Neural Predictor and ProxylessNAS can be seen as complementary strategies in neural architecture search, with the choice between them largely dependent on the trade-offs between model quality and computational resource constraints.
