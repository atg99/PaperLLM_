The paper discusses the search space of ProxylessNAS, which utilizes a linear graph structure with specific optional operations for convolutional layers. The focus is on Inverted Bottleneck (IB) layers with various kernel sizes and expansion factors, while a zero operation is included for layer skipping. The search space size is notably large, indicating a complex architecture to explore.

In developing the Neural Predictor, the authors adapted their approach from NASBench-101, noting that the stability of models in ProxylessNAS allows for a single-stage predictor. The node representation was simplified to a one-hot vector to accurately describe architectures within the linear graph structure. 

For training and validation, the authors randomly sampled 119 models, splitting them into 79 for training and 40 for validation. They optimized the hyperparameters of a Graph Convolutional Network (GCN) based on mean squared error (MSE) across multiple splits. The final GCN configuration included 18 layers and achieved an average MSE of 0.109 over 10 validation runs. The predictor demonstrated strong performance, indicated by a Kendall rank correlation coefficient of 0.649 and a coefficient of determination (R²) of 0.648895 for validation samples.

Furthermore, the generalization of the Neural Predictor was tested on unseen architectures, where a sample of 100,000 models was used to predict accuracies. The predictor performed remarkably well, with a Kendall rank correlation coefficient of 0.956 and an R² score of 0.929 for the true accuracies of selected models, even predicting higher accuracies than those observed during training.
