The supplementary material presents an ablation study of various neural predictor architectures applied to NASBench-101, comparing Graph Convolutional Networks (GCN) and Multi-layer Perceptrons (MLP). The study reveals that a simple MLP, which combines one-hot encoded node operations with the adjacency matrix, outperforms state-of-the-art Regularized Evolution, while the GCN architecture achieves the best performance. Attempts to utilize Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN) were unsuccessful, yielding results close to random search. To enhance MLP and CNN performance, data augmentation techniques were introduced, involving random permutations of node orders during training. However, to maintain the distribution consistency during validation, permutations were applied only during training. The GCN’s design promotes permutation invariance, solidifying its selection as the preferred architecture.

Additionally, the paper details the reproduction of Regularized Evolution based on guidelines from the original NASBench-101 publication, noting key differences in accuracy reporting methods. Unlike the original approach, which relied on single training runs, this study averages test accuracy over three runs to better account for variability. The implementation details for the Neural Predictor include specifications for NASBench-101 and ProxylessNAS on ImageNet, highlighting the architecture's configurations, training hyperparameters, and the rationale behind the choices made during the experiments. The predictor architecture for NASBench-101 consists of three bidirectional GCN layers, while ProxylessNAS utilizes 18 layers, with specific node representation sizes and training parameters detailed for both scenarios.
