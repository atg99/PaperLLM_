The paper "Neural Predictor for Neural Architecture Search" by Wei Wen et al. presents a simplified approach to Neural Architecture Search (NAS) that significantly enhances sample efficiency compared to traditional methods. The authors outline a three-step process to identify high-performing neural architectures without the extensive computational costs typically associated with NAS.

First, the method involves training a set of N random architectures to gather pairs of architecture and their corresponding validation accuracies. This data is then used to train a regression model that predicts validation accuracy based on architecture. In the second step, this regression model is employed to predict the validation accuracies of a larger pool of random architectures. Finally, the top K architectures, as determined by the regression model's predictions, are trained and evaluated to select the one with the highest validation accuracy for deployment.

The proposed Neural Predictor is shown to be over 20 times more sample efficient than Regularized Evolution on the NASBench-101 benchmark and competes effectively with more complex architectures like ProxylessNAS on ImageNet. This approach not only reduces the computational burden of architecture search but also simplifies the implementation process, making it more accessible for a broader range of machine learning practitioners.
