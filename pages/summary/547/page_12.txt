The paper presents an ablation study of a Neural Predictor utilizing various architectures within the NASBench-101 framework. The experiments, averaged over 600 trials, evaluate performance metrics such as validation and test accuracy against the number of samples and total training time. Key findings illustrate the performance of different methods, including GCN, MLP, regularized evolution, random search, and oracle, showing how they converge in accuracy as the sample size increases.

The study also details the training setup for image classification models on the ImageNet dataset, which consists of over 1.28 million training images and 50,000 validation images. A standard procedure was followed where the validation set was used as the test set due to the unavailability of the official test set. The training process employed distributed synchronous SGD across multiple TPU instances, utilizing specific hyperparameters such as a batch size of 128 per core and RMSProp optimization.

Overall, the findings emphasize the significance of computational resources and training methodologies in optimizing neural architecture search, highlighting the efficiency of the proposed Neural Predictor in various configurations.
