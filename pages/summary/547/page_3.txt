The paper discusses a method for optimizing neural network architectures through a process called Neural Architecture Search (NAS). It specifically focuses on utilizing Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) to represent and analyze potential architectures. The operations involved in the architecture include input, various convolutional layers (1x1 and 3x3), max-pooling, and the output layer, represented through a one-hot encoding scheme for each node in a directed acyclic graph (DAG).

The authors present a novel approach where the GCNs leverage both the original adjacency matrix and its transpose to facilitate bi-directional information flow. This is achieved by averaging the outputs of two GCN layers—one propagating information in the forward direction and the other in the reverse. The method enhances the learning of node representations, which are aggregated to evaluate the overall network performance.

The experiments section details two studies: the first analyzes the behavior of the Neural Predictor within the NASBench-101 dataset, a benchmark designed for evaluating NAS algorithms. It highlights the dataset's comprehensive nature, providing training, validation, and test results for all architectures, and emphasizes the importance of consistent training to reduce bias. The second part of the experiments focuses on searching for high-quality mobile models within the ProxylessNAS search space.

The paper also introduces an "oracle" baseline, representing an ideal scenario where all possible architectures are evaluated to find the best one, serving as a benchmark for the proposed method's effectiveness. The results indicate a strong correlation between validation and test accuracies, reinforcing the potential of the GCN-based approach in optimizing neural architectures.
