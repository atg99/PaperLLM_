In this paper, a two-stage model selection framework is proposed to enhance the efficiency of neural architecture search (NAS). The first stage filters out models predicted to achieve accuracy below 91%, allowing the second stage to focus on a more reliable accuracy range. Both stages utilize the same Graph Convolutional Network (GCN) architecture but differ in their output layers. The introduction of the filtering stage significantly reduces the mean squared error (MSE) for validation accuracy from 1.95 to 0.66, indicating improved predictive performance.

The results demonstrate that using 172 models for training (a mere 0.04% of the search space) allows the Neural Predictor to outperform Regularized Evolution in sample efficiency. After 1000 samples, the predictor achieves validation accuracy comparable to an oracle, with faster performance in both validation and test accuracies, reaching the same results in significantly reduced time frames.

An ablation study on the optimal value of N reveals that a very small N leads to poor predictions, while a large N with a small K does not compensate for decreased evaluation budgets. 

To validate the approach's generalizability, experiments on the ImageNet dataset with the ProxylessNAS search space are conducted. The goal here is to find efficient models that meet specific inference time constraints. The study compares results against a random search baseline and a reimplementation of ProxylessNAS. The random search samples models with inference times between 75ms and 85ms, while ProxylessNAS employs a weight-sharing and reinforcement learning strategy to explore the search space effectively.

The paper concludes with a successful reproduction of ProxylessNAS results, achieving an accuracy of 75.0%, which serves as a solid benchmark for evaluating the proposed method's effectiveness in larger-scale applications.
