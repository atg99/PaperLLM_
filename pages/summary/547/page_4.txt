The paper discusses the challenges in predicting test accuracy based on validation accuracy in neural architecture search (NAS), specifically using the NASBench-101 dataset. It highlights that even with knowledge of validation accuracies, one cannot reliably select the model that will perform best on the test set. The oracle model, which selects based on the highest validation accuracy of 95.15%, achieves a test accuracy of 94.08%, indicating that it does not correspond to the highest test performance. The global optimum test accuracy is 94.32%, yet this model cannot be identified through extensive validation.

The paper introduces the concept of variance in oracle methods by averaging results over multiple training runs, leading to a mean validation accuracy of 95.13% and a test accuracy of 94.18%. In comparing architecture search methods, the authors replicate random search as a baseline, which shows a significant gap to the oracle, with an average test accuracy of 93.66% after training 2000 models.

Regularized evolution is also evaluated and found to outperform random search, achieving an average accuracy of 93.97% after the same number of models, but still falling short of the oracle's performance. The paper outlines the setup for a Neural Predictor, which combines a classifier and regressor to filter out inaccurate models and predict the accuracies of accurate ones. The architecture of the Neural Predictor involves a graph representation of models using a directed acyclic graph, with nodes represented by one-hot encoded features. This setup aims to improve the selection process in NAS by addressing the limitations identified in traditional methods.
