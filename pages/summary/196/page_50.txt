The references provided focus on various approaches to exploration in reinforcement learning, particularly in complex and high-dimensional environments. Key themes include adaptive procedural task generation, model-based exploration, and the use of uncertainty in value functions to enhance exploration strategies.

Fang et al. discuss adaptive procedural task generation for difficult exploration challenges, emphasizing the need for more effective methods in reinforcement learning. Guestrin et al. present algorithm-directed exploration within factored Markov decision processes, highlighting the importance of structured approaches in model-based settings.

Abel et al. introduce exploratory gradient boosting as a method for reinforcement learning in complex domains, while Kovač et al. focus on robust goal sampling through learning progress in visual deep reinforcement learning. Osband and Van Roy explore the advantages of posterior sampling over optimistic approaches in reinforcement learning, indicating a shift in understanding exploration techniques.

Jung and Stone investigate the use of Gaussian processes for sample-efficient reinforcement learning, proposing Rmax-like exploration methods. Xie et al. contribute to model-based reinforcement learning by integrating parametrized physical models with optimism-driven exploration, enhancing the ability to navigate uncertain environments.

D'Eramo et al. exploit action-value uncertainty to inform exploration strategies, which is further examined by Osband and colleagues through randomized value functions that promote generalization and exploration. The culmination of these works points to a growing recognition of the interplay between exploration strategies and the underlying models used in reinforcement learning, advocating for methods that balance exploration and exploitation effectively.
