The paper discusses significant challenges and potential solutions in reinforcement learning, particularly focusing on the noisy-TV problem, optimal exploration, safe exploration, and transferability.

The noisy-TV problem remains unresolved, with memory-based solutions limited by their requirements. Clustering methods have shown promise in managing noisy states, but they depend on the effective design of clusters.

Optimal exploration is an area that is often overlooked. The paper highlights a proposed solution involving a demand matrix to track state-action exploration counts, which allows for efficient exploration without prior knowledge of the matrix. This approach requires further development.

Safe exploration is crucial for real-world applications, but current methods primarily rely on hand-designed rules, which struggle to prevent catastrophic outcomes. This issue is compounded by the lack of a clear definition of unsafe situations, making scalability and transferability problematic. A rigorous definition of unsafe situations could enhance the effectiveness of safe exploration strategies.

Lastly, the paper addresses transferability challenges in exploratory approaches, which typically perform poorly in new environments with varying state spaces and reward functions. The authors suggest that training agents in smaller scenarios could lead to better performance in larger, more complex environments, thus alleviating computational challenges.
