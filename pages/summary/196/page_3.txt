This paper provides a comprehensive review of exploration in reinforcement learning, tailored for beginners. It emphasizes a broad range of exploration approaches with simplified explanations. The term "reinforcement learning" is used generically to encompass both traditional and deep reinforcement learning.

The authors note that while there are several existing review articles on reinforcement learning, such as those by Aubert et al. on intrinsic motivation and Li on techniques and applications, none focus specifically on exploration in depth. The only prior review dedicated to exploration dates back to 1999, rendering it outdated.

Key contributions of this study include: 
1. A systematic overview of exploration methods in deep reinforcement learning, filling a gap in current literature.
2. A categorization of exploration strategies, designed to facilitate comparison among different approaches.
3. Identification and discussion of future challenges in the field.

The paper begins with foundational concepts in reinforcement learning, introducing the Markov Decision Process (MDP) framework. An MDP is characterized by a tuple including states, actions, transition probabilities, and rewards, with the objective of maximizing cumulative rewards over time. The authors also briefly mention value-based methods, which involve calculating state and action-value functions to guide the agent's decision-making process. 

Overall, this review aims to enhance understanding of exploration in reinforcement learning and provide a basis for further research in this area.
