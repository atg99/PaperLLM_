The paper discusses enhancements to standard random exploration methods in reinforcement learning. It identifies three key improvements: modifying states for exploration, adjusting exploration parameters based on the agent's learning progress, and applying noise to network parameters to promote exploration before weight convergence. 

In the context of state modification, certain states and actions are excluded from random selection if they have been sufficiently explored. The exploration parameter methods involve automatic adjustments depending on the agent's learning state. The network parameter noise approach adds randomness to parameters, facilitating exploration.

The paper evaluates the methods based on ease of implementation, computational cost, and overall performance. It finds that rewarding novel states, promoting diverse behaviors, and employing random-based strategies are the simplest to implement and require minimal computational resources. Among these, random-based approaches are particularly efficient due to their lightweight components. 

The highest-performing methods identified are goal-based and reward novel states methods, with goal-based methods excelling in complex exploratory tasks like Montezuma's Revenge, albeit with increased implementation complexity. Reward novel states methods strike a balance between ease of use and effectiveness, making them a favorable choice.
