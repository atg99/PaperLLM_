The paper discusses various intrinsic reward mechanisms for agents exploring environments, focusing on how these rewards can be structured based on uncertainty and prediction errors. It begins by outlining a grid-based environment where intrinsic rewards are placed at nodes, with a reset to default values upon episode termination. Sound is highlighted as a novel intrinsic reward source, modeled through the alignment of sound with corresponding frames, indicating novel states when misaligned.

The paper categorizes intrinsic reward methods based on uncertainty, emphasizing that higher uncertainty in exploration yields higher rewards. Several techniques are discussed: Bayesian approaches, which are often intractable for large spaces but can be approximated using methods like the Dirichlet distribution; ensemble models that generate intrinsic rewards based on model disagreements; and information-theoretic approaches that compute rewards based on information gained from actions. One notable method, variational information maximization exploration (VIME), uses Kullback-Leibler divergence to assess information gain.

The discussion highlights the advantages and challenges of different methods. Prediction error methods are advantageous for their reliance solely on environmental models, avoiding the need for complex approximations. However, they require learning to recognize diverse states, which can be challenging in environments with many states. State representation methods can handle large state spaces but increase designer burden and may lead to inaccuracies due to similar representations for different states. A priori knowledge methods rely on defining elements of the state space as sources of error for reward computation. Overall, the paper presents a comprehensive overview of intrinsic reward mechanisms, emphasizing their implications for agent learning in uncertain environments.
