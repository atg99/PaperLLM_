The referenced works focus on various aspects of curiosity-driven exploration in reinforcement learning (RL). Bougie and Ichise explore fast and slow curiosity mechanisms for enhancing high-level exploration in RL environments, emphasizing the need for intrinsic motivation in learning processes. They also discuss high-level intrinsic exploration strategies, suggesting that curiosity can drive agents to discover novel states effectively.

Osband et al. introduce randomized prior functions, enhancing deep RL by incorporating uncertainty into the learning process. Pathak and colleagues present self-supervised prediction methods that leverage curiosity to foster exploration, demonstrating the effectiveness of intrinsic rewards in complex environments.

Burda et al. conduct a large-scale study on curiosity-driven learning, providing insights into its impact on agent performance. Raileanu and Rocktaschel propose RIDE, a method that rewards agents for exploration based on their impact on procedurally generated environments.

Li et al. investigate random curiosity-driven exploration, while Pathak et al. introduce self-supervised exploration via disagreement, highlighting the importance of diverse experiences. Kim et al. present EMI, which uses mutual information to guide exploration, and Kim et al. also propose a curiosity-bottleneck approach that focuses on task-specific novelty.

Mirowski et al. address navigation in complex environments, showcasing the potential of RL methods in real-world applications. Collectively, these studies contribute to a deeper understanding of intrinsic motivation and curiosity in RL, providing frameworks and methodologies to enhance exploration and learning efficiency.
