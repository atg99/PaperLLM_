This paper discusses various exploratory goal-based reinforcement learning methods, highlighting different approaches and their performance in specific environments. The authors summarize results from multiple studies, showcasing advancements in techniques like DQN, M-UVFA, and IMGEP, which utilize different state representations and action spaces. Notable findings include:

- Machado et al. explored a four-room domain using a state identification approach.
- Abel et al. applied DQN in a visual hill climbing scenario in Malmo, achieving significant exploration.
- Forestier et al. focused on Minecraft's mountain car challenge, achieving 84% exploration with a state identification method.
- Colas et al. implemented M-UVFA for robot joint angle manipulation in OpenAI's goal fetch arm task, yielding an 0.8 success rate.
- Ghafoorian et al. and Riedmiller et al. demonstrated effective goal attainment in taxi driving and block stacking tasks using Q-learning and auxiliary rewards, respectively.
- Fang et al. explored task parameterization in GirdWorld, achieving a top score with robot joint angles and images.

The paper also delves into probabilistic methods, categorizing them into optimistic and uncertain approaches. Optimistic methods rely on upper confidence bounds of reward estimates, while uncertainty-based methods focus on minimizing environmental uncertainty by leveraging value function and state predictions. This distinction is crucial for understanding how agents utilize probability in decision-making processes.
