The paper discusses the differences between parameter sampling and action/Q-value sampling methods in reinforcement learning. Parameter sampling involves sampling from policy parameters, while action/Q-value sampling relies on the probability distribution of actions and Q-values, allowing for faster updates due to dynamic policy adjustments. However, action/Q-value sampling faces challenges in estimating exact probabilities, necessitating simplifications. Network ensembles are proposed as a solution to approximate the distribution over actions/states, allowing agents to either sample from this distribution or follow a specific model, albeit with higher computational demands.

One major issue with optimistic exploration is that confidence sets are constructed independently, leading to redundant exploration of states that do not yield rewards. This problem is deemed computationally intractable to resolve. In contrast, uncertainty methods build confidence bounds that are interdependent, thus avoiding unnecessary exploration.
