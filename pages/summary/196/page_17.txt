The discussion highlights the evolution of novelty search from a stand-alone technique to a more effective combined approach with other methods. This integration enhances performance by directing diverse behaviors toward higher rewards. In the realm of policy learning, recent studies have focused on incorporating diversity measures, assessing the distance between policies using methods like KL divergence and mean squared error. Promising outcomes have been observed, particularly in approaches that maximize skill entropy, infer behaviors from the current state, and promote randomness within skills. A noteworthy method involves the skew-t algorithm, which skews the goal distribution to facilitate exploration of less visited states, demonstrating effectiveness in both simulations and real-world robotics. Additionally, an experience replay mechanism allows agents to store successful policies and minimize the gap between current and optimal policies while maximizing parameter entropy to encourage exploration. The findings indicate a significant advantage over traditional strategies in environments with sparse rewards.
