The paper discusses goal-based methods in reinforcement learning, focusing on meta-controllers and sub-goal approaches. Meta-controllers are trained on a goal-state space, where each subtask is learned by a separate learner. To enhance performance, starting points for learning are shared among these learners. 

Sub-goal methods involve identifying specific sub-goals for the agent to reach, which can be categorized into three types: bottleneck states that facilitate exploration, progress towards the main goal that likely leads to rewards, and uncertainty-based sub-goals. Bottleneck states, often identified using ant colony optimization or proto-value functions, are frequently visited states that can guide exploration. 

Fang et al. proposed a method for generating sub-goals that are progressively closer to the main task by employing a learning progress estimator and a task generator. Uncertainty-based methods aim to position sub-goals in states with high reward uncertainty, with early work by Guestrin et al. estimating reward bounds to identify these states. Clustering techniques, such as k-means or Gaussian mixture models, are also utilized to manage exploration and avoid pitfalls like the couch potato problem.

In summary, the paper highlights two primary exploratory goal methods: meta-controllers, which enable agents to autonomously set goals but pose training challenges, and sub-goals, which rely on human-designed goals, placing a burden on designers to create meaningful objectives. The effectiveness of these goal-based methods has been demonstrated in challenging exploratory games, indicating their potential in advancing reinforcement learning strategies.
