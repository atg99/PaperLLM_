The paper discusses imitation-based methods in reinforcement learning, where agents learn from expert demonstrations to improve their behavior. These methods can be categorized into two approaches: direct learning from demonstrations or using them as a foundation for further exploration techniques. Deep Q learning from demonstration (DQfD) is highlighted for its unique pre-training on demonstrations and controlled sampling ratios between agent-run experiences and demonstrations. 

Additionally, the paper addresses the use of one or two experience replays for storing states, noting that while both approaches have similar performance outcomes, a single replay is simpler and allows for the discontinuation of imitation experiences when they are no longer beneficial.

The paper also explores the combination of imitation with exploration strategies. This method involves using demonstrations to initiate exploration, with examples such as using a single demonstration to guide the agent's random exploration or employing auxiliary rewards from demonstration videos. Notably, the approach where an agent can request assistance from a human demonstrator in unfamiliar environments is discussed.

Overall, leveraging imitation as a starting point for exploration has proven effective in challenging exploratory games, as evidenced by the results of various studies.
