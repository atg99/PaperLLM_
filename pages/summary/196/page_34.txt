This paper discusses various approaches to enhancing exploration strategies in reinforcement learning, particularly focusing on state reduction and the introduction of random noise. 

One method involves using the adaptive resonance theorem (ART), which categorizes actions into positive, negative, and empty chunks. Agents randomly select actions from positive and empty chunks, promoting exploration of rewarding actions. Wang et al. extended this approach by incorporating the probability of selecting actions based on prior knowledge.

The discussion highlights the challenge of adjusting exploration parameters based on learning progress, reward, or state value. While these methods can improve exploration efficiency, they may overlook unseen states after eliminating others. Adjusting learning rates based on exploration can prevent learning from uncertain information, albeit at the cost of slower training.

Another exploration strategy involves the introduction of random noise, either applied to network parameters or generated through state interactions. Fixed noise is a straightforward method, while chaotic networks and gradient-adaptable parameters offer more dynamic approaches. Comparing decisions made by noisy and noiseless policies helps to impose exploration when significant differences arise.

The paper also addresses the issue of credit assignment when an agent encounters the same state multiple times, proposing a random action generation function that maintains consistency in action selection for identical states.

In summary, the use of reduced states for exploration and random noise offers distinct advantages in improving exploration efficiency, with the latter showing promising results in policy-based methods, particularly in enhancing performance across various tasks, as evidenced by improvements in Atari game performance.
