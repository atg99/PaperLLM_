The paper discusses probabilistic methods used by agents to navigate uncertainty in their environment. It highlights two main approaches: optimistic behavior and uncertainty-based methods. 

In the optimistic approach, the agent leverages uncertainty to make decisions that can potentially lead to better outcomes. This involves utilizing models that provide uncertainty estimates, such as Gaussian Processes (GP) for reward estimation or linear Gaussian algorithms. Techniques like Bootstrapped Deep-Q Networks (DQN) and Thompson sampling are employed to facilitate optimistic decision-making by providing distributions over rewards and values. The agent can hold multiple value functions during exploration, selecting the most optimistic one for each episode and updating their distributions based on observed performance.

In contrast, uncertainty-based methods involve the agent maintaining a probability distribution over actions or states to represent environmental uncertainty. The agent then selects actions that aim to minimize this uncertainty. This category includes five subtypes: parameter uncertainty, value uncertainty, network ensemble, and information-theoretic approaches. Parameter uncertainty specifically focuses on the agent's uncertainty regarding the parameters of its policy, allowing it to sample from these parameters, execute the policy, and refine based on performance feedback.

Overall, the discussion emphasizes the importance of modeling rewards and value functions to effectively navigate uncertainty, especially in scenarios characterized by sparse rewards, where partial rewards can enhance learning.
