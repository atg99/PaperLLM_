The cited papers collectively explore various aspects of curiosity-driven reinforcement learning, a field that aims to enhance agent performance in navigation and exploration tasks. Dhiman et al. critically analyze deep reinforcement learning methods for navigation, while Li et al. introduce ACDER, which leverages augmented curiosity-driven experience replay to improve learning efficiency. Stanton and Clune emphasize the benefits of intra-life exploration in overcoming challenging problems. Dean et al. investigate audio-visual associations to foster curiosity, and Kolter and Ng propose a polynomial-time exploration strategy with near-Bayesian properties.

Still and Precup present an information-theoretic framework for curiosity-driven learning, advocating for interactive learning approaches. Houthooft et al. introduce VIME, focusing on maximizing exploration through variational information, while Mohamed and Rezende delve into intrinsically motivated reinforcement learning using similar principles. De Abril and Kanai propose a homeostatic regulation approach to curiosity-driven learning, and Chien and Hsu enhance exploration via stochastic curiosity maximization. Lastly, Savinov et al. explore episodic curiosity through reachability, contributing to the ongoing discourse on effective exploration strategies in reinforcement learning.
