The paper discusses the challenges associated with goal-based methods in reinforcement learning, particularly focusing on the need to store states and trajectories and navigate towards goals. It highlights that while using the agent's position as a state representation can mitigate some issues, it remains highly problem-specific. The complexity of devising exploratory goals increases with the sparsity of rewards, although some approaches attempt to address this by allowing task parameterization.

A comparison table (Table 3) is presented, detailing various goal-based methods, their input types, and performance on key benchmarks. The methods include different algorithms such as A2C, PPO, DQN, and DDPG, applied to various environments like Atari games and Mujoco tasks. Key findings include:

- Guo et al. achieved a top score of 6600 in Montezuma's Revenge using A2C and CoEX.
- The work by Florensa et al. reached a score of 0.55 in Mujoco's Key Insertion using TRPO.
- Matheron et al. utilized a state storage method to achieve a reward in a maze task.
- Several methods demonstrated significant improvements by leveraging access to the agent's position or employing teleportation abilities.

Overall, the paper underscores the importance of effective state representation and exploration strategies in advancing goal-based reinforcement learning approaches.
