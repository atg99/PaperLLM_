The paper discusses future challenges in exploration within reinforcement learning, highlighting issues related to evaluation, scalability, the exploration-exploitation dilemma, intrinsic rewards, noisy TV problems, safety, and transferability. 

In terms of evaluation, the lack of a common benchmark, evaluation strategy, and effective metrics complicates the comparison of various exploration algorithms. The community primarily relies on four major benchmarks: VizDoom, Minecraft, Atari Games, and Mujoco, each presenting unique complexities in state space, reward sparsity, and action space. While these benchmarks offer diverse scenarios that expose agents to different levels of difficulty, the varying complexities hinder algorithm comparisons.

Currently, most algorithms evaluate performance based on rewards accrued after a set number of steps, which can refer to episodes, iterations, or epochs. This inconsistency in testing conditions and reward reporting complicates direct comparisons between algorithms. Additionally, the method of reporting rewards—whether as average or maximum—often lacks clarity, and comparisons to average human performance are frequently ambiguous.

The paper argues that rewards may not be the most appropriate evaluation metric, as they do not account for the learning speed, which should improve with more efficient exploration. Although some attempts have been made to develop new metrics addressing these shortcomings, they have not gained widespread acceptance. The challenge is further exacerbated in continuous action space problems, where assessing novelty is significantly more complex.
