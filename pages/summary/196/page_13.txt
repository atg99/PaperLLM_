Memory methods focus on intrinsic rewards based on the distinctiveness of states, where more easily distinguishable states are considered more novel. Various strategies have been developed to manage the computational demands of state comparison, including exemplar models that train classifiers for specific states and memory-based approaches that utilize experience replay combined with prediction errors. While exemplar models can be resource-intensive, they can be optimized by updating a single exemplar or sampling from a buffer. The integration of intrinsic episodic experience replay and intrinsic motivation methods, such as Random Network Distillation (RND), enables the agent to evaluate long-term novelty effectively. Overall, memory-based methods offer a trade-off between memory efficiency and computational complexity, requiring careful consideration of model parameters and memory management strategies.
