The review discusses various techniques for exploration, beginning with random exploration, specifically the epsilon-greedy method, which reduces exploration over time. While this method has shown success in certain environments like Atari games and autonomous systems, it is often impractical due to extended learning times. 

Another technique mentioned is reward shaping, where designers provide more frequent rewards to guide the agent's learning. However, this approach poses risks, such as the agent becoming stagnant due to excessive punishment or repeating actions indefinitely due to over-rewarding. 

The paper highlights the ongoing development of exploration strategies since their inception in 1991, noting significant advancements in performance through innovative methods. Notable techniques include Diversity is all you need (DIYAN), random network distillation (RND), and Agent57, which have achieved remarkable results in various challenging environments.

The review emphasizes exploratory approaches that adaptively determine the level of exploration based on the agent's learning, actively seek new outcomes, and maintain motivation to explore in the absence of rewards. These criteria guide the selection of methods discussed in the paper, underscoring the need for sophisticated exploration algorithms to effectively tackle sparse reward problems in reinforcement learning.
