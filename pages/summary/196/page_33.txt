In section 3.7, the paper discusses random-based methods aimed at improving the efficiency of simple random exploration, which often leads to inefficiencies due to state revisits. Three primary approaches are highlighted: reducing states/actions for exploration, modifying exploration parameters, and introducing network parameter noise.

The exploration parameter methods are elaborated upon, where exploration is parameterized (e.g., epsilon-greedy) and adjusted according to the agent's learning progress. One technique involves adapting the exploration rate based on the received rewards, although using pure rewards can create challenges, particularly with sparse rewards. To address this, some methods propose linking the exploration parameter to the error of value-function estimates or using environmental model entropy. Additionally, the learning rate can also be influenced by exploration, with a parameter introduced to slow learning during high exploration periods. Khamassi et al. proposed using averages of long-term and short-term rewards to modulate exploration and exploitation, suggesting increased exploration when short-term averages fall below long-term averages. Chang et al. introduced a multi-agent system where agents (ants) choose actions randomly, influenced by pheromone trails left by others, to further adjust exploration parameters.
