The paper discusses the challenges of exploration in reinforcement learning, focusing on two specific problems: noisy-TV and sparse reward scenarios. In the noisy-TV problem, the agent is continuously exposed to new, meaningless states that do not yield any rewards, which can be likened to a couch potato scenario where the agent is engaged but makes no progress. In contrast, sparse reward problems involve situations where rewards are infrequent, making it difficult for the agent to learn effective behaviors due to long gaps between actions and rewards. For instance, in a maze, the agent may take numerous steps before receiving any reward, which can hinder its learning process.

The paper also introduces commonly used benchmarks in reinforcement learning, specifically highlighting four: Atari Games, VizDoom, Minecraft, and Mujoco. The Atari Games benchmark consists of 57 games categorized into easy and difficult exploration problems, where easy problems provide more accessible rewards, while difficult ones present complex relationships between states and rewards. This section sets the stage for understanding the varying degrees of exploration difficulties faced in reinforcement learning environments.
