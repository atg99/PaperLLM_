This paper reviews various exploration methods in reinforcement learning, highlighting key approaches. 

1. Reward Novel States: Agents receive rewards for discovering new or surprising states, calculated through prediction error, count, or memory-based methods. Prediction error assesses the accuracy of the agent’s environmental model, count-based methods reward frequent visits to states, and memory-based methods evaluate state uniqueness against others in a buffer.

2. Reward Diverse Behaviours: Agents are incentivized to explore diverse behaviours, categorized into evolutionary strategies, which promote diversity among agent populations, and policy learning, which encourages diverse policy parameters.

3. Goal-Based Methods: These methods involve setting exploration goals. Agents may explore from a chosen goal, leading to efficient exploration of unknown areas, or explore while progressing toward a goal, where exploration-friendly goals are emphasized.

4. Uncertainty Methods: Agents maintain an uncertainty model about their environment, using optimistic and uncertainty sampling techniques. Optimistic methods focus on the most promising rewards, while uncertainty methods target the least explored areas.

5. Imitation-Based Methods: Exploration is enhanced through demonstrations, either by merging demonstration samples with agent experiences or using demonstrations to inform exploration strategies.

6. Safe Exploration: This approach ensures agents behave safely during exploration by establishing boundaries based on human knowledge, predicting harmful actions to prevent them, and applying negative rewards to deter visits to dangerous states.

Overall, the paper underscores the diverse strategies and methodologies aimed at improving exploration in reinforcement learning, each contributing uniquely to the agent's ability to navigate and learn from its environment.
