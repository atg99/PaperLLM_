In reinforcement learning, Q-learning is a prominent off-policy method that updates a target policy using samples from any stochastic behavior policy. The Q-learning algorithm employs the Bellman equation and temporal difference (TD) learning for action-value function updates, represented by the equation Q(st, at) = Q(st, at) + α [rt + max a'∈A Q(st+1, a') - Q(st, at)], where α is the learning rate. Actions are selected based on an ε-greedy exploration strategy, allowing for both exploitation of known information and exploration of new actions.

In contrast, policy-based methods directly optimize the policy parameters, aiming to maximize expected returns across states. The REINFORCE algorithm, introduced by Williams et al., updates the policy network using gradient ascent on the objective function J(θ) = E[Gt]. The gradient is calculated as rθJ(θ) = E[∇θ log πθ(a|s)Gt], where πθ is the policy distribution over actions.

Exploration in reinforcement learning is crucial for maximizing the reward function, especially when the reward structure is complex. Various exploration strategies exist, including random actions, following known solutions, or actively seeking new possibilities. Exploration is particularly beneficial in scenarios where local optima obscure the path to the global solution, making it essential for agents to discover true rewards. Examples include search and rescue operations and delivery tasks in unknown environments, where rewards are contingent on successful outcomes.
