The discussion starts with reward novel state methods, which incentivize agents to discover new states by providing additional intrinsic rewards alongside extrinsic rewards from the environment.

Reward novel state approaches are designed to encourage exploration by granting intrinsic rewards (rint) for novelty, as shown in Figure 2. These methods rely on two main components: an intrinsic reward module that predicts or compresses data to generate rewards, and a reinforcement learner that adapts behaviors based on these rewards. 

The intrinsic rewards can be classified into three main categories, following the classification in previous studies: prediction error methods, count-based methods, and memory methods. Prediction error methods specifically utilize the prediction errors from a model to calculate intrinsic rewards, with higher errors indicating less frequent visits to a state, thus generating a higher intrinsic reward. The paper highlights the need to explore different mechanisms for leveraging prediction errors to compute intrinsic rewards, referencing a comparison of intrinsic reward functions by Achiam et al. 

Overall, the paper aims to provide clarity on how various approaches to intrinsic rewards can enhance agent behaviors through exploration, ultimately contributing to the broader understanding of reinforcement learning in dynamic environments.
