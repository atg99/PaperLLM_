The section discusses the critical role of sample selection in deep metric learning, which comprises informative input samples, the network model structure, and a metric loss function. Informative samples significantly enhance the classification or clustering success of deep metric learning. The simplest sampling method involves randomly selecting positive or negative pairs, but this approach may slow down learning after achieving acceptable performance levels. To improve efficiency, hard negative mining has been adopted, which focuses on more discriminative samples. 

Triplet networks use an anchor, a positive, and a negative sample for training, but easy triplets often lack discriminative power and waste resources. Therefore, selecting informative sample triplets is recommended to enhance model training strategies. The text also introduces concepts like hard negative samples, semi-hard negative mining, and distance-weighted sampling to mitigate issues with noisy samples and improve the model's learning ability. Ultimately, the effectiveness of the network's learning is contingent on the discriminative quality of the presented samples, emphasizing the need for carefully chosen training examples for optimal representation.
