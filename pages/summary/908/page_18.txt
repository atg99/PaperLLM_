The table provides an overview of various loss metrics utilized in machine learning, particularly focusing on their application in patient similarity, image retrieval, and person re-identification. Each metric is associated with specific sampling strategies and datasets, highlighting their intended purposes and the years they were introduced. 

Key metrics include Quadruple Loss, which effectively captures patient similarity in datasets related to ischemic heart and cerebrovascular conditions, introduced in 2017. Clustering Loss, also from 2017, employs a structural prediction approach for image clustering, incorporating a clustering quality metric. Hierarchical Triplet Loss, introduced in 2018, focuses on image retrieval and face recognition by utilizing an online class-level tree update for sample collection. 

Mixed Loss, from 2018, enhances image retrieval by integrating multiple positive and negative samples. Part Loss, introduced in 2019, aims to minimize classification risks in person re-identification by segmenting images into parts. Lastly, Multi-Similarity Loss, also from 2019, emphasizes the weighting of informative paired samples within image retrieval tasks. Overall, the metrics reflect advancements in the effectiveness of sample selection and learning strategies in various domains.
