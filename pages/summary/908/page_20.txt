The paper discusses key aspects of deep metric learning, emphasizing the importance of optimizing various elements within neural networks, such as margin values, sampling strategies, layers, parameters, and shared weights. The authors highlight that shared weights enhance the organization of in-class and inter-class relationships while promoting efficient memory usage and reducing training time. Additionally, the choice of metric loss function is critical for network performance, with existing literature focusing on distance metrics to achieve higher discriminative features in embedding space. The authors note a gap in research regarding the combination of metric loss functions and the relationships between pair samples, suggesting this as a valuable area for future exploration. While interest in deep metric learning is growing, the authors point out that current studies are limited in scope and that there are numerous unexplored aspects, including the limitations of existing methods and the flexibility of utilizing local and global features.
