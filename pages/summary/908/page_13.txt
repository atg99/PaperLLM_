The paper discusses the importance of sample selection in deep metric learning, emphasizing that careful selection can enhance model performance and prevent overfitting. It notes that negative mining studies significantly impact the effectiveness of deep metric learning, as they help mitigate issues like slower learning and local optimization by focusing on informative samples rather than all possible triplets, which can be computationally expensive.

The paper also outlines various loss functions utilized in deep metric learning, highlighting their role in adjusting the distance between objects based on their similarity. The Siamese network, initially applied for signature verification, is presented as a key model that learns from pairs of images, determining their class similarity through a binary output. The use of Contrastive Loss with the Siamese network is noted for its effectiveness in maximizing or minimizing distances between objects, thereby enhancing classification performance.

Additionally, the paper mentions the integration of Siamese networks with Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), which allows for simultaneous learning from image pixels, color, and texture. A specific application in person re-identification is discussed, where a combination of Siamese CNNs and the Mahalanobis metric is employed. The paper concludes by presenting the combination of softmax loss and center loss for face recognition, where center loss helps minimize distances between class features while softmax loss maximizes distances between different classes.
