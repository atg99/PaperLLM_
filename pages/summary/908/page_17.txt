The paper presents a comprehensive overview of various loss metrics used in image recognition and related tasks. It categorizes these metrics based on their sample selection strategies and applications, detailing their objectives and the datasets they utilize.

1. **Contrastive Loss** (2006) targets image recognition by maximizing the distance between dissimilar object pairs while minimizing it for similar pairs, applied to datasets like MNIST and NORB.
2. **Triplet Loss** (2014) employs easy sampling to measure the distance between anchor-positive and anchor-negative samples, aiming to bring similar objects closer together, with applications in MNIST, CIFAR10, and others.
3. **Histogram Loss** (2016) focuses on image retrieval and person re-identification, adjusting the distributions of similarities between positive and negative pairs in datasets such as CUB-200-2011 and Market-1501.
4. **Structured Loss** (2016) introduces a metric learning algorithm that utilizes a lifted dense pairwise distance matrix, enhancing training efficiency across various datasets.
5. **N-Pair Loss** (2016) innovates on triplet loss by considering multiple negative samples, pushing positive samples away from these negatives in a range of applications including face verification.
6. **Magnet Loss** (2016) emphasizes image annotation by retrieving local neighborhoods of nearest clusters and managing overlaps, tested on datasets like Stanford Dogs.
7. **Angular Loss** (2017) restricts the angles within negative samples of triplet triangles, contributing to improved performance in image retrieval and clustering tasks.

Overall, the paper highlights the evolution and diversification of loss metrics in machine learning, particularly in enhancing the accuracy of image-related tasks.
