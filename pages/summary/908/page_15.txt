The paper discusses various loss functions used in deep metric learning, focusing on how they enhance the performance of models in tasks such as person re-identification. The authors highlight the hierarchical triplet loss, which employs a class-level hierarchical tree for improved sample informativeness and online adaptive updates. They also introduce angular loss, which emphasizes angular relationships between samples, pushing negative samples away from positive clusters while maintaining scale invariance.

Additionally, the paper presents quadruple loss, which includes an extra sample in each training batch to establish more meaningful relationships among samples. This method, along with histogram loss, which utilizes similarity distributions without requiring tuning parameters, demonstrates superior performance on datasets like CUHK03 and Market-1501.

Part loss is also introduced, aiming to minimize risk by evaluating different body parts of images, enhancing the model’s focus on local patterns. The paper provides mathematical representations for various loss functions, including contrastive loss, triplet loss, quadruple loss, and angular loss, illustrating their calculations based on distances in the embedding space. The discussion underscores the critical role of loss functions in developing effective deep metric learning models.
