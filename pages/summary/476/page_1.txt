This paper presents an Intrusion Detection System (IDS) based on Deep Belief Networks (DBNs) to address the challenges posed by zero-day attacks in the context of the growing number of connected devices. Traditional IDSs, primarily signature-based, struggle with the rapid evolution of cyber threats and the need for timely signature generation. In contrast, behavior-based systems utilizing machine learning can effectively detect a wider range of attack variants.

The authors focus on the importance of high-quality datasets for training Deep Neural Networks (DNNs), noting that underrepresented samples can lead to poor detection performance. They employ the CICIDS2017 dataset to train and evaluate their DBN approach, applying various class balancing techniques to improve results. 

The findings indicate that the proposed DBN model offers competitive performance, particularly in detecting attacks that are underrepresented in the training dataset. The paper concludes by comparing the effectiveness of the DBN approach to a conventional Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) model and existing state-of-the-art methods, highlighting significant improvements in detection performance.
