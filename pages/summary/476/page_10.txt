The paper discusses the design and performance of a Deep Belief Network (DBN) and a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) for network intrusion detection systems (NIDS). The model parameters for both architectures are detailed in Table 2, highlighting the pre-training and fine-tuning phases for the DBN, which involves various epochs, learning rates, batch sizes, and optimization techniques. The DBN architecture consists of five stacked Restricted Boltzmann Machines (RBMs) with specified input and hidden node configurations, culminating in a fully connected layer for multi-class classification using the Softmax function. 

The results section compares the effectiveness of different class balancing techniques based on evaluation metrics such as F1-score, precision, and recall. The DBN demonstrates strong performance across several attack classes, particularly "Benign," "Brute Force," "DoS/DDoS," and "PortScan," while facing challenges with the "Web Attack" and "Botnet" classes. The analysis indicates a necessary trade-off between precision and recall, emphasizing the importance of high recall in cybersecurity to ensure detection of malicious activities, even at the cost of some false alarms. Notably, the combination of SMOTE and random undersampling yielded the best results, achieving a recall of at least 99% for all classes and the highest precision for the Botnet class.
