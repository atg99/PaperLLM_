This paper investigates the application of Deep Belief Networks (DBNs) for Network Intrusion Detection Systems (NIDS). Two models were developed, one based on DBNs and another on Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs). Experiments conducted using the CICIDS2017 dataset demonstrated that the DBN-based NIDS achieved an F1-score of 94%, significantly outperforming the MLP-based model, which attained an F1-score of 87.3%. The results indicate that DBNs are particularly effective for classifying network intrusions, especially when dealing with classes that have limited sample sizes.

The study also compared the proposed DBN-based method against various existing multi-class classification techniques, showing superior performance in terms of recall and overall classification accuracy. The research highlighted the necessity of a centralized network sampler for monitoring traffic, which poses challenges in certain scenarios due to data volume and privacy concerns. As a future direction, the authors are exploring the potential of deploying DBNs in a distributed manner to enhance their applicability in diverse network environments.
