The paper discusses an Intrusion Detection System (IDS) that utilizes Deep Belief Networks (DBNs), specifically focusing on Restricted Boltzmann Machines (RBMs) as foundational components. RBMs consist of two layers: a visible layer and a hidden layer, which capture dependencies between their units by associating an energy with each configuration of these variables. The energy function is designed to yield low values for compatible configurations and higher values for less compatible ones.

The energy function for a joint configuration of visible vector \( v \) and hidden vector \( h \) is defined mathematically. The joint probability distribution is derived from this energy function, allowing for the calculation of the visible vector's probability through marginalization of the hidden vector. 

During the training phase, RBMs employ the Contrastive Divergence (CD) algorithm to adjust weights, approximating the gradient descent's second expectation term using Gibbs sampling. The weight update rule is detailed, emphasizing the importance of the learning rate and the differences between data and reconstructed expectations.

Overall, the paper outlines the theoretical underpinnings of RBMs within the context of IDS, highlighting their role in capturing and modeling complex dependencies for effective intrusion detection.
