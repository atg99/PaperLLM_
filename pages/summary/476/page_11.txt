This paper presents an Intrusion Detection System (IDS) utilizing Deep Belief Networks (DBN) for multi-class classification of network traffic. The study evaluates the performance of the proposed DBN model against a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) and other state-of-the-art intrusion detection systems using the CICIDS2017 dataset. Key metrics such as F1-Score, precision, and recall are analyzed for various balancing techniques, including SMOTE and RandomUnderSampler.

Results indicate that the DBN model significantly outperforms the MLP, particularly in classifying "Web Attacks," achieving a precision of 83% compared to the MLP's 49%. This enhanced performance is attributed to the DBN's two-stage training process, which enables it to learn more meaningful patterns associated with different types of attacks.

The comparison with other leading methods, such as DeepGFL, LSTM, and CNN-based approaches, reveals that the proposed DBN model also surpasses these models in recall and F1-Score, demonstrating its effectiveness in multi-class classification tasks. Overall, the findings suggest that the DBN-based IDS is a robust solution for detecting various types of network intrusions.
