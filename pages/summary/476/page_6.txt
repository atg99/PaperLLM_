The paper discusses the use of Deep Belief Networks (DBNs) for network intrusion detection systems (NIDS), particularly focusing on addressing the challenge of class imbalance in network traffic datasets. The architecture involves several stages including data cleaning, class imbalance handling through SMOTE and RandomUnderSampler, data standardization using Quantile Transformer, and dimensionality reduction via PCA.

DBNs, composed of multiple stacks of Restricted Boltzmann Machines (RBMs), perform non-linear transformations on input data, with each layer serving as both a hidden layer and an input layer for the subsequent layer. They can be utilized for both unsupervised tasks, like image generation, and supervised tasks such as classification, with training achieved through layer-wise pre-training followed by fine-tuning via back-propagation.

The methodology section outlines the process of preparing the dataset, which is divided into training, validation, and testing sets to ensure effective model training and performance evaluation. A stratiﬁed data split is emphasized to maintain the distribution of classes, especially important in datasets like NSL-KDD and CICIDS2017, which exhibit significant class imbalance, with benign samples making up 90-95% of the total. The paper aims to demonstrate the effectiveness of DBNs in improving detection rates in the context of this imbalance.
