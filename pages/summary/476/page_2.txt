This paper presents a multi-class classification Network Intrusion Detection System (NIDS) utilizing Deep Belief Networks (DBNs) to enhance the detection of malicious behavior based on labeled datasets. The authors highlight the significance of dataset quality in the performance of Intrusion Detection Systems (IDS), noting that underrepresentation of certain attack types can lead to poor detection rates for those variants. To address the common issue of imbalanced datasets, the authors propose a methodology that incorporates data sampling and class balancing techniques.

The key contributions of the paper include:

1. Demonstrating that the DBN-based NIDS outperforms Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP)-based systems, particularly in scenarios with limited attack samples.
2. Conducting experiments on the imbalanced CICIDS2017 dataset, which consists of benign traffic and twelve attack types, showing that their class balancing approach significantly enhances detection performance, as measured by F1-score.
3. Comparing the classification results of their NIDS with state-of-the-art methods, achieving a notable improvement in F1-score from 0.873 to 0.94 after applying their class balancing technique.

The paper is structured as follows: Section 2 discusses related work on IDSs and DBNs. Section 3 details the proposed methodology for the DBN-based NIDS. Section 4 analyzes the preprocessing steps taken on the CICIDS2017 dataset, the model architecture employed, and presents performance results in comparison to existing methods. Finally, Section 5 concludes the paper and suggests directions for future research.
