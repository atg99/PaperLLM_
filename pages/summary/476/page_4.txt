This paper investigates the use of Restricted Boltzmann Machines (RBMs) and Deep Belief Networks (DBNs) for network intrusion detection systems (NIDS). The authors highlight the effectiveness of a CNN-based IDS in recognizing DDoS attacks with an impressive accuracy of 99.7% on the CICIDS2017 dataset. They reference the Whisper approach, which utilizes frequency domain analysis for real-time detection of various sophisticated attacks, achieving at least 90% accuracy on the WIDE MAWI dataset, suggesting potential future exploration of this method.

Previous research utilizing RBMs for feature extraction followed by SVM classification has demonstrated reduced training times while maintaining classifier performance. Additionally, a deep learning NIDS employing RBMs with the KDD CUP'99 dataset showcased significant accuracy improvements compared to traditional machine learning methods. The authors also note a study where a DBN-based IDS reached approximately 97.5% accuracy using only 40% of the dataset for training, although this evaluation focused solely on accuracy without considering other metrics like recall and precision.

Motivated by the effectiveness of RBMs in enhancing classifier performance and the limited exploration of DBNs in NIDS, as well as the challenges posed by imbalanced cybersecurity datasets, this research aims to provide a more comprehensive analysis and application of these models in intrusion detection. The paper emphasizes the potential of RBMs and DBNs in improving the accuracy and efficiency of cybersecurity measures.
