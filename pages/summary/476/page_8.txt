The paper presents a detailed analysis of the CICIDS2017 dataset, which is used for training a Deep Belief Network (DBN) model aimed at network intrusion detection. The dataset is categorized into various labels, including benign, DoS/DDoS attacks, port scanning, brute force attacks, web attacks, and botnet activities, with a total of 1,807,787 benign samples and significant numbers of attack samples.

The model training process involves two main phases: unsupervised pre-training and supervised fine-tuning. During the pre-training phase, each Restricted Boltzmann Machine (RBM) in the DBN is trained to reconstruct its input, a process that continues until all layers are pre-trained. The fine-tuning phase optimizes the weights using stochastic gradient descent and back-propagation. Given the high class imbalance in intrusion datasets, traditional accuracy metrics are deemed unreliable; thus, the paper emphasizes the use of F1-score, precision, and recall for model evaluation.

Experiments were conducted using a 64-bit Intel Core i7 processor with 16GB RAM and implemented in Python using the PyTorch library. The CICIDS2017 dataset, created by the Canadian Institute for Cyber-security, contains samples of benign activities and attacks over a five-day period, with benign traffic generated to simulate realistic user interactions based on standard network protocols.
