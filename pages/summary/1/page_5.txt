The paper discusses the evolution of feature engineering and model building in machine learning (ML) and deep learning (DL). Traditional handcrafted feature design is labor-intensive and requires domain expertise, making it time-consuming and inflexible. In contrast, deep neural networks facilitate automated feature learning, allowing for the extraction of discriminative representations with minimal human effort. This capability enables DL to effectively handle large-scale, noisy, and unstructured data through a hierarchical learning process.

During model building, learning algorithms identify patterns relevant to the task at hand. Shallow ML relies on well-designed features, employing various mechanisms like decision trees and support vector machines (SVMs) to build models. Each algorithm has unique advantages and disadvantages based on the input data and features. However, DL can operate directly on high-dimensional raw data, integrating feature learning and model building into an end-to-end system. DL architectures, such as convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and recurrent neural networks (RNNs), are tailored for specific data types and applications, particularly in electronic markets.

Model assessment involves evaluating performance, computational resources, and interpretability. Performance metrics, such as k-fold cross-validation, help prevent overfitting and assess a model's effectiveness on unseen data. Regression models are typically evaluated using estimation errors, including root mean square error (RMSE) and mean absolute percentage error (MAPE). This comprehensive approach to model building and assessment underscores the advancements and flexibility offered by deep learning in comparison to traditional machine learning methods.
