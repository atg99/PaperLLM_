This paper discusses the advancements in machine learning (ML) and deep learning (DL), particularly in the context of electronic markets. It highlights the evolution of artificial neural networks (ANNs) into deeper architectures that enhance learning capabilities, resulting in superhuman performance for specific applications. Despite these advancements, the implementation of analytical models in real business settings presents challenges, including data bias, model transparency, and the selection of appropriate implementation strategies.

The authors aim to provide a foundational understanding of ML and DL, emphasizing the need for scholars and professionals to grasp the underlying concepts and challenges associated with these technologies. The article is structured to first clarify key terms related to artificial intelligence (AI), ML, ANNs, and DL, followed by a discussion on automated analytical model building and the environmental factors affecting the implementation of intelligent systems.

AI is defined broadly as any technique enabling computers to mimic human behavior and decision-making. ML is presented as a subset of AI that improves performance through experience, allowing for the automation of analytical model building. The paper categorizes ML into supervised, unsupervised, and reinforcement learning, noting their applications in various areas such as fraud detection, customer analysis, and market forecasting.

In summary, the article seeks to equip the community with essential knowledge to leverage technological advancements in ML and DL for analyzing high-dimensional data and designing intelligent systems within electronic markets. The subsequent sections will delve into the specifics of model building and the challenges faced during implementation.
