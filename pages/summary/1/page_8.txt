The paper discusses the challenges and considerations in applying machine learning (ML) models to intelligent systems, particularly in dynamic environments such as electronic markets. It highlights the issue of concept drift, where historical data becomes less relevant due to market changes, making it difficult for models to maintain accuracy. Techniques exist for managing drift, but automated solutions for addressing business-related problems remain scarce.

Bias in data is a critical concern, particularly due to human influence in data collection. The paper emphasizes the need for researchers to acknowledge and disclose any biases in their datasets, as these can affect model predictions. It is unrealistic to eliminate all biases, but understanding their impact is essential for building trust in ML models. Continuous monitoring and retraining of models are necessary to adapt to ongoing changes in the market.

The unpredictability of predictions from complex deep learning (DL) models poses another challenge, as their black-box nature makes it difficult for users to understand their decision-making processes. This lack of explainability can hinder organizational adoption and may even conflict with legal requirements. The field of explainable AI (XAI) is proposed as a solution to enhance transparency, allowing users to comprehend the factors influencing model outputs, particularly in critical applications.

Resource constraints are also addressed, as developing robust analytical models can be costly and data-intensive. Transfer learning is presented as a viable strategy, allowing models trained on general datasets to be adapted for specific tasks with smaller, targeted datasets. However, caution is advised when using pre-trained models due to potential biases and vulnerabilities.

The emergence of AI as a service (AIaaS) platforms is noted as a means to facilitate access to AI technologies for businesses, offering both cloud-based solutions and opportunities for transfer learning. Overall, the paper underscores the importance of addressing bias, ensuring explainability, and leveraging innovative approaches to effectively implement intelligent systems in today’s complex environments.
