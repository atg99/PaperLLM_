This collection of references encompasses a range of studies and reports that explore various aspects of artificial intelligence, machine learning, and their applications. Key contributions include Spooner et al.'s work on market making through reinforcement learning, which highlights innovative approaches in financial markets. Stone et al. provide a comprehensive overview of the implications of artificial intelligence for society in their 2016 report from Stanford University, projecting future developments and challenges.

Viola and Jones present a seminal method for rapid object detection using a boosted cascade of features, which has influenced computer vision significantly. Wang et al. address security concerns with their study on backdoor attacks in transfer learning, emphasizing vulnerabilities in deep learning models. Wanner et al. investigate decision factors influencing AI technology adoption, shedding light on organizational considerations.

Westerlund reviews the emergence of Deepfake technology, discussing its societal impacts. Widmer and Kubat focus on learning in dynamic environments, particularly in the presence of concept drift. Wu et al. evaluate the effectiveness of handcrafted features in sequence labeling tasks, contributing to natural language processing methodologies. Young et al. summarize recent advancements in deep learning applications for natural language processing, while Zhang and Ling propose strategies for applying machine learning to small datasets in materials science.

Overall, these works collectively advance the understanding of AI and machine learning, addressing both theoretical foundations and practical applications across diverse fields.
