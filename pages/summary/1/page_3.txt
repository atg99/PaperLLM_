The paper discusses various classes of machine learning (ML) algorithms, emphasizing the family of artificial neural networks (ANNs) due to their adaptability across different ML contexts. ANNs mimic biological information processing through interconnected artificial neurons, where each connection's strength is modifiable by weights during learning. The structure typically includes an input layer for data reception, an output layer for results, and hidden layers that facilitate non-linear mappings between input and output. Hyperparameters, such as the number of layers and neurons, must be manually set or optimized.

Deep neural networks (DNNs) extend this concept with multiple hidden layers and advanced operations, enabling them to process raw data and automatically learn representations. The paper categorizes ML into three types: supervised learning, which uses labeled datasets to predict outcomes; unsupervised learning, which identifies patterns in unlabeled data; and reinforcement learning, where models learn through trial and error to achieve specified goals. Each type serves distinct purposes, such as predicting user behavior, clustering customer segments, or optimizing actions in dynamic environments.
