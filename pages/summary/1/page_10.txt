This collection of references spans various aspects of machine learning, deep learning, and artificial intelligence, highlighting both theoretical frameworks and practical applications. 

Fuchs discusses the dangers of human-like bias in machine learning algorithms, emphasizing the need for awareness and mitigation strategies. Gama et al. provide a comprehensive survey on adapting to concept drift, which is crucial for maintaining model accuracy over time. García and Herrera extend statistical comparisons of classifiers, enhancing evaluation methods across multiple datasets.

Goodfellow et al. offer foundational knowledge in deep learning, while Goyal and Pabla review condition-based maintenance of machine tools, illustrating the intersection of AI and manufacturing. Grigorescu et al. present a survey on deep learning techniques specifically for autonomous driving, showcasing the technology's advancements in real-world applications.

Haselton et al. explore the evolution of cognitive bias, providing insights relevant to AI systems. Heinrich et al. contribute to AI security discussions with a taxonomy of attack and defense patterns. They also reflect on the evolution of deep learning architectures and introduce novel neural network models for next-event prediction.

Howard et al. address bias in algorithms through a pilot study on emotion recognition, highlighting the importance of fairness in AI. Jayanth Balaji et al. empirically assess deep learning models for stock price forecasting, demonstrating practical financial applications.

Jordan and Mitchell summarize trends in machine learning, while Kotsiantis et al. review classification techniques. Kühl et al. discuss AI's role in customer-oriented marketing, emphasizing the importance of understanding customer needs through social media analysis.

LeCun et al. and Liu et al. provide insights into deep learning and representation learning, respectively. Lowe introduces distinctive image features for computer vision, and Madani et al. apply deep learning in medical imaging for echocardiograms.

Miller emphasizes the need for explainability in AI, advocating for insights from social sciences. Pan et al. survey advancements in generative adversarial networks, while Paula et al. explore deep learning for fraud detection in exports.

Pentland et al. investigate the dynamics of drift in digitized processes, and Peters et al. propose reinforcement learning for decision-making in smart electricity markets. Pouyanfar et al. survey deep learning algorithms and applications, while Ramaswamy and DeClerck analyze customer perception using NLP.

Rudin argues for the use of interpretable models in high-stakes decisions, and Russell and Norvig provide a modern approach to artificial intelligence education. Salton and Buckley discuss term-weighting in text retrieval, and Schmidhuber offers an overview of deep learning in neural networks.

Searle's philosophical insights on minds and programs add depth to the discussion of AI's capabilities. Selz reflects on the transition from electronic markets to data-driven insights, and Shmueli and Koppius focus on predictive analytics in information systems research.

Lastly, Shrestha et al. highlight the potential of deep learning algorithms in enhancing organizational decision-making, while Silver et al. present a general reinforcement learning algorithm capable of mastering complex games, showcasing the versatility and power of AI technologies. 

Overall, these references collectively underscore the advancements, challenges, and implications of machine learning and AI across various domains.
