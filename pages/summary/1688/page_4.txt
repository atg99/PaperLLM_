The paper discusses the challenges of using Model-Agnostic Meta-Learning (MAML) for fast adaptation to new tasks, particularly focusing on the concept of "conflict" arising from shared initializations. The authors highlight that deeper layers in neural networks tend to exhibit a greater degree of conflict due to their encoding of more task-specific features. This conflict complicates the learning process as it leads to a compromised initialization that hinders effective adaptation.

To address this, the authors propose a method to attenuate the initialization parameters based on the degree of conflict observed at different layers. They demonstrate through experiments that deeper layers benefit from stronger attenuation, while lower layers require minimal to no attenuation. The proposed approach learns layer-wise attenuation parameters, allowing for a more tailored initialization that enhances fast adaptation by minimizing the impact of irrelevant or conflicting prior knowledge.

The analysis includes metrics for measuring the degree of conflict among tasks and how this varies across layers and tasks. The findings support the hypothesis that optimizing the initialization through learned attenuation can improve the learning landscape, thereby facilitating quicker and more effective adaptation to new tasks.
