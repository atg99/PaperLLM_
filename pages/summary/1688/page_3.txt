This paper addresses the limitations of Model-Agnostic Meta-Learning (MAML) by analyzing its initialization process and proposing improvements to enhance performance while maintaining generalizability. MAML aims to provide a common initialization for fast adaptation across different tasks, but issues arise due to conflicts among tasks regarding the optimal initialization point in the optimization landscape. These conflicts can lead to a compromised initialization that hinders fast adaptation.

The authors propose a method that focuses on attenuating the compromised parts of the MAML initialization, arguing that this approach yields significant performance gains. They introduce a novel way to generate task embeddings by utilizing gradients, which provide task-specific optimization information. This method is more effective and efficient than previous approaches that required complex techniques for learning task embeddings.

The proposed method not only improves MAML's performance but also retains its simplicity and generalizability, demonstrating consistent improvements across various domains, including reinforcement learning. The paper outlines the generic meta-learning algorithm framework, detailing how tasks are sampled and how the model adapts to new tasks through inner-loop updates. Overall, the authors present a compelling argument for their approach, highlighting the importance of addressing initialization conflicts in meta-learning frameworks.
