The paper "Learning to Forget for Meta-Learning" by Sungyong Baik, Seokil Hong, and Kyoung Mu Lee addresses the challenges of few-shot learning, where models must generalize from very few examples. The authors critique model-agnostic meta-learning (MAML) for its approach of sharing a common initialization across tasks, which can lead to conflicts that hinder task adaptation.

To mitigate these conflicts, the authors propose a method called L2F (Learn to Forget), which employs task-and-layer-wise attenuation of the compromised initialization. This method dynamically controls the influence of prior knowledge for each task and layer, allowing for more effective adaptation. The results indicate that L2F not only accelerates adaptation but also enhances performance significantly. Additionally, L2F can be integrated with existing MAML-based frameworks, demonstrating its versatility and ease of application.

The paper highlights the importance of managing the initialization conflicts in meta-learning, suggesting that individual tasks may have varying degrees of conflict that can be addressed through selective attenuation, leading to improved generalization in few-shot learning scenarios.
