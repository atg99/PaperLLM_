The paper discusses the optimization landscape in meta-learning, specifically analyzing the stability and smoothness of the loss landscape during fast adaptation tasks using MAML (Model-Agnostic Meta-Learning) and a proposed method called L2F (Learn to Forget). The authors measure various aspects of the loss landscape, including variations in loss and gradients, to understand the learning dynamics during training on miniImageNet classification tasks.

The findings reveal that MAML's learned initialization can lead to a sharp loss landscape, which negatively impacts generalization to new examples. The authors hypothesize that conflicts arise at different layers of the neural network, particularly in deeper layers that contain more task-specific knowledge. They observe that the severity of these conflicts varies by layer and task, which motivates their approach to learn selective forgetting.

L2F introduces a task-and-layer-wise attenuation mechanism to control the influence of prior knowledge during adaptation. This method enhances the quality of initialization, resulting in a smoother loss landscape and improved performance across various domains while maintaining MAML's simplicity and generalizability.

The paper also situates its contributions within the broader context of meta-learning, categorizing existing systems into metric-based, network-based, and optimization-based approaches, with a focus on how L2F addresses the limitations of MAML in few-shot learning scenarios.
