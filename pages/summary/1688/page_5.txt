The paper presents a meta-learning algorithm aimed at improving task adaptation through a proposed method that incorporates task-dependent attenuation parameters. The algorithm begins with randomly initializing network parameters and iteratively samples tasks from a distribution. For each task, it computes a loss based on training data, adjusts layer-wise attenuation parameters using gradients, and updates the network initialization accordingly. This process enhances the network's adaptability to specific tasks by leveraging task-specific gradient information.

Experiments demonstrate the method's effectiveness in few-shot classification scenarios using datasets like miniImageNet and tieredImageNet. The results indicate that the proposed approach outperforms several existing meta-learning techniques, achieving higher accuracy rates across various few-shot learning settings. The findings suggest that the task-dependent attenuation mechanism significantly contributes to improved performance in meta-learning tasks.
