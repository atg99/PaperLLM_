The paper discusses the limitations of the Model-Agnostic Meta-Learning (MAML) framework, particularly the conflicts that arise from sharing a common initialization across tasks. These conflicts can lead to a suboptimal starting point for fast adaptation, as evidenced by a sharp loss landscape. The authors propose a solution called Layer-wise Task-dependent Forgetting (L2F), which aims to attenuate irrelevant information that may hinder quick adaptation. L2F allows for selective forgetting based on task-specific needs, improving the adaptability of the model without sacrificing MAML's simplicity and generalizability.

Experimental results demonstrate that MAML combined with L2F outperforms standard MAML in various scenarios, including few-shot regression and reinforcement learning tasks. The findings indicate that L2F leads to a smoother loss landscape over time, enhancing the model's ability to adapt quickly to new tasks. The paper concludes that addressing the conflicts between tasks through selective forgetting significantly enhances fast adaptation capabilities in meta-learning frameworks.
