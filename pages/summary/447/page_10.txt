The section on skin cancer segmentation discusses the use of a dataset from the 2016 Kaggle skin lesion segmentation competition, which consists of 900 training images and 379 testing images, all originally sized at 700x900 pixels and rescaled to 128x128 pixels for this study. The training set includes original images and corresponding binary target images indicating cancerous lesions.

In the experimental results, the dataset underwent preprocessing through mean subtraction and normalization. The ADAM optimization technique was employed with a learning rate of 2x10^-4 and binary cross-entropy loss for training. A validation set constituted 10% of the training samples, with a batch size of 32 over 150 epochs. The proposed R2U-Net and RU-Net models outperformed ResU-Net and U-Net models, demonstrating superior accuracy during training and validation.

Quantitative results showed that the R2U-Net model achieved a testing accuracy of 0.9472 and an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.9430, with Jaccard index (JA) and Dice coefficient (DC) values of 0.9278 and 0.9627, respectively. Although the R2U-Net ranked third in accuracy compared to ISIC-2016 and FCRN-506, it excelled in DC and JA metrics. The architecture of R2U-Net consisted of 34 layers and approximately 13.34 million parameters, outperforming FCRN-38 networks.

The performance of RU-Net and R2U-Net was also compared to VGG-16 and Inception-V3 models, which had significantly more parameters, demonstrating higher accuracy and AUC for the proposed models. Overall, the results indicate that RU-Net and R2U-Net models provide enhanced performance over traditional segmentation models such as SegNet, U-Net, and ResU-Net for skin cancer lesion segmentation tasks.
