The paper presents a novel approach to medical image segmentation through the development of two recurrent U-Net models: RU-Net and R2U-Net. These models leverage the strengths of U-Net, residual networks, and recurrent convolutional neural networks to enhance segmentation performance. Key advantages of the proposed architectures include improved training of deep networks through residual units, better feature representation due to recurrent residual layers, and the ability to achieve superior performance without increasing the number of network parameters.

The models were evaluated on three benchmark datasets focusing on blood vessel segmentation in retinal images, skin cancer segmentation, and lung lesion segmentation. Results demonstrate that RU-Net and R2U-Net outperform existing models such as SegNet, U-Net, and residual U-Net in segmentation tasks. The paper emphasizes the potential of deep learning techniques in medical imaging, addressing challenges like data scarcity and class imbalance, and highlights the importance of efficient algorithms for rapid and accurate segmentation, which is crucial for computer-aided diagnosis.
