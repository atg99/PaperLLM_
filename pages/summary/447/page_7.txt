This paper discusses the implementation of a recurrent residual U-Net for segmenting two-dimensional images, specifically focusing on retinal blood vessel segmentation using Keras and TensorFlow on a machine equipped with an NVIDIA GEFORCE GTX-980 Ti GPU. It outlines a range of evaluation metrics for assessing performance, including accuracy (AC), sensitivity (SE), specificity (SP), F1-score, Dice coefficient (DC), and Jaccard index (JA). The paper presents equations for calculating these metrics based on true positive (TP), true negative (TN), false positive (FP), and false negative (FN) values.

The experimental results are derived from three well-known datasets: DRIVE, STARE, and CHASE_DB1. The DRIVE dataset consists of 40 color retina images, split evenly for training and testing, with images cropped to a uniform size of 565x565 pixels. A total of 190,000 patches of 48x48 pixels are randomly selected for training and validation. The STARE dataset includes 20 color images of 700x605 pixels each, chosen due to its limited sample size. The paper provides examples of training datasets, illustrating original images alongside their field of views (FOVs) and target outputs.

Additionally, the study includes an analysis of the Dice index loss function and the Jaccard similarity score, highlighting the importance of these metrics in evaluating segmentation performance. The area under the curve (AUC) and receiver operating characteristics (ROC) curve are also mentioned as common measures in medical image segmentation tasks, with results compared to existing state-of-the-art methods.
