This study focuses on the importance of lung segmentation (LS) for diagnosing lung-related diseases, including lung cancer segmentation and lung pattern classification. The experimental setup involved using 80% of images for training and 20% for testing, with images resized from 512×512 to 256×256 pixels. The ADAM optimizer was implemented with a learning rate of 2×10^-4, and a DI loss function was applied. Validation utilized 10% of samples, with a batch size of 16 over 150 epochs.

The results indicated that the proposed R2U-Net model outperformed traditional models, showing a 0.26% and 0.55% improvement in testing accuracy compared to SegNet and U-Net, respectively, and a 0.18% improvement over ResU-Net, despite having the same number of parameters. Qualitative assessments demonstrated that R2U-Net produced segmentation results that closely matched ground truth, highlighting its effectiveness in preserving internal details. Visual comparisons in the study illustrated the superior performance of R2U-Net over SegNet and U-Net in segmenting lung images.
