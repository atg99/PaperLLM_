The paper discusses the training and testing approaches used with the STARE and CHASE_DB1 datasets for retinal blood vessel segmentation. Two primary methods are highlighted: random sampling from all images and the "leave-one-out" method, which avoids overlap between training and testing samples. The authors employed the "leave-one-out" approach for STARE and a random division for CHASE_DB1, utilizing a total of 250,000 patches from STARE and CHASE_DB1 images for training and validation.

The study emphasizes the advantages of a patch-based approach, which provides local pixel information and maintains class balance. The training utilized random initialization, stochastic gradient descent, and categorical cross-entropy loss over 150 epochs with a batch size of 32.

Experimental results indicate that the proposed R2U-Net and RU-Net models outperform U-Net and ResU-Net models across datasets. For the DRIVE dataset, the proposed models achieved higher accuracy and area under the curve than existing methods, with R2U-Net yielding the best performance with 0.841 million parameters. In the STARE dataset, R2U-Net achieved an accuracy of 0.9712, surpassing previous top performances. Similarly, in the CHASE_DB1 dataset, RU-Net and R2U-Net demonstrated superior results compared to U-Net and ResU-Net. Overall, the proposed models show significant improvements in accuracy and segmentation quality across all datasets analyzed.
