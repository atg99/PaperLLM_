This paper presents an extension of the U-Net architecture, introducing two new models: RU-Net and R2U-Net, which incorporate recurrent convolutional neural networks (RCNNs) and recurrent residual CNNs. These models were evaluated for three medical imaging applications—retinal blood vessel segmentation, skin cancer lesion segmentation, and lung segmentation (LS). The experimental results indicate that both RU-Net and R2U-Net outperformed existing methods, including SegNet and ResU-Net, while maintaining the same number of parameters. The models showed superior learning capabilities during training, leading to improved testing performance.

In terms of computational efficiency, the training times for blood vessel segmentation were approximately 209, 217, and 283 seconds per epoch for the DRIVE, STARE, and CHASE_DB datasets, respectively, while skin cancer and LS tasks took 23 and 14 seconds, respectively. Testing times for the datasets were 2.84, 6.42, and 8.66 seconds per sample. In contrast, R2U-Net achieved a testing time of around 6 seconds per sample, which is favorable for clinical applications. For skin cancer and LS segmentation, the entire images were processed in 0.32 and 1.145 seconds, respectively.

Future work aims to extend these models to a 3-D architecture for more advanced medical imaging analysis, including 3-D LS and brain tumor segmentation from MRI images.
