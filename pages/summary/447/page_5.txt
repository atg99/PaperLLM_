The paper introduces two deep learning models, RU-Net and R2U-Net, which leverage the strengths of various deep learning architectures, particularly Recurrent Convolutional Neural Networks (RCNN) and their variants. These models are designed to enhance object recognition tasks and medical image segmentation.

The authors mathematically describe the operations of recurrent convolutional layers (RCLs) within the RRCNN block. They define the relationship between input samples and the output at discrete time steps, detailing how the output is computed using standard convolutional operations combined with recurrent components. The outputs from these RCLs are processed through a ReLU activation function and subsequently used in downsampling and upsampling layers in RU-Net, while in R2U-Net, they are further processed through residual units.

The paper evaluates four architectures: the traditional U-Net with forward convolution layers, a residual U-Net (ResU-Net) that incorporates residual connectivity, RU-Net utilizing forward RCLs, and R2U-Net which incorporates recurrent convolution layers with residual connectivity. Each architecture is represented visually to illustrate the differences in their structures.

Overall, RU-Net and R2U-Net are presented as advanced models for improving segmentation tasks in medical imaging, with detailed mathematical formulations and architectural comparisons provided to support their efficacy.
