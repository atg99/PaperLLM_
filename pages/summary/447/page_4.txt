The paper discusses advancements in deep learning architectures for medical image segmentation, primarily focusing on U-Net and its variants. The U-Net architecture comprises convolutional encoding and decoding units, utilizing max-pooling for downsampling and transposed convolutions for upsampling. U-Net is notable for its ability to effectively handle limited training data while preserving the full context of images, making it superior to patch-based segmentation methods.

Following the original U-Net, several modifications and variants have emerged, including a simplified U-Net that employs element-wise summation of feature maps from different layers, demonstrating improved training performance, although no testing benefits were noted. The importance of skip connections is emphasized, particularly in biomedical segmentation tasks.

The paper also highlights various models like the deep contour-aware network and Nabla-Net, as well as three-dimensional segmentation architectures such as 3-D U-Net and V-Net, which incorporate residual connections and novel loss layers. The authors propose two new models based on U-Net: the recurrent CNN (RU-Net) and the residual recurrent CNN (R2U-Net), which leverage the principles of residual learning and recurrent connections for enhanced segmentation capabilities. The architectural details of these models are elaborated in the paper.
