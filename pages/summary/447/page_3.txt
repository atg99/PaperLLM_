The paper discusses advancements in medical image segmentation, particularly focusing on deep learning (DL) approaches that have gained popularity over traditional machine-learning techniques. It highlights the importance of assigning class labels to pixels for accurate lesion boundary identification in medical images. Traditional methods included histogram-based amplitude segmentation and graph-cut approaches, but the emergence of DL has transformed the field, allowing for universal models applicable across various imaging modalities like MRI, CT, and X-ray.

The authors introduce two novel segmentation models: Recurrent U-Net (RU-Net) and Recurrent Residual U-Net (R2U-Net), which aim to improve segmentation performance. These models are evaluated across three medical imaging modalities—retinal blood vessel segmentation, skin cancer segmentation, and lung segmentation—using different performance evaluation methods. The results demonstrate that the proposed models outperform existing state-of-the-art methods while maintaining a similar number of network parameters.

The paper is structured into sections that cover related work, detailed architecture of the proposed models, experimental setup and metrics, dataset information, and comparative results against other models like U-Net and SegNet. The authors conclude by discussing their findings and suggesting future research directions. The study emphasizes the necessity for efficient DL architectures in segmentation tasks to optimize performance without excessive parameter counts.
