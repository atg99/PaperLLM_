The paper presents the development of two novel convolutional neural network architectures, RU-Net and R2U-Net, designed for medical image segmentation. These models build upon the traditional U-Net framework by integrating recurrent convolutional layers (RCLs) and residual units, enhancing both training and testing performance without increasing the number of network parameters. Key differences from U-Net include the use of RCLs in both encoding and decoding units, an efficient feature accumulation method, and the elimination of cropping and copying, favoring concatenation operations.

The proposed architectures demonstrate improved segmentation performance across various medical imaging tasks, such as blood vessel segmentation and skin cancer lesion detection, by ensuring stronger feature representation through element-wise feature summation. The empirical evaluation shows that RU-Net and R2U-Net outperform traditional models like U-Net, SegNet, and ResU-Net while maintaining similar parameter counts.

The experimental setup involved testing these models on multiple datasets, including DRIVE, STARE, and CHASE_DB1 for blood vessel segmentation, and assessing their performance through various metrics. Architectural details, including the number of convolutional layers and feature maps, were thoroughly documented, supporting the effectiveness of the proposed models in medical imaging applications. The results indicate that the proposed architectures are not only efficient but also generalizable to other deep learning models, enhancing their applicability in segmentation tasks.
