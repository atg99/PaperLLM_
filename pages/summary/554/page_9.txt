This paper presents an analysis of neural architecture search (NAS) methods applied to the CIFAR-10 dataset, focusing on the impact of batch normalization (BN) strategies on architecture performance. The authors utilize parameter sharing-based NAS methods and evaluate architectures derived from these methods across multiple searching epochs. Key findings include:

1. **Architecture Performance**: The study demonstrates that DARTS-V1 and DARTS-V2 tend to quickly converge to architectures predominantly using skip connections. This is attributed to the hyper-parameters being potentially misaligned with the specific search space utilized in this study.

2. **Impact of Batch Normalization**: The choice between using batch statistics versus running estimates for BN layers significantly influences NAS performance. The results indicate that using batch statistics yields better validation and test accuracies.

3. **Efficiency of NAS Methods**: The research highlights that methods like REA, REINFORCE, and RS can complete searches in seconds, thereby reducing search costs and allowing researchers to concentrate on search algorithms rather than computational overhead.

4. **Comparative Analysis**: The authors compare the performance of various methods, noting that while DARTS tends to overfit after several epochs, other methods like RSPS, GDAS, SETN, and ENAS require longer training times but exhibit different variances in the architectures they search.

The authors acknowledge challenges in achieving optimal results due to sensitivity to hyper-parameters and express openness to incorporating additional NAS algorithms and results from other researchers to enhance their findings. Overall, the paper emphasizes the critical role of BN strategies in NAS and the efficiency of various methods in architecture search.
