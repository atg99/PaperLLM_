The paper introduces NAS-Bench-201, a benchmark designed to enhance the reproducibility and comparability of Neural Architecture Search (NAS) methods. It addresses issues found in existing benchmarks like NAS-Bench-101 and NAS-HPO-Bench, which have limitations in search space and architecture evaluation. NAS-Bench-201 features a fixed cell search space inspired by popular neural cell-based algorithms, where each architecture consists of a predefined skeleton and a stack of searched cells represented as densely-connected directed acyclic graphs (DAGs). Each cell comprises four nodes and five operation candidates, resulting in a total search space of 15,625 architectures.

The benchmark provides comprehensive training logs and performance metrics for each architecture across three datasets, including training and test accuracy, loss, number of parameters, and floating point operations (FLOPs). The benefits of NAS-Bench-201 include a unified benchmark for current NAS algorithms, reduced validation time, avoidance of repetitive training, and the ability to evaluate model transferability across datasets. The paper emphasizes that NAS-Bench-201 will facilitate more focused research on robust search algorithms within the NAS field and offers systematic analysis of the proposed search space.
