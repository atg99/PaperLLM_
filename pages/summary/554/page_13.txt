This paper discusses the development and evaluation of NAS-Bench-201, a benchmark for neural architecture search (NAS) that facilitates the comparison of different NAS algorithms. The authors highlight the encoding of architectures as 6-dimensional vectors, allowing for a total of 15625 unique models based on five possible operations. The study also identifies unique topology structures, revealing that considering isomorphic cells reduces the number of unique architectures to 6466.

The paper evaluates the correlation of various training strategies across different datasets, including CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet-16-120. The correlation coefficients are presented in Table 6, demonstrating that validation accuracy after a specific number of epochs is a good predictor of full model performance. The authors emphasize that training models with longer budgets correlates more strongly with performance than shorter training durations.

Additionally, the paper provides insights into the implementation of ten NAS algorithms on NAS-Bench-201, with a focus on the time budget allocated for each algorithm. The authors establish a baseline for the first-order DARTS algorithm and compare it with other algorithms like RS, REINFORCE, ENAS, and BOHB, setting the total time budget uniformly at 12000 seconds for evaluation.

Overall, the paper contributes to the field of NAS by offering a comprehensive benchmark and detailed implementation strategies, promoting better understanding and comparison of various NAS methods.
