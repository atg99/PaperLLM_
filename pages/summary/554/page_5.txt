This paper presents NAS-Bench-201, a new benchmark for Neural Architecture Search (NAS) that addresses limitations of existing benchmarks like NAS-Bench-101. NAS-Bench-201 is designed to be applicable to a wide range of contemporary NAS algorithms, unlike NAS-Bench-101, which restricts evaluation to certain types of algorithms. The benchmark provides comprehensive performance metrics across three datasets—CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet-16-120—allowing for a robust evaluation of NAS algorithms.

A key feature of NAS-Bench-201 is the inclusion of diagnostic information, which offers valuable insights into architecture performance during training. This information includes fine-grained statistics on loss and accuracy, providing indications of convergence speed, stability, and potential overfitting or underfitting. Such insights can enhance the design of NAS algorithms and support techniques like platform-aware NAS and accuracy prediction.

Additionally, NAS-Bench-201 supplies computational metrics for each architecture, such as the number of parameters, FLOPs, and latency, facilitating the development of architectures suited for resource-constrained environments. The dataset also includes the trained parameters for each architecture, allowing researchers to leverage pre-trained models for hypernetwork-based methods or mutation-based NAS approaches.

In summary, NAS-Bench-201 distinguishes itself from NAS-Bench-101 by being algorithm-agnostic, providing extensive diagnostic information, and offering detailed computational metrics, thereby advancing the field of NAS and enabling more effective architecture exploration.
