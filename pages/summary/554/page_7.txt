The paper presents a comprehensive evaluation of neural architecture search (NAS) methods using the NAS-Bench-201 dataset. It highlights the performance of the ResNet architecture, which, while competitive across CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet-16-120 datasets, still has room for improvement, particularly against the best architectures on these datasets. A significant observation is the consistent ranking of architectures across the three datasets, despite slight variances, indicating the robustness of the search algorithm.

The correlation between validation and test accuracies is examined, revealing a high correlation within individual datasets but a dramatic decrease when comparing across different datasets. This suggests that directly transferring the best architecture from one dataset to another does not guarantee high performance, underscoring the need for improved transferable NAS algorithms.

The paper also discusses the dynamic ranking of architectures over time, showing that validation rankings eventually align with final test accuracy rankings. In the benchmarking section, the authors evaluate ten recent NAS methods, including random search, evolutionary strategies, reinforcement learning algorithms, differentiable methods, and hyperparameter optimization techniques. The results indicate how each method can leverage the NAS-Bench-201 dataset to enhance searching and evaluation efficiency, showcasing its utility for future NAS research.
