The paper presents an evaluation of ten different neural architecture search (NAS) algorithms using the NAS-Bench-201 dataset, focusing on their performance on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet-16-120. The results are organized into three blocks: the first includes parameter sharing-based methods, the second includes methods that utilize batch normalization with fixed statistics, and the third covers methods without parameter sharing.

Key findings include:

1. **Performance Comparison**: Algorithms without parameter sharing, such as REA, RS, REINFORCE, and BOHB, generally outperform those with parameter sharing, such as DARTS and ENAS. This advantage is attributed to the ability to train models for a short duration with a converged learning rate scheduler, resulting in better relative performance.

2. **Efficiency of NAS-Bench-201**: The use of NAS-Bench-201 allows for significant reductions in search time for NAS algorithms without parameter sharing, with some methods achieving results in mere seconds. However, parameter sharing methods still require several GPU hours to complete their searches.

3. **Accuracy Metrics**: The paper reports mean and standard deviation of accuracy across multiple runs, emphasizing the consistency and reliability of the results obtained through the evaluated algorithms. The optimal architectures achieved higher mean accuracy across all datasets compared to the searched architectures.

4. **Visual Representation**: The paper includes figures that illustrate the performance of various algorithms, showcasing validation and test accuracies across different datasets, further supporting the findings.

Overall, the study highlights the advantages of certain NAS approaches in terms of both performance and efficiency, contributing to the ongoing discourse in the field of neural architecture search.
