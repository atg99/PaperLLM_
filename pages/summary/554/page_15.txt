This paper presents an evaluation of various methods for neural architecture search (NAS) using the CIFAR-10 validation set. It specifically analyzes the correlation between one-shot validation accuracy (OSVA) and the ground truth accuracy for different architectures. The methods compared include DARTS-V1, DARTS-V2, SETN, and GDAS, with results indicating varying levels of correlation.

A significant portion of the study discusses the use of parameter sharing in NAS, which enhances efficiency by allowing shared parameters across numerous architecture candidates. However, the authors note that these shared parameters may not be optimal for all candidates. They conducted experiments using DARTS, GDAS, and SETN to optimize these shared parameters and evaluate architecture candidates based on their OSVA.

Key findings include that the correlation between the probability derived from architecture encoding and ground truth accuracy is low, suggesting that simply optimizing shared parameters does not guarantee good architecture selection. The study also highlights the importance of batch normalization (BN) behavior, finding that using accumulated BN statistics from the training set can negatively impact one-shot accuracy. Conversely, recalculating BN statistics for each architecture candidate improved accuracy. Additionally, the Gumbel-softmax sampling introduced by GDAS resulted in a higher correlation for learned probabilities compared to DARTS.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the need for better strategies in deriving optimal architectures after optimizing shared parameters and provides insights into the factors influencing one-shot validation accuracy.
