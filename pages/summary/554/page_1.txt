The paper titled "NAS-Bench-201: Extending the Scope of Reproducible Neural Architecture Search" discusses advancements in the field of Neural Architecture Search (NAS) and introduces NAS-Bench-201, an extension of NAS-Bench-101. The authors, Xuanyi Dong and Yi Yang, highlight the challenges in comparing the performance of various NAS algorithms due to differences in search spaces, training setups, and hyper-parameters.

NAS-Bench-201 aims to address these comparability issues by providing a fixed search space that encompasses all possible architectures generated by 4 nodes and 5 operation options, resulting in 15,625 neural cell candidates. This unified benchmark allows researchers to avoid repetitive training for selected architectures, thereby enhancing the efficiency of NAS algorithms and making the field more accessible to a wider range of researchers.

The paper also emphasizes the importance of diagnostic information, such as fine-grained loss and accuracy metrics, which can inspire new NAS designs. The authors benchmarked 10 recent NAS algorithms using NAS-Bench-201, demonstrating its applicability and effectiveness in advancing the NAS community.

In conclusion, NAS-Bench-201 offers a structured and reproducible framework for evaluating NAS algorithms, thereby promoting further research and development in automatically designed neural architectures.
