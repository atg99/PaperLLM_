The paper presents NAS-Bench-201, a comprehensive framework for Neural Architecture Search (NAS) that is algorithm-agnostic, enabling its application across various contemporary NAS methodologies. The search space is inspired by cell-based NAS designs and evaluates architectures on three prominent datasets: CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet-16-120. This approach aims to provide insights for future NAS algorithm designs through empirical analysis.

The architecture of NAS-Bench-201 includes a macro skeleton characterized by a starting 3-by-3 convolution layer followed by three stacks of cells connected by a residual block. Each cell within the search space is structured as a densely connected Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG), allowing for a flexible topology that accommodates various operations from a predefined set, including zeroize, skip connection, and different convolution types. The design ensures that the search space is broad and compatible with numerous NAS algorithms.

To facilitate fair comparisons among NAS methods, the paper emphasizes standardized training, validation, and test splits across the chosen datasets, addressing inconsistencies in previous approaches. The proposed framework aims to advance the understanding and effectiveness of NAS by providing a structured environment for evaluating architectures and their performance.
