The paper discusses the datasets used for image classification tasks, specifically CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet-16-120, along with the training architecture and evaluation metrics involved in their analysis.

CIFAR-10 consists of 60,000 32x32 color images divided into 10 classes, with a training set of 50,000 images and a test set of 10,000 images. To create a validation set, the training images are split into two equal groups of 25,000 each. CIFAR-100 follows a similar structure but classifies images into 100 fine-grained classes, also having a training set of 50,000 images and a test set of 10,000 images, which is split for validation and testing.

ImageNet-16-120 is derived from a down-sampled version of ImageNet, containing 151,700 training images, 3,000 validation images, and 3,000 test images across 120 classes. 

The paper details the training architectures, employing a consistent set of hyper-parameters across different datasets to ensure comparability. The training utilizes Nesterov momentum SGD with a specified learning rate schedule and data augmentation strategies tailored to the image resolutions of CIFAR and ImageNet datasets.

Evaluation metrics include training and validation losses and accuracies across the datasets, enabling researchers to efficiently query results and enhance their neural architecture search (NAS) algorithms. The paper emphasizes the negligible computational costs associated with querying the NAS-Bench-201 metrics, facilitating a focus on the core aspects of NAS research.
