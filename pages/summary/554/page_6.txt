This paper presents NAS-Bench-201, an architecture search benchmark designed to address limitations of existing benchmarks like NAS-Bench-101. The authors highlight the challenges posed by the vast search space of NAS-Bench-101, which makes it difficult for many NAS algorithms to be evaluated effectively due to constraints on the maximum number of edges in the directed acyclic graph (DAG). NAS-Bench-201 simplifies this by reducing the number of nodes while allowing all possible edges, making it algorithm-agnostic.

The paper provides additional diagnostic information, including computational costs and training times, which can inform the design of more efficient NAS algorithms. The authors also analyze the performance of architectures across different datasets (CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, ImageNet-16-120), revealing that the number of parameters impacts performance, but the connections between operations are equally crucial. This underscores the significance of operation selection and architecture connectivity in NAS. Overall, NAS-Bench-201 aims to facilitate better evaluation and design of NAS algorithms by providing a more manageable and informative search space.
