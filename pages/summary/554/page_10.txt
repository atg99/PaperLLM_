The paper discusses the NAS-Bench-201 benchmark, aimed at providing a fair and computationally efficient environment for Neural Architecture Search (NAS) algorithms. It highlights the risk of overfitting to the best architecture due to the known performance of architectures within the benchmark. To mitigate this, the authors propose guidelines: avoiding specific regularization that targets the best architecture, adhering to provided performance metrics for fair comparisons, and reporting results from multiple search runs to ensure stability.

The paper also addresses limitations in hyper-parameter optimization (HPO), noting that the same hyper-parameter configuration is used across all architectures, which may introduce bias. The authors recognize the computational challenges of optimizing both architecture and hyper-parameters simultaneously.

Additionally, the paper suggests that diagnostic information within NAS-Bench-201 could lead to insights that enhance NAS efficiency, particularly regarding parameter sharing, which currently compromises accuracy.

The generalization of observations from NAS-Bench-201 is also discussed, with preliminary evidence suggesting consistency with findings from previous benchmarks. 

In conclusion, NAS-Bench-201 facilitates reproducible NAS evaluations, encompassing 15,625 architectures across three datasets, and serves as a baseline for future research. Future work will focus on integrating HPO with NAS and exploring larger search spaces. The authors invite the research community to utilize NAS-Bench-201 and contribute results.
