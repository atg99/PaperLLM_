This paper discusses various approaches to neural architecture search (NAS), focusing on methods such as Random Search (RS), Regularized Evolution for Architecture Search (REA), REINFORCE, DARTS, Random Search with Parameter Sharing (RSPS), Gradient-based Search using Differentiable Architecture Sampler (GDAS), and Self-Evaluated Template Network (SETN). 

The authors highlight that RSPS and GDAS optimize fewer parameters than DARTS, which leads to a longer searching procedure. The architecture selection process involves using validation accuracy after a specified number of training epochs, with RS relying on randomly selecting architectures until a time budget is reached. REA employs a population-based approach with an infinite number of cycles and evaluates architectures based on their fitness derived from validation accuracy.

For the REINFORCE method, the authors follow established protocols to encode architectures and utilize validation accuracy as the reward signal. The paper evaluates the impact of different learning rates on REINFORCE's performance. DARTS methods, both first and second order, are trained with specific hyper-parameters, including a decay in learning rate and gradient clipping.

The RSPS approach incorporates training with batch normalization in different modes during training and evaluation, and it evaluates a subset of architectures to balance search accuracy and computational cost. GDAS and SETN share similar hyper-parameters with DARTS and focus on training for a set number of epochs while employing specific techniques like Gumbel-Softmax for architecture sampling.

Overall, the paper provides a comprehensive overview of these NAS techniques, detailing their methodologies and configurations to optimize architecture performance effectively.
