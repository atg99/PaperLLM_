The paper introduces ContextNet, a novel convolutional neural network (CNN) architecture designed to enhance automatic speech recognition (ASR) performance by incorporating global context information. ContextNet features a fully convolutional encoder that integrates squeeze-and-excitation (SE) modules, allowing local feature vectors to access global context, thus addressing the limitations of traditional CNNs which typically operate with a limited kernel size. 

The authors argue that the lack of global context in CNN models is a primary reason for their underperformance compared to RNN and transformer-based models. ContextNet achieves significant improvements in word error rate (WER) on the LibriSpeech benchmark, recording a WER of 1.9%/4.1% with a language model, outperforming previous CNN models like QuartzNet and even surpassing some transformer and LSTM models. 

Additionally, the paper discusses a progressive downsampling technique that reduces computation costs while maintaining model accuracy and introduces a scaling method for adjusting the number of channels in convolutional filters to optimize the trade-off between model size and performance. The findings suggest that ContextNet not only advances the state of CNN-based ASR but also offers a more efficient architecture for future developments in the field.
