The model employs a total of 140 million parameters and is designed to optimize the trade-off between accuracy and model size in speech recognition tasks. The architecture is built on an RNN-Transducer framework, consisting of an audio encoder, a label encoder, and a joint network. The audio encoder utilizes depthwise separable convolutions, batch normalization, and a swish activation function to efficiently process input audio signals.

Key components of the encoder include a squeeze-and-excitation module for enhancing feature representation and a progressive downsampling strategy to reduce the temporal length of input sequences while maintaining accuracy. The paper presents empirical results demonstrating that ContextNet outperforms existing models, such as LSTM and Transformer variants, in terms of word error rate relative to model size. Overall, ContextNet's design focuses on achieving high performance in end-to-end speech recognition while maintaining computational efficiency.
