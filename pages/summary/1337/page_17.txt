The paper discusses the essential role of high-level robotic planning in the development of autonomous robotic systems, following successful low-level robotic control. Unlike low-level control, which focuses on system response and robustness, high-level planning aims to find near-optimal solutions among various feasible options within specific constraints. Reinforcement learning (RL) emerges as a powerful tool for enhancing planning capabilities in complex systems like humanoid robots with high degrees of freedom. However, challenges such as the high cost and time associated with real-world data collection and the curse of dimensionality complicate the training process.

The integration of digital twins (DT) with reinforcement learning is highlighted as a promising approach to address these challenges. Several studies are cited, showcasing applications of this combination, including motion planning for robotic manipulators, multitasking robot arm schemes, automation in smart manufacturing, and collision avoidance in UAVs.

Additionally, the paper emphasizes the significance of digital twins in facilitating safer human-robot interaction (HRI) and human-robot collaboration (HRC) within the context of Industry 4.0. These scenarios present increased complexity due to environmental uncertainties and the unpredictable nature of human behavior. AI-equipped digital twins possess enhanced capabilities to respond to these variables, offering advantages over conventional systems. An example is provided of a real-time process-level digital twin developed for collaborative construction work involving humans and robots.
