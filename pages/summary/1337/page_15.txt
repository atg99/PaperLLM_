The paper discusses the implementation of digital twins (DT) in robotics, highlighting their application across various domains such as kinematics, communication, control, planning, and energy modeling in industrial robots. It identifies specific use cases including welding, cleaning, pick-and-place operations, assembly, manufacturing, warehouse management, maintenance, and construction. Notable robotics simulation tools mentioned are Gazebo, MuJoCo, and CoppeliaSim.

The paper emphasizes the recent integration of artificial intelligence (AI) in developing semi- and fully autonomous robotic systems, particularly through techniques like transfer learning and imitation learning. It contrasts traditional model-based DTs, which rely on solid understanding of systems, with data-driven, AI-equipped DTs that are more suited for complex robotic systems where high-fidelity models are impractical.

A table summarizes AI-enabled DTs categorized by their learning algorithms and applications in subfields such as control, planning, and human-robot interaction. Specific methods like supervised learning, reinforcement learning, and computational intelligence are noted for their roles in applications ranging from object recognition to disaster-response support. An illustration of a joint project demonstrating DT in improving robotic precision is also referenced.
