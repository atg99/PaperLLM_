The paper discusses the integration of artificial intelligence (AI) with digital twin (DT) technology in robotics, focusing on various applications and methods within different subfields. Key areas highlighted include planning, human-robot interaction (HRI), predictive maintenance, and workspace modeling.

In the planning subfield, reinforcement learning methods such as Proximal Policy Optimization and Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (DDPG) are applied to tasks ranging from industrial robotic arms to UAV collision avoidance. Computational intelligence techniques like ant colony optimization are utilized for path planning.

For HRI, supervised learning techniques, including convolutional neural networks (CNN) and artificial neural networks (ANN), are used for tasks such as posture recognition and obstacle avoidance. Advanced methods like Fast Fourier Transform combined with PCA and SVM are employed for analyzing welder behavior.

Predictive maintenance is addressed through deep neural networks (DNN) for system health monitoring, enhancing plant availability in manufacturing systems. In workspace modeling, Monte Carlo methods are used for simulating robotic mechanisms.

The control section emphasizes the significance of feedback in robotic systems, where real-time data from sensors is critical for performance. AI-driven approaches enhance the adaptability and generalizability of robotic systems. Examples include a smart soft-robotic gripper using triboelectric nanogenerator sensors for motion and tactile information and the use of reinforcement learning for a humanoid robot's weight-lifting tasks. The paper also presents applications in aerospace maintenance and disaster response, showcasing the effectiveness of AI and DT in improving robotic capabilities.
