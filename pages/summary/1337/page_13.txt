The paper discusses advancements in the development of AI-driven digital process twins (DTs) for manufacturing, particularly in the context of composite materials. Ghanem et al. developed an inference method to enhance simulations of composite systems, while Kanyuck et al. introduced the ISRES algorithm to identify sheet material parameters, facilitating defect-free prepreg composite sheet layup in collaborative environments.

The interim summary highlights the potential of AI-driven DTs to learn from diverse data sources to optimize manufacturing processes, production ramp-up, and quality assurance. It emphasizes the importance of novel sensor technologies and networked digital process chains as critical components for effective DT development and deployment. The paper notes that emerging manufacturing techniques, such as 3D printing, are still largely experimental and require maturation to improve efficiency and sustainability throughout the product lifecycle, ultimately reducing energy consumption and carbon emissions.

The section on challenges and outlook identifies key issues for future work, including the need for vertical interoperability through a standardized framework for multi-scale DTs and horizontal interoperability, which relies on high-quality data for effective AI-driven systems. Interdisciplinary collaboration is deemed essential to address these challenges and improve the overall implementation of DTs in manufacturing.
