The paper discusses advanced methodologies in manufacturing, focusing on the application of probabilistic modeling and model order reduction techniques to enhance control and predictive capabilities in machinery and equipment. It highlights the challenges faced in transferring analytical and empirical research findings to real-world industrial settings due to complex operating conditions and limited datasets. The integration of networked production systems offers a unique opportunity to utilize manufacturing environments as test benches, thereby improving data availability for machine learning and deep learning applications in equipment fault diagnosis and system behavior prediction, which are vital for sustainable manufacturing.

The role of digital twins in manufacturing is emphasized, particularly in condition monitoring (CM) and predictive maintenance (PdM), supported by advancements in 5G technology that facilitate high data rates and the sensing of high-frequency phenomena. The paper also addresses quality assurance in the manufacturing process, noting that the quality of parts is typically assessed post-machining. It advocates for the use of digital process twins to optimize quality control loops, enabling real-time estimation of surface quality through GPU-enabled simulations. This approach reduces latency in defect detection and supports robust process design and control, ultimately enhancing productivity across various production processes, including welding, injection molding, and additive manufacturing.
