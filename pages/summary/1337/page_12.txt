This section of the paper discusses various applications of artificial intelligence (AI) in different manufacturing processes, particularly focusing on metal cutting, additive manufacturing (AM), and composite material processing. 

In metal cutting, the quality of the part is influenced by the machine's behavior and the interaction between the cutter and workpiece, especially in complex multi-axis machining. Zhao et al. developed a self-learning model to predict surface roughness using pigeon-inspired optimization and support vector machines (SVM) to enhance quality stability. Other studies combined artificial neural networks (ANN) with semantic modeling for fatigue and quality predictions, while Zhou et al. employed the deep deterministic policy gradient (DDPG) method to optimize decision-making, thus reducing cycle times and costs. Moreover, Hardt et al. explored the use of particle swarm optimization (PSO) to identify material model parameters in finite element simulations of cutting processes.

In the realm of advanced metal AM and laser processing, researchers are examining thermal effects on materials during non-contact processes. Gaikward et al. utilized a graph-theoretical approach to predict temperature distributions and integrated this with SVM to identify quality faults in printing. They also proposed a grey box modeling technique that combines physical process knowledge with shallow ANNs for enhanced performance. Ko et al. applied classification and regression trees (CART) to predict additive manufacturability, contributing to the development of an AM knowledge base. Additionally, hidden Markov models (HMM) and k-means clustering were used for quality assessment and monitoring.

For composite material processing, which is still developing, hybrid modeling of non-measurable variables is expected to improve understanding and transparency. Stieber et al. introduced a convolutional neural network (CNN) for monitoring the polymerization process in resin transfer molding. Hürkamp et al. used machine learning techniques like AdaBoost and random forest to predict temperature distribution in thermoplastic composite fabrication. Lastly, Pfrommer et al. applied deep neural networks (DNN) for optimizing manufacturing parameters in composite textile draping.

Overall, the integration of AI in these manufacturing domains aims to enhance quality, efficiency, and process understanding, thereby facilitating advancements in production techniques.
