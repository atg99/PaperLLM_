The paper discusses the concept of the digital thread, initially proposed by the U.S. Air Force, as a framework for integrating detailed design models with model-based systems engineering (MBSE). This concept was further advanced by the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) to facilitate information exchange throughout the product lifecycle, encompassing aspects such as product design, quality, and equipment performance. The digital thread allows for a unified model-based data approach that promotes real-time collaboration and traceability among project participants.

The survey aims to provide a comprehensive overview of AI-driven digital twin (DT) technologies in the context of Industry 4.0. It emphasizes the shared philosophy of leveraging digital replicas with near real-time capabilities to enhance traditional processes and improve industry competitiveness while optimizing resource allocation. The focus is specifically on digital twins, excluding discussions on 5G communication, IoT technologies, and standalone machine learning applications.

The paper is organized into several sections: Section 2 analyzes digital production twins for sustainable manufacturing; Section 3 explores DT applications in robotics and human-robot collaboration; Section 4 compares various AI methods; and Section 5 concludes with the paper's contributions and future work directions. Over 300 manuscripts are reviewed to support the insights presented.
