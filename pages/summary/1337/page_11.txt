The paper discusses the integration of artificial intelligence (AI) into manufacturing processes, highlighting the benefits of using machine learning (ML) and deep learning (DL) as surrogate models to enhance efficiency and understanding in production. As manufacturing models become more complex, their simulation requires significant time; thus, AI methods are employed to streamline this process. The use of GPUs for parallel computing allows for near real-time simulations, while ML/DL models provide rapid updates to time-consuming numerical simulations.

These lightweight AI models facilitate faster production ramp-up and inline-quality monitoring, ultimately improving process understanding and optimizing yield. The paper also presents a summary of AI-driven digital twins (DTs) across various manufacturing techniques, including metal cutting, metal additive manufacturing (AM), laser material processing, and composite material processing. 

In metal cutting, various supervised learning techniques are employed for predicting surface roughness and modeling fluid behavior. In metal AM and laser processing, methods like support vector machines and convolutional neural networks are utilized for defect prediction and quality assurance. For composite material processing, AI techniques such as transfer learning and deep neural networks help in detecting production issues and predicting material properties.

Overall, the integration of AI in manufacturing enhances process efficiency, quality monitoring, and development speed across multiple domains.
