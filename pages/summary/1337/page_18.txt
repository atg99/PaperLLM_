The paper discusses the integration of immersive virtual reality and building information modeling (BIM) with evolving as-built workspace geometry from on-site sensors, enhancing planning and improvisation capabilities in robotics. It highlights various applications of machine learning, including deep learning for human posture recognition and reinforcement learning to improve medical equipment assembly efficiency during COVID-19.

In the realm of robot maintenance, the authors emphasize the importance of predictive maintenance using data-driven methods, with examples of anomaly detection and health monitoring through digital twins (DT). Challenges in developing DTs include the complexities of multibody physical simulations, the need for real-time feedback for effective decision-making, and the unpredictability introduced by human interactions in collaborative environments. Safety measures compliant with standards are crucial for ensuring safe human-robot interactions, and the integration of AR or VR technologies is suggested to enhance intuitiveness.

The discussion section underscores the potential of digital twins to boost industrial economic growth while promoting sustainability through reduced resource consumption and improved working conditions. The application of AI techniques enables digital twins to create accurate models from historical data, enhancing data analysis efficiency and prediction accuracy across various engineering domains.
