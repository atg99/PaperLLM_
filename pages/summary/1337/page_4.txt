Sustainable resilient manufacturing is focused on producing high-quality products efficiently while navigating the challenges posed by a volatile and uncertain market environment. Recent advancements in Industry 4.0, cyber-physical production systems, and integrative production techniques are essential in addressing these challenges. The integration of simulation models and digital twins throughout the product lifecycle is pivotal for manufacturing transformation, particularly in the context of the circular economy and sustainability initiatives like the European Green Deal.

The concept of "sustainable productivity" emerges as a response to traditional resource-intensive approaches, incorporating sustainability factors (FESG) to quantitatively evaluate production performance and value creation systems. This shift encourages manufacturing companies to adopt sustainable practices and technologies.

The discussion on sustainable resilient manufacturing is organized into three levels: factory and shop floor, machinery and equipment, and process and material. At the factory level, the need for adaptive production systems is emphasized, driven by individual customer demands and market volatility. Automated systems, enhanced with AI and sensor technologies, can be modularized and reconfigured, enabling the development of distributed control systems. Digital twins facilitate the management of complex operational challenges by creating detailed, dynamic production environments.
