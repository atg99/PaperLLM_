The paper discusses the integration of artificial intelligence (AI) in digital twins (DTs) for sustainable development, focusing on factors related to efficiency, environmental impact, and social governance (FESG). It categorizes AI techniques into four main classes: supervised learning, unsupervised learning, reinforcement learning, and other intelligent computational methods.

Supervised learning involves models trained on labeled data, employing algorithms such as support vector machines, decision trees, and neural networks. However, it requires extensive labeled datasets, making it resource-intensive. Unsupervised learning does not require labeled data, instead inferring patterns from input data through clustering algorithms and generative models. The challenge lies in determining the number of clusters and appropriate similarity metrics.

Reinforcement learning focuses on optimizing decision-making through actions that maximize cumulative rewards, applicable in scenarios like box sorting and conveyor systems. Its effectiveness hinges on accurate data logging and reward structure design.

The fidelity of AI-driven DTs is influenced by model granularity, which relates to the complexity of the environment and the quality of datasets. The paper reviews over 300 manuscripts to propose a pathway for AI integration in multiscale and fidelity DTs, highlighting the importance of real-time capabilities across different levels of the automation hierarchy. This multiscale approach encompasses both spatial and temporal dimensions, addressing the varying response times required for effective management and planning.
