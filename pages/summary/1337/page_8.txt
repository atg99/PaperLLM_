The paper discusses the integration of digital twins (DT) in condition monitoring (CM) and predictive maintenance (PdM), emphasizing their role in managing the health status of critical components such as tools, bearings, and pumps. It highlights the challenges faced by small and medium enterprises (SMEs) in implementing PdM due to limited resources, which often leads to unplanned maintenance and increased costs. The paper also explores the potential of DT-based optimal control and machine dynamics in improving equipment energy efficiency and overall performance.

Furthermore, the paper outlines the advantages of integrating artificial intelligence (AI) with DT, particularly through machine learning (ML) methods. Traditional model-based approaches rely on direct sensor measurements, which can be costly and may affect machine stability. In contrast, ML offers a cost-effective means of estimating indirectly measurable parameters, enhancing the accuracy of monitoring and predictive capabilities.

Table 2 in the paper summarizes various AI-enabled applications within CM and PdM across different methodologies, including supervised and unsupervised learning techniques. Notable applications include the prediction of process forces, defect recognition, and tool wear assessment. The use of advanced techniques like generative adversarial networks (GANs) and long short-term memory networks (LSTM) is also highlighted for their effectiveness in predicting machine component health and tool conditions.

Overall, the integration of DT and AI presents a significant opportunity to enhance the efficiency and reliability of manufacturing processes, particularly for SMEs facing operational constraints.
