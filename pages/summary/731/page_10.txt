The collection of references highlights significant advancements in the field of reinforcement learning, particularly focusing on actor-critic algorithms and their theoretical underpinnings. Borkar and Konda (1997) introduce the actor-critic algorithm as a multi-time-scale stochastic approximation method, laying foundational concepts for subsequent research. Cai et al. (2019) discuss efficient exploration strategies in policy optimization, while Di Castro and Meir (2010) present a convergent online actor-critic algorithm that operates on a single time scale.

Recent studies such as those by Cen et al. (2020) and Chen et al. (2019) explore the global convergence of natural policy gradient methods and the stability of Q-learning with linear function approximation, respectively. The finite sample analysis by Dalal et al. (2017) and Gupta et al. (2019) provides insights into two-timescale stochastic approximation, addressing performance bounds and adaptive learning rates.

Konda and Tsitsiklis (2000, 2004) contribute to the understanding of actor-critic algorithms and their convergence rates, while various works delve into the sample complexity and sensitivity of these methods (Kumar et al., 2019; Mitrophanov, 2005). Mnih et al. (2016) demonstrate the application of asynchronous methods in deep reinforcement learning, showcasing practical implementations.

Overall, this body of work emphasizes the theoretical advancements and practical implications of actor-critic algorithms, exploring their convergence, efficiency, and adaptability in reinforcement learning contexts.
