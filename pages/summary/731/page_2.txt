The paper discusses the two time-scale actor-critic algorithm in reinforcement learning, emphasizing the simultaneous updates of the actor and critic, with the actor updating more slowly than the critic. This method aims to ensure that the critic provides a well-approximated value function, facilitating accurate policy gradient estimation for the actor. The authors highlight the existing asymptotic analysis of these methods but point out the lack of finite-time analysis, which is crucial for understanding sample efficiency and the appropriate selection of learning rates.

The key contributions of the paper include proving that the actor converges to an ε-approximate stationary point of the non-concave performance function J after accessing a maximum of eO(ε^-2.5) samples. This contrasts with previous analyses of actor-critic methods, which often relied on decoupled approaches and unrealistic i.i.d. assumptions. The authors provide a new proof framework that more accurately characterizes estimation errors in two time-scale algorithms and improves convergence rates compared to previous techniques.

The paper also briefly reviews related work on stochastic bias characterization in reinforcement learning, noting the challenges posed by non-i.i.d. data and the correlation between samples and parameters. Techniques from information theory and stochastic approximation are referenced as methods for addressing these challenges in deriving convergence results for various algorithms.
