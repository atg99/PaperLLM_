In Remark 4.8, the authors discuss the removal of the term O(t^(-1)) from equations (4.3) and (4.4) by using the iterative refinement technique, leading to a bound on the error norm. This refinement indicates that the error can be effectively managed, with the dominating terms being influenced by the actor's movement relative to the critic's. The second term arises from Markovian noise and semi-gradient variance, while the third term reflects the slow drift of the actor. The relationship between the actor's and critic's speeds determines the dominance of these error components.

Corollary 4.9 summarizes the convergence rate and sample complexity of Algorithm 1, indicating that the expected error of the actor's policy approaches an ε-approximate stationary point within T iterations. The specific choice of parameters results in a convergence rate of eO(t^(-2/5)) and a sample complexity of eO(ε^(-2.5)).

In Remark 4.10, the authors compare their results with previous studies, noting that existing algorithms have higher sample complexities. They highlight that Kumar et al. and Qiu et al. establish a sample complexity of eO(ε^(-4)), which is significantly worse than their findings, demonstrating the efficiency of their two time-scale actor-critic approach.

Remark 4.11 contrasts the two time-scale actor-critic method with the decoupled actor-critic method, emphasizing the latter's inefficient sample usage due to its disregard for historical information. The two time-scale method retains the critic's parameters, enhancing sample efficiency.

Finally, Remark 4.12 discusses the sample complexity of policy gradient methods, suggesting that if the critic converges faster, the two time-scale actor-critic's complexity aligns with that of policy gradient methods up to logarithmic factors. This insight underscores the potential advantages of the proposed algorithm in terms of convergence speed and sample efficiency.
