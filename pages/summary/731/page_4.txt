The paper discusses the evaluation of overall rewards in a reinforcement learning context, introducing the state-value function and the state-action value function, or Q-value function. The state-value function Vπ(s) is defined as the expected reward when starting from state s0 and following a behavior policy π. The Q-value function Qπ(s, a) similarly evaluates the expected reward for taking action a in state s and then following the policy.

The paper highlights the importance of the performance function J(θ), which reflects the expected reward under the stationary distribution induced by the policy. The policy gradient theorem, as established by Sutton et al., is presented, showing how to compute the gradient of the performance function with respect to policy parameters. This gradient can be expressed in two forms: one in terms of the Q-value function and the other using the log probability of the actions taken under the policy.

Furthermore, the REINFORCE method is introduced as a Monte Carlo approach to estimate the policy gradient by utilizing the summed return along trajectories as an estimate for Q-values. The paper also notes the distinction between the average-reward and start-state formulations, with the latter being more complex in its gradient estimation.

Finally, the concept of a baseline function b(s) is introduced, which depends solely on the state. The inclusion of a baseline helps in reducing the variance of the policy gradient estimates without biasing them, thus facilitating more efficient learning. The paper emphasizes the relevance of these theoretical foundations in the context of reinforcement learning algorithms.
