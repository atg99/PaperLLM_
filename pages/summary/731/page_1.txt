This paper presents a finite-time analysis of two time-scale actor-critic methods in reinforcement learning. Actor-critic methods combine the strengths of actor-only and critic-only approaches, achieving significant empirical success. The actor utilizes policy gradient techniques for policy enhancement, while the critic employs temporal difference learning to estimate this gradient. Although the asymptotic convergence of actor-critic methods has been previously explored, the authors focus on non-asymptotic convergence and finite sample complexity, which remain largely unaddressed.

The authors demonstrate that under a non-i.i.d. setting, the two time-scale actor-critic method can guarantee the discovery of a first-order stationary point of the non-concave performance function with a sample complexity of O(ε^-2.5). This result marks a significant contribution, as it is the first to provide a finite-time analysis and sample complexity bounds for these methods.

The introduction elaborates on the mechanics of actor-critic methods, highlighting their ability to mitigate the high variance associated with actor-only methods and the divergence issues faced by critic-only methods when using function approximation. The paper underscores the collaborative nature of the actor and critic, where the critic learns the value function based on the actor's policy, and the actor, in turn, refines the policy using the value function estimates provided by the critic.
