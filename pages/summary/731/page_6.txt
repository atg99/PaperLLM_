This section outlines the foundational assumptions and propositions related to Temporal Difference (TD) learning with linear function approximation for estimating state-value functions. The state-value function is expressed as a linear combination of features, and the feature mapping is assumed to have a bounded norm. The limiting point of TD(0) algorithms under a behavior policy is denoted, and two key components, A and b, are defined, with their relationship establishing that the limiting point satisfies a specific equation.

The approximation error of the linear function class is defined and assumed to be uniformly bounded across all potential policies. To ensure the uniqueness of the limiting point of TD and the problem's solvability, a negative definiteness condition on the matrix A is introduced as Assumption 4.1. This condition is crucial for guaranteeing convergence and is linked to a projection step in the algorithm.

Assumption 4.2 addresses Markovian noise and establishes uniform ergodicity of the Markov chain generated by the policy and transition dynamics. It specifies the existence of parameters that control the total variation distance between distributions.

Assumption 4.3 sets regularity conditions on the policy, ensuring convergence of the actor component in policy gradient methods. It includes bounds on the gradients of the log-policy and differences between policies.

Overall, these assumptions collectively ensure that the limiting point of TD(0) is Lipschitz, facilitating the analysis of TD learning in this context.
