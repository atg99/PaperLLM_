The paper discusses the advantages of using a specific action in reinforcement learning through the concept of advantage values, which compare the expected reward of an action to the expected reward at a given state. It references the policy gradient algorithm "REINFORCE with a baseline," which mitigates variance issues commonly faced by policy gradient methods. To further address this variance, actor-critic methods are introduced, which utilize a separate model to approximate the state-value function.

The two time-scale actor-critic algorithm is analyzed, where the critic employs linear function approximation and is updated using the TD(0) algorithm. This algorithm operates simultaneously to update both the critic and actor based on the advantage, or temporal difference error. This on-line approach allows for implementation in both episodic and continuing settings, leading to the development of the Asynchronous Advantage Actor-Critic (A3C) algorithm, which has shown empirical success. The synchronous variant, known as Advantage Actor-Critic (A2C), is also discussed.

The algorithm's steps include initializing parameters, drawing from an initial distribution, taking actions based on the policy, observing states and rewards, and updating the critic and actor using the calculated temporal difference error. The paper emphasizes the algorithm's practical advantages, including the use of naturally generated sample paths without the need for independent samples or stationary distributions.

In the final section, the authors outline standard assumptions in reinforcement learning literature that support the convergence of algorithms, followed by the presentation of theoretical results related to the two time-scale actor-critic methods.
