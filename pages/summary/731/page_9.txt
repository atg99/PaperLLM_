The paper presents a finite-time analysis of two time-scale actor-critic methods, particularly focusing on their sample complexity and convergence rates when using non-i.i.d. Markovian samples along with linear function approximation. The authors demonstrate that the established sample complexity of O(ε^2.5) is the lowest achievable for classic two time-scale actor-critic methods. They suggest that further improvements can be made through advanced policy evaluation algorithms like GTD and TDC, which may reduce the sample complexity.

The analysis pertains to an online, one-step actor-critic algorithm that is both practical and efficient. The authors also note that their proof technique may be applicable to other reinforcement learning algorithms using two time-scales. They discuss potential enhancements, such as utilizing compatible features to mitigate function approximation errors and applying regularization techniques for the critic to ensure stability.

The broader impact of this research includes aiding industrial applications of actor-critic algorithms and enhancing the understanding of their sample complexity, which can inform training time estimates for reinforcement learning models. Additionally, the findings could streamline the hyper-parameter tuning process, thereby conserving human and computational resources. The authors affirm that their work does not raise any ethical concerns.
