Proposition 4.4 establishes that under certain assumptions, there exists a constant L such that the target point moves slowly compared to the actor's parameter updates. This observation is crucial for the two time-scale analysis, which describes the actor's slow movement while the critic adjusts to the slowly changing target defined by the actor.

The convergence of two time-scale actor-critic methods is then addressed, introducing a mixing time defined in relation to the learning rates. This mixing time, denoted as 𝜏_t, is used to manage the Markovian noise encountered during training.

Theorem 4.5 provides the convergence rate for the actor's updates, indicating that if the averaged mean squared error between the actor's estimated parameters and the true value function, as well as the error between the actor's policy and the reward function, are small, the convergence can be characterized by specific rates dependent on the learning parameters. The theorem confirms that the results from the decoupled case are applicable by setting a specific value for the learning rate, but also highlights the complexity introduced by simultaneous updates under non-i.i.d. data.

Theorem 4.7 characterizes the convergence of the critic, showing that under certain learning rate choices, the estimation error converges to zero at specific rates. This is vital for ensuring that the critic accurately reflects the value function as the learning progresses, thus supporting the overall convergence of the actor-critic framework. The results are accompanied by specific error bounds that detail how the convergence behavior is influenced by the chosen learning rates and the inherent noise in the data.
