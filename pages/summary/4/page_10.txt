The paper discusses the concept of "good scaling" in deep learning models, emphasizing that smaller models may appear to scale well but can exhibit significant performance deterioration with slight reductions in computational resources. It uses examples like ResNet and NasNet to illustrate that models showing rapid scaling may not actually be competitive for state-of-the-art performance, suggesting that their performance declines quickly as computation decreases.

The authors argue that misinterpretation of scaling results can lead to erroneous conclusions about a model's effectiveness. They highlight the importance of analyzing and comparing model performance accurately to avoid confusing rapid performance decline with good scaling. Their findings align with previous studies on scaling laws, indicating that while some models like EfficientNet perform well and stay near the performance frontier, others like ResNet and NasNet may not be as robust.

The paper also touches on the emerging scaling laws in generative AI, noting discrepancies in scaling results and recommendations across different models. The authors stress the significance of cross-model comparisons and acknowledge the challenges posed by the diverse nature of models, particularly in the context of data availability for generative language models. Overall, the analysis aims to provide a clearer understanding of how model performance evolves with increasing computational power.
