The paper "The Computational Limits of Deep Learning" by Neil Thompson et al. explores the significant dependence of deep learning advancements on increasing computational power. The authors conduct a meta-analysis of 1,527 research papers across various domains, revealing that the performance improvements in deep learning applications are strongly correlated with escalated computing requirements. This dependency raises concerns about the economic, technical, and environmental sustainability of deep learning as the computational burden grows.

The authors argue that the high computational costs associated with deep learning are inherent to its design, which allows for flexibility and superior modeling capabilities but also leads to increased resource demands. They suggest that the current trajectory of deep learning is unsustainable, necessitating either a shift towards more computationally efficient methods or significant enhancements in the efficiency of existing deep learning techniques.

The paper highlights the historical context of computational constraints in deep learning, noting that these limitations have been temporarily alleviated through advancements in specialized hardware and increased investment. However, the authors warn that as computational needs continue to rise, these constraints will likely resurface. The findings underscore the urgency for the machine learning community to address the growing computational challenges to sustain progress in the field.
