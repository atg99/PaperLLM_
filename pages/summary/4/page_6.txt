The paper presents a regression analysis examining the relationship between deep learning performance and computing power growth, specifically focusing on image classification tasks using the Imagenet dataset. The analysis employs Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) and Quantile Regression methods to assess how network operations and hardware burden influence performance metrics such as Top 1 error, BOX AP, EM, and F1 score across different tasks.

Key findings indicate a significant negative correlation between the logarithm of network operations and Top 1 error, suggesting that increased computational operations lead to improved model performance. The results show high R-squared values, indicating a strong explanatory power of the models. The analysis also highlights that performance improvements in deep learning are not solely confined to this field but are observed across various domains, including weather prediction and strategic games, where increased computing resources are essential.

The authors further explore the implications of these findings, suggesting that the future of deep learning may necessitate even greater computational power to achieve benchmark performance levels. The paper emphasizes the ongoing dependence on computing resources for advancing deep learning capabilities and raises concerns about the sustainability of such trends in computational demands.
