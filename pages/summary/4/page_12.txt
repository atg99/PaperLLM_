The paper discusses the environmental impact of deep learning models, highlighting the significant carbon emissions associated with training these models. Previous studies have indicated that training a single language model can generate as much carbon dioxide as the lifetime emissions of five cars. The analysis suggests that future performance improvements in deep learning will necessitate enormous increases in computing power, further exacerbating environmental concerns.

To mitigate these damages, the authors propose examining three key factors: the amount of computation required, the energy consumed per computation, and the carbon emissions associated with energy use. They argue that reducing computation is the most effective strategy, as it directly correlates with lower carbon emissions. Techniques such as pruning, quantization, early stopping, and model distillation can help decrease computational demands.

The paper also notes advancements in hardware, such as specialized chips that improve performance and energy efficiency. Researchers are increasingly focusing on algorithms designed to minimize energy usage, alongside strategies for efficient model updating and data center management. Finally, the choice of energy source for data centers is crucial, with the use of renewable energy significantly reducing carbon emissions.

In conclusion, while algorithmic improvements have been made, the authors emphasize the need for a holistic approach to address the escalating environmental impact of deep learning, combining reductions in computation, energy efficiency, and sustainable energy sourcing.
