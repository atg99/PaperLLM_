The paper discusses the challenges facing deep learning in terms of increasing performance while managing the economic and environmental costs associated with computational requirements. It emphasizes the need for strategies to enhance performance without a proportional increase in computing power. The authors focus primarily on the training costs of deep learning models, acknowledging that these costs form a lower bound for total computational requirements.

Several approaches to lessen the computational burden are explored:

1. **Increasing Computing Power**: The paper highlights the transition to more efficient hardware platforms, such as GPUs, TPUs, and FPGAs, which have provided significant gains in computing efficiency. However, there are concerns about the sustainability of these gains due to diminishing returns. Alternative hardware solutions like analog computing, neuromorphic computing, and quantum computing are also discussed, with quantum computing showing the most potential for future exponential growth in computing power.

2. **Reducing Computational Complexity**: Techniques such as network compression and acceleration are examined, where trained neural networks are optimized to require fewer computations during prediction tasks. Methods like pruning, quantization, and low-rank compression have shown some success, but the overall reductions in computation have not been sufficient to counter the increasing demands in deep learning.

3. **Finding Efficient Architectures**: The paper addresses the rise of neural architecture search and meta-learning, which aim to discover computationally efficient network architectures. While these approaches can yield promising results, they often require substantial computational resources themselves, limiting their practicality.

4. **Improved Data Curation**: It suggests that training models on higher-quality, curated data could reduce computational needs. However, this approach may involve higher costs related to data annotation, complicating the overall cost-benefit analysis.

5. **Exploring Alternative Machine Learning Techniques**: The authors propose that exploring less computationally intensive models, such as expert systems or symbolic approaches, might provide a path forward. These methods can outperform deep learning in specific applications, especially where domain knowledge can be directly integrated.

In conclusion, while there are various promising avenues for addressing the computational limits of deep learning, none have yet achieved the significant improvements necessary to sustain the field's growth effectively. The paper calls for continued exploration of innovative strategies to mitigate the rising computational demands associated with deep learning.
