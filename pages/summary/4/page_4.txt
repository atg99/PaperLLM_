The paper discusses the increasing computational demands of deep learning models and their implications for sustainability in training practices. It highlights that significant performance improvements in deep learning are largely attributed to extended training times on more hardware rather than advancements in hardware efficiency or algorithmic improvements. Historical examples illustrate this trend, with models like AlexNet and ResNeXt-101 requiring increasingly extensive computational resources.

To analyze deep learning's reliance on computation, the authors conducted a comprehensive review of 1,527 research papers across various domains, including image classification, object detection, and machine translation. They focused on benchmarks that provide precise error rates, excluding generative models due to their lack of exact performance metrics. The analysis reveals a concerning trend: many papers fail to report adequate computational details, emphasizing a historical focus on accuracy over computational transparency.

The authors present two measures of computational burden: Network Operations, which quantifies the floating-point operations in a model, and Hardware Burden, reflecting the computational capacity of the hardware used. They specifically examine image classification, finding a polynomial relationship between computation and performance. Their results indicate that reducing error rates in image classification tasks requires exponentially more computation, with estimates suggesting that halving the error may demand over 4,000 times the computational resources.

Overall, the work underscores the need for better reporting of computational requirements in deep learning research and raises concerns about the long-term sustainability of current training practices as computational demands continue to rise.
