The paper discusses the intrinsic reliance of deep learning on computational power, particularly in the context of overparameterization. Overparameterization, where a neural network has more parameters than training data points, has been shown to yield significant benefits, allowing for effective fitting of training data while maintaining reasonable predictions on unseen data. This phenomenon is exemplified by the CoCa image classification system, which has 2.1 billion parameters for 1.2 million data points.

However, the paper highlights the challenge that as the number of data points increases, the number of parameters in deep learning models must also grow. The computational cost of training scales with the product of the number of parameters and data points, implying a quadratic growth in computational requirements relative to data size. Yet, to improve performance, a faster-than-linear increase in training data is often necessary, leading to a back-of-the-envelope estimate that computational needs could grow at least as a fourth-order polynomial concerning performance.

This relationship is supported by statistical learning theory, which indicates that improvements in performance typically require a significant increase in data points. The paper draws an analogy with linear regression, where the computational burden increases dramatically as the number of parameters rises, even with regularization techniques like the lasso estimator. The findings suggest that even with regularization, the computational power needed for enhanced performance in deep learning models grows rapidly, reinforcing the notion that deep learning is highly dependent on computational resources.
