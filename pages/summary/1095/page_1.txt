The paper "Towards a Unified View of Parameter-Efficient Transfer Learning" addresses the challenges associated with fine-tuning large pretrained language models (PLMs) in natural language processing (NLP). As the size of PLMs and the number of tasks increase, conventional methods that fine-tune all parameters become impractical. The authors highlight the emergence of parameter-efficient transfer learning methods that fine-tune only a limited number of parameters while still achieving strong performance.

This work aims to clarify the critical components contributing to the success of these methods and to establish connections among them. The authors propose a unified framework that re-frames existing techniques as modifications to specific hidden states within pretrained models. They identify various design dimensions that differentiate these methods, such as the computation function for modifications and their application positions.

Through extensive empirical studies on tasks like machine translation, text summarization, language understanding, and text classification, the authors evaluate the design choices of past methods. Their framework facilitates the transfer of design elements across different approaches, leading to the development of new parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods. These new methods require fewer parameters to be tuned compared to previous strategies while achieving results comparable to full fine-tuning across all tested tasks.

Overall, the paper provides a comprehensive analysis of parameter-efficient transfer learning, contributing to a better understanding of its methodologies and paving the way for more effective applications in NLP.
