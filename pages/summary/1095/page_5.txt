This paper presented at ICLR 2022 discusses parameter-efficient tuning methods for pre-trained language models (PLMs), focusing on the distinctions and similarities among various techniques such as adapters, prefix tuning, and LoRA. A key contribution is the decomposition of these methods along defined design dimensions, which helps in understanding their operational mechanics and parameter efficiency.

The authors illustrate how prefix tuning can be conceptualized as a plug-in module similar to adapters, highlighting that both methods use low-rank matrices to compute modification vectors. They identify the bottleneck dimension in prefix tuning, noting its similarity to the rank limitation in adapters. The paper emphasizes three main differences between prefix tuning and adapters: the use of input versus output for computing the modification vector, the flexibility of insertion points in the network, and the expressiveness in handling multiple attention heads.

The unified framework proposed in the paper categorizes these tuning methods by defining specific design dimensions, such as functional form, modified representation, and insertion form. Each method's characteristics are detailed, showcasing how they fit within this framework. The paper aims to provide a comprehensive understanding of how different methods can be adapted and improved for efficient tuning of PLMs, ultimately contributing to advancements in natural language processing tasks.
