The paper presents experimental setups and methodologies for evaluating parameter-efficient tuning methods across various NLP tasks, specifically summarization and machine translation. It utilizes datasets including XSum, WMT16 en-ro, MNLI, and SST-2, detailing the statistics of training, development, and testing samples.

The authors implement their experiments using the Hugging Face Transformers library, employing BART LARGE and mBART LARGE for summarization and translation tasks, and RoBERTa BASE for classification tasks. They specify maximum token lengths for training data, with particular configurations for each dataset to optimize performance and memory usage.

Training hyperparameters are discussed, including the use of the Adam optimizer and a polynomial learning rate scheduler. The warm-up steps differ between tasks, with dropout rates consistently set at 0.1. Evaluation metrics include ROUGE-2 for summarization and perplexity for machine translation. The decoding process employs beam search, with specified generation lengths for both tasks.

The paper also elaborates on the implementation of prefix tuning and LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) methods. Prefix tuning involves reparameterizing vectors through an MLP network, enabling effective learning through shared parameters. LoRA employs distinct parameter initialization methods compared to traditional adapters, which the authors found beneficial for performance. Overall, the paper provides a comprehensive overview of the experimental framework and methodologies used for evaluating the efficacy of parameter-efficient tuning in NLP tasks.
