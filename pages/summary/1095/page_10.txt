The paper presents a method for efficient fine-tuning of pre-trained language models, which have numerous beneficial applications such as summarization and translation. It acknowledges ethical concerns associated with language models, including bias in generated text, potential leakage of private information, and environmental impacts of model training. The proposed method minimizes alterations to existing model parameters, raising questions about its effects on bias and information leakage—areas not thoroughly researched to date.

Regarding environmental impact, the method introduces a minimal number of additional parameters, resulting in a slight increase in training time (100% - 150% of full fine-tuning) while maintaining similar inference times. Importantly, it could reduce the overall resource costs for model deployment. The majority of experiments were conducted on a data center powered by renewable energy.

The paper includes a reproducibility statement detailing experimental setup and public access to source code to facilitate result replication. Acknowledgments are given for support from various projects and fellowships. The references list includes significant works related to language modeling and fine-tuning techniques.
