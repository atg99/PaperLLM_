This paper investigates the effectiveness of different modifications to transformer architectures, specifically focusing on attention and feed-forward network (FFN) layers. The authors compare various methods including parallel adapters and prefix tuning, analyzing their performance on tasks such as XSum and English-Romanian translation.

Key findings indicate that modifications to the FFN consistently outperform those applied to the attention mechanism across all tested scenarios, often achieving better results with fewer parameters. For instance, LoRA applied to the FFN improved upon its attention counterpart by a notable margin. The authors hypothesize that FFN modifications are more effective at capturing task-specific textual patterns compared to attention modifications, which focus on pairwise positional interactions.

When exploring the impact of very small parameter budgets (0.1%), the results show that prefix tuning and multi-head parallel adapters perform best, suggesting that attention modifications excel under restricted conditions. The authors emphasize the importance of allocating parameter budgets strategically, recommending a preference for FFN modifications over attention in larger configurations.

Additionally, the paper evaluates different composition functions for integrating modifications, concluding that while gated addition (used in prefix tuning) provides some benefits, simple and scaled additions are also effective. Overall, the findings advocate for a nuanced approach to modifying transformer architectures, particularly in balancing the use of attention and FFN layers based on available parameters and specific task requirements.
