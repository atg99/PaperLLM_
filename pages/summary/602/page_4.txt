The paper discusses the challenges of model selection in deep unsupervised domain adaptation (UDA), particularly focusing on the domain shift between source and target distributions. The authors introduce a method to address the difficulty of estimating the target risk without labeled data from the target domain. The core idea revolves around estimating the expected loss using labeled source data and importance-weighted cross-validation (IWCV).

The authors define a joint distribution J(x,d), where x is the input and d indicates the domain. They propose that the model selection process can be framed as minimizing the expected loss over a finite set of candidate models. A significant challenge arises due to the lack of access to target labels, which necessitates using importance weights to adjust the loss estimates from the source domain to approximate the target risk.

The paper highlights that the variance of IWCV is bounded by the Rényi divergence between the source and target distributions, suggesting that as domain adaptation progresses, the divergence in feature space decreases, even if the input space distributions remain unchanged. To improve model selection, the authors propose a shift from covariate-level to feature-level validation, leveraging deep features to estimate density ratios more effectively.

In summary, the paper presents a framework for model selection in deep UDA that incorporates feature adaptation and density ratio estimation, ultimately aiming to enhance performance in scenarios where target labels are unavailable. This approach not only addresses the limitations of existing methods but also emphasizes the necessity of developing validation techniques tailored for deep UDA contexts.
