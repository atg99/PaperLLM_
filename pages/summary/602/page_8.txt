The paper presents Deep Embedded Validation (DEV), a novel approach for model selection in Deep Unsupervised Domain Adaptation (UDA). DEV improves the accuracy of model selection by embedding deep adapted representations into the validation process, which allows for more reliable estimation of the density ratio. It also utilizes a control variate method to minimize variance, enhancing the overall effectiveness of model selection.

The results from various experiments, including those on the Digits and Office-31 datasets, show that DEV consistently yields higher accuracy compared to traditional methods, often matching or exceeding the performances of state-of-the-art techniques. For instance, DEV outperformed the original methods in both GTA and PADA, demonstrating its robustness across different validation scenarios.

Additionally, the paper discusses the implications of partial domain adaptation, where DEV effectively addresses model selection challenges without modifications. An analysis of density ratios further supports the method's validity, revealing insights into class distributions and their relationships.

The ablation study indicates that adapting feature representations significantly contributes to the success of DEV, with the control variate method enhancing performance even further. The findings underscore DEV's potential as a reliable, non-intrusive model selection tool, particularly valuable in scenarios lacking labeled target domain data.
