In the context of Deep Unsupervised Domain Adaptation (UDA), model selection becomes more complex due to the absence of target labels and the significant domain gap between source and target data. Traditional model selection methods, such as Source Risk and Target Risk, face challenges; Source Risk can be a biased estimator, while Target Risk raises ethical concerns regarding the use of labeled target data.

The paper highlights the limitations of existing methods like Importance-Weighted Cross-Validation (IWCV) and discusses the dilemma faced in the VisDA Challenge, where the organizers must navigate the challenges posed by the availability of labeled data.

To address these challenges, the authors propose a novel model selection method called Deep Embedded Validation (DEV). This method utilizes adapted feature representations during the validation process to provide an unbiased estimate of target risk with controlled variance. The theoretical and empirical analyses presented in the paper demonstrate the efficacy of DEV, indicating that it can achieve performance comparable to Target Risk without requiring labeled target data.

The review of related work underscores the need for robust model selection techniques in both supervised learning and domain adaptation, ultimately advocating for the adoption of DEV to enhance the accuracy of model selection in Deep UDA.
