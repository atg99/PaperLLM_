The paper discusses a method for improving model selection in Deep Unsupervised Domain Adaptation (UDA) through a technique called Deep Embedded Validation (DEV). The authors introduce a model that discriminates between source and target samples, focusing on estimating the density ratio between these distributions. The density ratio is broken down into components that can be estimated using a discriminative model trained on domain information.

A significant aspect of the proposed method is variance reduction in target risk estimation. The authors utilize the control variate method, which employs a known expectation that is independent of the model, to lower the variance of the risk estimate. This method enhances the reliability of the risk estimation by embedding deep feature representations into the model selection process.

The paper outlines two algorithms: one for calculating the risk of a candidate model (GetRisk) and another for ranking models based on their DEV risks. The DEV approach not only applies to deep models but also extends to shallow models, although deep models benefit more due to their smaller R'enyi divergence.

Moreover, the authors emphasize that their method is not confined to feature adaptation models; it can also be applied to generative models that generate auxiliary distributions closer to the target distribution. This flexibility allows for a broader application of the DEV method in various contexts of domain adaptation. Overall, the approach aims to provide an unbiased estimation of target risk while ensuring a bounded variance through theoretical guarantees.
