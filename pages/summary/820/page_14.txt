This appendix details the replication of the SNIP algorithm as proposed by Lee et al. in 2019. The key aspect of SNIP is the introduction of a virtual parameter, ci, which acts as a coefficient for each parameter, wi, initially set to one. The algorithm scores each parameter based on the derivative of the loss with respect to ci, pruning those with the lowest scores. The authors highlight three main design choices: utilizing the derivative of the loss for scoring, taking the absolute value of this derivative, and pruning parameters with the lowest scores. The rationale is that a high magnitude of the derivative indicates a significant impact on the loss, necessitating the preservation of the corresponding connection.

In terms of implementation, the scoring formula is refined to express the score as the gradient of wi multiplied by its value. For selecting examples for SNIP, a mini-batch of ten randomly chosen examples per class is used, aligning with the approach from Wang et al. for a related method. Unlike Lee et al., the authors do not reinitialize the network after pruning. To ensure consistent results, they execute all SNIP computations on CPU before training the pruned network on TPU.
