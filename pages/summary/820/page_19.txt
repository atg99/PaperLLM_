The paper presents a comparison of the performance metrics of the GraSP pruning method as reported by Wang et al. and the authors' reimplementation. The results for various models, including VGG-19, WRN-32-2, and ResNet-50, are outlined in a table showing unpruned accuracy, pruned accuracy, and sparsity levels. 

For VGG-19, the authors achieve slightly lower unpruned accuracy (93.5%) compared to the reported 94.2%, with pruned accuracies declining at different levels of sparsity. Similar trends are observed for WRN-32-2 and ResNet-50, where the authors' results often lag behind those of Wang et al. by 0.6 to 1.1 percentage points in pruned accuracy, despite starting from higher baseline accuracies in some cases.

The authors attribute these discrepancies to variations in hyperparameter selections, such as the method for selecting examples for GraSP, batch size, and learning rate. Overall, while the implementation aligns with Wang et al. in behavior, it reveals notable differences in accuracy outcomes.
