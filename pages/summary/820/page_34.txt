This appendix presents experiments on the fully-connected LeNet-300-100 architecture for the MNIST dataset, which were excluded from the main paper due to their limited relevance to larger-scale settings. The aim is to assess how various pruning techniques perform in this specific context, where weights are linked to individual input pixels, potentially influencing the impact of random shuffling of pruned weights.

Key observations include that among the standard methods, SNIP outperforms others, while magnitude pruning at initialization shows the poorest performance. The differences in performance diminish at lower sparsities, indicating that results from LeNet-300-100 may not generalize to larger networks. 

Ablation studies reveal that for magnitude pruning, shuffling and reinitializing do not significantly impact accuracy. For SNIP, these factors affect accuracy primarily at higher sparsities. GraSP shows a complex relationship, where shuffling and reinitializing can enhance accuracy at lower sparsities but detract from it at higher levels. Conversely, SynFlow remains stable against shuffling and reinitialization across sparsities.

The hypothesis that random shuffling might negatively affect accuracy in fully-connected networks is supported, but only at extreme sparsities. At these high levels of pruning, accuracy declines significantly, while shuffling impacts magnitude pruning after training at much lower sparsities. Overall, the findings highlight the nuanced effects of pruning strategies in different network architectures, reinforcing the limited applicability of results from simple models to more complex ones.
