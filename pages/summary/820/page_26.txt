This appendix presents findings on the sensitivity of pruning methods when applied after model initialization, expanding upon previous results. The study assesses the effects of various ablation techniques at different training phases and their impact on accuracy across multiple architectures.

In the first section, the authors analyze ablation methods at the end of training. Results indicate that magnitude pruning leads to decreased accuracy when subnetworks are shuffled or reinitialized, particularly in ResNet-20, where accuracy drops significantly after reaching 49% sparsity. In contrast, SNIP shows smaller accuracy differences, with performance varying based on the architecture and level of sparsity. GraSP exhibits limited sensitivity to ablations, with some configurations outperforming unmodified networks. SynFlow demonstrates mixed results, improving accuracy for ResNet-20 but decreasing it for VGG-16 and ResNet-18 at higher sparsities.

The second section focuses on ablations post-initialization. Findings reveal that magnitude pruning becomes sensitive to ablations early in training, with performance improvements stalling after specific iterations. Notably, in VGG-16, shuffling outperforms reinitialization, whereas the opposite is true for ResNet-18. SNIP mirrors the behavior of magnitude pruning, while GraSP shows minimal sensitivity, primarily to shuffling. SynFlow's response to ablations varies, improving accuracy for ResNet-20 and VGG-16 but not for ResNet-18.

In summary, the results support the hypothesis that pruning methods are more sensitive to initialization and connection-specific factors at the start of training. Magnitude pruning, SNIP, and SynFlow maintain or enhance accuracy under random shuffling at initialization, but post-initialization, these modifications generally lead to accuracy degradation.
