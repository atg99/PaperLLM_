This paper investigates the state of the art in pruning neural networks at initialization, specifically comparing methods such as SNIP, GraSP, SynFlow, and magnitude pruning to random pruning. The authors emphasize the performance of these methods in achieving accuracy comparable to ResNet-50 on ImageNet at specific sparsities, where magnitude pruning after training typically yields superior results. 

Key findings indicate that no single method consistently outperforms the others across all networks and datasets, with SNIP demonstrating consistent performance and magnitude pruning showing unexpected effectiveness. The paper highlights that the subnetworks generated by pruning methods perform similarly when weights are shuffled or reinitialized, a behavior not observed in state-of-the-art post-training weight-pruning methods, which suffer accuracy loss under such alterations.

The authors explore potential limitations of pruning at initialization, suggesting that the observed insensitivity to shuffling and reinitialization may indicate inherent challenges with these methods when performed early in training. They argue that while pruning at initialization is promising, it may not achieve the same accuracy as post-training pruning without significant modifications or later intervention in the training process. The paper concludes by suggesting a need for new methodologies that can effectively prune networks early in training, given the current methods' limitations.
