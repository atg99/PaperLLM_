The appendix presents experiments conducted on a modified version of ResNet-18 using the TinyImageNet dataset, following the methodology of Tanaka et al. (2020) for evaluating SynFlow. This examination is crucial as TinyImageNet lacks the standardization evident in other benchmarks like CIFAR-10 and ImageNet. The results reinforce the main findings of the paper, demonstrating that the modified ResNet-18, when coupled with different augmentation strategies, yields consistent outcomes.

Figure 3 reveals that for early pruning methods, magnitude pruning at initialization excels at lower sparsities, while GraSP outperforms at higher sparsities. Notably, SynFlow does not demonstrate improved performance compared to random pruning, and magnitude pruning post-training achieves similar results only at lower sparsities.

Figure 4 illustrates that all methods either maintain or enhance their accuracy when subjected to random shuffling. SynFlow shows significant gains, while SNIP also improves. Performance remains stable across methods when reinitializing, except for magnitude pruning, which suffers when the initialization distribution is altered. GraSP retains its performance under inversion, contrasting with the other methods that experience degradation.
