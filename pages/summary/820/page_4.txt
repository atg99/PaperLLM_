This paper discusses various early pruning methods in neural networks, specifically focusing on their performance at initialization. The methods evaluated include SNIP, GraSP, and SynFlow, each employing different strategies for optimizing weight removal to maintain gradient flow. SNIP identifies weights that hinder gradient flow, while SynFlow computes scores based on logit gradients to iteratively prune low-scoring weights.

The paper compares these early pruning methods against benchmark techniques like magnitude pruning after training and Lottery Ticket Rewinding (LTR). The benchmarks serve to illustrate the maximum accuracy achievable at specific sparsities. The authors categorize sparsity levels into trivial, matching, and extreme, emphasizing their focus on matching and low extreme sparsities.

Experimental results on image classification tasks using various architectures (ResNet-20, VGG-16, ResNet-18, and ResNet-50) demonstrate that SNIP, SynFlow, and magnitude pruning yield competitive performance at matching sparsities. Notably, SNIP and SynFlow achieve full accuracy at certain non-trivial sparsities, outperforming random pruning and approaching the performance of magnitude pruning after training.

Overall, the findings indicate that early pruning methods can be effective at initialization, with specific advantages depending on the network architecture and sparsity level.
