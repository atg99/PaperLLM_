This section of the paper discusses the replication of networks and results from the SNIP paper by Lee et al. (2019) in the context of computer vision. Various networks are examined, including fully-connected and convolutional architectures, with a focus on their performance on the MNIST and CIFAR-10 datasets. Table 1 summarizes the networks and datasets used, indicating the authors' naming conventions and the availability of GitHub repositories for the networks.

In the results section, the authors present their findings comparing unpruned and pruned network accuracies from their implementation against those reported in the original SNIP paper. Table 2 shows that their implementation achieved higher unpruned accuracies for VGG-16 and wide ResNets, suggesting that the original networks may not have been well-tuned. The authors note that while their pruned network results align closely with those from the original paper, the higher starting accuracies lead to more significant drops in performance post-pruning.

Additionally, the paper references results from the GraSP paper by Wang et al. (2020), which also replicates SNIP, highlighting the differences in reported outcomes. The authors express confidence in their implementation correctness, although the lower accuracies of the unpruned networks in the original SNIP paper raise questions about their performance standardization.
