This paper evaluates various early pruning methods in neural networks, specifically focusing on their performance at different levels of sparsity. The findings reveal that no single method is the best across all settings. While SNIP consistently performs well, SynFlow is often competitive, and magnitude pruning shows surprising effectiveness despite its simplicity. GraSP tends to underperform at lower sparsities but excels at extreme sparsities.

The authors investigate how these pruning methods extract information from networks at initialization. They conduct an experiment involving random shuffling of pruning masks to determine if methods prune specific connections or make layerwise decisions. Results indicate that all methods either maintain or improve accuracy when masks are shuffled, suggesting that their effectiveness relies more on layerwise proportions rather than specific weight decisions. This contrasts with magnitude pruning, which is sensitive to such alterations.

Overall, while early pruning methods generally outperform random pruning, they do not achieve the accuracy or sparsity efficiency of magnitude pruning after training. The paper raises questions about whether the insensitivity of early pruning methods to shuffling may hinder their potential performance.
