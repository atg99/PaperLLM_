The appendix discusses the replication of the SynFlow pruning algorithm as described by Tanaka et al. in 2020. SynFlow is an iterative pruning method that aims to achieve maximal critical compression of neural networks by maintaining connectivity until the highest feasible sparsity level. The algorithm operates over N iterations, pruning weights with the lowest scores calculated based on their absolute values, forward propagation of a uniform input, and gradient computation.

Key steps in the scoring process include replacing weights with their absolute values, performing a forward pass, summing logits, calculating gradients, and issuing scores based on the product of gradients and weights. The paper emphasizes that any iterative, conservative pruning technique that issues positive scores can achieve maximal critical compression.

The replication uses 100 iterations and aligns input sizes with corresponding datasets, such as CIFAR-10 and ImageNet. Notably, the authors do not reinitialize the network post-pruning and conduct computations on CPUs to eliminate variations from distributed training. They also employ double precision for score calculations to prevent activation issues in deeper networks.

The appendix lists various networks and datasets evaluated, noting discrepancies between the original and replicated setups, particularly for CIFAR-100 and TinyImageNet, where modifications were necessary.
