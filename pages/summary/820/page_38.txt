The paper discusses the effects of different pruning methods on neural networks, specifically focusing on SynFlow and its tendency to cause neuron collapse. It highlights that random shuffling can mitigate this collapse. Additionally, the performance implications of pruning weights based on GraSP scores are examined, revealing that while pruning low-magnitude weights using GraSP does not negatively impact performance, it can enhance performance in certain scenarios. This suggests a nuanced relationship between pruning methods and model efficacy.
