This paper discusses early pruning methods in neural networks, particularly focusing on their effectiveness compared to baseline methods. It highlights various pruning techniques like SNIP, GraSP, and SynFlow, providing a comparison in terms of their application and performance. The authors note the historical context of neural network pruning, which dates back to the 1980s, but has gained renewed interest in recent years, especially in improving training efficiency alongside inference efficiency.

The paper outlines different pruning strategies, including random, magnitude, and methods like SNIP, which utilizes gradient information to determine the importance of weights. It also emphasizes the significance of re-training after pruning to ensure that the modified network converges effectively. The authors categorize pruning into one-shot and iterative methods, detailing the scoring and masking processes involved.

The study concludes by examining early pruning methods, asserting that any new proposals should outperform naive methods like random and magnitude pruning. The paper serves as a foundation for understanding the evolution and efficacy of various pruning techniques in the context of neural network training.
