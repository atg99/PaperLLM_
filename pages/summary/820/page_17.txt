The appendix discusses the replication of the GraSP algorithm, which aims to maintain gradient flow in sparse networks resulting from weight pruning. GraSP assesses the impact of pruning on the change in loss after the first training step, defined as the difference in loss before and after the weight update. The change in loss is evaluated through a first-order Taylor expansion, resulting in a formulation that relates the loss change to the Hessian and gradient of the loss function.

GraSP treats pruning as a perturbation of the original parameters, allowing for the calculation of the effect of removing individual weights. Each weight is assigned a score based on its contribution to the gradient flow, with negative scores indicating that removal would reduce gradient flow and positive scores suggesting an increase. Consequently, weights with higher scores are considered less important and are prioritized for pruning to maximize gradient flow post-pruning.

The authors note a potential correction regarding the definition of the change in loss, suggesting that the updated loss should be subtracted from the original to accurately reflect improvement. Overall, the appendix outlines the theoretical foundation and practical application of the GraSP algorithm in weight pruning.
