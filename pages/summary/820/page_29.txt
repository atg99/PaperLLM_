The paper discusses the comparison between one-shot SNIP, as originally proposed by Lee et al. in 2019, and its iterative variants introduced by de Jorge et al. and Verde-nius et al. in 2020. In the iterative approach, SNIP scores are recalculated after pruning a set number of weights, repeating this process over multiple iterations. The authors conduct experiments to evaluate the performance of both one-shot SNIP and iterative SNIP across various sparsity levels.

The results, illustrated in Figure 11, indicate that the performance of one-shot SNIP (red) and iterative SNIP (green) is largely similar at the sparsities examined, suggesting that the iterative approach does not significantly enhance performance under these conditions. The authors speculate that the benefits of iterative SNIP may be more pronounced at extreme sparsity levels, which were not the focus of their study.

Additionally, Figure 12 presents ablation studies for both SNIP and iterative SNIP, demonstrating that shuffling and reinitialization do not substantially impact accuracy in either case. Overall, the findings suggest that while iterative SNIP is an interesting variant, its advantages may be limited within the sparsity ranges tested in the paper.
