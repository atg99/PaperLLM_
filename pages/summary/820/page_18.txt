The paper details the implementation and results of the GraSP algorithm as applied to various neural networks and datasets, specifically focusing on the CIFAR-10 and ImageNet benchmarks. The authors implemented GraSP using the PyTorch framework, adhering to the original GitHub code by Wang et al. (2020), which computes the Hessian-gradient product. For scoring parameters, they sampled ten examples per class for CIFAR-10 and 10,000 examples for ImageNet, though the exact sampling method for ImageNet in Wang et al.'s work remains unclear.

The authors chose not to reinitialize their models post-pruning and opted to conduct all GraSP computations on CPU to eliminate the influence of distributed training, subsequently training the pruned networks on TPU. They compared their results against the networks and datasets outlined by Wang et al., noting differences in hyperparameters, particularly for ResNet-50.

Results indicated that the unpruned networks achieved comparable accuracy to those reported by Wang et al., with minor discrepancies attributed to different hyperparameters. However, pruned networks exhibited lower accuracy, with performance drops closely mirroring those seen in Wang et al.’s findings. The paper emphasizes the challenges in replicating VGG networks for CIFAR-10, which are known to be particularly difficult to reproduce. Overall, the authors' findings align closely with previous work, showcasing the robustness of the GraSP algorithm across different implementations.
