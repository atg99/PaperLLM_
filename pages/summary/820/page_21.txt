This paper presents the replication of network performance results reported by Tanaka et al. on various architectures using the CIFAR-10 and TinyImageNet datasets. The authors detail their findings for unpruned networks, noting that their replicated results closely match those of Tanaka et al. for VGG-11 and VGG-16 on CIFAR-10, with accuracies of 92% and 93.5% respectively. However, discrepancies arise with the modified ResNet-18, where their accuracy on CIFAR-10 was lower than reported (93.6% vs. approximately 95%) and on TinyImageNet (58.8% vs. 64%).

The authors attribute the differences in performance to potential unknown discrepancies in model implementation or training configurations, including variations in hyperparameters and initialization schemes. They observe that while their results for VGG-style networks align closely with those of Tanaka et al., the performance of ResNet-18 differs significantly, with different ordering of pruning methods and variations in accuracy drop-off rates.

The paper also discusses the comparison of various pruning methods, such as random pruning and SNIP, across sparsity levels, revealing that their results for VGG networks are consistent with Tanaka et al. In contrast, their findings for ResNet-18 indicate a lack of alignment, suggesting a possible brittleness in performance at high sparsities.

Additionally, the authors identify a potential bug in Tanaka et al.'s GraSP implementation related to batch normalization, which could further explain observed performance differences. Overall, the paper highlights the challenges of replicating experimental results in deep learning and the implications of minor changes in model configurations on performance outcomes.
