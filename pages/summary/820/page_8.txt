The paper discusses the effects of different pruning methods on neural networks, particularly focusing on the timing of pruning—specifically at initialization versus after training. It highlights that pruning at initialization generally leads to lower accuracy compared to pruning after training. The authors argue that this lower accuracy is not an intrinsic issue with the pruning methods themselves, but rather a consequence of the timing of when pruning is applied.

The research explores whether pruning methods, such as magnitude pruning, SNIP, and SynFlow, can achieve higher accuracy when applied later in training rather than at initialization. The authors demonstrate that these methods do improve in accuracy as training progresses, suggesting that the performance gap observed when pruning at initialization is not inherent to the methods. Additionally, as accuracy improves, the sensitivity of the subnetworks to various ablations also increases, further supporting this claim.

The paper also compares the performance of these pruning techniques to random pruning, showing that all methods outperform random pruning at certain sparsities. However, no single pruning method stands out as the best across all scenarios; each has conditions under which it excels. Furthermore, it notes that SynFlow and magnitude pruning can be effective even without training data during initialization, while methods like LTR and magnitude pruning after training require data for effective pruning.

In conclusion, the findings suggest that challenges associated with pruning may extend beyond just initialization, indicating potential difficulties for a significant period after training begins.
