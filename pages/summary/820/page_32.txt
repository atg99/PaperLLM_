This paper discusses the impact of layerwise pruning proportions on the accuracy of neural network models, specifically analyzing various pruning methods applied to architectures such as ResNet and VGG. Figure 15 illustrates the per-layer sparsities achieved by different pruning techniques at the highest matching sparsity. 

Key observations include that varying layerwise proportions yield comparable accuracy across different pruning methods, with minimal performance differences (less than 2.5 percentage points) observed among early pruning techniques on various networks. However, the specific layerwise sparsity distributions differ significantly; for instance, SynFlow prunes early layers of ResNet-20 to less than 30% sparsity, while GraSP prunes these layers to over 60%. Notably, SNIP and SynFlow tend to focus on pruning later layers more heavily compared to earlier layers, whereas GraSP maintains a more uniform pruning strategy. 

The findings suggest that simply determining how to prune layers may not be enough to achieve the high performance seen in benchmark methods, as early pruning techniques produce a wide range of layerwise proportions yet still face performance limitations.

Additionally, the paper highlights the role of skip connections in ResNet architectures, noting that SynFlow prunes these connections more aggressively than other methods. This results in significant reductions in sparsity for downsample layers, especially in ResNet-50, where SynFlow eliminates three of the four downsample connections. 

Finally, it is observed that all pruning methods, except random pruning, apply lower pruning rates to the output layer, indicating its critical importance for maintaining high accuracy due to its fewer connections and direct influence on network outputs.
