In this paper, the authors explore the connection between SynFlow, a method for pruning neural networks, and the path norm, a metric that has implications for the generalization of neural networks. The study focuses on a specific neural network architecture, characterized as a directed, acyclic graph with layers where each neuron connects to its adjacent layers. The path norm is defined as the sum of the products of weights along all paths from input to output neurons. This norm serves as a capacity measure for networks employing ReLU activations and is invariant under certain balanced rescaling of weights.

The authors further establish a relationship between the path norm and gradients in a modified network where all weights are positive. They demonstrate that the path norm can be expressed in terms of the gradients of the output neurons with respect to the weights. This leads to the conclusion that SynFlow can effectively prune weights that have the greatest impact on preserving the local path norm, thus maintaining the network's performance. The paper outlines how the removal of a weight affects the path norm, employing a first-order Taylor series approximation to quantify the decrease in the path norm when a weight is pruned. This connection provides insights into the mechanisms of SynFlow and its effectiveness in optimizing neural network architectures.
