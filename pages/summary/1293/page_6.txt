This paper evaluates the performance of StyleALAE in generating and reconstructing images using two datasets: FFHQ (Flickr-Faces-HQ) and LSUN Bedroom. The results are quantified using Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) scores, where lower values indicate better performance. The FID scores for StyleALAE show competitive results compared to other methods like StyleGAN and PGGAN, particularly noting that StyleALAE achieves higher FID scores despite a significantly shorter training duration (1 million images compared to 15 million for StyleGAN).

The paper also discusses perceptual path lengths (PPL) as a measure of representation disentanglement, where StyleALAE exhibits lower PPL values in both the Z and W spaces compared to StyleGAN, suggesting a more linear and less entangled representation in W space. Qualitative results indicate that StyleALAE's reconstructions are less prone to semantic label flipping compared to BiGAN, and the model demonstrates effective style mixing capabilities even when using images from different distributions.

In the experiments, StyleALAE was trained on FFHQ with a structured approach, gradually increasing the resolution from 4x4 to 1024x1024 over 147 epochs, leveraging multiple GPUs for efficiency. The findings underscore the model's strength in achieving high-quality image generation and reconstruction while maintaining a lower computational burden. Overall, StyleALAE shows promise as an effective alternative in the realm of generative models for image synthesis.
