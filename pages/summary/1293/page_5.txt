The paper discusses the performance of two generative models, BiGAN and ALAE, on the MNIST dataset. It presents reconstruction results, illustrating the capabilities of both models in generating images. The training methodology involves alternating updates for the discriminator and generator networks, employing an Adam optimizer with specific parameters.

ALAE is trained in a permutation-invariant manner, treating MNIST images as 784D vectors. The architecture consists of a three-layer MLP with a latent space of 50D. The results of representation learning are evaluated through classification and reconstruction tasks, with a focus on disentanglement analysis. 

The experiments use different settings: same writers (SW) and different writers (DW), with varying feature lengths (short and long). The classification accuracy is reported using both 1NN and linear SVM classifiers. ALAE demonstrates stability in performance across different classifiers, suggesting better disentanglement compared to other models. However, it does not consistently outperform all baselines, particularly with 1NN where AE(ℓ2) shows competitive accuracy.

Overall, the findings highlight the effectiveness of ALAE in generating representations that maintain a degree of disentanglement, which is crucial for tasks requiring linear separability in feature spaces.
