The paper discusses an experiment involving two sets of real images: the Source set and the Destination set. The goal is to transfer styles from the Source set to the Destination set, following a method similar to a prior study. Three different levels of styles are copied: coarse, middle, and fine. 

When coarse styles are copied, high-level attributes such as pose, general hair style, and face shape from the Source set are transferred, while finer details like colors and specific facial features are drawn from the Destination set. In contrast, copying middle styles focuses on smaller scale features, such as hair style and eye position, while retaining the pose and overall face shape from the Destination set. Lastly, copying fine styles primarily affects the color scheme and micro-level details, further blending the two sets. This structured approach allows for a nuanced analysis of style transfer in image processing.
