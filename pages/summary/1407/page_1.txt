In this paper, the authors present Objects365, a large-scale object detection dataset comprising 365 categories and over 600,000 training images, with more than 10 million manually labeled bounding boxes. This dataset is the largest of its kind, providing a challenging benchmark for the object detection community. The authors emphasize three key aspects: scale, quality, and generalization.

The scale of Objects365 significantly surpasses existing datasets like PASCAL VOC and COCO, offering five times more images, four times more categories, and ten times more bounding boxes than COCO. This extensive scale allows for more robust model training and evaluation.

Quality is ensured through a meticulously designed three-step annotation pipeline involving annotators, inspectors, and examiners, which enhances the reliability and efficiency of the annotation process. This approach minimizes ambiguities and maximizes annotation quality.

Generalization capabilities of models trained on Objects365 are demonstrated through improved performance on various localization-sensitive tasks, such as object detection and semantic segmentation. Pre-trained models on Objects365 outperform those trained on ImageNet by a notable margin, achieving a 5.6 point gain in accuracy on the COCO benchmark with fewer iterations. Additionally, finetuning time is reduced significantly, indicating better model efficiency.

The dataset and pre-trained models are publicly accessible at www.objects365.org, contributing valuable resources for further advancements in object detection research.
