The paper discusses the annotation process for the Objects365 dataset, which comprises 365 object categories and aims to enhance the efficiency and quality of image labeling. The dataset's statistics are compared with existing benchmarks, highlighting its size and comprehensive coverage.

The annotation pipeline is structured into three steps. The first step filters images through a two-class classification to identify non-iconic images that contain at least one object instance from eleven super-categories. The second step involves labeling these images with tags corresponding to the super-categories. In the final step, annotators focus on labeling object instances within a specific super-category using bounding boxes.

To ensure quality, the annotation team is divided into three roles: annotators, inspectors, and examiners. Annotators perform the initial labeling and must complete training and pass a test before starting. Inspectors review the annotations for errors, while examiners conduct a final review of batches of images. If any errors are found, the entire batch is rejected, impacting the income of the annotators and inspectors.

The paper also emphasizes the need for consistency across the annotation process due to the large number of annotators involved. To minimize discrepancies, clear classification and bounding-box rules are established to guide annotators in making uniform decisions when faced with ambiguities. The function-first principle is employed in classification to prioritize the most relevant label in cases of overlap. This systematic approach aims to enhance the overall quality and reliability of the dataset.
