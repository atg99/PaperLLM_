The paper presents an evaluation of the generalization ability of object detection and segmentation models trained on various pretraining datasets, specifically focusing on the performance improvements achieved by using the Objects365 dataset. The analysis employs the Feature Pyramid Network (FPN) with a ResNet50 backbone across several tasks, including object detection on the PASCAL VOC dataset, pedestrian detection on the CityPersons dataset, and semantic segmentation on PASCAL VOC and ADE datasets.

Key findings include:

1. **Object Detection on PASCAL VOC**: The model pretrained on Objects365 achieved a mean Average Precision (mAP) of 86.2, outperforming other pretraining datasets like ImageNet (80.2) and COCO (85.1). This indicates a significant improvement in classification and localization abilities.

2. **Pedestrian Detection on CityPersons**: The Objects365 pretrained model demonstrated a miss-rate (MR) of 12.12, which is notably lower than other pretraining datasets, indicating superior performance in detecting pedestrians by more than 4 points compared to the baseline.

3. **Semantic Segmentation on PASCAL VOC**: The model showed an mIOU of 76.7 when pretrained on Objects365, surpassing the performance of models pretrained on ImageNet (74.5) and OpenImages (74.1), highlighting the effectiveness of Objects365 for segmentation tasks.

4. **Semantic Segmentation on ADE**: The Objects365 model achieved a score of 42.9, providing a substantial advantage over the ImageNet (41.2) and COCO (41.5) pretrained models, further affirming its robustness across various tasks.

Overall, the results underscore the significant improvements in detection and segmentation tasks attributed to the rich diversity of object categories in the Objects365 dataset, establishing its value as a pretraining resource.
