The paper presents the Objects365 dataset, highlighting its annotation methodology and statistical characteristics. It introduces a function-first consistency rule for bounding box annotations to address ambiguities, ensuring that the largest relevant bounding box is used to avoid misclassification of object categories. For example, decorations on a clock must be included in the bounding box, while external features that may denote different categories should be excluded.

The dataset consists of approximately 740,000 annotated images, divided into training (600,000), validation (38,000), and testing (100,000) sets. Statistical analysis reveals that Objects365 is denser and more diverse compared to existing benchmarks like VOC, COCO, and OpenImages. On average, each image in Objects365 contains five object categories, indicating greater density. Additionally, the variance in object categories per image is significantly higher than in other datasets, demonstrating its diverse nature.

The paper also discusses image resolution, noting that Objects365 features larger and more varied resolutions. Comparisons with COCO and VOC reveal that Objects365 has more boxes per image (9.04 vs 7.34 for COCO) and a higher ratio of annotated areas to total image areas (63% vs 57% for COCO and 53% for VOC). This indicates a richer annotation quality in Objects365, making it a valuable resource for object detection research.
