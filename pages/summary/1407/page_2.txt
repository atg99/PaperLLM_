The paper discusses the limitations of using ImageNet pretraining for object detection and segmentation algorithms, particularly its lack of localization sensitivity and the initialization of the head part with random weights. To address these issues, the authors introduce the Objects365 dataset, which significantly outperforms ImageNet-based features in object detection tasks, even with less training time.

The paper reviews the evolution of object detection algorithms, highlighting the shift from traditional methods like DPM to deep learning approaches, which can be categorized into single-stage and two-stage detectors. Notable examples include SSD, YOLO, and Faster R-CNN, each contributing to the field's advancement.

The significance of large-scale datasets in improving object detection algorithms is emphasized, with Objects365 being notably larger than previous benchmarks like PASCAL VOC and COCO. The dataset comprises around 60 times more images than VOC and five times more than COCO, with a higher number of bounding boxes per image.

The Objects365 dataset was collected primarily from Flickr to ensure diversity and includes 442 categories, narrowed down to 365 target categories that align with existing benchmarks. Non-iconic images, which are not suitable for object detection, were filtered out to enhance the dataset's quality.

Overall, the paper presents the Objects365 dataset as a valuable resource for advancing object detection research, addressing the shortcomings of existing datasets and pretraining methods.
