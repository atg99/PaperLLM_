The paper discusses two fundamental attention units for handling different types of input features in a multimodal context: Self-Attention (SA) and Guided-Attention (GA). Both units utilize multi-head attention, which enhances representation capacity by processing multiple attention heads in parallel.

The SA unit operates on a single group of input features, denoted as X. It computes pairwise relationships among samples in X, producing attended features Z through a weighted summation of all instances in X. This process involves a multi-head attention layer followed by a feed-forward layer, with residual connections and layer normalization applied to improve optimization.

Conversely, the GA unit takes two groups of input features, X and Y, where Y guides the attention mechanism applied to X. This unit models the relationship between paired samples from both groups, allowing for cross-modal attention learning. The attended features for samples in X are reconstructed based on their similarities to samples in Y.

The paper also outlines the mathematical operations involved in computing the attention weights and the attended features, emphasizing the significance of multi-head attention in both units. It presents flowcharts for three variants of a multimodal context-aware (MCA) model designed for Visual Question Answering (VQA), illustrating the interplay between self-attention and guided-attention mechanisms.
