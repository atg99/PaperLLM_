The paper presents a Modular Co-Attention Networks (MCAN) architecture designed for Visual Question Answering (VQA). It introduces two primary strategies for deep co-attention learning: stacking and encoder-decoder models, which consist of multiple cascaded Modular Co-Attention (MCA) layers. These layers enhance the interaction between visual and textual features, allowing for more refined representations of both images and questions.

The architecture utilizes two basic attention units to create three MCA layers, enabling deep integration of multimodal features without loss of information. The ID(Y)-GA(X,Y) layer serves as a baseline, allowing for direct mapping of question features while modeling inter-modal interactions. Enhanced layers, such as SA(Y)-GA(X,Y) and SA(X)-SGA(X,Y), add intra-modal interaction capabilities, further improving feature representation.

Input representations are derived from visual features extracted using a Faster R-CNN model and GloVe word embeddings for textual data. The paper details the process of feature extraction, including the dynamic selection of visual objects based on a confidence threshold and the transformation of question words into vector embeddings processed through an LSTM network.

Finally, the MCAN architecture culminates in a simple multimodal fusion model, which combines the refined features before feeding them into a multi-label classifier to predict answers. The MCAN model variations are labeled as MCAN sk-L for stacking and MCAN ed-L for encoder-decoder strategies, where L indicates the number of MCA layers used. The overall flowchart and modular compositions are illustrated to provide clarity on the proposed methods.
