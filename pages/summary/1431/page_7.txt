The paper discusses the performance of different attention mechanisms in a multi-modal context, particularly focusing on the MCAN (Multi-modal Co-Attention Network) architecture. It presents visualizations of attention maps from various layers, illustrating the effectiveness of self-attention and guided attention in understanding and counting objects in images.

The encoder-decoder model consistently outperforms the stacking model, especially with larger layers, due to the accuracy of learned self-attention from the later units. The paper emphasizes that using an early self-attention unit directly in guided attention can negatively impact performance.

MCAN is noted for its parameter efficiency, achieving a 66.2% accuracy with 27 million parameters, compared to other methods with significantly higher parameters and lower accuracy. The analysis indicates that self-attention for images is crucial for tasks like object counting, with specific attention mechanisms showing marked improvements in performance.

Ablation studies reveal that pre-trained word embeddings (GloVe) yield better results than randomly initialized embeddings, and further refinements like fine-tuning and using LSTM networks for temporal information enhance performance.

Qualitative analysis of attention maps highlights the role of question self-attention, showing that early layers function as question classifiers while later layers focus on relevant keywords, such as "sheep," in the context of the task. The findings underscore the importance of attention mechanisms in effectively processing and interpreting multi-modal data.
