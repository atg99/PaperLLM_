This paper presents a novel approach to Visual Question Answering (VQA) through the introduction of a Deep Modular Co-Attention Network (MCAN). VQA requires a nuanced understanding of both visual content in images and textual content in questions, making it essential to develop effective co-attention models that link keywords in questions with relevant objects in images. Previous co-attention models have largely relied on shallow architectures, yielding limited improvements when deep learning methods were applied.

The proposed MCAN consists of cascaded Modular Co-Attention (MCA) layers, which perform self-attention on both questions and images, while also enabling question-guided attention on images. This modular architecture employs two basic attention units to enhance the learning process. The authors validate the effectiveness of MCAN through quantitative and qualitative evaluations on the VQA-v2 benchmark dataset, alongside extensive ablation studies to understand its performance.

Results indicate that MCAN significantly surpasses previous state-of-the-art models, achieving an overall accuracy of 70.63% on the test-dev set. The paper highlights the importance of co-attention mechanisms in VQA, emphasizing that the simultaneous learning of visual and textual attention can lead to more precise representations and improved answer predictions. Overall, this work contributes to the field of multimodal learning by advancing techniques for integrating vision and language in VQA tasks.
