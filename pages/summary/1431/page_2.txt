The paper discusses advancements in Visual Question Answering (VQA) systems, specifically focusing on the limitations of existing co-attention models that fail to adequately capture the complex interactions between image regions and question words. The authors note that traditional approaches often utilize global features for image representation, which can overlook critical local details necessary for accurate responses.

To address these limitations, two dense co-attention models, BAN and DCN, have been introduced to facilitate dense interactions between any image region and question word. While these models can be deepened for improved reasoning capabilities, they show minimal performance gains compared to shallow counterparts. The authors identify a key issue in these deep models: the lack of simultaneous modeling of dense self-attention within each modality.

Inspired by the Transformer model, the authors propose a Modular Co-Attention Network (MCAN) that incorporates two attention units: self-attention (SA) for intra-modal interactions and guided-attention (GA) for inter-modal interactions. By composing these units into Modular Co-Attention (MCA) layers, the MCAN achieves greater depth and significantly outperforms existing co-attention models on the VQA-v2 benchmark, showcasing the effectiveness of combining self-attention and guided-attention for improved multimodal reasoning.

The paper also reviews prior research on VQA, highlighting the evolution from simple multimodal fusion models to more sophisticated approaches that leverage visual attention mechanisms. Despite advancements, many earlier models still struggle with fine-grained interactions between question and image modalities, leading to the development of dense co-attention models that facilitate complete interaction and enhance performance. The Modular Co-Attention Layer is introduced as the foundational component of the MCAN, setting the stage for improved VQA capabilities.
