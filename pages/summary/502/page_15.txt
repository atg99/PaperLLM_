This study investigates the immunogenic potential of virus-like particles (VLPs) made from the recombinant core antigen of hepatitis B virus genotype G (HBc/G), which incorporates fragments of the SARS-CoV-2 Delta variant's spike (S) and nucleocapsid (N) proteins. The aim was to assess the neutralization ability of sera from mice immunized with HBc/G-RBM against a pseudotyped SARS-CoV-2 virus.

Results showed limited neutralization efficacy, with only two out of six individual mouse sera demonstrating statistically significant infection inhibition (15.47% and 11.86% reduction, p=0.0215 and p=0.0496, respectively). Notably, the overall pooled HBc/G-RBM sera did not exhibit significant neutralization compared to control groups (p=0.0936 and p=0.0874). Furthermore, the correlation between neutralization efficiency and anti-RBD titer was weak (Pearson coefficient r = -0.5847, p=0.2229).

The findings suggest that while some neutralizing effects were observed with fresh sera, these were not consistent after freezing and thawing, indicating that the potential for infection inhibition cannot be conclusively established in this study. The authors conclude that further improvements to the vaccine candidate are necessary to enhance its efficacy.
