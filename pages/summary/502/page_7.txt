The study focused on the characterization of purified HBc/G fusion proteins, which were analyzed for purity, nucleic acid content, and particle size. Techniques employed included SDS-PAGE, NAGE, electron microscopy, and dynamic light scattering.

Following characterization, BALB/c mice were immunized with various formulations of HBc/G proteins. Each group of six mice received subcutaneous injections of 25 µg of protein in a specific adjuvant at designated intervals. Serum was collected for analysis, while spleens were harvested post-sacrifice for further examination. Negative controls were established using sera from mice that only received the adjuvant.

Immunological responses were assessed through enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays (ELISA). Plates were coated with the appropriate proteins and incubated with diluted mouse sera to detect anti-RBM, anti-Bep, and anti-HBc/G titers. The study utilized specific protein solutions and a series of dilutions to determine the end-point titers through absorbance measurements.

Overall, this research contributes to the understanding of the immunogenicity and potential application of HBc/G fusion proteins in vaccine development, particularly against SARS-CoV-2.
