In this study, a modified HBc/G protein lacking the polyarginine domain was examined, revealing lower solubility and partial purification issues. The use of Addavax, a squalene-based adjuvant, facilitated enhanced antibody responses in BALB/c mice. Although the study did not produce exact virus-like particles (VLPs) from the fusion proteins with MIR insertions, significant B-cell responses were observed.

Flow cytometry was employed for detailed immunogenicity analysis, highlighting the presence of polyfunctional T-cells capable of secreting multiple cytokines. A notable difference in polyfunctional T-cell responses was observed in mice immunized with the HBc/G161-CTL fusion protein compared to controls, suggesting its protective potential. However, despite high antibody titers against the receptor-binding domain, the neutralization of SARS-CoV-2 was inadequate, indicating that high antibody levels alone do not guarantee protection. The study concludes that while the HBc/G161-CTL construct shows promise for eliciting strong immune responses, further exploration is needed to enhance its neutralization capacity and overall protective efficacy.
