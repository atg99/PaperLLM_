The paper details a method for the production and analysis of pseudotyped MLV-SARS-CoV-2 particles for neutralization and infection assays. Cells were transfected with specific plasmids using the calcium phosphate precipitation method. After transfection, the medium was refreshed, and the supernatant containing pseudoparticles was harvested, filtered, and stored at low temperatures.

For the neutralization and infection assays, genetically modified HEK293/T17-ACE2-TMPRSS2 cells were cultured and subsequently infected with the pseudotyped particles. Various dilutions of pooled sera from mice immunized with HBc/G-RBM protein were tested against the pseudoparticles, with controls including sera from other immunized mice and naïve sera. A specific polyclonal antibody served as a positive control for neutralization.

The process involved preparing serum dilutions, mixing them with thawed pseudoparticles, and incubating with the target cells. The transduction efficiency was assessed based on the percentage of GFP-positive cells, analyzed via flow cytometry. Neutralization percentages were calculated and presented using GraphPad Prism software, with statistical analyses performed using the Mann–Whitney U test and unpaired t-test for significance.

The study effectively outlines a protocol for assessing the neutralization capabilities of sera against SARS-CoV-2, providing a framework for further immunological investigations.
