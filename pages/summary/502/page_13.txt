The study investigates the immune responses elicited by fusion proteins containing receptor-binding motifs (RBM) or B-cell epitopes (Bep) inserted into the major immunogenic region (MIR) of the HBc/G protein in BALB/c mice. The results indicate that the average titers for anti-RBM and anti-Bep antibodies were 1:13,000 and 1:40,000, respectively, while the average titers for the HBc/G-RBM and HBc/G-Bep proteins were 1:57,000 and 1:44,000. In contrast, the control group immunized with pure HBc/G had an average anti-HBc titer of 1:105,000.

Additionally, the study assessed the cellular immune response by analyzing the production of cytokines IFN-γ, IL-2, and TNF-α in splenocytes from immunized mice. The analysis revealed a significant CTL response in mice immunized with the HBc/G161-CTL fusion protein, particularly in CD8+ T-cells, suggesting effective activation against SARS-CoV-2 CTL epitopes. In contrast, the HBc/G175-CTL protein did not induce a specific T-cell response, although T-cell activation against the carrier HBc/G was observed with both fusion proteins.

Overall, the findings demonstrate that the HBc/G161-CTL protein effectively generates both humoral and cellular immune responses against SARS-CoV-2, highlighting its potential as a candidate for vaccine development.
