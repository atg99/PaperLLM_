This paper discusses the development of vaccine candidates utilizing HBc/G fusion proteins as carriers for SARS-CoV-2 antigens. The authors focus on inserting specific epitopes from the nucleocapsid (N) and spike (S) proteins of the virus into HBc/G to enhance immune responses. They reference previous studies that identified immunodominant B-cell and cytotoxic T lymphocyte (CTL) epitopes, particularly from the N protein, which showed promise for inducing T-cell responses.

In their methodology, the authors selected a 66-amino acid fragment of the receptor-binding motif (RBM) from the S protein and a B-cell epitope from the N protein for insertion into the major immunodominant region (MIR) of HBc/G. Additionally, five conserved CTL epitopes from the N protein were designed as a C-terminal extension. The construction of expression vectors for these fusion proteins was performed using an E. coli system.

The paper highlights the importance of the MIR for effective antigen presentation and the need for evaluating the competence of chimeric constructs, as different inserts may exhibit distinct structural properties. Overall, the study aims to contribute to the design of effective vaccines against SARS-CoV-2 by leveraging the structural and immunological characteristics of the selected epitopes.
