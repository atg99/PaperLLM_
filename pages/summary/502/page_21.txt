The referenced papers focus on various aspects of vaccine development against SARS-CoV-2, highlighting innovative approaches and findings in the field. Key contributions include the development of virus-like particles (VLPs) as vaccine candidates, notably those incorporating receptor-binding motif (RBM) domains to enhance immunogenicity. Studies demonstrate that these VLPs can effectively stimulate polyclonal memory responses capable of neutralizing variants of concern.

Research emphasizes the nucleocapsid protein of SARS-CoV-2 as a significant target for vaccine development, with studies indicating that it elicits robust immune responses. The potential of immunoinformatics to identify epitopes in structural proteins is also explored, providing insights into vaccine targets.

Additionally, the papers discuss the importance of T cell immunity, showing that memory T cell responses can persist long after infection, which is crucial for long-term immunity. The structural basis of receptor recognition by SARS-CoV-2 and the design of fusion protein linkers for enhanced delivery of vaccine components are further highlighted.

Overall, this body of work underscores ongoing efforts to develop effective vaccines against COVID-19, leveraging advanced techniques in immunology and structural biology.
