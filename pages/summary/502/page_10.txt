Results from the study focused on the expression and purification of HBc/G fusion proteins. Four designed proteins were expressed under the T7 late promoter, revealing that MIR-derived proteins had consistent expression levels in different media, while C-terminally modified proteins showed increased expression with IPTG induction. 

The localization analysis indicated that the majority of HBc/G-RBM and HBc/G-Bep proteins were found in the cell debris after soft lysis or ultrasonic disintegration, with some solubility observed in HBc/G-RBM. For the C-terminally modified proteins, solubility was noted, particularly for HBc/G175-CTL, which was predominantly in the supernatant.

The purification process for HBc/G-RBM and HBc/G-Bep involved stepwise extraction using increasing urea concentrations, followed by dialysis to promote refolding. Results showed that HBc/G-RBM was present in both soluble and insoluble fractions after dialysis, while HBc/G-Bep was primarily found in the soluble fraction. Dynamic light scattering (DLS) and electron microscopy (EM) analyses indicated that both proteins, post-refolding, formed aggregated particles, with HBc/G-Bep exhibiting better homogeneity and reduced aggregation compared to HBc/G-RBM.
