The study evaluated the immune responses in BALB/c mice immunized with fusion proteins containing receptor binding motifs (RBM) or B cell epitopes (Bep) inserted into the core protein HBc/G. The results indicated significant B-cell responses, with high titers of anti-RBM and anti-Bep antibodies observed post-immunization. The average titers for the HBc/G-RBM and HBc/G-Bep groups were 1:13,000 and 1:40,000, respectively, compared to a 1:105,000 average titer for the control group.

Cytotoxic T lymphocyte (CTL) responses were assessed by measuring cytokine production (IFN-γ, IL-2, TNF-α) from splenocytes. The data revealed a notable presence of these cytokines in both CD4+ and CD8+ T-cells from immunized mice when stimulated with specific antigens. 

Additionally, the neutralization capability of sera from immunized mice against SARS-CoV-2 pseudotyped particles was analyzed. While the pooled sera showed weak but statistically significant neutralization at a dilution of 1:100, further dilutions led to insignificant results. Notably, individual samples from the HBc/G-RBM group exhibited enhanced neutralization, particularly sample 6, which indicated a significant ability to inhibit viral infection.

In conclusion, the study highlights the potential of HBc/G fusion proteins containing RBM and Bep to elicit strong humoral and cellular immune responses, suggesting their promise as candidates for effective vaccines against SARS-CoV-2.
