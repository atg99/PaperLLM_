This paper discusses the development of chimeric virus-like particles (VLPs) using the hepatitis B core (HBc) protein as a carrier for SARS-CoV-2 antigens, focusing on the insertion of specific epitopes to enhance immunogenicity. The study highlights the use of flexible GS linkers to improve the stability and folding of the inserted receptor-binding motif (RBM) of the SARS-CoV-2 spike protein, as well as a string of cytotoxic T lymphocyte (CTL) epitopes linked by AAY linkers. 

Despite the promise of these constructs, both the HBc/G fusion proteins containing the RBM and B-cell epitopes were initially found to be insoluble, necessitating denaturation and refolding processes for purification. The research also references two previous studies on genetically engineered HBc-based vaccines, which similarly encountered issues with insoluble proteins and utilized urea extraction methods.

The authors report that their HBc/G175-CTL fusion protein formed homogeneous VLPs, confirming its potential as a vaccine candidate. Size analysis indicated that these VLPs were larger than unmodified HBc/G VLPs, suggesting successful incorporation of the CTL epitopes. Overall, the findings underscore the challenges and advancements in creating effective chimeric VLP vaccines for SARS-CoV-2 using HBc protein.
