The paper discusses the expression and analysis of HBc/G fusion proteins using various expression vectors and E. coli strains. The study outlines the construction of plasmids containing sequences for the HBc/G protein and various inserted sequences such as the receptor binding motif (RBM), B-cell epitope (Bep), and cytotoxic T lymphocyte (CTL) epitopes. 

Table 1 details the expression vectors used, including the pET28-based vectors for different fusion proteins, specifying the amino acid lengths and insertion sites within the HBc/G protein. 

The methodology describes the transformation of E. coli XL1 cells with the designed plasmids, followed by the selection of transformed clones on LB agar with kanamycin. The E. coli BL21(DE3) strain is then used for protein expression, with cultures grown in either rich medium or auto-induction growth medium. The conditions for protein expression include the addition of IPTG to induce protein synthesis, with samples taken at various time points for analysis.

For the expression analysis, cell samples are processed and subjected to SDS-PAGE to evaluate the solubility and presence of the fusion proteins. The paper provides a systematic approach to producing and analyzing these recombinant proteins, which may have implications for vaccine development or therapeutic applications.
