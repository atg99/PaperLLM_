The paper discusses the design and construction of expression vectors for HBc/G fusion proteins incorporating various SARS-CoV-2 epitopes. Key elements include the insertion of the receptor binding motif (RBM) from the S protein and a B-cell epitope peptide from the N protein into the HBc/G sequence. The B-cell epitope corresponds to a sequence from the Delta variant of SARS-CoV-2. Additionally, five conserved cytotoxic T lymphocyte (CTL) epitopes from the N protein were selected and linked to create a CTL epitope string for further insertion into truncated HBc/G proteins.

The construction involved using the pET-28a(+) plasmid to create vectors such as pET28-RBM and pET28-Bep, with specific insertion sites for the RBM and B-cell epitope between amino acids 90 and 91. Two additional vectors, pET28-CTL161 and pET28-CTL175, incorporated the CTL epitope string after the 161st and 175th amino acids, respectively. The expression of these constructs was driven by a strong bacteriophage T7 promoter, ensuring effective protein production in E. coli.

The paper highlights the significance of these fusion proteins in vaccine development against SARS-CoV-2, providing a detailed overview of the vectors used and the rationale behind the chosen epitopes, which are critical for eliciting immune responses.
