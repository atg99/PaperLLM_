The paper discusses the development of a vaccine against SARS-CoV-2 utilizing virus-like particles (VLPs) as a carrier system. Specifically, it explores the use of the recombinant core antigen from Hepatitis B virus genotype G (HBc/G) to present peptides derived from the spike receptor binding motif (RBM) and nucleocapsid protein of the SARS-CoV-2 Delta variant. The authors highlight the effectiveness of the E. coli expression system for producing HBc VLPs and detail modifications made to the HBc/G protein to enhance its immunogenicity.

Key findings indicate that the modified HBc/G proteins, despite being insoluble initially, were successfully solubilized and refolded, leading to the generation of high levels of antibodies in BALB/c mice. These antibodies showed specific recognition of both eukaryotic and bacterial expressed SARS-CoV-2 proteins. Additionally, the paper reports on the purification of CTL-epitope-containing proteins as VLPs and the analysis of T-cell responses, noting that only a variant lacking a polyarginine domain effectively activated T-cells.

The research underscores the potential of HBc/G as a carrier for SARS-CoV-2 antigens, aiming to contribute to the ongoing efforts to create effective vaccines amid the continuing global health crisis posed by COVID-19. The study emphasizes the necessity for adaptable and economically viable vaccine solutions in the face of evolving viral variants.
