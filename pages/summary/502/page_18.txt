This paper presents a novel approach to developing SARS-CoV-2 vaccines through the design of recombinant proteins based on the HBc/G antigen, which incorporates specific epitopes from the SARS-CoV-2 spike protein. The study highlights the potential of the recombinant RBM-containing protein to elicit specific antibodies with neutralizing activity in HEK293/T17-ACE2-TMPRSS2 cells. The authors reference Bai's suggestion that integrating SARS-CoV-2 N antigen epitopes could enhance the immunogenicity of future vaccine candidates. 

Utilizing their prior experience with multivalent HBc/D VLPs containing HBV and HCV epitopes, the researchers plan to combine B- and T-cell epitopes from SARS-CoV-2 within a single HBc protein molecule. The findings demonstrate the production of these recombinant proteins in E. coli and their immunological characterization, with initial tests conducted using a pseudotyped MLV/SARS-CoV-2 system to evaluate the virus neutralization efficiency of sera from immunized BALB/c mice. 

The study concludes that the next step involves validating the most promising vaccine candidates using K18-hACE2 mice, emphasizing the potential for future research in this area. Supplementary materials include detailed methods and results from various chromatography techniques used to analyze the expression and purification of the HBc/G fusion proteins.
