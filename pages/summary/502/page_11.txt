The paper investigates the purification and characterization of HBc/G fusion proteins, specifically HBc/G-RBM and HBc/G-Bep, using dynamic light scattering (DLS) and transmission electron microscopy (EM). The DLS results indicate that HBc/G-RBM has an average particle diameter ranging from 924 to 1077 nm, while HBc/G-Bep exhibits a smaller average diameter of 164 to 170 nm. Both proteins demonstrate good homogeneity and reduced aggregation levels.

The purification process for the HBc/G175-CTL and HBc/G161-CTL proteins involves gel filtration and ion exchange chromatography. The HBc/G175-CTL protein showed solubility post-ultrasonic disintegration, allowing it to be purified through established protocols. Conversely, the HBc/G161-CTL protein was only partially soluble, necessitating direct loading onto a gel filtration column. Analysis revealed that the first fractions contained aggregates, while subsequent fractions contained VLP-like structures with minimal nucleic acid presence. Consequently, ion exchange chromatography was deemed unnecessary for HBc/G161-CTL. The final concentrated protein from gel filtration displayed capsid-like structures of high homogeneity. These findings contribute to the understanding of HBc/G fusion proteins and their potential applications in vaccine development.
