The paper outlines methods for assessing immune responses to SARS-CoV-2, focusing on the detection of epitope-specific T-cells and neutralization assays using pseudotyped retroviruses. 

For T-cell analysis, splenocytes were isolated and cryopreserved, then thawed and stimulated with a mix of SARS-CoV-2 N protein CTL epitopes or recombinant proteins. The study employed flow cytometry to evaluate T-cell responses, using specific peptides for stimulation and mitogenic agents as controls. Key markers for T-cell identification and cytokine production were analyzed using a FACSAria II cytometer.

Additionally, the paper details the process for generating SARS-CoV-2 pseudoparticles, which involved using plasmids for murine leukemia virus packaging and encoding the SARS-CoV-2 spike protein. HEK 293-T/17 cells were cultured for the production of these pseudoparticles, serving as a basis for further neutralization assays.

Overall, the methods described aim to facilitate the assessment of T-cell immunity and the effectiveness of vaccines against SARS-CoV-2, contributing to the understanding of immune responses in the context of viral infections.
