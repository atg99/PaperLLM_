SARS-CoV-2 vaccines, which utilize parts or the entirety of the spike (S) protein, can be developed using various platforms, including virus-like particles (VLPs). VLPs are notable for enhancing immune responses due to their ability to display antigens effectively. They can stimulate both the innate and adaptive immune systems, inducing robust humoral and cellular immunity without the need for adjuvants in some cases. Examples of successful VLP-based vaccines include those for hepatitis B and human papillomavirus, which have shown high efficacy and safety profiles.

Currently, COVIFENZ, a plant-based VLP vaccine developed by Medicago Inc., is the only VLP vaccine specifically targeting COVID-19. It demonstrated efficacy rates of 71% overall, with higher efficacy against specific variants. However, production was halted due to market competition. As of March 2023, VLPs remain a focus in vaccine development, with several candidates in clinical and pre-clinical stages targeting the receptor-binding domain (RBD) of the S protein, which is crucial for neutralizing antibodies. The use of HBV core protein (HBc) as a VLP carrier has proven effective due to its high-level expression and self-assembly capabilities in various systems. Understanding the immunogenicity and neutralization potential of specific epitopes, particularly in the RBD, is essential for developing effective vaccines against SARS-CoV-2.
