The study investigates the solubility of target proteins expressed in cells using two different lysis methods: soft lysis (SL) and ultrasound disintegration (USD). For SL, cells were lysed in a buffer containing Tris-HCl, EDTA, PMSF, lysozyme, MgCl2, DNase I, and urea, followed by gentle stirring and centrifugation. In contrast, USD involved suspending cells in a specific lysis buffer and treating them with ultrasound before adding urea, DNase I, and MgCl2, followed by incubation and centrifugation.

The purification of fusion proteins containing the receptor binding motif (RBM) or B-cell epitope (Bep) was performed after cell cultivation, lysis, and clarification. The proteins underwent a series of wash and extraction steps with varying urea concentrations to aid in solubilization. The supernatants from these extractions were dialyzed against phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) to gradually remove urea.

Additionally, the study details the purification of fusion proteins with a cytotoxic T lymphocyte (CTL) epitope string at the C-terminus of truncated HBc/G proteins. Cells were lysed and clarified, followed by ammonium sulfate precipitation, gel filtration chromatography, and ion-exchange chromatography to isolate the desired proteins. The methodology emphasizes the importance of optimizing lysis and purification conditions for efficient recovery of functional proteins.
