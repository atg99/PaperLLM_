The paper discusses the immunological responses elicited by various fusion proteins in mice, specifically HBc/G175-CTL, HBc/G161-CTL, and HBc/G-Bep, in comparison to a control group immunized with Addavax. The study involved measuring the production of cytokines by CD4+ and CD8+ T-cells following stimulation with SARS-CoV-2 CTL peptides and unmodified recombinant HBc/G protein. Statistical analysis was performed using the Mann–Whitney U test, revealing no significant differences between the groups.

Additionally, the research included an evaluation of neutralization capabilities against a pseudotyped SARS-CoV-2 virus using serum from mice immunized with HBc/G-RBM protein. Flow cytometry was utilized to assess infection inhibition, with a positive control provided by commercially available neutralizing antibodies. A correlation analysis between antibody titers and neutralization efficacy was also conducted.

The study was conceptualized by I.S. and I.P., with contributions from various authors in methodology, investigation, and writing. The research was funded by the Latvian Council of Science, and all authors declared no conflicts of interest. Acknowledgments were given for technical assistance and protein provision.

Overall, the findings contribute to understanding the immune response generated by different vaccine candidates against SARS-CoV-2, emphasizing the need for further exploration in vaccine development.
