The paper presents a comprehensive analysis of the secure evaluation of quantized neural networks, specifically focusing on the MobileNets family. The data in Table 3 outlines the running times, measured in seconds, for different network configurations under various security settings, including passive and active security for both honest and dishonest majorities. The results demonstrate the impact of network width and resolution multipliers on evaluation times and accuracy metrics, such as Top-1 and Top-5 accuracy.

The study distinguishes between probabilistic truncation and exact rounding methods, noting their effects on performance. It emphasizes the significance of the corruption threshold as a critical factor influencing evaluation times, particularly highlighting the stark contrast between protocols with honest and dishonest majorities. This discrepancy can lead to evaluation times being up to 200 times longer in scenarios with dishonest majorities, primarily due to the extensive pre-processing required in these cases.

Overall, the paper underscores the challenges and considerations in securely evaluating neural networks, particularly in terms of the trade-offs between security measures and computational efficiency. The findings contribute valuable insights into the optimal configurations for deploying quantized neural networks in secure environments.
