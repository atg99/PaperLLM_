The paper presents a detailed analysis of the communication complexity involved in the secure evaluation of quantized neural networks, specifically focusing on the MobileNets family. The data is organized by different model variants, characterized by width and resolution multipliers, and evaluates their performance based on accuracy metrics such as Top-1 and Top-5 accuracy.

Key findings indicate that as the model's parameters increase, both accuracy and communication complexity also rise. For example, the variant with a width multiplier of 1.0 and a resolution multiplier of 224 achieves a Top-1 accuracy of 70.0% and a Top-5 accuracy of 89.0%, with a communication complexity of 4356.2 Gigabytes under passive security conditions. Conversely, the same model under active security with a dishonest majority condition sees a significant increase in communication complexity, reaching up to 37798.3 Gigabytes.

Additionally, the paper includes timing benchmarks, revealing a range from 110 seconds for passive honest-majority computations to 28,000 seconds for active dishonest-majority computations, highlighting the substantial impact of the security model on performance.

Overall, the results underscore the trade-offs between accuracy, communication overhead, and security in the context of quantized neural networks, providing valuable insights for future research and application in secure machine learning.
