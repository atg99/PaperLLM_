The paper discusses the secure evaluation of quantized neural networks, focusing on the complexities of truncation in secure multiparty computation (MPC). It highlights the challenges of fixed-point arithmetic, where the multiplication of two fixed-point numbers necessitates a non-linear truncation operation, complicating secure computations. 

The authors introduce a subprotocol for truncation based on a public value, which simplifies the process by approximating rational numbers through probabilistic truncation. This method introduces a small error, which is biased towards the nearest integer, resulting in minimal impact on neural network accuracy. The authors argue that probabilistic truncation protocols outperform deterministic ones due to reduced reliance on complex binary operations.

Furthermore, the paper presents a novel method for secure truncation over the ring Z2k, addressing challenges posed by the inability to simply divide by powers of two in this setting. The proposed protocol requires only a one-bit increase in share size relative to secrets and maintains a constant number of rounds, yielding a manageable error rate.

An additional improvement for scenarios with three parties and passive security is also proposed. This optimization allows for efficient random value generation and enhances the overall performance of the truncation protocol. The authors emphasize the practical benefits of their approach while acknowledging the need for further experimental verification of accuracy impacts.
