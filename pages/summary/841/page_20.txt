It reviews prior work in the field, particularly focusing on homomorphic encryption (HE) and garbled circuits (GC) as methods for secure computation. The paper highlights that early efforts, such as those by Orlandi et al., laid the groundwork for secure neural network evaluation using HE, but faced limitations like inefficiency and the inability to handle common activation functions.

CryptoNets and subsequent improvements, such as those by Chabanne et al. and Bourse et al., are mentioned for their attempts to enhance network evaluation through HE techniques. However, these methods struggle with activation functions and complexity. Gazelle proposes a hybrid approach combining GC with additive HE for better efficiency, while MiniONN and Chameleon further explore mixed protocol techniques for secure evaluations.

The paper emphasizes the significance of using different protocols to optimize prediction times and showcases DeepSecure as an early GC-based solution. XONN is highlighted for its efficient handling of binarized networks, demonstrating the feasibility of evaluating deep networks. Additionally, the work references SecureML, which employs a three-party protocol for secure evaluations, noting improvements in performance through effective arithmetic handling.

Overall, the paper provides a comprehensive overview of advancements in secure inference for neural networks, focusing on the challenges and innovations in the field.
