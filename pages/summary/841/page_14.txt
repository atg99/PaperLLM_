The paper discusses the secure evaluation of quantized neural networks, specifically focusing on the efficiency of sum-of-products operations in the context of secure multi-party computation protocols. It presents empirical results on runtime and communication costs associated with various protocols, comparing both passive and active security models.

Table 2 summarizes the performance metrics for running variable numbers of sum-of-products, revealing significant differences in runtime and communication overhead. For instance, as the number of terms in the sum-of-products increases, the active security protocol shows a more pronounced increase in runtime compared to passive security. The paper highlights that the main efficiency gains are achieved because the dot-product operations are effectively free in certain protocols.

Additionally, the paper evaluates 16 pre-trained MobileNet V1 models under varying conditions, including different corruption thresholds and security models. The results indicate that the performance varies significantly depending on the security model used, emphasizing the importance of selecting appropriate protocols based on the operational context.

Overall, the findings underscore the trade-offs between security and computational efficiency in the secure evaluation of neural networks, providing insights into optimizing these processes for practical applications. The authors also share their implementation through the MP-SPDZ framework, facilitating further research and application in the field.
