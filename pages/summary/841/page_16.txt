The paper discusses the secure evaluation of quantized neural networks, highlighting the efficiency and performance of different security models and algebraic structures used in multi-party computation (MPC). It notes that transitioning between corruption models incurs a relatively low overhead, increasing inference time by a factor of 3 to 30 when moving from passive to active security. 

The authors observe that protocols based on rings typically outperform those based on fields in passive security scenarios, while the opposite is true for active security. This discrepancy is attributed to the use of homomorphic encryption with fields and oblivious transfer with rings, with the latter requiring more communication. The paper also emphasizes that ring operations can be performed with smaller bit sizes, enhancing performance.

A less precise truncation method is proposed to speed up inference, achieving efficiency boosts of 15% to 80% at the potential cost of accuracy. The study evaluates the scaling of protocols with multiple parties, noting that the simple network scales differently based on the corruption model used.

The paper benchmarks its implementation against CrypTFlow, demonstrating that their special truncation method consistently outperforms CrypTFlow, especially in the context of secure operations. Special truncation reduces communication costs significantly, making it a more efficient choice compared to traditional methods that require quadratic communication overhead.

In conclusion, the authors affirm that it is feasible to securely evaluate complex networks, such as those used in ImageNet, using existing MPC protocols. They emphasize that the networks can be trained using standard frameworks like TensorFlow, PyTorch, or MXNet, making this approach accessible to end users.
