The paper discusses the secure evaluation of quantized neural networks, focusing on the quantization scheme and its application to core operations like dot products in convolutional neural networks (CNNs). The quantization process involves defining parameters m and z during training to ensure that the range of tensor entries is appropriately captured. This process is illustrated with a visualization that shows how continuous intervals are mapped to discrete values, which can lead to multiple numbers mapping to the same integer due to rounding.

In the context of dot products, two vectors are represented with their respective quantization parameters. The paper outlines how to compute the dot product using integer-only arithmetic and fixed-point multiplication, which is essential for maintaining efficiency in quantized networks. It derives an approximation for the dot product result and explains how to handle potential rounding errors that may occur during calculations. The result is clamped to ensure it remains within the appropriate interval.

The addition of bias in CNNs is also addressed, where the bias is quantized and incorporated into the existing summation without altering the established arithmetic framework. Furthermore, the paper mentions that operations involving layers like ReLU and max pooling can be implemented directly on quantized values, maintaining consistency across quantization parameters.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of correctly managing quantization during neural network operations to ensure accurate and efficient evaluations.
