The paper discusses the secure evaluation of quantized neural networks, particularly focusing on the implementation of these networks using multiparty computation (MPC). It begins by explaining how quantized operations, such as the ReLU6 activation function, can be efficiently integrated into the computation process. By selecting appropriate quantization parameters, the paper demonstrates that clamping operations can be effectively managed within the quantized framework.

The authors then outline the system and threat model for secure CNN evaluation. In this model, both the model owner and data owner share their inputs with a set of servers, typically two or three, depending on the security requirements. The servers execute a secure MPC protocol to evaluate the quantized model on the provided input while maintaining the privacy of sensitive information.

The paper emphasizes the flexibility of their approach, noting that various secret-sharing-based MPC protocols can be utilized. It discusses the implications of different corruption thresholds and the algebraic structures involved in the computation. The client-server model presented is also shown to be compatible with reactive computation, allowing outputs to be efficiently used in subsequent operations. Overall, the paper provides a comprehensive framework for secure inference in quantized neural networks using MPC, highlighting the practicality and robustness of the proposed methods.
