The paper discusses the secure evaluation of quantized neural networks, particularly focusing on Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) and their architecture. A CNN consists of layers that transform input data, specifically RGB images of size 128x128 pixels, into output classes, typically 1000 for ImageNet models. The primary operation within each layer involves a convolution followed by a Rectified Linear Unit (ReLU), expressed mathematically as the maximum of the linear transformation of input data.

To reduce the spatial dimensions of the output, pooling operations, such as average pooling, are employed. Additionally, batch normalization is introduced to accelerate training by normalizing layer inputs. During inference, batch normalization parameters can be integrated into the weights, simplifying the computation.

The paper highlights a quantization technique by Jacob et al., which aims to enable efficient secure inference. Although not the most advanced method, it is chosen due to its implementation in TensorFlow Lite, facilitating user-friendly model training and access to pre-trained ImageNet models. This approach is beneficial for multiparty computation (MPC) as it simplifies the arithmetic operations in CNN evaluations. The quantization process is defined for both 8-bit and 16-bit integers, with a focus on the former. It introduces a scale and zero-point to transform data into a quantized format, emphasizing that this quantization occurs on a per-tensor basis. 

Overall, the paper provides a foundational understanding of CNN architecture, the importance of quantization in secure inference, and the specific methodologies employed for efficient evaluation.
