The paper discusses the secure evaluation of quantized neural networks, highlighting advancements in secure multi-party computation (MPC) protocols. It introduces SecureNN, which extends SecureML by employing three- and four-party protocols, allowing secure operations even with one party compromised. CrypTFlow builds on SecureNN, enabling the evaluation of large networks while supporting standard TensorFlow models.

The paper categorizes MPC protocols into two main types: dishonest majority and honest majority. 

In the dishonest majority setting, protocols are more complex and often rely on additive secret sharing and authentication tags for active security. Notable protocols include SPDZ2k, which uses multiplication triples and oblivious transfer, and OTSemi2k and OTSemiPrime, which simplify SPDZ2k and MASCOT by omitting authentication tags.

For the honest majority scenario, protocols typically use Shamir or replicated secret sharing. The paper details several protocols, such as Replicated2k and ReplicatedPrime, which efficiently share values among three parties. PsReplicatedPrime and PsReplicated2k extend these protocols to achieve active security.

The extended results section presents communication costs for different protocols when evaluating ImageNet models, indicating that dishonest majority protocols require significantly more preprocessing than honest majority protocols. It also notes that protocols over finite fields are more communication-efficient compared to those over rings.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of secure evaluation methods for neural networks and provides a comprehensive overview of existing MPC protocols, their complexities, and their communication requirements.
