The paper discusses the secure evaluation of quantized neural networks, emphasizing the differences from traditional multiparty computation. It highlights that model owners keep weights in clear text, enabling computations that rely solely on additions and subtractions. The work references XONN, which employs a quantization scheme converting weights into bits, requiring an increase in the number of neurons. CrypTFlow is mentioned for its custom protocol that converts floating-point weights from TensorFlow models into fixed-point representations without sacrificing accuracy.

The paper introduces frameworks for secure evaluation, notably MiniONN and CrypTFlow, which facilitate the conversion of existing models for secure evaluation. MiniONN demonstrates its capabilities across various problem domains, while CrypTFlow lays out a protocol based on SecureNN for secure inference.

It further elaborates on deep learning and quantization, noting that while deep learning models excel in tasks like computer vision and natural language processing, their deployment on resource-constrained devices is hindered by high storage and energy demands. Quantization techniques that allow weights to be represented with lower-width integers, sometimes down to 1 bit, have been developed to address these constraints while maintaining accuracy comparable to full-precision models.

The paper includes notation definitions for values and clamping operations, setting the stage for a detailed exploration of secure inference protocols, implementation details, and benchmarks in subsequent sections.
