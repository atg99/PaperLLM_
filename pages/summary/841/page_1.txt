In this paper, the authors Anders Dalskov, Daniel Escudero, and Marcel Keller explore the compatibility of secure multi-party computation (MPC) with quantized neural networks (NNs) and assess the support for MPC-friendly models in major machine learning frameworks like TensorFlow and PyTorch. They highlight that existing protocols often rely on fixed-point integers and specialized activation functions, which are not well-supported by these frameworks, complicating the secure evaluation of models designed using them.

The authors also examine the capabilities of general-purpose MPC frameworks, noting that these frameworks are better documented and more adaptable to varying threat models compared to existing secure evaluation protocols that are often limited in scope and practical implementation. They conclude that quantization techniques in TensorFlow, PyTorch, and MXNet can yield models suitable for secure evaluation through general-purpose MPC frameworks.

Extensive benchmarks are conducted to analyze trade-offs between different corruption models, network sizes, and evaluation efficiency, revealing insights into the costs associated with active versus passive security and honest versus dishonest majority scenarios. The findings suggest that the gap between existing machine learning frameworks and MPC protocols may be less significant than previously thought.

The paper includes updated figures illustrating the full implementation of inference for various networks in MPC and acknowledges contributions from various individuals and institutions, while also noting a change in authorship. The research is supported by grants from the European Research Council and the Danish Independent Research Council, among others.
