The paper discusses a secure evaluation framework for quantized neural networks, particularly focusing on the secure computation of quantized convolutional neural networks (CNNs). It begins by establishing the security of the truncation protocol, which ensures that the operation does not leak information while producing correct shares. The authors highlight the importance of the bit-length of the modulus in secret sharing schemes to prevent overflow during computations.

Quantized CNNs utilize weight tensors that are associated with scale and zero-point values, allowing the conversion of quantized 8-bit integers into floating-point numbers. The model owner shares these quantized weights and biases securely with participating servers. The computation of dot products in the network involves fixed-point multiplication, and the authors outline a method to securely compute the necessary expressions using shares of zero points, quantized inputs, and scales.

The evaluation process involves several steps, including computing the dot products, performing secure multiplications, and applying truncation protocols. The final result undergoes local addition and clamping to ensure it remains within specified limits. The paper also addresses other layers, such as average pooling and max pooling, by utilizing secure arithmetic and comparison protocols.

For the output layer, the authors propose techniques to compute the argmax of the output vector securely, which allows the identification of the most probable label without revealing the entire prediction vector. This approach maintains confidentiality while enabling effective decision-making in neural network predictions. Overall, the paper presents a comprehensive framework for securely evaluating quantized neural networks, emphasizing the significance of secure multiparty computation techniques in this context.
