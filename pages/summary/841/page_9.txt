The paper discusses the secure evaluation of quantized neural networks through the use of various multi-party computation (MPC) protocols. It categorizes these protocols based on their security levels—passive versus active—and the arithmetic properties they utilize, such as computations modulo a prime or a power of two. The authors focus on two scenarios: honest majority, where fewer than half of the parties can be corrupted, and dishonest majority, where at least one party must remain honest. The paper notes that protocols designed for an honest majority are generally simpler and more efficient than those for a dishonest majority.

The authors highlight the importance of multiplication protocols and their limitations in expressing certain functionalities. They emphasize that the quantization scheme by Jacob et al. allows for the majority of operations in evaluating a quantized neural network to be represented as additions and multiplications, which are supported by the selected MPC protocols. However, the evaluation process also involves non-arithmetic operations such as truncations and comparisons, which necessitate specialized protocols.

The paper presents secure comparison as a crucial primitive, enabling parties to compare secret-shared values without revealing the inputs themselves. It suggests that existing proposals for secure comparison can be utilized, including efficient methods specifically for replicated secret sharing over Z2k. The authors argue that the ability to perform secure comparisons is essential for integrating quantized models into their secure evaluation framework.

Overall, the work outlines a comprehensive approach to secure computation in the context of quantized neural networks, detailing the necessary protocols and primitives while addressing the complexities of secure operations in multi-party settings.
