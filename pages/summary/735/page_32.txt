The paper discusses the dynamics and control of a double-pendulum system, providing a mathematical framework for modeling its motion. Key equations are presented, including the mass matrix, gravitational forces, Coriolis forces, and frictional forces, which are essential for understanding the system's behavior. The model considers two links with defined lengths, masses, and moments of inertia, and incorporates gravitational acceleration.

The goal state for the system is defined as q=[π/2,0], with a reward function designed to measure the squared distance from this goal while including a penalty for the magnitude of the action. The learning process involves collecting data through six episodes of 500 steps each, acknowledging that the double-pendulum requires more steps than a single-pendulum for effective training. The policy evaluation is performed over 1000 episodes.

In terms of the learning setup, the paper employs an actor-critic approach similar to that used for a single-pendulum, with specific adjustments: the use of 300 Fourier features, a policy characterized by a full covariance matrix, and a regularization coefficient for temporal difference learning initialized at 0.1.

Furthermore, the experiments utilize the OpenAI Gym with the MuJoCo physics simulator, ensuring consistent hyperparameters and neural network architecture across various continuous control tasks. The performance of the algorithms is evaluated through multiple trials with different random seeds to ensure robustness.
