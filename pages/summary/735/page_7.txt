The paper discusses modifications to improve the optimization of reinforcement learning objectives, particularly focusing on constraints related to state-action pairs. 

The first modification introduces a weighted objective function, L(θ,η), that integrates expected values over state-action pairs while penalizing deviations from the constraints. The weights, b(s,a), are crucial as they correspond to the importance of each constraint.

The second modification addresses the computational challenge of integrating over the entire state-action space by narrowing down the focus to a subset of constraints. This is achieved by sampling candidates based on the state distribution μπ(s) and the current policy π(a|s;θ), allowing for a more manageable calculation of the objective function.

The third modification tackles the double-sampling issue, which arises from needing independent samples to estimate expectations within a squared term. The authors propose pulling the expectation over transition probabilities, P(s′|s,a), outside the square. This leads to a new form of the objective function that utilizes the temporal difference (TD) error, δQ(s,a,s′;θ,ω), which can be estimated using TD methods, thus resolving the sampling complications.

Overall, these modifications aim to enhance the efficacy and feasibility of reinforcement learning algorithms by refining the handling of constraints and sampling methods.
