The paper presents an analysis of TD-regularization (TD-REG) in reinforcement learning, particularly focusing on its application to different algorithms such as Deterministic Policy Gradient (DPG) and Stochastic Policy Gradient (SPG). The authors highlight that TD-REG enhances policy stability by mitigating issues related to inaccurate critic estimates that can lead to divergence in standard approaches. 

In their experiments, they compare the performance of SPG and SPG TD-REG against REINFORCE, noting that TD-REG consistently outperforms the others, even when the number of trajectories sampled per iteration is increased. The paper also delves into the impact of the TD-regularization coefficient, discussing the outcomes of various decay and growth factors (κ) on the learning process. For DPG TD-REG, values of κ close to 1 led to optimal convergence, while smaller values were ineffective. Conversely, in SPG TD-REG, a broader range of κ values yielded similar performance due to gradient normalization, with a smaller κ (0.9) achieving the fastest convergence.

The authors also address the influence of non-uniform observation noise on the critic's accuracy, suggesting that noise can contribute to high TD errors and affect learning efficiency. Overall, the findings emphasize the significance of TD-REG in enhancing the robustness of policy updates in reinforcement learning frameworks.
