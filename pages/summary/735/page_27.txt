The paper discusses the dynamics of policy parameter updates in reinforcement learning algorithms, particularly focusing on the effects of TD-regularization and delayed policy updates. It illustrates how TD-regularization facilitates more stable and efficient learning trajectories compared to traditional methods. 

The experiments reveal that while TD-regularization helps avoid divergence during early learning stages due to the initial inaccuracy of the critic, it can also slow down convergence, especially in the TD3 TD-REG variant where both delayed updates and two critics are utilized. Without delayed updates, both TD3 and TD3 TD-REG benefit from faster learning, though the performance varies with feature complexity. For quadratic features, removing delays improves performance, while for cubic features, TD3 without delays diverges more frequently than TD3, though TD3 TD-REG without delays shows improved outcomes.

The conclusion drawn is that delaying policy updates may not be necessary when the right features are employed, as overfitting is less of a concern. The combination of two critics and TD-regularization emerges as the most effective approach, with TD-regularization providing significant advantages in learning stability and convergence. 

Additionally, the paper evaluates stochastic policy gradient (SPG) methods, highlighting that SPG and SPG TD-REG optimize Q-function estimates from a learned critic, while REINFORCE relies on Monte Carlo estimates. This comparison underscores the various strategies employed in optimizing reinforcement learning algorithms.
