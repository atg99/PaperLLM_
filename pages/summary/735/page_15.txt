The paper investigates the performance of various algorithms in reinforcement learning, particularly focusing on Deep Policy Gradient (DPG) and its TD-regularized version (DPG TD-REG), as well as Twin Delayed DPG (TD3) and TD3 TD-REG. The evaluation involves tracking the expected return and comparing the mean squared temporal difference (TD) errors between the learned Q-function and the true Q-function relative to the current policy.

Key findings indicate that DPG, while maximizing a specific objective, suffers from substantial divergence due to overfitting of the critic, leading to an underestimation of true TD errors. In contrast, TD3 shows improved performance but still experiences some divergence. Notably, the TD-regularized algorithms consistently avoid divergence, with DPG TD-REG demonstrating the ability to converge to the optimal policy within the time limit.

The results are supported by graphical data illustrating the convergence rates and TD errors for each algorithm. SPG (Stochastic Policy Gradient) algorithms are also examined, revealing that SPG TD-REG achieves rapid convergence compared to SPG, which struggles due to higher TD errors. Overall, the TD-regularized methods show enhanced stability and efficiency in learning optimal policies.
