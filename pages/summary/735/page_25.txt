The paper discusses the implementation and performance of TD3 TD-REG, a variant of the TD3 algorithm that incorporates TD-regularization into policy updates. The method utilizes clipped noise to keep the target actions close to the original actions, removing the target policy while retaining the noise during updates. Key hyperparameters include a maximum of 150 steps per trajectory, Gaussian noise for exploration starting at a standard deviation of 5, and a discount factor of 0.99. 

Data collection begins with 100 steps to initialize experience replay memory, with mini-batches of 32 samples used for gradient computation every 100 steps. The paper highlights the impact of various learning rates for both the critic and policy updates, noting that stability issues arose with higher learning rates. Regularization coefficients start at 0.1 and decay over time.

Results indicate that using quadratic features leads to similar performance across algorithms, with TD-regularization promoting a more direct path to optimal policy parameters. While DPG and DPG NO-TAR converge slightly faster, the combination of TD3 and TD-REG can slow learning. Notably, the TD error estimates consistently underestimate the true TD error, a behavior attributed to the stochastic nature of the behavior policy. Overall, the findings underscore the trade-offs between stability and learning speed in reinforcement learning algorithms.
