The paper discusses the concept of n-step returns in reinforcement learning, defined as the sum of discounted rewards plus an estimated value of the state reached after n steps. The n-step return is represented as \( R_{t:t+n} \) and is crucial in deriving the TD(λ) method, which incorporates multiple n-step returns weighted by a decay factor λ. This approach allows for a balance between bias and variance in value function estimation.

The TD(λ) target, or λ-return, is calculated as a weighted average of these n-step returns, ensuring that the weights sum to one. The paper also highlights that when λ is set to 0, the λ-return reduces to the standard TD target, while a value of 1 corresponds to the Monte Carlo method.

The authors propose replacing the TD error with the TD(λ) error to achieve TD(λ) regularization. Notably, this regularization aligns with the Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE) method, which uses exponentially weighted averages of n-step advantage estimators. The GAE advantage estimator similarly allows for control over bias and variance by adjusting λ, providing a bridge between TD and Monte Carlo methods.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of the bias-variance trade-off in reinforcement learning and presents a unified approach to regularization through TD(λ) and GAE estimators.
