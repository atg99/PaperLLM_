The paper presents an analysis of different algorithms for policy gradient methods in reinforcement learning, specifically focusing on Stochastic Policy Gradient (SPG) approaches with and without Temporal Difference (TD) regularization. The results are based on experiments conducted over multiple runs, highlighting the performance of various configurations in terms of expected return and mean squared TD error.

In the first set of experiments, where only one episode was collected per iteration, SPG with TD regularization (SPG TD-REG) demonstrated robust learning, quickly achieving optimal policies without being adversely affected by sample size. In contrast, vanilla SPG exhibited slower convergence and was more sensitive to sample size, although it did not diverge. The REINFORCE algorithm faced significant challenges, diverging multiple times due to high variance in Monte Carlo estimates.

In the second set of experiments, where five episodes were collected per iteration, REINFORCE showed improved convergence due to reduced variance in estimates but still lagged behind SPG TD-REG. Vanilla SPG diverged in two instances, reinforcing its instability, while SPG TD-REG maintained consistent performance across all runs. The choice of function approximator also influenced performance, with quadratic features providing greater stability.

The analysis further reveals that the TD regularization gradient helps steer the learning process in the right direction, especially in early stages when the critic's estimates are less accurate. As training progresses, the influence of the regularization diminishes, allowing the algorithm to converge effectively.

Overall, the findings underscore the advantages of incorporating TD regularization in SPG methods, particularly in terms of stability and convergence speed across different sampling conditions and function approximators.
