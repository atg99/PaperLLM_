The paper discusses the impact of different values of the regularization parameter κ on reinforcement learning algorithms, specifically focusing on the TD-regularization approach. It highlights the necessity of maintaining a sufficiently large κ throughout the learning process to ensure effective regularization. Smaller values of κ can lead to the disappearance of the TD-regularization effect, which can adversely affect learning outcomes.

The paper also addresses the challenge of learning in environments with non-uniformly distributed noise, particularly in the context of partially observable states. The authors analyze how TD-regularization influences the learning process when Gaussian noise is added to the true state observations. They present a specific case where the observed state is derived from the true state with added noise, emphasizing that the algorithm's tendency to avoid high TD errors could lead it away from the desired goal.

Results indicate that the SPG TD-REG algorithm performs robustly across trials, successfully reducing TD error to zero, while other algorithms like SPG exhibit divergence under noisy conditions. Additionally, the REINFORCE algorithm remains relatively unaffected by noisy observations due to its lack of a critic component.

The paper further explores pendulum swing-up tasks as benchmarks for reinforcement learning, where the objective is to swing and stabilize a pendulum from various initial positions. These tasks are identified as more complex than linear quadratic regulator (LQR) problems due to their nonlinear transition and value functions, especially in the case of double-link pendulums.
