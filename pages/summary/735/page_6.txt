The paper discusses a modification to actor-critic methods in reinforcement learning, specifically focusing on a TD-regularized approach to ensure unbiased value function estimates. It begins by highlighting that the critic's estimate of the value function can be biased, while Monte Carlo estimates are generally unbiased. The authors present the Bellman equation as a means to maintain the unbiased property of the estimator, which ensures that the estimates converge to the true value function in expectation.

To incorporate this property into the actor's learning goal, the authors propose a constrained optimization problem that maximizes expected returns while adhering to the Bellman equation as a constraint. This approach, termed Bellman-constrained policy search, ensures that when the critic's estimates are unbiased, the optimization aligns with the original goal of maximizing returns.

However, the authors acknowledge the computational challenges posed by the large number of constraints, especially in continuous state and action spaces. To address this, they introduce a modification using the quadratic penalty method, which reformulates the constrained problem into an unconstrained one. This method involves optimizing a penalized objective function that balances the original objective with penalties for violating the constraints, controlled by parameters that manage the trade-off between the two.

Overall, the paper presents a structured approach to enhancing the reliability of actor-critic methods by ensuring unbiased value function estimates through the integration of Bellman constraints, while also providing practical solutions to computational difficulties.
