The paper investigates the performance of various reinforcement learning algorithms, specifically focusing on DPG and SPG, under conditions of non-uniform noise and with different decay factors. The study includes comparisons with the twin delayed version of DPG, which has achieved state-of-the-art results. 

The evaluations are conducted on several tasks, including single- and double-pendulum swing-up tasks and continuous control benchmarks using the MuJoCo physics simulator. The paper employs TD- and GAE-regularization techniques with TRPO, alongside other stabilization methods like Retrace and double-critic learning.

In the LQR problem, described by discrete-time dynamics, the authors highlight challenges such as the potential for policy-induced instability and unbounded rewards. They emphasize the need for careful policy parameter updates to prevent divergence. The Q-function is computed in closed form, allowing for a clear assessment of algorithm performance, and the paper demonstrates the susceptibility of actor-critic algorithms to instability due to function approximation errors. 

Overall, the research provides insights into the behavior of reinforcement learning algorithms in complex environments, while identifying critical factors influencing stability and performance.
