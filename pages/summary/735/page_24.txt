The paper discusses the application of Deterministic Policy Gradient (DPG) methods in the context of Linear Quadratic Regulator (LQR) problems, focusing on the evaluation of five variations of DPG. It clarifies that the true Q-function Qπ(s,a) is not the optimal Q-function but rather pertains to the current policy. The learning of diagonal entries of the matrix K in the policy parameter space is highlighted as these parameters are crucial for optimal performance.

The evaluation includes three traditional DPG approaches where the critic is updated independently from the policy, leveraging a target Q-function for stability. The first method involves a target actor for computing Q-function targets, while the second uses a penalty function for full derivative calculations without a target policy. The third method, DPG NO-TAR, tests the impact of removing the target policy.

Additionally, the paper introduces two advanced variations based on Twin Delayed DPG (TD3), which mitigates overestimation bias through dual critics and introduces noise to the target policy to counteract function approximation errors. The modifications in TD3 aim to enhance performance and reduce errors during updates, with the paper demonstrating the effectiveness of TD-regularized approaches compared to standard DPG methods. Overall, the findings suggest that TD-regularization significantly contributes to improved learning outcomes in DPG frameworks.
