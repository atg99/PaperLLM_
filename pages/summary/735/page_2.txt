This paper addresses the instability issues commonly encountered in actor-critic methods within reinforcement learning. While existing approaches have primarily focused on stabilizing either the actor or the critic, this work proposes a novel method aimed at stabilizing their interaction. The authors introduce the TD-regularized actor-critic method, which penalizes the actor's learning objective when the critic's value function estimate is significantly inaccurate. This penalty is applied specifically in cases of substantial violations of the Bellman equation and large temporal difference (TD) errors.

The method involves adding the critic's TD error as a regularization term in the actor's objective function, allowing for a straightforward gradient update process. This approach not only enhances the stability of actor-critic methods but also improves their performance and data efficiency. The authors demonstrate the applicability of their method across various frameworks, including stochastic and deterministic actor-critic methods, trust-region policy optimization, proximal policy optimization, as well as Retrace and double-critic methods.

The paper also reviews related work, highlighting various strategies implemented to enhance the stability of TD-based critic learning methods, such as the use of target networks, minimum value approaches, and constrained learning of value functions. The proposed TD-regularized method is presented as a complementary technique that can be integrated with existing stabilization methods to further bolster the effectiveness of actor-critic algorithms.
