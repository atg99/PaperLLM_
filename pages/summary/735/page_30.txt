This paper discusses the application of the SPG TD-REG algorithm in the context of reinforcement learning, specifically focusing on pendulum swing-up tasks. The visual representation in Figure 16 illustrates the path followed by the algorithm in the policy parameter space during a trial, highlighting the initial policy parameter vector and the gradients influencing the policy's direction. Red arrows indicate the gradient related to the expected return, while blue arrows represent the direction of a secondary gradient that assists in maintaining the policy's trajectory despite initial inaccuracies in the critic's estimates, as evidenced by the TD error.

The experiments involve two types of pendulum tasks: a single-pendulum and a double-pendulum, as depicted in Figure 17. The single-pendulum task is defined by a state characterized by the angle position and velocity of the link, both of which are initialized within specified bounds. This setup is aligned with the frameworks established in OpenAI Gym, emphasizing the practical application of the proposed algorithm in real-world scenarios. The findings suggest that although the initial policy estimates may be flawed, the use of dual gradients can effectively guide the policy toward optimal performance.
