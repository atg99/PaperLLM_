This paper discusses experiments related to the Linear Quadratic Regulator (LQR) problem in a discrete-time setting. The LQR is defined by specific dynamics involving matrices A, B, X, and Y, with the goal of learning control policy parameters represented as θ, the vectorization of matrix K. The study highlights several challenges inherent to the LQR, such as the risk of system instability due to the need for the matrix (A+BK) to have eigenvalues with magnitudes less than one. It also addresses issues related to unbounded rewards and states, which complicate the normalization process commonly used in reinforcement learning.

The Q-function, crucial for evaluating the performance of learned policies, is shown to be quadratic in both state and action. It is expressed in closed form, allowing for easy assessment of algorithm quality. The experiments utilize a 2-dimensional LQR setup, leading to four policy parameters, and evaluate two types of feature approximations: quadratic and cubic. The quadratic features are deemed sufficient given the true nature of the Q-function, while cubic features risk overfitting despite offering more parameters.

The experimental setup involves initializing the state and Q-function parameters uniformly and averaging results over 50 trials with a fixed random seed. The paper presents expected return plots, bounding the values for clarity due to potential instability in policies. The analysis includes trends of mean squared TD errors derived from both estimated TD errors and closed-form computations.
