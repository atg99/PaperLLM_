The paper discusses the implementation of reinforcement learning algorithms for controlling a pendulum system, specifically focusing on an underactuated single pendulum and a double pendulum. The transition dynamics of the pendulum are detailed, with specific parameters such as gravitational acceleration, mass, link length, and time step defining the system's behavior. The reward function is structured to encourage the pendulum to reach a goal state, minimizing the angle and angular velocity.

For the learning process, the authors collect data through episodes, utilizing a critic that approximates the value function with linear functions based on random Fourier features. The policy is modeled as Gaussian, incorporating these features, and is optimized using the Trust Region Policy Optimization (TRPO) method, with specific constraints on KL divergence to ensure stable updates.

In the double pendulum task, the state representation includes angles and velocities of the links, with actions constrained to a defined range. The dynamics are described using a transition function that accounts for forces acting on the pendulum, including gravitational, Coriolis, and frictional forces.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the use of advanced reinforcement learning techniques, such as Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE) and TRPO, to effectively learn control policies for complex dynamic systems. The methodology is supported by detailed mathematical formulations and parameter settings that guide the learning process.
