The paper discusses the challenges of convergence and stability in actor-critic methods for reinforcement learning, specifically focusing on the deterministic policy gradient (DPG) approach. It illustrates the instability of DPG through experimental runs that fail to converge, highlighting the inadequacy of using an incompatible linear function to approximate the value function. To address these issues, the authors propose a novel method that stabilizes the interaction between the actor and critic by penalizing the critic's mistakes during the actor's learning process. This contrasts with existing methods that either stabilize the actor or critic independently or delay actor updates until the critic is accurate, which can be inefficient.

The paper also references prior works that have used Kullback-Leibler divergence constraints to ensure stable actor updates and discusses alternative approaches that involve dual objectives for the actor and critic. However, the authors argue that their method promotes cooperation between the actor and critic, leading to improved stability and performance without the need for excessive sample requirements or slow convergence. The paper sets the stage for a deeper exploration of reinforcement learning frameworks and the specific sources of instability in actor-critic methods.
