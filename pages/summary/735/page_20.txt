This paper investigates the stability issues in actor-critic methods, primarily caused by function approximation errors in the critic. It proposes a TD-regularized approach that penalizes the actor for deviating from the critic's Bellman equation, thus enhancing the stability of the learning process. The study includes experiments on continuous control tasks using the MuJoCo physics simulator, where both TD- and GAE-regularization methods are evaluated against standard algorithms like TRPO and PPO.

The results indicate that the combination of Retrace and GAE-REG yields significant improvements, particularly in tasks with complex dynamics such as Ant-v2, where traditional PPO struggled. The experiments reveal that the regularization coefficient's value is crucial, as it must be tuned according to the advantage estimator's magnitude and the TD error. While TD-REG consistently outperformed or matched TRPO across various tasks, GAE-REG showed mixed results, excelling in some tasks but underperforming in others.

Overall, the paper concludes that both TD-REG and GAE-REG can significantly enhance the performance of TRPO and PPO, emphasizing the importance of careful tuning of the regularization coefficients for optimal results.
