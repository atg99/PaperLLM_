The paper presents a detailed evaluation of policy optimization methods, specifically Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) and Trust Region Policy Optimization (TRPO), using a structured approach to training neural networks for reinforcement learning. The evaluation is based on a series of 20 episodes, utilizing trajectories collected with exploration noise for PPO while maintaining a noise-free environment for TRPO.

Both the actor and critic networks are implemented as two-layer neural networks with hyperbolic tangent activation functions, ensuring that the actor's output remains within the action bounds of [-1, 1]. The training process involves collecting at least 3000 transition samples per batch, with a maximum trajectory length of 1000 steps. The Generalized Advantage Estimator (GAE) is employed to compute advantages, and the critic is trained to minimize the mean squared Temporal Difference (TD) error using the ADAM optimizer.

Key hyperparameters for TRPO include 128 hidden units per layer, a policy initial standard deviation of 1, and specific GAE parameters (λ=0.97, γ=0.995). The optimization process is constrained by a KL divergence bound of 0.01 and includes a damping factor for numerical stability. For PPO, the architecture uses 64 hidden units per layer, a higher initial standard deviation of 2, and slightly different GAE parameters (λ=0.95, γ=0.99), with a clipping value of 0.05 for policy updates.

The references cited provide a foundation for the methodologies discussed, highlighting previous research on constrained policy optimization and reinforcement learning techniques. The paper concludes that while TRPO employs a more complex method for regression problems, empirical results indicate that simpler batch gradient descent methods can yield satisfactory performance.
