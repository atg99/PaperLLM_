The paper evaluates the performance of various reinforcement learning algorithms, specifically focusing on the Twin Delayed Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (TD3) method with and without policy update delays. The results indicate that both TD3 and TD3 with regularization (TD-REG) converge faster when policy update delays are eliminated, as shown in the provided figures.

When cubic features are applied, TD3 without delay performs poorly compared to TD3, diverging significantly. In contrast, TD3 with regularization consistently converges within the time limit, showcasing its robustness. The policy is modeled using a Gaussian distribution, with specific parameters initialized for optimization. Key hyperparameters include the number of trajectories collected, steps per trajectory, and the learning rate for policy updates, which are critical for the algorithm's efficiency.

The findings highlight the challenges faced by the REINFORCE algorithm, particularly its high variance in Q-function estimates when fewer episodes are used for collecting samples. Overall, the study emphasizes the importance of policy update mechanisms and regularization in enhancing the performance of deep reinforcement learning methods.
