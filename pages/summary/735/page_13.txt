The paper discusses the Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE) and its role in regularizing actor-critic methods in reinforcement learning. It establishes that the GAE advantage estimator is equivalent to the TD(λ) error estimator, allowing for the formulation of GAE-regularization, which introduces a quadratic penalty based on the squared advantage. This approach does not add computational cost to existing methods like TRPO and PPO, while enabling tuning of the bias-variance trade-off between TD and Monte Carlo methods.

The authors highlight that minimizing the squared advantage can be interpreted as maximizing average reward with a penalty on the divergence between new and old state-action distributions, and a constraint that maintains stationarity. The GAE-regularization approximates the Bellman equation constraint, replacing it with a soft constraint, which helps avoid overconfident policy updates.

The paper also connects GAE-regularization to mean-variance optimization, emphasizing that reducing the variance of the GAE estimator is crucial for risk-averse reinforcement learning. High variance indicates an inaccurate critic, which can lead to ineffective policy updates.

In the evaluation section, the authors propose three empirical studies, starting with a linear-quadratic regulator (LQR) to analyze the benefits of TD-regularization. This allows for closed-form calculations of the true Q-function, expected return, and TD error, facilitating visualization of the policy parameter space. The initial penalty coefficient for this evaluation is set to a small value, allowing for systematic exploration of the effects of regularization on performance.
