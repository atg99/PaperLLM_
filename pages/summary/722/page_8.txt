The paper presents a comparison of different reinforcement learning algorithms in the context of the Bidirectional Diabolical Combination Lock problem, illustrating the performance of the proposed PC-PG algorithm against others like PPO and PPO with RND. The key findings are summarized as follows:

1. **Performance Metrics**: The success rates of various algorithms are provided for different horizons (2, 5, 10, and 15), showing that PC-PG consistently achieves a success rate of 1.0 across all horizons, while PPO struggles significantly, achieving a success rate of 0.0.

2. **Sample Complexity**: The paper discusses the sample complexity of PC-PG, which is polynomial in several parameters, ensuring with high probability that it can return a policy close to the optimal policy.

3. **Exploration Challenges**: The Bidirectional Diabolical Combination Lock presents unique challenges for exploration due to sparse rewards, dense negative rewards, and the need for the agent to remember the rewards associated with two different paths.

4. **Algorithm Flexibility**: PC-PG demonstrates the ability to leverage existing policy gradient methods, particularly PPO, and outperforms them in challenging exploration scenarios. The algorithm employs a rebalancing strategy to maximize policy coverage over the state space.

5. **Experimental Setup**: The experiments utilize fully connected or convolutional neural networks for policy parameterization and employ a specific kernel to compute exploration bonuses based on the empirical covariance matrix of the policy cover.

Overall, the paper establishes that PC-PG is a robust algorithm capable of efficiently navigating complex exploration tasks while outperforming traditional methods in terms of success rates and sample efficiency.
