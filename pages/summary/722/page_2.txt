The paper presents the POLICY COVER GUIDED POLICY GRADIENT algorithm (PC-PG), which aims to enhance policy gradient methods by addressing exploration challenges while maintaining robustness to model misspecification and function approximation issues. The motivation stems from the desire to create algorithms that can withstand violations of underlying assumptions, emphasizing the end-to-end nature of policy gradient methods that directly optimize the desired cost function.

PC-PG introduces a model-free approach that utilizes a learned ensemble of policies to create a policy cover over the state space, thereby facilitating effective exploration. This method also mitigates the "catastrophic forgetting" problem common in reward bonus-based policy gradient approaches, while avoiding the "delusional bias" seen in Bellman backup methods. The authors argue that their approach is distinct from existing reinforcement learning algorithms, which tend to be either model-based or value-based.

The exploration mechanism is implemented through an exploratory actor-critic method that estimates critic functions using a linear feature representation. The main theoretical contribution shows that the algorithm can compete with a given policy's value in the initial state, provided it can estimate the critic functions for all discovered actor policies with a manageable mean squared error under the state-action distribution of the comparison policy. This result has implications for linear MDPs, state aggregation, and other settings, demonstrating that PC-PG is sample and computationally efficient both in exact and approximate scenarios.

Key findings include the ability of PC-PG to operate within an infinite dimensional Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Space (RKHS) for linear MDPs, and the provision of a novel approximation guarantee that only requires local accuracy in state aggregation settings. The paper emphasizes the importance of average case error measures over strict misspecification conditions, contributing to the theoretical foundation of policy optimization methods in exploration contexts.
