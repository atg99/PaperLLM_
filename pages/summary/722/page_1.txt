The paper introduces the Policy Cover Guided Policy Gradient (PC-PG) algorithm, addressing a critical limitation of direct policy gradient methods in reinforcement learning, which is their inadequate exploration of the environment. While direct policy gradient methods are effective due to their model-free nature and ability to optimize performance metrics directly, they often struggle with exploration, particularly in sparse reward scenarios. 

In contrast, model-based approaches and Q-learning can theoretically handle exploration but face challenges related to model misspecification and function approximation. The PC-PG algorithm aims to balance exploration and exploitation by utilizing an ensemble of learned policies known as the policy cover. The authors demonstrate that PC-PG maintains polynomial sample complexity and runtime for both tabular and linear Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) in infinite-dimensional Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Spaces (RKHS).

Moreover, PC-PG offers robust guarantees under model misspecification, going beyond standard worst-case assumptions. It includes approximation guarantees for state aggregation under average-case error assumptions and controls for approximation errors during distribution shifts. The theoretical claims are substantiated with empirical evaluations across various domains, both in reward-free and reward-driven settings.

The introduction outlines the historical context of policy gradient methods, their recent theoretical advancements, and the established algorithms for exploration in reinforcement learning, setting the stage for the contributions of PC-PG.
