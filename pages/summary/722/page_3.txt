The paper discusses a novel approach called PC-PG, which addresses challenges in policy gradient methods for Markov Decision Processes (MDPs). It generalizes state aggregation through state distribution and establishes that PC-PG exhibits favorable sample complexity when transfer error is minimal, as outlined in Theorem 4.3.

The empirical evaluation demonstrates the effectiveness of PC-PG in scenarios where traditional bonus-based approaches, such as Random Network Distillation, struggle to identify optimal policies. The results indicate that PC-PG complements existing deep learning methods and confirms its robustness even outside the theoretically established sample complexity bounds.

The setting of the study defines an MDP characterized by a state space, action space, transition model, reward function, discount factor, and starting state. The authors emphasize the importance of exploration and detail the formulation of value functions and state-action distributions. The goal is to maximize the expected value from the starting state by optimizing the policy.

The paper introduces the PC-PG algorithm, motivated by the limitations of standard policy gradient ascent, which may lead to suboptimal stationary points due to insufficient coverage. In response, the authors propose a wide coverage objective that enhances exploration by maximizing the expected value of the state-action function across diverse starting states and actions. This approach aims to improve the learning process and ensure better policy outcomes.
