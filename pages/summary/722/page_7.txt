The paper discusses model-misspecification in reinforcement learning, focusing on the discrepancies in transition dynamics and rewards for state-action pairs mapped to the same abstracted state. It highlights a folklore result that indicates algorithms like UCB, Q-learning, and regular policy gradient can achieve success with an additional error term related to the maximum disagreement measure (denoted as k✏misspec k1) and that their sample complexity is polynomial in the size of the abstracted state space |Z|.

A significant contribution is Theorem 4.2, which establishes that the Policy-Corrected Policy Gradient (PC-PG) algorithm requires a local guarantee, specifically under the distribution of abstracted states that an optimal policy frequently visits. The theorem provides a bound on the sample complexity of PC-PG and shows that it can return a policy close to a comparator policy with high probability, incorporating an additional error factor due to reward bonuses.

The paper also addresses the limitations of guarantees in broader reinforcement learning contexts that go beyond global `1 bounds. It suggests that the lack of strong guarantees is not just an analytical challenge but also an algorithmic one, positing that algorithms like PC-PG are necessary to achieve robust performance under model misspecification.

Further, the paper introduces the concept of bounded transfer error, which measures the prediction error when transferring from one distribution to another. This is crucial for analyzing the PC-PG algorithm under general Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) without strict linear modeling assumptions. Theorem 4.3 provides a sample complexity guarantee for PC-PG under the assumption of bounded transfer error, reinforcing the algorithm's robustness in the face of model misspecification. 

Overall, the paper emphasizes the need for local guarantees and the importance of understanding transfer errors to improve reinforcement learning algorithms in complex environments.
