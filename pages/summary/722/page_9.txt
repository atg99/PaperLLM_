This paper presents a novel policy gradient algorithm called PC-PG, designed to effectively balance exploration and exploitation in reinforcement learning (RL). The authors compare PC-PG against traditional Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) and a variant with Random Network Distillation (PPO+RND) across various environments, including maze and continuous control scenarios.

In maze environments, PC-PG demonstrates superior state coverage within 10 million steps, quickly visiting a significantly higher proportion of locations compared to both PPO, which maintains a constant exploration rate, and PPO+RND, which shows limited exploration due to its deterministic policy. The results indicate that while PPO+RND succeeds part of the time, it tends to forget to explore alternative paths after reaching rewards.

In the continuous control task of MountainCar, the PPO agent converges to a locally optimal policy of inaction, while the PPO+RND agent shows variability in performance across different seeds. In contrast, PC-PG consistently discovers effective policies, highlighting its robustness.

The paper underscores the provable sample efficiency of PC-PG in linear and kernelized settings and suggests that combining this approach with neural policy optimization could enhance performance further. Future work may explore integrating PC-PG with feature learning in complex observation environments to improve feature representation.
