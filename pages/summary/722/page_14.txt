The referenced works collectively explore advancements in reinforcement learning, particularly focusing on statistical methods, regret bounds, and feature mapping techniques. 

Williams (1992) discusses simple gradient-following algorithms for connectionist models in reinforcement learning, providing foundational insights into the optimization of learning strategies. Yang and Wang (2019) contribute to the discourse by examining reinforcement learning in feature spaces, specifically addressing matrix bandits and establishing regret bounds, which measure the performance of algorithms compared to optimal strategies. They also present sample-optimal parametric Q-learning that utilizes linearly additive features, further enhancing efficiency in learning processes.

Zanette et al. (2020) offer frequentist regret bounds for randomized least-squares value iteration, contributing to performance guarantees in reinforcement learning algorithms. Zhou et al. (2020) focus on efficient reinforcement learning for discounted Markov decision processes (MDPs) with feature mapping, highlighting the importance of effective feature representation in achieving efficient learning outcomes. Lastly, Zinkevich (2003) discusses online convex programming and generalized infinitesimal gradient ascent, which are relevant for developing robust algorithms in dynamic environments.

These studies collectively advance the understanding of reinforcement learning, emphasizing the significance of statistical methods, regret analysis, and feature optimization in improving learning efficacy.
