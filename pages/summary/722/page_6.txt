This paper discusses an algorithm designed to be robust against model misspecification in a more general agnostic setting, measured by a novel concept of transfer error. The authors illustrate how previous methods often fail due to biases in Bellman backups, especially under function approximation and model misspecification.

In Section 4.1, the authors focus on linear Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) within a Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Space (RKHS). They define a linear MDP where the reward function and transition operator reside in the RKHS, and outline assumptions regarding parameter norms and initial state properties. They introduce the maximum information gain, a crucial measure that assesses the sample complexity of the underlying MDP, which relates to the covariance matrix of any policy.

Theorem 4.1 establishes that the proposed PC-PG algorithm can achieve a sample complexity that scales polynomially with various parameters, ensuring that the resulting policy approximates a comparator policy within an acceptable margin of error. The authors note that this theorem extends to tabular MDPs and highlights the algorithm's applicability to infinite-dimensional cases.

In Section 4.2, the discussion shifts to state-aggregation under model misspecification, where errors arise from aggregating state-action pairs into abstracted states. The authors define a measure of model misspecification based on the discrepancies in transition probabilities and rewards for aggregated states.

Overall, the paper presents a robust framework for dealing with model misspecification in reinforcement learning, highlighting the importance of proper feature mapping and information gain in ensuring effective policy learning.
