The paper discusses the performance of VideoGPT, particularly focusing on the impact of latent sizes and spatio-temporal downsampling in VQ-VAE latent space on video generation quality. It presents ablation studies comparing different latent configurations on the BAIR dataset, analyzing metrics such as R-FVD and FVD. The findings indicate that larger latent sizes generally lead to better reconstruction quality, although the largest configuration (16x32x32) suffers from limited compute capacity, resulting in suboptimal sample quality. Conversely, smaller latent sizes (4x16x16 and 16x8x8) yield poor reconstruction and sample quality.

The optimal balance is found at a latent size of 8x32x32, which provides the best sample quality due to a balanced spatio-temporal downsampling ratio. The study also reveals that increasing the number of codebook codes does not significantly enhance performance, with the best results observed at 4096 codes. Overall, the research highlights the trade-offs between latent size, reconstruction quality, and sample quality in the context of video generation.
