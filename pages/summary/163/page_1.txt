The paper presents VideoGPT, a novel architecture designed for generative modeling of natural videos using a combination of VQ-VAE and transformer models. VideoGPT employs 3D convolutions and axial self-attention to create downsampled discrete latent representations of videos. A GPT-like autoregressive model then utilizes these representations along with spatio-temporal position encodings to generate video samples.

The authors highlight the simplicity and effectiveness of their approach, demonstrating that VideoGPT achieves competitive results compared to state-of-the-art generative adversarial networks (GANs) on the BAIR Robot dataset and can produce high-quality videos from datasets like UCF-101 and TGIF. The paper emphasizes the potential of their architecture as a minimalistic and reproducible framework for transformer-based video generation models.

The introduction outlines the broader context of generative models and their advancements across various modalities, noting the challenges associated with video generation due to its complexity, which requires modeling spatial and temporal correlations. The authors argue that developing effective generative models for videos is crucial for applications such as video prediction and forecasting, which have implications in fields like autonomous driving and weather prediction.
