The paper discusses the enhancements in video reconstruction and generation performance using the VQ-VAE architecture, particularly with the integration of axial attention. Figure 5 illustrates the improved reconstructions of videos from the BAIR Robot Pushing dataset, with original videos in green boxes and reconstructions in blue. Figure 6 shows that a single-frame conditioned VideoGPT model produces varied trajectories from the same starting frame, highlighting the model's capability to generate diverse outputs.

An ablation study presented in Table 3 compares VQ-VAE with and without axial attention blocks. Results indicate that incorporating axial attention significantly improves reconstruction performance, as evidenced by lower NMSE and better FVD scores. The study also notes that the "No Attention" version was adjusted with additional convolutional residual blocks for fair comparison.

Furthermore, the paper emphasizes the importance of a larger prior network capacity for performance enhancement. Table 4 presents ablation results on the number of transformer layers, demonstrating that increasing the number of layers leads to better bits per dim and FVD scores, particularly up to 8 layers. The method's computational efficiency allows for downsampling by space-time, enabling the training of larger, more expressive prior networks under memory constraints, which ultimately improves the model's ability to learn complex data distributions.
