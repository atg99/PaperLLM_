The paper presents experimental results for VideoGPT, focusing on the impact of varying the number of codes in the VQ-VAE codebooks on video generation quality. The experiments reveal that while increasing the number of codes enhances reconstruction quality, it does not significantly improve sample quality beyond a certain threshold, particularly for the BAIR dataset. The findings suggest that using a single VQ-VAE codebook yields better performance compared to multiple codebooks, which can complicate the architecture without proportional benefits in sample quality.

In the related work section, the paper discusses video prediction and generation techniques. It highlights various approaches, including methods for predicting future frames based on past frames and actions, as well as the vid2vid framework that leverages semantic information for video translation. The paper also reviews modern generative models, including Video Pixel Networks and DVD-GAN, noting their complexity and computational demands. The authors emphasize that their approach builds on VQ-VAE for video generation, aiming for simplicity and reproducibility while presenting the first VQ-VAE-based video generation model. Overall, the paper positions VideoGPT as a novel contribution to the field of video generation.
