The paper discusses the development of VideoGPT, a novel architecture designed for video generation. It begins by reviewing various generative model families, including likelihood-based models and adversarial models, highlighting their trade-offs in sampling speed, diversity, quality, and optimization. The authors advocate for likelihood-based models due to their easier training and evaluation processes, especially for challenging video data.

Among likelihood-based models, autoregressive models are favored due to their proven success with discrete data and established training methodologies that utilize Transformer architectures. The paper explores the advantages of performing autoregressive modeling in a downsampled latent space, which reduces spatial and temporal redundancies inherent in videos. This approach allows for more efficient computation and improved sampling speed.

VideoGPT is presented as a minimal adaptation of VQ-VAE and GPT architectures, utilizing 3D convolutions and axial attention to learn downsampled discrete latents from video frames. These latents are then modeled using a GPT-like autoregressive prior and decoded back to high-resolution videos. The authors assert that VideoGPT's architecture closely resembles traditional Transformers, making it a significant contribution to the field of video generation.
