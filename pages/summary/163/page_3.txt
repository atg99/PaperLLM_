The training pipeline consists of two main stages: first, the VQ-VAE encodes video data into discrete latent sequences, and second, an autoregressive transformer is trained on these latent sequences. 

Key findings of the study include:

1. VideoGPT demonstrates competitive performance on the BAIR Robot Pushing dataset, achieving Fréchet Video Distance (FVD) scores of 103 and 94 against real samples and reconstructions, respectively, outperforming existing methods like TrIVD-GAN.
2. The model successfully generates realistic samples from complex datasets such as UCF-101 and the Tumblr GIF dataset.
3. Ablation studies reveal the importance of design choices in VideoGPT, including axial attention blocks, the size of the VQ-VAE latent space, the number of codebooks, and the autoregressive prior's capacity.
4. The framework is adaptable for action conditional video generation, with qualitative results provided for the BAIR Robot Pushing dataset and Vizdoom simulator.

The paper also discusses the foundational concepts of VQ-VAE, which compresses high-dimensional data into a discretized latent space, and GPT, which utilizes autoregressive transformers for modeling discrete data. VideoGPT represents a significant advancement in modeling complex video data efficiently.
