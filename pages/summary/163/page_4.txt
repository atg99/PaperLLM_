The proposed method, VideoGPT, utilizes a two-stage approach to generate video samples. In the first stage, a Vector Quantized Variational Autoencoder (VQ-VAE) is trained on video data to learn discrete latent codes. The architecture includes 3D convolutions and attention residual blocks for both the encoder and decoder, with learned spatio-temporal embeddings for position encodings.

The second stage involves learning a prior over the VQ-VAE latent codes, inspired by the Image-GPT architecture. This stage introduces conditioning methods, including cross-attention for video frame conditioning and conditional norms for action and class conditioning.

Experiments are designed to evaluate the method's capability in generating high-fidelity samples from complex video datasets and to analyze the impact of different architectural choices. Training involves scaling image data and stabilizing VQ-VAE training with normalized MSE loss. The Moving MNIST dataset is specifically highlighted, demonstrating the performance of the model in generating conditional samples based on a single frame. 

Overall, the architecture is optimized for performance using Sparse Transformers and is tested on various datasets to assess its effectiveness in video generation tasks.
