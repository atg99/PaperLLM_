The paper investigates the impact of different pre-training tasks on the performance of Visual Question Answering (VQA) models. It categorizes pre-training tasks into object detection and image classification, analyzing their effects on accuracy using various datasets, including ImageNet, YFCC, COCO, and Visual Genome (VG). The results indicate that models pre-trained on VG, which includes a larger number of categories and additional attribute annotations, outperform those trained on COCO.

A significant finding is the role of attribute loss weight during pre-training. The analysis shows that excluding attribute loss during pre-training leads to a decrease in VQA performance, suggesting attributes contribute substantially to model accuracy. The study also highlights that grid features consistently outperform region features across various settings, including different backbones and VQA models, even achieving state-of-the-art results with the MCAN model.

The paper further explores the generalization of grid features across different VQA tasks, such as the VizWiz dataset, where grid features maintain competitive performance while being significantly faster. Additionally, the features are applied to image captioning tasks, yielding comparable results to region-based methods.

Finally, the paper discusses the potential for end-to-end training to address domain shifts between pre-training tasks and downstream applications, advocating for a more adaptable approach to feature representation in VQA systems.
