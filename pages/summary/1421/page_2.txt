This paper discusses the advantages of using bottom-up attention features, specifically grid features, for visual question answering (VQA) tasks. The authors highlight two critical aspects: the large-scale object and attribute annotations from the Visual Genome dataset and the high spatial resolution of input images. They note that the choice between region or grid features minimally impacts accuracy but significantly affects inference speed. 

The authors conducted extensive experiments confirming that their findings are consistent across various VQA models, benchmarks, and related tasks like image captioning. The switch to grid features allows for a substantial reduction in running time—from 0.89 seconds to 0.02 seconds per image—while achieving slightly better accuracy. This efficiency allows for end-to-end training of VQA models directly from pixels to answers, overcoming the computational challenges associated with fine-tuning region features.

The paper also reviews the evolution of visual features in vision and language tasks, noting the historical reliance on region features, particularly those derived from R-CNN models. However, the authors advocate for grid features, demonstrating that they can match the performance of region features while providing significant speed advantages.

In addressing the debate between regions and grids, the authors emphasize the potential for grid features to be equally competitive in object detection and VQA tasks. They suggest that their findings could inspire new methodologies in vision and language research. Overall, the paper argues for the reconsideration of grid features as a viable alternative to region features in future model designs, potentially leading to enhanced performance and efficiency in VQA applications.
