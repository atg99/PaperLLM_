The paper discusses the effectiveness of visual question answering (VQA) models, particularly focusing on the impact of grid features derived from different pre-training tasks and input image sizes. The study compares the performance of models pre-trained on ImageNet for classification and those trained on Visual Genome (VG) for object and attribute detection. Key findings include that larger input sizes improve accuracy for grid features, with VG models benefiting more from increased image dimensions compared to ImageNet models. The results indicate that while ImageNet pre-trained models exhibit performance saturation around a certain input size, VG models continue to improve with larger inputs. Additionally, the paper highlights that the choice of pre-training task significantly affects VQA accuracy, with models pre-trained on larger datasets like YFCC outperforming traditional ImageNet models in classification tasks, while VG's combination of object and attribute training enhances detection-based performance. Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of both input size and pre-training tasks in optimizing VQA model performance.
