The paper discusses the identification of sentiment words by a model, categorizing them into positive and negative sentiments. It outlines a method for constructing lists of negations and amplifiers, starting from existing lists and filtering them based on their classification as neutral by an LSTM model. This process results in a refined list comprising 8 negations and 29 amplifiers, eliminating biased modifiers to focus on their compositional effects.

For semantic compositions, the paper categorizes them into four types. In type 1, which examines "negation of positive sentiment," 60% of constructed bigrams are classified as negative. Only negations that consistently predict negativity (at least 40% of the time) are retained. For type 2 ("amplification of positive sentiment") and type 3 ("amplification of negative sentiment"), specific amplifiers are identified based on their ability to enhance sentiment classification, leading to lists of effective modifiers for both types.

Type 4 focuses on "negation of amplified positive sentiment," where trigrams are analyzed for correct identification of both negation and amplification effects. However, the study finds that "negation of negative sentiment" yields limited useful results, as most bigrams remain classified as negative or very negative, indicating the model's difficulty in detecting such negations. This asymmetry in the impact of negation across different sentiments is supported by previous studies, suggesting that certain semantic compositions occur more frequently in natural language and training data.
