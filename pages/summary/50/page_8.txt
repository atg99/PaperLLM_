The paper investigates the effectiveness of various relevance visualization methods for interpreting neural network predictions in sentiment analysis. The methods evaluated include Average, Occlusion, Gradient×Input, Class Discriminative (CD), Layer-wise Relevance Propagation (LRP), and its variant LRP hT. The performance metrics indicate that LRP stands out with an accuracy of 84%, while LRP hT shows a notable decline at 68%. 

Figure 1 presents a PCA projection of sentence-level representations derived from word embeddings, highlighting the variance explained by the first three components. The representations range from less structured to more structured based on their relevance. 

In the section on interpreting single predictions, the paper emphasizes the challenges of visualizing relevance when ground truth is unavailable. The authors utilize bigram and trigram samples to generate average relevance heatmaps, which depict how different semantic compositions affect sentiment predictions. For instance, negation words like "not" are shown to influence sentiment transformations significantly, while amplifiers such as "very" support sentiment predictions but are less impactful than the core sentiment words.

Additionally, the analysis of misclassifications reveals critical insights into the model's behavior. In certain cases, the model failed to account for negations due to the overpowering sentiment of specific words, demonstrating the complexity of sentiment interpretation and the need for careful consideration in model design and evaluation. Overall, the paper underscores the importance of relevance visualization techniques in understanding model decisions in natural language processing tasks.
