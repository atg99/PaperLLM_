The paper presents an analysis of relevance attribution methods in neural networks, specifically focusing on toy arithmetic tasks and a sentiment prediction task using a bidirectional LSTM model. The results from various methods are summarized in a table showing the correlation and mean squared error (MSE) for different relevance attribution techniques, including Gradient, Occlusion, Layer-wise Relevance Propagation (LRP), and Class Discriminative (CD) methods. 

For the toy tasks—addition and subtraction—Gradient and Occlusion methods show high correlation with the input values and predicted outputs, while LRP methods exhibit lower performance, particularly LRP-prop and LRP-abs, which indicate significant variability. The paper posits that the unreliability of the Occlusion method may stem from the neural network's training on two relevant inputs, leading to confusion when one is omitted. Similarly, the limitations of the CD method are attributed to activation saturation causing inaccurate linearization.

In the sentiment analysis section, the Stanford Sentiment Treebank dataset is utilized, where a bidirectional LSTM achieves notable accuracy. The authors conduct perturbation experiments to examine the selectivity of word relevances by removing words based on their relevance scores and measuring the impact on classification performance. The results differentiate between correctly and falsely classified sentences, highlighting the effectiveness of relevance methods in maintaining or altering classification accuracy.

Overall, the study emphasizes the importance of relevance attribution methods in understanding neural network decisions, suggesting that reliable methods are crucial for more complex tasks where ground truth relevance is less clear. The findings underscore the need for careful evaluation of these methods to ensure trustworthy explanations in deeper neural architectures.
