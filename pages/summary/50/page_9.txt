The paper presents an analysis of semantic compositions using LSTM models and the Layer-wise Relevance Propagation (LRP) method for interpreting model predictions. It categorizes various types of sentiment expressions, such as negated positive sentiment and amplified negative sentiment, and evaluates their relevance through computed heatmaps. The LRP-all method is highlighted as superior to other interpretation methods, particularly for sentiment classification tasks, and the authors emphasize that LRP is not equivalent to Gradient-Input under certain conditions, especially with LSTM networks that utilize different non-linearities.

The findings suggest that LRP can provide more reliable explanations when the classifier’s predicted class is used as the target class for analysis. The authors also note that some existing methods, like Occlusion and CD, yield inconsistent results, indicating the need for careful selection of interpretative techniques. Future research directions are suggested, focusing on the application of decomposition-based methods for analyzing character-level models and understanding the significance of individual word embeddings and hidden layer neurons.
