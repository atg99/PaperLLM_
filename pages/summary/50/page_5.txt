The paper discusses various methods for evaluating the relevance of input features in machine learning models, particularly in the context of neural networks. It highlights different approaches, including using synthetic tasks to verify the relevance assigned to specific input positions. For instance, Yang et al. (2018) trained an LSTM model to determine the presence of zero digits in sequences of MNIST digits, while Adebayo et al. (2018) employed randomization techniques as sanity checks.

A central evaluation in the paper involves a toy arithmetic task where the authors investigate whether the model can recover the correct input values from relevance scores when adding two numbers. This setup tests the faithfulness of the model's explanations without explicit markers for the sequence's start and end. The authors use a sequence of random real numbers, focusing on two relevant positions sampled uniformly.

The paper details two tasks solvable by an LSTM model: the addition of signed numbers and the subtraction of positive numbers. The model is trained to minimize Mean Squared Error (MSE) across various sequences, with separate datasets for training, validation, and testing. The authors measure the correlation between the computed relevances and the actual input numbers, as well as the distribution of relevance across time steps.

Results indicate that several methods, such as Gradient-Input, Occlusion, LRP-all, and CD, perform well in the addition task, correlating perfectly with the input numbers and assigning relevance primarily to the relevant time steps. However, in the subtraction task, only Gradient-Input and LRP-all show strong correlations, reflecting the sequential nature of the task. The authors emphasize the difference between addition, which can be approached as a Bag-of-Words problem, and subtraction, which requires the model to remember the order of inputs through its gating mechanisms.
