The paper discusses various methods for computing relevance in neural networks, particularly focusing on recurrent architectures. It presents a table summarizing the complexity of different explanation methods, highlighting that methods like Gradient and LRP require extensive computational resources, particularly with LSTM networks. The paper also notes that while Integrated Gradients and DeepLIFT are alternative methods, they have shown inferior performance compared to CD and LRP in prior studies. 

The authors emphasize the challenges of evaluating explanations in a consistent and objective manner across different domains and tasks. They cite perturbation analysis as a common technique in computer vision for assessing relevance, while acknowledging that NLP evaluations often rely on task-specific metrics or correlations with simpler models that overlook contextual information. The paper critiques these methods for their inadequacies and stresses the need for more robust evaluation approaches in understanding and validating explanation methods in neural networks.
