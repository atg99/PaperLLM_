The paper discusses methods for determining input relevance in neural networks, with a focus on layer-wise relevance propagation (LRP) and contextual decomposition (CD). LRP, introduced by Bach et al. in 2015, is a technique that explains neural network predictions by redistributing relevance from the output layer back to the input layer through a series of layer-specific propagation rules. Each neuron is assigned relevance based on the output neuron's value, conserving overall relevance throughout the process. Various propagation rules are outlined, including the epsilon-LRP, LRP-all, LRP-prop, LRP-abs, and LRP-half, each with distinct approaches for redistributing relevance in different layer types.

Contextual decomposition is highlighted as a method tailored for long short-term memory (LSTM) networks. This approach linearizes activation functions to differentiate contributions from specific subsequences within the input data, allowing for a clearer understanding of the relevance associated with particular words or phrases in the context of the entire input sequence.

Overall, both LRP and CD provide efficient ways to interpret neural network predictions, applicable to a range of architectures, including convolutional and recurrent neural networks.
