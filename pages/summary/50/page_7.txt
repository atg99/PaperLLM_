The paper explores the impact of word removal on accuracy in classification tasks, analyzing different methods for assessing word relevance. The average change in accuracy is reported when removing words from sentences in both decreasing and increasing order of their relevance. The results indicate that methods such as LRP-all and CD show high accuracy change when words are removed based on their relevance, performing comparably to the occlusion-based relevance method, Occlusion P-diff. In contrast, gradient-based methods like Gradient-Input perform poorly in identifying influential words in increasing order of relevance, as they focus more on identifying unimportant words rather than those that contradict a classification decision.

The study further investigates whether the word relevance can be utilized to create sentence-level representations akin to word embeddings. By combining word embeddings weighted by their relevance, it was found that LRP provides the most structured representations, with PCA projections revealing clear distinctions among different sentiment categories. The findings suggest that these representations could be beneficial for visual analysis of semantics and in information retrieval applications. Overall, the analysis highlights the effectiveness of LRP and CD in detecting influential words, while also demonstrating the potential of using relevance scores for building more meaningful sentence representations.
