This paper explores the application of Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) models for forecasting river discharge at the Hoa Binh Station on the Da River in Vietnam. The Da River, a significant tributary of the Red River system, is characterized by its abundant flow and the presence of large hydropower dams, which complicate downstream flow forecasting. The research constructed multiple LSTM models to predict one-day, two-day, and three-day flow rates, utilizing rainfall and flow data from meteorological and hydrological stations as input.

The study area is located in northwest Vietnam, where the Da River basin covers 52,900 km², with roughly 50% of that area in Vietnam. Rainfall is predominantly concentrated from May to September, contributing to flood flows. The paper also emphasizes the evaluation of model performance in predicting flood peaks and the impact of dataset characteristics on these predictions. Two scenarios with different input data combinations were tested for the forecasting cases.

Key data on maximum daily precipitation and peak flood discharge in the Da River basin over a 24-year period is provided, highlighting significant values that inform the modeling process. The findings indicate the potential of LSTM-based models in effectively forecasting river discharge in the context of hydrological challenges posed by upstream dam operations.
