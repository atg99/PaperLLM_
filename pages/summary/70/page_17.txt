The paper discusses the application of Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) neural networks, particularly those utilizing Gated Recurrent Units (GRU), for streamflow prediction, which is a crucial aspect of hydrology and flood forecasting. It highlights that LSTM models are particularly effective for sequential data, such as streamflow, which varies continuously over time. The study emphasizes that LSTM models can produce accurate forecasts for various time steps, adapting to the frequency of the observed data, and can outperform traditional methods, especially in areas influenced by tidal regimes where conventional hydrological models may fail.

However, the paper also addresses limitations of LSTM models, noting that they are data-driven and may not adequately simulate hydrological processes compared to physical-based models. The authors suggest that combining LSTM with meteorological models, such as rainfall forecasting, could enhance long-term forecasting performance.

The findings indicate the potential of LSTM models for real-time flood warning systems, particularly in developing countries and large river basins like the Da River basin, which spans from China to Vietnam. The authors believe that such models can effectively manage the fluctuating streamflow downstream due to upstream dam and reservoir discharges.
