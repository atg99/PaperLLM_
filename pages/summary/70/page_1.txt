This paper presents the application of a Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) neural network for flood forecasting, emphasizing its importance in integrated water resource management. The study utilizes daily discharge and rainfall data to develop the model, focusing on the Da River basin in Vietnam. Two data sets prior to 1985, before the construction of the Hoa Binh dam, were analyzed for one-day, two-day, and three-day flowrate forecasts at Hoa Binh Station. The results demonstrated impressive predictive capabilities, achieving Nash–Sutcliffe efficiency (NSE) values of 99%, 95%, and 87% for the respective forecasting periods.

The introduction highlights the critical need for accurate flood flow forecasting due to the significant socio-economic impacts of flooding, particularly in densely populated regions adjacent to major rivers. The complexity of river discharge prediction arises from its dynamic nature, influenced by numerous factors including rainfall, river basin characteristics, and climatic conditions. The paper contrasts traditional mathematical models, which often require extensive data and may not perform well in data-scarce areas, with the LSTM approach, which offers a promising alternative for effective flood forecasting.

Overall, the findings suggest that LSTM neural networks can serve as a viable solution for managing flood risks in the Da River basin, addressing the challenges posed by sudden fluctuations in downstream flows from upstream reservoirs.
