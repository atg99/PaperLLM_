The paper discusses the development and optimization of Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) models for forecasting discharge at Hoa Binh Station, utilizing daily rainfall and flow data from the Da River basin. Unlike traditional models that incorporate physical principles like mass and momentum conservation, the LSTM approach learns these dynamics through training with observed data, allowing for accurate discharge predictions.

Key training parameters include a learning rate of 0.0001, varying numbers of units (20, 30, 50), and a maximum of 100,000 epochs. The raw data is reformatted into three-dimensional vectors to align with LSTM architecture, consisting of samples, time steps, and features.

To enhance learning, the input data is organized into a sequential format with a sequence length of ten time steps. This structure allows the model to capture trends from the ten most recent observations, which is crucial for time series forecasting in hydrology. The choice of sequence length is critical, as it affects the model's ability to detect trends while balancing computational efficiency. The study finds that a sequence length of ten yields optimal performance without excessive resource demands.

Additionally, the paper emphasizes the importance of selecting appropriate training parameters, recommending the Adam optimizer for its efficiency in updating network weights during training.
