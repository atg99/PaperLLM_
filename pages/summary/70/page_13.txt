The study presents an analysis of a forecasting model's performance in predicting river flow rates, particularly during flood events. The model achieved a Nash-Sutcliffe Efficiency (NSE) value exceeding 99% for one-day forecasts, with an average error (Root Mean Square Error, RMSE) of 150 m3/s. For two-day and three-day forecasts, NSE values were approximately 95% and 87%, respectively. The correlation between observed and predicted values was strong, as illustrated in Figures 5 and 6, highlighting the impact of rainfall on flow rates due to the timing of rain and flood peaks.

The validation process demonstrated that the model maintained impressive performance despite variations in parameters, such as sequence length and number of units. Different configurations yielded forecast results comparable to those in the original setup, indicating stability in the model's predictive capability. 

In the testing phase, the model was evaluated using data from 1984, specifically targeting the peak flood flow recorded on July 15, with a flow rate of 10,000 m3/s. The results, summarized in Table 5, show that for one-day forecasts, the model predicted peak flow rates with a relative error of 6.6% and 4.9% for two different scenarios. For two-day forecasts, the relative errors were 15.2% and 13.7%, while three-day forecasts had higher relative errors of 28.2% and 24.7%. These results confirm the model's efficacy in predicting flood peaks, demonstrating its reliability for operational forecasting.
