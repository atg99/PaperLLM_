The performance of the LSTM model is evaluated using metrics such as the Nash-Sutcliffe Efficiency (NSE) and Root Mean Square Error (RMSE). A perfect fit is indicated by an NSE of 1 and an RMSE of zero, which signifies that the model accurately predicts observed discharges.

The study presents two scenarios for flood forecasting. The first scenario utilizes rainfall and discharge data from eleven upstream gauge stations to predict flow rates at the Hoa Binh Station. The second scenario relies solely on observed discharge data from five hydrological stations, including the target station. 

Data is divided into three sets: a training set covering 18 years, a validation set spanning five years, and a testing set comprising observations from 1984. The maximum discharge recorded at the Hoa Binh Station during the testing period was 10,000 m3/s on July 15, 1984. The LSTM model's design emphasizes the importance of the actual collected data, which serves as boundary conditions for predicting flood events, distinguishing it from traditional hydraulic models.
