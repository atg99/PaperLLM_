The paper discusses the limitations of traditional Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) and introduces Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks as a solution. The backpropagation through time (BPTT) algorithm is employed in RNNs to adjust weights based on output errors. However, this process can lead to instability due to the accumulation of error gradients, often resulting in gradient vanishing and exploding issues. These challenges hinder the ability of RNNs to learn long-term dependencies effectively.

To overcome these drawbacks, LSTMs were developed by Hochreiter and Schmidhuber. Unlike standard RNNs, which utilize a single neural network structure, LSTMs consist of four interacting layers that enhance their capability to remember information over extended periods. This unique architecture allows LSTMs to manage long-term dependencies more efficiently, making them a significant advancement in the field of neural networks. The paper emphasizes the structural differences between RNNs and LSTMs, highlighting the latter's effectiveness in addressing the training difficulties faced by recurrent neural networks.
