LSTM networks are designed to address the issues of memory and sequential data processing that traditional ANN models face, making them particularly suitable for hydrological forecasting. The ability of LSTM to remember long-term dependencies in data allows for improved prediction accuracy in river flow forecasting.

The paper discusses the limitations of traditional predictive models for river flow, particularly in the context of sudden fluctuations caused by dam discharges. It highlights the advantages of data-driven methods, particularly Artificial Neural Networks (ANNs), which can model both linear and nonlinear relationships without preconceived assumptions. The review of literature indicates a growing trend in utilizing AI, specifically ANNs, for hydrological forecasting, with various studies demonstrating their effectiveness in predicting river discharge and water levels across different geographical contexts.

Further advancements in deep learning techniques, such as Long Short-Term Memory networks, show promise for overcoming the limitations faced by traditional ANNs, particularly in handling sequential time series data. The paper emphasizes the potential of these models in enhancing the accuracy of hydrological forecasts and suggests that ongoing research and development in this area could lead to more robust forecasting systems for managing water resources and mitigating flood risks.
