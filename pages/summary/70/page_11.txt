The paper discusses the development and validation of a Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) model for forecasting water flow. Key adjustments were made in the model's training process, including a reduced learning rate of 0.0001 to ensure smoother loss function transitions. The model's structure, particularly the number of LSTM units and epochs, was optimized, with a maximum of 100,000 epochs suggested for training. Techniques such as dropout and early stopping were implemented to mitigate overfitting, allowing the model to halt training if validation performance did not improve after 2,000 epochs.

Validation and testing were conducted on independent datasets, utilizing the Nash-Sutcliffe Efficiency (NSE) and Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) for performance evaluation. The validation phase used a five-year dataset (1979-1983), while testing employed a one-year dataset (1984). Results indicated that the first forecasting scenario, which combined rainfall and flow rate data, produced slightly better outcomes compared to the second scenario that relied solely on flow rate data. However, the differences were minimal, suggesting both scenarios yielded comparable forecasting accuracy. The figures illustrated the alignment between observed and predicted flow values, reinforcing the model's effectiveness in one-day flow forecasting.
