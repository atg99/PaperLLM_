The learning process in traditional DBNs utilizes a greedy, layer-wise training approach where each layer is trained independently before passing information to the next layer. This method, while computationally efficient, does not reflect biological learning processes and limits the model's applicability to developmental phenomena.

This paper proposes an iterative learning scheme for Deep Belief Networks, termed iDBN, which modifies the original Contrastive Divergence learning algorithm to allow for simultaneous tuning of the entire hierarchy of connections. The authors demonstrate that this new approach can achieve comparable accuracy to the greedy method while effectively tracking developmental trajectories in cognitive skill acquisition.

The research includes extensive simulations using well-known datasets, such as the MNIST dataset for handwritten digits, and explores continual learning scenarios to prevent catastrophic forgetting. Additionally, the authors conduct graph theoretical analyses to examine how network properties evolve during learning. They also apply iDBN to a recent dataset focused on visual numerosity perception, further illustrating its potential in simulating cognitive development.

The paper critiques the limitations of traditional training methods in DBNs, emphasizing the need for more biologically plausible models that reflect the dynamics of human cognitive development. By presenting iDBN, the authors aim to enhance the understanding of learning processes in neural networks and their implications for cognitive modeling.
