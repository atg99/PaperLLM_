The paper discusses the structural analysis of deep belief networks (DBNs) by examining the impact of varying connection strength thresholds on network properties. A range of thresholds was tested, and weights within a specified interval were discarded to focus on significant connections. The analysis compared the structural properties of real networks against random network replicas that maintained similar architectural characteristics, specifically through the generation of binomial graphs with identical node counts.

The study highlights the challenge of characterizing node degree distributions in DBNs due to their layered architecture, which constrains connections between different layers. This leads to architectural biases that differ from typical random graphs, which often follow a power-law distribution. To address this, the authors proposed a method for weighting node degrees based on their maximum potential connections.

The paper also explores a specific application of DBNs using the "numerosity" dataset, which investigates how networks develop number acuity through unsupervised learning. The DBN architecture used consists of a visible layer and two hidden layers, with specific initialization and training parameters. The network's ability to discriminate between different numerosities was assessed using a linear classifier, allowing for the evaluation of the internal Weber fraction, a measure of number acuity.

Results were obtained from the MNIST dataset, focusing on readout accuracy and reconstruction error trends. The study primarily reports findings from a DBN configuration with Normal initialization, noting that variations in initialization methods did not significantly impact the outcomes. The analysis also aims to compare the performance of the proposed DBN with previous models trained using greedy schemes, as well as developmental trajectories from deep autoencoders, providing insights into the learning processes involved in number acuity development.
