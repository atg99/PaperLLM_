This paper investigates the robustness of an iterative learning scheme in comparison to greedy training, focusing on performance under various weight initialization methods and regularization techniques. The authors expand on their previous findings by demonstrating that similar performance equivalence is maintained when employing Glorot initialization and incorporating dropout as a regularization method.

The results indicate that when weights are initialized using the Glorot method, the readout and reconstruction profiles align with those obtained from Normal initialization, affirming the consistency of the findings. Additionally, dropout, which randomly deactivates neurons during training to prevent overfitting, does not adversely affect the model's overall performance, as shown by the results.

In a separate analysis regarding graph structures derived from Deep Belief Networks (DBNs), the paper emphasizes the challenges in selecting an appropriate cut-off threshold for pruning networks based on learned weights. The authors argue against fitting a specific probability distribution to the emergent weight configurations due to the complexity and variability in connection strengths. Instead, they propose using a user-defined cut-off threshold to prune networks effectively, allowing for a more heuristic approach. 

The paper also discusses the degree distribution characteristics, noting that real networks typically exhibit a power-law distribution, contrasting with random graphs. This analysis underscores the importance of understanding network properties in the context of the learning process and model performance.
