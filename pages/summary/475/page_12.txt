This paper discusses the development of a novel unsupervised learning algorithm for deep belief networks, referred to as the iterative Deep Belief Network (iDBN). The primary aim is to characterize cognitive phenomena such as learning disabilities, specifically dyscalculia, which currently lacks computational representation. The iDBN approach holds potential for application across various cognitive domains and supports the creation of developmental models that can be quantitatively validated with empirical human data.

The study validates the iDBN algorithm on benchmark domains, showing performance comparable to state-of-the-art methods. Additionally, it highlights the algorithm's extension to realistic learning scenarios, facilitating psychometric analysis of behavioral performance changes and the exploration of network properties through network theory.

Future research could leverage the iDBN to explore the emergence of developmental disorders, linking them to both initial system conditions and learning phases. This exploration could provide significant insights into how learning dynamics affect cognitive development. The paper includes figures demonstrating the dataset used and the performance of the iDBN compared to traditional learning schemes, underscoring the algorithm's effectiveness.
