The paper discusses the analysis of degree distributions in directed bipartite networks (DBNs) and proposes a method for normalizing these distributions due to architectural constraints. It begins by noting that traditional power-law and binomial distributions do not adequately describe the degree distributions in DBNs, as the network's structure limits the connectivity of nodes to those in neighboring layers. This limitation leads to a deviation from expected distributions typically seen in scale-free networks.

The authors present a model to address this issue by penalizing nodes based on their potential maximum degree, which is derived from the adjacency matrix of the network. They introduce a normalized distribution to correct the degrees, ensuring the probability mass function remains valid.

To enhance the robustness of their analysis, the authors compare the DBN degree distributions with those from synthetic null models constructed using the Erdős-Rényi (ER) model. The generated random networks maintain the same architecture as the DBNs but exhibit a degree distribution that diverges from the expected Poissonian trend due to the bipartite nature of the networks.

The paper further proposes a correction strategy for normalizing node degrees, allowing for a more accurate representation of the distributions by adjusting for the architectural bias. The authors conduct experiments with various synthetic graphs to qualitatively assess the performance of different degree distribution models, aiming to identify which model best fits the observed data.

Overall, the study highlights the complexities in analyzing degree distributions in bipartite networks and presents a methodological framework for overcoming these challenges.
