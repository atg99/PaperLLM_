The paper discusses the limitations of traditional deep belief networks (DBNs) in terms of convergence and results when utilizing both feed-forward and top-down learning phases. It emphasizes that the activation of neurons in the deepest layer cannot simply be fed back to the visible layer but must undergo local processing at each layer to compute learning signals, a process implemented in the iterative DBN (iDBN). This approach aligns with the local recurrent processing observed in cortical areas.

To enhance the cognitive relevance of the iDBN, the authors introduced an interleaved training method that addresses continual learning tasks, demonstrating the network's capability to incrementally acquire new knowledge. This method facilitates the exploration of continual learning schemes that leverage the top-down generative aspects of DBNs for sampling stimuli required in interleaved training.

The research also includes a comprehensive analysis of the structural properties of the DBN using graph theoretical metrics, showcasing how iterative learning refines these properties over time. This opens avenues for a systematic study of the topological development of hierarchical systems.

The iDBN was evaluated on a dataset related to visual numerosity perception, achieving accuracy comparable to traditional methods while allowing for detailed tracking of internal representation development. The resulting learning curves mirrored those observed in human developmental studies, indicating a strong correlation with skill acquisition patterns seen in children and recent models based on deep autoencoders. The paper presents psychometric data on numerosity discrimination tasks, showing the progression of learning and the stability of the Weber fraction throughout the training process.
