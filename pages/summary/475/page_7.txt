This paper investigates the performance of greedy versus iterative learning schemes in deep belief networks (DBNs) during unsupervised learning. The study finds that readout accuracy improves with depth, indicating more disentangled internal representations. Reconstruction errors decrease monotonically, converging at different rates depending on the learning scheme used. While the greedy scheme shows higher readout accuracy in fewer epochs for the second and third layers due to prior training, the final accuracy is similar for both methods. However, a full-stack propagation variant demonstrates suboptimal convergence, as evidenced by poor reconstruction errors.

In terms of generative capabilities, both greedy and iterative DBNs perform equivalently after training, although the completion task proves challenging due to the complexity of generating plausible completions for corrupted images. The full-stack variant fails to achieve satisfactory generative performance across tasks.

The paper also explores emergent internal representations by analyzing receptive fields, which helps visualize the features learned at various hierarchical levels. The first hidden layer's weights can be easily inspected, while the second layer requires a composite approach for visualization. Overall, the findings provide insights into the effectiveness of different training strategies and their impact on the learning dynamics and generative abilities of DBNs.
