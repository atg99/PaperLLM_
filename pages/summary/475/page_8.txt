The paper investigates the learning dynamics of neural networks, specifically focusing on the resilience to catastrophic interference, the emergence of structural properties during learning, and the comparison between greedy and iterative learning schemes.

The authors demonstrate that the greedy and iterative learning methods yield similar receptive fields across the processing hierarchy, indicating a consistent learning pattern. However, a significant finding is the impact of learning setups on performance. The sequential learning approach is shown to suffer from catastrophic interference, where accuracy on previously learned data declines as new data is introduced. In contrast, the interleaved learning setup effectively integrates new information while preserving previous knowledge, maintaining classifier accuracy on earlier datasets.

The paper further explores how learning dynamics affect network structure, examining global properties such as mean degree, mean geodesic distance, and the number of connected components. Results indicate that non-trivial network properties emerge during unsupervised learning, particularly in the greedy network, suggesting that iterative learning fosters complex circuit development. The evolution of these properties reflects a transition from isolated nodes to interconnected components, with a corresponding decrease in mean geodesic distance as connections strengthen.

Additionally, the authors compare the behaviors of the greedy and iterative networks with their binomial counterparts, revealing similarities in overall behavior and emphasizing that learning dynamics significantly influence the internal structure of real networks. The findings underscore the differences between real and random networks, particularly in degree distribution, with real networks exhibiting characteristics that deviate from those of random networks.

In summary, the study highlights the importance of learning strategies in neural network performance and structural evolution, providing insights into the resilience of networks to interference and the emergence of complex properties during training.
