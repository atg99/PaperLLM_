The paper presents a novel approach to training Deep Belief Networks (DBNs) through an iterative learning algorithm called iDBN. DBNs, known for their capability to extract complex internal representations from sensory data, have played a significant role in the evolution of deep learning by demonstrating the effectiveness of unsupervised learning in multi-layered networks. Traditionally, DBNs are trained using a greedy, layer-wise method, which limits their ability to model the holistic development of cognitive processes.

The authors propose iDBN as a solution that enables simultaneous updates of connection weights across all layers, allowing for a more integrated representation of cognitive development similar to biological maturation. The effectiveness of iDBN is evaluated using visual stimuli, focusing on its generative capabilities and performance in supervised tasks. The study also examines the development of the network through graph theoretical properties and explores the potential for continual learning.

Results indicate that DBNs trained with iDBN achieve comparable performance to those trained with traditional greedy methods while offering insights into the evolution of internal representations and task performance over time. This work suggests that iDBN could be a valuable tool for modeling neurocognitive development and enhances our understanding of deep learning mechanisms in relation to cognitive processes.
