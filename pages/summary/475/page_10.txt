This paper presents a study on the development of numerosity in cognitive computation through the use of deep belief networks (DBNs). The authors compare their findings with previous research, specifically noting that their Weber fraction value of 0.17 aligns closely with Stoianov and Zorzi's reported value of 0.15. The trajectory of the Weber fraction during the learning phase shows a significant decrease, particularly in early development stages, mirroring human developmental patterns.

The methodology involved averaging 20 model runs and training multiple classifiers for each data point, resulting in a robust statistical representation across 100 data points per epoch. The data was fitted using a modified power-law function, effectively describing the progression of the Weber fraction during learning.

The paper argues that the proposed iterative learning algorithm allows for a more accurate tracking of developmental trajectories in DBNs compared to traditional greedy training methods. This iterative approach was validated on the MNIST benchmark, where it demonstrated comparable performance in readout accuracy and generative tasks. The findings suggest that the iterative training method can effectively capture the emergence of global properties in the network, facilitating a systematic comparison with biological developmental processes.

Overall, the results indicate that deep belief networks can be effectively trained to model cognitive processes, providing insights into the evolution of number sense and developmental learning trajectories.
