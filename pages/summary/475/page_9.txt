The paper examines the effects of dropout on network structures within deep belief networks (DBNs) by analyzing the networks at the end of training. It presents visualizations of degree distributions for real and binomial graphs under Normal initialization, comparing scenarios with and without dropout. The findings reveal that despite using a modified degree distribution to reflect potential connectivity, the resulting degree distributions differ qualitatively, indicating that the learning dynamics of DBNs lead to a non-random graph structure. Notably, dropout appears to align the structural characteristics of random replicas more closely with those of real networks. The paper also discusses the implications of iterative versus greedy training algorithms on network analysis, highlighting the importance of observing changes across the entire network during the learning process. Overall, the study suggests the need for further refinement of the degree distribution model to enhance accuracy in representing node degrees.
