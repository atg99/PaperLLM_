This paper presents a proof of convergence for gradient descent (GD) and stochastic gradient descent (SGD) in non-linear neural networks with more than two layers. The authors demonstrate that overparameterization allows GD/SGD to avoid poor local minima during training, a phenomenon previously observed but not theoretically justified. The focus is on recurrent neural networks (RNNs), highlighting their capability to learn mappings from distinct input tokens simultaneously.

Key concepts include:

1. **Bounds on Perturbations**: The paper establishes bounds on certain derivatives and sparsity conditions that facilitate induction without exponential growth in complexity. Specifically, it shows that certain norms of perturbations remain manageable, which is crucial for stability analysis.

2. **Stability of Intermediate Layers**: Utilizing the derived bounds, the authors outline stability criteria for intermediate layers and backward propagation, demonstrating that these layers maintain controlled behavior in the presence of perturbations.

3. **Special Rank-1 Perturbation**: The authors address rank-one perturbations in the context of adversarial attacks, providing bounds that ensure stability under these conditions.

4. **Conclusion and Future Directions**: The paper concludes by summarizing its contributions and emphasizing the need for extensive proofs, which are elaborated upon in supplementary sections. The techniques introduced not only clarify the behavior of ReLU activations but also provide a foundation for future research on deep learning stability and resilience against adversarial perturbations.

Overall, this work represents a significant advancement in understanding the dynamics of multi-layer neural networks, particularly RNNs, and lays the groundwork for further exploration in the field.
