The referenced papers explore various theoretical and practical aspects of deep learning, particularly focusing on neural networks. 

Tian (2017) presents an analytical formula for the population gradient in two-layered ReLU networks, contributing to convergence and critical point analysis, which aids in understanding training dynamics. Xiao et al. (2018) investigate dynamical isometry in convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and propose a mean field theory to facilitate the training of extremely deep networks, addressing challenges associated with training architectures of 10,000 layers. 

Yang and Schoenholz (2018) introduce deep mean field theory, emphasizing layerwise variance and width variation as strategies to mitigate gradient explosion, which is a common issue in deep learning. In another work, Yang and Schoenholz (2017) discuss mean field residual networks, examining their behavior near critical points, referred to as being on the edge of chaos. 

Zhang et al. (2017) argue that a comprehensive understanding of deep learning necessitates a reevaluation of generalization principles, highlighting the complexities involved in model performance and learning dynamics. Zhong et al. (2017) present methods for learning non-overlapping convolutional neural networks with multiple kernels, thereby enhancing network efficiency and performance. In a related study, Zhong et al. (2017) provide recovery guarantees for one-hidden-layer neural networks, establishing theoretical foundations for their learning capabilities. 

Overall, these works collectively contribute to the theoretical underpinnings and practical methodologies for advancing deep learning research and applications.
