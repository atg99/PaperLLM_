The section discusses the optimization of a weight matrix W in the context of a regression objective using gradient descent and stochastic gradient descent. The objective function is defined as a sum of individual loss functions, and the gradient with respect to W is derived using the chain rule. 

Key results include two main theorems that demonstrate the convergence properties of gradient descent and stochastic gradient descent under certain conditions. Both methods achieve linear convergence rates, showing that the number of layers L influences the convergence polynomially, which prevents issues like exponential gradient explosion or vanishing during training.

The paper also presents two technical theorems that underpin the main results. The first theorem, akin to the Polyak-Łojasiewicz condition, indicates that the norm of the gradient is at least proportional to the objective value, ensuring a relationship between the gradient and the objective. The second theorem highlights a semi-smoothness property of the objective function, providing a bound on the function’s behavior around a perturbed weight matrix.

Overall, the findings indicate that random initialization of weights can lead to effective optimization in recurrent neural networks, maintaining robust performance even with long input sequences.
