The paper "On the Convergence Rate of Training Recurrent Neural Networks" by Zeyuan Allen-Zhu, Yuanzhi Li, and Zhao Song addresses the effectiveness of local-search methods, particularly stochastic gradient descent (SGD), in training multi-layer neural networks, specifically recurrent neural networks (RNNs). The authors highlight the challenges of analyzing RNNs due to their repeated application of recurrent units over time, which complicates the landscape compared to feedforward networks.

The key findings indicate that when the number of neurons is sufficiently large—specifically polynomial in both the training data size and the time horizon of the RNN—SGD can minimize regression loss at a linear convergence rate. This observation provides theoretical support for how RNNs can effectively memorize data.

Additionally, the authors develop analytical toolkits for multi-layer networks utilizing ReLU activations. They demonstrate that ReLU activations help prevent issues like exponential gradient explosion or vanishing gradients, and introduce a perturbation theory for examining first-order approximations of these networks.

Overall, the paper contributes to the understanding of why SGD can avoid bad local minima in deeper networks and offers insights into the training dynamics of RNNs, affirming their capability to achieve global minima under suitable conditions.
