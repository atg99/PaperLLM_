The paper discusses the limitations of using spectral bound arguments to derive specific results related to neural network stability and backpropagation. It highlights that the spectral norm of the weight matrix W remains at a high level, even when ReLU activations are considered, leading to exponential growth in complexity with depth L. Instead, the authors propose an alternative approach, demonstrating that the norm of a certain expression is bounded by O(1) with high probability for fixed sparse vectors. This conclusion is extended to dense vectors by dividing them into smaller chunks.

The authors also address backpropagation, presenting a result concerning the spectral norm of diagonal matrices. The proof follows a similar strategy to previous results but acknowledges different spectral norms involved.

In the section on stability after adversarial perturbation, the authors examine the behavior of recurrent neural networks (RNNs) post-perturbation. They define a framework for understanding the matrix W after adversarial changes and introduce the concept of "randomness decomposition." This technique allows for a more nuanced analysis of the network's behavior during training updates.

The first main result outlines the forward stability of the network, providing bounds on various norms related to the perturbed matrices. The proof is complex, requiring a recursive expansion rather than simple induction due to the challenges posed by adversarial changes. The authors detail their approach, which involves balancing terms arising from the recursive expansion.

Overall, the paper presents significant insights into the stability of neural networks under adversarial conditions and the implications for training and backpropagation techniques.
