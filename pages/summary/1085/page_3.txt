The paper presents a significant advancement in the training of recurrent neural networks (RNNs) through gradient descent (GD) and stochastic gradient descent (SGD). The main theorem asserts that if the number of neurons is polynomially large, weight matrices can be found such that the RNN achieves a specified training error after a polynomial number of iterations, starting from random Gaussian initializations.

Key contributions include the first proof of convergence for GD/SGD in training hidden layers of RNNs with activation functions, providing theoretical backing for previous empirical observations on the ability of SGD to navigate local minima. The authors introduce new analytical tools for multi-layer networks with ReLU activation, which have broader applications, including guarantees on testing error in PAC-learning terms.

The findings extend to feedforward neural networks, deep RNNs, and various loss functions beyond `2 regression loss, indicating the robustness of the results. The paper also references related works, highlighting the distinction from methods using tensor initialization and literature employing mean-field theory, which lacks theoretical convergence rates for finite neuron counts.

Overall, the work enhances understanding of neural network training dynamics and offers a framework for further exploration in both RNN and deep learning contexts.
