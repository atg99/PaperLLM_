The paper presents a summary of various representative GraphDA works focused on graph semi-supervised learning, detailing different approaches to augmenting data and improving model performance. Key methods are categorized into four main topics: Graph Consistency Training, Graph Self-/Co-Training, Graph Data Interpolation, and Graph Imbalanced Training.

1. **Graph Consistency Training**: This method emphasizes maintaining consistency across predictions from various stochastic augmentations of the same input. Notable works include NodeAug, which employs feature corruption and edge perturbation, and GRAND, which utilizes node dropping and feature masking. These methods minimize the KL-divergence or consistency losses between representations from original and augmented graphs, facilitating better learning under low-resource conditions.

2. **Graph Self-/Co-Training**: This approach leverages unlabeled data to enhance the limited labeled data, mainly through pseudo-labeling. Methods like CGCN and M3S combine self-training with graph neural networks to generate and refine pseudo labels. Co-training methods, such as Co-GCN, further enhance this by allowing multiple classifiers to label unlabeled data for mutual benefit.

3. **Graph Data Interpolation**: Techniques like G-Transplant and GraphMix utilize interpolation-based strategies, such as feature and label mixing, to create synthetic training examples. This is crucial as graphs possess arbitrary structures, making it challenging to meaningfully connect original and synthetic nodes.

4. **Graph Imbalanced Training**: Addressing data imbalance, methods like GATSMOTE and GraphSMOTE apply feature rewriting and node insertion strategies to augment the training set, ensuring a more balanced representation across classes.

Overall, the paper highlights the advancements in graph semi-supervised learning through diverse augmentation techniques, which are essential for improving model robustness and accuracy in scenarios with limited labeled data.
