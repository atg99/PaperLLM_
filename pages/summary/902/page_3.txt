The paper discusses techniques in graph data augmentation (GraphDA) and their application in enhancing graph neural networks (GNNs) for various tasks, particularly focusing on link prediction and graph classification. It defines two main types of tasks: link-level tasks, which involve predicting edges between specific node pairs, and graph-level tasks, which predict properties of entire graphs based on their embeddings.

GraphDA aims to enhance the information preserved in graphs through transformation functions that generate augmented graphs. The methods are classified into non-learnable and learnable categories. Learnable methods are further divided into three training strategies: decoupled training, joint training, and bi-level optimization.

In decoupled training, the augmenter and predictor are trained separately in two stages, first optimizing the augmentation loss and then the utility loss related to downstream tasks. Joint training involves simultaneous optimization of both losses, treating the process as multi-task learning. Bi-level optimization alternates between updating the augmenter and predictor, with the updates of each dependent on the current state of the other.

The paper proposes a systematic taxonomy of GraphDA techniques based on the modality of augmentation: feature-oriented, structure-oriented, and label-oriented techniques. The discussion on structure-oriented augmentations is introduced, indicating a comprehensive overview of various augmentation strategies that can be applied to improve GNN performance on downstream tasks.
