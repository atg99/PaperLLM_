The paper presents a summary of various representative works in graph self-supervised learning, particularly focusing on GraphDA (Graph Data Augmentation) techniques. It categorizes the approaches into two main areas: Graph Generative Modeling and Graph Contrastive Learning, detailing their methodologies, year of publication, and the types of data augmentation employed.

In Graph Generative Modeling, several methods such as GraphMAE, GMAE, and MGM utilize techniques like feature masking and node dropping to generate augmented graphs for training. These methods aim to enhance node and graph representation learning through various augmentation strategies.

Graph Contrastive Learning methods, including S3-CL and SUGRL, often combine multiple augmentation strategies to optimize the learning process. Techniques like edge perturbation and graph sampling are highlighted as common practices to create diverse views of graph data, improving the robustness of the learned representations.

The paper also discusses the evolution of augmentation strategies from predefined methods to learnable approaches, exemplified by works like JOAO and LG2AR, which automatically select the best augmentation techniques based on the dataset characteristics.

Furthermore, it touches on Graph Semi-Supervised Learning, particularly Graph Consistency Training, which leverages unlabeled data to enhance model performance, paralleling the principles of contrastive learning.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of diverse and adaptive data augmentation techniques in advancing graph self-supervised learning methodologies.
