The paper discusses various methods for graph data augmentation (GraphDA) aimed at enhancing the robustness, expressiveness, and scalability of deep graph learning (DGL) models. It highlights the cascading effects of adding edges in graph data, which can significantly alter semantic meanings. To address these challenges, techniques such as Manifold Mixup and GraphMix have been employed. These methods facilitate semi-supervised node classification by training fully connected networks alongside graph neural networks (GNNs) through parameter sharing and pseudo-labeling.

The paper also addresses the issue of class imbalance in graph data, where minority classes are underrepresented. Techniques such as node insertion, feature mixing, and graph rewiring are suggested to augment minority classes effectively. For instance, GraphSMOTE and GraphMixup enhance minority class nodes and predict edges between synthetic and existing nodes. The paper notes that challenges remain, particularly in scenarios with very few labeled minority nodes, necessitating further research into knowledge transfer from majority to minority classes.

Graph structure learning is presented as a solution to optimize graph structures that may contain fake connections or other inefficiencies. Techniques such as graph rewiring and edge prediction based on node embeddings are discussed, along with optimization methods that incorporate graph data directly into the objective functions.

Additionally, the paper covers graph feature denoising, emphasizing the need for methods to handle missing or abnormal node features. Techniques such as feature propagation and Gaussian mixture models are mentioned as ways to reconstruct missing features.

Lastly, the paper highlights the vulnerabilities of GNNs to adversarial attacks, suggesting that GraphDA can help recover and bolster graphs affected by such attacks. Overall, the work underscores the importance of graph data augmentation in achieving reliable and robust graph learning in various applications.
