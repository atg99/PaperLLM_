This paper provides a comprehensive survey on data augmentation methods for deep graph learning, focusing on the challenges and techniques involved in augmenting graph-structured data. Graph neural networks (GNNs) have shown significant success in various graph learning tasks, but their performance is often hindered by issues such as data noise and scarcity. The authors formally define the problem of graph data augmentation (GraphDA) and categorize existing techniques based on the modalities of augmented information.

The survey introduces a taxonomy of graph data augmentation methods and reviews their applications in two main areas: reliable graph learning, which aims to enhance the quality of input graphs and the model's capacity, and low-resource graph learning, which seeks to increase the scale of labeled training data. For each area, the authors provide a hierarchical taxonomy and examine relevant literature.

The necessity for effective data augmentation in graph learning is emphasized, particularly due to the complexities of graph data, which include heterogeneous information and intricate properties. The paper identifies the lack of a systematic approach in current GraphDA research, which often leads to arbitrary application of techniques. The authors advocate for clearer design principles to facilitate the effective use of GraphDA in addressing specific deep graph learning challenges.

Lastly, the paper outlines promising future research directions and highlights the challenges that remain in the field of graph data augmentation, underscoring the need for a structured understanding to advance the effectiveness of deep graph learning.
