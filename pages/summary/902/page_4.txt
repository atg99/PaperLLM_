The paper discusses various techniques and applications for Graph Data Augmentation (GraphDA), highlighting the unique relational nature of graphs where the connections between nodes are crucial for analysis. It categorizes these techniques into structure-oriented and feature-oriented augmentations.

Key structure-oriented methods include:

1. **Edge Perturbation**: This method involves randomly adding or dropping edges in the adjacency matrix of the graph. It maintains the original node order while altering some entries through a corruption matrix, typically generated from a prior distribution.

2. **Graph Rewiring**: Unlike edge perturbation, graph rewiring aims to enhance the utility of the graph by strategically modifying edges based on the downstream task's learning objectives.

3. **Graph Diffusion**: This technique improves graph utility by integrating global structure knowledge, connecting nodes with their indirectly linked neighbors. It can be mathematically expressed using a generalized transition matrix and is exemplified by methods like Personalized PageRank and heat kernel.

4. **Graph Sampling**: This method creates augmented views through various sampling techniques (vertex-based, edge-based, traversal-based) to retain connected subgraphs while preserving desired properties of the original graph.

The paper emphasizes the importance of these augmentation techniques in enhancing the performance of Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) and addresses challenges such as over-smoothing, scalability, and expressiveness in GNN training. Techniques like feature corruption and pseudo-labeling are also mentioned as part of feature-oriented augmentations, contributing to the broad scope of GraphDA applications.
