The paper presents a comprehensive survey on GraphDA, marking it as the first of its kind in addressing data-centric deep graph learning (DGL) problems. The authors offer a formal definition of GraphDA and review recent advancements to enhance understanding and encourage future research. 

Key contributions include a detailed taxonomy of GraphDA techniques categorized by augmentation modalities—feature-oriented, structure-oriented, and label-oriented—providing a framework for developing tailored GraphDA methods for various DGL challenges. The survey also explores GraphDA applications in two significant data-centric DGL problems: optimal graph learning and low-resource graph learning, highlighting prevalent learning paradigms and outlining open issues and future directions in the field.

The paper distinguishes itself from existing surveys that focus on singular DGL issues by providing a systematic overview of GraphDA methods applicable to multiple problems. It also identifies under-explored areas that could inform future research in DGL.

The structure of the survey begins with an introduction to graph neural networks (GNNs) and deep graph learning, followed by a taxonomy of GraphDA techniques. Subsequent sections detail applications in low-resource and reliable graph learning, discussing techniques for graph self-supervised and semi-supervised learning, as well as methods to enhance the robustness and scalability of DGL models. The final section addresses challenges and potential future research avenues in GraphDA.

Key notations and definitions are provided for clarity, focusing on undirected attributed graphs and outlining the mathematical foundations of GNNs and DGL tasks, including node-level and edge-level tasks. The paper serves as a foundational resource for understanding GraphDA and its applications in advancing DGL methodologies.
