The paper discusses the implementation of a multi-task model through the `RegisterMultiTaskModel` class. The `MyMultiTaskParams` class inherits from `MultiTaskModelParams` and includes methods for training and defining model parameters for multiple tasks. In the `Train` method, parameters for two tasks, `task1` and `task2`, are defined, allowing for flexibility in model configuration.

The `Model` method initializes the model's architecture by defining parameters for both tasks and setting up task probabilities, ensuring the model can handle multiple tasks effectively. The model is named 'my_multitask_model' and assigns equal probabilities to both tasks.

The paper also highlights the differences between training and inference phases, emphasizing the importance of latency and the constraints influencing computational choices such as available operations, desired latency, parallelizability, and resource consumption. It advocates for allowing model designers to explicitly export inference graphs to optimize performance, rather than relying on automatic graph conversion. This approach enables the reuse of training functions and the possibility of modifying low-level implementations for complex inference requirements.
