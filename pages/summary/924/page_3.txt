This paper reviews several prominent approaches in metric learning, focusing on Triplet Loss, Proxy Loss, and Multi-Similarity Loss, while also identifying a common shortcoming in these methods.

Triplet Loss, introduced by Schroff et al., enhances Contrastive Loss by minimizing the distance between an anchor and a positive sample while maximizing the distance to a negative sample. The loss is formulated to encourage a specified margin between these distances, and semi-hard negative sample mining is utilized for efficient convergence. However, it fails to optimally direct the movement of negative samples based on the positive sample's presence.

Proxy Loss addresses the limitations of previous methods by using proxy embeddings instead of actual samples, allowing for simultaneous learning of sample and proxy embeddings. This method minimizes the distance from an anchor to its corresponding class proxy while maximizing the distance to proxies of other classes. Despite its advantages, it lacks an explicit mechanism for directing the optimization towards negative proxies.

Multi-Similarity Loss seeks to improve upon existing formulations by focusing on the most informative pairs for optimization. It incorporates relative similarities among positive and negative samples and utilizes a binomial deviance loss. However, it primarily optimizes distances from individual pairs without considering the broader relationships among all samples, which could enhance performance.

The paper identifies a key shortcoming in current metric learning methods: they often do not effectively consider the directions of repulsion from multiple samples. This leads to a proposed enhancement in the optimization criterion, particularly in the context of the Triplet Loss framework, where the gradients and update directions for the embeddings are computed. The analysis aims to refine the loss formulation to achieve better optimization performance in metric learning tasks.
