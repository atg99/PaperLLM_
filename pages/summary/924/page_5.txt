This paper discusses the integration of a direction regularization term into the standard triplet loss formulation for metric learning. The direction regularization aims to enhance the dynamics of minimizing the loss by controlling the spatial relationship between anchor, positive, and negative samples. The modified loss incorporates a term that penalizes configurations where the anchor is collinear with the negative and positive samples, which can lead to suboptimal placements.

The direction regularization parameter, denoted as gamma, adjusts the magnitude of regularization applied to the loss. By minimizing the negative cosine similarity between the anchor and positive samples, the method encourages a more orthogonal arrangement, thus improving the distances between the anchor and negative sample. This approach is particularly effective in cases where the positive sample is close to the negative sample, as it allows for adaptive weighting of gradients based on the relative distances of embeddings.

The paper provides gradient equations that demonstrate how the direction regularization alters the behavior of the gradients associated with the anchor, positive, and negative samples. Specifically, the incorporation of scaling factors allows for a nuanced adjustment of the gradient contributions, prioritizing the movement of the anchor away from the negative sample while maintaining a focus on the positive sample.

Overall, the direction regularization term enhances the triplet loss by dynamically adapting the gradient vectors based on the current configuration of samples, thus encouraging the system to seek more informative examples for updating the embedding space. This adaptive mechanism is argued to address the limitations of traditional metric learning losses, which do not explicitly enforce orthogonality between negative and positive samples.
