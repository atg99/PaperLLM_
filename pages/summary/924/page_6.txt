The paper discusses the integration of regularization terms into various metric learning loss functions, specifically focusing on Triplet Loss, Proxy Loss, and Multi-Similarity Loss. 

For Triplet Loss, the formulation is presented in a clearer manner, highlighting the distance between anchor and positive/negative samples, along with a cosine similarity regularization term. Proxy Loss is described as using a single anchor and proxy embedding for positive samples, with multiple negative proxies. The loss is formulated to incorporate cosine regularization with the anchor and positive proxy fixed. Multi-Similarity Loss is similarly adjusted to include regularization for negative samples based on the hardest positive sample.

The experimental section details the methodology used for evaluating the proposed models, employing GoogLeNet with batch normalization and fine-tuning it on three datasets: CUB-200-2011, Cars-196, and In-Shop Clothes Retrieval. The datasets are described in terms of image counts and class distributions, specifying training and testing splits.

Performance comparisons are made using the Recall@K metric, demonstrating that the proposed direction regularized version of Multi-Similarity Loss (DR-MS) outperforms existing methods across the datasets. Notably, DR-MS achieves significant improvements in Recall@1, indicating its effectiveness in fine-grained image retrieval tasks, particularly with an embedding dimension of 64.

Overall, the results suggest that the integration of directional regularization enhances the performance of metric learning approaches in fine-grained classification scenarios.
