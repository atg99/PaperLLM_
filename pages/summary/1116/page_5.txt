The section discusses two types of evolution strategies (ES): ES(l+k) and ES(l,k), and introduces evolutionary programming (EP) as well as the challenges faced by evolutionary algorithms (EAs).

ES(l+k) is an extension of ES(1+1) that incorporates a self-adaptive mutation range, replacing the 1/5 success rule. Each individual has an additional chromosome for standard deviation values, which are utilized during the mutation process. Crossover occurs before mutation, affecting both the variable values and the standard deviation parameters. 

In contrast, ES(l,k) focuses on selection, where the new population is formed exclusively from the best individuals of the "children" population, ensuring that the number of parents exceeds that of children. This selection method mitigates the risk of a single dominating individual, which can skew the mutation parameters in ES(l+k).

The state-of-the-art CMA-ES (Covariance Matrix Adaptation Evolution Strategy) is highlighted for its efficiency and robustness. It employs global weighted recombination and adapts the full covariance matrix for mutation, effectively handling poorly scaled functions and maintaining performance across different search space rotations.

EP, initially developed for grammar discovery, has gained traction as a numerical optimization technique. It differs from ES(l+k) in that it creates a new population by mutating all individuals from the parental population, with equal probability for selection. The newly formed and parental populations are of equal size, and ranking selection is used to generate the next generation.

Finally, the section notes that EAs continue to present numerous challenges, particularly in balancing exploration and exploitation.
