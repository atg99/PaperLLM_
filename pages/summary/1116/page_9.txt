Genetic algorithms (GAs) serve as a versatile optimization tool applicable to various real-life problems, including constrained, multimodal, continuous, combinatorial, and multi-objective optimization challenges. This section highlights several applications of GAs.

One application is presented by Lv et al., who utilized GAs to optimize the solar array layout for stratospheric airships. Their results indicated that this optimization significantly enhances the output power of solar panels. Ma et al. developed a GA-based optimization model aimed at reducing energy consumption in high-sulfur natural gas purification, achieving a 12.7% reduction in a case study.

Yin et al. introduced a GA-inspired strategy in a sequential evolutionary filter, eliminating the need for resampling and improving particle diversity, resulting in superior state estimation compared to other filters. The hybridization of GAs with local search methods was explored by another team for routing and spectrum allocation in elastic optical networks, where their method outperformed competitors while maintaining population diversity through Baldwin effects.

Ramos et al. presented a GA-based approach to solve a local-inventory-routing model for perishable products, integrating warehouse location, inventory levels, and vehicle routes. Their method effectively provided high-quality near-optimal solutions despite the NP-hard nature of the problem.

Additionally, a new container loading algorithm was introduced using a multi-population biased random-key GA, which successfully addressed load balance, weight limits, and stability constraints.

In summary, GAs are widely utilized across various fields, demonstrating their effectiveness in optimizing complex real-world problems.
