The paper discusses the advantages of Differential Evolution (DE) over traditional Genetic Algorithms (GAs). DE is characterized by its ease of use, efficient memory utilization, lower computational complexity, and faster convergence, making it better suited for large problems. The standard DE procedure involves optimizing problems with multiple decision variables, utilizing parameters such as mutation scaling factor (F) and crossover rate (CR). DE operates by computing the difference between randomly selected individuals, which helps avoid local extrema.

The paper also contrasts DE with Evolution Strategies (ES), highlighting differences in their selection procedures. In ES, a temporary population of varying size is created, and the best individuals are selected for the next generation based on deterministic criteria. ES operates on floating-point vectors, unlike GAs, which use binary vectors. The oldest ES approach, ES(1+1), evolves a single individual through mutation without crossover, adapting the mutation range based on a success ratio.

The paper includes a table summarizing various modifications of DE, showcasing advancements such as Colonial Competitive DE and Memory-based DE, which incorporate sociopolitical models and swarm operators, respectively, into the DE framework. Overall, the paper emphasizes the evolution and adaptability of DE and ES as optimization methods.
