The paper presents a series of studies that utilize genetic algorithms (GA) and genetic programming (GP) to address various real-life problems across different fields. 

In the work by Yan et al., a framework is introduced for optimizing investment plans to enhance railway systems against earthquake hazards. This framework, which includes an investment optimization model solved using GA, proved to be more effective than traditional topology-based methods when applied to the Chinese railway system.

Ascione et al. focus on optimizing operating costs for building energy performance by using a GA to adjust hourly setpoint temperatures based on weather and occupancy forecasts. Their approach results in operating cost reductions of up to 56% compared to standard strategies.

Lin et al. design a time-optimal train running reference curve that minimizes time while maximizing energy efficiency through a multi-point coasting control strategy. They employ a multi-population GA to solve this complex optimization problem, demonstrating significant energy savings.

In addressing urban traffic congestion, a study applies GA to minimize average delays at signalized intersections under oversaturated conditions, achieving a reduction in average delay to 55 seconds per vehicle.

Zhang et al. utilize a flexible GA for node placement in engineering applications, showing superior performance in solving such problems compared to existing methods.

Reddy presents a GA-based strategy for optimal scheduling in hybrid generation systems that incorporates uncertainties in renewable energy sources and load forecasts, leading to a notable reduction in real-time adjustment costs.

The applications of GP are also highlighted, including a handwriting recognition system that outperforms existing methods for Persian/Arabic characters and a gene expression programming model for flood routing that shows superior performance to traditional techniques.

Abkenar et al. propose a GP-based power management strategy for fuel cells in all-electric ships, optimizing performance and fuel consumption without DC/DC converters. Lastly, a GP algorithm is used to predict pavement distress in urban road networks, enabling better resource allocation for maintenance.

Overall, these studies illustrate the versatility and effectiveness of GA and GP in solving complex optimization and prediction problems in various sectors.
