The paper discusses several advancements and areas for improvement in the field of evolutionary algorithms (EAs), particularly focusing on self-adaptive control of steering parameters, the reduction of CMA-ES algorithm parameters, and the introduction of new selection schemes to enhance effectiveness. These developments are crucial for applications in evolutionary design and evolvable hardware, emphasizing the need for more efficient constraint handling techniques.

A significant area of research highlighted is the application of EAs to dynamic optimization problems, optimization in noisy and non-stationary environments, and multi-objective optimization problems, especially those with a large number of decision variables. The authors call for further exploration into population size adaptation across different optimization scenarios and the development of novel strategies to address expensive problem-solving more competitively.

The paper categorizes constraint handling methods in EAs into six main types: penalty methods, methods evolving in the feasible region, parallel population approaches, methods favoring feasible individuals, multi-objective optimization techniques, and hybrid methods. Each category can be subdivided further, and the authors reference a taxonomy of these techniques from previous work. They stress the importance of selecting the appropriate constraint handling method based on specific questions related to the objective function and its feasibility.

Additionally, the paper lists 24 selected algorithms from the evolutionary strategies (ES) and evolutionary programming (EP) families, providing brief descriptions and the years of their introduction, demonstrating the evolution and variety of techniques in this field.
