The paper provides an overview of evolutionary algorithms (EAs), focusing on three key types: genetic algorithms (GAs), genetic programming (GP), and differential evolution (DE). 

Genetic algorithms are established optimization techniques inspired by natural selection and Darwinian evolution. They operate on a population of potential solutions, represented as chromosomes, and evaluate their quality through a fitness function. The GA employs three main operators: selection, crossover, and mutation, which facilitate the generation of new solutions based on the performance of existing ones. Various modifications of the standard GA are acknowledged.

Genetic programming, a specialized form of GA, aims to automatically generate program code based on known evaluation criteria. Unlike GAs, GP represents solutions as tree structures rather than linear chromosomes, utilizing genetic operators tailored for tree manipulation, such as subtree crossover and node mutation.

Differential evolution is highlighted as an algorithm primarily focused on continuous function optimization. While there are adaptations for combinatorial problems, the main application of DE remains in continuous search spaces.

The paper concludes with a discussion of recent advances and current trends in these evolutionary algorithms, emphasizing their relevance in optimization tasks.
