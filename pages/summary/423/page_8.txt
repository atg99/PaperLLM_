The paper discusses the Deep SORT algorithm, which is employed to effectively track objects across multiple frames in a video sequence. This algorithm uses a matching cascade strategy that combines two metrics: the Mahalanobis distance (D1) for short-term location predictions based on object movement, and the cosine distance (D2) to incorporate appearance information and manage long-term occlusions. 

The association between the current Kalman state and new measurements is optimally determined using the Hungarian algorithm. The pseudocode for the Hungarian algorithm outlines the steps to solve the assignment problem, initializing variables and processing the bipartite graph to find a complete matching.

The weighted association sum is calculated for each track and bounding box as a function of both distances, regulated by a hyperparameter (la) that determines the influence of each metric. The Mahalanobis distance is defined in relation to the bounding box detection and track projection, while the cosine distance is derived from the appearance descriptors of the bounding boxes. 

Overall, the paper emphasizes the integration of movement and appearance information to enhance the robustness of object tracking in video sequences.
