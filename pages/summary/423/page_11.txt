The paper evaluates various datasets used for object detection and tracking tasks, highlighting the number of sequences and frames involved. For object detection, the mean Average Precision (mAP) is employed as the evaluation metric, while Precision and Success Rate are used for tracking performance, calculated through the one-pass evaluation process. The tracking precision is assessed using the center location error, and the success rate is measured via the intersection over union of the tracked and ground truth bounding boxes.

The proposed method is implemented using Pytorch on specific hardware, and hyper-parameters such as a learning rate of 0.001 with the AdamW optimizer are utilized. The model's performance is compared with the standard Faster R-CNN after fine-tuning on the UMCD dataset, which contains unique object classes. Results indicate that the proposed model achieves a mAP of 97.3%, outperforming the Faster R-CNN's 95.6% in certain scenarios, particularly in distinguishing objects accurately without misclassifying shadows. This demonstrates the effectiveness of the proposed method in aerial object detection and tracking tasks.
