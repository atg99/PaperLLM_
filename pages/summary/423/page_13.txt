The study presents a thorough evaluation of the proposed tracking method, labeled as MS, against existing state-of-the-art techniques using precision and success metrics across different datasets, including UAVDT, UAV123, and UAV20L. The results indicate that MS achieves the highest precision on the UAVDT dataset (0.710) and performs competitively on UAV123 and UAV20L, securing second place in both cases. In terms of success rates, MS excels on the UAV20L dataset, while it ranks second on UAVDT and UAV123, demonstrating its effectiveness in tracking objects over extended sequences.

Despite its superior tracking capabilities, the MS model records lower Frames per Second (FPS) compared to its competitors, mainly due to the choice of the Faster R-CNN base model, which prioritizes accuracy over speed. The paper suggests that limiting the number of proposed regions can enhance performance without significantly sacrificing accuracy. Overall, the findings highlight the trade-off between tracking precision and computational efficiency in UAV tracking applications.
