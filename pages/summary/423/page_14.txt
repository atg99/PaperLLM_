The paper discusses the performance limitations of a model running on CPU, attributing low frames per second (FPS) to the specific CPU used, which has a lower base clock frequency compared to a more powerful Intel processor. An ablation study is performed to evaluate the effectiveness of different model streams. The baseline model, consisting only of the first stream with 3x3 convolutional layers, shows a significant drop in performance, achieving a mean average precision (mAP) of 58.7% on the UMCD dataset. Models utilizing only the second or third streams, which employ larger filter sizes of 5x5 and 7x7 respectively, yield even poorer results, with mAPs of 53.5% and 52.8%. This decline in performance is attributed to the larger filter sizes extracting less fine-grained features, emphasizing the importance of the first stream for optimal model performance. The study concludes that the combination of all streams is crucial for achieving better results in object detection and tracking tasks.
