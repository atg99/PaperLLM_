The generalizability experiment presented in this paper evaluates the robustness of the proposed XIC model by comparing its performance with the XLNet and XLNet-IDCNN models on the MARS dataset. The authors highlight a significant challenge due to the scarcity of Chinese datasets for Named Entity Recognition (NER) in the fungal domain, which limits model application and generalization. Despite the MARS dataset not being specific to edible fungi, it serves as a diverse benchmark for assessing model performance across various text types and named entities.

Experimental results indicate that the XLNet-IDCNN-CRF model performs notably well, achieving high F1-scores, precision, and recall. This outcome demonstrates the model's ability to effectively recognize named entities, showcasing its generalization capabilities despite training on a non-domain-specific dataset. The success is attributed to XLNet's pre-training mechanism, which captures rich contextual information essential for NER tasks, along with the IDCNN's capacity to identify local text features through its convolutional structure. The integration of a CRF layer enhances the model's ability to produce globally optimal labeled sequences, further boosting performance.

Moreover, the analysis reveals that the model exhibits minimal performance fluctuations on the MARS dataset, underscoring its robustness. Overall, the findings affirm the effectiveness of the XLNet-IDCNN-CRF model in NER tasks, even in the absence of specialized datasets.
