The paper evaluates the proposed model using three key metrics: precision (P), recall (R), and F1-score. Precision measures the ratio of true positive entities to the total labeled entities, recall assesses the ratio of true positive entities to the actual entities that should have been labeled, and the F1-score provides the harmonic mean of precision and recall. The calculations for these metrics utilize true positives (TP), false positives (FP), and false negatives (FN).

The ablation experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of the IDCNN-CRF model compared to the XLNet model. The XLNet-IDCNN model shows significant improvements with P, R, and F1-scores of 0.878, 0.961, and 0.917, respectively, indicating enhancements of 0.416, 0.166, and 0.333 over XLNet. The addition of the IDCNN layer contributes to better recognition performance across various entity types, notably improving the identification of 28 specific entities related to edible fungus information.

The IDCNN layer integrates Inception architecture and inflated convolution, enhancing feature extraction by processing input data at multiple scales and capturing long-term dependencies. This results in improved accuracy, especially for entities with unique length and construction styles.

Further performance improvement is achieved by incorporating a Conditional Random Field (CRF) on top of the IDCNN layer, optimizing recognition performance at 50 epochs. Overall, the proposed model demonstrates higher precision and recall, supporting future research and applications in related fields.
