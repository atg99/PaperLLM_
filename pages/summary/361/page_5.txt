This paper discusses the integration of the XLNet model and dilated convolutional neural networks (IDCNN) for enhancing entity recognition in the field of edible fungus. The XLNet model, built upon the BERT architecture, improves upon traditional autoregressive models by reordering input text through permutations, allowing for effective contextual analysis and bidirectional prediction. This model addresses data inconsistency issues present in BERT by utilizing a two-stream self-attention mechanism and relative positional encoding, which aids in capturing long-distance semantic information and improves feature extraction.

To further enhance the model's capabilities, the paper introduces IDCNN. Traditional convolutional networks often struggle with depth and computational efficiency, but dilated convolution expands the receptive field without increasing the number of parameters, thereby improving model performance. The paper illustrates how varying expansion widths in convolutional blocks can effectively increase the receptive field size while maintaining the same kernel size.

Overall, the combination of XLNet and IDCNN presents a robust framework for extracting contextual features and improving entity recognition in complex text datasets.
