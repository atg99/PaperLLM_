This paper addresses the challenges of Named Entity Recognition (NER) in the domain of edible fungi, particularly due to the independent positioning of entities within the text. It presents the creation of a specialized Chinese corpus for NER in edible fungus, derived from the "Atlas of Chinese Macrofungal Resources." This corpus underwent data cleaning, formatting, and entity annotation to facilitate NER tasks.

The proposed NER model, named XIC, integrates an IDCNN layer with the XLNet framework to enhance feature capture between statements. The IDCNN layer expands the convolutional kernel's receptive field, while a CRF layer is employed to optimize labeling sequences and reduce errors. The main contributions include the transformation of the Atlas into a usable corpus for NER, the introduction of an effective NER methodology, and the demonstration of the model's performance in recognizing entities related to edible fungi.

The dataset was meticulously prepared, involving the removal of non-text elements and segmentation of lengthy texts to improve recognition accuracy. Entity types were classified based on detailed analysis of the Atlas, leading to the formulation of 31 specific entity types for the NER model. The annotation process was conducted manually to ensure high accuracy, resulting in the identification of approximately 23,980 entities from a corpus of around 370,000 words. The study emphasizes the significance of this work for future NER tasks in the edible fungus domain.
