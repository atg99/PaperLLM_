The paper discusses the development of the XIC model, designed to enhance Named Entity Recognition (NER) in edible mushroom information. The model builds upon the XLNet architecture by integrating an Inception-Depthwise Convolutional Neural Network (IDCNN) layer and a Conditional Random Field (CRF) layer to address the complexities of the text, characterized by intricate vocabulary and sentence structures.

The choice of XLNet as a base model stems from its proven efficacy in natural language processing tasks. The IDCNN layer is pivotal for capturing long-distance dependencies within the text, allowing the model to recognize entities that may span multiple words and sentences. This layer enhances feature extraction by processing features at various scales and considering their interactions, thus improving recognition accuracy.

The CRF layer further optimizes the NER task by predicting the most probable labeling sequence. It effectively handles the dependencies between labels, reducing labeling errors that could skew the overall predictions. The experimental results demonstrate the superiority of the XIC model, achieving notable precision, recall, and F1 scores compared to other models such as Bert-CRF and BILSTM-CRF.

In summary, the XIC model significantly advances the performance of NER tasks in edible mushroom information by leveraging advanced neural network techniques tailored to the specific challenges of this domain.
