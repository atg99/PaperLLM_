The paper discusses a novel model, XIC, designed for named entity recognition (NER) in edible fungus information text, which addresses the label dependency problem common in sequence annotation tasks. By incorporating a Conditional Random Field (CRF) layer, the model improves both the accuracy of individual label predictions and the overall coherence of the label sequence, outperforming existing models like XLNet-IDCNN.

Experimental comparisons with several common NER models, including BERT-CRF, BiLSTM-CRF, IDCNN-CRF, and BERT-BiLSTM-CRF, reveal that while BERT-CRF and BiLSTM-CRF achieve decent performance, they struggle with long-distance contextual features. IDCNN-CRF, which relies on static Word2Vec embeddings, performs the poorest due to its limitations in feature extraction. In contrast, the XIC model, leveraging the strengths of XLNet, shows significant improvements in performance metrics, surpassing BERT-IDCNN-CRF by notable margins in precision, recall, and F1-scores.

The results indicate that the XIC model not only captures features more effectively across statements but also minimizes labeling errors, leading to enhanced NER performance for edible fungus texts. The paper emphasizes the importance of the CRF layer in achieving globally optimal label sequences, thereby offering robust support for applications in related fields.
