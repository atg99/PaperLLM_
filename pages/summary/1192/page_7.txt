The paper investigates the effectiveness of the Class-Aware Contrastive Semi-Supervised Learning (CCSSL) method in improving performance on various datasets, particularly in noisy environments. The authors demonstrate that CCSSL enhances the performance of the FixMatch algorithm on the CIFAR100 and STL10 datasets, achieving significant performance gains over original FixMatch and other state-of-the-art semi-supervised learning (SSL) methods. Specifically, on CIFAR100, improvements of up to 10.04% were noted with varying numbers of labeled data points. However, the performance gains tend to diminish as the noise level decreases, indicating that CCSSL is particularly beneficial in noisier contexts.

In a realistic evaluation using the Semi-iNat 2021 dataset, CCSSL consistently improved SSL performance under different noise levels, with more significant improvements observed in high-noise scenarios. The authors also highlight that training from pre-trained models with less noise yielded smaller performance gains, suggesting that CCSSL is most effective when noise levels are high.

Qualitative evaluations revealed that CCSSL enhances the focus on relevant objects in images, improving the model's ability to identify and locate features accurately. Additionally, the paper discusses the convergence speed of CCSSL methods, noting that they achieve better performance with fewer training epochs, attributed to the noise reduction effect of CCSSL.

The ablation study identifies the contributions of key components of CCSSL—contrastive learning, class-aware techniques, and re-weighting—showing that each component adds value to the overall performance.
