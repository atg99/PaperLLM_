The paper presents Class-aware Contrastive Semi-Supervised Learning (CCSSL), a novel method designed to enhance the quality of pseudo-labels and improve model robustness in semi-supervised learning (SSL) scenarios, particularly in real-world applications that include out-of-distribution data. Traditional pseudo-label-based SSL methods often suffer from confirmation bias due to noise in self-generated labels, leading to deteriorated performance, especially when faced with unbalanced or unknown classes.

CCSSL addresses this challenge by employing a strategy that treats reliable in-distribution data and noisy out-of-distribution data separately. It utilizes class-wise clustering for reliable data to optimize downstream tasks while implementing image-wise contrastive learning for noisy data to improve generalization. Additionally, the method incorporates target re-weighting to prioritize clean label learning and mitigate the impact of noisy labels.

The authors demonstrate that CCSSL significantly outperforms existing SSL techniques on standard datasets such as CIFAR100 and STL10. Notably, it improves the performance of FixMatch and CoMatch on the real-world dataset Semi-iNat 2021 by 9.80% and 3.18%, respectively. The paper emphasizes the importance of effective raw data utilization and presents a promising solution to enhance SSL performance in practical applications.
