This paper introduces a Class-Aware Contrastive Learning (CCSSL) module that enhances the training efficiency of semi-supervised learning (SSL) by leveraging the strengths of both self-supervised learning (Self-SL) and SSL. The CCSSL approach focuses on utilizing class-aware information for in-distribution data while minimizing noise from out-of-distribution data. 

The method employs pseudo-labels to create a supervised contrastive matrix, where embeddings from the same category are treated as positive pairs. To address the potential noise in model predictions, a threshold Tpush is established for clustering and contrasting in the feature space. Images with predictions above Tpush are considered likely in-distribution and are pulled closer together, while those below revert to standard contrastive learning practices.

To mitigate noise from out-of-distribution data, a foreground re-weighting module is introduced. This module emphasizes training on high-confidence in-distribution data while reducing bias from out-of-distribution samples. The target matrix is formulated by re-weighting the class-aware contrastive matrix based on confidence scores from model predictions.

The loss function for the CCSSL module is defined by minimizing the cross-entropy between a similarity matrix and the target matrix. The total loss is a weighted sum of supervised loss, semi-supervised loss, and class-aware contrastive loss.

The CCSSL method can be integrated with existing pseudo-label-based SSL approaches, such as MixMatch, FixMatch, and CoMatch, to alleviate confirmation bias and enhance training performance. Experimental results indicate that CCSSL improves SSL methods' performance and accelerates the training process across various datasets with varying noise levels.
