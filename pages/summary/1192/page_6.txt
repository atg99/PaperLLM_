This paper evaluates the performance of various semi-supervised learning (SSL) methods, particularly focusing on the proposed method CCSSL, across different datasets, including CIFAR10, CIFAR100, STL10, and Semi-iNat 2021. The results, summarized in tables, demonstrate that CCSSL enhances the performance of existing methods like FixMatch, particularly in high-noise scenarios.

In CIFAR100 and STL10, CCSSL applied to FixMatch achieves the best performance, indicating its effectiveness in handling noisy data. In contrast, on the CIFAR10 dataset, which has lower noise, the improvement is marginal. The paper also highlights the performance of CCSSL in the real-world dataset Semi-iNat 2021, where it significantly enhances accuracy in scenarios with substantial noise but shows limited improvement when starting with a pre-trained model due to reduced noise.

The datasets are described in detail, noting their structure and the experimental setup. For instance, CIFAR10 consists of 60K images divided into 10 classes, while CIFAR100 includes 50K training images across 100 classes. The experimental settings involve varying the number of randomly selected images to assess the model's performance under different conditions.

Qualitative comparisons using Grad-CAM illustrate that CCSSL improves the focus of SSL methods on relevant objects in images, enhancing their interpretability and effectiveness.

Overall, the findings suggest that CCSSL is a valuable addition to existing SSL frameworks, particularly in environments characterized by high noise levels.
