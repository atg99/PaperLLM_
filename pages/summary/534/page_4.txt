The paper discusses various transformation techniques for training an Autoencoder Transformation (AET) model, focusing on parameterized transformations, GAN-induced transformations, and non-parametric transformations. 

Parameterized transformations are modeled by the loss function ℓ(tθ,tˆθ), which measures the difference between target and estimated transformations. The study compares different parameterized transformations to demonstrate their effectiveness in training AET. GAN-induced transformations leverage a GAN generator to manipulate high-level semantics in images, allowing for more expressive representations, such as altering facial attributes or furniture layouts.

Non-parametric transformations are addressed by defining a loss function that measures the average difference between transformations of random images. While these transformations may not fit within a parameterized framework, they can still contribute to unsupervised learning by helping to develop feature representations. However, the paper finds that using parameter-based loss functions yields better performance compared to non-parametric approaches due to the insufficient sampling of images.

The authors emphasize their focus on parameterized transformations for AET training, as they simplify the process by avoiding additional models and enabling fair comparisons with existing unsupervised methods. The paper acknowledges the limitations of GAN-induced transformations, particularly their dependency on the quality of generated images, which are not yet at high resolution with fine details.

The experimental section evaluates the AET model on CIFAR-10, ImageNet, and Places datasets, comparing it against various unsupervised methods, particularly highlighting the performance of AET with affine and projective transformations on the CIFAR-10 dataset.
