Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) have gained prominence in unsupervised learning for training network representations of data, differing from auto-encoders by generating data directly from random noise rather than reconstructing input data. GANs aim to capture the entire distribution of data on a manifold, which enhances their ability to generalize and expressively represent images. 

In addition to GANs and auto-encoders, self-supervised representation learning leverages self-supervised signals derived from data without manual labeling. Various methods have emerged, such as using the relative positions of image patches, solving Jigsaw puzzles, counting features, and classifying image rotations. However, these methods can lead to over-discrimination among similar images and incur high training costs due to the creation of numerous surrogate classes.

The proposed approach, Auto-Encoding Transformations (AET), introduces a framework for learning representations through transformations. AET involves learning an encoder to extract features from original images and a decoder to estimate transformations based on these features. The model is trained by minimizing a loss function that quantifies the difference between actual and estimated transformations. 

AET can incorporate various types of transformations, including parameterized, GAN-induced, and non-parameterized transformations. Parameterized transformations are characterized by parameters sampled from a distribution, allowing for the representation of geometric changes through matrices that define transformations. This flexible formulation enables AET to effectively learn from diverse transformations, enhancing its application in representation learning.
