The paper presents a comparison of the performance of AET (Auto-Encoding Transformations) against RotNet on the CIFAR-10 and ImageNet datasets. AET is evaluated using various classifiers, including KNN and fully connected layers, indicating significant improvements over the RotNet baseline. The results show that AET consistently achieves lower error rates across different classifiers, with AET-project leading in performance metrics.

In the CIFAR-10 evaluations, AET demonstrates a considerable reduction in error rates compared to RotNet, with improvements of up to 17.0% in certain configurations. AET outperforms other unsupervised methods, including ExamplarCNN, suggesting a more effective exploration of image transformations during training. The comparison is made fair by using the same architecture for both AET and RotNet, ensuring the validity of results.

The paper also extends the evaluation to the ImageNet dataset, using AlexNet as the backbone for unsupervised feature learning. The AET-project model shows superior performance, achieving a top-1 accuracy of 53.2% on Conv4 and 47.0% on Conv5 layers, significantly surpassing other unsupervised methods and approaching the upper bounds set by fully supervised models.

Training details include using stochastic gradient descent with a substantial batch size and specific learning rate adjustments. Overall, the findings suggest that AET can effectively learn representations that closely rival those obtained through supervised learning, emphasizing its potential in unsupervised settings.
