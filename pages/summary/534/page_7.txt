The paper presents an evaluation of various unsupervised models on ImageNet using AlexNet as the backbone. The focus is on the Top-1 accuracy achieved through a 1,000-way linear classifier trained on different convolutional layers, specifically Conv1 to Conv5. The results, summarized in Table 4, compare the performance of the proposed AET-project against other models, including fully supervised and random models, which serve as upper and lower performance bounds respectively.

The findings indicate that the AET model consistently outperforms other unsupervised approaches, including BiGAN, which combines GAN and auto-encoder strengths. Notably, AET narrows the performance gap to the upper bound significantly, achieving a reduction of 33% and 19% in the gaps for Conv4 and Conv5 compared to previous models like RotNet and DeepCluster.

Additionally, experiments on the Places dataset further reinforce the effectiveness of the AET model, demonstrating its capability to generalize unsupervised features across different datasets. While AET excels in most comparisons, Counting shows slight superiority on Conv1 and Conv2 layers.

The paper also addresses the accuracy of predicting transformations, highlighting its relevance to the overall objective of learning robust image representations. Overall, AET-project stands out as a leading unsupervised model in this evaluation, showcasing its advantages in both performance and generalizability.
