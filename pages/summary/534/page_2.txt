This paper discusses the development of unsupervised feature representations through a novel paradigm called Auto-Encoding Transformations (AET), contrasting it with traditional Auto-Encoding Data (AED) methods and Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs). While AED focuses on reconstructing input data through an encoder-decoder framework, AET aims to learn representations by reconstructing transformations applied to the data instead of the data itself. This approach enables the exploration of dynamic visual structures and their changes due to various transformations, allowing for a broader range of transformation types without limitations.

The authors argue that by utilizing AET, they can derive informative features that capture the essence of transformations, which can subsequently be decoded to reveal visual structures. They posit that AET offers improved performance over existing unsupervised models, bringing them closer to the effectiveness of fully supervised methods.

The paper is organized into sections that review related work on auto-encoders, present the AET model, conduct comparative experiments with state-of-the-art unsupervised models, and summarize the findings. The related work highlights various auto-encoder architectures and their focus on data reconstruction, emphasizing the distinction between these approaches and the AET framework, which seeks to minimize discrepancies between input and output transformations.
