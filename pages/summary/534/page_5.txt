In section 4.1.1, the paper outlines the architecture and implementation details of the proposed AET model, which utilizes the Network-In-Network (NIN) architecture known for its strong performance on the CIFAR-10 dataset in unsupervised learning. The NIN comprises four convolutional blocks, each with three convolutional layers. AET features two NIN branches that process original and transformed images separately, concatenating their outputs to form a 384-dimensional feature vector for transformation parameter prediction. The model is trained using stochastic gradient descent (SGD) with specific hyperparameters, including momentum, weight decay, and a learning rate schedule. Transformations applied include affine and projective transformations, with AET-project showing superior performance compared to AET-affine.

In section 4.1.2, the evaluation protocol is described, where the quality of the learned representations is assessed by training a classifier on top of the features extracted from the second convolutional block. Both model-based and model-free classifiers are utilized, including a non-linear classifier with fully connected layers and a KNN classifier, allowing for a direct evaluation of unsupervised feature representations.

Results in section 4.1.3 demonstrate that AET models outperform existing unsupervised methods on CIFAR-10. Notably, the AET-project model with a convolutional classifier achieves an error rate of 7.82%, nearly matching the fully supervised NIN's rate of 7.20%, indicating the effectiveness of the AET approach in unsupervised feature learning.
