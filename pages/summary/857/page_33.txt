The paper discusses the evolution of Federated Averaging (FedAvg) in a synchronous context, noting its reliance on unrealistic assumptions regarding resource heterogeneity, varying computational abilities, network settings, and unbalanced data distributions. These factors lead to divergent training times and unknown communication costs. Previous research by Sprague et al. (2019) compared asynchronous aggregation schemes with FedAvg, yielding satisfactory outcomes. The paper highlights the concept of generous asynchronous training modes in federated learning (FL), which include asynchronous local updates and aggregation.

At the client level, Chen, Sun, and Jin (2019) proposed an asynchronous approach for updating client models by differentiating between deep and shallow layers based on their update frequency. On the server side, asynchronous aggregation can be implemented, as demonstrated by Chen et al. (2019) in an online FL framework that updates the central model asynchronously through feature learning and dynamic learning step sizes. Wu et al. (2019) introduced a semi-asynchronous protocol balancing the benefits of synchronous updates and asynchronous training, allowing straggling clients to participate in training without being tightly coupled with the central server. This semi-asynchronous method suggests that combining asynchronous modes with synchronous schemes could minimize overhead and enhance fault tolerance for stragglers.

In terms of gradient aggregation, the paper notes that the global model's gradient typically results from the weighted sum of gradients from each client, where weights are determined by sample ratios. However, there is a lack of evidence to support that this weighted averaging of gradients from local clients accurately reflects the true global gradient.
