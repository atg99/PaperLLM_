In this paper, we aim to establish a universal definition of Federated Learning (FL) by reviewing related studies and addressing the gaps in the existing literature. The structure of the paper includes an introduction, an overview of FL, challenges and improvements, applications, and future directions.

Section 2 provides a detailed overview of FL, highlighting its characteristics and mainstream open-source frameworks. FL is a form of distributed learning that emphasizes privacy and security, differentiating it from traditional distributed systems that focus on computational efficiency. While both involve distributed computation, FL prioritizes the protection of individual data by ensuring that sensitive information remains on local devices.

In Section 3, we identify three significant challenges in FL, including issues related to communication efficiency, system heterogeneity, and indirect information leakage. We also discuss relative improvements that can be made to address these challenges, particularly concerning existing privacy-preserving methods utilized in FL.

Section 4 explores practical applications of FL in Internet of Things (IoT) devices, industry engineering, and healthcare, demonstrating its versatility and relevance in contemporary technological contexts. We conclude by presenting frontier achievements in FL and suggesting promising directions for future research that could enhance our understanding and application of this innovative approach to machine learning.
