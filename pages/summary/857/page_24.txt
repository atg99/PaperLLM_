Kharitonov (2019) introduced ε-local differential privacy, a stricter approach that emphasizes user-level privacy rather than applying privacy-preserving methods post data aggregation. In federated learning (FL), homomorphic encryption serves as a significant privacy policy, preventing information leakage during parameter exchanges among clients. This encryption mechanism allows operations on encrypted parameters, achieving results equivalent to those from unencrypted functions. Liu et al. (2018) utilized additively homomorphic encryption to adapt neural network models with minimal impact on training accuracy. Ilias and Georgios (2019) incorporated this encryption into a more robust FL framework, enabling aggregation computations on encrypted client data. However, employing cryptographic models may lead to increased communication overhead due to the need to transmit additional data, such as private keys.

Locality-sensitive hashing (LSH), as discussed by Gionis et al. (1999), is another effective method for maintaining confidentiality. It maps features into an encrypted form using p-stable hash functions, preserving similarity between samples while ensuring that distinct samples generate dissimilar hash values. This method protects raw data since many samples can yield the same output, and it incurs less communication overhead compared to homomorphic encryption and does not compromise accuracy like differential privacy. Lee et al. (2018) applied LSH for identifying similar patients in federated settings, while Li et al. (2020) developed a practical gradient boosting decision tree model utilizing LSH for efficient discovery of similar samples across different clients. The sum of gradients from these similar samples is then used for model training, further enhancing privacy and efficiency in FL scenarios.
