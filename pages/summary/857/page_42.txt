The referenced papers collectively explore various methodologies and advancements in privacy-preserving techniques for machine learning, particularly focusing on federated learning and distributed systems. 

Gascón et al. (2017) present a framework for privacy-preserving distributed linear regression on high-dimensional data, emphasizing the importance of maintaining data privacy while enabling effective data analysis. Geyer et al. (2018) further discuss differentially private federated learning from a client-level perspective, highlighting the need for privacy at individual client interactions.

The work by Gionis et al. (1999) introduces hashing techniques for similarity search in high dimensions, which is crucial for efficient data retrieval in large datasets. Godinho et al. (2016) conduct a systematic review of monitoring technologies for assessing Parkinson's disease, underscoring the relevance of technology in healthcare applications.

Google's TensorFlow Federated (2019) provides tools for implementing federated learning, facilitating collaborative machine learning without centralized data storage. Han et al. (2019) investigate the application of federated learning in visual inspection tasks, demonstrating its potential in industrial contexts. 

Hao et al. (2019) focus on enhancing the efficiency and privacy of federated learning for industrial artificial intelligence, while their subsequent work (Haob et al., 2019) aims at improving privacy-preserving techniques in federated deep learning. Hard et al. (2019) explore federated learning applications for mobile keyboard prediction, showcasing practical implementations of federated approaches.

Finally, Hardy et al. (2017) address the challenges of private federated learning on vertically partitioned data using advanced encryption methods, signifying the ongoing efforts to safeguard data privacy in distributed learning environments. Collectively, these works contribute to the growing body of knowledge on balancing privacy and functionality in machine learning systems.
