The paper discusses the development of a collaborative model that prioritizes privacy while differentiating between Federated Learning (FL) and distributed learning. Key characteristics of FL are highlighted, notably its universality for cross-organizational scenarios. FL, initially proposed by Google, is an encrypted distributed machine learning technology that allows participants to collaboratively build a training model without sharing their local data. This concept has evolved to encompass all privacy-preserving decentralized collaborative machine learning techniques.

FL effectively addresses both horizontally partitioned data, which is divided by samples, and vertically partitioned data, which is split by features. This flexibility allows for collaborations across organizations, such as a bank sharing client purchasing power data with an e-commerce platform that has product feature data, ultimately leading to the creation of a joint recommendation model. This collaborative approach enables cross-platform and regional value creation while safeguarding data privacy.

Another significant aspect of FL is its handling of massively non-identically independent distribution (Non-IID) of data. In FL, data is distributed across numerous edge nodes or mobile devices, often with each node containing limited data compared to the total number of nodes. This contrasts with distributed systems, which primarily aim to enhance parallel processing to reduce the computational or storage burden on a central server. The scale of nodes in FL is considerably larger, especially with the rise of wearable devices that generate minimal data for health monitoring, underscoring the unique challenges and capabilities of FL in leveraging decentralized data for collaborative learning without compromising privacy.
