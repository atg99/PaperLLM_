The potential of FL in healthcare is significant due to its ability to maintain patient data privacy while enabling collaboration across medical institutions. Many hospitals possess substantial patient data, but often lack the volume necessary to develop effective predictive models independently. By integrating FL with disease prediction, barriers to data analysis among hospitals can be overcome.

Early efforts in applying FL in healthcare include the work by Kim et al. (2017), who utilized tensor factorization models for phenotyping analysis, revealing hidden insights in electronic health records (EHRs) without sharing individual patient data. Pfohl et al. (2019) explored differentially private learning in a federated context for EHRs, achieving performance levels comparable to centralized training. Huang et al. (2019) demonstrated the use of distributed EMRs to predict heart disease mortality rates without data transmission between hospitals, ensuring data privacy through pre-encoded central server data.

Moreover, Brisimi et al. (2018) investigated hospitalization predictions for heart disease patients using a FL algorithm known as cluster Primal Dual Splitting (cPDS), facilitating predictions without compromising patient information. Lee et al. (2018) introduced a federated patient hashing framework to identify similar patients across different hospitals while preserving patient privacy. This method assists healthcare providers in leveraging collective knowledge to enhance patient treatment. Finally, Huang et al. (2019) employed loss-based adaptive boosting to improve prediction accuracy within FL frameworks. Overall, these advancements highlight the transformative potential of FL in the healthcare sector, particularly in preserving data privacy while fostering collaborative analysis.
