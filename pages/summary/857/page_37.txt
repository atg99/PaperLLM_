The paper discusses a blockchain-based privacy-preserving federated learning (FL) framework proposed by Awan et al. in 2019, which incorporates a variation of the Paillier cryptosystem to prevent privacy leakage. By utilizing blockchain technology, the contributions of each participant can be traced, enabling the establishment of an incentive mechanism. However, existing FL frameworks based on blockchain lack a specific rewarding mechanism to encourage client participation in training.

To enhance FL performance, Kim and Hong in 2019 proposed a dynamic weighting method that considers learning accuracy and participation frequency to incentivize high-quality clients. Additionally, the Block-FL framework by Kim et al. in 2019 rewards clients based on the number of samples they hold, aiming to reduce convergence time. The integration of blockchain with FL is promising due to its decentralized nature, which eliminates the need for a central server, thereby addressing bandwidth limitations. This integration not only facilitates secure updates but also allows for the implementation of activation mechanisms to enhance FL services. However, the introduction of blockchain may lead to increased latency in exchanging learning models, suggesting the need for a design that minimizes latency.

Furthermore, the paper highlights that existing FL frameworks predominantly focus on supervised learning methods, effectively applied in neural networks, support vector machines, and linear classifiers. In contrast, unsupervised learning methods are essential in scenarios where labeled data is scarce or absent, making it reasonable to utilize unsupervised learning to infer potential information in these contexts.
