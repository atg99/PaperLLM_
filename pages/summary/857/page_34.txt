(2020) highlight that increased mutual information among clients correlates with greater divergence in parameter distances during iterations, suggesting that simple gradient averaging may not be effective. To address gradient bias during local training, Yao et al. (2019) propose tracking global parameters throughout local training epochs, allowing for unbiased gradient aggregation.

Further advancements are made by Ji et al. (2019), who introduce a recurrent neural network aggregator to optimize gradient aggregation, while Wang et al. (2019) present a layer-wise aggregation method for generating parameters in neural networks. The paper emphasizes that traditional averaging methods are inadequate, especially under non-iid distributions among clients, and suggests the incorporation of adaptive weights or machine learning techniques for more effective gradient aggregation.

Additionally, the paper discusses the importance of establishing an incentive mechanism to improve participation in FL. The original FedAvg approach randomly selects clients, potentially leading to inconsistent training due to non-participation from certain clients. By implementing an incentive mechanism, the cloud server can reward participants based on their contributions, encouraging broader participation and ultimately leading to a more robust model. This approach aims to create a positive feedback loop that enhances training outcomes.
