The paper discusses various applications and advancements in the field of federated learning (FL) across different domains, including UAV systems, energy demand prediction, financial fraud detection, text mining, and healthcare. 

In UAV systems, Dempster-Shafer theory is highlighted for its potential in improving decision-making. In the energy sector, Saputra et al. (2019) focused on enhancing prediction accuracy through a clustering-based learning method, emphasizing the need for stability and flexibility.

Yang et al. (2019) addressed credit card fraud detection in finance, achieving a 10% higher average test AUC compared to traditional methods, while stressing the importance of reliable measurements for privacy protection. Wang et al. (2020) introduced a Random Response with Priori (RRP) approach for spam filtering and sentiment analysis, noting that noise from the perturbation could slightly affect performance.

In healthcare applications, Brisimi et al. (2018) utilized a Cluster Primal Dual Splitting algorithm to predict hospitalizations, requiring more iterations for convergence. Silva et al. (2019) developed a federated analysis framework for MRI analysis, which efficiently handled high-dimensional feature variability but was limited to a small dataset.

Liu et al. (2019) proposed a two-stage federated NLP method for extracting clinical notes, suggesting a preprocessing step for accuracy improvement, though it was not suitable for smaller cases. Gao et al. (2019) designed a hierarchical heterogeneous horizontal FL framework for EEG classification, marking the first classifier for heterogeneous EEG data but only tested on three datasets.

Li and Liu (2019) introduced a community-based FL model for predicting mortality and hospital stay time, achieving higher predictive accuracy with fewer communication rounds than baseline models, though community model parameters could increase communication overhead. Pfohl et al. (2019) established the efficacy of FL over centralized learning while performing it in a differentially private manner, noting potential underestimations of privacy costs.

Huang et al. (2019) utilized an adaptive boosting method for mortality prediction based on drug utilization data, addressing non-iid data challenges through data-sharing technology. Kim et al. (2017) applied federated tensor factorization for privacy-preserving computational phenotyping, achieving accuracy with small or skewed data. Lastly, Lee et al. (2018) developed a federated patient hashing framework for similar patient matching, aiming to prevent security attacks, albeit facing inevitable computational complexity.

Overall, the paper illustrates the diverse applications of federated learning and the ongoing challenges in achieving stability, privacy, and efficiency across different fields.
