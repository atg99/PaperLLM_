The table summarizes various studies on the application of federated learning across different fields, particularly focusing on mobile devices and industrial engineering. 

In mobile device applications, several researchers have explored enhancements in smartphone keyboards and voice assistants. Chen et al. (2019) aimed to expand keyboard vocabulary while protecting sensitive text, though their method depends heavily on a learned probabilistic model. Leroy et al. (2019) developed an adaptive strategy for wake word detection but noted limitations in handling background noise. Hard et al. (2019) achieved improvements in next-word prediction using RNN models, despite high communication costs. Yang et al. (2018) focused on improving search suggestions but faced challenges with large parameter models. Other studies, such as those by Ramaswamy et al. (2019) and Wang et al. (2019), highlighted the performance of emoji prediction and optimization in mobile edge computing, respectively, while addressing issues like heterogeneous computation loads and the need for privacy-aware service placement.

In industrial engineering, Hu et al. (2018) proposed an environmental monitoring framework that enhances inference accuracy through regional characteristics but requires further development for multi-layer structures. Han et al. (2019) addressed automated defect inspection, emphasizing the need for rapid model deployment across industries. Liu et al. (2020) worked on image representation, finding their approach more effective for smaller datasets in specific federated learning settings. Lastly, Mowla et al. (2020) focused on detecting malicious attacks in UAV communications, noting the necessity for improved reliability in global updates.

Overall, while these studies demonstrate significant advancements in federated learning applications, they also highlight various constraints and areas for further research, particularly in model robustness, deployment flexibility, and privacy considerations.
