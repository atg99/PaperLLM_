In a federated learning (FL) context, it is essential to develop Byzantine fault-tolerant systems focused on fault detection to mitigate potential threats. Blockchain technology, a rapidly evolving innovation originating from Bitcoin, serves as a decentralized, immutable, and transparent distributed ledger. This technology has been applied in various industrial data-sharing schemes, such as monitoring the quality of 3D-printed items, facilitating privacy-preserving energy trading in smart grids, and enhancing emergency medical services.

While existing studies have primarily explored blockchain's role in improving medical information sharing, the collaborative training necessary for maximizing data utilization has not been fully realized. Recent research indicates that blockchain can significantly address challenges in FL, as both technologies complement each other. Blockchain's inherent security features make it suitable for integration with FL, particularly as it can operate effectively even with a minority of malicious nodes.

Majeed and Hong proposed a robust FL-chain that leverages blockchain to verify local model updates, ensuring the security of the architecture. However, this security does not inherently address privacy concerns related to individual nodes. The risk remains that a malicious participant, such as a clinic or hospital, could compromise the privacy of others involved in the training. To address these concerns, Ilias and Georgios introduced the use of blockchain smart contracts in conjunction with homomorphic encryption, providing additional privacy assurances for participants in the collaborative training process.
