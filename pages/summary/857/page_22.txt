The paper discusses various attacks on federated learning models, focusing on targeted adversarial attacks and inferring attacks. Bagdasaryan et al. (2018) highlight the vulnerability of global models to attacks from a few malicious adversaries, which can lead to misclassification in targeted models. They note that while integrated models show convergence and maintain accuracy on most tasks, Byzantine-resilient aggregation methods are insufficient against such attacks in federated settings. 

Zhang et al. (2019) introduce model poisoning attacks using Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), where malicious participants masquerade as benign agents to generate misleading training data and labels, compromising the integrity of the learning process. The paper points out that existing defenses against poisoning attacks are inadequate in federated learning contexts. Future research directions include employing anomaly detection on the server side and obscuring classification results to counteract these threats.

Inferring attacks are categorized into tracing attacks, which determine if a client is included in a dataset, and reconstruction attacks, which recover features about individual participants. Nasr et al. (2019) developed a white-box membership inference attack directed at neural networks, successfully applied in federated settings to extract information from a curious server or participant. Wang et al. (2019) proposed a framework called mGAN-AI to reconstruct private information for specific clients, addressing the limitations of previous methods that focused primarily on malicious server assumptions.
