Data poisoning attacks aim to undermine data integrity or bias training results by embedding malicious or disguised data. There are two primary types of data poisoning attack modes: model skew and feedback weaponization. Traditional machine learning systems are particularly vulnerable to these attacks, as adversaries can manipulate triggers to mislead the global model. However, in federated learning (FL), traditional data poisoning methods are less effective due to the lack of direct access to raw data by attackers, as noted by Bagdasaryan et al. (2018).

Building on this research, Yang et al. (2019) introduced a novel distributed backdoor attack, which involves dividing an attack trigger into multiple slices and distributing these slices among different attackers. This approach challenges the notion that FL can completely avoid data poisoning and provides a new framework for security analysis in FL.

Model poisoning, also referred to as adversarial attacks, involves intentionally designing specific inputs to produce incorrect results from a machine learning model. It can be categorized into non-targeted adversarial attacks, which lead to general incorrect outcomes, and targeted adversarial attacks, which aim to induce a specific type of error. In FL, secure aggregation is employed, making it difficult for the aggregator to detect anomalies or verify the correctness of local updates. This vulnerability allows malicious participants to insert backdoors into the federated environment using model-replacement techniques, ultimately misleading the joint model. This new attack method can effectively disrupt federated training tasks, including those involving image data.
