While existing algorithms can prevent adversaries from accessing the central server or clients, encrypted parameters still pose a risk of information leakage through advanced attack methods. Hybrid approaches have been introduced to bolster privacy, yet they often introduce noise through differential privacy, which can reduce model accuracy.

The Hybrid-One scheme addresses this issue by combining differential privacy with secure multi-party computation (MPC), thereby minimizing noise compared to traditional local differential privacy methods. However, this approach can lead to high communication costs and extended convergence times, akin to those seen with homomorphic encryption. In response, the HybridAlpha method integrates functional encryption with secure multi-party computation protocols, achieving high performance without compromising privacy.

Additionally, sketching algorithms are well-suited for FL as they do not store data identities, necessitating extra mechanisms for data tracing. This concept was further developed by Liu, Li, Smith, and Sekar, who established a connection between FL and sketching algorithms to enhance confidentiality.

FL has emerged as a prominent model for collaborative efforts without legal concerns. Despite the challenges and limitations identified, early adopters have recognized significant potential in FL, leading to various explorations and attempts to implement FL in practical applications.
