Federated transfer learning is presented as an effective solution for safeguarding data security and user privacy while addressing the issue of data silos. The foundational framework for federated learning (FL) is FedAvg, which has shown some capability in handling lightweight non-IID data. However, it faces significant challenges, including high communication overhead and structural heterogeneity.

Recent advancements focus on optimizing algorithms to enhance efficiency and accuracy, as well as improving participant privacy to bolster data protection. The paper discusses the evolution of FL, highlighting the optimization processes and security advancements. Since the introduction of FL in 2016, research has progressed significantly, yet challenges such as high communication costs and heterogeneity remain prominent.

The optimization efforts in FL are categorized based on their development processes and methods, aiming to tackle these challenges. The initial studies have primarily targeted reducing communication costs, as illustrated in the referenced figures. This ongoing evolution reflects a concerted effort to make FL more applicable in practice while maintaining the integrity of user data and privacy.
