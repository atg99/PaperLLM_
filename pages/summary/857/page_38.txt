This paper discusses the advancements and challenges of federated transfer learning (FL) in handling messy and dispersed data, particularly in the fields of image registration and classification. While significant progress has been made, there remain bottlenecks in applying unsupervised learning within FL frameworks. Collaborative training methods, such as the Collaborative and Adversarial Network (CAN), demonstrate effectiveness in addressing the challenge of limited labeled data. Additionally, the introduction of Federated Unsupervised Representation Learning marks a breakthrough by significantly reducing the need for labeled data while maintaining competitive performance against supervised and transfer learning methods. 

The conclusion highlights the contributions of this study to the understanding of FL applications in industrial engineering and computer science. It summarizes the characteristics and challenges of FL, outlines optimization paths for addressing privacy concerns and algorithm efficiency, and emphasizes the need for further research to extend FL frameworks in unsupervised learning contexts. Overall, this work serves as a foundational overview of the development prospects of FL in industrial applications.
