In this scenario, different medical institutions may have access to different patient data but share some common identifiers. By using vertical FL, they can collaboratively train a model that leverages the shared patient identifiers while utilizing their unique feature sets. This allows for improved predictive capabilities without the need for centralized data sharing, which addresses privacy concerns.

In summary, federated learning presents two primary frameworks in the context of smart devices and the Internet of Things: horizontal FL and vertical FL. Horizontal FL is applicable when data across different devices shares the same feature space but varies in sample space. This is exemplified by Google's federated model for Android updates and enhanced by hierarchical heterogeneous FL, which addresses the challenge of limited labeled data through domain adaptation. Vertical FL, on the other hand, is suited for scenarios where data is distributed across different organizations with some overlapping sample identifiers but differing features. This is particularly relevant in medical applications, where institutions can collaborate on predictive modeling without compromising patient confidentiality.
