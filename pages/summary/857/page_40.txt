This document compiles a series of research studies and articles related to privacy-preserving machine learning, federated learning, and data security in medical contexts. 

Key contributions include:

1. **Bonawitz et al. (2017)** discuss practical secure aggregation techniques that enable privacy-preserving machine learning, emphasizing the need for confidentiality in data processing.
2. **Bos et al. (2014)** explore private predictive analysis on encrypted medical data, providing a framework for ensuring data security while enabling meaningful analysis.
3. **Brisimi et al. (2018)** present federated learning models applied to electronic health records, highlighting the benefits of decentralized data processing in healthcare.
4. **Cai and Venkatasubramanian (2018)** focus on detecting data manipulation attacks in wearable medical systems, addressing security vulnerabilities in physiological data.
5. **Chai et al. (2019)** tackle the challenges of resource and data heterogeneity in federated learning, proposing strategies to optimize performance across diverse data sources.
6. **Chen et al. (2017)** revisit distributed synchronous stochastic gradient descent methods, providing insights into improving distributed learning efficiency.
7. **Chen et al. (2019)** introduce federated learning techniques for handling out-of-vocabulary words, enhancing natural language processing applications.
8. **Chen et al. (2020)** propose a training-integrity privacy-preserving federated learning scheme utilizing trusted execution environments, ensuring both privacy and data integrity.
9. **Chen et al. (2019)** also discuss asynchronous online federated learning tailored for edge devices, optimizing communication and processing efficiency.

This collection of studies underscores the significance of privacy and security in machine learning applications, particularly within the healthcare sector, while advancing methodologies for effective data handling and analysis in federated environments.
