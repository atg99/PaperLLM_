The paper identifies the challenges posed by both insider and outsider adversaries in federated learning (FL). Insider adversaries include honest-but-curious aggregators and colluding or malicious participants, who may attempt to extract privacy-sensitive information during the training process. Outsider adversaries can access intermediate outputs or the final model, further threatening data confidentiality.

To address these vulnerabilities, the paper discusses existing privacy-preserving methods that primarily emphasize data encryption and secure aggregation techniques. At the client side, differential privacy (DP) is highlighted as a key approach for enhancing privacy. This technique introduces noise to the data, which helps obscure individual records while maintaining overall query accuracy. The paper references studies that have successfully implemented DP in FL, such as Geyer et al. (2019), who applied it to conceal client participation, and McMahan et al. (2018), who enhanced the FedAvg algorithm by incorporating Gaussian noise.

Overall, the exploration of stronger protection methods, including data encryption before cloud upload and the implementation of differential privacy, is essential for advancing privacy preservation in federated learning systems.
