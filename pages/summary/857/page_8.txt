The paper discusses the advantages of Federated Learning (FL) over traditional distributed systems, particularly in handling unbalanced and non-IID data due to the heterogeneity of device resources. FL employs decentralized technology, where there is no definitive central node, allowing each client to influence the central model without a centralized authority. This contrasts with parameter server models, which rely on a central server for data distribution and computation, leading to increased communication overhead and strain on computing resources. In FL, clients operate autonomously, maintaining their data locally and participating collaboratively in model training without server oversight. This decentralized approach fosters equality among nodes, allowing all parties to participate equally in the learning process, unlike traditional systems where data owners with larger datasets hold more power. Overall, FL represents a shift towards a more collaborative and efficient model training paradigm.
