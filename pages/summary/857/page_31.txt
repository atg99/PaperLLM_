The research highlighted the Federated Averaging algorithm applied to drug usage data from the MIMIC-III database, focusing on patient mortality prediction. It emphasized the importance of computational complexity, communication costs, and accuracy for individual clients, successfully outperforming baseline methods.

In the realm of natural language processing (NLP), studies by Liu, Dligach, and Miller in 2019 marked a significant advance by utilizing FL for processing unstructured clinical notes. Their two-stage federated training model involved a preprocessing stage for patient representation and a phenotyping training stage for illness analysis.

FL's applications have also expanded into biomedical imaging, as demonstrated by Silva et al. in 2019 with federated principal components analysis (fPCA) for extracting features from MRI data across various medical centers. Additionally, Gao et al. introduced a hierarchical heterogeneous horizontal FL framework for EEG classification, addressing challenges related to limited labeled data and privacy.

The paper concludes that FL is poised for widespread adoption in data-sensitive fields, with a notable increase in applications in 2019. It anticipates that FL will play a crucial role in future medical data analysis, particularly in collaborative training where data features are similar across different institutions.
