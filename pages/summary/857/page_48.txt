The paper "Resources in Mobile Edge" discusses resource allocation strategies tailored for mobile edge computing environments, emphasizing the importance of optimizing resource usage in distributed systems. 

Nock et al. introduce the concept of federated resolution, combining entity resolution and federated learning to enhance data privacy and collaboration across decentralized systems. 

Pan et al. explore cross-domain sentiment classification using spectral feature alignment, providing insights into how features can be aligned across different domains to improve classification accuracy.

Pandey et al. propose a crowdsourcing framework for on-device federated learning, enabling efficient data collection and model training while preserving user privacy.

Pfohl et al. investigate the application of federated and differentially private learning methods to electronic health records, highlighting the importance of maintaining privacy in sensitive health data.

Qian et al. present a privacy-aware service placement strategy for mobile edge computing, utilizing federated learning to optimize service delivery while safeguarding user data.

Ramaswamy et al. focus on federated learning for emoji prediction in mobile keyboards, showcasing practical applications of federated learning in enhancing user experience.

Saputra et al. address energy demand prediction for electric vehicle networks through federated learning, contributing to the understanding of energy management in smart grid contexts.

These studies collectively underline the potential of federated learning in various domains, emphasizing privacy, collaboration, and efficient resource utilization in the context of modern computing environments.
