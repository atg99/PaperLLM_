The paper discusses various advancements in Federated Learning (FL) aimed at enhancing model performance and reducing communication overhead during training. Yao et al. (2018) introduced an algorithm that allows local models to share knowledge among training devices, thereby accelerating convergence. Yurochkin et al. (2019) developed a Bayesian Nonparametric FL framework that aggregates local models into a federated model without the need for additional parameters, achieving satisfactory accuracy with only one communication round.

Despite optimizing communication rounds, the challenge of speeding up model updates remains. McMahan et al. (2017) proposed two strategies to address this: structured updates, which involve transmitting only parts of the model updates through low-rank models or random masking, and sketched updates, which utilize compressed models. Zhu and Jin (2019) enhanced sparse evolutionary training to convey only essential parameters to the server, aligning with the sketched update approach.

Additionally, Jiang and Ying (2020) introduced an adaptive method for local training that adjusts the number of epochs based on server feedback regarding training time and loss, thereby optimizing local training duration. While these strategies predominantly rely on stochastic gradient descent (SGD), Liu et al. (2020) proposed using momentum gradient descent to incorporate previous gradient information, thus improving convergence speed. However, the paper notes that these algorithms may not be universally applicable across all federated settings, indicating a need for further research.
