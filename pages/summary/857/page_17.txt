The paper discusses the need for efficient communication methods in the medical industry, particularly in the context of statistical heterogeneity in data. Traditional machine learning approaches typically assume that data is identically independent and identically distributed (IID), which is often not the case in medical data collected from various devices or institutions. This leads to skewed characteristics and variations in clinical validation across different equipment. Additionally, the data sizes at different nodes can vary significantly, resulting in an unbalanced distribution.

To address these challenges, the paper suggests focusing on a global model or modifying local training methods, including additional data preprocessing procedures. The FedAvg algorithm is introduced as a solution that averages local updates from devices to create a global model. However, issues of fairness and bias in centralized models are highlighted, with Mohri et al. proposing improvements to accommodate diverse client distributions. 

The paper also emphasizes the importance of convergence behavior in the presence of heterogeneity, noting that misconvergence can occur in global models. Wang et al. discuss convergence bounds for federated learning (FL) under non-IID data conditions and propose an adaptive method to minimize loss within resource constraints. Furthermore, Li et al. present four convergence theorems for the FedAvg algorithm, taking into account different parameter settings and premises in non-IID contexts.
