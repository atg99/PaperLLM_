The references listed encompass various studies and developments in the field of federated learning, focusing on privacy preservation and resource-efficient implementations. 

Truex et al. (2019) propose a hybrid approach to enhance privacy in federated learning, addressing security concerns in distributed data environments. Van Berlo et al. (2020) explore federated unsupervised representation learning, aiming to leverage decentralized data for improved model training without requiring labeled datasets. 

Wang et al. (2019) introduce matched averaging as a technique to optimize federated learning processes, enhancing model accuracy through better parameter aggregation. Another study by Wang et al. (2019) discusses adaptive federated learning specifically tailored for resource-constrained edge computing systems, ensuring efficient use of limited computational resources.

The concept of In-Edge AI is presented by Wang et al. (2019), which integrates intelligent functionalities in mobile edge computing through federated learning, optimizing caching and communication. Wang et al. (2020) contribute a framework for federated latent Dirichlet allocation, applying local differential privacy to safeguard user data.

Wang et al. (2019) also highlight user-level privacy concerns in federated learning, revealing potential vulnerabilities in inferring user data from class representatives. The Webank projects, including FATE and FedAI, demonstrate practical implementations and tools for federated AI technologies.

Lastly, Wu et al. (2019) propose SAFA, a semi-asynchronous protocol designed to accelerate federated learning while minimizing overhead, contributing to the efficiency of collaborative machine learning processes. Overall, these works collectively advance the understanding and implementation of federated learning, emphasizing privacy, efficiency, and practical applications in real-world scenarios.
