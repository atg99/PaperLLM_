This paper addresses the theoretical gap in the convergence speed of federated learning (FL) algorithms and explores the impact of parameter adjustments on this speed. It highlights the importance of data preprocessing, citing Huanga et al. (2019), who proposed a community-based FL method that clusters independent data to tackle the non-IID problem. However, this approach is limited by its high parameter conversion overhead, making it unsuitable for large-scale data training. 

Additionally, the paper discusses modifications to local training modes to enhance personalization for individual devices. For instance, MOCHA utilizes multi-task learning to leverage shared representations, as demonstrated by Smith et al. (2017). Zhao et al. (2018) also addressed non-IID data by sharing a small data subset among local models. Huangb et al. (2019) built upon this idea by incorporating cross-entropy loss in the transmission process and varying local update frequencies for clients.

The paper further touches on structural heterogeneity, emphasizing that disparities in computing and storage capabilities among nodes can lead to unbalanced training times due to the diverse types of chips used in different devices.
