The paper discusses the application of federated learning (FL) in smart home and industrial engineering contexts, emphasizing its role in data privacy and model development. In smart home environments, devices upload user data to cloud servers, which raises concerns about data breaches. To address this, Aïvodji et al. propose a secure federated architecture for joint model building, while Yu et al. introduce a federated multi-task learning framework to analyze user behavior and detect hazards. Liu et al. present a data fusion approach using FL for robotic imitation learning, applicable to self-driving cars for emergency prediction.

In industrial engineering, FL is seen as a promising solution for leveraging dispersed datasets while adhering to legal constraints. The paper highlights the potential of FL in sensitive areas, such as environmental monitoring, where Hu et al. designed a federated region learning framework to enhance collaborative model performance using data from various sensors. Overall, the paper underscores the growing relevance of FL across diverse fields, facilitating efficient data usage while maintaining privacy.
