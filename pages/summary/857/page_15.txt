The paper discusses the evolution of addressing statistical and structural heterogeneity in federated learning (FL), highlighting various approaches represented by different symbols and the reference frequency of related research as indicated by line thickness. A significant challenge identified is the high communication cost associated with FL, which is crucial due to the fast-paced nature of modern data. To enhance FL's flexibility and efficiency, particularly with large datasets, minimizing communication overhead is essential.

Key strategies to reduce communication costs include decreasing the number of communication rounds and improving model upload speed. Research by McMahan et al. (2017) is noted as foundational, advocating for increased computation on clients to minimize communication needs. They also emphasize enhancing parallelism by involving more clients in each training round. Following this, Nishio and Yonetani (2019) developed the FedCs framework, aiming to maximize client participation in training rounds to improve efficiency.
