The paper discusses the challenges and advancements in vertical federated learning (FL), particularly in the context of health monitoring for individuals at risk of diabetes. It highlights a case of a young man who, despite not being obese or hypertensive, consumes excess calories and lacks physical activity, making him susceptible to diabetes. Current predictive models are limited due to insufficient data.

The paper emphasizes the potential of FL to collaborate with companies that utilize smartphone applications to gather data on users' dietary habits and physical activity levels. Unlike horizontal FL, where data from similar entities can be easily aggregated, vertical FL faces complexities due to the need for entity resolution and the correspondence between different data owners.

To address these challenges, the paper references various solutions, including a modified token-based entity resolution algorithm and an end-to-end scheme using additive homomorphic encryption to protect data during training. Current applications of vertical FL include traffic violation assessment and credit risk evaluation, utilizing frameworks like FATE and SecureBoost. However, the authors note that existing methods primarily support simple machine learning models, indicating that vertical FL has significant potential for enhancement to accommodate more complex algorithms.
