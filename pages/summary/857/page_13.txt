Federated transfer learning (FTL) addresses the challenges of scenarios where data does not share sample or feature space, leading to issues such as lack of data labels and poor data quality. It leverages transfer learning to transfer knowledge from a source domain to a target domain, enhancing learning outcomes. Liu, Chen, and Yang introduced FTL as a comprehensive framework for federated learning that incorporates training, evaluation, and cross-validation. This framework utilizes neural networks with additive homomorphic encryption to safeguard privacy while maintaining accuracy comparable to non-privacy-preserving methods. However, communication efficiency is a concern.

Sharma et al. proposed improvements to FTL by implementing secret sharing technology, which reduces overhead and maintains accuracy, while also providing protection against malicious servers. Chen et al. further advanced this field with the FedHealth model, which aggregates data from various organizations to deliver personalized healthcare services through transfer learning, facilitating the sharing of disease diagnosis and treatment information across hospitals.

Despite these advancements, FTL research is still in its early stages, indicating significant potential for development, particularly in enhancing flexibility with diverse data structures. Current challenges include addressing data islands and privacy protection issues in the large-scale application of machine learning.
