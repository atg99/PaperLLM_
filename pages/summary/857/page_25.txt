The paper discusses advancements in secure aggregation within the context of federated learning (FL). Traditional methods relied on the gradient of a single instance for updating, but newer approaches emphasize using similar instances for more effective gradient updates. Secure multi-party computation (SMC) is central to these advancements, allowing clients to calculate functions securely without a trustworthy third party. Bonawitz et al. (2017) introduced the first secure aggregation protocol, ensuring that individual model updates remain confidential until a sufficient number of devices have contributed to the aggregated model. However, this method's quadratic communication cost limits its scalability.

To address this, Haoa et al. (2019) proposed a more efficient privacy-preserving scheme that combines differential privacy with lightweight homomorphic encryption, designed for stochastic gradient descent. This protocol is resilient against servers that may be curious yet honest, as well as potential collusion between the cloud and server. Nonetheless, there are concerns regarding the reliability of the global model returned by cloud servers, which can be either incorrect or incomplete due to malicious intent or computational limitations.

In response to these challenges, Xu et al. (2020) developed VerifyNet, the first protocol capable of verifying the correctness of models returned by the cloud. This approach incorporates variations of secret sharing and key agreement protocols to ensure gradient confidentiality. Furthermore, Chen et al. (2020) refined secure aggregation techniques by introducing a public parameter for each client, ensuring uniform training methods and facilitating the detection of malicious clients during the aggregation process.
