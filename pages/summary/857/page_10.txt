Federated Learning (FL) has seen significant real-world application, particularly with frameworks achieving implementation on over ten million devices and aiming for scalability to support billions. One notable framework is Federated AI Technology Enabler (FATE), developed by the Webank team. FATE is the first open-source industrial-level framework designed for cross-organizational architecture, ensuring client privacy through homomorphic encryption and secure multiparty computing. It supports various machine learning algorithms, including logistic regression, deep learning, and transfer learning, while also allowing traditional methods to be adapted for its use. The Webank team has successfully implemented FATE in areas such as credit risk control, object detection, and anti-money laundering.

FL can be categorized into three main types based on data partitioning: horizontal FL, vertical FL, and federated transfer learning. According to Yang et al. (2019), horizontal FL involves data from different nodes having overlapping features but differing sample spaces. Current FL algorithms primarily focus on this horizontal approach, which allows for effective collaboration across diverse datasets while preserving privacy.
