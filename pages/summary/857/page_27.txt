This section explores various applications of federated learning (FL) within industrial engineering and computer science, particularly focusing on mobile devices. FL gained prominence following Google's introduction of the concept for predicting user input on Gboard for Android. Subsequent advancements in keyboard prediction have been reported by researchers such as Chen et al. and Leroy et al., while emoji prediction has emerged as another significant area of research. Additionally, FL models have potential applications in predicting human trajectories and behaviors.

Despite the rapid advancements in mobile device storage and computing power, the demand for high-quality services from mobile subscribers is hindered by communication bandwidth limitations. Consequently, many service providers are opting for mobile edge computing (MEC), which places services closer to customers to alleviate network congestion. However, MEC also introduces risks related to information security. To address these challenges, researchers like Wang et al. have proposed integrating FL with MEC, creating an 'In-Edge AI' framework that employs deep reinforcement learning to optimize resource allocation. Furthermore, Qian et al. have developed a privacy-aware service placement scheme that enhances service quality by caching services on edge servers near users.
