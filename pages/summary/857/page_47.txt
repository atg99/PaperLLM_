This collection of works addresses various aspects of federated learning, emphasizing privacy, security, and efficiency. Ma et al. focus on safeguarding privacy and security within the federated learning framework, highlighting the challenges and potential solutions. Majeed and Hong propose FLchain, integrating federated learning with a blockchain network to enhance security in mobile edge computing environments. 

McMahan et al. discuss communication-efficient strategies for decentralized data learning, while McMahan et al. present methods for differentially private recurrent language models. Mohammad and Sorour explore adaptive task allocation in asynchronous federated mobile edge learning, which optimizes resource usage. 

Mohri et al. introduce agnostic federated learning, tackling the limitations of traditional models in heterogeneous environments. Mowla et al. apply federated learning to cognitive detection of jamming attacks in flying ad-hoc networks, demonstrating practical applications of the technology. Muñoz-González et al. propose adaptive model averaging to enhance robustness against Byzantine attacks in federated learning. 

Nakamoto's foundational work on Bitcoin underpins the decentralized nature of federated learning systems, while Nasr et al. conduct a comprehensive privacy analysis of deep learning, examining vulnerabilities in both centralized and federated settings. Lastly, Nishio and Yonetani address client selection in heterogeneous data scenarios, vital for optimizing federated learning performance. Overall, these studies contribute to the evolving discourse on federated learning, emphasizing the need for robust, privacy-preserving methodologies in distributed data environments.
