This paper presents Grid-GCN, a novel architecture that integrates the strengths of volumetric and point-based models to enhance data structuring and computational efficiency in point cloud processing. The authors highlight the limitations of existing models, particularly the high data structuring costs associated with popular point-based methods, which can account for up to 88% of computational expenses. 

Grid-GCN features a two-stage process in its GridConv layers: a data structuring stage that samples representative centers and queries neighboring points, and a convolution stage that constructs a local graph for each point group to aggregate information. The Coverage-Aware Grid Query (CAGQ) module is introduced to accelerate center sampling and neighbor querying while ensuring comprehensive coverage of the point cloud, significantly improving data structuring efficiency.

In addition, the Grid Context Aggregation (GCA) module is proposed to extract context features from grid neighborhoods, aiding in edge relation computation without incurring additional overhead. Experimental results demonstrate that Grid-GCN achieves state-of-the-art classification accuracy of 93.1% on the ModelNet40 dataset, while being five times faster than existing models. For segmentation tasks on ScanNet and S3DIS datasets, it shows an average speed-up of ten times compared to other approaches. Notably, Grid-GCN can process 81,920 points in a scene within 20 milliseconds, showcasing its capability for real-time large-scale point-based learning.

The paper also reviews related work in voxel-based and point-based methods, emphasizing the trade-offs between efficiency and data granularity. It critiques various data structuring strategies, highlighting the advantages of the CAGQ module over traditional methods like FPS and RPS. Overall, Grid-GCN represents a significant advancement in the field of point cloud learning, addressing critical performance bottlenecks while maintaining high accuracy and speed.
