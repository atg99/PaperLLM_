The paper discusses regularity conditions necessary for the analysis of policy gradient methods within the context of Markov decision processes (MDPs). Assumption 4.4, described as a mild regularity condition, is satisfied when the Markov chain generating the state-action pairs mixes sufficiently quickly and when the second moments of the Q-values are upper bounded for all policies. This condition is supported by prior work from Zhang et al. (2019) and Xu et al. (2019a,b), which demonstrate that under similar conditions, unbiased policy gradient estimators exhibit upper bounded norms, reinforcing Assumption 4.4.

Assumption 4.5 introduces a regularity condition concerning the discrepancy between the state-action visitation measure and the stationary distribution for the same policy. It stipulates the existence of a constant κ such that the L2-norm of the Radon-Nikodym derivative is bounded. This condition is particularly relevant when the MDP starts at the stationary distribution, aligning the visitation measure with the stationary distribution. The assumption parallels a condition from Scherrer (2013), which focuses on the L∞-norm instead.

Furthermore, Assumption 4.6 posits that the expected total reward's gradient with respect to policy parameters is Lipschitz continuous, with L being a positive constant. This holds when both the transition probabilities and the reward function are Lipschitz continuous, as verified by several studies. The paper restricts the policy parameters to a defined space B and defines a stationary point as one where the gradient indicates no improvement in all directions within that space.

Finally, the authors assert that the sequence generated by the neural policy gradient method converges to a stationary point at a sublinear rate, indicating the effectiveness of the proposed regularity conditions in ensuring the stability and convergence of the policy optimization process.
