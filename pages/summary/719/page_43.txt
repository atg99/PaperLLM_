The paper discusses the projection error of a function space FR,m, which is a subset of a larger function space FR,∞. The key result, derived from Rahimi and Recht (2009), is presented in Lemma B.2, indicating that for functions f belonging to FR,∞, the deviation of the projection of f onto FR,m can be bounded with high probability. Specifically, it shows that the norm of the difference between the original function and its projection decreases with the square root of m, alongside a logarithmic factor related to the probability δ.

Following this, the paper establishes a relationship between the expected error of the projection and the random initialization of the parameters. By applying the results from Lemma B.2, the authors derive expressions for the expected initialization error when evaluating Qπ, the action-value function associated with a policy π. This leads to an upper bound on the expected squared error of the output of their algorithm, denoted as Qωi, when compared to the target function Qπθi.

The conclusion of the analysis shows that the expected error can be controlled by terms involving R (a constant related to the problem setup) and m (the dimension of the function space), demonstrating that as m increases, the error decreases. The final result indicates that with an appropriate choice of TTD, the expected error converges to a desirable bound, supporting the effectiveness of the proposed algorithm.
