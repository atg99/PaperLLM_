In section 3.2.2, the paper discusses the critic update process necessary to derive the action-value function Qω for a given policy π. The action-value function is defined as the unique solution to the Bellman equation, represented through the Bellman operator Tπ. The objective is to minimize the difference between the current estimate Qω and the Bellman operator applied to Qω, leading to an optimization problem. The update for the critic is performed using neural temporal-difference learning, which employs stochastic semigradient descent to iteratively adjust the parameters ω.

The update process involves calculating a semigradient step based on the difference between the estimated action-value and the expected reward, followed by a projection step to ensure the updated parameters remain within the defined parameter space. The state-action pairs used for updates are sampled from a stationary distribution, achieved through a Markov chain induced by the policy. 

The combination of these critic updates with actor updates leads to the formulation of neural policy gradient and natural policy gradient methods, which are detailed in Algorithm 1. The subsequent section (4) aims to establish the global optimality and convergence of these neural policy gradient methods, under the condition that the reward function has a bounded absolute value.
