The paper discusses the derivation of inequalities related to the performance of a policy in a stochastic environment, specifically focusing on the relationship between the optimal policy and a given policy. The key equation derived, (5.24), establishes an upper bound on the expected difference in performance between the optimal policy and the policies under consideration. 

The paper defines several terms, including εQ,i, which measures the discrepancy in expected rewards, and εi, which quantifies the variability in policy performance. The authors utilize assumptions regarding the properties of the policies and the stochastic environment, leading to inequalities that bound the Kullback-Leibler divergence, DKL, between the optimal policy and the initial policy.

The analysis includes bounding expectations related to the policies' performance, indicating that the divergence is constrained by the logarithm of the action space size and other constants defined in earlier assumptions. The authors further provide bounds for εi and εQ,i, which are crucial in deriving the final inequality.

The culmination of these derivations leads to the proof of Theorem 4.13, which presents a comprehensive upper bound on the performance gap between the optimal and given policies, demonstrating the efficacy of the proposed approach in analyzing policy performance in stochastic settings.
