Theorem 4.7 establishes a convergence result for the neural policy gradient method, showing that under certain conditions, the method achieves a convergence rate of 1/√T to a stationary point. The parameters are defined as τi=1, η=1/√T, and TTD=Ω(m). The actor update is derived from Algorithm 1, with the projection operator ΠB ensuring that updates remain within a feasible set B. The theorem demonstrates that the expected squared norm of the gradient mapping, ρi, converges to zero, indicating that the sequence of parameters {θi} converges to a stationary point in B.

The proof relies on the assumptions outlined in previous propositions, and it highlights that when the neural networks are sufficiently wide and the batch size is large enough, the neural policy gradient achieves the desired convergence rate. Furthermore, it notes that the projection operator can be omitted without affecting the convergence rate, and a projection-free version of the method also converges similarly.

Theorem 4.8 further investigates the global optimality of the stationary point found. It states that the difference in expected total rewards between the stationary policy πθ and the global optimum π* is bounded. This bound relates the rewards to the gradient of the stationary point, indicating that the stationary point is close to optimal under certain conditions, with Qmax representing the maximum reward. The theorem employs Radon-Nikodym derivatives to formalize the relationship between the policies and their corresponding rewards.
