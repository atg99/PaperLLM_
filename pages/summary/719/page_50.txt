In this section, the authors present a theorem (Theorem C.3) which establishes a relationship between various parameters in a stochastic setting. The main result asserts that for certain conditions on T, B, and m, the expected difference between the optimal policy's performance (J(π∗)) and that of a given policy (J(πθi)) is bounded by a term decreasing with T. Specifically, it shows that this difference is O(R0·T−1/4).

To prove the theorem, the authors aim to upper bound the expectation of a specific term involving the gradient of a function related to the policy's value. They utilize the triangle inequality to simplify the expression and introduce a projection operator onto a defined space, ensuring that the modified parameter lies within a specified set.

The authors then establish a critical bound that relates the expected gradient differences to the projection of the value function. They conclude that under the assumptions made, the expectation can be bounded in terms of the parameters R0 and m, leading to the final result. The paper meticulously follows through with these calculations, providing a structured approach to derive the necessary inequalities and expectations.
