The paper titled "Neural Policy Gradient Methods: Global Optimality and Rates of Convergence" addresses the convergence properties of neural policy gradient methods, particularly those using actor-critic architectures. Despite their empirical success, questions remained about their ability to converge to globally optimal policies. The authors affirmatively answer these questions under the assumption of over-parameterized two-layer neural networks.

The key findings include a proof that the neural natural policy gradient converges to a globally optimal policy at a sublinear rate, while the neural vanilla policy gradient achieves sublinear convergence to a stationary point. The authors establish a relationship between the suboptimality of stationary points and the representation capabilities of neural networks used for actors and critics. They demonstrate that under certain regularity conditions, all stationary points are globally optimal.

A significant factor in achieving global optimality and convergence is the "compatibility" between the actor and critic, which can be ensured by sharing neural architectures and random initializations. This research provides the first guarantees on global optimality and convergence for neural policy gradient methods, contributing valuable insights to the field.
