This section discusses the convergence and optimality of the neural natural policy gradient as outlined in Theorem 4.13 and Corollary 4.14. Assumption 4.11 defines a term related to the expected optimality gap, which is influenced by various factors including the batch size and the number of parameters in the model. The proof for these results is detailed in §5.3.

The optimality gap is expressed as the minimum expected difference between the optimal policy's reward and the reward of the policy being evaluated. This gap is bounded by two terms: the first term, which decreases with the square root of the number of iterations T, reflects the convergence properties of the policy gradient methods as the model size m and batch size B increase. The second term accounts for errors arising from finite sample sizes and the compatibility of the actor and critic networks.

Specifically, the errors related to the finite batch size and the architecture compatibility diminish as B and m grow, respectively. Additionally, the policy evaluation error is manageable when both m and T are sufficiently large, as demonstrated in Proposition 4.3.

Corollary 4.14 formalizes the convergence rate and global optimality of the neural natural policy gradient, asserting that with appropriate choices for m and B, the expected total reward converges to the global optimum at a rate of 1/√T. It also highlights that while both neural policy gradient and neural natural policy gradient achieve this convergence rate using overparameterized networks, the neural policy gradient requires more stringent conditions for convergence to a stationary point.

In summary, the findings emphasize that with appropriately large model parameters and batch sizes, the neural natural policy gradient method guarantees convergence to optimal solutions, providing a significant advantage over traditional policy gradient methods under certain conditions.
