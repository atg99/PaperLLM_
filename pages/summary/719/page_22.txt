This paper investigates the representation power of overparameterized two-layer neural networks in the context of reinforcement learning, specifically focusing on the neural natural policy gradient (NNPG) approach. It highlights that NNPG can achieve global optimality in large parameter settings without the need for additional regularity conditions, showcasing the advantages of advanced optimization techniques in reinforcement learning. The paper draws parallels to findings in the linear quadratic regulator (LQR) scenario, where NNPG demonstrates enhanced convergence rates.

The work of Liu et al. (2019) is referenced, which explores the global optimality and convergence rates of neural proximal policy optimization (PPO) and trust region policy optimization (TRPO). While both approaches attain a similar convergence rate to the global optimum, neural PPO differs from NNPG by necessitating the resolution of a policy improvement subproblem in function space, involving multiple stochastic gradient steps. In contrast, NNPG streamlines this process with a single stochastic natural gradient step, complicating the analysis.

The paper proceeds to present proofs for key theorems, utilizing a lemma from Kakade and Langford (2002) that establishes one-point convexity at the global optimum. This lemma indicates that the difference in performance between any policy and the optimal policy can be expressed in terms of the advantage function and the state-action visitation measures, thus laying the groundwork for the subsequent proof of main results.
