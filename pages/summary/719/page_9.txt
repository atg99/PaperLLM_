The paper by Sutton et al. (2000) introduces the concept of compatible function approximations, which are crucial for creating unbiased estimators of the policy gradient in reinforcement learning. The authors define compatibility between the action-value function Qω and the policy πθ through the relationship ∇ωAω(s,a) = ∇θlogπθ(a|s) for all state-action pairs (s,a). This relationship allows for optimality and convergence in policy gradient methods, as established by prior research.

To achieve compatible function approximations when both the actor and critic are represented by neural networks, the authors propose using a shared architecture for Qω and πθ, initializing both with the same parameters from a normal distribution. They derive a centered feature mapping φ0 that reflects the shared initialization and demonstrate that in an over-parameterized setting, where the number of parameters is large, Qω becomes approximately compatible with πθ.

The paper further elaborates on the definitions of centered feature mappings φθ and φω, which help in establishing the relationship between the advantage function Aω and the log-probability of the policy. The authors conclude that under the conditions of sufficient width in the architecture, both φθ and φω can be well approximated by the initial mapping φ0, confirming that Qω is approximately compatible with πθ in the over-parameterized regime.
