The referenced papers explore various aspects of reinforcement learning and optimization techniques in machine learning. Williams (1992) introduces simple statistical gradient-following algorithms for connectionist reinforcement learning, laying foundational work in this area. Wu et al. (2018) analyze how stochastic gradient descent (SGD) selects global minima in over-parameterized learning from a dynamical stability perspective, providing insights into the behavior of learning algorithms in complex landscapes. 

In 2017, Wu et al. present a scalable trust-region method for deep reinforcement learning, utilizing Kronecker-factored approximation to enhance efficiency. Xu et al. (2019a) contribute to the convergence analysis of stochastic variance-reduced policy gradients, while their subsequent work (2019b) focuses on sample-efficient methods with recursive variance reduction, addressing the challenges of data efficiency in reinforcement learning.

Yang et al. (2019a) investigate the global convergence of actor-critic methods in the context of linear quadratic regulators with ergodic costs, providing theoretical grounding for these approaches. Their later work (2019b) offers a theoretical analysis of deep Q-learning, contributing to the understanding of deep reinforcement learning frameworks. 

Zhang et al. (2019) examine the global convergence of policy gradient methods, demonstrating their tendency to reach nearly optimal policies. Lastly, Zou et al. (2018) discuss the optimization of over-parameterized deep ReLU networks through stochastic gradient descent, further highlighting the intricate dynamics of modern machine learning optimization techniques. Collectively, these studies advance the understanding of convergence, efficiency, and stability in reinforcement learning and deep learning contexts.
