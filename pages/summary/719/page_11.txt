The paper discusses the Markov transition kernel of an artificial Markov Decision Process (MDP) where the next state is sampled from an initial state distribution with a probability of 1 minus gamma. This process allows the stationary state distribution of the induced Markov chain to align with the state visitation measure, demonstrating that the marginal distribution of sampled trajectories converges to the state-action visitation measure.

The paper also addresses the challenges of inverting the Fisher Information Matrix in the context of natural policy gradients, especially in overparameterized settings where the estimator may be high-dimensional and potentially non-invertible. To tackle this, the authors propose a method to estimate the natural policy gradient by minimizing a specific objective function that involves the gradient of the policy performance. 

The update mechanism for the neural natural policy gradient is presented, detailing how both the policy parameters and the temperature parameter are adjusted at each iteration. The approach allows for approximate solutions to the optimization problem, aligning with common practices in second-order optimization methods. The overall methodology combines projected gradient ascent with natural policy gradient techniques to refine policy parameters effectively.
