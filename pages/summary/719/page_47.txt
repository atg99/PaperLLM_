The paper discusses the lower bounding of an expression involving expectations and gradients in the context of a certain optimization problem. By applying the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality and a specific assumption, a bound is derived, leading to an inequality that relates the expected changes in a function with respect to its parameters. The results show that the expectations are influenced by constants and parameters such as Qmax, η, T, and σ, which are defined in the assumptions.

The derived inequalities are used to connect the expected changes in the objective function J with the gradients of the policy parameters. Notably, the results indicate that under certain conditions, including specific values for η and B, the upper bound on the expected gradient differences remains manageable as T grows. The paper also discusses the implications of these bounds on the convergence behavior of the optimization process, with R0(T) defined as a function of T that has a specific growth rate.

Finally, the analysis extends to bounding the difference between the gradient of the value function and its estimated counterpart, leading to further bounds on the expectations involved in the optimization problem. The results contribute to understanding the stability and convergence of the optimization approach being considered.
