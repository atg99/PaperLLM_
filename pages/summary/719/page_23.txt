The paper discusses the relationship between the expected total rewards of two policies, πθi+1 and πθi, using a series of mathematical proofs. It starts by establishing a lower bound on the difference in expected rewards based on the Lipschitz continuity of the policy gradient. The key equation shows that the difference in performance, J(πθi+1) - J(πθi), can be expressed in terms of the policy gradient and the increment of the actor update.

The proof of Lemma 5.1 is derived from combining two key equations, leading to a conclusion about the performance difference that incorporates the expected values of the action-value function Q under both the optimal policy π* and the policy π. The analysis highlights the importance of the variance in estimating the policy gradient, as well as the error in this estimation.

Furthermore, Lemma 5.2 establishes a bound on the first term of the error in estimating the policy gradient, linking it to the difference in Q-values between the current policy and another policy, and incorporating a stationary state-action distribution. The results are framed within the context of theoretical assumptions and provide a rigorous foundation for understanding policy performance in reinforcement learning.
