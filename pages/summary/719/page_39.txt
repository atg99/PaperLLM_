The paper discusses a mathematical bound on the difference between two functions represented in terms of parameter vectors θ and θ′. Specifically, it establishes inequalities using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality to demonstrate that the squared difference |φθ(s,a)⊤θ′−φ0(s,a)⊤θ′|² can be controlled by a function of the norms of gradients related to θ and θ′, as well as a parameter R that bounds these gradients. 

Key inequalities are derived, showing that the squared difference is bounded above by a term involving R, m (the number of samples), and the gradients of the parameters. The authors also invoke Assumption 4.2, which provides a constant c to further tighten the bounds. 

Ultimately, the paper takes the expectation of the derived bounds over random initializations of parameters, leading to additional inequalities that reinforce the control on the difference between the functions. The results contribute to understanding the behavior of the functions in the context of optimization or learning scenarios, ensuring that the differences remain small under certain conditions.
