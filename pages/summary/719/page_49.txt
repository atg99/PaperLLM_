The paper discusses the global optimality of a parameter sequence in the context of neural policy gradients. It establishes that the sequence {Bi} characterizes this optimality, with specific inequalities derived from the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality governing the relationship between the gradient of the objective function and the parameter differences. The analysis leads to a bound on the suboptimality of the parameter sequence, expressed in terms of a function J and the parameter space BT, which includes sequences that meet certain norm conditions.

The paper introduces the parameter space BT and its subspace Bi, providing a definition that bounds the norm of the difference between the parameters and an initial set of weights. The term R(T) quantifies the growth of this bound over time, incorporating factors such as Qmax and a summation related to the parameter sequence.

A key result is presented in Theorem C.3, which provides conditions under which the output of a projection-free neural policy gradient algorithm achieves global optimality. This theorem relies on specific choices of parameters, emphasizing the role of the feature mapping and the relationship between the approximated function and the actual output of the neural network.

Overall, the paper outlines conditions under which neural networks can achieve optimal performance in policy gradient methods, highlighting the significance of overparameterization and approximation capabilities in achieving these results.
