The paper discusses the use of the Fisher information matrix, denoted as F(θ), in the context of natural policy gradient methods for reinforcement learning. The Fisher information matrix is defined as the expected value of the outer product of the gradient of the log policy with respect to the parameters θ. The natural policy gradient algorithm updates the policy parameters iteratively by incorporating the inverse of the Fisher information matrix to adjust the gradient of the objective function, J(πθ).

The section on Neural Policy Gradient Methods introduces a specific implementation where the policy πθ is represented by a two-layer neural network. This network takes state-action pairs as input and produces policy outputs. The authors assume a particular structure for the input space and ensure that the gradients are normalized appropriately. The neural network employs the ReLU activation function, and the parameters are initialized from specific distributions.

In training, the authors propose to keep certain parameters fixed while updating others, specifically the weights of the neural network, to simplify the optimization process. This approach is supported by previous research, which emphasizes the relevance of maintaining certain parameters constant during training to achieve effective learning outcomes.
