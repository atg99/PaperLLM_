The paper discusses the relationship between feature mappings and their effects in a stochastic environment, focusing on the expectations taken over random initializations. It introduces feature mappings φθ and φ0 corresponding to parameters θ and Winit, respectively, and establishes a distribution σ over the state-action pairs (S × A), under the assumption that Assumption 4.2 holds.

The proof begins by analyzing the difference between the feature mappings, leading to an inequality that relates the mappings to their initial weights. Specifically, it utilizes the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality to derive bounds on the differences in the feature mappings when evaluated at state-action pairs where the mappings yield positive values.

It further establishes that the magnitude of the differences can be constrained by the gradients of the feature mappings, which are shown to be bounded. By substituting these bounds into the initial equations, the proof demonstrates that the deviations between the feature mappings can be controlled, ultimately leading to an expression that quantifies the relationship between the initialized weights and the updated parameters.

The findings emphasize the importance of the initialization in the learning process and provide a theoretical foundation for understanding the convergence and stability of the learning algorithms in stochastic settings.
