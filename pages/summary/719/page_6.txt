The paper discusses the relationship between the value function Vπ and the action-value function Qπ in the context of reinforcement learning. Specifically, it establishes that Vπ(s) can be expressed as the expected value of Qπ(s, a) with respect to the policy π. The transition dynamics defined by the policy π create a Markov chain over the state space S, leading to a stationary state distribution ̺π. The paper further defines the stationary state-action distribution ςπ(s, a) and introduces state visitation measures νπ and σπ for states and state-action pairs, respectively.

The expected total reward function J(π) is formulated as a function of the policy π, with the objective of maximizing J(π) to find the optimal policy π∗. In scenarios with large state spaces, the paper suggests optimizing over a class of parameterized policies {πθ: θ ∈ B} and presents the optimization problem max θ∈B J(πθ).

Policy gradient methods are introduced as a technique to maximize J(πθ) by computing the gradient ∇θJ(πθ). The policy gradient theorem is cited, which provides a formula for the gradient in terms of the action-value function Qπθ and the log probability of the policy. The paper outlines a generic update mechanism for policy parameters through gradient ascent and mentions the natural policy gradient approach that accounts for the geometry of the parameter space, enhancing the efficiency of the optimization process.
