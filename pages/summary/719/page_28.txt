The paper discusses the definition and analysis of the term \( H_i \) as expressed in equation (5.21) of Lemma 5.4. This term comprises three components: 

1. The first term (i) addresses the error in estimating the natural policy gradient.
2. The second term (ii) pertains to the error in estimating \( Q_{\pi_i} \) using \( Q_{\omega_i} \).
3. The third term (iii) represents a remainder.

The paper provides upper bounds for these three terms in Section D.8, leading to Lemma 5.5, which states that under specific assumptions (4.2 and 4.12), the expected absolute value of \( H_i \) is bounded by a function of various parameters, including the concentration coefficients \( \phi'_i \) and \( \psi'_i \), and error terms \( \epsilon_{Q,i} \) and \( \epsilon_i \).

The expectation is taken over randomness, and the expressions for \( \epsilon_{Q,i} \) and \( \epsilon_i \) are detailed, incorporating constants and other parameters defined in previous assumptions. 

Furthermore, the relationship between the performance of the optimal policy \( J(\pi^*) \) and the current policy \( J(\pi_i) \) is established, showing that the difference in their expected values is bounded by a function involving the Kullback-Leibler divergence between the policies, along with terms dependent on \( \eta \), \( R \), and the concentration coefficients. 

The proofs for both Lemmas 5.4 and 5.5 are referenced in Sections D.7 and D.8, respectively.
