This paper addresses the global optimality of neural policy gradient methods, particularly in the context of actor-critic schemes utilizing overparameterized two-layer neural networks. It focuses on two settings: one employing vanilla policy gradient and the other utilizing natural policy gradient, both of which incorporate TD(0) for policy evaluation with independent sampling.

In the first setting, the authors prove that the neural vanilla policy gradient converges to a stationary point of the expected total reward at a rate of 1/√T, where T represents the number of policy improvement steps. They also establish that all stationary points can achieve global optimality under certain regularity conditions, linked to the representation power of the neural networks involved.

In the second setting, the paper demonstrates that the neural natural policy gradient converges to a globally optimal policy at the same rate in expected total reward, emphasizing the importance of compatibility between the actor and critic. This compatibility is achieved through shared architectures and random initializations, which is a common heuristic in practice.

The authors highlight that their analysis provides the first guarantees of global optimality and convergence for neural policy gradient methods, aligning theoretical insights with the methods' empirical successes. The paper also reviews related work, noting the limited theoretical results on convergence for policy gradient methods compared to their extensive empirical literature. Previous analyses primarily established asymptotic convergence and required specific parameterizations for the actor and critic.
