The paper discusses the properties and convergence of neural policy gradient methods within reinforcement learning frameworks. The authors establish that the values of the policy value functions, action-value functions, and advantage functions are bounded by a constant, Qmax. This setting enables the analysis of the convergence rates of different policy gradient algorithms.

In Section 4.1, the authors focus on the convergence of the neural policy gradient algorithm, showing that it converges to a stationary point of the objective function J(πθ) at a sublinear rate. They characterize the geometry of J(πθ) and confirm the global optimality of the stationary point reached. 

Assumption 4.1 introduces a regularity condition on the action-value function Qπ, defining it as belonging to a generalized function class FR,∞. This class encompasses a range of functions that can be represented through neural networks, capturing the properties of the reproducing kernel Hilbert space (RKHS) related to random features.

In Section 4.2, the authors extend their analysis to the neural natural policy gradient, proving that this approach converges to the global optimum of J(πθ) at a sublinear rate as well.

Additionally, Assumption 4.2 imposes a regularity condition on the state visitation measure and the stationary state distribution, ensuring certain boundedness properties of expectations over these measures. This condition is crucial for the analysis of policy convergence under different policies.

Overall, the paper presents a comprehensive examination of convergence properties in neural policy gradient methods, supported by theoretical assumptions that facilitate a deeper understanding of the underlying dynamics in reinforcement learning.
