Lee et al. (2018) explore the relationship between deep neural networks and Gaussian processes, providing insights into their underlying mechanics. In a follow-up study (2019), they demonstrate that wide neural networks, regardless of depth, behave as linear models under gradient descent, emphasizing the impact of network width on learning dynamics. 

Li (2017) offers an overview of deep reinforcement learning, while Li and Liang (2018) discuss the learning process in overparameterized neural networks using stochastic gradient descent, particularly on structured data. 

Liu et al. (2019) introduce a method for policy optimization that guarantees globally optimal policies, and Malik et al. (2018) investigate derivative-free methods for policy optimization within linear quadratic systems. 

Martens and Grosse (2015) focus on optimizing neural networks through Kronecker-factored approximate curvature, which enhances the efficiency of training processes. Mnih et al. (2016) present asynchronous methods for deep reinforcement learning, which improve learning efficiency in complex environments. 

Munos and Szepesvári (2008) provide finite-time bounds for fitted value iteration, contributing to theoretical foundations in reinforcement learning. Nesterov (2018) offers insights on convex optimization, essential for various machine learning algorithms. 

Pan and Srikumar (2016) examine the expressiveness of rectifier networks, furthering understanding of neural network architectures. Lastly, Papini et al. (2018) discuss stochastic variance-reduced policy gradients, enhancing the robustness of policy gradient methods. Peters and Schaal (2006) focus on the application of policy gradient methods in robotics, illustrating practical implementations of the theoretical concepts discussed. 

Overall, these works collectively advance the understanding of neural networks, optimization strategies, and reinforcement learning methodologies.
