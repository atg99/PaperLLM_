The paper discusses a regularity condition related to upper bounded concentrability coefficients, which is commonly used in reinforcement learning literature for theoretical analysis. A specific assumption is introduced regarding the initial parameter in Algorithm 1, labeled as Assumption 4.12. It asserts that there exists a constant M such that the expected supremum of a certain feature mapping, when initialized randomly, is bounded by M squared. This condition implies that as the number of features increases, the two-layer neural network converges to a Gaussian process, suggesting that the maximum of this process over a compact index set is sub-Gaussian.

The paper then presents Theorem 4.13, which establishes global optimality and convergence rates for the neural natural policy gradient. It specifies parameters such as the learning rate and conditions for the actor update. Under certain assumptions, it derives a bound on the expected difference between the optimal policy and the policy derived from the parameters, involving factors like the logarithm of the action space size, the constant M, and terms that depend on the convergence properties of the algorithm. The derived expression indicates the conditions under which the learning process remains efficient and converges to optimal solutions in reinforcement learning settings.
