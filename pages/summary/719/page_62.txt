The paper discusses the properties of a sequence of functions defined over a state-action space, specifically focusing on the relationship between different parameter sets and their gradients. It establishes that for all indices greater than one, the parameters are contained within a bounded set. Using this boundedness, the paper derives inequalities involving the gradients of these functions and the parameters, showing that the difference between function evaluations at two different parameter settings can be controlled.

Key results include an upper bound on the difference between the function values at the current and initial parameters, which is shown to be limited by a constant R. This leads to a bound on the changes in a composite function involving the parameters and state-action values across iterations.

Additionally, the paper applies Pinsker's inequality to relate the changes in the distributions defined by the parameters, indicating that the divergence between them is also constrained. The implications of these results are significant for understanding convergence and stability in the context of the optimization or learning process being analyzed.

Overall, the findings reinforce the boundedness of the parameter sequences and the continuity of function gradients, which are crucial for establishing convergence in iterative algorithms.
