This paper presents a proof related to the convergence properties of a particular optimization problem. It begins by recalling a specific parameter setting, where η is defined as 1/√T. The main inequality established is that for T≥4L², the minimum expectation of a certain gradient term is bounded above by a combination of terms that depend on the expectations of different functions over a series of iterations.

Through a series of telescoping steps, the proof arrives at a critical inequality that involves expectations of gradient norms related to two functions, ρ and δ. The inequalities leverage properties such as the boundedness of certain terms and the application of prior lemmas and propositions to derive upper bounds on these expectations.

The proof further utilizes a specific assumption regarding the behavior of the gradients, leading to a refined bound that combines both the performance of the optimization algorithm and the inherent randomness in the model. The resulting expression includes terms that are inversely proportional to the square root of T, indicating convergence as T increases.

In the subsequent section, the proof of Theorem 4.8 is introduced, establishing that at a stationary point of the objective function J(πθ), the gradient condition holds, ensuring that the optimization landscape behaves appropriately near that point. This contributes to the overall understanding of the optimization algorithm's convergence and stability properties.
