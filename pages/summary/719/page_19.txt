In this section, the paper explores the convergence properties of the neural natural policy gradient method, detailed in Algorithm 1. This approach employs neural temporal difference (TD) methods for policy evaluation and updates the actor using specific equations where both the policy parameters and the associated trajectories are iteratively refined. To assess the critic's performance, the authors rely on Assumptions 4.1 and 4.2, which support the validity of Proposition 4.3. 

For the actor update analysis, additional regularity conditions are introduced. Specifically, Assumption 4.10 establishes a variance upper bound for the estimators of the policy gradient and the Fisher information matrix. The authors define a set of parameters and a function related to the policy gradient, ensuring that certain expectations remain bounded by a constant.

Furthermore, Assumption 4.11 addresses the upper bounded concentrability coefficients of the visitation measures associated with the policy, denoting the state and state-action visitation measures at the optimal policy. The concentrability coefficients are defined through Radon-Nikodym derivatives and are assumed to be uniformly bounded by a constant, ensuring that the measures do not diverge significantly across iterations. This foundational work lays the groundwork for understanding the stability and convergence of the proposed neural natural policy gradient framework.
