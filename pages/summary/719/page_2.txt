We begin by discussing the foundations of reinforcement learning and the role of policy gradient methods in optimizing policies within stochastic environments modeled as Markov decision processes. We highlight the empirical successes of these methods in various applications, including gaming and robotics, while noting the limited theoretical understanding that accompanies them. 

The REINFORCE algorithm, a foundational policy gradient method, is analyzed for its convergence properties, particularly in relation to first- and second-order stationary points. Despite recent advancements in nonconvex optimization theory, questions regarding the global optimality of stationary points remain unresolved.

Furthermore, we investigate the actor-critic framework, which presents additional challenges in convergence analysis due to its reliance on both policy evaluation and improvement steps. The complexities introduced by nonlinear function approximators, such as neural networks, complicate the convergence behavior of the policy evaluation step, which may lead to biased estimates of the policy gradient.

In summary, while policy gradient methods have demonstrated substantial practical success, a comprehensive theoretical understanding of their convergence and optimality, particularly in the context of neural networks, is still lacking. This paper seeks to shed light on these theoretical aspects to enhance the robustness and reliability of policy gradient methods.
