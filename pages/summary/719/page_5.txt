This paper focuses on deep reinforcement learning using policy gradient methods, specifically highlighting the challenges posed by the interaction between the actor and critic during policy evaluation steps based on the TD(0) algorithm. Unlike traditional methods that employ stochastic gradient, this approach utilizes a stochastic semigradient framework. 

The paper introduces essential notations for various norms, including the Lp(µ) and L∞(µ) norms, which are used to measure functions within the context of distributions. The reinforcement learning framework is defined through a discounted Markov decision process (MDP), characterized by state and action spaces, a transition kernel, a stochastic reward function, and an initial state distribution. The policy dictates the probability of taking actions in given states, and the state-value and action-value functions are defined to evaluate the expected cumulative rewards under a specific policy. The advantage function is also introduced, illustrating the difference between action-value and state-value functions, thereby providing a measure of the relative benefit of taking a specific action in a given state.
