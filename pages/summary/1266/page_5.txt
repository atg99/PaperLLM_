The paper discusses the network architectures of two object detection models: Yolov5 and EfficientDet. Yolov5 consists of three main components: the Backbone (CSPDarknet) for feature extraction, the Neck (PANet) for feature fusion, and the Head (Yolo Layer) for outputting detection results such as class, score, location, and size. 

EfficientDet, developed by Google, is highlighted as a superior object detector that demonstrates enhanced efficiency across various resource constraints. It performs well in benchmarks like Pascal VOC and Microsoft COCO, making it applicable in real-world scenarios. The architecture of EfficientDet integrates EfficientNet as its backbone, enabling it to capture complex features of forest fires. 

Three key reasons for selecting EfficientDet as a second learner are presented: 
1. The use of EfficientNet provides strong learning capabilities for complex features.
2. The Bi-directional Feature Pyramid Network (Bi-FPN) enhances multi-scale feature fusion with learnable weights, outperforming PANet in terms of performance and efficiency.
3. EfficientDet employs a compound scaling method that optimally adjusts resolution, depth, and width across the network, maximizing accuracy and efficiency under limited resources.

Overall, EfficientDet's distinct architecture allows it to learn different information compared to Yolov5, thereby broadening the scope of detection capabilities.
