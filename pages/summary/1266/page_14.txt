Through experimental comparisons, it was found that no single detector could effectively manage all fire scenarios. Yolov5 excels in long-area fire detection but struggles with object misses, while EfficientDet, despite its poorer performance in long-area detection, captures fire instances overlooked by Yolov5. The integration of these two models enhanced detection performance by 2.5 to 10.9% across various metrics.

However, the combined model resulted in a higher false positive rate, prompting the introduction of a third learner, EfficientNet, trained on a diverse dataset to distinguish between actual fires and fire-like objects. This addition significantly reduced the false positive rate to 0.3%, although it slightly lowered some performance metrics due to missed detections of certain fire images.

Latency analysis showed that while the Yolov5 family provides superior speed, it does not perform adequately in forest fire detection. The ensemble of the three learners achieved a latency of 66.8 ms, balancing detection performance and efficiency suitable for real-time applications.

Future work will focus on improving labeling strategies for training data, exploring network architectures tailored for forest fire detection, and developing a tracking system to monitor different fire types and their evolution. The study concludes that while convolutional neural networks enhance object detection significantly, the unique characteristics of forest fires present ongoing challenges that require further research and development.
