The paper discusses the application of the Yolov5 object detection model in the context of forest fire detection. It begins by presenting representative images from the non-fire section of the dataset, highlighting normal wild scenes that may contain fire-like objects such as the sun.

Yolov5 is introduced as a state-of-the-art, real-time object detector that builds upon its predecessors, Yolov1 through Yolov4. The model has demonstrated superior performance on recognized object detection benchmarks, including Pascal VOC and Microsoft COCO.

Three key reasons are provided for selecting Yolov5 as the primary model for this research. First, the incorporation of Cross Stage Partial Network (CSPNet) into the backbone architecture, CSPDarknet, enhances efficiency by reducing parameters and floating-point operations per second (FLOPS), which is crucial for achieving high detection speed and accuracy while maintaining a compact model size suitable for resource-constrained environments.

Second, Yolov5 utilizes a Path Aggregation Network (PANet) to improve information flow. PANet's design enhances the propagation of low-level features and optimizes the utilization of localization signals, leading to better object location accuracy.

Lastly, the Yolov5 model generates feature maps in three different sizes to facilitate multi-scale predictions, allowing it to effectively detect fires as they evolve from small ground fires to larger trunk and canopy fires. This capability is essential for accurately monitoring fire progression in forest environments.
