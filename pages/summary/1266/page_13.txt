The paper discusses advancements in forest fire detection through an improved ensemble model that integrates the capabilities of existing detectors, Yolov5 and EfficientDet, along with a third learner, EfficientNet. The study highlights the dynamic nature of forest fires, which can start small and evolve into larger fires, making detection challenging due to their varying shapes, sizes, and colors.

The authors note that no single detector excels at identifying all types of fires. Yolov5 performs well with long-area fires but struggles with smaller ones, while EfficientDet is more cautious and can detect fires that Yolov5 misses. By combining these two models, the proposed ensemble model enhances detection performance by 2.5 to 10.9%, improving average precision and recall for small, medium, and large fires, including those occurring at night.

However, the ensemble approach also leads to an increased false positive rate, rising from 22.6% to 51.6%. To mitigate this, EfficientNet was trained on a dataset of fire and fire-like images, achieving high accuracy in differentiating between actual fires and similar objects. This incorporation significantly reduced the false positive rate to 0.3%.

In terms of latency, the Yolov5 model is faster than EfficientDet, but the latter offers superior detection accuracy. The ensemble model maintains a balance between performance and latency, achieving a total latency of 66.8 ms while leveraging the strengths of all three learners.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of sensitivity in fire detection systems and presents a robust solution through the integration of multiple detection models, demonstrating improved accuracy while managing false positives effectively.
