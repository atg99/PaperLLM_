The paper discusses the training strategies and performance of various object detection models, particularly focusing on forest fire detection. It presents a detailed comparison of models including Yolov5, EfficientDet, and EfficientNet, highlighting their training parameters such as learning rates, batch sizes, and epochs. Notably, while Yolov5 and EfficientDet demonstrate strong detection capabilities, they also exhibit high false positive rates and missed detections.

The introduction of a third learner, EfficientNet, significantly reduces the false positive rate from 51.6% to 0.3%, despite some minor decreases in other metrics like average precision and recall. The authors argue that this trade-off is justified given the substantial improvement in false positive detection. Overall, the proposed model utilizing three learners outperforms other detectors across various metrics, including average precision and average recall, and effectively identifies different types of forest fires, such as ground, trunk, and canopy fires, even under challenging conditions like nighttime or in the presence of fire-like objects.
