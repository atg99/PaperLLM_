The paper presents a model for fire detection that integrates three deep learning algorithms in parallel: two object detectors, Yolov5 and EfficientDet, for detecting fire objects, and a classifier, EfficientNet, for confirming the presence of fire. The model's structure is designed to enhance the accuracy of fire detection by utilizing the strengths of each learner.

To refine the detection process, the paper includes an algorithm for Non-Maximum Suppression (NMS), which helps filter out overlapping detection boxes based on their corresponding scores. This algorithm ensures that the most accurate detections are retained while eliminating redundant ones.

For model evaluation, the authors employ the Microsoft COCO criteria, commonly used for object detection assessments. Recognizing the unique challenges posed by fire's varying shapes, textures, and colors, they introduce two additional metrics: frame accuracy (FA) and false positive rate (FPR). Frame accuracy measures the proportion of correctly identified fire images, while false positive rate assesses the accuracy of detecting fire-like objects. The evaluation is based on a test set of 476 forest images and a non-fire dataset of 641 images containing fire-like objects.

Overall, the proposed model aims to improve fire detection effectiveness through a robust ensemble of deep learning techniques, complemented by comprehensive evaluation metrics tailored to the complexities of fire characteristics.
