The dataset consists of images representing different fire scenarios including ground fires, trunk fires, and canopy fires, ensuring that the model is trained on a diverse array of fire conditions.

The proposed method utilizes an ensemble learning approach, integrating two individual object detectors, Yolov5 and EfficientDet, which operate in parallel to enhance detection accuracy. These detectors leverage their unique strengths to identify different types of forest fires. Additionally, the model incorporates the EfficientNet image classifier to consider global image context, addressing the limitations of object detectors that may misidentify fire-like objects.

The final detection outcome is determined through a decision strategy that synthesizes the outputs of the three learners, leading to improved detection performance and reduced false positives. This multi-faceted approach aims to overcome the complexities of fire detection in varied environments, which individual learners may struggle to address effectively.

In summary, the study highlights the transition from traditional image processing methods to advanced deep learning techniques for fire detection. By employing an ensemble of learners, the proposed method enhances the reliability and accuracy of forest fire detection, paving the way for more effective fire monitoring and management strategies.
