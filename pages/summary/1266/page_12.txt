The figures presented in the paper highlight the performance of an ensemble model comprising three learners in detecting various types of fires, specifically ground fires, trunk fires, and canopy fires. Figure 12 illustrates the comparative detection capabilities of the ensemble model against two other models, Yolov5 and EfficientDet. The ensemble model outperformed both in detecting six ground fires, one trunk fire, and three canopy fires, while Yolov5 failed to detect a trunk fire and had mixed results with canopy fires. EfficientDet showed better detection than Yolov5 but still lagged behind the ensemble model.

Figure 13 further emphasizes the improved performance of the ensemble model in detecting small-scale, medium-scale, and big-scale fires at night. It highlights that while Yolov5 and EfficientDet were able to detect some fires, the ensemble model demonstrated superior detection across all scales of fire. This indicates that the ensemble model is more effective for fire detection, particularly in challenging conditions such as nighttime. Overall, the results suggest that the ensemble approach significantly enhances fire detection capabilities compared to existing models.
