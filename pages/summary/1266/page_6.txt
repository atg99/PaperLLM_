The paper discusses the architecture and performance of EfficientDet, a network designed for object detection, particularly in the context of forest fire detection. EfficientDet consists of three main components: the Backbone (EfficientNet), the Neck (Bi-FPN), and the Head. The architecture allows for feature extraction and fusion, ultimately producing detection results that include class, score, location, and size.

EfficientNet, developed by Google, employs a compound scaling method to optimize network depth, width, and image resolution, resulting in improved accuracy while maintaining resource efficiency. It has outperformed other networks, such as ResNet and DenseNet, in image classification tasks. The authors highlight the importance of EfficientNet in their model as it serves as the third learner, crucial for analyzing the entire image and guiding detection processes.

In the context of real-world forest fire detection, the model must differentiate between various fire types, which pose challenges due to their diverse characteristics. The authors note that Yolov5 excels in detecting long-area fires but occasionally overlooks objects, while EfficientDet, despite being less sensitive to long-area fires, offers more precise detection. This complementary nature of the two models enhances overall detection capabilities in forest fire scenarios.
