The paper introduces essential notations and assumptions relevant to Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) and their analysis. 

Assumption 1 states that rewards are bounded by a finite constant, Rmax. Assumption 2 introduces the transpose Bellman operator, asserting that it has a unique fixed point solution. Assumption 3 indicates that the target density ratio is also bounded by a constant, Wmax. Assumption 4 characterizes the function f as convex with a continuous derivative, with its Fenchel conjugate f* being closed and strictly convex.

The paper defines a range for ν, denoted as N, which is crucial for the derivation of the average return of a policy π. This is formalized through Theorem 3, which presents the average return in both primal and dual forms. The primal form involves minimizing a function subject to constraints related to the Bellman operator, while the dual form focuses on maximizing expected rewards with a different set of constraints.

Under the assumptions, strong duality holds, linking the optimal solutions of both forms. The optimal primal solution corresponds to the Q-function, while the optimal dual solution relates to the density function dπ. The paper also discusses the Lagrangian associated with the primal problem, reparametrizing the dual variable for stability. The optimal solution to this reformulated problem is noted to be unaffected by the expansion of the variable's range, although numerical stability remains a concern.

Overall, the paper provides a structured approach to understanding the interplay between the primal and dual formulations of MDPs, emphasizing the importance of the assumptions in establishing the theoretical framework.
