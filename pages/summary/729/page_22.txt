This section extends the analysis to an undiscounted case where the discount factor γ equals 1. It introduces an additional scalar component, λ, to the primal variable and adheres to Assumption 2, which asserts that the Markov chain induced by policy π is ergodic, leading to a unique invariant distribution dπ and a defined mixing time Tmix. The mixing time indicates how quickly the state distribution approaches dπ in total variation.

The primal and dual forms of the average return of policy π are characterized through linear programming (LP) formulations. The primal problem involves minimizing λ subject to constraints on ν, while the dual problem maximizes the expected return under specific conditions. Under an additional assumption, strong duality is established, indicating that optimal solutions for the primal and dual problems coincide.

The section notes that, unlike the discounted case, there are infinitely many optimal solutions for ν, which allows for constant offsets. The Primal and Fenchel AlgaeDICE optimization problems for γ=1 are defined, with a focus on maximizing the expected return while minimizing the associated costs. The optimal solution for the Fenchel AlgaeDICE problem is shown to meet the necessary constraints without requiring self-normalization, contrasting it with the GenDICE method. Overall, this extension provides insights into the behavior of policies in an undiscounted framework while maintaining the structure of linear programming.
