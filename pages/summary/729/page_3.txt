The paper begins by discussing the dual formulation of the maximum return objective in reinforcement learning (RL), emphasizing its expression through normalized state-action occupancies rather than traditional methods involving policies or value functions. While this approach has been deemed unattractive due to the need for on-policy expectations or function approximators, the authors propose a solution by introducing a controllable regularizer and a strategic change of variables. This results in a joint objective that incorporates both a policy, referred to as the actor, and an auxiliary dual function, interpreted as a critic.

Importantly, this new formulation allows for the use of samples from any off-policy data distribution, which can be gathered from various behavior policies under specific mild conditions. Unlike conventional actor-critic methods that optimize separate objectives for the actor and critic, this method aligns their training toward the same goal. The paper further establishes that optimizing the dual function yields the on-policy policy gradient for the proposed objective, effectively mitigating distribution mismatch issues without needing explicit importance weights.

Additionally, the authors provide an alternative derivation of these findings from a primal-dual perspective, which broadens the applicability of their results to undiscounted settings and unregularized max-return objectives. Empirical experiments demonstrate that their approach, termed AlgaeDICE, performs effectively on benchmark RL tasks.

The background section outlines the RL framework as a Markov Decision Process (MDP), detailing the components such as state space, action space, reward function, transition probability function, and initial state distribution. The objective remains to discover a return-maximizing policy, expressed in both primal and dual forms, thereby establishing a foundation for the proposed methods.
