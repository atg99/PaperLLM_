This paper discusses the optimization of policy π in reinforcement learning, particularly in the context of function approximation and off-policy learning. The policy gradient theorem is introduced, providing a way to compute the gradient of the expected return JP(π) using on-policy samples. The gradient estimation requires interaction with the environment and maintaining estimates of the Q-value function Qπ(s,a). This leads to the development of actor-critic algorithms, where the actor updates π and the critic approximates Q.

The critic is trained to satisfy Bellman consistency through minimizing the difference between the Bellman operator Bπ and the Q-function. While the critic may be learned off-policy, effective performance typically requires incorporating on-policy experience through a replay buffer. The paper emphasizes the focus on off-policy settings, where a dataset of state-action pairs and rewards is utilized, with an unknown sampling process for actions.

The authors introduce their method, AlgaeDICE, which uses density regularization to derive a dual max-return objective. Further details and formal results will be presented in subsequent sections, particularly in relation to the linear programming formulation of the max-return objective.
