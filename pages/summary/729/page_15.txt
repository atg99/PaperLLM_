The paper discusses advancements in reinforcement learning, particularly focusing on effective policy iteration techniques. Key contributions include the concept of Bellman-residual minimization for fitted policy iteration, which allows for the learning of near-optimal policies using a single sample path. The authors reference various foundational works, including Baird's residual algorithms that incorporate function approximation, and recent developments in f-divergence constrained policy improvement by Belousov and Peters. 

The work draws upon established optimization principles, such as those outlined by Bertsekas, and integrates practical tools like OpenAI Gym for experimentation. The discussion extends into stochastic methods and sample complexity in reinforcement learning, as explored by Chen and Wang, emphasizing scalable learning approaches through state and action features.

The paper also highlights dual embeddings and the boosting of actor-critic frameworks, showcasing innovations in reinforcement learning techniques that address convergence and efficiency. The authors cite important theoretical contributions on fixed points in approximate value iteration and temporal-difference learning, as well as regularized policy iteration within nonparametric function spaces.

Overall, the paper synthesizes a range of methodologies and theoretical insights to advance the understanding and application of reinforcement learning strategies.
