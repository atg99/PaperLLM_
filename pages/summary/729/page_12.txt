The paper presents a detailed analysis of the AlgaeDICE algorithm applied to the Four Rooms domain. In Figure 1, the authors illustrate the evolution of learned policies (π) and dual variables (ν) during training. The agent starts at an orange square, with a reward structure that only provides positive feedback at the target state marked by a green square. The training utilizes a near-uniform offline experience data distribution. The policy π is depicted through arrows, where the opacity indicates the probability of each action at a given state. The dual variables ν are illustrated by their Bellman residuals, showing high residuals near the initial state at the start of training, which decrease as the training progresses, indicating the agent's learning of preferred trajectories toward the target state.

Quantitative results in Figure 2 compare the average per-step rewards of AlgaeDICE against an actor-critic approach in both online and offline scenarios. While all methods improve upon a baseline behavior policy, AlgaeDICE demonstrates robust performance across different datasets, unlike the actor-critic, which suffers in offline settings. 

Additionally, the paper discusses the application of AlgaeDICE to continuous control benchmarks using MuJoCo and OpenAI Gym, focusing on the performance of Primal AlgaeDICE with a specific empirical objective. Overall, the findings indicate that AlgaeDICE effectively learns to optimize policies while correcting for distribution shifts in off-policy experience distributions.
