The paper discusses a method that provides both regularized Q-values and desired state-action corrections by utilizing a Lagrangian primal-dual approach to the linear programming (LP) characterization of the Q-function, resulting in a saddle-point problem. This method is compared to existing works that also leverage saddle-point formulations for policy improvement, though from different perspectives. Notably, while some works focus on the optimal V-function, the authors emphasize that their focus on the Q-function is essential for behavior-agnostic policy optimization in AlgaeDICE. 

The authors argue that using a regularizer on the dual variable is crucial for transitioning the optimization from values and state-action occupancies to values and policies, which is more practical and enhances generalization. They note that their approach to regularization, which involves divergence over state-action densities, differs significantly from previous methods that primarily regularize action distributions conditioned on state.

Furthermore, they draw parallels with the work of Belousov and Peters, highlighting that while both approaches utilize Lagrange multipliers, their objectives and practical implementations differ in key aspects. The authors assert that their findings extend to both exploratory and unregularized objectives, unlike the conservative regularization of Belousov and Peters. Overall, the paper situates its contributions within the broader context of reinforcement learning, particularly in relation to regularization techniques and policy optimization strategies.
