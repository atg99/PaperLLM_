The paper discusses the optimization problem related to an exploratory regularizer with a parameter α<0, leading to a complex max-min-max optimization structure. The challenge posed by the presence of Bπ in the second term is addressed, as it requires handling expectations over the transition function T with limited empirical samples, a situation referred to as double sampling. To overcome this, the authors employ a dual embedding technique, transforming the problem into a more manageable form.

The equivalence between the objective functions is established by examining the gradients concerning the policy π. The optimal solutions for both the value function and the policy gradient are derived, revealing that the gradient can be expressed in a way that incorporates the expected values of the regularizers and transitions. This establishes a connection between off-policy data and the policy gradient, highlighting the framework's potential for consistent policy improvement using off-policy data. The findings suggest that under certain conditions, strong duality allows for simplification of the optimization process, facilitating better convergence in policy learning tasks.
