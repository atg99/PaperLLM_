The paper discusses the application of Danskin's theorem to derive insights about the optimization of off-policy objectives in reinforcement learning. By optimizing the dual function ν, the authors demonstrate that the gradient of the off-policy objective JD,f(ν,π) aligns with the on-policy policy gradient, characterized through a Q-value function denoted as ν∗π. This Q-value function is defined with respect to an augmented reward that incorporates a regularization term derived from the dual function.

The authors present Theorem 2, which asserts that when the dual function ν is optimized, the gradient of the off-policy objective with respect to the policy π reflects a regularized on-policy policy gradient. Specifically, the Q-value function ˜Qπ(s,a) is analyzed, leading to a formulation that connects the proposed off-policy objective with traditional actor-critic methods. The relationship is highlighted when a specific convex function f is chosen, showing that the proposed objective closely resembles the actor-critic framework.

A notable distinction is made between the challenges faced in standard actor-critic methods, particularly concerning the mismatch between off-policy and on-policy distributions. The authors argue that their approach effectively transforms an off-policy actor-critic algorithm into an on-policy one, eliminating the need for importance weights and unifying the objectives for both policy and value functions.

Additionally, the paper introduces a Lagrangian perspective on the AlgaeDICE algorithm, which is derived from a linear programming formulation of the Qπ-function. Further details are provided in the appendix.
