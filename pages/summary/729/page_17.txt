The referenced works collectively contribute to the field of reinforcement learning, particularly focusing on off-policy methods and the interplay between value-based and policy-based approaches. Liu et al. (2019) introduce off-policy policy gradient methods that correct for state distribution, enhancing learning efficiency. Lu et al. (2018) discuss non-delusional Q-learning, which aims to stabilize learning by avoiding overestimation of value functions.

Meyn and Tweedie (2012) provide foundational concepts on Markov chains and stochastic stability, crucial for understanding the theoretical underpinnings of reinforcement learning algorithms. Mnih et al. (2013) showcase the application of deep reinforcement learning in playing Atari games, marking a significant milestone in the domain.

Munos et al. (2016) propose methods for safe and efficient off-policy reinforcement learning, addressing the challenges of learning from past experiences without compromising performance. Nachum et al. (2017a, 2017b) bridge the gap between value and policy methods and introduce Trust-PCL, an off-policy trust region method for continuous control, respectively. They further explore smoothed action value functions for Gaussian policies (2018) and multi-agent manipulation through hierarchical simulation to reality techniques (2019a).

Lastly, DualDICE (Nachum et al., 2019b) offers a behavior-agnostic approach for estimating stationary distribution corrections, while Neu et al. (2017) present a unified perspective on entropy-regularized Markov decision processes. Nguyen et al. (2010) contribute to the theoretical framework by estimating divergence functionals through convex risk minimization, enhancing the understanding of policy evaluation and optimization in reinforcement learning contexts.
