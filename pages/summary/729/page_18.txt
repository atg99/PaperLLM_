The references provided represent a collection of significant contributions to the field of reinforcement learning and Markov Decision Processes (MDPs). 

1. Precup, Sutton, and Singh discuss eligibility traces for off-policy policy evaluation, highlighting a method that allows for more efficient learning in reinforcement learning frameworks. 

2. Puterman's book on MDPs serves as a foundational text, detailing discrete stochastic dynamic programming and its applications in decision-making tasks.

3. Rubin, Shamir, and Tishby examine the trade-off between value and information in MDPs, emphasizing how imperfect decision-making can impact outcomes.

4. Schulman et al. introduce proximal policy optimization algorithms, a popular method for training reinforcement learning models that balances exploration and exploitation.

5. Silver et al. focus on deterministic policy gradient algorithms, presenting a framework that enhances learning efficiency in continuous action spaces.

6. Sutton, Precup, and Singh propose a framework that bridges MDPs and semi-MDPs, enabling temporal abstraction in reinforcement learning.

7. Sutton et al. also address policy gradient methods, offering insights into reinforcement learning with function approximation, which is crucial for scaling learning algorithms.

8. Swaminathan and Joachims present a method for batch learning from logged bandit feedback, emphasizing counterfactual risk minimization as a key technique.

9. Tang et al. discuss doubly robust bias reduction in off-policy estimation, providing strategies for improving the reliability of policy evaluation.

10. Todorov introduces linearly-solvable MDPs, presenting a novel approach that simplifies the solution process for certain classes of decision problems.

11. Together with Erez and Tassa, Todorov develops Mujoco, a physics engine designed for model-based control applications in robotics.

12. Uehara and Jiang focus on minimax weight and Q-function learning for off-policy evaluation, contributing to the understanding of robust evaluation techniques.

13. Wang presents a randomized linear programming approach to solving discounted MDPs efficiently, showcasing advancements in computational methods.

14. Lastly, Wang et al. explore dual representations for dynamic programming, offering new perspectives on solving dynamic programming problems.

These works collectively advance the understanding and application of reinforcement learning and decision-making processes, providing key methodologies and theoretical frameworks that have influenced ongoing research in the field.
