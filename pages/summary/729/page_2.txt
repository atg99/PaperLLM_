The paper discusses the challenges of applying reinforcement learning (RL) in real-world scenarios where interactions with the environment are costly or limited, necessitating off-policy methods. Off-policy RL algorithms learn from previously collected experience rather than relying on current policy interactions. Traditional policy gradient methods face difficulties in off-policy contexts, as they typically require samples from the on-policy distribution to estimate gradients. While importance weighting can align off-policy samples with on-policy objectives, it introduces high variance and instability.

Many existing off-policy policy gradient methods ignore the distribution shift in samples, justifying this by asserting that the maximum return policy remains optimal regardless of the sampling distribution. However, this assumption is flawed in function approximation contexts, potentially leading to poor optimization and convergence. Value-based methods, such as actor-critic and Q-learning, offer an alternative approach, as they are inherently off-policy. Yet, they still struggle with defining the ideal expectation when using function approximation, often defaulting to on-policy expectations despite the challenges.

The authors propose a new algorithm called ALgorithm for policy Gradient from Arbitrary Experience via DICE (AlgaeDICE), which aims to provide a more effective means of policy optimization in off-policy settings. This algorithm is inspired by previous work on distribution correction estimation in off-policy policy evaluation, focusing on policy optimization while addressing the limitations of existing methods.
