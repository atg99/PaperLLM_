The paper discusses a novel approach called AlgaeDICE, which integrates policy and value learning into a single, unified objective, differentiating it from existing methods that typically utilize separate objectives. The authors highlight that many existing works apply policy gradient techniques on off-policy data through distribution correction, with the key distinction being in their computation methods. Traditional approaches, such as those involving eligibility traces, suffer from high variance due to the "curse of horizon" as trajectory lengths increase. 

In comparison, AlgaeDICE implicitly estimates state-action distribution corrections, streamlining the optimization process into a single level, unlike previous methods that rely on bi-level optimization. Additionally, while existing methods require knowledge of the behavior policy, AlgaeDICE is behavior-agnostic, making it potentially more applicable in real-world scenarios. 

The empirical evaluation of AlgaeDICE is conducted in two settings: first, in a tabular environment using the Four Rooms domain and then on continuous control benchmarks with MuJoCo and OpenAI Gym. In the Four Rooms experiment, the authors implement Primal AlgaeDICE by solving the dual value function using matrix operations and optimizing the policy iteratively. They collect off-policy data through a uniformly random policy and analyze the learned policies and value functions across multiple training iterations, showcasing the effectiveness of their approach.
