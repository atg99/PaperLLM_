Chapter 3 of the paper discusses the foundational theories of ethics as they relate to robots and AI systems. It distinguishes between ethics and morality, where morality encompasses the rules and norms guiding actions, while ethics focuses on the theoretical principles behind these moral frameworks. The chapter traces the etymology of ethics to the Greek term "ethos," which relates to customs and conventions, and highlights Immanuel Kant's perspective on ethics as addressing the question of what individuals should do.

The chapter introduces three major approaches to moral theory: deontology, which emphasizes duties and rules; consequentialism, which considers the outcomes of actions; and virtue ethics, which focuses on the character of the moral agent. It also addresses the relationship between ethics and law, noting that while law may reflect ethical principles, the two are not synonymous.

Furthermore, the chapter briefly touches on descriptive ethics, which examines and describes existing normative systems, differentiating it from normative ethics, which prescribes how people ought to act. Overall, the chapter sets the stage for understanding how these ethical theories can be implemented in machines to facilitate ethical decision-making in robotics and AI.
