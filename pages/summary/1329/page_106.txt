The paper discusses the military applications of artificial intelligence (AI), particularly focusing on autonomous weapons systems (AWS). Proponents argue that AWS can enhance military effectiveness, especially in defensive scenarios, where these systems may outperform human units, thereby reducing human casualties. This perspective emphasizes the ethical obligation of military leaders to protect their personnel by developing such technologies.

Conversely, opponents raise concerns about the potential for an arms race, as nations like Russia and China increase their AI investments, with the belief that dominance in this area could determine global power dynamics. Critics also highlight the moral implications of AWS, suggesting that their use may lead to an escalation in conflict due to reduced human costs of warfare. The paper references evidence from the U.S. military's shift to unmanned aerial vehicles in Iraq, which correlated with an increase in targeted missions.

Philosophically, the dignity argument posits that being killed by a machine undermines human dignity, advocating for the necessity of human involvement in lethal decisions. This claim asserts that a fundamental human right exists against being killed by an algorithm, emphasizing the importance of human judgment in combat situations.

In conclusion, the paper outlines the definitions and regulatory considerations surrounding AWS, reflecting on the complex ethical, strategic, and philosophical debates that accompany their development and deployment in military contexts.
