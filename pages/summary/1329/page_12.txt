Chapter 1 introduces the book focused on the ethics of robots and artificial intelligence, targeting university students, policymakers, and professionals while remaining accessible to a broader adult audience. It aims to present balanced and occasionally conflicting viewpoints regarding the benefits and drawbacks of AI through an ethical lens. The authors emphasize that ethical questions are complex and vary across different nations, communities, and individuals, highlighting the importance of diverse perspectives in these discussions.

The chapter also introduces Christoph Bartneck, one of the authors, who is an associate professor and director of postgraduate studies at the HIT Lab NZ, University of Canterbury. With a background in Industrial Design and Human-Computer Interaction, Bartneck's research examines the impact of anthropomorphism on human-robot interaction, alongside interests in bibliometric analyses and social simulations. His work has been featured in prominent media outlets, reflecting his contributions to the fields of design and technology ethics.
