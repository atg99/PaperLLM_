Chapter 11 of the paper focuses on the military applications of artificial intelligence, particularly the contentious issue of autonomous weapons systems (AWS). The chapter defines key terms and presents varying perspectives on the debate surrounding AWS, which are capable of making autonomous lethal decisions regarding human targets. 

The discussion highlights that while some military systems, such as cruise missiles and the Patriot missile system, can be classified as lethal autonomous weapons, others are primarily defensive in nature. Examples include the AEGIS and Phalanx systems. The chapter also addresses the Israeli Harpy, an offensive weapon designed to target enemy radar systems.

In addition to lethal applications, the text acknowledges a range of non-lethal uses for military robots, including mine clearance, explosive ordnance disposal, reconnaissance, and support operations. The chapter aims to provide an objective overview of the principal arguments for and against the deployment of AWS in warfare, reflecting the complexities and ethical considerations inherent in their use.
