The paper discusses the complexities surrounding trust and fairness in AI systems, particularly in the context of social media and ethical principles. It highlights the challenges faced by platforms like Twitter and Facebook in balancing the removal of illegal content with the risk of over-censorship, raising concerns about freedom of speech versus the need to combat hate speech. 

The principle of Beneﬁcence is emphasized, asserting that AI should prioritize doing good for society. This principle, rooted in bioethics, suggests that the benefits of AI applications must outweigh potential harms. Examples include reducing accidents through autonomous vehicles, providing care for the elderly, enhancing telemedicine, promoting sustainability, preserving biodiversity, and facilitating individualized education.

Additionally, the principle of Autonomy is addressed, which mandates that AI systems respect individuals' goals and wishes. While autonomy often relates to an AI's operational independence, the paper focuses on the ethical dimension, particularly in bioethics, where it pertains to individuals' rights to make informed decisions about their lives. Overall, the paper underscores the need for AI systems to align with ethical principles to foster trust and fairness in their deployment.
