The paper discusses various risks associated with the business of artificial intelligence, emphasizing ethical and functional risks that can significantly impact companies. 

1. **Ethical Risks to Reputation**: A company's reputation is a critical asset, and ethical missteps can severely damage it. For instance, major brands like Apple and BMW have substantial brand values that they must protect. Short-term gain should not come at the cost of long-term brand integrity.

2. **Ethical Risks to Stock Price**: Ethical scandals can lead to drastic declines in stock prices. The case of BP illustrates this, as its stock plummeted from USD 60 to USD 27 following the Deepwater Horizon disaster. Such declines can have dire consequences for executives and lead to company takeovers.

3. **Ethical Risks of Legal Fines**: Companies that underestimate ethical risks may face hefty legal fines. BP, for example, incurred over USD 65 billion in costs related to the Deepwater Horizon incident. Similarly, Siemens faced around EUR 2 billion in fines due to systemic corruption issues. The Dieselgate scandal has also cost Volkswagen approximately USD 30 billion as of 2017.

4. **General Business Risks**: The paper outlines functional risks, which pertain to the potential failure of a system's functionality. This includes the risk of software or hardware malfunctions when released to the public, as well as the possibility that updates may inadvertently disrupt system operations or compatibility with other systems.

In summary, the paper highlights the importance of managing ethical and functional risks to safeguard a company's reputation, stock value, and financial stability in the face of technological advancements in AI.
