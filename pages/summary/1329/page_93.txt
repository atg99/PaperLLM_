The paper discusses the ethical implications and benefits of autonomous vehicles (AVs), particularly regarding their legal status and safety concerns. It raises the question of whether society has an ethical obligation to allow AVs on public roads. One argument presented is that while AVs follow traffic laws, their presence may inadvertently lead to more accidents as human drivers may navigate around them when they adhere to lower speed limits, creating dangerous situations. 

The paper also highlights existing incidents involving AVs, noting that although the number of accidents is relatively small, they have attracted significant media scrutiny. A notable example is the 2016 Tesla accident where the vehicle failed to detect a truck, resulting in the driver's death. Improvements in sensory technology have followed since this incident. Another case discussed is the 2018 Uber Volvo accident in Tempe, Arizona, where a pedestrian was killed. This incident raised questions about the vehicle's emergency braking system, which was deactivated to prevent false alerts, and the driver's lack of proper attention. Overall, the paper emphasizes the complexities surrounding the integration of AVs into society and their potential ethical ramifications.
