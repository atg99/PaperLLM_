The paper discusses the ethical dilemmas associated with the application of artificial intelligence (AI) in enhancement technologies. It highlights the development of non-invasive mechanical and electrical devices, such as exo-skeletons, that aim to enhance human capabilities. The ethical concerns are particularly pronounced in competitive contexts, such as athletics, where enhancements may provide significant advantages to those who can afford them.

A key example is the use of prosthetics, which have advanced to the point of potentially offering competitive edges in sports. This raises questions about fair competition and the health risks involved for athletes. The Superhuman Sports Society represents a unique case where enhancements are embraced and acknowledged, differing from traditional sports that rely on fairness.

The paper also contrasts cognitive and physical enhancements, citing historical uses of drugs like amphetamines in military contexts and the rising prevalence of unprescribed cognitive enhancers among college students. This leads to questions about the ethics of using "Smart Drugs" for academic advantages and whether such practices are akin to doping in sports.

Moreover, mechanical methods for cognitive enhancement, such as transcranial magnetic stimulation, are emerging, along with potential genetic modifications for enhancement. While these technologies can provide significant societal benefits, the paper emphasizes the need for careful consideration of their implications, particularly regarding the distinction between enhancement and shortcuts to excellence. The discussion calls for ongoing debate about the ethical boundaries of enhancement technologies.
