The section discusses the concepts of responsibility and liability concerning AI systems, highlighting the distinction between moral and legal responsibility. Moral responsibility refers to ethical obligations and accountability based on moral principles, while legal responsibility pertains to obligations enforced by law, often involving punitive measures. 

In the event of wrongdoing by an AI, accountability may be attributed to various parties: the software itself for its actions, the programmers for their design choices, or the company for its overall governance and production practices. The determination of blame may vary based on the context and the specifics of the incident.

Compensation for individuals severely injured by an autonomous vehicle could involve financial restitution covering medical expenses, lost wages, and pain and suffering. The rationale for this approach is rooted in the principle of ensuring justice for the victim and holding accountable those responsible for the AI's operation, be it the manufacturer or the developers.

Further reading is suggested for those interested in deeper explorations of responsibility and liability within the context of criminal law, philosophical perspectives on responsibility, and the ethical obligations of online service providers.
