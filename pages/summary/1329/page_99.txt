The paper discusses ethical considerations surrounding autonomous vehicles (A Vs), particularly the potential for them to become mandatory and the implications of such a shift. The authors argue that it is premature to mandate A Vs, emphasizing the need for public discourse on the matter. Currently, society is expected to coexist with both autonomous and traditional vehicles for the foreseeable future, which studies suggest will significantly reduce accidents and save lives.

The paper poses several discussion questions aimed at exploring individual comfort levels with various degrees of vehicle autonomy, the legal responsibilities outlined in the Vienna Convention concerning A Vs, and whether algorithms should differentiate between young and old pedestrians in decision-making scenarios.

Further readings are suggested, highlighting works that delve into the ethical dilemmas of programming morality into autonomous systems and the development of ethical codes for automated driving. The overall tone is one of caution and the necessity for thorough examination and dialogue regarding the integration of A Vs into society.
