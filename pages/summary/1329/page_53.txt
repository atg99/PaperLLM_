The paper discusses the concept of strict liability in the context of autonomous vehicles and complex liability scenarios, particularly focusing on the challenges that arise from the opacity of decision-making processes in such systems. 

In autonomous vehicles, liability has shifted from drivers or owners to manufacturers or software developers, facilitated by updates to the Vienna Convention on Road Traffic. The German Ethics Code for Automated and Connected Driving emphasizes the need for monitoring devices to determine control during incidents, thereby clarifying responsibility.

The paper further explores the issue of complex liability, illustrated by the AWS case, where multiple parties contributed to a wrongful death, complicating the attribution of blame. In these situations, it is often difficult to pinpoint an individual at fault, leading to the assignment of collective blame to entities such as states or corporations rather than individuals.

Lastly, the paper touches on the implications of liability, suggesting that sanctions may be more effectively directed at corporations rather than individuals. Historical examples, such as substantial fines against Volkswagen and Bank of America, demonstrate the potential for significant corporate liability. Overall, the discussion highlights the evolving nature of liability in technology-driven contexts and the complexities involved in legal accountability.
