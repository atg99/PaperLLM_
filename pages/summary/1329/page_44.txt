The paper discusses the complexities of trust and fairness in AI systems, particularly in the context of moral decision-making. It highlights the limitations of human understanding in moral intuitions, noting that while moral theories may differ, there are commonly accepted actions deemed right or wrong. The paper argues that AI can make specific moral decisions when the scope of application is narrowed, and relevant information is accessible. 

One significant application of AI is in determining creditworthiness, where banks utilize AI to assess credit applications based on available data. This process can enhance decision-making speed and rationality but may also introduce biases, particularly against individuals from certain neighborhoods. 

The paper further examines racial bias in algorithms used for high-risk care management. A study by Obermeyer et al. revealed that even algorithms excluding race can inadvertently perpetuate racial disparities through their reliance on healthcare cost data. The assumption that those with the highest healthcare costs will need the most care leads to inequities, particularly affecting poor patients who face barriers to healthcare access. The authors emphasize that the core issue lies in the formulation of problems and the challenges in creating algorithms that accurately reflect complex social realities without reinforcing structural inequalities. These concerns are prevalent across various industries employing algorithmic decision-making.
