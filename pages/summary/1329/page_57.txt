The section discusses various general business risks, focusing on systemic risk, risk of fraud, and safety risk. 

Systemic risk refers to risks that can impact an entire system, particularly in finance. The 2007-08 Global Financial Crisis exemplified systemic risk, stemming from widespread defaults on subprime loans, which undermined major mortgage lenders and led to a loss of confidence in financial markets. Lehman Brothers' bankruptcy highlighted the dangers of inadequate risk assessment models, resulting in a freeze in interbank lending and necessitating extensive government bailouts. The use of advanced AI systems in finance, while prevalent, raises concerns about potential market destabilization, as seen in the Flash Crash of 2010.

The risk of fraud is illustrated by the Dieselgate scandal involving Volkswagen, where the company employed deceptive software to manipulate emissions tests, leading to significant environmental and reputational damage.

Safety risk addresses the physical dangers posed by automation in industrial settings. Incidents such as the Deepwater Horizon explosion and fatal accidents involving autonomous vehicles from Uber and Tesla highlight the potential hazards associated with robotic systems and autonomous technologies, emphasizing the importance of safety measures in these environments.
