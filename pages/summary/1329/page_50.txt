The paper discusses the complexities of responsibility and liability in the context of fully autonomous systems, focusing on two key examples: autonomous vehicles and autonomous weapons.

In the case of fully autonomous vehicles, particularly those operating at level 3 automation, the vehicle can manage all driving functions without human oversight. This raises critical questions about liability in the event of an accident occurring while the vehicle is in control. The dilemma lies in determining who is responsible—the driver, who is not actively controlling the vehicle, or the manufacturer, who may have taken all necessary precautions yet still encountered an accident.

The second example examines the liability issues surrounding autonomous weapons in a military context. The paper explores a hypothetical situation where an autonomous drone misidentifies a civilian, Jennifer, as a combatant due to facial recognition errors. This leads to her wrongful death. The discussion focuses on accountability for the incident, questioning whether the blame lies with the autonomous system, the operators, or the military command. It emphasizes the importance of distinguishing between remote piloting scenarios and fully autonomous actions, highlighting the challenges in prosecuting such cases under International Humanitarian Law.

Overall, the paper underscores the need for clear frameworks of responsibility and liability as AI systems become more integrated into both civilian and military applications.
