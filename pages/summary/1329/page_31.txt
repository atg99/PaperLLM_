The section addresses two branches of ethics: meta-ethics and applied ethics. Meta-ethics focuses on the meaning of moral terms and how moral truths can be known, while applied ethics involves the practical application of ethical judgments in specific fields such as medicine, biotechnology, and business. The relationship between normative ethics and applied ethics is bidirectional, meaning that general ethical principles can influence specific cases, and specific conditions can inform general ethical considerations. For instance, the principle of solidarity may entail different actions depending on the context, such as sharing resources among friends versus competing fairly in a broader societal context.
