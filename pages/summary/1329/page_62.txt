The paper discusses the risks associated with artificial intelligence (AI) and the common fear of mass technological unemployment. It argues against the notion that AI and automation will lead to widespread job losses by pointing out that, prior to the COVID-19 pandemic, employment rates in the United States were at their lowest in 30 years, indicating that new jobs are being created to replace those lost to automation. The paper highlights that predictions of robots replacing human workers are not new, citing past cover stories from Der Spiegel in 1964, 1978, and 2017 that made similar claims. 

The central argument is that while technological advancement does disrupt employment, it also enables workers to achieve more with less and engage in more valuable work as society's wealth increases. Historically, new jobs have emerged to replace those rendered obsolete by technological progress. Therefore, the lack of high unemployment rates in technologically advanced nations suggests that the predictions of mass unemployment due to AI are unfounded.
