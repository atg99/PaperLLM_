The section discusses the dangers associated with private data collection, particularly focusing on auto insurance discrimination and the Chinese Social Credit System. Auto insurance companies utilize extensive data to assess driving behavior and categorize drivers into risk classes, which raises concerns about potential bias and discrimination stemming from AI applications in this context.

The Chinese Social Credit System, initiated in 2014, represents a significant expansion of data collection by the government, going beyond traditional credit ratings to include personal behaviors such as web browsing. This system, already operational in assessing financial credit scores, poses serious implications for citizens, including potential restrictions on flying, access to education, and job opportunities based on their social credit score. The centralized nature of this system, combined with the government's technical capabilities and plans for future data usage, raises alarms about the potential for control, misuse, and systemic bias, exacerbated by a lack of transparency and a free press.

Looking ahead, the paper highlights the ease with which programmatic advertisement principles could be applied in other domains, such as autonomous vehicles making life-and-death decisions based on passengers' social credit scores. It warns of a dystopian future where societal indifference to privacy issues could normalize such practices. The evolving attitude towards private data sharing is noted, contrasting past hesitations with contemporary trends where individuals, especially social influencers, readily share personal information online.
