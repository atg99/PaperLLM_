The discussion around autonomous vehicles (AVs) emphasizes the need for clear regulations and ethical guidelines to ensure safe usage. Incidents involving Tesla's autopilot feature highlight the dangers of driver complacency, underscoring the necessity for manufacturers to communicate usage rules effectively. In response to these challenges, a national ethics committee in Germany was formed to establish 20 ethical guidelines for AVs, aiming to influence industry practices and policies across Europe.

Critics caution against overestimating the safety of AVs, noting potential biases in data related to ideal driving conditions and the divergence between human driving norms and AV behavior. The RAND Corporation has posited that AVs need to demonstrate extensive safe driving records before being deemed safe, yet also advocates for their deployment to enhance road safety. Ethical considerations are further complicated by the terminology used to describe AV capabilities, which may mislead users regarding their operational limits and responsibilities.
