The paper discusses the concepts of artificial intelligence (AI) and robotics, focusing on the mechanisms of reinforcement learning and the operational framework of robots. Reinforcement learning is described as a method where an AI agent, such as a robot, receives feedback based on its actions, allowing it to learn a policy that maps actions to expected rewards. This process helps the robot navigate toward desired goals by evaluating states of the world as more or less desirable.

In defining what constitutes a robot, the paper highlights that robots are physical entities situated in the real world, unlike typical AI agents that may operate in simulated environments. The "sense-plan-act" framework is central to how robots function; they first sense their environment, then plan their actions based on perceptions and knowledge, and finally execute those actions.

The challenges of robot sensing are emphasized, noting that real-world sensors like cameras and laser scanners have limitations that can complicate perception. For instance, cameras may struggle with color shifts under varying light conditions, while laser scanners may have difficulty with transparent objects. The paper contrasts human sensory capabilities, which involve a greater number of integrated sensors and complex brain structures for learning and knowledge integration, with the more limited capabilities of machines. Planning in robots must account for uncertainties in the environment and utilize any available historical knowledge to inform decision-making.
