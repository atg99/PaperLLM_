The concept of assigning responsibility for actions taken by automated weapon systems (AWS) raises significant legal and ethical questions. In a scenario where AWS inadvertently kills an innocent person, the challenge lies in determining who should be held accountable, as the system itself cannot experience suffering or punishment. 

The paper argues that responsibility for AWS actions should be assigned to the humans or state that operate these systems, utilizing the legal principle of strict liability. This principle holds individuals or organizations accountable for damages caused by their products, even in the absence of direct wrongdoing. For instance, a pet owner is liable for damages caused by their pet, regardless of intent. Similarly, with AI technologies, including autonomous vehicles, strict liability could apply to unforeseen harms resulting from their operation.

The discussion highlights that the unpredictable nature of AI systems complicates liability, as harm may not always be anticipated. As such, strict liability provides a necessary framework to address these challenges and ensure accountability in the deployment of AI technologies.
