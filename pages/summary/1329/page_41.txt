The section discusses ethical principles surrounding autonomy in the context of trustworthy and fair AI. Autonomy is defined as an individual's capacity to make personal decisions, including the right to refuse medical treatment, which courts generally uphold for adults. The text highlights the importance of informed risk-taking, as illustrated by examples like Sherpas earning more money at the risk of avalanches.

However, ethical limits to autonomy are acknowledged. For instance, a robot should not comply with harmful requests from children or adults, as AI systems should not facilitate illegal or immoral actions. The design of AI must carefully consider the implications of allowing machines to inflict harm, even in cases where individuals request it, such as in euthanasia scenarios.

The text also addresses the role of robots in law enforcement and military contexts, where they may be used to harm individuals under specific circumstances. The moral philosophy underpinning these decisions relates to autonomy and moral responsibility, drawing from Immanuel Kant's deontological ethics, which emphasizes acting according to universal moral laws.
