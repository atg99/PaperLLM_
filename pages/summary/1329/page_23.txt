In this section, the paper discusses the nature of robots and artificial intelligence (AI) agents, emphasizing that they lack innate knowledge about the world, unlike humans who are born with certain abilities. Robots must utilize their body schema and knowledge of their embodiment to execute movements according to a predetermined plan. After acting, they may need to inform their sensing processes to guide future actions. 

The paper contrasts the blank slate nature of AI agents with the pre-programmed abilities of human infants, highlighting that AI systems only know what they are explicitly programmed to know and struggle to generate meaningful knowledge from perception. The discussion includes the distinction between symbolic and non-symbolic representations; while symbolic representations are human-readable, they are limited in their connection to a robot's sensors. In contrast, non-symbolic representations relate better to sensor data but are less comprehensible to humans.

Additionally, the paper addresses the complexities of system integration in robotics. Developing a functional robotic system requires the coordination of various systems, programs, and processes, which is often challenging due to the different types of data generated by sensors, each with unique limitations and potential failures. This integration is crucial for creating effective AI agents and robots.

Lastly, the text clarifies that not all software qualifies as AI, distinguishing between simple algorithms and truly intelligent systems, and noting that some non-AI systems can exhibit smart behavior.
