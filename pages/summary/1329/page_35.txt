Machine ethics faces significant challenges due to the absence of consensus on a correct moral theory, complicating the implementation of moral competence in AI and robots. To address this, ethical test cases can be developed, allowing AI systems to demonstrate their moral capabilities. As these systems encounter and respond to various test cases, their moral competence may evolve, potentially providing insights into moral theory.

Research into testing and certifying AI fairness and ethics has gained traction, exemplified by initiatives like the IEEE Standards Project addressing algorithmic bias. Toolkits, such as the AI Fairness 360 Open Source Toolkit, have been created to assist developers in identifying biases in their algorithms. Some companies, including O’Neil Risk Consulting and Algorithmic Auditing, provide services to evaluate algorithmic fairness, while larger organizations like Facebook are developing tools like Fairness Flow.

Despite ongoing efforts in this field, some researchers express skepticism regarding the feasibility of machine morality. Additionally, various research groups are working on establishing ethical codes for robotics engineers and human-robot interactions. 

The discussion questions prompt exploration of concepts such as the distinction between normative ethics and meta-ethics, the ethical evaluation of AI company managers from deontological and consequentialist perspectives, and the potential for programming ethics into machines.
