Chapter 10 of the paper focuses on autonomous vehicles (AVs), outlining their definition, functionality, and the associated risks and benefits from ethical, legal, and environmental perspectives. The chapter emphasizes key issues such as liability and safety in relation to AVs. 

A significant discussion point is the terminology surrounding AVs. Critics argue that the term "autonomous" implies a level of decision-making and self-governance akin to human autonomy, which does not accurately reflect the capabilities of AI in vehicles. Instead, terms like "highly automated vehicles" or "fully automated vehicles" may be more appropriate. Nonetheless, the established term "autonomous vehicles" will be used for consistency.

The chapter also introduces the classification of autonomous driving levels, highlighting systems developed by the US Society of Automotive Engineers (SAE) and the German Association of the Automotive Industry (VDA). While the classifications differ in specifics, they share a common framework. The first level discussed is Level 0, which refers to traditional vehicles lacking any automated features. 

Overall, the chapter aims to clarify the complexities surrounding AVs while addressing the ethical implications and the need for clear definitions in the discourse on automation in vehicles.
