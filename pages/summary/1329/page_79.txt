The paper discusses the growing significance of privacy in the context of increasing data collection and the evolving role of artificial intelligence in data utilization. It emphasizes that cultural and historical factors heavily influence the perception of privacy among populations. As AI enhances the ability to predict and analyze data, future generations will face the challenge of defining the boundaries of privacy and data usage.

Key discussion questions are posed, prompting reflection on what personal information individuals might want to keep private from digital assistants, who within the population might be most susceptible to privacy invasions by AI, and the potential benefits of a social credit system.

For further exploration, the paper recommends two texts: "Ethics for the Information Age" by Michael J Quinn and "A Gift of Fire" by Sare Baase and Timothy M. Henry, which delve into the ethical, social, and legal implications of technology.
