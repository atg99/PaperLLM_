The section discusses the ethical risks associated with AI, particularly focusing on social risks that impact individuals and communities. These risks encompass issues such as increased traffic, noise pollution, technology-induced social isolation, and rising inequality. The example of Google goggles illustrates privacy concerns stemming from the integration of technology in private spaces. 

To manage these risks, the paper highlights the importance of risk assessment and quantification, particularly for large companies, as financial estimates can significantly impact a business's valuation and reputation. Various methodologies, including expert consultations and employee surveys, are employed to gauge reputational risk.

The paper outlines several strategies for AI companies to address these risks. First, legal frameworks must evolve, as seen with the adaptation of laws for autonomous vehicles. Additionally, regulations for related industries, such as insurance for autonomous cars, may need updating to align with technological advancements. Lastly, companies are encouraged to exceed minimum legal standards, driven by ethical considerations and the need for responsible corporate citizenship, as exemplified by the public backlash against multinational companies following incidents like the Rana Plaza collapse and issues related to child labor in supply chains.
