Chapter 7 focuses on the psychological aspects of human interaction with robots and autonomous systems. It highlights the tendency for humans to anthropomorphize these machines, attributing human-like desires and feelings to them despite their lack of genuine emotional capacity. This phenomenon, known as anthropomorphism, leads to unidirectional relationships where trust can be both beneficial and harmful. 

The chapter addresses the ethical implications of this trust, noting that humans may be manipulated or persuaded by AI systems using big data. Such manipulation can result in misplaced emotional attachments or excessive trust in machines, raising concerns about potential exploitation, especially in extreme cases involving weaponized AI.

The section on the problems of anthropomorphization discusses how people interact with robots as social entities, a phenomenon referred to as the "Media Equation." This interaction often involves applying social norms and values, leading to the formation of friendships or even intimate relationships with machines. The chapter suggests that this inclination may be hard-wired in humans, possibly rooted in evolutionary factors. Even without intentional social design by engineers, users may still perceive social signals from robots, reflecting the human mind's natural predisposition to recognize and interpret these cues.
