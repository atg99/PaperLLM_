The conclusion emphasizes that autonomous weapon systems (AWS) cannot be lawfully employed for acts such as genocide or civilian massacres, as these actions are already prohibited by humanitarian law. It underscores the necessity for AWS to be utilized in compliance with existing international laws, while acknowledging the evolving nature of these systems and the potential ethical dilemmas they may introduce. Commanders bear responsibility for the actions of both their soldiers and AWS. The discussion prompts questions regarding the justification of AWS in military conflicts, the conditions for their use, and the need for new international humanitarian law (IHL) specific to AWS. Further readings provide additional insights into the governance and implications of autonomous weapons in warfare.
