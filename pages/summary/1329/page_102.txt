The paper outlines the definitions of autonomy in AI and robotics, emphasizing that while robots can operate independently for extended periods, they typically lack the autonomy to set their own goals. The discussion highlights the current limitations of autonomous weapons systems (AWS) in terms of critical functions like targeting. Although some weapons can select and engage targets without human intervention, the paper notes that no existing AWS can independently define what classes of objects to engage.

The MIM-104 Patriot system serves as a case study, illustrating the balance between autonomy and human oversight in military applications. It can autonomously select targets but requires human confirmation to launch an attack. This example underscores the ongoing debate about the role of human operators in lethal decision-making processes and the implications for military ethics and accountability.
