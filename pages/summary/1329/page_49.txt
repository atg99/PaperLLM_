Chapter 5 of the paper addresses the complexities of responsibility and liability when accidents occur involving AI systems or robots. The core issue is determining who is accountable when damage results from the operation of these technologies. The chapter emphasizes that liability concerns typically arise only after an incident occurs, such as an accident or harm, prompting the need to establish who is responsible for damages or compensation.

The discussion includes specific cases, notably an autonomous vehicle crash and mistargeting by an autonomous weapon, to illustrate the intricacies of assigning blame in these situations. It highlights that the concept of liability is not a concern until something goes awry, as people generally do not seek accountability when technology functions as intended. The chapter suggests that the challenges of assigning responsibility are compounded by the systemic nature of potential problems associated with AI technologies.

In the example of an autonomous vehicle, the chapter references existing guidelines that categorize various levels of automated driving, indicating that understanding the specific level of autonomy is crucial to analyzing liability in such cases. Overall, the chapter underscores the significance of addressing responsibility in the context of advanced technologies, particularly when they cause harm.
