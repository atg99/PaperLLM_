The section discusses the ethical principle of Non-maleﬁcence in the context of trustworthy and fair AI, emphasizing that AI systems must not cause harm to individuals, animals, or property. This principle is particularly relevant in addressing issues such as bullying and hate speech, which have escalated with the rise of digital technologies.

Social media bullying is highlighted as a critical concern, with numerous cases of bullying in schools and workplaces leading to severe consequences, including suicide. The expansion of social networks has increased the visibility and impact of such bullying, prompting several countries to enact laws against digital bullying, starting with Sweden in 1993.

Hate speech is another significant issue, defined as speech that targets individuals or groups based on characteristics such as ethnicity, sexual orientation, or religion. The International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights prohibits incitement to discrimination or violence, leading to the establishment of hate speech laws in various countries. Notable examples include Belgium's 1981 law against incitement to hatred and Germany's 2017 legislation that criminalizes hate speech on social media, imposing hefty fines on platforms that fail to remove such content promptly. This law has sparked debate regarding its implications for freedom of speech and its enforcement.
