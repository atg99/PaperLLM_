Chapter 4 addresses the significance of trust and fairness in the acceptance of AI systems. It connects these concepts to five guiding principles: Non-maleficence, Beneficence, Autonomy, Justice, and Explicability. Trust is framed within various human contexts, where vulnerability to harm is a common theme. Examples include concerns about data privacy with companies like Facebook and Google, potential misuse of health data by governments, or personal safety with caregivers. 

The chapter emphasizes that trust inherently involves uncertainty, as vulnerability is lessened when outcomes are known. Lee and See define trust as the attitude that an agent will assist in achieving an individual's goals amidst uncertainty and vulnerability. In the realm of AI and robotics, trust dynamics shift. Unlike human interactions, where the trustor is aware of their vulnerability to another's actions, trusting an AI system raises questions about whether the AI is making autonomous decisions or simply executing preprogrammed responses. Users may lack relevant experiences with AI, making it challenging to form trust based on past interactions. Overall, the chapter highlights the complexities of establishing trust in AI systems and the importance of fairness in fostering acceptance.
