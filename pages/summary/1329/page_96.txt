The paper discusses the ethical considerations surrounding autonomous vehicles, particularly in scenarios similar to trolley problems. It highlights that while these vehicles are designed to avoid obstacles without identifying them, they can recognize the number of passengers through existing safety systems. Additionally, it notes the potential for car-to-car communication to negotiate driving behavior during imminent crashes, allowing for prioritization based on passenger count. However, the decision on whether such a system is desirable rests with society.

The paper emphasizes that the German legal framework does not permit the offsetting of individual victims against each other in accident scenarios, referencing the 2006 ruling by the German Federal Constitutional Court against the Aviation Security Act. This Act would have allowed for the sacrifice of individuals in hijacked planes for the greater good, contrasting with the anonymity of victims in autonomous vehicle scenarios where individual identities are not predetermined.
