The paper discusses ethical questions surrounding autonomous vehicles (AVs), particularly focusing on accountability, liability, and unavoidable accidents. It highlights a past scandal involving Nestle, emphasizing the need for companies to clearly outline responsibilities for users of AV technology. 

A key issue is the question of accountability in cases of accidents involving AVs. According to the 1968 Vienna Convention, the driver is typically responsible; however, this concept becomes problematic when an autonomous system is in control. The German Ethics Code suggests that liability should shift to the manufacturer and software developers, indicating a need for product liability frameworks and monitoring systems akin to flight recorders in aviation.

The paper also delves into ethical dilemmas similar to the "trolley problem," where AVs may face situations requiring them to choose between two harmful outcomes. It raises questions about how AVs should make decisions in unavoidable accidents, such as whether to swerve to avoid hitting multiple people at the expense of one. The discussion highlights the complexity of programming AVs to minimize overall harm without bias based on personal characteristics, which is prohibited by the German Ethics Code. The paper concludes that current AVs do not engage in such ethical dilemmas, but the potential for these scenarios necessitates careful consideration in their development.
