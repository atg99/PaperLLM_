The principle of justice in AI emphasizes the need for systems to operate in an unbiased and fair manner. This concept is often symbolized by the Roman Goddess Justitia, who is depicted with a sword, scales, and a blindfold, representing impartiality, the weighing of evidence, and punishment, respectively. However, defining justice for AI poses significant challenges due to the contested nature of moral theory. Historical debates among various ethical schools, as noted by Mill and supported by Bourget and Chalmers' polling, reveal that there is no consensus on ethical frameworks, with no single theory commanding majority support. This lack of agreement complicates the task of programming machines to understand and apply concepts of justice, as humans possess inherent moral intuition developed over time, which machines lack.
