Chapter 8 of the paper addresses the privacy issues arising from the use of artificial intelligence in data collection, storage, and analysis. It highlights the pervasive nature of surveillance both in private and public settings, underscoring the ethical concerns associated with extensive data gathering, which, while problematic, enhances AI performance.

Section 8.1 defines privacy as the right to not be observed, explaining that individuals engage in various behaviors to protect their privacy, such as closing doors or choosing specific clothing. The chapter emphasizes the intrinsic value of privacy, which facilitates non-coerced decision-making, strategic social interactions, and actions that may diverge from societal norms.

The relationship between individual privacy and government is described as contentious, with individuals advocating for privacy rights while governments seek the ability to monitor citizens. Governments often justify privacy intrusions by citing the need for effective law enforcement, claiming that the argument for privacy can be misused to facilitate criminal activities. In certain situations, like prisons, governments argue that there is little to no expectation of privacy.
