The section discusses ethical principles essential for building trustworthy and fair artificial intelligence (AI) systems. It highlights the importance of transparency, where systems must keep logs akin to flight recorders to provide explanations for their actions. Such transparency aids in investigating failures and assigning accountability.

The conclusion emphasizes that user trust in AI systems hinges on several factors: users must believe that the systems will act benevolently towards them, avoid causing harm, and respect their autonomy. Trust is further reinforced when systems can demonstrate fairness and justice within their operational framework, although the current lack of consensus on moral theories poses challenges. The paper asserts that for AI to gain user trust, it must be explicable, ensuring intelligibility and accountability through clear documentation of its decision-making processes.

Discussion questions prompt readers to reflect on personal risk tolerance with AI, explore practical implementations of algorithmic explicability, and consider the limitations of expecting AI to be more fair and unbiased than humans.
