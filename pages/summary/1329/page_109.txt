The paper discusses the strategic challenges of ethics in artificial intelligence (AI) and robotics, focusing on initiatives from Finland and China, as well as the European Union's actions. By mid-2018, the European Commission encouraged member states to develop AI strategies, which were formally presented in December 2018, outlining actions related to research, development, investment, education, and infrastructure in AI.

Global AI strategies typically address key stakeholders such as professionals, society, research organizations, companies, and government. These entities require support in various forms, including infrastructure, data, finance, and information, to foster an environment that promotes effective AI deployment. Government strategies emphasize cooperation, research excellence, training, and regulation, while also highlighting the importance of societal discourse and ethical considerations.

Despite the potential benefits of AI, public discourse often centers on its risks, particularly concerning job displacement, human autonomy, and privacy. Dystopian narratives regarding AI's impact on the workforce are prevalent, with predictions of significant job losses across sectors traditionally thought to be safe from automation. Additionally, there are concerns about AI systems undermining privacy and autonomy, as articulated in various journalistic accounts.

This apprehension about AI's societal implications has influenced policymakers globally, prompting discussions about AI's potential harms. For instance, Germany's AI strategy calls for extensive societal dialogue on these impacts, and France's strategy aims to assess labor market changes and implement ethics-by-design practices. Central themes in these dialogues include the effects of AI on the workforce, privacy, security, safety, and the necessity for effective regulation.
