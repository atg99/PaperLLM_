The paper explores the concept of artificial intelligence (AI) through a practical example involving a digital assistant like Siri. It discusses different ethical frameworks Siri might employ when advising a user, such as a deontological approach, which emphasizes duty and truthfulness, and a virtue ethics approach, focusing on character traits like honesty. The paper highlights the complexities involved in seemingly simple questions, revealing that machines must not only process sensory data but also understand and represent meaning, engage in logical reasoning, and adapt to new information to function autonomously.

The concept of autonomy is examined, noting that its definition varies across contexts, from machines operating without human intervention to human capacity for self-determination in ethical decisions. The text posits that autonomy in AI can be seen as self-rule, a principle that underlies various interpretations of autonomy. The discussion sets the stage for a deeper analysis of artificial intelligence, emphasizing that the field has evolved significantly and that the definition of AI is fluid, often humorously described as encompassing tasks that current computers cannot perform. This introduction prepares the reader for a more detailed exploration of the characteristics that define artificial intelligence and agency in subsequent sections.
