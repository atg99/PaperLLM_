The paper discusses the importance of business ethics for AI companies, emphasizing the need for ethical considerations and Corporate Social Responsibility initiatives in their operations. It argues that adopting ethical responsibilities can provide competitive advantages, such as increased profits, enhanced innovation, improved market positioning, better risk management, and higher customer satisfaction. However, it also acknowledges that certain sectors, like banking, may face challenges in implementing these responsibilities due to stringent regulations. 

The paper highlights the necessity for specific ethical guidelines for AI, as AI technologies can significantly impact decision-making processes. These guidelines should emerge from societal discussions and address pressing ethical issues, even if they do not provide comprehensive solutions.

Additionally, the paper addresses the risks of AI to employment, particularly the fear of mass technological unemployment. While some predictions suggest that automation could lead to significant job losses, these forecasts, such as those by Frey and Osborne, have been criticized for being overly pessimistic. Alternative analyses, like one from the OECD, suggest a much lower risk of job displacement, estimating that only about 9% of jobs are vulnerable to automation, countering the more alarming predictions.
