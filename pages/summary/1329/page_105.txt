The paper discusses the regulations governing Autonomous Weapons Systems (AWS) and presents ethical arguments regarding their use in military contexts. It emphasizes that AWS must comply with International Humanitarian Law (IHL) and highlights the need for meaningful human control. This includes the ability to distinguish between combatants and non-combatants, calculate proportionality, and adhere to command responsibility principles.

The paper also explores arguments for and against the use of artificial intelligence (AI) in warfare. Proponents argue that IHL permits harm under the doctrine of military necessity and that just war theory provides a framework for assessing the morality of war. This theory includes criteria for justifying war (jus ad bellum) and conducting it (jus in bello), aiming to protect the rights of war victims and minimize civilian harm.

Supporters of AI in warfare argue that AWS could potentially reduce civilian casualties by eliminating emotional responses that lead to atrocities. AI systems could be designed to follow rules of engagement strictly, potentially deterring human soldiers from committing war crimes. The paper concludes that if AI can indeed prevent atrocities and minimize civilian harm, military leaders may have an ethical obligation to utilize such technology in warfare.
