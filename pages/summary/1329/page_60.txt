The paper discusses the competitive landscape of the AI business, particularly highlighting DuckDuckGo's strategy of not collecting user data to differentiate itself from Google. This approach raises questions about whether DuckDuckGo can challenge Google's dominance in search and targeted advertising, emphasizing the significant value of personal data in AI.

Furthermore, the text explores the concept of business ethics within AI companies, framing it as a form of risk management. The example of the Malampaya pipeline project by Shell in the Philippines illustrates the complexities of ethical decision-making. Shell had three options for constructing the pipeline, with the cheapest option jeopardizing local biodiversity. Instead, Shell opted for a more expensive route that avoided a holy territory of indigenous people, despite having no legal obligation to do so. This decision was likely influenced by previous backlash from the Brent Spar case, where Shell faced severe public criticism and consumer boycotts.

Interestingly, independent calculations later indicated that the chosen route was more economical when accounting for potential delays and legal challenges from indigenous groups. This case exemplifies how ethical considerations can align with economic interests, suggesting that ethical business practices can ultimately benefit companies financially.
