The paper discusses the concept of artificial intelligence (AI) and distinguishes between weak and strong AI. Weak AI refers to systems designed to perform specific tasks without genuine understanding or consciousness, while strong AI encompasses machines that possess human-like cognitive abilities and understanding. Examples from science fiction illustrate these concepts, with weak AI represented by characters like HAL 9000 from "2001: A Space Odyssey," and strong AI exemplified by Data from "Star Trek: The Next Generation."

The text also addresses the issue of societal biases in supervised machine learning classifiers. These biases can originate from skewed training data reflecting societal prejudices, leading to unfair outcomes. Removing biases may enhance fairness but could potentially compromise the classifier's overall performance, creating a dilemma for stakeholders who must navigate the tradeoff between achieving unbiased results and maintaining accuracy.

Furthermore, the sense-plan-act paradigm is examined, highlighting how errors in one step can adversely affect subsequent steps. This interdependence can lead to degraded robot performance over time, suggesting a need for careful monitoring and adjustment.

For further reading, the paper references works by Russell and Norvig on AI approaches, Michalski et al. on machine learning, and Perkowitz on digital humanity. The chapter is open access under a Creative Commons license, promoting sharing and adaptation with appropriate attribution.
