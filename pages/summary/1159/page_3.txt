The paper discusses experiments conducted to model heat transfer rates in heat exchangers using machine learning techniques, specifically Support Vector Machines (SVM). The experiments varied the Reynolds number on the shell side from 300 to 7000 and on the tube side from 3000 to 4000, while the heat transfer rate was adjusted between 20 kW and 50 kW. A total of 39 datasets were collected for training the proposed machine learning model. 

The training data, outlined in Table 1, includes various geometric parameters and Reynolds numbers for three different heat exchanger setups (HX1, HX2, HX3). Table 2 presents test data for predicting heat transfer rates, categorized for mathematical modeling, Artificial Neural Networks (ANN), and SVM.

The SVM algorithm is highlighted as an effective method for classification and regression of unknown data. It operates in two phases: training, where a model is created from known data, and testing, where unknown data is classified based on the trained model. The SVM model utilizes four inputs (Nb, Sb, Nt, Dc) to predict one output (heat transfer rate, Reo).

The performance of the SVM model is evaluated using data from shell and tube exchangers and is implemented in MATLAB. The results are compared against a conventional ANN with back-propagation training, using metrics such as confusion matrix, precision, recall, and accuracy to assess effectiveness. The findings are illustrated in a performance comparison chart.
