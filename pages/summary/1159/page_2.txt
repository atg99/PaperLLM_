This paper discusses various types of heat exchangers, including shell and tube, double pipe, plate type, condensers, evaporators, and boilers. Shell and tube heat exchangers are widely used and consist of a cylindrical pressure vessel with tubes allowing two fluids to exchange heat. Double pipe heat exchangers are a simpler variant where heat transfers between two fluids in concentric tubes. Plate type heat exchangers utilize corrugated plates to create channels for fluid flow, facilitating heat transfer.

Condensers, evaporators, and boilers are essential in domestic and automotive applications, primarily using a two-phase heat transfer mechanism where heat transfer occurs during phase changes of fluids. While heat exchangers have extensive applications, their design can be complex and costly, necessitating thorough performance analysis before manufacturing. The field of manufacturing simulation has emerged to computationally evaluate mechanical components, with heat exchanger simulation being particularly challenging.

The paper proposes a novel machine learning approach for predicting the heat transfer rate in shell and tube heat exchangers, building on previous work by Wang et al., who utilized artificial neural networks (ANN). However, the ANN's performance was limited due to reliance on standard backpropagation algorithms. This study introduces a support vector machine (SVM) method to enhance prediction accuracy by minimizing errors. The proposed technique is validated using experimental data from prior research, which involved two heat exchangers, HX1 and HX2, and considers various geometric parameters such as the number of tubes, baffle pitch, and baffle count.
