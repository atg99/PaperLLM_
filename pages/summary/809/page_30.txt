The paper presents a summary of commonly used pruning evaluation metrics across various applications in machine learning, specifically focusing on tasks such as image classification, object detection, generative tasks, adversarial robustness, image denoising, human-pose estimation, natural language processing, speech recognition, image-text retrieval, and visual question answering. 

For image classification, datasets like CIFAR-10 and ImageNet are evaluated using models such as ResNet and VGG, with metrics including accuracy and computational efficiency measured by FLOPs and MACs. In object detection, metrics like mean Average Precision (mAP) are highlighted for datasets like COCO and PASCAL VOC, with models like Faster R-CNN and SSD.

Generative tasks are assessed with metrics such as the Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) and Structural Similarity Index (SSIM) using models like DDPMs and LDMs on datasets like CIFAR-10 and CelebA-HQ. For adversarial robustness, performance is evaluated through accuracy on datasets like CIFAR-10 and ImageNet using models such as VGG and ResNet.

In the domain of natural language processing, various tasks are covered including translation and understanding, with metrics such as BLEU and accuracy being utilized for models like Transformers and BERTs. Speech recognition metrics include Word Error Rate (WER) and Character Error Rate (CER) across datasets like Common Voice and LibriSpeech.

The summary also notes the use of metrics like TR@1 and IR@1 for image-text retrieval tasks and accuracy for visual question answering tasks, emphasizing the importance of both performance and efficiency metrics across diverse applications.
