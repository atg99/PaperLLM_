The paper provides a comprehensive overview of various pruning methods in neural networks, specifically focusing on techniques employed before and during training. It categorizes the methods based on criteria such as whether they are unstructured or structured, data-driven or not, and whether they can be performed in a single shot. Notable methods discussed include Single-shot Network Pruning (SNIP), Gradient Signal Preservation (GraSP), and Iterative Synaptic Flow Pruning (SynFlow), each with unique approaches to identifying which weights can be pruned with minimal impact on performance. 

The findings emphasize the significance of initialization and layer-wise sparsity in effective pruning. Additionally, the paper explores Pruning During Training (PDT) methods, which involve the dynamic updating of weights and masks during the training process, leading to the formation of subnetworks without requiring retraining from scratch. The authors categorize PDT methods into four paradigms: sparsity regularization based, dynamic sparse training based, score-based, and differentiable pruning based, noting that they are less explored due to their complexity compared to Pre-training Pruning (PBT) methods.

Overall, the research highlights the ongoing evolution of pruning techniques and their implications for optimizing neural network architectures, particularly within the context of convolutional neural networks (CNNs).
