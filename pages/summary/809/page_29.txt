The paper presents a comprehensive summary of various pruning methods for large language models (LLMs), particularly focusing on their performance metrics across different datasets. Key metrics include the reduction ratio of parameters, prediction accuracy, and common sense reasoning capabilities. The methods are categorized based on their approach: dense or sparse, one-shot or local, and whether they involve post-training or pre-training.

For instance, methods like SparseGPT and Wanda demonstrate varying degrees of effectiveness in reducing model size while maintaining performance, with SparseGPT achieving significant parameter reduction (up to 70%) on the LLaMA-7B model, although this comes with a trade-off in some predictive tasks. In contrast, LLM-Pruner shows a balanced approach, yielding competitive results across multiple datasets for the Vicuna-7B model with lower parameter ratios.

The paper also highlights the importance of model selection and the specific conditions under which these pruning techniques were tested, indicating that results can vary significantly based on the model architecture and the datasets used. Overall, the study underscores the ongoing advancements in LLM pruning methods, emphasizing the need for further research to optimize performance while minimizing computational costs.
