This paper provides an overview of various methods for pruning neural networks after training, highlighting significant techniques and their underlying principles. Pruning is categorized into unstructured and structured approaches, with several representative methods listed in Table 4. Notable methods include Auto-Balance, Lottery Ticket Hypothesis (LTH), and differentiable pruning techniques, each employing different scoring criteria to determine the importance of weights or filters.

The paper emphasizes the popularity of Pruning After Training (PAT), which typically follows a Pretrain-Prune-Retrain process. This involves initially training a dense network, followed by pruning less significant weights, and then fine-tuning the pruned network. The LTH is a key concept discussed, proposing that a sparse subnetwork can be effectively retrained from scratch rather than relying solely on pre-trained weights. Variants of LTH have emerged, exploring its robustness across various neural network architectures and its transferability between datasets.

The paper also outlines advancements in differentiable pruning methods, which allow for more flexible and efficient pruning strategies by enabling gradient-based optimization. These developments contribute to a deeper understanding of network sensitivity and enhance the effectiveness of pruning techniques. Overall, the paper illustrates the evolving landscape of neural network pruning and its implications for model efficiency and performance.
