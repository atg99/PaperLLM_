The paper discusses future directions for neural network pruning, outlining four key areas: theories, techniques, applications, and evaluation. 

In the realm of theories, it highlights unresolved questions regarding the theoretical limits of pruning without impacting performance, as well as the need for explainability in pruning methods to better understand their impact on model structure and weights. 

For techniques, the integration of Automated Machine Learning (AutoML) and Neural Architecture Search (NAS) into pruning is emphasized, along with the importance of energy-aware pruning and hardware deployment. 

Applications of pruning are expanding beyond traditional areas, with increasing interest in complex tasks such as visual question answering and natural language understanding, particularly in the context of large foundation models like GPT-4, which require efficient pruning methods to enhance their usability.

The evaluation section points out the necessity for standardized benchmarks and metrics to assess the effectiveness of various pruning methods, as current disparities in techniques and settings complicate fair comparisons.

The conclusion reiterates the significance of pruning as a compression technique, summarizing the reviewed topics, including different pruning strategies and their combinations with other techniques. It also emphasizes the importance of providing resources for researchers and practitioners to stay updated on pruning advancements. 

Appendix A clarifies key terms and notations used in the paper, including definitions for prune ratio, compression ratio, sparsity ratio, speedup ratio, and one-shot pruning, noting that these terms may vary in definition across different studies.
