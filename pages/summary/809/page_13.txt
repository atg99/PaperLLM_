It highlights the differences between pruning methods—global and local—with global pruning providing a more adaptable approach but facing challenges due to variability in layer magnitudes. The authors note that while local pruning is predominant, some emerging global methods aim to address its limitations.

The paper also examines the debate on training strategies post-pruning: whether to train from scratch or fine-tune the pruned network. Evidence suggests that fine-tuning often yields better performance than training from scratch, particularly in sparse mobile networks. The authors present findings that support the use of fine-tuning for better accuracy recovery after pruning.

Additionally, the paper introduces the concept of transfer pruning, which improves accuracy by utilizing knowledge from a source task to enhance performance on a target task. It differentiates between original task pruning and transfer pruning, with the latter showing promise in transferring subnetworks across different datasets and architectures.

Overall, the study emphasizes the importance of pre-trained weights, the effectiveness of fine-tuning, and the potential of transfer learning techniques in optimizing the pruning process for improved model performance.
