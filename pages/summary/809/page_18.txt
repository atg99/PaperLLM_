This paper presents a detailed examination of various pruning methods for neural networks, focusing on comparisons between unstructured and structured pruning, one-shot versus iterative pruning, data-free versus data-driven pruning, and the effects of initialized versus pre-trained weights.

In the experiments, SparseGPT's iterative pruning utilized a blocksize of 128, while DeiT-Tiny was pre-trained on ImageNet before being retrained on CIFAR-10. Additionally, ResNet-152 models were pruned using GraSP, with results indicating that unstructured pruning generally outperforms structured pruning across different models and prune ratios. Specifically, unstructured methods achieved higher prune ratios and better performance metrics, as shown by the comparative accuracy losses at equivalent prune ratios.

The paper also highlights the advantages and disadvantages of each pruning type. Unstructured pruning allows for high sparsity and minimal accuracy loss but lacks support from specific hardware or software. In contrast, structured pruning is less effective in achieving high sparsity but can leverage hardware optimizations.

In comparing one-shot and iterative pruning, results indicated that iterative methods typically yielded lower accuracy losses at high prune ratios. Furthermore, data-free pruning methods showed promising results, outperforming data-driven approaches in some cases, particularly in pruning scenarios that do not rely heavily on data for performance maintenance.

Lastly, the impact of using initialized versus pre-trained weights in pruning was analyzed, with findings suggesting that pre-trained models may offer better performance outcomes.

Overall, this section provides extensive comparative results that illustrate the effectiveness of various pruning strategies, contributing valuable insights into optimizing neural network performance while reducing model size.
