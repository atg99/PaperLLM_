This paper discusses various pruning methods for neural networks, focusing on their effectiveness based on initialization, weight pre-training, and application to different tasks. Key findings include the observation that pruning pre-trained weights does not always enhance Top-1 accuracy, as evidenced by the performance of methods like SynFlow and SNIP on models such as VGG-16 and ResNet-32. The analysis shows that global pruning methods generally outperform local ones, with Bonsai notably achieving significantly better results than LLM-pruner and LoRAPruner at a similar pruning ratio for LLaMA-7B.

The paper also contrasts the performance restoration techniques of fine-tuning versus training from scratch after pruning. Results indicate that fine-tuning typically incurs a smaller accuracy loss compared to training from scratch, with specific losses detailed for ResNet-50.

In addition, the paper categorizes pruning applications into image classification, object detection, and image style translation. It highlights that image classification is the most common domain for pruning research, primarily using ImageNet for evaluations. Object detection poses greater challenges due to its complex architectures and larger input sizes, while image style translation involves unique considerations for GANs, such as the need for less compact discriminator networks.

Overall, the paper underscores the importance of network initialization, pruning methodology, and the specific application context in determining the effectiveness of pruning strategies.
