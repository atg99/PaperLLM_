The paper discusses advanced techniques for pruning neural networks, focusing on methods that assess the importance of channels and weights to enhance model efficiency. It begins by presenting a reformulation of Batch Normalization (BN) that incorporates a scale factor γ and a shift term β, which is treated as channel saliency. This approach allows for the pruning of unimportant channels based on the distribution of γ.

The paper then delves into measuring the importance of weights through loss change criteria, employing first-order and second-order Taylor expansions to estimate the impact of removing specific weights or filters. The first-order approximation indicates that the change in loss can be represented by the gradient with respect to the weights, while the second-order expansion includes both gradient and Hessian terms for a more nuanced evaluation of weight significance.

The section on learning to prune introduces various methodologies, including sparsity regularization, meta-learning, and graph neural networks. Sparsity regularization involves jointly training network weights and scaling factors to promote sparsity, while meta-learning approaches, like PruningNet, predict suitable weights for pruned networks based on network encoding vectors. Graph neural network-based pruning treats the neural network as a graph, utilizing aggregators to compute weights for the pruned model.

Overall, the paper provides a comprehensive overview of contemporary strategies for network pruning, emphasizing the importance of evaluating weight contributions to loss and leveraging advanced learning techniques for effective model compression.
