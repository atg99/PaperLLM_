The paper discusses deep neural network pruning, focusing on three primary aspects: speedup dependency on hardware/software, the timing of pruning in relation to training, and the criteria for pruning weights. 

Pruning methods are classified into three categories based on speedup: unstructured, semi-structured, and structured. Unstructured pruning, also known as weight-wise pruning, is the most granular technique and involves optimizing the weights of a neural network while adhering to constraints on the number of non-zero weights. This method typically requires special hardware and software to achieve acceleration, thus it is categorized as a specific speedup technique. 

The paper outlines various pruning strategies, including when to prune (before, during, or after training) and the use of specific criteria or learning methods for determining which weights to prune. It emphasizes that these factors influence the characteristics of pruning algorithms, leading to a taxonomy of methods presented in subsequent sections. 

Overall, the paper provides a comprehensive overview of the mechanisms and considerations involved in deep neural network pruning, highlighting its significance for optimizing model performance while maintaining computational efficiency.
