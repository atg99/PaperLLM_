The paper discusses various methods for pruning neural networks during training, categorized into three main approaches: sparsity regularization, dynamic sparse training, and score-based methods. 

1. **Sparsity Regularization Methods**: These methods start with dense networks and incorporate sparse constraints within the loss function. Techniques such as Structured Sparsity Learning (SSL) and MorphNet are highlighted. SSL uses group LASSO regularization but requires complex gradient computations. MorphNet applies sparsity regularization to Batch Normalization parameters, although some networks may lack BN layers. Alternatives like Sparse Structure Selection (SSS) utilize scaling factors for CNN micro-structures to induce sparsity without requiring additional fine-tuning.

2. **Dynamic Sparse Training Methods**: This approach employs a randomly initialized sparse network and iteratively prunes and regrows weights. Methods such as Sparse Evolutionary Training (SET) and Dynamic Sparse Reparameterization (DSR) are discussed. SET focuses on removing the smallest weights while DSR adapts pruning thresholds and reallocates weights across layers. FreeTickets and Gradual Pruning with zero-cost Neuroregeneration (GraNet) further enhance this process by leveraging weight magnitudes and gradients to inform pruning decisions.

3. **Score-based Methods**: These techniques use scoring criteria to determine the importance of weights. Soft Filter Pruning (SFP) is presented as a method that can simultaneously train and prune networks by utilizing the l2 norm of filters as a measure of importance, allowing for dynamic adjustments of pruned weights.

Overall, the paper illustrates the diversity in pruning strategies, emphasizing the trade-offs in complexity, effectiveness, and the impact on model performance during training.
