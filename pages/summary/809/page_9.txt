This paper provides an overview of various pruning techniques for large language models (LLMs), highlighting methods for both static and dynamic pruning. The authors discuss several approaches, including SparseGPT, which focuses on minimizing loss without retraining, and Wanda, which employs weight magnitudes and input norms for unstructured post-training pruning. SliceGPT is noted for its structured pruning using orthogonal transformations and principal component analysis.

Dynamic pruning methods are also explored, which adaptively prune neural networks based on individual input characteristics. For instance, the Runtime Network Routing framework by Rao et al. enables dynamic routing tailored to specific inputs, while other methods such as CGNets and Feature Boosting and Suppression focus on identifying and skipping unimportant features at runtime.

The paper categorizes pruning criteria into several types, including magnitude-based pruning, which selects weights with the smallest absolute values due to their presumed lesser impact on network output. It details the use of lp norms to assess weight importance and discusses sensitivity and saliency measures that evaluate the potential loss change from pruning specific weights.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the significance of these pruning techniques in optimizing LLMs, enhancing their efficiency without compromising performance.
