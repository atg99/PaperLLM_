This paper discusses various pruning methods in deep learning, focusing on their applications across different domains such as computer vision, natural language processing, and audio processing. Key concepts include the evaluation of pruning results through metrics like Top-1 accuracy and FID scores, as well as the challenges of preserving adversarial robustness in sparse models. The authors highlight the importance of empirical and verifiable robust accuracy metrics when assessing the performance of pruned networks.

In the realm of computer vision, the paper reviews pruning techniques applied to tasks like semantic segmentation and image denoising, noting specific studies that target models such as CMU-pose and DnCNN. For natural language processing, it outlines early pruning methods for LSTMs and recent advancements involving transformer models, emphasizing metrics such as BLEU scores and perplexity for evaluating performance.

The paper also covers vision-and-language tasks, where pruning has been explored within multimodal research, particularly in Visual Question Answering and image-text retrieval. Finally, it addresses audio and speech processing, detailing efforts to prune models like Deep Speech 2 and wav2vec 2.0, with Word Error Rate as a standard accuracy metric.

Overall, the survey comprehensively categorizes the landscape of pruning literature, highlighting the diversity of applications and the importance of maintaining model performance post-pruning.
