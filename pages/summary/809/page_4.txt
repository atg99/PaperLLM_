The paper discusses various pruning techniques for neural networks, focusing on structured and semi-structured pruning methods. Structured pruning aims to minimize performance degradation while maximizing speed improvements by removing entire filters, channels, or attention heads from a neural network. This approach allows for model compression without the need for specialized hardware, thereby enhancing both inference speed and model size reduction.

Semi-structured pruning, which is introduced to enhance the flexibility of structured pruning, seeks to maintain higher accuracy levels even at greater pruning rates. This method, also referred to as pattern-based pruning, involves strategies such as treating filters as stripes and applying sparsity patterns like 2:4 or 4:8 to optimize parameter reduction in large language models. It can take advantage of specific GPU architectures to accelerate computations.

The paper also differentiates between static pruning and run-time pruning, outlining three pipelines for static pruning. One of these methods, Pruning Before Training (PBT), involves pruning a network with randomly initialized weights before training, which helps to eliminate pre-training costs. This method aims to enhance training and inference efficiency by training the pruned network to converge to a desired performance level without the need for extensive pre-training.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the significance of pruning techniques in optimizing neural networks for improved performance and efficiency.
