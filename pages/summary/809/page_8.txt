The paper discusses various approaches to pruning techniques in neural networks, particularly focusing on the Lottery Ticket Hypothesis (LTH) and its implications for model optimization. Chen et al. introduce the Graph Lottery Ticket (GLT), extending LTH to Graph Neural Networks (GNNs). Prasanna et al. investigate winning tickets in fine-tuned BERT models, identifying subnetworks that maintain performance.

The literature is divided into two perspectives: one that supports LTH's validity through theoretical frameworks like dynamical systems theory and empirical observations of improved generalization, and another that challenges its existence. Some researchers emphasize that identifying winning tickets is contingent on training settings, including learning rates and architectural features.

LTH is positioned within the context of pruning methods, specifically as a Post-Training (PAT) technique rather than a Pre-Training (PBT) method, as it relies on a pre-trained network for mask selection.

Additionally, the paper outlines various score-based methods for pruning, which include evaluating weight importance through norms and loss changes, as seen in techniques like Second-order Structured Pruning (SOSP) and LLM-Pruner. Sparsity regularization methods, such as LASSO regression and energy consumption modeling, are also highlighted.

Pruning strategies in early training are explored, where methods like Early-Bird tickets demonstrate that winning tickets can be identified with minimal training. Post-training pruning methods are discussed as a cost-effective alternative for large models, with techniques like structured pruning frameworks and SparseGPT achieving significant sparsity without extensive retraining.

Overall, the paper presents a comprehensive overview of pruning methodologies, emphasizing their evolution, theoretical underpinnings, and practical applications in modern neural network architectures.
