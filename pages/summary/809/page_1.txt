This paper provides a comprehensive survey of deep neural network pruning, addressing its significance due to the increasing size and resource demands of modern models, particularly large language models. With over three thousand pruning papers published between 2020 and 2024, the authors identify a gap in up-to-date reviews, which they aim to fill through a structured taxonomy that includes universal versus specific speedup, timing of pruning, methods of pruning, and integration with other compression techniques.

The authors conduct a comparative analysis of various pruning settings, such as unstructured versus structured pruning and one-shot versus iterative approaches, while also highlighting emerging topics like pruning for large language models and vision transformers. They explore the implications of pruning on model robustness and efficiency, particularly in resource-constrained environments.

Furthermore, the paper provides recommendations for selecting pruning methods and outlines future research directions. To support ongoing research, the authors have created a curated repository that includes datasets, networks, and evaluations related to neural network pruning. This repository, which will be regularly updated, aims to serve as a valuable resource for scholars in the field. The paper concludes by emphasizing the importance of pruning in enhancing the practicality of deep neural networks across various applications, particularly in edge computing scenarios.
