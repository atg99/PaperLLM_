This paper provides a comprehensive overview of various pruning methods used in object detection, image classification, and natural language processing, specifically focusing on architectures like Faster R-CNN, MobileNet, Vision Transformers (ViTs), and BERT. Key metrics such as the original mean Average Precision (O-mAP) and pruned mean Average Precision (P-mAP) are compared across methods, along with model ratios and FLOPs.

For object detection, pruning methods such as LTH, CP, and GFP are evaluated using datasets like PASCAL VOC and COCO. The results highlight the trade-offs between model efficiency and accuracy, with some methods achieving minimal drops in performance even with significant reductions in model complexity.

In the context of ViTs for image classification, methods like SViTE and SAViT demonstrate varying effectiveness in maintaining accuracy while reducing computational costs. The results indicate that certain pruning strategies can yield comparable performance to the original models while significantly lowering FLOPs.

For BERT models, the paper discusses pruning methods like LadaBERT and CAP-m, showcasing their effectiveness across multiple natural language processing tasks. The findings reveal that even with aggressive pruning, models can retain high performance levels, particularly in tasks such as sentiment analysis and question answering.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of developing efficient pruning techniques that balance model performance with reduced computational demands, thereby advancing the practicality of deploying deep learning models in resource-constrained environments.
