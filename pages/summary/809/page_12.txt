This paper examines the effectiveness of one-shot versus iterative pruning methods in neural networks, specifically focusing on their impact on accuracy and performance. The study evaluates various models, including VGG-16 and ResNet-32 on CIFAR-10, and LLaMA-7B on PTB, using both SynFlow and Magnitude-based pruning techniques. The findings indicate that iterative pruning generally yields better performance compared to one-shot pruning, which tends to suffer from layer collapse and a significant accuracy drop.

The paper also categorizes pruning methods into data-free and data-driven approaches. Most existing techniques are data-driven, but the study highlights the effectiveness of data-free methods like SynFlow and Random pruning, showing that SynFlow performs particularly well. Furthermore, it discusses the robustness of subnetworks pruned from randomly initialized weights versus those from pre-trained weights. The results suggest that subnetworks derived from randomly initialized weights maintain performance better under various conditions compared to those from pre-trained weights.

Overall, the paper contributes valuable insights into the comparative performance of pruning strategies and their dependency on data usage and model initialization, emphasizing the importance of iterative methods for maintaining accuracy in pruned models.
