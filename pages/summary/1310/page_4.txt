The paper discusses RandAugment, an automated data augmentation technique that eliminates the need for a separate search phase typically required in learned augmentation methods. This search phase complicates the training process and is computationally intensive, often yielding sub-optimal results. RandAugment aims to integrate data augmentation parameters directly into the model's hyper-parameters, significantly reducing the complexity of the parameter space.

The authors note that previous augmentation strategies involved numerous parameters to optimize, often exceeding thirty, and were based on selecting transformations from a set of fourteen available options. To simplify this, RandAugment employs a parameter-free approach that randomly selects transformations with uniform probability, allowing for a potential combination of transformations expressed as KN policies.

Furthermore, the paper highlights the importance of the distortion magnitude for each transformation, proposing a single global magnitude M for all transformations instead of individual magnitudes. Four scheduling methods for M during training were tested: constant, random, linearly increasing, and random with an increasing upper bound.

Ultimately, RandAugment is defined by just two parameters, N (the number of transformations) and M (the magnitude), which are straightforward and interpretable. The authors suggest that despite the small search space, naive grid search is an effective method for hyperparameter optimization. The efficacy of RandAugment is validated against previous learned augmentation methods throughout the paper.
