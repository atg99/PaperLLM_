In this paper, the authors focus on improving AutoAugment and related data augmentation methods by redefining the search space for augmentation operations. Their analysis indicates that the search space configuration significantly impacts the effectiveness of augmentation policies. They propose a method called RandAugment, which simplifies the search process by jointly optimizing all augmentation operations with a single distortion magnitude, while uniformly setting the probability of each operation. This reduction in complexity allows for a simple grid search to outperform more complex learned augmentation methods that depend on separate search phases.

Key contributions of the paper include:

1. **Model and Dataset Size Dependency**: The authors demonstrate that the optimal strength of data augmentation distortions varies based on the size of the model and the training dataset. This finding suggests that using a smaller proxy task for optimization may lead to sub-optimal policies.

2. **Simplified Search Space**: They analyze existing AutoAugment methods and identify the importance of the search space, leading to the introduction of a simplified search space with two interpretable hyper-parameters, eliminating the need for a separate expensive search process.

3. **Performance Improvement**: Despite the simplifications, RandAugment achieves state-of-the-art results across various datasets, including CIFAR, SVHN, and ImageNet. The method shows notable accuracy improvements over AutoAugment and baseline augmentations, indicating its effectiveness.

The authors challenge the assumption that a small proxy task is suitable for learned data augmentation, particularly regarding model and dataset sizes. Their systematic evaluation on CIFAR-10 reveals that larger models require greater data distortions for optimal regularization, highlighting the limitations of fixed distortion magnitudes derived from proxy tasks. Overall, the paper presents a compelling argument for the benefits of a simplified approach to data augmentation, emphasizing the relationship between model size, dataset size, and distortion magnitude.
