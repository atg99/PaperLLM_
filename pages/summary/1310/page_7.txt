The paper investigates the impact of various image transformations on model performance, specifically focusing on their effects when applied during training on the CIFAR-10 dataset using the Wide-ResNet architecture. Table 5 presents the average validation accuracy improvements associated with individual transformations, highlighting that rotation yields the most significant benefit, while posterization consistently detracts from performance. The analysis shows that combining all transformations generally leads to better results.

Additionally, the paper explores the potential for enhancing performance through learning the probabilities of selecting different transformations, moving beyond the equal-probability approach of RandAugment. It introduces a differentiable optimization technique based on density matching, referred to as the 1st-order method. Results in Table 6 demonstrate that this method improves accuracy for both reduced and full CIFAR-10 datasets, outperforming RandAugment and matching or exceeding AutoAugment's performance in certain cases.

The findings indicate that while learning transformation probabilities can yield better results, the computational demands of applying multiple transformations may limit scalability for larger images. The paper concludes that while promising, further exploration is warranted, particularly for larger-scale tasks. The related work section emphasizes the importance of data augmentation in deep learning, noting various techniques utilized across different datasets to enhance model generalization.
