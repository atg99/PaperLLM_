In the paper "Randaugment: Practical automated data augmentation with a reduced search space," the authors Ekin D. Cubuk, Barret Zoph, Jonathon Shlens, and Quoc V. Le from Google Research address the limitations of existing automated data augmentation strategies, particularly the computational expense associated with the separate search phase required to optimize hyperparameters. The authors propose a novel approach that simplifies the search process by focusing on a single distortion magnitude that controls all augmentation operations, rather than optimizing each operation's magnitude and probability independently.

This redesign significantly reduces the search space and eliminates the need for a proxy task, which has been a common workaround in previous methods. The authors demonstrate that their method, RandAugment, matches or exceeds the performance of previous automated augmentation techniques across various datasets, including CIFAR-10, SVHN, ImageNet, and COCO. For instance, using the EfficientNet-B7 architecture, RandAugment achieves an accuracy of 85.0% on ImageNet, outperforming baseline augmentation by 1.0% and AutoAugment by 0.6%. Additionally, RandAugment shows improvements in object detection tasks as well.

The findings suggest that the traditional reliance on proxy tasks and independent hyperparameter searches is sub-optimal, and that a unified approach can yield effective data augmentation strategies with reduced computational costs. Overall, RandAugment represents a significant advancement in the field of automated data augmentation, providing a more efficient and effective solution for enhancing the performance of vision models.
