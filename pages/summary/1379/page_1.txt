The review by Daniel S. Drew evaluates the current status of multi-robot systems in search and rescue applications, highlighting their potential and existing challenges. The paper notes a significant need for technological advancements in disaster recovery, especially as global disasters become more frequent and severe. Although robots have been utilized in disaster response since 2001, their effectiveness can be enhanced through multi-robot systems that allow for cooperative task execution.

Recent advancements include the integration of machine learning with classical control techniques, improving autonomy, decentralized coordination, and human-robot interaction. Reliable ad hoc mesh networking has emerged as a solution for communication in safety-critical scenarios. New modular and multimodal robotic platforms may also address mobility issues without elevating costs.

Despite these improvements, multi-agent systems are not yet ready for deployment in search and rescue missions. The paper emphasizes the importance of realistic evaluations of technologies and addressing challenges such as simulation-to-reality transfer, algorithmic bias in machine learning, and the balance between novelty and reliability. The review underscores the potential of multi-robot systems to enhance disaster response efforts, advocating for continued research and development in this domain.
