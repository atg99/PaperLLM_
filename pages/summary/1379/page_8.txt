The paper discusses the challenges and opportunities in the field of multi-robot search and rescue, highlighting a significant lack of funding for research in this area, particularly in the United States. It notes that most relevant programs and teams are based in the European Union. While initiatives like the DARPA SubT challenge show promise, their narrow focus on underground environments does not adequately support multi-robot systems. The high costs associated with developing and testing multiple robotic platforms necessitate more sustained funding sources, as prize-only models may hinder participation and innovation.

The paper emphasizes the difficulties in translating laboratory research into practical applications, particularly for search and rescue robots that must operate in complex environments. A misalignment exists between academic goals, which prioritize novelty, and the practical needs of operators, who focus on system reliability and robustness. Addressing this disconnect will require coordinated efforts from funding agencies and academic publishing.

The conclusion suggests that as coordination algorithms and robotic functionalities improve, multi-robot systems will become more effective in disaster response. Future research should align stakeholder values with realistic technology integration to accelerate the deployment of life-saving robotic solutions. The paper also anticipates increased autonomy in robotic systems for various phases of disaster response, including preparation and recovery.
