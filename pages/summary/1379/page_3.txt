The paper discusses the critical abilities required for robot operation, particularly in the context of multi-robot systems engaged in search and rescue missions. Key aspects include the ability to reconstruct the environment from sensory data, navigate obstacles, and make timely decisions on actions. The complexity increases with multi-robot systems, which necessitate both individual autonomy and effective information sharing among agents, as well as task assignment capabilities.

A significant focus is on simultaneous localization and mapping (SLAM), which is essential for creating operational maps for first responders. The paper highlights recent advancements in SLAM and multi-robot SLAM, emphasizing the challenges posed by unstructured and dynamic environments typical of disaster scenarios. Fully distributed multi-robot SLAM techniques may help address communication issues without requiring complete agent connectivity. Additionally, semantic segmentation-based SLAM shows potential for handling ambiguous environments.

The paper also addresses challenges in motion planning for autonomous navigation, noting that while motion planning is well-developed in structured environments like warehouses, it remains problematic in disaster areas due to dynamic obstacles and adverse conditions. Recent reviews of motion planning in both general and dynamic contexts are referenced.

Overall, the paper identifies the importance of improving human-robot interaction to enhance the autonomy and reliability of multi-robot systems in search and rescue operations, while also proposing the development of accessible robot collections for better demonstration and testing of these technologies.
