The rapid development of autonomous systems capable of functioning in unstructured and dynamic environments is highlighted, particularly in the context of UAV maneuvering through on-board computation. Multi-robot systems face the challenge of avoiding collisions during deployment, with recent advancements in formation control and collision-free trajectory planning being notable. Decentralized formation control utilizing visual information has shown promise, especially in low-light conditions with multiple quadrotors.

Task allocation remains a critical challenge for the scalability of multi-robot systems, influenced by communication range limits, state estimation inaccuracies, and control delays, particularly in search and rescue operations. The concept of cross-schedule dependencies is essential for maintaining communication during task execution, and approaches that account for uncertainty in state estimation are anticipated to be important. Additionally, heterogeneous robotic deployments, involving various platforms, necessitate task allocation strategies that consider differing capabilities.

Communication is a significant hurdle for autonomous multi-agent systems, with architectural choices between centralized and decentralized control impacting data routing. Explicit communication methods face scalability and bandwidth trade-offs, especially during disaster operations where reliable communication can be compromised. Emerging ad hoc wireless mesh networking solutions offer robust communication by dynamically reconfiguring topologies. Techniques like channel-hopping have been shown to enhance reliability, although trade-offs exist between data throughput and the number of agents in the system.

Exploring supplementary communication methods, such as line-of-sight, ultrasonic, and acoustic communication, could enhance reliability and functionality. While some methods have been demonstrated in various conditions, their effectiveness in realistic disaster scenarios remains under-researched. The integration of open-source Internet-of-Things packages with robotic platforms is suggested as a promising avenue for future development. Lastly, moving towards decentralized control is posited as a means to improve cooperative operations among multi-agent systems.
