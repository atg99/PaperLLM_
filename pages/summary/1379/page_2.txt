The paper discusses the advancements and challenges in deploying multi-robot systems for disaster response, highlighting the significant gap between current capabilities and the requirements for effective operation in real-world scenarios. Despite notable progress in academic research, field data indicates that from 2001 to the present, there have been only a limited number of instances where multiple robots were used simultaneously in disaster response, with few exhibiting autonomous operation or cooperative behavior.

The review categorizes disaster robotics, search and rescue robotics, and urban search and rescue robotics under a unified terminology, aiming to provide a comprehensive overview applicable to various disaster contexts. It emphasizes that the specific technical challenges can vary significantly based on the type of disaster, as illustrated by the case of the Fukushima meltdown, where existing communication infrastructure influenced design requirements.

Key technical challenges are identified, particularly concerning scalability, which affects system functionality as the number of agents increases. The paper outlines three critical performance metrics for multi-robot systems: reliability, autonomy, and mobility. It stresses that while enhancements in human-robot interaction can improve individual robot mobility, the unique challenges of multi-robot systems, such as one-to-many control, are more pertinent to enhancing autonomy and reliability.

Overall, the paper underscores the need to address these technical challenges to realize the full potential of multi-robot systems in search and rescue operations.
