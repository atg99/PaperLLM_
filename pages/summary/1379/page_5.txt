The paper discusses the challenges and advancements in communication and human-robot interaction (HRI) within multi-robot systems, particularly in search and rescue operations. It highlights that machine learning approaches can help reduce inter-robot network bandwidth requirements, particularly through implicit communication strategies like stigmergy, which draws inspiration from social insects. These strategies involve modifying the environment or using implicit signals from robot actions to facilitate coordination without direct communication.

HRI remains a critical barrier for robot adoption in emergency scenarios, as operators need extensive training to manage teleoperation effectively. The complexity of controlling multiple robots increases cognitive load, making it essential to explore methods that simplify this interaction. Research in human-swarm interaction aims to reduce operator effort, ideally achieving constant effort regardless of the number of robots involved.

The paper also distinguishes between proximal and remote human-robot interactions, noting that while remote control is predominant, there is growing interest in proximal teaming using gesture-based cues. Advances in HRI are closely linked to improvements in robot autonomy, with a focus on shared or sliding autonomy that allows for flexible control based on situational context. This approach aims to minimize operator burden while enhancing operational effectiveness.

Lastly, the paper points out the trade-offs between cost, functionality, and agent count in multi-robot systems, emphasizing the need for balancing these factors within a constrained budget to achieve optimal performance in disaster response scenarios.
