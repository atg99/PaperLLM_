The paper addresses the critical issue of internet security, particularly concerning the Internet of Things (IoT), mobile devices, and medical data. It highlights the increasing demand for IT services and the corresponding rise in cyberattacks, with a focus on Distributed Denial of Service (DDoS) attacks. DDoS attacks aim to disrupt legitimate users' access to web-based services by overwhelming a target with traffic from numerous compromised devices, or bots. The paper distinguishes between DDoS and Denial of Service (DoS) attacks based on the number of attackers involved.

DDoS attacks can occur at various layers of the network and utilize different protocols, including TCP, UDP, ICMP, and HTTP. The attackers typically take control of vulnerable machines, exploiting their weaknesses to create a botnet for launching large-scale attacks. The evolving nature of these attacks is emphasized, as attackers adapt their methods and motivations over time. The paper notes recent high-profile DDoS attacks, such as the one on Estonian government websites, underscoring the significant impact these attacks can have. Two primary techniques for executing DDoS attacks are discussed: vulnerability attacks, which confuse protocols or applications, and flooding attacks, which overwhelm network resources to disrupt legitimate user connections.
