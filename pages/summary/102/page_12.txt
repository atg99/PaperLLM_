This paper discusses various machine learning models utilized for analyzing the CICDDoS2019 dataset, focusing on their unique advantages and applications. 

1. The Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) addresses the vanishing gradient problem inherent in standard recurrent neural networks (RNNs). It is akin to Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks, designed to yield similar outcomes in certain scenarios. The GRU model is favored in this research due to its quicker training, faster execution, and lower memory consumption.

2. The Recurrent Neural Network (RNN) is characterized by its internal memory, allowing it to process sequences of data where current outputs are influenced by previous computations. RNN is particularly suitable for the CICDDoS2019 dataset due to its large size, as the model size remains constant regardless of input size.

3. Naive Bayes (NB) is a classification method grounded in Bayes’ Theorem, predicated on the assumption that features are independent of each other. This model is employed in the research due to its effectiveness with the CICDDoS2019 dataset.

4. Bayes’ Theorem calculates the posterior probability of a class given certain predictors, factoring in prior probabilities and likelihoods. The use of the NB model is justified for its applicability to the dataset at hand.

5. The Sequential Minimal Optimization (SMO) algorithm is introduced as a solution to the quadratic programming challenges faced during the training of support vector machines (SVM). It is noted for its speed and robustness, making it suitable for high-dimensional datasets like CICDDoS2019.

In the evaluation of results, the performance of these models is measured by Accuracy, Precision, Recall, and F1-score. While Accuracy is a common metric, the F1-score is highlighted as a crucial measure when dealing with imbalanced datasets.
