The paper discusses the increasing prevalence of DDoS attacks and the challenges in updating detection techniques to counter them. It references various studies and datasets, including the "DDoS Attack 2007" dataset, which contains traffic traces and details about attack responses. The authors classify DDoS attacks based on factors such as automation, vulnerability, source address validity, and impact on victims. Different classification frameworks have been proposed, including one that uses the K-means algorithm for detection, though it lacks experimental validation.

Further research in 2016 expanded on DDoS taxonomy in cloud computing, focusing on attack characteristics like automation degree and impact. Unique features of this study included the ability to detect zero-day attacks. Analysis of DDoS attacks in cloud environments highlighted various attack types, including bandwidth and resource exhaustion attacks.

Additionally, the paper mentions the development of a Network Intrusion Detection System (NIDS) that combines the Naive Bayes classifier with Snort, a signature-based detection system. This system categorizes captured packets into intrusion and non-intrusion sets, logging and denying the former while processing the latter for further analysis.
