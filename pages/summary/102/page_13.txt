The paper evaluates the performance of various classifiers in detecting NTP and UDP DDoS attacks, using the F1-score as a key metric. For NTP attacks, the GRU algorithm achieved the highest accuracy of 99.52%, followed closely by RNN at 99.35%, SMO at 98.89%, and Naive Bayes (NB) at 96.65%. The lower performance of the naive Bayes method is attributed to its requirement for more data instances. The accuracy and loss convergence for NTP attacks demonstrated stability after the 42nd epoch, with training accuracy increasing from 97.5% to 99.5% and the lowest loss reaching 0.01% by the 44th epoch.

In the case of UDP attacks, both GRU and RNN classifiers also achieved the highest accuracy of 99.69%, with SMO and NB following at 99.60% and 99.20%, respectively. The accuracy convergence for UDP attacks peaked at 99.76% by the 42nd epoch, with training accuracy starting at 98.7% and stabilizing thereafter. The lowest loss for UDP attacks was recorded at 0.01% at the 46th epoch. Overall, the results indicate that the GRU and RNN algorithms are particularly effective in detecting both types of DDoS attacks.
