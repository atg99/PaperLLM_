The paper addresses the challenge of accurately detecting and identifying new types of Distributed Denial of Service (DDoS) attacks, which can exhaust network resources or disrupt legitimate user services. Existing studies have limitations in detecting real-world DDoS attacks, such as NTP and UDP. The proposed solution, DIDDOS, utilizes a customized Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) model to enhance detection capabilities and address issues related to imbalanced datasets. 

Key contributions of the paper include:
1. Introduction of the DIDDOS approach for DDoS detection and classification.
2. Evaluation of DIDDOS against conventional machine learning classifiers (Naive Bayes, Sequential Minimal Optimization) and deep learning methods (GRU, RNN).
3. Comparative analysis demonstrating that GRU outperforms other algorithms in detection and identification efficiency.

The paper is structured as follows: Section 2 reviews related work on DDoS detection; Section 3 discusses the dataset; Section 4 details the DIDDOS approach; Section 5 outlines the experimental setup and results; Section 6 provides a comparative analysis; and Section 7 concludes with insights for future work. The paper highlights the growing prevalence of DDoS attacks, projecting a significant increase in their occurrence.
