The paper presents a novel approach called DIDDOS for detecting and identifying Distributed Denial of Service (DDoS) cyberattacks using Gated Recurrent Units (GRU), a specific type of Recurrent Neural Network (RNN). DDoS attacks can severely disrupt communication networks by overwhelming them with malicious traffic, creating a complex challenge for maintaining secure environments. 

Despite various existing detection methods, there remains a significant need for improvement in the efficiency and reliability of DDoS attack detection. The proposed DIDDOS framework incorporates multiple classification algorithms, including GRU, RNN, Naive Bayes (NB), and Sequential Minimal Optimization (SMO), to enhance detection capabilities. 

Performance is evaluated using metrics such as accuracy, recall, F1-score, and precision. The experimental results demonstrate that DIDDOS achieves remarkable accuracy rates of 99.69% for reflection attacks and 99.94% for exploitation attacks when utilizing GRU, indicating its potential as an effective solution for real-world DDoS attack detection.
