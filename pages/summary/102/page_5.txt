The paper discusses various approaches to intrusion detection systems (IDS) and their features, particularly focusing on multilevel protection mechanisms. It reviews related works and highlights the use of different machine learning techniques for anomaly detection, including Naive Bayes, Support Vector Machines (SVM), and Recurrent Neural Networks (RNN).

Key points include:

1. **Anomaly Detection Module**: A Naive Bayes classifier is employed to differentiate between normal and intrusion packets. Only normal packets are allowed into the system.

2. **Deep Learning Models**: Several authors propose deep learning frameworks for anomaly detection, particularly in connected vehicles.

3. **SVM Implementation**: Jing et al. outline a structured approach for SVM application, which involves data division, preprocessing, training, testing, and recording results.

4. **RNN Developments**: Multiple researchers explore RNNs for intrusion detection, employing techniques like rebalancing datasets and feature reduction to enhance detection rates. Long-Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks and kernel Principal Component Analysis (PCA) are also utilized to improve performance.

5. **Performance Metrics**: The paper reports a notable detection rate of 99.46% and a false alarm rate of 4.86% for the proposed models, showcasing their effectiveness in identifying intrusions.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the evolution and application of various machine learning techniques in developing robust intrusion detection systems.
