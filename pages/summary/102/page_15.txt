The paper presents an analysis of various models used for detecting different types of cyber attacks, specifically focusing on DDoS and LDAP attacks. The performance of these models is evaluated through metrics such as accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score, as detailed in Table 4. Among the models, the Support Vector Machine (SMO) achieved the highest accuracy of 99.75% for DNS attacks, followed closely by Recurrent Neural Network (RNN) and Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU), with accuracy rates of 99.72% and 99.51%, respectively.

For LDAP attacks, the SMO model again outperformed others, achieving an accuracy of 99.96%, while GRU, RNN, and Naive Bayes (NB) recorded accuracies of 99.95%, 99.94%, and 99.82%. The accuracy convergence for these models was monitored over epochs, with the highest accuracy reached at the 4th epoch. The training accuracy showed an increase from 99% to 99.9%, stabilizing thereafter, while test accuracy rose from 98.1% to 99.95%. Furthermore, the models exhibited low loss values, with training loss starting at 0.06% and decreasing to 0.005%, indicating effective learning during training. Overall, the results indicate that the SMO model is particularly proficient in detecting both DNS and LDAP attacks, while the other models also demonstrate strong performance metrics.
