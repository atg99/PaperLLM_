The paper presents a detailed analysis of model accuracy and loss in detecting DDoS malware, particularly in the context of NTP and DNS attacks. It provides performance metrics for various algorithms, including GRU, RNN, SMO, and NB, highlighting their proficiency in detecting UDP attacks. The highest accuracy reported for UDP attacks is 99.69% for both GRU and RNN, with SMO achieving a precision of 99.61%. The training loss for these models starts at 0.07% and decreases to a stable 0.01%.

For DNS attacks, the SMO algorithm achieves the highest accuracy of 99.75%, while GRU, RNN, and NB reach accuracies of 99.51%, 99.72%, and 99.35%, respectively. The training accuracy shows a positive trend, starting at 98.25% and stabilizing around 99.6%, with test accuracy following a similar pattern. The lowest training loss of 0.01% is reached at the 48th epoch, indicating effective model convergence. Overall, the findings demonstrate the strong performance of these algorithms in detecting DDoS attacks, particularly with regard to accuracy and loss metrics.
