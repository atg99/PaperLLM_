The paper discusses a method for optimizing class centers in a deep learning framework by regularizing the L2,1 norm of the rows in a matrix representing these centers. This approach aims to create a sparse set of centers that effectively capture the geometry of data. The authors formulate a final objective that combines a soft triple loss with a regularization term, allowing for better performance in classification tasks.

Experiments are conducted on three benchmark datasets: CUB-2011, Cars196, and SOP, utilizing the Inception architecture. The model is fine-tuned using data augmentation techniques and optimized with the Adam optimizer. The experiments compare the proposed soft triple loss against the normalized SoftMax loss, demonstrating that the soft triple loss yields superior performance, particularly in retrieval and clustering tasks.

In the CUB-2011 dataset, the proposed method outperforms existing metric learning techniques, indicating that the soft triple loss effectively captures the intricate relationships between data points. The results highlight that increasing the number of centers improves performance, confirming the inadequacy of a single center per class in complex datasets. Overall, both the soft triple and SoftMax losses show promising results, suggesting that effective embeddings can be learned without extensive sampling.
