The paper discusses the optimization of SoftMax loss and its limitations in capturing the complex geometry of data, particularly in scenarios with high intra-class variance. It establishes that the closed-form solution for the distribution defined in the SoftMax loss is derived from the K.K.T. conditions. The analysis reveals that the SoftMax loss effectively optimizes triplet constraints involving an example and two class centers, but it is limited by assuming a single center per class.

To address this issue, the authors propose the introduction of multiple centers for each class, allowing for a more nuanced similarity measure between examples and class centers. The triplet constraint is reformulated to ensure that an example is closer to its corresponding class center than to others, incorporating a margin to enhance robustness.

The HardTriple loss is derived from this framework, which provides multiple centers while maintaining the integrity of the triplet constraints. However, it notes that the max operator used for determining the nearest center may introduce sensitivity issues. To mitigate this, the authors suggest smoothing the max operator, inspired by the SoftMax loss, to enhance the robustness of the learned embeddings. 

Overall, the paper highlights the necessity of multiple centers in classification tasks and proposes a refined loss function to improve the learning of meaningful feature embeddings.
