This paper addresses the limitations of traditional distance metric learning (DML) methods that rely on hand-crafted features, which can be task-independent and lead to information loss, thereby hindering performance. The advent of deep neural networks has enabled the direct learning of embeddings from raw data, significantly enhancing performance by eliminating explicit feature extraction. However, a major challenge in deep metric learning is the effective generation of optimization constraints, particularly because training is conducted using stochastic gradient descent (SGD) with only a mini-batch of examples at each iteration.

The authors highlight that the mini-batch may not adequately represent the overall data distribution, especially in larger datasets, leading to suboptimal embedding learning. They emphasize the need for effective sampling strategies to enhance the quality of mini-batches and constraints, as existing methods can complicate optimization and cause information loss.

The paper proposes a novel approach to improve the SoftMax loss function, which is commonly used in classification tasks. The authors demonstrate that the SoftMax loss can be viewed as a smoothed triplet loss, where a single center for each class is insufficient to capture the inherent data structure due to potential multiple clusters within classes. To address this, they introduce the SoftTriple loss, which utilizes multiple centers per class to better represent the data distribution and reduce intra-class variance.

The SoftTriple loss allows for efficient optimization without requiring triplet sampling, as the number of triplets generated is linear concerning the number of examples. Additionally, the authors propose a strategy to initially set a large number of centers for each class and then apply L2,1 norm regularization to refine them into a compact set.

The paper is structured as follows: Section 2 reviews related work in DML; Section 3 analyzes the SoftMax loss and introduces the SoftTriple loss; Section 4 presents comparisons on benchmark datasets; and Section 5 concludes with future research directions. Overall, the proposed SoftTriple loss aims to enhance performance in fine-grained visual categorization tasks by effectively capturing local data clusters.
