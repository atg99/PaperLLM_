This paper introduces SoftTriple loss, a novel approach to deep metric learning (DML) that circumvents the need for triplet sampling, a common challenge due to the vast number of potential triplet constraints. Traditional DML methods optimize embeddings so that examples from the same class are closer together than those from different classes, often using triplet constraints. However, these methods rely on mini-batch sampling which can lead to sub-optimal embeddings due to their inability to adequately represent the overall data distribution.

The authors highlight that optimizing SoftMax loss with deep neural networks (DNNs) has shown superior performance in certain DML tasks. They reveal that SoftMax loss can be viewed as a smoothed triplet loss with a single center per class. However, real-world datasets often contain multiple local clusters within a single class, necessitating a more flexible approach.

To address this, the paper proposes SoftTriple loss, which extends the SoftMax loss by allowing multiple centers for each class. This modification enables the learning of embeddings without the sampling phase, simply by increasing the size of the last fully connected layer. The experiments conducted on benchmark fine-grained datasets demonstrate that SoftTriple loss effectively captures intra-class variance and improves the quality of learned embeddings compared to traditional DML methods.

In summary, SoftTriple loss offers a solution to the limitations of conventional DML by providing a framework that accommodates multiple class centers, thus enhancing the modeling of complex data distributions in real-world applications.
