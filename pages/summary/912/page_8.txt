The paper presents a comparative analysis of different loss functions for deep metric learning, specifically focusing on SoftMax norm and SoftTriple. The results indicate that SoftMax norm outperforms ProxyNCA by 3% in retrieval accuracy at rank 1 (R@1), while SoftTriple further enhances performance by an additional 2%. 

In the evaluation on the Cars196 dataset, SoftMax norm achieves R@1 of 76.8 and SoftTriple improves this to 78.6. Both methods surpass previous techniques, with SoftTriple demonstrating a notable gain when embeddings are larger, increasing R@1 by 3% over the state-of-the-art method HTL.

The Stanford Online Products dataset yields similar results, with SoftMax norm showing a 2% improvement over ProxyNCA at R@1 and SoftTriple achieving a 0.4% increase by utilizing multiple centers. With larger embeddings, SoftTriple again outperforms existing methods, confirming its efficacy without relying on triplet sampling.

The paper concludes that the SoftTriple loss function, which represents each class with multiple centers, optimizes embeddings more effectively than traditional methods that sample triplets from mini-batches. This approach allows for a more comprehensive learning process and aligns well with standard classification training pipelines, suggesting potential for future applications in classification tasks.
