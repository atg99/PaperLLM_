The paper identifies two types of risks linked to audio input: ungrounded inference (UGI) and sensitive trait attribution (STA). UGI involves making assumptions about a speaker that cannot be accurately inferred from audio alone, such as their race, socioeconomic status, or personal traits. This can result in harmful outcomes related to allocation of resources and representation. Conversely, STA pertains to inferences that can be reasonably drawn from audio, like accents or nationality, which can lead to profiling and reinforce stereotypes. Both categories highlight the potential for bias and inaccuracies in how audio data is interpreted, emphasizing the need for caution in automated assessments.
