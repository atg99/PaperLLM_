The study evaluated the persuasiveness of GPT-4o's text and voice modalities in influencing participant opinions on political topics. The voice modality was deemed low risk, while the text modality was classified as marginally medium risk based on pre-registered thresholds. 

In the text modality analysis, GPT-4o-generated articles and chatbots were compared to professional human-written content. Although AI-generated texts were not overall more persuasive than human-written articles, they outperformed human interventions in three specific instances out of twelve.

For the voice modality, the methodology was revised to assess effect sizes on hypothetical party preferences and their persistence after one week. The study compared GPT-4o's voiced audio clips and interactive conversations against human-generated audio clips and conversations. Results indicated that the GPT-4o voice model was not more persuasive than human counterparts. Specifically, AI audio clips achieved 78% of the effect size of human audio clips, while AI conversations reached 65% of the effect size of human conversations. One week later, the effect sizes for AI conversations and audio clips were minimal, at 0.8% and -0.72%, respectively. Participants were subsequently debriefed with opposing audio clips to reduce any lingering persuasive effects.
