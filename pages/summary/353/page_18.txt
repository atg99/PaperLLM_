The paper discusses the evaluation of the GPT-4o model in executing machine learning engineering tasks and its performance in autonomous coding tasks. The SWE-Bench benchmark indicated a pass rate of 19% at the first attempt, utilizing the best post-training and publicly available scaffolds. In contrast, GPT-4o performed poorly in the METR assessment, failing all ten trials of selected machine learning engineering tasks. The evaluation highlighted that while GPT-4o could perform individual substeps, it struggled with autonomous action, often engaging in trial-and-error debugging due to simple mistakes like hallucinations and API misuses. 

Despite some rollouts achieving a non-trivial amount of progress and passing automated grading, manual analysis revealed that the model did not fulfill the overall task requirements. The METR assessment involved testing GPT-4o on 86 long-horizon multi-step tasks across various domains, finding no significant improvement in capabilities compared to GPT-4. The findings underscore the limitations of GPT-4o in autonomous functionalities and its reliance on human-like debugging processes, which raises concerns regarding its application in real-world scenarios. Further details and methodological insights are available in METR's full report.
