The paper discusses the implementation and evaluation of a voice generation system designed to mitigate risks associated with unauthorized voice generation and speaker identification. The system operates by streaming audio generation while ensuring that the output matches a designated preset voice, effectively blocking any output that deviates from this standard. Internal evaluations show that the system successfully detects 100% of significant deviations, although there remains a minor risk of unintentional voice generation. To address this, secondary classifiers are employed to terminate conversations if such occurrences are detected. However, the system may over-refuse conversations conducted in languages other than English, which is an area targeted for improvement.

In terms of speaker identification, the paper outlines the potential privacy risks associated with recognizing individuals based on their voice in audio inputs. To counteract these risks, the model has been trained to refuse requests for identifying speakers unless the content explicitly identifies them, such as famous quotes. The evaluation indicates substantial improvements in the model's ability to refuse identification requests, with a 14-point increase in accuracy for refusals and a 12-point increase for compliance with legitimate requests. The results suggest a significant enhancement in safeguarding privacy while acknowledging some cases where the model may incorrectly refuse to identify well-known figures. Overall, the findings highlight the balance between maintaining functionality and addressing ethical considerations in voice technology.
