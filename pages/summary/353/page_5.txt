The paper discusses challenges and considerations in evaluating the speech-to-speech capabilities of the GPT-4o model, particularly in relation to user inputs and text-to-speech (TTS) functionalities. It highlights that inputs unlikely to be provided by users in Advanced Voice Mode may lead to model evaluation limitations. Mistakes in evaluations could stem from either the model's capabilities or inaccuracies in the TTS translation process.

The authors express concerns regarding the representativeness of TTS inputs compared to actual audio inputs from users, noting that various factors such as regional accents, voice intonations, background noise, and cross-talk are not fully captured in TTS evaluations. Additionally, the presence of artifacts or properties in generated audio, such as background sounds and voice variations, poses challenges.

To address safety risks associated with the model, several mitigation strategies are employed. Post-training methods are used to guide model behavior, and classifiers are integrated to block undesirable audio generation. The paper outlines specific risks, such as unauthorized voice generation and speaker identification, along with corresponding mitigations, including supervision of audio data and restrictions on voice usage. The evaluation results indicate that while risks are illustrative and not exhaustive, they focus on interactions between speech-to-speech capabilities and existing modalities like text and image.
