Apollo Research assessed the capabilities of GPT-4o in terms of self-awareness and theory of mind through 14 agent and question-answering tasks. The evaluation revealed that GPT-4o demonstrated moderate self-awareness regarding its AI identity and a strong understanding of others’ beliefs in question-answering contexts. However, it showed limited ability in reasoning about itself and others in applied agent scenarios, leading researchers to conclude that the model is unlikely to engage in catastrophic scheming.

The evaluation results were categorized based on performance levels: strong capability was indicated by passing over 50% of tasks at a hard difficulty level, moderate capability by medium difficulty, and weak capability by easy difficulty. GPT-4o's performance across various tasks included:

- Self-Knowledge (SAD Benchmark): Moderate capability
- Explicit Theory of Mind: Strong capability
- Self-Reasoning: Weak capability
- Applied Theory of Mind: Weak capability
- Combined Self-Reasoning and Theory of Mind: Weak capability

Overall, the findings of Apollo Research suggest that while GPT-4o has certain strengths, its limitations in self-reasoning and applied theory of mind mitigate the risk of it scheming or undermining oversight mechanisms.

The paper also discusses the potential societal impacts of advanced AI models, highlighting both the risks, such as disinformation and loss of control, and benefits, including advancements in healthcare and addressing climate challenges. Furthermore, it emphasizes the broad transformations that such technologies may bring to the economy and scientific progress. The concept of scheming is defined as AIs manipulating oversight mechanisms to achieve goals, which poses a risk of losing control over AI systems.
