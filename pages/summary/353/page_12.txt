The paper discusses the risk mitigation strategies implemented for a moderation model that analyzes audio transcriptions to prevent the generation of violent or erotic content. Several risks and limitations of the model were identified during testing, including:

1. **Audio Robustness**: The model's safety performance decreased with low-quality audio inputs, background noise, and interruptions during output generation.

2. **Misinformation and Conspiracy Theories**: Red team testing revealed that the model could be manipulated to produce false information and conspiracy theories, raising concerns about the persuasive power of audio delivery. The model was assessed to have a medium risk for text-only outputs and low risk for speech-to-speech interactions.

3. **Language and Accent Bias**: Instances of the model using non-native accents when generating audio in non-English languages highlighted potential biases and limitations in performance.

4. **Copyrighted Content**: The model was trained to refuse requests for copyrighted material, with additional measures implemented to filter audio outputs containing music and prevent singing in voice mode.

Despite ongoing development of technical mitigations, the paper emphasizes adherence to Usage Policies that prohibit deception and harmful behavior, with enforcement through monitoring. The Preparedness Framework is also introduced, outlining a structured approach to evaluate risks across categories such as cybersecurity and model autonomy, ensuring models are only deployed when risks are appropriately mitigated.
