This paper discusses the limitations of using the Softmax function in Capsule Networks (CapsNet) for routing coefficients, which are essential for establishing connections between lower and higher-level capsules. The authors argue that Softmax constrains the dynamic range of these coefficients, resulting in uniform probabilities that hinder optimal coupling between capsules. To address this issue, they propose Max-Min normalization, a scale-invariant technique that allows each lower-level capsule to adopt an independent value within defined bounds. 

The use of Max-Min normalization leads to consistent improvements in test accuracy across five datasets, enabling more routing iterations without sacrificing performance. Specifically, a CapsNet trained with Max-Min achieved a test error rate of 0.20% on the MNIST dataset, and a majority vote from three models reduced this error to 0.17%. 

The introduction outlines the significance of deep learning in various fields and emphasizes the potential of CapsNets to enhance object recognition by leveraging dynamic routing algorithms that calculate routing coefficients in real-time. The paper concludes that Max-Min normalization is a promising alternative to Softmax, enhancing the effectiveness of CapsNet architectures.
