The paper discusses the performance of CapsNets on the MNIST dataset, highlighting the misclassification of certain images. Out of 10,000 predictions, 17 were incorrect compared to the ground truth labels. A polling of 120 individuals on these misclassified images showed that in 6 cases, the network's predictions aligned with human judgments. Table 3 provides a detailed comparison of human and network predictions for these images, indicating varying probabilities for both ground truth and predicted labels.

The paper emphasizes the limitations of current machine learning methods, as illustrated by an example where the digit 7 was consistently misclassified as 1 by the network, while humans correctly identified it. This points to the challenges faced by machine learning models in achieving human-level accuracy.

Additionally, the study explores various normalization methods used for routing in CapsNets, including Winner-Take-All, sum, centered Max-Min, Z-score, and adjusted log normalizations. The authors aim to assess the effectiveness of these methods in creating routing coefficients, though they note that a comprehensive study was not conducted. The Winner-Take-All method, in particular, allows each lower-level capsule to contribute to only one higher-level capsule, based on the highest coefficient value.
