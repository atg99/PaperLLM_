The paper discusses the differences in routing mechanisms between Softmax and Max-Min normalization methods in capsule networks, particularly focusing on their effects on logits and routing coefficients during training with the CIFAR and MNIST datasets. Figure 3 illustrates the evolution of these logits and routing coefficients for a specific training image, showcasing how Max-Min allows lower-level capsules to maintain high assignment probabilities with multiple higher-level capsules. In contrast, Softmax's competitive nature reduces the likelihood of such probabilities across adjacent capsule layers.

Figure 4 further exemplifies these differences by presenting routing coefficients for lower-level capsules in PrimaryCaps as they relate to higher-level capsules in DigitCaps during the final routing iteration. It highlights that while Softmax produces tightly clustered logits leading to similar assignment probabilities, Max-Min results in a more varied and optimal distribution of probabilities, enhancing capsule separation. Overall, the findings underline the advantages of Max-Min normalization in achieving better representation and routing within capsule networks.
