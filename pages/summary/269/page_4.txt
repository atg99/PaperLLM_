The Max-Min Routing Procedure is a method utilized within the CapsNet architecture to optimize the routing of features represented by capsules. The procedure begins with inputs of prediction vectors and initializes routing coefficients to facilitate the dynamic binding of features to objects. The steps include calculating the weighted summation of the prediction vectors, applying a squashing function to produce output vectors, and updating the routing coefficients using a Max-Min approach.

The evolution of logits and routing coefficients is critical for effective object recognition. In the CapsNet, the routing coefficients determine how features from lower-layer capsules are assigned to higher-layer capsules. Initial values for these coefficients are set differently under Softmax and Max-Min normalization, influencing their evolution through the routing process. 

With Softmax, the coefficients start at a value of 0:1, resulting in limited separation among features as the logits cluster around zero. In contrast, Max-Min normalization initializes routing coefficients to 1:0, allowing for enhanced separation and clearer assignment of features. As training progresses, the behavior of logits and routing coefficients diverges, with Max-Min normalization showing a more effective separation of competing features in adjacent capsule layers. This adaptability is pivotal for the CapsNet's performance in recognizing objects across datasets such as MNIST and CIFAR10.
