The paper discusses a method for reconstructing images using parent-level capsules within a neural network framework. During the forward pass, the network computes these capsules to recreate the original image, with any reconstruction errors backpropagating to the prediction vectors and preceding layers. This iterative process allows the prediction vectors to adjust in alignment with the parent-level capsules through transformation matrices.

The routing procedure is modified from previous work, substituting the Softmax function with a Max-Min normalization approach. This change is detailed with the initialization of routing coefficients and the iterative process for routing. The training methodology follows established practices, utilizing TensorFlow and the Adam optimizer with specified hyperparameters.

The architecture includes a three-layer CapsNet, with the PrimaryCaps layer consisting of multiple vector capsules, and the DigitCaps layer used for margin loss calculations. The reconstruction sub-network processes the DigitCaps layer to reproduce the input image, applying margin and reconstruction loss functions.

Overall, the paper outlines a refined routing procedure and the structure of the CapsNet architecture, emphasizing the importance of parent-level capsules in image reconstruction and the adjustments made to the routing algorithm.
