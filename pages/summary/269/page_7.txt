The paper presents a comparative analysis of the performance of neural networks using Max-Min and Softmax normalizations across five datasets: MNIST, Background MNIST (bMNIST), Rotated MNIST (rMNIST), Fashion MNIST (fMNIST), and CIFAR10. The networks were trained using a consistent three-layer model and hyperparameters, with no data augmentations for most datasets, except for CIFAR10, which utilized random cropping techniques.

Results indicate that Max-Min normalization significantly enhances test accuracy over Softmax, particularly for datasets with non-zero backgrounds, such as bMNIST and CIFAR10. The analysis reveals that Max-Min normalization allows for more routing iterations without a decline in test accuracy, contrary to Softmax, where test accuracy decreases with additional iterations. Furthermore, Max-Min normalization helps mitigate overfitting, as evidenced by smaller discrepancies between training and test accuracies with increased routing iterations.

Overall, the findings suggest that Max-Min normalization is a more effective approach for training neural networks, leading to improved performance and robustness against overfitting.
