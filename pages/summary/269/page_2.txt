This paper discusses enhancements to Capsule Networks (CapsNets) by introducing the scale-invariant Max-Min normalization function, which improves performance over the traditional Softmax normalization. The authors focus on the CapsNet framework proposed by Sabour et al., where the normalization lower bound is set to 0.0, enabling higher-level capsules to ignore non-essential features from lower-level capsules, effectively acting as a dynamic dropout mechanism. The upper bound can be varied, and experiments found that using an upper bound of 1.0 allows for independent assignment probabilities of lower-level capsules to higher-level ones, which is beneficial for capturing multiple features.

The implementation of Max-Min normalization resulted in improved test accuracy across five datasets and allowed for more routing iterations without overfitting. A CapsNet trained with minimal data augmentation on the MNIST dataset achieved a test error of 0.2%, and with a majority voting system, this was further reduced to 0.17%, surpassing previous ensemble models.

The paper is structured as follows: Section 2 summarizes the three-layer CapsNet architecture and contrasts routing procedures between Softmax and Max-Min. Section 3 examines the evolution of logits and routing coefficients under both normalization methods. Section 4 presents tuning curves from the routing layer, while Section 5 details comparative results from training sessions. Finally, Section 6 focuses on performance metrics for the MNIST dataset, including tests with alternative normalization functions. 

The CapsNet architecture described consists of convolutional layers that process input images into lower-level capsules, which are then dynamically routed to form higher-level capsules. The routing algorithm leverages prediction vectors generated from learned transformation weights, and while gradients do not flow through the routing layer, the overall architecture maintains standard gradient flows during training, allowing for effective learning and reconstruction of input data.
