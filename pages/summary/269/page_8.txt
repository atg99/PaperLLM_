The paper presents a comparative analysis of Max-Min and Softmax normalizations on test accuracies across five datasets: MNIST, rotated MNIST, Fashion MNIST, Background MNIST, and CIFAR10. The results indicate that Max-Min normalization yields higher mean maximum test accuracies and lower standard deviations than Softmax across all datasets. For instance, Max-Min achieved 99.55% accuracy on MNIST, compared to 99.28% for Softmax. 

Figure 7 illustrates that as the number of routing iterations increases, Max-Min normalization enhances network performance without leading to overfitting, contrasting with Softmax normalization. This suggests that Max-Min supports better scalability in network capacity.

The paper also references prior work by Sabour et al., which achieved a low test error of 0.25% on MNIST with a three-layer CapsNet using Softmax. The authors argue that their findings show an average improvement of 0.27% in test accuracy with Max-Min normalization, indicating that a CapsNet trained with Max-Min and minimal data augmentations could surpass existing state-of-the-art results on MNIST.
