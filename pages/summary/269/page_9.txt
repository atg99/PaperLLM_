The paper presents a comparative analysis of CapsNet performance using two normalization techniques: Max-Min and Softmax, across various datasets including MNIST, Rotated MNIST, Fashion MNIST, Background MNIST, and CIFAR10. Figure 8 illustrates the differences between training and testing accuracies as the number of routing iterations increases, highlighting that Max-Min normalization effectively mitigates overfitting, especially with more routing iterations. 

The methodology included random image translations and rotations, with adjusted margin loss constraints and a training configuration of 400 images per batch over 500 epochs. Results from Table 2 indicate that CapsNet trained with Max-Min normalization achieved a test error of 0.20%, while a majority vote from a three-model ensemble yielded a slightly lower error of 0.17%. The paper notes that the experiments were repeated ten times, showcasing the robustness of the Max-Min approach in reducing test errors compared to Softmax, which had a maximum test error of 0.35%. Further analysis of the MNIST results and misclassifications is provided in Appendix A.
