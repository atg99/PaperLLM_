The paper discusses the effectiveness of various normalization techniques for routing coefficients in Capsule Networks, specifically focusing on their impact on performance when applied to the MNIST dataset. It highlights that while both centered max-min and Z-score normalization can yield negative values for routing coefficients, Z-score normalization has an unbounded range which complicates initialization. Centered max-min normalization, while bounded between -1 and 1, can lead to performance issues due to counterbalancing effects of large positive and negative values. Log transformations are noted for compressing high values, but also present initialization challenges due to their unbounded nature.

The performance of different normalization functions is quantitatively assessed, with Max-Min normalization achieving the highest test accuracy of 99.55%, followed closely by centered max-min and adjusted log transformations. The paper emphasizes the importance of widely separated routing coefficients for optimal class separation, revealing that the use of the Softmax function may lead to tightly clustered coefficients, reducing their dynamic range. In contrast, Max-Min normalization enhances the dynamic range, allowing for improved recognition errors across multiple datasets and enabling more routing iterations without overfitting. Ultimately, the study concludes that a single CapsNet can achieve state-of-the-art results on the MNIST test set with minimal data augmentation, demonstrating the effectiveness of the proposed normalization method.
