The paper discusses the use of Belousov-Zhabotinsky (BZ) reaction systems for short-term information storage and processing. The BZ platforms exhibit oscillations that can occur without physical actuation, allowing for up to eight repetitions, akin to volatile memory. By integrating mechanical stirrers for programmability with the memory capabilities of the BZ medium, the system can process and store information in the same space.

The authors employ a reservoir computing scheme, interfacing the BZ system with a neural network (NN) to classify various input patterns based on the generated wave propagation. This process involves image processing to identify BZ oscillations, creating a dataset of 750 features from 30 time points. The system demonstrates an accuracy of 80.5% in classifying 20 different input patterns, including digital numbers and letters.

Additionally, the paper illustrates methods for controlling oscillating cells within the BZ platform. By manipulating the connections between cells, users can specify which cells oscillate. The research highlights the dynamic input of different patterns during execution, showcasing the capability to adapt the BZ medium's behavior in real-time. Overall, the findings suggest potential applications in computational systems utilizing chemical reactions for information processing.
