The paper discusses the development of a programmable chemical computer that addresses the limitations of traditional computing architectures, specifically the von Neumann bottleneck. The authors present a chemical processor comprising a 5 by 5 array of cells that utilize the Belousov-Zhabotinsky reaction, allowing for individual cell activation in either 'on' or 'off' states. This setup enables the system to achieve over 2.9 × 10^17 chemical states through the detection of varying oscillation amplitudes via image processing. The programmable nature of this array facilitates chemically encoded memory and the creation of a chemical Autoencoder capable of performing pattern recognition tasks at a rate of one million operations per second. The findings illustrate the potential of chemical processes to enhance computational capabilities beyond conventional methods.
