Over the past five decades, the role of computers in modern life has expanded significantly, with an increase in processing power. However, traditional computer architecture, which separates processors from memory, creates data bottlenecks. While quantum computing offers potential solutions, it has yet to fully realize its capabilities. Nature, on the other hand, utilizes parallelism in collective networks to process information amidst noise. Notably, non-living chemical systems can perform computations, yet their programmability remains limited.

Many unconventional computational architectures leverage coupled-oscillator systems, including those based on chemical, spin-torque, and laser oscillators. These systems often face challenges such as weak coupling and noise. Progress has been made in chemical computation using reaction-diffusion systems to solve complex problems, exemplified by the Belousov-Zhabotinsky (BZ) reaction, which has been used to mimic logic gates, process images, and optimize solutions.

This paper introduces a programmable chemical processor utilizing interconnected BZ reaction cells, where each cell can be individually controlled by magnetic stirrers. The BZ reaction employs [Fe(Bpy)3]2/3+ as a catalyst, producing distinct color changes that indicate the reaction state. The system can be programmed for various tasks by adjusting the stirrer speeds and configurations, allowing for flexible computations based on the interaction of oscillatory patterns.

The BZ processing platform consists of four main components: a customizable reactor cell grid, an array of magnetic stirrers, a control interface, and a monitoring camera. The BZ reaction, driven by ferroin, can be manipulated to create specific oscillatory patterns. By selectively activating stirrers, users can influence the formation of excitation waves, leading to synchronized global patterns in the chemical system.

In conclusion, this research presents a novel approach to chemical computation, highlighting the potential of chemical systems for programmable and flexible processing tasks through innovative design and control mechanisms.
