The paper discusses a programmable platform utilizing a grid of fluidically connected cells to control wave patterns through a Belousov-Zhabotinsky (BZ) reaction. Initially designed as a 7 × 7 grid, only the central 5 × 5 cells were utilized. Early prototypes featured v-shaped openings between cells, which hindered wave propagation, causing the platform to behave like a static display. Modifications were made by removing the v-shaped openings, allowing fluid interactions among cells, enabling wave propagation through stirring.

The study highlights the reproducibility of wave patterns in response to specific input patterns, demonstrating the platform's consistency and programmability. As input patterns become more complex, human interpretation of the resulting wave patterns becomes challenging. However, the BZ system produces reliable outputs for identical inputs, establishing a basis for programmability. The research further illustrates that the global patterns generated are influenced by the historical input patterns over time, with variations in potassium bromate concentration affecting the system's state.

The methodology includes a digital interface for user input, a chemical domain where BZ oscillations are induced, and a read-out system using image processing to classify the states of each cell. The findings indicate that the interconnected cells can perform complex calculations by manipulating stirring speeds across active, processing, and inactive cells, leveraging the memory of BZ oscillations to influence the medium's global state based on past inputs.
