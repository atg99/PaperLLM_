This paper discusses the development of a programmable chemical processor driven by the Belousov-Zhabotinsky (BZ) reaction and an array of 25 switchable cells. The research demonstrates that the BZ system can produce consistent patterns in response to specific input, which can be effectively classified by a convolutional neural network, achieving an accuracy of 92.5%. This suggests that the BZ system exhibits the echo state property of reservoir computing, where each cell produces a nonlinear response that contributes to a desired output.

In a comparative experiment, a digital encoder was used to generate BZ oscillations from motor patterns. The neural network successfully classified oscillations produced by both the BZ platform and the digital encoder. This indicates that the BZ platform functions as a chemical processor, paralleling the role of a digital encoder.

The paper highlights the capability of the BZ-driven chemical processor to encode and decode information through oscillations. It can distinguish numerous patterns with low error rates, making it a sophisticated system for processing complex dynamics that are challenging to interpret theoretically. The findings suggest potential applications for the chemical processor in encoding data, leveraging the unique properties of chemical oscillations for pattern recognition.
