The paper "Multi-View Sequence-to-Sequence Models with Conversational Structure for Abstractive Dialogue Summarization" by Jiaao Chen and Diyi Yang addresses the challenge of summarizing conversations, which is critical in human-human and human-machine interactions. While existing text summarization research has focused on structured documents like news reports and scientific articles, conversations are often informal, verbose, and contain scattered information across multiple speakers' utterances. This paper introduces a multi-view sequence-to-sequence model that extracts conversational structures from unstructured daily chats, utilizing these structures to generate more coherent dialogue summaries.

The authors highlight the limitations of current summarization models that do not account for the unique characteristics of dialogue, such as informal language, interruptions, and the dynamic organization of utterances. They argue for the necessity of developing new methods specifically designed for dialogue summarization, rather than merely adapting existing document summarization techniques.

Experimental results on a large-scale dialogue summarization corpus show that their proposed methods significantly outperform previous state-of-the-art models, as evidenced by both automatic evaluations and human judgment. The paper also discusses the challenges faced by current approaches in this domain, emphasizing the importance of understanding conversational structures in effective dialogue summarization. The authors have made their code publicly available for further research and development in this area.
