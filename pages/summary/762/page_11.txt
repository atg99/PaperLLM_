The cited works primarily focus on advancements in spoken dialogue comprehension and summarization techniques using various neural network architectures and models. Liu and Chen (2019) introduced a hierarchical attention architecture aimed at improving comprehension in spoken dialogues. Their subsequent work (2019b) explored topic-aware pointer-generator networks specifically for summarizing spoken conversations.

Mayfield et al. (2012) contributed to the understanding of conversation structures in multi-party chats, while Nallapati et al. (2016) presented a sequence-to-sequence model for abstractive text summarization. Nikolov et al. (2018) emphasized data-driven approaches to summarizing scientific articles, and Oya and Carenini (2014) proposed an integrated probabilistic method for summarizing email threads.

Paul (2012) utilized mixed membership Markov models for unsupervised conversation modeling, whereas Paulus et al. (2018) developed a deep reinforced model for abstractive summarization. Radford et al. (2019) and Raffel et al. (2019) discussed the potential of large language models for multitask learning and transfer learning, respectively.

The techniques for summarization also include the use of sentence embeddings (Reimers and Gurevych, 2019) and attention mechanisms (Rush et al., 2015; See et al., 2017). Sacks et al. (1978) provided foundational insights into conversational turn-taking, which remains relevant in understanding dialogue interactions.

Recent advancements by Shang et al. (2018) and Xiao and Carenini (2019) highlight unsupervised methods for summarizing meetings and long documents by leveraging both global and local contexts. Zhang et al. (2019) focused on generating character descriptions for fiction summarization, while Zhao et al. (2019) introduced a hierarchical adaptive network for summarizing meetings. Overall, these studies reflect a diverse range of methodologies aimed at enhancing dialogue comprehension and summarization capabilities.
