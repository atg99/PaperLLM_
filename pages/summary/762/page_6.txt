The paper presents a detailed evaluation of various models for summarizing conversations using the SAMSum dataset, focusing on the effectiveness of different view combinations in improving ROUGE scores. The dataset statistics reveal a consistent structure across training, development, and test sets, with conversations averaging around 2.4 turns and lengths varying significantly.

The models evaluated include Pointer Generator, DynamicConv combined with GPT-2 and News, and various configurations of BART. The results show that BART, particularly with the addition of global and structured views, significantly outperforms earlier models. The introduction of reinforcement learning in the Fast Abs RL Enhanced model provides a slight increase in F scores, while pre-trained embeddings enhance performance further.

Key findings indicate that segmenting conversations into structured views (stage and topic) effectively captures nuanced information, leading to improved summarization outcomes. However, combining the global view with structured views did not yield performance improvements, suggesting that the global view's coarse granularity may not complement the detailed structured views effectively.

Overall, the study emphasizes the importance of utilizing structured views to synthesize informative conversation blocks, thereby enhancing the summarization process. The attention weight distributions visualization further supports the analysis of how different models prioritize information during summarization.
