This paper investigates the relationship between challenges in conversation summarization and the errors produced by a multi-view sequence-to-sequence model designed for this task. The analysis reveals several key findings: the model performs effectively in summarizing simple conversations, but struggles with various challenges that often result in missing information errors. Errors related to incorrect references are particularly tied to referral and coreference issues, which complicate the model's ability to correctly associate speakers with their actions. Additionally, interactions involving multiple participants and frequent co-references contribute to incorrect reasoning by the model.

The authors propose a novel multi-view sequence-to-sequence model that integrates various conversational structures, including topic and stage views, to enhance summary generation. Their experimental results demonstrate the model's effectiveness through both quantitative and qualitative evaluations. They also identify specific challenges faced by current models, which could guide future research in conversation summarization.

The study acknowledges limitations such as the absence of annotated data, which led to the use of unsupervised segmentation methods. Future work aims to incorporate annotated data, explore supervised segmentation models, and introduce additional conversational structures like dialogue acts to improve abstractive dialogue summarization.
