This paper presents an analysis of the Mogriﬁer, a model that consistently outperforms traditional LSTM architectures in language modeling tasks. The authors conducted an ablation study on the Penn Treebank (PTB) dataset, revealing that the Mogriﬁer achieves a perplexity score of 54.1 with 24 million parameters, outperforming the LSTM's score of 57.5 and the multiplicative LSTM (mLSTM) at 57.8. The study highlights the significance of hyperparameter tuning, particularly the number of rounds, which greatly influences performance.

The comparison with Transformer-XL indicates that while it performs comparably to LSTM on PTB, the Mogriﬁer holds a notable advantage. The authors emphasize that despite the complexity of the Mogriﬁer’s architecture, its gating mechanisms, conditioned on original inputs, do not complicate the model unnecessarily. The "no-zigzag" variant of the Mogriﬁer was tested but showed inferior performance compared to the full model, suggesting that the iterative refinement process is crucial for enhancing gating capabilities.

Overall, the paper underscores the effectiveness of the Mogriﬁer in language modeling, presenting its performance metrics and the impact of hyperparameter choices while positioning it against existing state-of-the-art models.
