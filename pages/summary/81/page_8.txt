The paper discusses the Mogriﬁer LSTM, an enhanced version of the LSTM architecture that demonstrates superior performance on various language modeling tasks. The authors explore several hypotheses to explain the advantages of the Mogriﬁer and conduct experiments to rule out each one. 

Key findings include:

1. The enhancement is not due to scaling of input and previous hidden state, as adding a learnable scaling factor showed no improvement, especially at very low-rank settings.
2. Optimization difficulty is unlikely to be the primary factor, as various optimizers and normalization techniques did not affect the performance gap between Mogriﬁer and LSTM.
3. The advantage does not stem from the flexibility in embedding tying, as untied embeddings also performed well.
4. Low-rank factorization of weight matrices does not explain the benefits, as full-rank Mogriﬁer outperformed plain LSTM, and factorized gate matrices showed no improvement.
5. Performance on rare words is not a significant factor, as experiments with character-based models showed similar advantages regardless of word frequency.
6. The benefits are not limited to the English language, as demonstrated by experiments in Finnish.
7. Long-range dependency handling does not account for the improvements, as similar performance gaps were observed in both episodic and non-episodic settings.
8. The hypothesis regarding input scaling leading to gate saturation was contradicted by observations of gate variance.

The authors conclude that conditioning input embeddings on recurrent states likely contributes to the Mogriﬁer's success, positioning it within the Multiplicative RNN framework. They suggest further exploration of related architectures and parameterization methods for future research.
