The paper presents the Mogrifier LSTM, an enhancement of the Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) model, aimed at improving generalization in Natural Language Processing (NLP). The authors, Gábor Melis, Tomáš Kociský, and Phil Blunsom, argue that existing recurrent networks struggle with generalization and systematic representation of language. The Mogrifier LSTM introduces a mutual gating mechanism between the current input and the previous output, allowing for a more nuanced interaction between inputs and their context. 

This design makes the LSTM's transition function context-dependent, which the authors demonstrate leads to significant improvements in language modeling performance. The experiments show a reduction in perplexity by 3–4 points on the Penn Treebank and Wikitext-2 datasets, with further enhancements in character-based datasets. The Mogrifier LSTM sets a new state-of-the-art performance on these datasets and notably reduces the performance gap between LSTMs and Transformer models on Enwik8.

The authors emphasize that while neural models have advanced NLP tasks, they still face challenges with generalization and understanding of grammar. By proposing this new architecture, the paper contributes to the ongoing evolution of recurrent neural networks, which have been foundational in various NLP applications. The findings suggest that the introduction of gating structures can lead to richer representations and improved performance in language modeling tasks.
