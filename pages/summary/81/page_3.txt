This paper, presented at ICLR 2020, discusses advancements in sequence modeling, particularly through the introduction of a new architecture that builds upon LSTM principles. The proposed method incorporates hyperparameter tuning, specifically the number of rounds, r, which when set to zero, reverts to the LSTM framework. The architecture employs factorization of transformation matrices into low-rank products to minimize additional parameters, enhancing efficiency.

A significant focus of the paper is on the optimization and scalability of models, particularly in relation to smaller-scale datasets. The authors argue that the historical challenges associated with model design and optimization are intertwined, as exemplified by the difficulties of Elman Networks. They contend that while current models can be scaled up to handle larger datasets, this approach does not adequately address generalization issues. Instead, they advocate for exploring smaller datasets, which can yield clearer insights into model generalization without the confounding effects of extensive domain adaptation.

The experiments conducted utilize both word-level and character-level datasets, including the Penn Treebank and Wikitext-2 for word-level tasks, and Enwik8 for character-level tasks. The choice of smaller datasets is intended to facilitate a better understanding of model biases while avoiding complications associated with larger vocabularies. The paper emphasizes that while evaluations on larger datasets are included for practical relevance, they come with increased uncertainty and limitations in guiding future research on model improvements.
