This document is a list of references from various papers and preprints related to advancements in neural language modeling, reading comprehension systems, and recurrent neural networks. 

Key contributions include:

- Jia and Liang's work on using adversarial examples for evaluating reading comprehension systems, highlighting the robustness of such systems.
- Kawakami et al. propose a method for creating and reusing words in open-vocabulary neural language models, advancing the flexibility of language models.
- Kingma and Ba introduce the Adam optimizer, a widely adopted method for stochastic optimization in machine learning.
- Krause et al. explore multiplicative LSTMs for sequence modeling and present dynamic evaluation methods for neural sequence models and transformer language models, contributing to improving model performance.
- Linzen et al. assess LSTMs' ability to learn syntax-sensitive dependencies, providing insights into their capabilities.
- The Penn Treebank, as detailed by Marcus et al., remains a foundational resource for annotated linguistic data.
- Mayer et al. describe a robotic system for heart surgery that employs recurrent neural networks, showcasing practical applications of RNNs.
- Melis et al. discuss the state of evaluation in neural language models and propose advancements in dropout techniques.
- Merity and colleagues analyze LSTM language models and their optimization, contributing to the understanding of neural language modeling at multiple scales.
- Mikolov et al. present a recurrent neural network-based language model, which has influenced subsequent research.
- Moosavi and Strube caution the use of lexical features in coreference resolution, indicating potential pitfalls in their application.
- Peters et al. introduce deep contextualized word representations, enhancing the understanding of word meanings in context.
- Press and Wolf demonstrate improvements in language models through the use of output embeddings.
- Sak et al. focus on LSTM architectures for large vocabulary speech recognition, emphasizing the effectiveness of RNNs in this domain.

The references collectively represent significant progress in the fields of natural language processing and machine learning, illustrating various methodologies, applications, and evaluations of neural network architectures.
