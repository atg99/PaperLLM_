This paper presents the Mogriﬁer, a novel architecture that enhances the capabilities of Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks by allowing for a more flexible interaction between input embeddings and the recurrent state. The authors conducted experiments on various tasks, particularly focusing on the reverse copy task and the Enwik8 dataset, to demonstrate the effectiveness of the Mogriﬁer compared to traditional LSTMs and the mLSTM variant.

In the reverse copy task, the Mogriﬁer outperformed LSTMs, especially at longer sequence lengths, showcasing its ability to effectively amplify salient features while attenuating nuisance ones. The experiments revealed that both the Mogriﬁer and LSTM could solve shorter sequences perfectly, but the Mogriﬁer displayed a significant advantage as sequence length increased. This was attributed to its capability to operate with smaller embedding sizes, aligning with the hypothesis that the Mogriﬁer’s two-way interaction mechanism provides enhanced flexibility.

The paper also revisits the claims made by Krause et al. regarding the mLSTM, specifically the recovery hypothesis, and finds no significant performance improvement of the mLSTM over the LSTM in several experiments. In contrast, the Mogriﬁer consistently outperformed the mLSTM, demonstrating its robustness and effectiveness in handling complex tasks.

Overall, the findings suggest that the Mogriﬁer is a promising alternative to LSTMs and mLSTMs, particularly in scenarios demanding longer sequence processing and more nuanced feature interaction.
