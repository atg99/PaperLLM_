This paper presents the performance of the Mogrifer model compared to various state-of-the-art models on word-level datasets, specifically the Penn Treebank (PTB) and Wikitext-2. The results indicate that the Mogrifer consistently achieves lower perplexity scores than the LSTM baseline, with improvements of 3 to 4 perplexity points across tests. The paper includes a comprehensive table showcasing various models, including ENFRAGE and AWD-LSTM, highlighting their respective perplexity scores on validation and test sets.

The experimental setup involved tuning hyperparameters using a Gaussian Process Bandit approach, with specific parameters being adjusted for both LSTM and Mogrifer models. The training utilized different batch sizes and backpropagation through time (BPTT) window sizes, depending on whether the task was word-level or character-level. The optimization was carried out using the Adam optimizer, with gradient clipping applied.

Additionally, the paper discusses model evaluation methods, noting the use of standard dropout and Monte-Carlo dropout techniques. The optimal softmax temperature was determined via validation sets, and results were reported for both dynamic evaluation scenarios and standard conditions. The findings emphasize the Mogrifer's competitive edge over traditional LSTM models, reinforcing its potential for improved performance in language modeling tasks. The authors also provide access to their code and hyperparameter tuning outputs for further exploration.
