The paper introduces a framework for classifying driving behaviors using a combination of speed and steering categories, denoted as B = (Bsp, Bst), where Bsp represents various speed levels and Bst indicates steering directions. This framework serves as a foundational approach for studying driving behaviors with Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and allows for integration of more complex maneuvers such as lane changes and overtaking.

The authors present two datasets, DriveLM-nuScenes and DriveLM-CARLA, aimed at enhancing the quality of question-answer pairs (QAs) related to driving scenarios. The DriveLM-nuScenes dataset is created through a meticulous three-step annotation process involving key frame selection, object identification, and frame-level QA annotation, utilizing a combination of automated and expert-generated questions. The quality of annotations is ensured through rigorous checks, making this dataset notable for its scale and complexity.

In contrast, the DriveLM-CARLA dataset is collected using the CARLA simulator within the updated Leaderboard 2.0 framework. The authors note challenges in data collection due to the lack of scalable methods in this new environment. They propose a new expert algorithm, PDM-Lite, designed to navigate these challenges effectively. Overall, the paper highlights the importance of comprehensive datasets for understanding autonomous driving processes, emphasizing rigorous quality control in the annotation phase.
