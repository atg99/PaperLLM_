The authors present an annotation pipeline for the DriveLM-nuScenes dataset that incorporates both ground truth annotations and human feedback to ensure high data quality. This semi-rule-based QA labeling pipeline is complemented by a multi-round quality check process aimed at maintaining consistency and accuracy in data collection. Similarly, the DriveLM-CARLA dataset utilizes a fully rule-based QA labeling approach leveraging a new expert algorithm named PDM-Lite.

Central to the DriveLM framework is the Generalized Visual Question Answering (GVQA) task, which reflects how human drivers make decisions by identifying and localizing key objects, predicting their future actions, and planning accordingly. The paper introduces a graph-structured representation of question-answer pairs, which allows for logical dependencies between questions. Each question-answer pair is treated as a vertex in a directed acyclic graph, where edges denote relationships between questions, enabling a more structured reasoning process.

The authors emphasize the importance of this approach in mimicking human decision-making, which could lead to advancements in the integration of language models with autonomous driving technologies. To foster further research, they plan to establish an official evaluation server with a public leaderboard to benchmark various methods and gather insights into the combination of language models and autonomous driving systems.
