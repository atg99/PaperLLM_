The paper discusses the performance metrics used for the motion stage of the DriveLM model, specifically focusing on standard metrics from the nuScenes and Waymo benchmarks, namely Average Displacement Error (ADE), Final Displacement Error (FDE), and the collision rate of predicted trajectories. ADE measures the average L2 distance between predicted and ground truth trajectories over multiple time steps, while FDE assesses the Euclidean distance between the predicted and true endpoints at the last predicted time step. The collision rate indicates the proportion of test frames where the predicted trajectory collides with objects.

The paper clarifies that the calculation methods for ADE, FDE, and collision rate are aligned with the UniAD benchmark, differing from the ST-P3 benchmark in how FDE and collision rates are computed over time. It also emphasizes that results from the full nuScenes validation dataset are not directly comparable to those from the DriveLM-nuScenes validation split, which focuses on keyframes with intention changes.

Additionally, the paper introduces the DriveLM-Agent, detailing its graph prompting scheme and trajectory tokenization process. During training, the model utilizes a teacher-forcing approach, where child questions are combined with the ground truth parent questions to form context. The training objective is next token prediction. In inference, the model operates interactively across multiple rounds, sequentially prompting with questions to derive context predictions needed for subsequent child questions.
