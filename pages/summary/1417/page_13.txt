This paper presents DriveLM, a novel approach that leverages Graph Visual Question Answering (VQA) to enhance decision-making in autonomous driving scenarios. The analysis reveals that incorporating prediction and planning stages into behavior questions significantly improves performance compared to relying solely on perception data. Specifically, the DriveLM model demonstrates superior accuracy and steering capabilities across various question types and contexts.

Table 3 illustrates the effectiveness of different stages in the question-answering process, showing that as more stages are included, the accuracy and performance metrics such as speed and steering improve. The results indicate that DriveLM-Agent outperforms the zero-shot BLIP-2 model across various contexts, with the graph-based structure facilitating step-wise reasoning that enhances overall performance.

In the related work section, the paper discusses the challenges of generalization in autonomous driving, particularly concerning corner cases, and emphasizes the need for improved zero-shot performance. Previous research has focused on data-driven methods and semantic supervision to address these issues. The authors propose that integrating logical reasoning through Graph VQA offers a promising solution for better generalization in autonomous driving systems.

Overall, the findings underscore the potential of DriveLM in advancing the capabilities of autonomous driving technologies by improving reasoning and understanding through graph-based methodologies.
