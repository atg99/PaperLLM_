The study conducted by C. Sima et al. focuses on creating a comprehensive dataset for autonomous driving evaluation within a simulated environment, utilizing various towns with diverse landscapes. The dataset comprises 90 training routes covering 780.6 kilometers and 20 evaluation routes spanning 247.6 kilometers, with routes segmented into 150-meter lengths. The traffic manager in the simulation generates random traffic composed of various vehicle types, including cars, trucks, vans, bicycles, and motorcycles. Weather conditions are randomized, although night-time scenarios are excluded due to poor visibility affecting data labeling accuracy.

Data collection involves executing expert maneuvers on each route to gather extensive sensor data, including RGB images, LiDAR point clouds, semantic segmentation images, depth maps, and Bird’s Eye View semantic segmentation. While the DriveLM-Agent uses only RGB images, the retraining of TransFuser++ requires additional data for auxiliary tasks. The study extracts detailed privileged information about the ego vehicle, other vehicles, pedestrians, traffic lights, stop signs, static cars, landmarks, and weather parameters. 

Additionally, the researchers create linguistic labels by forming questions and answers from the extracted simulator data using handcrafted sentence templates. To enhance linguistic diversity and avoid overfitting, future work may incorporate advanced language models like GPT-4, but this particular study utilizes a non-augmented version of the dataset.
