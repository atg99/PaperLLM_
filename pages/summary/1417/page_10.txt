In this paper, C. Sima et al. investigate the performance of Vision Language Models (VLMs) in open-loop planning for end-to-end driving, specifically using the DriveLM-nuScenes dataset. The authors implement fine-tuning with LoRA and conduct experiments to evaluate the impact of contextual information on vehicle trajectory prediction.

The training process involves fine-tuning BLIP-2 on specific datasets with defined epochs and GPU resources. For their experiments, the authors establish a baseline using a simple Command Mean approach and compare the performance of their models against the state-of-the-art method, UniAD. They also introduce variations of their proposed model, DriveLM-Agent, which includes a two-stage prediction process for behavior and motion, with different configurations regarding context utilization.

Results indicate that while the baseline BLIP-RT-2 struggles to match the performance of UniAD-Single, the DriveLM-Agent demonstrates a significant performance improvement, surpassing UniAD-Single when utilizing an intermediate behavior prediction step. However, the Chain and Graph variations of DriveLM-Agent do not show additional benefits in scenarios lacking generalization. The findings suggest that VLMs may require video inputs to effectively compete with models like UniAD that leverage video data. Further results regarding video-input VLMs are included in supplementary materials.
