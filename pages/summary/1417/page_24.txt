Q6 discusses the balance between inference time and generalization in large language models (LLMs), noting recent advancements that have significantly improved speed without sacrificing performance. The authors acknowledge the runtime limitations of their system DriveLM but express optimism that ongoing research will address these issues.

Q7 emphasizes the suitability of visual question answering (VQA) for training internet-scale models for autonomous driving. It highlights the reasoning capabilities of vision-language models (VLMs), which may enhance their performance in perception and planning tasks essential for driving.

Q8 raises the question of whether current VLMs possess the same understanding and reasoning capabilities about the visual world as LLMs do for text. The authors suggest that further exploration is needed to assess this capability.

Q9 addresses the limited improvements observed from the proposed graph reasoning scheme in VQA. Factors such as the simplicity of the prompting scheme, the scale of the VLMs, and the logical dependencies within the dataset may be contributing to this limitation. The DriveLM-CARLA platform will facilitate further investigation into these issues.

Q10 highlights the importance of formulating the right questions when developing the DriveLM system. The authors acknowledge the need for domain expertise to refine their approach and plan to provide more detailed protocols in future research.

Q11 questions the perception that generalization in autonomous driving is overstated. While many driving scenarios involve common objects, the authors stress that the ability to generalize to rare objects is vital for safety in commercial applications. They note that this remains a significant research challenge, underscoring the importance of addressing the long tail of rare objects in autonomous driving systems.
