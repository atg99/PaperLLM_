The paper presents DriveLM, an innovative approach to autonomous driving that leverages Graph Visual Question Answering (GVQA) to enhance decision-making in driving scenarios. The algorithm's design allows for flexibility in the size and structure of the inference graph, enabling adaptation based on specific tasks or computational resources. During inference, the model samples questions from subgraphs using heuristics while training on all available questions and answers.

A central concept is the aggregation of context through behavior, where diverse driving situations are distilled into discrete behaviors. The model generates natural language descriptions that communicate the vehicle's intended movements, encapsulating critical information from the graph. This comprehensive context is derived from all relevant questions and answers, underscoring its importance for effective driving.

To facilitate motion, the paper introduces trajectory tokenization, inspired by the RT-2 framework, which allows DriveLM-Agent to process images and behavior descriptions to generate trajectories. Waypoint coordinates are divided into bins, creating a refined vocabulary that is used to train the model for motion tasks, maintaining the same architecture but with independent fine-tuning.

The experimental section aims to explore several research questions, including the repurposing of Visual Language Models (VLMs) for autonomous driving, their generalization capabilities with unseen sensor setups, and the impact of various questions on perception, prediction, and planning. The results and additional details regarding the experimental setup are provided in the supplementary material, focusing on the performance of VLMs in the contexts of perception, prediction, and planning through GVQA.
