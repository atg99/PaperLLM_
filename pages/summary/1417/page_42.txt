The paper discusses the architecture and methodology of the DriveLM-Agent, particularly focusing on trajectory tokenization for generating action sequences representing ego-vehicle future trajectories. The authors use the RT-2 method for discretizing and tokenizing continuous trajectory data from the nuScenes dataset. To achieve this, they divide each (x, y) coordinate axis into 256 discrete intervals, allowing for a detailed yet manageable number of tokens for the language model.

Each discretized interval is mapped to a unique token within the model's vocabulary, while single-digit tokens are excluded to maintain coherence in representing numerical values. A total of 256 numeric token IDs are selected, along with two special tokens to indicate the start and end of a trajectory sequence. This tokenization approach enables the encoding of complex trajectory information into a sequence that the language model can process, facilitating the generation of predicted future trajectories by converting output tokens back into the coordinate space.

The paper also outlines the experimental section, which includes implementation details, additional metrics on the Visual Question Answering (VQA) component, and further analysis of computational complexity through ablation studies and comparisons.
