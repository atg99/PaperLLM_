The paper presents qualitative results from the generalization test set in DriveLM-CARLA, focusing on the behavior of the DriveLM-Agent in pedestrian scenarios. It illustrates three examples: the first demonstrates a successful interaction, while the second and third highlight typical failure cases encountered by the model. The findings emphasize the model's ability to handle various pedestrian situations, shedding light on both its strengths and weaknesses in real-world applications. The analysis of these scenarios provides insights into the predictive capabilities and limitations of the DriveLM-Agent in dynamic environments.
