The paper "DriveLM: Driving with Graph Visual Question Answering" discusses the potential advantages of using Vision-Language Models (VLMs) in autonomous driving scenarios. 

Key points include:

1. **Planning with VLMs vs. Conventional Methods**: VLMs are expected to outperform traditional end-to-end driving systems in novel scenarios due to their extensive pre-training on diverse datasets, enhancing their reasoning capabilities and generalization to rare driving situations.

2. **Adaptation of General VLMs**: Utilizing general VLMs, which have been trained on vast amounts of vision-language data, allows for effective adaptation to the driving domain through fine-tuning on smaller datasets. In contrast, driving-specific models, which rely on limited data, face challenges in integrating language inputs from broader contexts.

3. **Open-Loop Evaluation Challenges**: The paper addresses the limitations of open-loop evaluations in planning, where ego history can skew results. DriveLM mitigates this by focusing on keyframes where vehicle intentions change, ensuring that evaluations are more indicative of future behaviors.

4. **Closed-Loop Planning on CARLA**: The authors note the absence of closed-loop planning results on CARLA due to the technical demands of running large models at high frame rates. They suggest potential solutions, such as model distillation and executing certain stages at different frame rates.

5. **Efficiency of DriveLM-Agent**: While DriveLM-Agent is initially slower than other models like UniAD, proposed optimizations could enhance its runtime efficiency, making it more viable for real-world autonomous driving applications.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the promise of VLMs in enhancing the adaptability and performance of autonomous driving systems while addressing practical challenges in implementation and evaluation.
