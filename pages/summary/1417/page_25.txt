The paper discusses the challenges and advancements in the field of autonomous driving (AD) with a focus on the DriveLM-nuScenes dataset. It highlights the importance of generalization in AD and addresses the limitations of using multi-frame video inputs, which can lead to causal confusion, thereby advocating for the use of single-frame inputs in many cases. However, the authors acknowledge the potential benefits of multi-frame models, presenting preliminary results that suggest slight improvements with their use.

The technical novelty of the paper lies not in proposing a new algorithm but in the formulation of a standardized task and a comprehensive dataset that can facilitate the adaptation of general vision-language models (VLMs) for driving applications. The DriveLM-nuScenes dataset includes 4,072 training frames and 799 validation frames, featuring scene-level descriptions and frame-level question-answering (QA) tasks, categorized into perception, prediction, and planning. 

Perception queries involve detailed examinations of frames, while prediction questions focus on anticipating the future states of objects in the scene. The authors emphasize that while some answers are manually annotated, they also employ prompt design to generate insightful questions based on existing datasets. Overall, the paper aims to fill a gap in the driving with language domain by providing a robust dataset and evaluation framework, which is essential for advancing research in this area.
