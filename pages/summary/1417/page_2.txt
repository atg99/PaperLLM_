This paper introduces a novel task called Graph VQA, which utilizes graph-structured reasoning through perception, prediction, and planning question-answer pairs to better emulate the human reasoning process. The authors present two key contributions: the creation of datasets named DriveLM-Data based on nuScenes and CARLA, and the development of a baseline model, DriveLM-Agent, which integrates Graph VQA with end-to-end autonomous driving capabilities.

Experimental results indicate that Graph VQA provides an effective framework for understanding driving scenes, while DriveLM-Data serves as a challenging benchmark for evaluating performance. The DriveLM-Agent demonstrates competitive end-to-end autonomous driving performance compared to state-of-the-art architectures, particularly excelling in zero-shot evaluations on novel sensor configurations. An ablation study reveals that the performance improvements stem from the comprehensive annotations of prediction and planning QA pairs within the graph structure. All associated data, models, and an evaluation server are made publicly available, facilitating further research in this area.
