This section expands on related work in reasoning over graph structures, embodied planning with large language models (LLMs), and vision-language benchmarks for autonomous driving (AD). 

In reasoning over graph structures, the paper highlights the foundational role of reasoning in human thought, with various prior works utilizing graph-based approaches. Notable examples include XNMs which use scene graphs for explainable reasoning, KPRN which employs knowledge graphs for recommender systems, and GoT which models information as graphs to mimic brain mechanisms. The authors aim to connect perception, prediction, and planning in AD through graph structures, facilitating reasoning and scenario deduction.

Regarding embodied planning with LLMs, recent advancements leverage LLMs' reasoning capabilities for AI systems. Examples such as PaLM-E, CaP, and RT-2 demonstrate the potential of LLMs in robotic manipulation and action representation. This inspired the authors to address generalization challenges in AD, an area that remains underexplored.

The discussion on vision-language benchmarks for driving reveals a growing number of datasets aimed at enhancing AD systems. While datasets like NuScenes-QA and BDD-X provide perceptual information and rationales for vehicle actions, they often focus on scene-level contexts. The introduction of DriveLM addresses this limitation by structuring language annotations at both object and task levels within a graph framework.

The authors believe that integrating vision-language models (VLMs) into AD can significantly propel advancements in the field. While acknowledging potential ethical concerns such as hallucinations and resource demands, they emphasize the potential for improved human-interaction with AD systems, which could enhance public confidence and lead to safer transportation outcomes in the future.
