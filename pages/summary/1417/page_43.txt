The paper outlines the implementation details for the DriveLM model used in driving with Graph Visual Question Answering (GVQA). The fine-tuning process employs a learning rate of 0.0001 without a learning rate scheduler, utilizing a random seed of 1234, and adheres to the default settings of the LoRA configuration. For the BLIP-2 model, a maximum sequence length of 400 is maintained.

In the experiments detailed in Sections 4.1, 4.3, and 4.4, the training incorporates all question-answer pairs (QAs) per frame, with certain QAs having context derived from ground truth. The inference process accounts for the imbalanced nature of QAs across the dataset, particularly in the DriveLM-nuScenes, by computing GVQA Scores on a selected subset of QAs. This subset is determined using specific QA patterns for each stage to ensure representation across validation frames.

For the experiments in Section 4.2, the training follows the same methodology, while inference on the Waymo dataset involves generating questions based on general-purpose queries from the DriveLM-nuScenes, leveraging any matched objects from the annotations.

In the generalization experiment discussed in Section F.2, two additional questions are introduced to the graph to assess the presence of pedestrians and determine the vehicle's actions accordingly. These answers are integrated into the final behavior query context. Examples of graph structures from various sections are provided for clarity.
