The paper evaluates the generalization capabilities of the DriveLM-Agent in the DriveLM-CARLA environment, particularly focusing on its performance with the introduction of a new pedestrian object in a novel test set, DriveLM-CARLA-ped. Results indicate that both DriveLM-Agent and TransFuser++ significantly underperform on the new pedestrian dataset compared to the original DriveLM-CARLA dataset. 

TransFuser++, which utilizes advanced inputs such as LiDAR and additional annotations, sees its accuracy plummet from 70.19 to 8.72 when tested on DriveLM-CARLA-ped. Similarly, DriveLM-Agent's accuracy drops from 59.63 to 4.59 under the same conditions. However, the inclusion of a pedestrian-specific question in the GVQA graph for DriveLM-Agent leads to a notable improvement in performance, raising its accuracy to 27.04. This enhancement highlights the potential benefits of integrating general computer vision data during training, which contrasts with TransFuser++'s limited training scope.

The findings underscore the challenges of zero-shot generalization in driving scenarios, particularly with unseen objects, while also demonstrating the effectiveness of incorporating additional contextual questions to improve decision-making in complex environments. In a privileged setting where ground truth data is provided, DriveLM-Agent achieves near-perfect performance, suggesting that access to accurate contextual information can significantly boost the model's capabilities.
