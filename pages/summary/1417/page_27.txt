The paper presents the DriveLM framework, which focuses on driving scenarios using a graph-based visual question answering approach. The annotation process for the DriveLM-nuScenes dataset consists of three main steps: labeling the ego vehicle's behavior, selecting key objects, and generating question-answer pairs.

In the first step, annotators label the behavior of the ego vehicle throughout video clips to create scene-level descriptions. The second step involves identifying key objects in key frames that are relevant to the vehicle's driving, utilizing pre-annotated bounding boxes based on the nuScenes dataset while allowing for the inclusion of additional significant objects.

The third step focuses on QA labeling, where two types of questions are used: factual and open-ended. Factual questions are answered using a rule-based method, while open-ended questions are manually annotated, with options provided for flexibility. The process also includes free-form questions generated by annotators.

Quality is a priority throughout the annotation process. Clear criteria are established for each step, complemented by rigorous manual quality checks. Data is organized into batches, and if manual annotations fall short of quality expectations, feedback is provided for re-annotation. The final step involves translating the labeled data from Chinese to English to ensure accessibility.

Overall, the paper outlines a structured methodology for annotating driving-related video data, emphasizing the importance of quality control and comprehensive data generation for effective visual question answering.
