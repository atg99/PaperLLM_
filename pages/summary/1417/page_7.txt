The paper presents DriveLM, a system for driving with Graph Visual Question Answering (GVQA), which builds on existing methods like PDM-Closed. DriveLM employs PDM-Lite, a lightweight planner that utilizes the Intelligent Driver Model (IDM) to determine target speeds based on various objects in the environment. Unlike PDM-Closed, which considers multiple proposals through a complex cost function, DriveLM simplifies this by using only two proposals and a more straightforward cost function based on the TransFuser++ expert, resulting in a 44% improvement in decision success on CARLA validation routes.

To create the DriveLM-CARLA dataset, a variety of routes across urban, residential, and rural settings were established, and PDM-Lite was executed to gather sensor data and generate relevant questions and answers (QAs). This process involved extracting privileged information about the scene and its objects, which was then used to form logical relationships and structure the QAs into a graph. The dataset includes 1.6 million QAs, making it the largest driving-language benchmark available.

DriveLM-Metrics are designed to evaluate the performance of the system across three main components: motion (M), behavior (B), and performance metrics P1-3. Motion performance is assessed using standard metrics from nuScenes and Waymo benchmarks, including average and final displacement errors and collision rates. Behavior predictions are evaluated based on classification accuracy, with a focus on steering and speed. For performance metrics, SPICE is used to measure structural similarity between predicted and ground truth texts, while GPT Score assesses semantic alignment, providing a comprehensive evaluation of the system's capabilities.
