The paper presents a study on zero-shot generalization across different sensor configurations, focusing on the performance of various methods in behavior and motion tasks. The results indicate a notable correlation between the accuracy of the behavior task and the performance of the motion task. Specifically, the DriveLM-Agent demonstrates strong results, particularly when using ground truth behavior as context, achieving the highest accuracy and efficiency metrics among the methods tested.

The analysis includes performance metrics of several models, such as Command Mean, UniAD-Single, BLIP-RT-2, and various configurations of DriveLM-Agent. The findings suggest that while improving question-answering performance is challenging, refining the question setup could enhance the model's capability to understand scenes and execute tasks effectively.

Additionally, the paper discusses computational complexity, comparing DriveLM-Agent to UniAD-Single and suggesting future improvements such as caching vision tokens and batching question patterns to optimize inference times. Qualitative results are also presented, showcasing the effectiveness of the experiments conducted on DriveLM-nuScenes, Waymo, and DriveLM-CARLA. Overall, the study emphasizes the importance of behavior task accuracy in enhancing motion task performance and highlights potential avenues for further research.
