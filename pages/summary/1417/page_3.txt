The paper presents DriveLM, a novel approach to autonomous driving that utilizes Graph Visual Question Answering (GVQA) to mimic human reasoning processes. Unlike existing Visual Question Answering (VQA) tasks, which focus on single objects or scene-level reasoning, GVQA organizes the reasoning process into a directed graph of question-answer pairs, incorporating logical dependencies that enhance the answering process. This method is particularly relevant for understanding driving behavior and motion planning.

DriveLM introduces two datasets, DriveLM-nuScenes and DriveLM-CARLA, which feature annotated question-answer pairs linked to images of driving scenarios. These datasets offer more extensive text annotations compared to current benchmarks and include a rule-based expert algorithm, PDM-Lite, for evaluating driving performance in simulated environments.

The baseline model, DriveLM-Agent, employs a trajectory tokenizer and a graph prompting scheme to effectively adapt existing Visual Language Models (VLMs) for autonomous driving. Experimental results indicate that while GVQA is a challenging task, DriveLM-Agent performs competitively with state-of-the-art driving models and demonstrates improved zero-shot generalization capabilities. Specifically, it manages to generalize well to unseen data from Waymo after being trained on nuScenes data.

The research suggests that enhancing GVQA could significantly advance the development of autonomous driving agents, particularly in achieving strong generalization across diverse scenarios. The findings underscore the importance of logical dependencies in improving question-answering performance, ultimately contributing to more robust autonomous driving systems.
