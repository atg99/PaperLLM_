DriveLM introduces a novel approach to driving with a focus on Graph Visual Question Answering (VQA). The paper presents a comparison of the DriveLM dataset with existing datasets, highlighting significant advancements in annotation quantity, comprehensiveness, and logical structuring. DriveLM features two variants: DriveLM-nuScenes and DriveLM-CARLA, both of which outperform previous datasets in terms of average questions per frame and the breadth of annotated categories, including perception, prediction, and planning.

The dataset builds a logical edge set that incorporates object-level and task-level interactions. Object-level edges represent the relationships between different entities in a scene, while task-level edges illustrate the reasoning stages involved in driving tasks. These stages include perception (identifying and localizing objects), prediction (estimating actions of objects), planning (determining safe actions for the ego vehicle), behavior classification, and motion trajectory prediction.

The method defines the behavior representation as a bridge between perception, prediction, and planning stages to the future trajectory of the vehicle. The trajectory is represented as a series of points in a bird’s-eye view format, with calculations determining the vehicle's future positions based on current data. The behavior representation is categorized into predefined bins for speed and steering, allowing for a structured approach to driving decision-making. Overall, DriveLM aims to enhance the understanding and execution of autonomous driving tasks through advanced data annotation and logical reasoning frameworks.
