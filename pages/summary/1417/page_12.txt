The analysis reveals that incorporating question-answer (QA) pairs significantly enhances the behavior performance of interactive driving systems, particularly during the prediction and planning stages. The selected QA pairs represent critical information relevant to driving, making up about 60% of the total pairs across three stages.

Specifically, the study identifies key representative questions that address the important objects in the scene, the moving status of objects, future states, and potential actions for the ego vehicle. Training with QA pairs from the prediction and planning stages yields better results compared to using perception QA pairs alone. However, the addition of planning stage QA pairs does not lead to further significant performance improvements, suggesting that the future status of other vehicles already encompasses the necessary information for decision-making.

In the final experiment, baseline results for the P1-3 stages of GVQA highlight the influence of context on performance. The evaluation includes comparisons between a standard VQA approach without context and a GVQA approach that utilizes ground truth context. Results indicate that the DriveLM-nuScenes presents a greater challenge for the models, as evidenced by lower performance scores. Overall, the findings emphasize the importance of context in enhancing the efficacy of interactive driving systems through enriched QA pair training.
