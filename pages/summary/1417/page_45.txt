The paper presents DriveLM, a model that enhances visual question answering (VQA) through graph-structured reasoning. The results indicate that the use of graph structures improves VQA performance, as evidenced by various metrics. The study highlights that traditional metrics like BLEU-4, METEOR, CIDEr, ROUGE-L, and SPICE do not consistently reflect performance improvements, prompting the use of the GPT score as a more comprehensive evaluation metric.

Table 12 shows the performance of DriveLM compared to other models like BLIP-2 across different metrics. While the graph structure provides some improvements, the trends vary across metrics, emphasizing the need for a holistic approach to evaluation. Additionally, the paper discusses zero-shot generalization across sensor configurations, noting that higher accuracy in behavior tasks correlates with better motion task performance. 

Further evaluations of various visual language models (VLMs) on the DriveLM-nuScenes dataset reveal that using multi-frame inputs yields slight improvements in performance, particularly in the context of behavior and motion tasks. Overall, the findings suggest that DriveLM's graph-based approach significantly contributes to advancements in VQA within driving scenarios.
