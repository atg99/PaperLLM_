The paper presents a method called COMET for disentangling factors of variation in images, focusing on energy functions that correspond to different aspects of input images. The study evaluates the performance of COMET on datasets such as Falcor3D and CLEVR, demonstrating its ability to identify and manipulate individual factors like camera position, lighting direction, and object characteristics.

The authors illustrate the effectiveness of COMET by showcasing energy function decompositions and recombination techniques. They highlight how varying specific energy functions allows for controlled manipulation of individual factors, such as lighting intensity and facial expressions. The qualitative results are supported by quantitative comparisons using metrics like BetaV AE, Mutual Information Gap (MIG), and Mean Correlation Coefficient (MCC). The results indicate that COMET outperforms existing methods like Variational Autoencoders (VAE), MONet, and InfoGAN in terms of disentanglement across various hyperparameter settings.

Furthermore, the paper investigates object-level decomposition, applying COMET to isolate individual objects in datasets like Tetris and CLEVR. By employing smaller latent dimensions and positional embeddings, COMET effectively captures variations at the object level, reinforcing its capability in disentangling complex visual information. Overall, the findings suggest that COMET is a robust framework for understanding and manipulating visual data by effectively disentangling underlying factors.
