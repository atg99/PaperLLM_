The paper discusses the capabilities of COMET, a model designed for decomposing object-level factors of variation in images, specifically within the CLEVR and Tetris datasets. It demonstrates how COMET can effectively isolate individual energy functions that correspond to distinct objects in a scene, as shown in Figure 6. The model allows for combinatorial recombination of these energy functions, enabling the generation of new images that integrate multiple objects from different scenes while maintaining consistent occlusion, unlike previous models such as MONET.

Quantitative comparisons reveal that COMET outperforms MONET in segmentation accuracy, achieving an Adjusted Rand Index (ARI) of 0.916 compared to MONET's 0.873. Furthermore, COMET uniquely decomposes both global and local factors of variation, allowing for targeted control over which factors are captured. By manipulating the latent space size and using positional embeddings, COMET can effectively bias energy functions towards either object-level or global factors, enhancing its versatility in unsupervised object discovery. The findings underscore the model's strength in generating coherent and contextually accurate representations of complex scenes.
