The paper discusses a method called COMET for decomposing and recombining visual scenes from different datasets. It illustrates how energy functions discovered in one dataset can be integrated with those from another, allowing for the generation of novel images. This process is demonstrated through examples using datasets like CLEVR, CLEVR Lighting, and CLEVR Toy, where objects and lighting variations are recombined to create new compositions.

The analysis of local factor decomposition reveals that enabling both global and local factors leads to effective scene representation. The results show that by manipulating positional embeddings and latent dimensions, the model can accurately infer factors such as lighting and individual objects, although complete disentanglement is not achieved.

The study also evaluates the generalization capabilities of COMET, assessing its performance on multi-modal datasets. It finds that components learned from different datasets can be recombined, indicating strong compositional factor generalization. The paper emphasizes the potential of COMET for cross-dataset and cross-modality applications, highlighting its versatility in visual scene understanding.
