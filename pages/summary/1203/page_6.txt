The study also explores the recombination of these energy functions across different datasets. In Falcor3D, variations in a single energy function result in noticeable changes in camera position and lighting variations. In contrast, the CelebA-HQ dataset demonstrates the recombination of multiple energy functions across different images, highlighting the model's ability to synthesize diverse attributes from separate sources.

The paper further discusses the computational complexity of solving composed energy functions derived from multiple 3-SAT clauses. It establishes that while evaluating individual energy functions is polynomial in complexity, the composed function's evaluation is exponential, necessitating greater computational resources as the number of components increases. This underlines the efficiency of decoding individual data points with energy functions from a learning perspective.

In the evaluation section, the authors demonstrate COMET's capability to recover both global and local factors of variation in images, confirming its effectiveness across various datasets. The focus on using a consistent convolutional encoder and a specific latent dimension ensures a robust comparison across different modalities. Overall, the findings indicate that COMET is a powerful tool for disentangling and recombining image factors, offering insights into the underlying structures of visual data.
