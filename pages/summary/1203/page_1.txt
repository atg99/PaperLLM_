In this paper, the authors present COMET, a framework for the unsupervised discovery of compositional energy concepts, which captures both global scene descriptors and local object-level factors. The motivation stems from the human ability to quickly interpret scenes by utilizing concepts derived from prior experiences. Unlike previous approaches that focused solely on either global or local variations, COMET integrates both aspects by representing concepts as separate energy functions.

COMET operates by recomposing input images, which allows it to identify independent factors without requiring additional supervision. The framework formulates sample generation as an optimization process on these energy functions, facilitating the generation of images with varied and composed concepts. Furthermore, the paper highlights that the discovered visual concepts exhibit strong generalization capabilities, enabling compositional interactions between different modalities of images and concepts from separate instances of COMET trained on distinct datasets.

The authors underscore the importance of constructing a system that can autonomously discover and combine a wide array of compositional components, drawing parallels to human language's ability to generate infinite expressions from finite elements. This work contributes to the understanding of unsupervised learning by providing a unified method for capturing and manipulating complex visual concepts.
