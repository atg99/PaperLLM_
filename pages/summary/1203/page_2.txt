The paper introduces COMET, a novel approach for decomposing images into energy functions that capture both global and local factors of variation. The minimal energy state across these functions reconstructs the input image, allowing for the representation of diverse attributes such as facial expressions, background lighting, and object existence. Unlike previous methods that rely on disjoint segmentation masks, which complicate the representation of relationships between factors, COMET allows for a more flexible composition of factors by optimizing the sum of energies across all factors.

Inspired by recent advancements in energy-based models, COMET aims to discover energy functions in an unsupervised manner, enabling the combination of components inferred from different datasets. This approach not only facilitates the representation of complex scenes but also enhances the generalization of components across various data modes. The authors highlight the advantages of COMET over existing unsupervised methods, emphasizing its capability to scale to more realistic datasets and its unified framework for factor decomposition.

In summary, COMET provides a significant advancement in the field of image decomposition by allowing for the flexible composition of global and local factors, overcoming limitations of previous methods, and enhancing the ability to integrate knowledge from diverse sources.
