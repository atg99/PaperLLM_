The paper evaluates the performance of various segmentation networks, specifically SegNet, SR-SegNet v1, and SR-SegNet v2, on remote sensing images of lakes and rivers. The study highlights the disparity in the number of positive pixels (representing water bodies) compared to negative pixels (background) in the images, noting that lakes exhibit a significantly larger proportion of positive pixels than rivers. For example, in a 512x512 pixel image, lake pixels can be 6 to 10 times more abundant than river pixels.

The results indicate that while SegNet performs well on images with a higher number of positive pixels, it struggles with smaller targets, as seen with the "small lake" near Namtso Lake which was not recognized. In contrast, SR-SegNet v2 addresses this limitation through the use of dilated convolutions in its modified residual blocks, enhancing the network's performance and increasing the receptive field. The improved networks also reduce noise in segmentation results, outperforming SegNet in challenging cases like Chaohu Lake, where SegNet fails to accurately delineate the lake boundaries.

Despite not using images from Qinghai Lake and Chaohu Lake in the training set, SR-SegNet demonstrates strong generalization capabilities, successfully segmenting these lakes. Overall, the findings underscore the advantages of the proposed networks in accurately segmenting remote sensing images of water bodies.
