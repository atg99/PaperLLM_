The section discusses the process of data augmentation applied to remote sensing satellite images of Namtso Lake and a river in Central China, sourced from the China Center For Resources Satellite Data and Application between 2015 and 2019. Initially, 32 training images and 7 testing images were prepared after undifferentiated classification. Given that the water body represented a small portion of the images, Adobe Photoshop CS6 was utilized to segment the images into smaller 512x512 pixel pieces, with classification performed using Labelme. The lake was categorized as Category 1 and the background as Category 2. Notably, the training set only included images of Namtso Lake, without representation from other lakes or rivers, leading to the designation of different rivers as River 1, River 2, and River 3.

To address the challenge of limited training data necessary for deep neural networks and to prevent overfitting, data augmentation techniques were employed. This resulted in the generation of 5000 augmented images through scaling, translation, flipping, and rotation. The augmented dataset was then split into a training set of 3750 images and a validation set of 1250 images, as illustrated in the accompanying figures.
