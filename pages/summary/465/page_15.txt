This paper discusses the enhancements made to the SegNet architecture for semantic segmentation tasks, particularly for river detection. The proposed model, SR-SegNet v2, incorporates dilated convolutions to expand the receptive field, which improves its ability to extract spatial information, especially for small river segments. The results indicate that while SegNet struggles with small river detection, SR-SegNet v2 successfully segments River 3, demonstrating the effectiveness of the dilated convolutions.

The performance comparison between the classical SegNet and the proposed models (SR-SegNet v1 and v2) is summarized in tables detailing model-testing times and quantitative results. SR-SegNet v1 shows a 27% reduction in average testing time compared to SegNet, while SR-SegNet v2, despite increased computational demands due to additional dilated convolutions, is only 10% faster than SegNet.

In terms of segmentation accuracy, SR-SegNet v2 achieves an accuracy (Ac) of 99.56% and a mean intersection over union (Miou) of 94.92% for lake extraction, reflecting marginal improvements over the classical SegNet. However, the overall performance across different metrics shows only slight enhancements, with Miou being the most discriminative metric, where SR-SegNet v2 outperforms SegNet by 2.46%.

The findings highlight that while all models exhibit high accuracy and F1 scores, Miou serves as a more effective measure for distinguishing the performance of the networks, particularly in challenging scenarios with complex river structures.
