The paper reviews various methods for water area segmentation from remote sensing images, highlighting their limitations. Traditional methods such as spatial constraint models, threshold segmentation, support vector machines, and automatic water area segmentation indices have shown varying degrees of success but often struggle with issues like spatial resolution, segmentation accuracy, and generalization performance. For example, while some methods excel in shadow area segmentation, they fail to capture small targets effectively.

With the advent of artificial intelligence, deep learning has emerged as a powerful alternative, particularly convolutional neural networks (CNNs), which have achieved human-level classification accuracy. However, traditional CNNs face challenges in accurately localizing and delineating boundaries of water bodies. To address this, semantic segmentation models have been developed, allowing for pixel-level classification and improved detail extraction.

Key advancements in semantic segmentation include the fully convolutional network (FCN) proposed by Long et al., which transforms fully connected layers into convolutional layers for end-to-end processing. Other notable models include DeconvNet, which uses deconvolution for upsampling but struggles with fine granularity, and SegNet, which optimizes memory usage but increases training time. Additionally, the pyramid scene parsing network (PSPNet) utilizes dilated convolutions to enhance receptive fields without adding parameters.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the transition from traditional methods to deep learning approaches for water area segmentation, underscoring the ongoing development of more sophisticated semantic segmentation algorithms.
