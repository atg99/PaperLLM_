The paper discusses advancements in convolutional neural networks, particularly focusing on dilated convolutions and depthwise separable convolutions within the SegNet architecture, which is used for segmenting water body remote sensing images. 

Dilated convolutions enhance the receptive field without increasing the number of parameters, as described by Equation (2), which calculates the effective size of the convolution kernel based on the dilation rate. This method allows for capturing broader contextual information in the input data.

Depthwise separable convolutions are introduced to mitigate the high computational burden and parameter count associated with standard convolutions. This technique involves two stages: depthwise convolutions, which operate on each input channel separately, and pointwise convolutions, which combine the outputs across channels. The paper highlights that while standard convolutions can lead to a significant increase in parameters, depthwise separable convolutions reduce this count, thus optimizing the network for faster segmentation without sacrificing feature extraction capabilities.

Figures included in the paper illustrate the differences between standard and dilated convolutions, as well as the structure of depthwise separable convolutions, emphasizing the efficiency and effectiveness of the latter in processing high-resolution remote sensing images.
