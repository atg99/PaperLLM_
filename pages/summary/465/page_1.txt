The paper presents a new method for water area segmentation from remote sensing images using a Separable Residual SegNet (SR-SegNet) network. Accurate segmentation of lakes and rivers is critical for studying their changes, particularly in the context of global climate change. Traditional segmentation methods often suffer from high computational demands, poor generalization, and low accuracy. 

To address these issues, the SR-SegNet incorporates modified residual blocks in the encoder to mitigate network degradation, while employing depthwise separable convolutions to limit the number of parameters. Additionally, dilated convolutions are used to enhance feature extraction by expanding the receptive field. The architecture also reduces the number of convolution layers with more kernels in the encoding stage and utilizes a cascading method to fuse low-level and high-level features, improving spatial information retrieval.

Experimental results demonstrate that the SR-SegNet significantly outperforms several traditional methods, including Fully Convolutional Networks (FCN), DeconvNet, and the original SegNet. This advancement in segmentation techniques is crucial for more accurately monitoring and understanding the impacts of climate change on aquatic ecosystems.
