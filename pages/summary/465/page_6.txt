This paper discusses the development of a modified residual block for water body remote sensing image recognition, specifically in the context of the proposed architecture, SR-SegNet v2. The authors highlight the limitations of traditional bottleneck blocks, particularly the loss of semantic information due to the use of 1x1 convolution kernels. To address this, the authors introduce two successive 2x2 convolution kernels followed by a 1x1 convolution kernel, enhancing the network's ability to recognize small and medium-sized objects in water body images.

A key innovation in SR-SegNet v2 is the incorporation of dilated convolutions, which expand the receptive field without increasing the number of parameters. This allows for improved feature extraction, particularly for edge features and small targets, by maintaining detailed information during the training process. The paper notes that SR-SegNet v1 did not utilize dilated convolutions, marking a significant advancement in the proposed architecture.

The paper includes a detailed comparison between standard convolutions and dilated convolutions, illustrating how the latter can effectively increase the receptive field while maintaining computational efficiency. Overall, the modifications presented in SR-SegNet v2 aim to enhance performance in recognizing water bodies from remote sensing images by optimizing the structure of residual blocks and effectively leveraging dilated convolutions.
