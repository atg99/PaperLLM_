The paper discusses the evaluation metrics and experimental setup for assessing the performance of various segmentation models in the context of water area segmentation. Four key metrics were utilized: Accuracy (Ac), Dice coefficient, F1-Score, and Mean Intersection over Union (Miou). Accuracy measures the total correctly classified pixels, Dice assesses the similarity between two images, Precision and Recall evaluate the performance regarding positive pixel classification, and Miou quantifies segmentation accuracy.

In the experiments, VGGNet served as the backbone network, with pre-trained weights from Keras. The comparison networks included DeconvNet and various Fully Convolutional Networks (FCNs). The proposed models, SR-SegNet v1 and v2, differed in their use of dilated convolutions, with SR-SegNet v2 employing them to enhance feature extraction. The training utilized the Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD) optimizer with specific parameters and lasted for 300 epochs using a mini-batch size of 2.

The training process involved data augmentation to improve the dataset's complexity and mitigate overfitting. The dataset was split into training and testing sets, and the model was trained through a gradient descent algorithm, employing backpropagation to continuously update parameters based on the calculated loss function. The final model parameters were saved for evaluating lake and river images in the testing set.
