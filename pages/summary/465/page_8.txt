The paper discusses the implementation of depthwise separable convolutions in the context of water area segmentation, highlighting their advantages over standard convolutions. Depthwise separable convolutions generate significantly fewer parameters, specifically CK2+ NC parameters, which leads to reduced training time and computation while effectively minimizing overfitting. The architecture includes batch normalization and the ReLU activation function, enhancing model training efficiency.

The paper also presents a novel decoder design within the SR-SegNet model, which addresses potential spatial information loss during the decoding stage. By utilizing a combination of 4× unpooling followed by layer-by-layer 2× unpooling, the model retains essential high-level and low-level features, thereby improving the extraction of detailed features, particularly the edges of water bodies in remote sensing images.

Experiments conducted on the Lake and River dataset demonstrate that the proposed SR-SegNet outperforms existing semantic segmentation models across several evaluation metrics, including Accuracy, Dice, F1-Score, and Mean Intersection over Union (Miou). These results validate the effectiveness of the proposed model in water area segmentation tasks.
