The paper discusses the development of the Peach Diseases Dataset Islamia College Peshawar (PDDICP), aimed at enhancing disease detection in peach images. A critical refinement of the dataset was necessary to ensure its quality. Expert plant pathologists annotated the dataset, focusing on identifying diseases even when healthy fruits or leaves were present in the same image. This approach aimed to create robust models for disease detection. The dataset comprises six categories: healthy, brown rot, gummosis, nutrient deficiency, shot hole leaf, and shot hole fruit, with approximately 400 images per category.

To further enrich the dataset, extensive data augmentation techniques were employed. This included applying transformations such as rotations, scaling, translations, flipping, and cropping to artificially expand the dataset size, which is crucial for training deeper convolutional neural networks (CNNs) effectively. The paper highlights the importance of maintaining label integrity during augmentation, as certain cropping methods can lead to incorrect annotations. The study emphasizes that training CNNs on slightly noisy datasets can enhance their detection capabilities, enabling them to identify objects or regions of interest more effectively.
