The paper presents an experimental study focused on evaluating a framework for disease detection in peach plants using Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs). The experiments were conducted on a Windows 10 system with a Core i5 CPU, 16 GB RAM, and a GeForce RTX 2060 Super GPU. Various smartphone cameras with differing resolutions were utilized to capture images, resulting in a diverse dataset with resolutions ranging from 4 MP to 16 MP. TensorFlow v2.4.1 was employed for training and evaluating the CNN models.

The experiment design involved two main approaches. The first experiment fine-tuned pre-trained CNNs by training only the classification layer while freezing other layers, aiming to assess the feature extraction capabilities of the models. The second experiment involved fine-tuning entire networks with a smaller learning rate over a limited number of epochs, which allowed the networks to learn specific features relevant to the dataset.

Performance evaluation was conducted using metrics such as accuracy, precision, sensitivity, and specificity, derived from a confusion matrix comprising true positives, true negatives, false positives, and false negatives. These metrics provide insights into the models' ability to correctly classify samples, detect diseases, and avoid misdetections. The study emphasizes the importance of a comprehensive evaluation to ensure the robustness and effectiveness of disease detection methods in real-world scenarios.
