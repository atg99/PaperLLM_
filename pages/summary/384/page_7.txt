The paper discusses the enhancement of a dataset containing images of fruits, specifically focusing on peaches, to address the challenges posed by noise and imperfect images. The authors employed various transformations to increase the sample size and improve the realism of the images, simulating real-world conditions encountered by naïve users. 

Initially, they utilized a pre-trained object detection model, the Single Shot Multi-Box Detector (SSD), which was trained on fruits such as apples, lemons, and avocados. Despite the model not being specifically trained on peaches, its ability to recognize visually similar fruits allowed for effective detection of peach images. The authors isolated the detected fruits using bounding boxes generated by the model, followed by manual cleaning of labels to create a cleaner subset of samples with slight occlusions.

Additionally, they implemented data augmentation techniques by creating four random center crops from each original image, along with rotated and flipped versions. This augmentation process aimed to further expand the dataset while ensuring quality control through inspection and annotation by a plant pathologist, thereby minimizing noisy labels. The resulting dataset is expected to be more robust for subsequent analyses and applications in fruit recognition tasks.
