The paper discusses the results of a study focused on enhancing a disease detection model for fruit using various convolutional neural network (CNN) architectures. The best performance was achieved with EfficientNet-B2, which demonstrated significant improvements in sensitivity and precision after fine-tuning. The confusion matrix indicated reduced misclassification between healthy and unhealthy fruit, highlighting the model's enhanced feature extraction capabilities.

Field testing further validated the model's robustness, as it successfully detected diseases in images containing both infected and healthy samples without preprocessing. Class activation maps illustrated the model's focus on infected areas while disregarding healthy parts, confirming its effectiveness in distinguishing between healthy and diseased regions.

Table 5 summarizes the performance metrics of different fine-tuned CNN models, with EfficientNet-B2 achieving an average accuracy of 96.6%, sensitivity of 90.5%, specificity of 98.0%, and precision of 90.7%. In contrast, larger models (EfficientNet-B3, B4, and B5) exhibited overfitting when their feature extraction layers were altered during training. Overall, the study underscores the potential of fine-tuned CNNs for accurate disease detection in agricultural settings.
