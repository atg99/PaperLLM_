The paper discusses various approaches to plant disease detection using deep learning techniques, particularly focusing on Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs). It outlines the methodology involving image resizing, pre-processing, model training, testing, and validation. One model achieved an impressive average accuracy of 99.11%, with a sensitivity of 92.92% and specificity of 99.52% for detecting diseases in rice and maize.

The authors also highlight a simplified CNN model for identifying tomato crop diseases, which requires less storage. This model demonstrated an accuracy of 98.4%, outperforming traditional machine learning methods such as VGG16 and Inception V3, using a dataset of 55,000 leaf images from 14 different crops.

Further, the paper examines a study that employed transfer learning with deep CNNs, particularly MobileNet and SE-MobileNet, to enhance the detection of plant lesions. This approach utilized two datasets: one from PlantVillage with 54,306 leaf images and another with 600 real images of rice diseases. By utilizing a two-fold transfer learning strategy, the authors reported a high accuracy of 99.78%, surpassing other models like InceptionV3 and VGGNet-19.

The paper summarizes these findings, showcasing various methodologies for plant disease detection using deep learning, including their respective datasets, image counts, and accuracy rates, emphasizing the advancements in this field.
