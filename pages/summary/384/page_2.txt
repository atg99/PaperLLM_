The paper discusses the critical role of fruit farming in human health and the economic implications of fruit diseases, particularly in peach crops, which are significant in Pakistan. Chronic diseases linked to inadequate fruit consumption underscore the need for effective fruit production management. The Food and Agriculture Organization predicts a 70% increase in agricultural demand by 2050, making disease management essential to prevent crop loss, which currently stands at 10-15% due to untreated diseases.

The authors highlight the challenges faced by peach crops, including bacterial canker and peach scab, exacerbated by limited technological resources for early infection detection. Innovations in machine learning and the Internet of Things have transformed smart agriculture, allowing real-time disease identification through Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN). However, CNNs are computationally intensive, necessitating techniques like Transfer Learning to enhance performance on large datasets without manual feature extraction.

The paper makes several contributions: it presents a dataset of annotated peach images for healthy and infected fruits, details the training and fine-tuning of deep learning models for disease identification, and explores optimal feature extraction methods using class activation maps to validate model effectiveness. The structure of the paper includes a literature review, a detailed methodology, experimental results, and a conclusion discussing limitations and future research directions.
