The research highlighted the evolution of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) from their inception to the current advanced architectures. The performance of CNNs surged in 2012 with the introduction of AlexNet, which set a benchmark in image recognition. Subsequent innovations included deeper networks and the introduction of residual connections, which allowed for the design of more complex models without performance loss. 

EfficientNet emerged as a significant advancement, featuring eight variants (B0 to B7) that utilize a systematic scaling approach for depth, width, and resolution, leading to enhanced efficiency and accuracy. The introduction of the Swish activation function marked a notable improvement over traditional ReLU functions. The architecture's core component, the inverted bottleneck block (MBConv), optimizes channel management and reduces computational demands.

In summary, the study effectively employed EfficientNet models for peach plant disease detection, leveraging transfer learning on a more suitable augmented dataset to mitigate potential confusion from the original dataset's complexity. This approach aimed to ensure optimal model performance for practical application in mobile technology.
