The paper evaluates the performance of various pretrained models in feature extraction for disease detection using a frozen base model approach. In this setup, the feature extraction layers of the model were not modified during transfer learning, ensuring that the focus remained on the performance of the models themselves. An augmented dataset was used, with 80% allocated for training and 20% for testing.

Results indicated that the EfficientNet-B2 architecture outperformed others, achieving a balanced and optimal performance on the test set. Larger EfficientNet models (B3 to B5) exhibited tendencies to overfit, while other CNNs like ResNet50, InceptionV3, and MobileNetV2 performed acceptably. AlexNet, being shallower, had the lowest performance. The confusion matrix for EfficientNet-B2 revealed a high true positive rate of 0.94 for nutrient deficiency, although there was some confusion between nutrient deficiency and shot hole leaf, and brown rot and healthy samples.

Despite high overall detection accuracy, sensitivity and precision were notably low for certain classes, particularly brown rot and shot hole. The precision for the healthy class was also low, attributed to the mislabeling of healthy samples in the presence of unhealthy ones in the same image. The results underscore the capability of EfficientNet-B2 in extracting fine-grained visual features, although challenges in class discrimination remain.
