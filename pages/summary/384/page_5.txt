The paper investigates the effectiveness of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) for detecting diseases in peach plants using images captured under varying illumination conditions. It evaluates several advanced CNN architectures, including AlexNet, Inception, ResNet, MobileNet, and EfficientNet. The proposed framework utilizes pretrained models as feature extractors and for fine-tuning to enhance disease recognition accuracy.

For the dataset, 2500 images of peach fruits, leaves, and stems were collected using smartphone cameras from different regions of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Pakistan. This dataset was designed to be challenging, capturing a variety of environmental conditions. Some images feature multiple fruits or leaves, potentially including both healthy and diseased specimens, which may introduce noise into the dataset. While a slightly noisy dataset can aid in robust model training, the authors acknowledge the importance of managing this noise in order to improve the accuracy of disease detection.
