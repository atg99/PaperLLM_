The paper explores various methodologies for unsupervised video learning, focusing on the performance of different pre-training techniques on downstream tasks. It presents a comprehensive evaluation of different methods, including supervised and unsupervised pre-training strategies, across several benchmark datasets such as K400, UCF101, AVA, Charades, and Something-Something v2 (SSv2).

Key findings include that the BYOL method, when pre-trained, achieved the highest accuracy on SSv2, outperforming supervised pre-training on Kinetics by 3%. Additionally, using the IG-Curated-1M dataset for pre-training yielded better results than K400 pre-training for MoCo, highlighting the potential of web-sourced data. The performance of models trained on uncurated data was comparable to those trained on curated datasets, suggesting that unsupervised learning can effectively compete with supervised methods.

In comparisons to state-of-the-art techniques, the proposed BYOL model with temporal persistency outperformed previous methods significantly, achieving substantial gains in accuracy on UCF101 and HMDB51, as well as on K400 linear evaluations.

The study concludes that careful sampling, the choice of contrastive objectives, and the duration of training are vital for achieving optimal performance. Overall, the paper emphasizes the viability of unsupervised pre-training as a competitive alternative to traditional supervised methods, paving the way for future research in this area.
