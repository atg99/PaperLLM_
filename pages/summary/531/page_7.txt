The paper investigates the impact of cropping and aspect ratio augmentations on the performance of unsupervised learning models for video representation, particularly focusing on MoCo and BYOL. The default implementation employs VGG-style cropping, which involves resizing the shorter side of a video and extracting a clip. The authors explore Inception-style cropping with aspect ratio augmentation, which adjusts both the area and the aspect ratio of the input. 

The results indicate that stronger cropping and aspect ratio augmentations can enhance performance, with the best configurations yielding accuracy increases of 1.0% on K400 and 0.7% on UCF101. Specifically, using a smaller minimum area of 0.2 and a maximum area of 0.76 achieved a peak performance of 66.8% on K400. 

Furthermore, the paper combines the best color and cropping augmentations, leading to a performance improvement of approximately 1% on K400. The analysis also highlights the varying effectiveness of different models on downstream tasks, demonstrating that supervised pre-training generally outperforms training from scratch. Notably, BYOL pre-trained models surpassed their supervised counterparts on the A V A dataset, suggesting that the model's design may be more suited for certain tasks. In contrast, MoCo excelled in long-term action classification on Charades, indicating that contrastive learning may be more beneficial for tasks requiring recognition over extended temporal ranges. 

Overall, the findings emphasize the importance of augmentation strategies in unsupervised video representation learning and suggest that model effectiveness can vary significantly across different tasks.
