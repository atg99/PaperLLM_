This paper presents a comprehensive study on unsupervised spatiotemporal representation learning from videos, conducted by researchers from Facebook AI Research. The authors propose a simple yet effective objective that generalizes existing image-based learning frameworks to the spatiotemporal domain. This objective focuses on maximizing feature similarity across different temporal clips from the same video, promoting temporally-persistent features.

The study reveals that this approach is effective across various unsupervised frameworks, pre-training datasets, and backbone architectures, achieving state-of-the-art results on multiple benchmarks. Notably, the authors find that long timespans of up to 60 seconds can enhance representation quality, and in some cases, unsupervised pre-training surpasses supervised methods.

The research builds on previous methods that utilize similarity objectives for different views of the same image, leveraging the natural augmentations present in video content such as motion and occlusion. By encouraging consistency in visual representations over time, the authors demonstrate the potential of their objective to facilitate high-level categorical semantics learning in videos.

The study utilizes large-scale datasets, including Kinetics with 240,000 videos, and evaluates the learned representations on various classification and detection tasks. Overall, the findings suggest that simple modifications to existing frameworks can yield significant improvements in unsupervised learning performance in the spatiotemporal context.
