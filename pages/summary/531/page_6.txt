The paper evaluates the performance of four unsupervised learning methodologies—MoCo, BYOL, SimCLR, and SwAV—on different datasets, focusing on the effects of training duration, data curation, and augmentations. 

Key findings include:

1. **Performance by Method**: MoCo consistently outperforms the other methods across various datasets, with notable performance on curated data (K400) where it achieves 69.0% accuracy compared to SwAV's 64.3%. 

2. **Training Duration**: Both MoCo and SwAV show improved performance when trained for longer epochs (200 vs. 50), gaining about 3-4%. However, BYOL's performance declines with longer training, suggesting a need for different hyperparameters.

3. **Data Types**: On uncurated data, MoCo and SwAV perform approximately 1% better on videos of unconstrained duration. Conversely, BYOL and SimCLR excel with shorter videos, indicating that the benefits of video duration vary across methodologies.

4. **Impact of Augmentations**: The study highlights the importance of data augmentations in unsupervised learning. MoCo and BYOL demonstrate robustness with fewer augmentations, while SimCLR and SwAV show significant performance drops without temporal augmentations. Temporal consistency is particularly crucial for these methods.

5. **Stronger Color Augmentation**: Experiments with varying color augmentation strengths indicate that a slight increase in color strength improves performance on datasets, particularly UCF101. However, increasing grayscale probability does not yield benefits.

Overall, the findings suggest that while MoCo is the most effective method, the choice of training duration, data type, and augmentation strategy significantly influence performance in unsupervised video learning. Further exploration of hyperparameter tuning for different training schedules is warranted, particularly for BYOL.
