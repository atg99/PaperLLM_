The paper discusses the impact of the timespan between positive samples in video representation learning, specifically using the BYOL method with varying maximum distances between clips. It highlights that while global temporal sampling is employed, long durations between positive clips may affect semantic context, potentially hindering the learning of high-level features.

Experiments conducted across three datasets (K400, IG-Curated-1M, and IG-Uncurated-1M) reveal that performance remains robust even with increasing temporal distances, with accuracy peaking when positive samples are unrestricted. For the K400 dataset, accuracy drops by about 5% when using positives from the same time, but remains stable up to 10 seconds apart. In the IG-Curated-1M dataset, unrestricted sampling yields the highest accuracy (64.1%), suggesting that even longer videos benefit from broader sampling. The IG-Uncurated-1M dataset indicates that while increasing the distance between positives up to 36 seconds is beneficial, performance declines only slightly beyond that.

The paper also compares various backbone architectures for the spatiotemporal encoder, focusing on ResNet variants (R-18, R-50, R-101) and other architectures like R2+1D and S3D-G. Results indicate that the depth of the ResNet backbone significantly influences performance, with deeper networks generally providing better accuracy. The findings suggest that while MoCo's supervised performance improves with more parameters, smaller datasets like UCF101 may benefit from fewer parameters due to easier overfitting.

In summary, the paper emphasizes the importance of temporal sampling strategies and backbone architecture selection in enhancing the performance of video representation learning models.
