The paper discusses the challenges and methodologies in evaluating transfer learning techniques in Natural Language Processing (NLP), specifically addressing the WNLI dataset, which is considered adversarial due to its validation examples being perturbed versions of training examples with opposite labels. Consequently, WNLI is excluded from the average GLUE score except in specific test set analyses.

The authors conduct an empirical survey of various advancements in transfer learning for NLP, including pre-training objectives, model architectures, and data sets. Their goal is to systematically study the contributions of these techniques while maintaining a fixed experimental setup, employing a "coordinate ascent" approach to isolate the effects of individual factors. They acknowledge that while this method may overlook interactions between variables, a comprehensive combinatorial exploration would be impractical.

The paper outlines a baseline experimental setup and examines different aspects including model architectures, unsupervised objectives, pre-training datasets, transfer approaches, and scaling. The authors emphasize that while they do not replicate existing encoder-only models like BERT, they explore similar objectives and architectures. The culmination of their efforts aims to achieve state-of-the-art results across various tasks by integrating insights from their study with scaling strategies.
