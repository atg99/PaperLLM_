The paper investigates the limits of transfer learning, particularly focusing on the effects of varying corruption rates during training. The study presents empirical results on model performance across different datasets, including GLUE, CNNDM, SQuAD, SGLUE, EnDe, EnFr, and EnRo, with corruption rates set at 10%, 15%, 25%, and 50%. The findings indicate that while the corruption rate generally has a limited impact on performance, a 50% corruption rate significantly degrades results on GLUE and SQuAD. Consequently, the authors decide to maintain a 15% corruption rate, consistent with prior research using BERT.

Additionally, the paper explores the idea of corrupting spans of tokens instead of individual tokens, suggesting that this approach could enhance training efficiency by reducing the length of sequences processed. The authors propose a method where contiguous spans of tokens are randomly selected for corruption, allowing for a more structured and potentially effective pre-training objective. This method could lead to improved performance and speed in training, as indicated by previous studies on BERT. The paper highlights the importance of adjusting corruption strategies in transfer learning to optimize model performance.
