The paper discusses the evolution of transfer learning methodologies in natural language processing (NLP), highlighting the shift from explicit knowledge representation to learning through auxiliary tasks, such as using word vectors to map word identities into continuous representations. It notes that recent advancements have favored unsupervised pre-training on large datasets, which is particularly beneficial given the vast amounts of unlabeled text data available online. This approach has led to state-of-the-art performance across various NLP benchmarks.

The authors emphasize the challenges posed by the rapid development of diverse techniques in the field, which complicates comparisons and understanding of different algorithms. To address this, they propose a unified approach to transfer learning that treats all text processing tasks as "text-to-text" problems. This framework allows for a consistent application of models and procedures across various NLP tasks, including question answering and document analysis. By leveraging this methodology, the authors aim to systematically investigate and enhance the capabilities of transfer learning in NLP.
