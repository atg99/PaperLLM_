This paper examines the effectiveness of different mixing strategies in multi-task training compared to a pre-train and fine-tune approach. The authors present results in Table 11, highlighting the performance of various mixing strategies across several tasks, including GLUE, CNNDM, SQuAD, SGLUE, and translation tasks (EnDe, EnFr, EnRo).

The strategies evaluated include equal mixing, where examples are sampled uniformly from each task, and examples-proportional mixing, which samples based on the size of each dataset with an artificial limit (K). Additionally, temperature-scaled mixing adjusts sampling rates by a temperature parameter (T), also under a size limit.

Findings indicate that multi-task training generally underperforms compared to the baseline pre-train/ﬁne-tune method. The equal mixing strategy notably leads to poor performance due to potential overfitting on low-resource tasks and underfitting on high-resource ones. In contrast, examples-proportional mixing reveals an optimal range for K, with performance peaking at certain values. Notably, for the high-resource WMT English to French translation task, higher mixing proportions consistently yield better results. Temperature-scaled mixing, particularly with T=2, also achieves reasonable performance across most tasks.

The authors acknowledge that while multi-task models often lag behind individually trained models, they can be beneficial for closely related tasks. The paper concludes with a discussion on strategies to enhance multi-task training to diminish the performance gap when compared to the pre-train-then-fine-tune methodology.
