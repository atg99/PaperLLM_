The paper discusses the impact of dataset size and repetition during the pre-training phase of models on downstream performance. Table 9 presents results showing that as the size of the pre-training dataset decreases, model performance on various tasks also declines. This is attributed to potential memorization of the training data, evidenced by lower training losses with smaller datasets, which aligns with findings from Baevski et al. (2019). 

The authors note that while significant repetition (64 times) of the dataset may not adversely affect performance, it is advisable to use large datasets whenever feasible, particularly since additional unlabeled data is readily available. They also suggest that larger models may be more susceptible to overfitting when trained on smaller datasets.

In the section on training strategy, the authors evaluate different fine-tuning methods, emphasizing that fine-tuning all model parameters may not always yield the best results, especially for low-resource tasks. Previous research has indicated that focusing on a small classifier fed with embeddings from a fixed pre-trained model can be more effective for text classification tasks. This review of training strategies sets the stage for further exploration of optimal approaches in subsequent sections.
