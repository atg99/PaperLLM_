The paper by Raffel et al. presents an analysis of the performance of a span-corruption objective compared to an independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) corruption objective in natural language processing tasks. The results are summarized in Table 7, which illustrates the performance metrics across various tasks, including GLUE, CNNDM, SQuAD, SGLUE, and translation tasks (EnDe, EnFr, EnRo) for different average span lengths of corrupted text.

The study finds that using a span-corruption method, where 15% of the original text is corrupted, yields competitive performance across tasks, with an average span length of 3 achieving slightly better results than the i.i.d. approach in most non-translation benchmarks. The span length of 10, however, tends to underperform in certain scenarios. Notably, the span-corruption method also offers training speed advantages due to the shorter sequences generated.

In the exploration of unsupervised objectives, the authors follow a systematic approach, starting with various methods, narrowing down to a BERT-style denoising objective that yields the best performance, and subsequently experimenting with simplifications and corruption rates. The findings suggest that the span-corruption objective is a promising alternative to traditional methods, enhancing both performance and efficiency during training.
