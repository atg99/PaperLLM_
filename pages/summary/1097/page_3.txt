The paper "Exploring the Limits of Transfer Learning" investigates the capabilities and boundaries of transfer learning in natural language processing (NLP) by employing a unified text-to-text framework. This framework allows various tasks, such as translation and classification, to be framed as generating target text from input text using the same model and training processes. The authors introduce the Text-to-Text Transfer Transformer, or T5, which enables empirical comparisons across different transfer learning objectives and datasets.

The primary aim of the research is not to develop new methodologies but to provide a comprehensive overview of the current landscape of transfer learning techniques. The study includes a systematic exploration of existing methods, scaling up models to 11 billion parameters, and achieving state-of-the-art results across multiple tasks. A significant contribution of the paper is the introduction of the Colossal Clean Crawled Corpus (C4), a large dataset of clean English text sourced from the internet, which facilitates experiments at scale.

The structure of the paper is organized into sections discussing the base model, the formulation of text processing problems, a series of experiments, and a summary of findings, with a forward-looking conclusion. The authors also emphasize the importance of releasing their code, datasets, and pre-trained models to support further research in data-scarce environments.
