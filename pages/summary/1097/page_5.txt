This paper investigates the limitations of transfer learning within the context of Transformer models. It details the architecture of a Transformer, emphasizing the role of self-attention mechanisms in both the encoder and decoder. The decoder employs autoregressive self-attention, which restricts attention to past outputs, while the final output is processed through a dense layer with shared weights between the input embedding matrix and the softmax layer.

The paper discusses the importance of position signals in self-attention due to its order-independent nature. It highlights the shift from traditional sinusoidal position signals to relative position embeddings, which adapt based on the offset between keys and queries. The authors implement a simplified scalar-based position embedding approach, sharing parameters across layers but allowing different learned embeddings per attention head. They utilize 32 embeddings for various key-query offsets, with a logarithmic range up to 128 tokens.

The authors also acknowledge modifications to the original Transformer model, including the removal of Layer Norm bias and adjustments in layer normalization placement. These architectural changes are presented as orthogonal to the primary focus of their empirical investigation into transfer learning, with plans for further exploration of their impacts in future work.

Additionally, the paper addresses the scalability of model performance as parameters and layers increase, noting challenges in training large models due to resource constraints. They employ model and data parallelism on Cloud TPU Pods, utilizing the Mesh TensorFlow library for implementation efficiency.

Furthermore, the study examines the impact of the quality, characteristics, and size of unlabeled datasets, specifically utilizing the Colossal Clean Crawled Corpus derived from Common Crawl as a source of web-scraped text. This research aims to deepen the understanding of transfer learning dynamics in natural language processing.
