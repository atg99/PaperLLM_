The paper presents a comparison of various architectural variants for transformer models, focusing on their performance across multiple tasks such as GLUE, CNNDM, and SQuAD. The results highlight the effectiveness of different configurations, particularly the encoder-decoder models with denoising objectives, which achieved the highest scores in most benchmarks. The authors emphasize the significance of the unsupervised objectives used during pre-training, as these objectives provide the foundational knowledge necessary for the models to perform well on downstream tasks.

The analysis includes a detailed examination of various unsupervised objectives, showcasing how they are adapted to fit the text-to-text encoder-decoder framework. The authors note that while some objectives are derived from existing methods, others are modified or combine elements from multiple approaches to enhance performance. The training process involves predicting a target sequence from a corrupted input sequence, with maximum likelihood estimation employed to optimize the model.

Overall, the paper underscores the importance of both architectural design and the choice of unsupervised objectives in developing effective transformer models for various natural language processing tasks.
