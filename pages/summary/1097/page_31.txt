The paper "Exploring the Limits of Transfer Learning" discusses the dynamics of multi-task learning within a unified text-to-text framework. It emphasizes the importance of effectively managing the proportion of data from different tasks to avoid under-training or over-training. The authors highlight the challenge of achieving a balance where the model has enough exposure to each task without memorizing the training data.

A significant concern is the issue of task interference, where good performance on one task could negatively impact another. The paper explores strategies for setting data proportions, including examples-proportional mixing, which involves sampling data based on the size of each task's dataset. However, this approach can lead to under-training on smaller supervised tasks due to the overwhelming size of unsupervised datasets.

To address this, the authors propose setting an artificial limit on dataset sizes when calculating sampling proportions. Additionally, they introduce temperature-scaled mixing as a method to balance the disparities in dataset sizes. By adjusting the mixing rates through a temperature parameter, the authors can ensure better training across tasks, particularly for low-resource languages, while still applying the dataset size limit.

Overall, the paper provides insights into optimizing multi-task learning strategies and managing the complexities arising from varying dataset sizes and task interactions.
