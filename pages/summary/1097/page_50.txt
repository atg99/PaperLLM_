The paper discusses the processing of input data for two tasks: COPA and MultiRC. 

In the COPA task, a scenario is presented where political violence leads to two choices regarding citizens' reactions. The processed input clearly identifies the premise, question, and choices, with the original target indicating that the first choice is the correct effect of the premise.

For the MultiRC task, the focus is on a narrative about a squirrel named Joey who is surprised to find only pie for breakfast instead of traditional foods. The processed input includes the relevant question, answer, and paragraph context, highlighting Joey's surprise due to the absence of typical breakfast items.

Overall, the paper illustrates how structured input can facilitate the understanding of effects and reasoning in narrative contexts, emphasizing the importance of clear connections between premises, questions, and answers.
