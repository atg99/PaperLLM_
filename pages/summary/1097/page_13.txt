The paper explores the limits of transfer learning through a novel baseline model that employs a "word dropout" regularization technique. This method involves randomly corrupting 15% of tokens in an input sequence, replacing consecutive spans of dropped tokens with unique sentinel tokens. The output sequence includes these sentinel tokens, allowing the model to predict the original dropped spans. This design choice aims to reduce computational costs during pre-training while maintaining model effectiveness.

The authors conduct performance evaluations of the baseline model across various downstream tasks, emphasizing the need for multiple experimental repetitions to establish confidence intervals. Due to resource constraints, they perform ten runs of the baseline model with different initializations and data shuffling to estimate variance and significance of changes across experimental variants. Additionally, they assess the model's performance after 218 training steps without pre-training, providing insights into the benefits of pre-training within the baseline framework. The results of these evaluations are detailed in the subsequent sections of the paper.
