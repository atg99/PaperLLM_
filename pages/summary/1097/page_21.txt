The paper explores the effectiveness of various unsupervised pre-training objectives in transfer learning, particularly in the context of language modeling. It presents examples of different input-output configurations for three main objectives: prefix language modeling, BERT-style masked language modeling, and deshuﬄing.

Prefix language modeling involves predicting the next tokens in a sequence based on prior context. The BERT-style objective, based on the masked language model, corrupts a portion of the input tokens and tasks the model with reconstructing the original text. This objective aims to enhance the performance of encoder-decoder models by utilizing the entire uncorrupted sequence as the target. Deshuﬄing, on the other hand, involves shuffling a sequence of tokens and then predicting the original order.

Performance metrics for these objectives are compared across several tasks, including GLUE, CNNDM, and SQuAD, with results indicating that the BERT-style objective consistently outperforms the others, particularly in language understanding tasks. The prefix language modeling shows comparable results in translation tasks, while the deshuﬄing method significantly underperforms relative to the others. Overall, the findings suggest that BERT-style pre-training is superior for language tasks, reinforcing its design goal to exceed traditional language model-based approaches.
