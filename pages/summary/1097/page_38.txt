The paper discusses the evaluation of various model sizes on several benchmarks, including WMT, GLUE, SuperGLUE, and SQuAD. For the WMT tasks, specific newstest datasets were utilized for English-German, English-French, and English-Romanian translations. The authors indicated that while they aimed to evaluate on the SQuAD test set, resource limitations restricted their ability to run the largest models, leading them to report on the validation set instead. They maintained consistent training procedures and hyperparameters, utilizing the AdaFactor optimizer and dropout regularization.

The results revealed state-of-the-art performance in 18 out of 24 tasks, particularly with their largest model variant (11 billion parameters), which outperformed the T5-3B model on various tasks. The model achieved an average GLUE score of 90.3, significantly surpassing previous benchmarks, especially in natural language inference tasks like MNLI, RTE, and WNLI. The authors noted that their 11B model was the largest submitted to GLUE, whereas others, like ALBERT, achieved high scores through extensive ensembling, making T5-11B potentially more efficient in terms of computational resources.

In SQuAD, the model surpassed the previous state-of-the-art by over one point in the Exact Match score. The authors highlighted that recent advancements in this benchmark had only marginally improved results, raising questions about the significance of further enhancements. Overall, the paper emphasizes the effectiveness of scaling model sizes in achieving superior performance across various natural language processing tasks.
