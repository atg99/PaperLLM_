The paper "Exploring the Limits of Transfer Learning" investigates the effectiveness of transfer learning techniques, particularly in the context of encoder-decoder models. It highlights the limitations of traditional fine-tuning methods, which often require adjusting the entire model. Instead, the authors propose two alternative approaches to fine-tuning that focus on modifying only a subset of model parameters.

The first method involves the use of adapter layers, which are additional dense-ReLU-dense blocks inserted into the existing architecture of the Transformer model. This technique allows for the majority of the original model to remain unchanged while only the adapter layers and layer normalization parameters are updated during fine-tuning. The primary hyperparameter for this approach is the inner dimensionality of the feed-forward network, which affects the number of parameters added.

The second method discussed is gradual unfreezing, where model parameters are fine-tuned incrementally over time. This approach starts with the final layer of the model and progressively includes earlier layers, allowing for more controlled training. The authors adapt this strategy for their encoder-decoder architecture by unfreezing layers in parallel across both the encoder and decoder.

The paper also analyzes the impact of dataset size on pre-training loss, revealing that smaller datasets may lead to memorization rather than generalization. Overall, the research presents innovative strategies for optimizing transfer learning in complex models, emphasizing the need for tailored approaches to effectively leverage pre-trained networks.
