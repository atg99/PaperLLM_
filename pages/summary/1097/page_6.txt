The paper discusses the use of Common Crawl, a publicly-available web archive, as a source of text data for natural language processing (NLP). It highlights the challenges posed by the vast amount of non-natural language content in the data, which includes gibberish, boilerplate text, and offensive language. To improve the quality of the dataset, the authors implemented several cleaning heuristics: 

1. Retaining only lines that end with terminal punctuation.
2. Discarding pages with fewer than five sentences and lines with fewer than three words.
3. Removing pages containing any words from a predetermined list of inappropriate terms.
4. Excluding lines with the word "Javascript" and those containing "lorem ipsum" placeholder text.
5. Eliminating pages that include curly brackets, which are common in programming languages.
6. Deduplicating the dataset by keeping only one instance of any repeated three-sentence span.
7. Filtering out non-English pages with low confidence using a language detection tool.

The authors note that their approach draws inspiration from previous research but aims to create a new dataset that is more robust in its filtering criteria and publicly available for broader use. This dataset is particularly focused on English-language text, addressing the limitations of prior datasets that were often narrower in scope or not accessible to the public.
