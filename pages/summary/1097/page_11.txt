The paper discusses the establishment of a baseline model for transfer learning in natural language processing (NLP) using a standard encoder-decoder Transformer architecture, as introduced by Vaswani et al. in 2017. The authors aim to reflect modern practices by pre-training their model with a denoising objective and fine-tuning it separately on various downstream tasks.

The model comprises an encoder and decoder, each structured similarly to a "BERT BASE" configuration, consisting of 12 blocks that include self-attention, optional encoder-decoder attention, and feed-forward networks. Each block has a dense layer with an output dimensionality of 3072, followed by a ReLU nonlinearity and another dense layer. The attention mechanisms utilize 12 heads with key and value matrices having an inner dimensionality of 64, and all sub-layers and embeddings maintain a dimensionality of 768, resulting in approximately 220 million parameters—double that of BERT BASE due to the dual layer stacks.

Training is conducted by framing all tasks as text-to-text, allowing for the use of maximum likelihood estimation with teacher forcing and cross-entropy loss. The optimization method employed is AdaFactor, and during testing, greedy decoding is utilized. Pre-training occurs over 524,288 steps on the C4 dataset, with a maximum sequence length of 512 and a batch size of 128 sequences, totaling around 34 billion tokens. This training setup, while less extensive than BERT and RoBERTa, provides a reasonable computational budget and sufficient pre-training for acceptable performance.
