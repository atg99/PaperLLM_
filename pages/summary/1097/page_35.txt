The paper investigates various scaling strategies for enhancing the performance of transfer learning models across different tasks. The results, summarized in Table 13, show that increasing either the training time or the model size generally leads to improved performance over a baseline model. Notably, while there was no definitive advantage between increasing training steps or batch size, both strategies proved beneficial. Increasing model size consistently yielded better performance than solely extending training time or batch size.

The findings indicate that scaling up the model size and training time can work synergistically to enhance model effectiveness. Ensembling, which involves combining multiple separately trained models, emerged as a particularly effective strategy for certain tasks, such as CNN/DM and WMT translation tasks. However, this approach did not significantly improve results for SuperGLUE.

The paper also highlights the trade-offs associated with different scaling methods. Larger models can incur higher costs during fine-tuning and inference, while longer pre-training times for smaller models may be advantageous when applied across multiple tasks. The cost of ensembling models is comparable to that of using a single larger model, suggesting that the choice of scaling method should consider the intended application of the model.
