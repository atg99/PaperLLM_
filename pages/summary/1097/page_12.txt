The paper discusses the pre-training and fine-tuning strategies employed for language models, particularly focusing on the use of a large dataset known as C4. The authors highlight that during pre-training, they utilize an inverse square root learning rate schedule, which begins with a constant learning rate of 0.01 for the first 10,000 steps before exponentially decaying it. They also mention experimenting with a triangular learning rate, which yielded slightly better results but was less flexible for varying training steps.

For fine-tuning, a total of 262,144 steps is employed, balancing the needs of high-resource tasks that benefit from longer training against low-resource tasks that risk overfitting. A constant learning rate of 0.001 is used during fine-tuning, with checkpoints saved every 5,000 steps to capture the best performing model for validation purposes.

The vocabulary for the models is created using SentencePiece, resulting in a set of 32,000 WordPiece tokens that can process multiple languages, including English, German, French, and Romanian. This vocabulary is trained on a mixture of English data from C4 and data classified by language.

Lastly, the paper emphasizes the importance of employing an unsupervised objective for pre-training. While previous models often used causal language modeling, recent advancements have shown that denoising objectives, such as masked language modeling, yield superior performance. This approach involves training the model to predict missing or corrupted tokens, aligning with the methods popularized by BERT.
