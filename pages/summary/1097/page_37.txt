This paper explores the capabilities and limitations of transfer learning, particularly through the implementation of large-scale models with varying architectures. The authors describe configurations for two model variants, "3B" and "11B," which incorporate different feed-forward network dimensions and attention heads, resulting in approximately 2.8 billion and 11 billion parameters, respectively. This scaling is attributed to the efficiency of modern accelerators in handling large dense matrix multiplications prevalent in Transformers.

The study emphasizes the benefits of multi-task pre-training, combining unsupervised and supervised tasks, which is shown to enhance performance compared to pre-training on unsupervised tasks alone. This approach allows continuous monitoring of downstream performance throughout training. The authors propose specific artificial dataset sizes for unlabeled data across model variants to optimize training, while also capping the effective dataset sizes for certain translation tasks.

During fine-tuning on GLUE and SuperGLUE benchmarks, the authors note a trade-off between logistical simplicity and performance, as concatenating all datasets may lead to overfitting on low-resource tasks. To mitigate this, they adopt a smaller batch size during fine-tuning and frequently save model checkpoints to prevent overfitting.

Additionally, the paper discusses the shift from greedy decoding to beam search for tasks requiring longer outputs, reporting improved performance with a specific beam width and length penalty. The final results are presented on the test set for a comprehensive evaluation of the proposed methodologies.
