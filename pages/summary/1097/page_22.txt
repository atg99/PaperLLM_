The paper by Raffel et al. investigates modifications to the BERT-style denoising objective to improve efficiency and performance in an encoder-decoder framework for text-to-text tasks. The authors compare various pre-training objectives, including the original BERT-style objective and two alternatives: the MASS-style objective, which replaces 15% of input tokens with a mask token for reconstruction, and two strategies that involve either replacing consecutive corrupted tokens with a unique mask token or completely dropping corrupted tokens.

Results from their empirical comparison, presented in Table 5, indicate that while all variants performed similarly, dropping corrupted tokens yielded a small improvement on the GLUE benchmark, particularly in the CoLA task, which assesses grammatical acceptability. However, this approach underperformed on the SuperGLUE benchmark compared to the other methods. The authors highlight the potential advantages of the two variants that avoid predicting the complete original sequence, as they lead to shorter target sequences, thus simplifying training. Overall, the findings suggest that certain modifications to the BERT objective can enhance performance in specific contexts while maintaining efficiency.
