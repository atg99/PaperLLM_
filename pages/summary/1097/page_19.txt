The paper investigates the efficiency of transfer learning in various model architectures, particularly focusing on encoder-decoder and decoder-only language models. The study evaluates different configurations, including an encoder-decoder model with L layers in both components, a parameter-shared encoder-decoder model, a model with reduced layers, and a decoder-only model. The computational costs and parameter counts are analyzed, noting that the encoder-decoder model with a denoising objective achieved the best performance, despite having the highest parameter count.

The objectives include both a traditional language modeling task and a novel unsupervised denoising objective, highlighting the historical significance of language modeling as a pre-training objective. For the encoder-decoder and pre-fix language model, a random split of text into input and target portions is employed, while the standard language model predicts the entire text span.

Results indicate that the encoder-decoder model with the denoising objective outperformed all others, demonstrating that sharing parameters across the encoder and decoder also yielded competitive performance. Conversely, reducing the number of layers negatively impacted results. The findings align with previous research suggesting that parameter sharing can reduce total parameter counts without compromising performance, and they reinforce the notion that denoising objectives enhance downstream task effectiveness compared to standard language modeling tasks.
