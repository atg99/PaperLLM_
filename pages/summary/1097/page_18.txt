In their paper, Liu et al. (2018) and Dong et al. (2019) discuss an architecture that integrates a shared encoder-decoder model with full attention, enhancing its effectiveness for various text-to-text tasks. This structure bears resemblance to the BERT model, particularly in classification tasks, as illustrated through an example from the MNLI benchmark. Here, a premise and hypothesis are transformed into a sequence that includes a task prefix, allowing the model to predict a class label, such as "entailment." The key distinction between the prefix language model and BERT lies in the integration of the classifier into the output layer of the Transformer decoder.

The authors emphasize the challenges in comparing different model structures, particularly between encoder-decoder models and language models. They note that while an encoder-decoder model with L layers in both components has a similar parameter count to a language model with 2L layers, the computational costs differ. The encoder-decoder's structure allows for a more efficient computational process, applying layers only to their respective sequences. Despite these differences, empirical observations indicate that L-layer language models and L+L-layer encoder-decoder models exhibit comparable step times, leading to the assumption that they have roughly equivalent computational costs. The paper proposes various configurations for the encoder-decoder model, referring to a BERTBASE-sized layer stack in terms of layers and parameters for consistent comparison.
