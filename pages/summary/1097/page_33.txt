The paper investigates the effectiveness of various transfer learning strategies in natural language processing, specifically focusing on multi-task learning and fine-tuning methods. It presents a comparative analysis of different training strategies across multiple benchmarks, including GLUE, CNNDM, SQuAD, SGLUE, and language translation tasks (EnDe, EnFr, EnRo).

Key findings from the analysis include:

1. **Training Strategies**: The results indicate that unsupervised pre-training followed by fine-tuning yields the highest performance across most tasks. Multi-task training and multi-task pre-training with fine-tuning also perform well, though slightly lower than the unsupervised approach.

2. **Combining Multi-Task Learning with Fine-Tuning**: The study explores a relaxed version of multi-task learning, where a model is trained on multiple tasks and evaluated with different parameter settings. The "MT-DNN" method, which pre-trains on a mixture of tasks before fine-tuning on individual tasks, demonstrates state-of-the-art results.

3. **Leave-One-Out Multi-Task Training**: This variant allows for assessing the model's ability to adapt to tasks not seen during pre-training. Results suggest that fine-tuning after a diverse multi-task pre-training approach can enhance performance, indicating that early exposure to varied tasks is beneficial.

4. **Performance Metrics**: The paper presents detailed performance metrics in Table 12, showing that fine-tuning after multi-task pre-training can mitigate some trade-offs in performance across different tasks.

Overall, the paper concludes that integrating multi-task learning with fine-tuning strategies can effectively leverage the strengths of transfer learning, leading to improved model performance in various natural language processing tasks.
