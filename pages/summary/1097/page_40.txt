The authors present significant advancements in the SuperGLUE benchmark, improving the average score from 84.6 to 88.9, closely approaching human performance of 89.8. Their model outperforms humans in reading comprehension tasks, indicating potential biases in evaluation metrics. However, it struggles with tasks like COPA and WSC, where human accuracy remains at 100%. 

In translation tasks, the authors did not achieve state-of-the-art results, attributed partly to their use of an English-only unlabeled dataset. They note that leading results often involve sophisticated techniques like backtranslation and cross-lingual unsupervised training, which their model lacks. Additionally, comparisons to the best results on certain datasets are complicated by differences in training set sizes.

The authors report state-of-the-art performance on the CNN/Daily Mail summarization task, particularly on the ROUGE-2-F score, though they caution that ROUGE improvements do not always correlate with summary coherence. They highlight that while extractive methods can be effective in this context, their model generates coherent and mostly accurate summaries.

To understand the contributions of their design choices, they compare three model configurations: a standard baseline, a baseline trained on 1 trillion tokens, and T5-Base. Results show that while additional pre-training enhances performance, T5-Base outperforms the baseline-1T across all tasks, indicating that factors beyond scale significantly influence T5's success.
