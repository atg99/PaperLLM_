The paper "Exploring the Limits of Transfer Learning" examines various aspects of transfer learning in natural language processing (NLP), particularly focusing on the self-attention mechanism within Transformer architectures. It highlights different attention mask patterns that dictate the relationships between input and output sequences. The fully-visible mask allows complete access to the input at each output timestep, while the causal mask restricts access to future input elements, and a prefix causal mask permits full visibility for a portion of the input.

The paper also discusses inter-run variance, noting that for most tasks, the standard deviation across runs is less than 1% of the task's baseline score. However, exceptions are noted for low-resource tasks such as CoLA, CB, and COPA, where variability can complicate model comparisons. For instance, the CB task showed an average F1 score of 91.22 with a standard deviation of 3.237, influenced by its limited validation set of 56 examples.

Furthermore, the paper reviews different architectural variants of Transformers, emphasizing the importance of the masking strategies used in attention mechanisms. It underscores that the self-attention operation generates an output sequence by computing a weighted average of input sequence entries, which varies significantly depending on the architecture employed. Overall, the findings suggest that careful consideration of architectural choices and their implications for transfer learning is crucial for optimizing performance in NLP tasks.
