This paper investigates the boundaries of transfer learning in language models, particularly focusing on how input is processed and output generated through a text-to-text framework. The authors emphasize the use of autoregressive sampling for tasks like automatic summarization, where a prefix prompts the model to generate a concise summary. Unlike previous work by Keskar et al., which unifies various NLP tasks into span extraction, this framework accommodates both generative tasks such as machine translation and summarization, where output choices cannot be exhaustively listed.

The study successfully reformulates numerous tasks into a text-to-text format, with the exception of the STS-B regression task. For STS-B, similarity scores are rounded to the nearest increment of 0.2 and converted to string representations, transforming the regression problem into a 21-class classification challenge.

Additionally, the authors adapt the Winograd tasks, which involve ambiguous pronouns, into a format suitable for their framework. They highlight the ambiguous pronoun within a passage and task the model with predicting the noun it refers to. For the WSC task, only "True" labeled examples are used for training, with evaluation based on whether the model's output matches the candidate noun phrase. The inclusion of the DPR dataset, which provides annotated examples, supplements the training data.

To prevent validation data leakage, the authors refrain from training on the WNLI dataset, which overlaps with WSC. This approach ensures the integrity of the multi-task experiments presented later in the paper. Overall, the study effectively demonstrates the adaptability of transfer learning models across various NLP tasks while addressing challenges encountered in traditional frameworks.
