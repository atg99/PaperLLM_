The paper investigates the effectiveness of various pre-training objectives for transfer learning, revealing that denoising objectives tend to outperform language modeling and deshuffling. It notes that while different denoising objectives do not show significant performance variations, they can influence sequence lengths and training speeds. Therefore, the choice of objective should primarily consider computational cost. The authors suggest that further exploration of similar objectives may not yield substantial improvements, advocating instead for innovative approaches to utilizing unlabeled data.

The pre-training data set is identified as a critical factor in the transfer learning pipeline, yet it is often overlooked in favor of new methods or models. The paper highlights the scarcity of comparative studies on pre-training data sets and the absence of a standardized data set. It references recent work that compares large, newly sourced data sets like Common Crawl to smaller, established ones such as Wikipedia.

To assess the impact of different pre-training data sets on performance, the authors compare variants of their C4 data set, which was developed through specific filtering heuristics applied to Common Crawl data. They analyze the performance of a baseline model pre-trained on several data set versions: the original C4, an unfiltered version that omits the applied heuristics, and a RealNews-like variant that focuses on news content. The paper aims to provide insights into how these variations affect downstream task performance.
