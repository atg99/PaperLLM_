The paper presents a comparison of various methods for processing network flows, as summarized in Table 2. The average flow rates per second for each method are as follows: Logistic Regression (LR) leads with 7,344,471 flows/s, followed closely by Gradient Descent (GD) at 7,307,621 flows/s. The Deep Neural Network (DNN) processes 87,561 flows/s, while the Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) processes 18,318 flows/s. The Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) handles 6,469 flows/s, the Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) method manages 5,298 flows/s, the k-Nearest Neighbors (kNN) processes 1,597 flows/s, and the Support Vector Machine (SVM) performs the worst at 1,470 flows/s.

This real-world data, gathered over a week from about 7,000 active hosts, serves to validate the feasibility of the tested methods in practical scenarios. The paper emphasizes the significant performance disparity among the methods, particularly highlighting the superior efficiency of LR and GD in processing flow rates.
