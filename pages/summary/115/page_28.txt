The paper evaluates the performance of various machine learning methods for identifying normal and attack IP flows, specifically focusing on GRU, LSTM, CNN, SVM, kNN, GD, LR, and DNN. The results indicate that GRU outperformed the other methods with specificity and recall rates of 99.7% and 98.3%, respectively. Other methods such as SVM and CNN achieved lower performance, with specificity rates of 98.2% and 97.7%, followed by kNN, GD, LR, and DNN, which had significantly lower rates.

The superior performance of GRU is attributed to its capability of learning long-term dependencies, which enhances the classification accuracy for both normal and abnormal flows, leading to more balanced precision and recall rates. Although LSTM also possesses this capability, it performed worse than GRU in the tested scenarios. The paper references findings by Jozefowicz et al. (2015), which suggest that while both GRU and LSTM may yield similar results, GRU tends to be more efficient due to its simpler structure, resulting in faster training times. The choice between these methods may depend on specific application contexts, including dataset size and feature analysis.
