The paper discusses the application of Gated Recurrent Units (GRU), a modified version of Long-Short Term Memory (LSTM) networks, for detecting Distributed Denial of Service (DDoS) and intrusion attacks in Software Defined Networking (SDN) environments. LSTMs, introduced by Hochreiter and Schmidhuber in 1997, utilize gates to manage learning and forgetting, ensuring that long-term data influences recent predictions. Cho et al. in 2014 proposed GRUs, which streamline LSTM's operations by using fewer gates while preserving the importance of long-term memory. 

While both LSTM and GRU offer similar classification accuracy, GRUs have a faster training process due to fewer tensor operations. The GRU employs two gates: the Update gate, which determines what new information to retain and what to forget, and the Reset gate, which controls the retention of long-term information. The gates operate using a sigmoidal activation function, outputting values between 0 and 1, where 0 indicates forgetting and 1 indicates retention. The computation of the hidden state at time t involves concatenating the previous hidden state and the current input, followed by processing through the Reset and Update gates.

Overall, the paper advocates for the use of GRUs in security contexts, emphasizing their efficiency and effectiveness in managing time-dependent data for attack detection.
