GRUs are a type of recurrent neural network that incorporates gating mechanisms to better manage the flow of information, thereby mitigating the vanishing gradient problem. By utilizing reset and update gates, GRUs can effectively decide which information to retain or discard, allowing them to maintain relevant past information while processing sequences.

The paper emphasizes the significance of leveraging deep learning techniques, particularly recurrent networks, for detecting network anomalies and attacks. Unlike feedforward networks, GRUs and RNNs utilize their memory capacity to analyze both current and historical data, which enhances their predictive capabilities. This is crucial in network security, where understanding the context of previous IP flows can lead to more accurate detection of malicious activities.

Despite their advantages, the paper acknowledges challenges associated with RNNs, particularly their struggle with long-term dependencies due to the vanishing gradient problem. Various strategies have been proposed to overcome these limitations, with GRUs being highlighted for their effectiveness in handling sequential data in network communications. Overall, the research supports the notion that advanced neural network architectures can significantly improve classification outcomes in the detection of network threats.
