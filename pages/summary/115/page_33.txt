The study presents a Software Defined Networking (SDN) defense system aimed at protecting the SDN central controller from intrusion and Distributed Denial of Service (DDoS) attacks. The system comprises two main components: the Detection module, which identifies attacks, and the Mitigation module, which implements countermeasures to lessen the attack's impact. The approach classifies IP flows into normal and abnormal categories, facilitating quicker detection and reducing the attack influence on the SDN controller.

The paper highlights the effectiveness of the Gated Recurrent Units (GRU) method within the Detection module, which offers a streamlined alternative to Long-Short Term Memory (LSTM) methods while achieving comparable performance. During testing, the GRU demonstrated the capability to analyze significantly higher flow rates, especially during peak traffic, while maintaining balanced accuracy, precision, recall, and F-measure metrics.

In conclusion, the findings suggest that GRU is a viable option for real-world anomaly detection within the proposed SDN defense system. The study emphasizes the importance of individualized IP flow analysis for swift detection and identification of potential attackers, setting the stage for future work in enhancing SDN security measures.
