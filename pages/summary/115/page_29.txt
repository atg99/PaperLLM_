This paper examines the efficiency of various detection methods for Distributed Denial of Service (DDoS) and intrusion attacks, highlighting the superior performance of Gated Recurrent Units (GRU) in handling massive datasets. The analysis includes a comparison of different algorithms, such as CNN, LSTM, SVM, Logistic Regression (LR), and k-Nearest Neighbors (kNN), in terms of their effectiveness in identifying legitimate and malicious flows. 

A key finding is that GRU demonstrates a lower number of legitimate flows dropped while maintaining a higher rate of detecting malicious flows, as illustrated in the provided figures. The study emphasizes the importance of analyzing individual IP flows for accurate attack detection, which allows for direct identification of attacker nodes. 

The proposed mitigation strategy involves dropping packets from identified targets immediately upon detection, with the effectiveness of this approach being contingent on the detection method used. The paper evaluates mitigation outcomes using two metrics: the total number of legitimate flows dropped and the total number of malicious flows that were not dropped, providing a comprehensive assessment of each detection method's efficiency.
