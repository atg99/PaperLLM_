This paper presents a novel system designed to defend Software-Defined Networks (SDN) against intrusion and Distributed Denial-of-Service (DDoS) attacks through a dual approach encompassing detection and mitigation. The detection module utilizes a binary flow classification method to analyze individual IP flows, categorizing them as normal or abnormal. A key feature of this method is its ability to identify the address of the attacking node, enabling a targeted mitigation strategy that aims to restore the SDN's normal operation efficiently.

To assess the effectiveness of the Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) detection method, the authors conducted experiments against seven other shallow and deep learning techniques using two public datasets: CICDDoS 2019 and CICIDS 2018. These datasets were chosen due to their diverse range of attack types and extensive IP flow features, which are crucial for deep learning applications. The experiments aimed to evaluate both the detection methods and the efficiency of the proposed mitigation approach across various attack scenarios.

The main contributions of this work include:
- Development of a system for defending SDN against intrusion and DDoS attacks.
- Implementation of an anomaly detection scheme based on isolated IP flow analysis, facilitating near real-time detection and prompt mitigation responses.
- Comprehensive evaluation and comparison of different anomaly detection techniques using public datasets, along with measurement of the proposed mitigation process's efficiency.

The paper is structured to first review related work, followed by a detailed explanation of the proposed system's organization, the GRU method for anomaly detection, and a discussion of the performance outcomes compared to other methods.
