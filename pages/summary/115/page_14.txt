The paper discusses a method for detecting and mitigating network anomalies in a Software Defined Networking (SDN) environment using a structured approach to analyzing IP flow records. The detection process begins with the export of flow records that primarily consist of quantitative features. Qualitative dimensions, such as the protocol, undergo MD5 hashing to convert them into quantitative values. Importantly, the source and destination IP addresses and ports are excluded from analysis to prevent the detection method from learning specific patterns that could impair its generalization capabilities, especially in the context of DDoS attacks.

Once the flow record is processed, it is analyzed by a Detection module employing a GRU-based binary classification method. If an anomaly is identified, an alarm is triggered, activating the Mitigation module. This module generates a drop policy based on the detected attack, which includes extracting the attacker's IP address. The policy is then sent to the SDN controller for implementation, allowing network devices to execute the necessary mitigation actions.

The paper emphasizes the autonomous operation of this defense system, which streamlines the process from flow extraction to detection and mitigation, thus ensuring a rapid response to minimize the impact of attacks. Additionally, while the network administrator receives a notification of the alarm, it serves merely as a warning rather than an immediate intervention.
