The paper discusses the critical need for effective protection mechanisms in Software Defined Networks (SDNs) to ensure network availability and service quality. It highlights the concept of anomaly detection, which identifies when network behavior deviates from normal patterns. Despite the various methods proposed for anomaly detection, the field remains open for research due to the diversity of network scenarios and architectures.

In SDN environments, security is a major concern, prompting significant interest from the scientific community. Among the different detection methods, IP flow-based approaches have shown effectiveness due to their ability to provide detailed information about regular network operations. However, existing research often relies on sampling methods that can overlook subtle attacks, such as port scans.

The authors propose a novel defense system that focuses on analyzing individual IP flow records to enhance intrusion and denial-of-service attack detection in SDNs. This approach allows for quicker mitigation responses, thereby maintaining service quality. The proposed system comprises two main components: the Detection Module and the Mitigation Module. The Detection Module utilizes a Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU), a type of recurrent deep learning algorithm, to analyze individual IP flows for anomalies. This method leverages deep learning's capability to learn complex data representations, which is increasingly applied in network security contexts.
