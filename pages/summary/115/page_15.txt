The paper discusses a system designed for detecting network attacks using a combination of flow analysis and deep learning techniques. It begins by illustrating a directed mitigation approach, where the system records flow data, identifies the attacker's IP address, and implements a drop policy to block traffic from the malicious source. This process involves sending flow records to a Mitigation Module, which extracts the attacker's IP and enforces a drop policy.

The paper then highlights the role of deep learning, specifically Gated Recurrent Units (GRUs), in identifying network attacks. Deep learning has gained traction in various fields due to its ability to uncover patterns in complex data without the need for manual feature engineering. Unlike shallow learning models, which typically use one or two layers, deep learning models employ multiple layers, allowing for more sophisticated representation of data. This depth enables the automatic identification of significant features during training, facilitated by weight matrices that prioritize influential characteristics for classification.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the effectiveness of deep learning in enhancing network security by automating the detection of anomalies and attacks within network traffic.
