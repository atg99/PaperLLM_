In Nanda et al. (2016), machine learning algorithms were trained on historical data to identify network attacks, with Bayesian Network achieving approximately 91% prediction accuracy. Kornycky et al. (2017) explored low-cost WLAN dongles for monitoring networks and classifying traffic, where their proposed TRAP-VQ technique outperformed others in terms of f-measure, demonstrating efficiency in WLAN traffic characterization. 

Fukuda et al. (2017) utilized DNS backscatter to classify malicious traffic activity, employing algorithms such as CART, random forest, and SVM, which yielded accuracy and precision rates around 75%. This indicates the potential of DNS backscatter as a valuable source of network activity information and the effectiveness of the tested machine learning methods for detecting malicious actions.

Additionally, some approaches to network anomaly detection involve sampling data in time intervals, as seen in studies like Cortez et al. (2006) and Bereziński et al. (2015), which use five-minute intervals. Faster anomaly detection is achievable with smaller intervals, albeit at the cost of increased processing demands. Sun et al. (2016) introduced a method based on Streaming Performance Metrics and Logs that operates in one-minute intervals.

The emergence of Deep Learning techniques is also noted, as they leverage multiple layers for data representation learning, particularly effective with large datasets. This growing trend indicates a shift towards more sophisticated methods in the realm of network anomaly detection.
