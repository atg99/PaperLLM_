This paper evaluates the efficiency of a proposed method for anomaly detection in software-defined networks (SDN) by comparing it with seven established techniques: Dense Neural Network (DNN), Convolutional Neural Network (CNN), Long-Short Term Memory (LSTM), Support Vector Machine (SVM), Logistic Regression (LR), k-Nearest Neighbors (kNN), and Gradient Descent (GD). 

Two test scenarios were conducted using public datasets. The first utilized the CICDDoS 2019 dataset, focusing on various types of DDoS attacks, where most methods performed similarly well. The second scenario involved the CICIDS 2018 dataset, which included different types of intrusion attacks in a more complex network environment, leading to varied results. In both scenarios, the Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) method achieved the best overall results, demonstrating superior accuracy, precision, recall, and f-measure. The GRU's ability to learn long-term dependencies is identified as a key factor in its performance, particularly in classifying legitimate traffic flows.

The analysis also included the throughput of each method, emphasizing the importance of speed for the proposed system. The results indicated that GRU is a promising approach for real-world anomaly detection in SDN environments. Additionally, the paper introduces a directed mitigation schema that leverages individualized flow information from the detection module to create targeted drop policies against identified attacker IPs. 

Future work aims to enhance the GRU method as a multi-label classifier to detect and identify anomalies and to optimize the drop time window in the mitigation module to improve efficiency and effectiveness. These proposed modifications are expected to significantly enhance the system's performance.
