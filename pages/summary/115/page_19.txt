The paper discusses the architecture and configuration of a Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) network designed for binary classification of network flows as either legitimate or malicious. The network consists of a GRU layer with 32 cells, a Dropout layer with a drop rate of 0.5 to mitigate overfitting, and a Fully-Connected layer with 10 neurons for global classification. The output layer is a single neuron employing a sigmoid activation function.

Empirical testing was conducted to determine the optimal parameters, as illustrated in Figures 6 and 7. Figure 6 demonstrates that increasing the number of GRU cells enhances classification accuracy while balancing the flow throughput, establishing that 32 cells provide an effective compromise for the model's performance. Figure 7 evaluates the impact of varying the number of neurons in the Fully-Connected layer, revealing minimal influence on flow throughput. The analysis indicated that the lowest accuracy occurred with no neurons in this layer. Given that accuracy remained consistent with 10 or more neurons, the model was configured with 10 neurons to optimize computational efficiency. The Dropout rate was selected based on prior research indicating that a rate of 0.5 is generally optimal for many network tasks.
