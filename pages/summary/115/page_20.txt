In this section, the performance of various detection and mitigation methods for a security system is analyzed, focusing on the application of Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) cells. The study evaluates the accuracy and processing speed (flows per second) of different configurations of GRU cells using the CICDDoS 2019 dataset. Results indicate a direct correlation between the number of GRU cells and accuracy, with configurations achieving up to 100% accuracy.

The GRU method was compared against several established detection approaches, including Deep Neural Network (DNN), Convolutional Neural Network (CNN), Long-Short Term Memory (LSTM), Support Vector Machine (SVM), Logistic Regression (LR), k-Nearest Neighbors (kNN), and Gradient Descent (GD). These methods were chosen based on their applicability in similar environments.

The implementation utilized Python with Keras for GRU, LSTM, CNN, and DNN, and Sklearn for SVM, LR, kNN, and GD. The hardware setup included a Windows 10 system with an Intel Core i7 processor and 8GB of RAM. The DNN was structured with three hidden layers of 100, 40, and 10 neurons, while the CNN comprised three layers with varying filter sizes. The LSTM and GRU were both configured with 32 units, and the SVM employed a linear configuration.

Overall, the findings suggest that the GRU method, particularly with the right number of cells, offers competitive performance in terms of accuracy and processing efficiency compared to other machine learning approaches.
