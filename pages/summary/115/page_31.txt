The paper presents an analysis of the flow data available for testing in two datasets: CICDDoS 2019 and CICIDS 2018. It highlights a significant disparity in the volume of flows, with CICIDS 2018 containing six times more flows than CICDDoS 2019. This difference impacts the complexity of detecting intrusion attacks, which are generally stealthier compared to flooding attacks. The authors emphasize that a larger Software Defined Networking (SDN) environment correlates with more pronounced variations in detection efficacy, particularly affecting legitimate users.

The mitigation strategy discussed relies heavily on the efficiency of the detection method. While the approach has demonstrated efficiency and a quick mitigation process, it poses risks of recurring misclassifications. The authors suggest that implementing a drop time window could enhance mitigation outcomes in future research.

Regarding feasibility, the proposed SDN defense system focuses on individual flow analysis, necessitating a lightweight and efficient detection method to avoid delays that could exacerbate damage within the SDN. The paper measures the efficiency of various anomaly detection methods by analyzing the average number of flows they can process per second across ten executions, showing minimal variation and confirming the reliability of results. The Logistic Regression (LR) and Gradient Descent (GD) methods emerged as the fastest, although their classification accuracy was not detailed in the excerpt.
