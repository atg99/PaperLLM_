This paper discusses various approaches to anomaly detection in network environments, particularly focusing on IoT systems and industrial control systems. It highlights several frameworks and methods, including Kao and Jiang's anomaly detection framework that classifies time series data into stationary, periodic, and non-stationary categories, employing statistical and deep learning techniques like GRU, LSTM, and SARIMA. Their results demonstrate superior performance in precision, recall, and f-measures compared to existing methods.

Additionally, Qin et al. explored LSTM networks for anomaly detection in IP networks, yielding promising precision and recall rates. Xie et al. introduced a model using 1D-CNN and GRU for predicting parameters in industrial control systems, validated with the Secure Water Treatment dataset, and achieved favorable metrics. Qu et al. modified traditional GRU for identifying user anomalies in web logs, showing improved outcomes over LSTM and SVM models. Liu et al. presented a GRU and Support Vector Domain Description (SVDD) approach for network log anomalies, employing Principal Component Analysis (PCA) for dimensionality reduction and demonstrating greater efficiency over classical GRU-MLP and LSTM algorithms on the KDD Cup 99 dataset.

The paper proposes a novel Software-Defined Networking (SDN) defense system against DDoS and intrusion attacks. This system distinguishes itself by operating without sampling and analyzing individual IP flow data directly, which facilitates faster detection and mitigates attack impacts through a multidimensional analysis using GRU deep learning methods.
