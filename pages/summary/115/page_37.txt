This paper presents an overview of various research efforts focused on intrusion detection systems (IDS) and security mechanisms, particularly in the context of the Internet of Things (IoT) and Software-Defined Networking (SDN). 

Divyatmika et al. propose a two-tier network-based IDS architecture utilizing machine learning to enhance detection capabilities. Farris et al. survey emerging security mechanisms in SDN and Network Function Virtualization (NFV) for IoT systems, highlighting the need for robust security frameworks. Fernandes et al. provide a comprehensive survey on network anomaly detection, discussing various detection techniques and their effectiveness.

Fukuda et al. investigate the detection of malicious activities through DNS backscatter over time, contributing to the understanding of network threats. Gkountis et al. introduce a lightweight algorithm aimed at protecting SDN controllers from Distributed Denial of Service (DDoS) attacks, showcasing the importance of security in SDN environments.

Guo et al. focus on unsupervised anomaly detection in IoT systems, particularly in smart cities, emphasizing the significance of identifying unusual patterns without labeled data. Hajiheidari et al. conduct a comprehensive investigation into IDS within IoT, addressing the unique challenges posed by this environment.

Lastly, He and Droppo explore the application of Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) structures in deep neural networks for speech recognition, while Hochreiter and Schmidhuber's foundational work on LSTM provides a theoretical basis for advancements in sequential data processing.

Overall, these contributions reflect a growing body of work aimed at enhancing security and detection mechanisms across diverse networking and IoT contexts.
