2.3 Model architecture and training
The study compares Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks and Gated Recurrent Units (GRU) in terms of their ability to learn from the generated symbolic sequences. Both architectures are designed to capture dependencies in the data, but they differ in their gating mechanisms and complexity. The models are trained using the generated sequences, with a focus on optimizing parameters such as learning rate and batch size to enhance learning efficiency.

The performance of each model is evaluated based on its accuracy in predicting subsequent characters in the sequences, using a text similarity metric to quantify the predictions' closeness to the actual sequences. This metric allows for a comprehensive assessment of the models' effectiveness in handling sequences of varying complexities.

3 Results and discussion
The results indicate that both LSTM and GRU networks exhibit strong capabilities in learning symbolic sequences, with performance varying based on the complexity of the input strings. The study finds that GRUs tend to perform comparably or better than LSTMs in certain scenarios, suggesting that their simpler architecture may provide advantages in specific applications.

The findings contribute to the understanding of how different RNN architectures handle symbolic sequence learning and offer insights into optimal parameter choices for future research in this area. The use of LZW complexity as a measure of string complexity proves instrumental in analyzing the models' learning behaviors.

In conclusion, this research highlights the strengths and limitations of LSTM and GRU networks in the context of symbolic sequence learning, providing valuable information for practitioners and researchers in the field.
