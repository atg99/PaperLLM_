The paper compares the performance of Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks and Gated Recurrent Units (GRU) in learning symbolic sequences. It finds that both LSTM and GRU achieve similar forecasting accuracy, but LSTMs have the advantage of faster training times. LSTMs are noted for their superior ability to capture long-term dependencies compared to GRUs. 

The tests conducted demonstrate that both network types can learn sequences with high accuracy, although challenges arise with very complex strings. As string complexity increases, a decline in mean text similarity and a rise in outlier scattering were observed, indicating limitations in the networks' learning capacity. The results highlight a saturation effect in learning, particularly with seed strings of 52 symbols, where both LSTM and GRU showed a decrease in mean text similarity with increasing complexity. The findings emphasize the need for careful consideration of network choice and complexity when working with symbolic sequences.
