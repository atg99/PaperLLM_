The study conducted by Roberto Cahuantzi et al. investigates the impact of increasing the number of RNN layers on accuracy and training time, utilizing a consistent loss-based stopping criterion and a learning rate of 0.01. The research focuses on sequences of a minimum length of 1,100 characters, with training and testing based on leading strings, and validation using trailing characters. The LZW complexities of the seed strings vary from 2 to 12, containing between 2 and 6 distinct symbols.

Both LSTM and GRU networks employ a single hidden layer, with the number of units ranging from 25 to 250 in ten geometrically spaced increments. The Adam optimizer is utilized with learning rates of 0.001 and 0.01. Each configuration is executed five times, and results are averaged.

The findings indicate that while adding layers marginally increases accuracy for deep learning (DL) and Jaro-Winkler (JW) text similarities, the significant increase in training time raises concerns regarding the justification for deeper networks. Specifically, the median training time for LSTMs was 37.19 seconds, while for GRUs it was 19.72 seconds, suggesting GRUs are generally faster to train for the same loss function value. Both RNN types exhibit comparable forecast accuracy, with median distance metrics for JW and DL being 1.0 for both networks. However, LSTMs displayed a slight advantage in accuracy, achieving values of 0.93 and 0.97 for DL and JW distances, respectively, compared to GRU's 0.88 for DL and 0.96 for JW. The study emphasizes that small differences in JW distances correspond to more contrasting strings than those in DL, highlighting the nuanced performance differences between the two RNN architectures.
