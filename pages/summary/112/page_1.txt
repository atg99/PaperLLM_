This paper investigates the capabilities of Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks and Gated Recurrent Units (GRUs) in learning symbolic sequences of varying complexity. The authors analyze how different configurations affect the memorization abilities of recurrent neural networks (RNNs). Through their experiments, they reveal that simply increasing the depth of the RNN does not guarantee improved memorization, particularly when training time is limited. The study identifies critical hyperparameters, notably the learning rate and the number of units per layer, which significantly impact performance. The findings indicate that GRUs generally excel in learning low-complexity sequences, whereas LSTMs tend to perform better with high-complexity sequences. The research underlines the importance of tuning hyperparameters to enhance the learning efficiency of RNN architectures in sequence learning tasks.
