The paper discusses the implementation of Gated Recurrent Units (GRUs) and the associated equations that define their functioning, specifically highlighting the candidate hidden state (_ht). The GRU architecture is compared with Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks, as illustrated in Figures 2 and 3.

In the context of text similarity metrics, the authors emphasize the importance of using appropriate metrics for evaluating string prediction accuracy, given that their experiments are based on string sequences rather than numerical data. They outline two key text edit metrics: the Damerau-Levenshtein (DL) distance and the Jaro-Winkler (JW) distance. 

The DL distance quantifies the number of edits required to transform one string into another, while the JW distance provides a more nuanced measure that accounts for string insertions and character position changes. The paper includes a formal definition of the DL distance algorithm, detailing how it calculates the minimum edit distance through various operations like deletion, insertion, substitution, and transposition. The JW distance is introduced as based on Jaro similarity, with its own formal definition provided to clarify its calculation methodology.

Overall, the paper effectively integrates the technical aspects of GRUs and text similarity metrics to enhance the understanding of string prediction accuracy in their experiments.
