The paper presents a comparison between Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks and Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) networks in the context of learning symbolic sequences. It introduces two distance metrics for evaluating the similarity between sequences: the Damerau-Levenshtein (DL) distance and the Jaro-Winkler (JW) distance.

DL distance is defined by the maximum allowable distance between strings, taking into account transpositions and matching characters. It is calculated based on the lengths of the strings and the number of transpositions. The JW distance, on the other hand, incorporates a common prefix length and a scaling factor that adjusts the score based on prefix similarity, where the scaling factor should not exceed 0.25, with a standard value of 0.1.

Both distance measures are normalized, yielding values between 0 and 1, where 0 indicates completely different strings and 1 indicates identical strings. The paper illustrates the progression of several string transformations, highlighting that the DL distance provides a broader range of values, while the JW distance offers a more nuanced understanding of prefix similarities and character positions.
