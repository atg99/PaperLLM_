This paper compares Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks and Gated Recurrent Units (GRUs) in the context of learning symbolic sequences. It begins by outlining the architecture of an LSTM unit, which includes components such as the candidate cell state, cell memory, and output computation. The candidate cell state is derived from the current input and the previous hidden state. The cell memory is updated through a combination of the forget gate, previous cell state, input gate, and candidate cell state. The final output is generated by applying weights to the hidden state.

In contrast, GRUs simplify the architecture by utilizing fewer parameters and introducing only two gates: the update gate and the reset gate. The update gate regulates the speed of hidden state updates, while the reset gate determines the retention of past information. This comparison highlights the structural differences between LSTMs and GRUs, emphasizing the efficiency of GRUs in certain contexts while maintaining the essential functionalities of LSTMs.
