The paper presents a comparison between Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks and Gated Recurrent Units (GRU) for learning symbolic sequences. It evaluates the performance of both architectures in handling time series data and symbolic sequence patterns. The authors analyze the strengths and weaknesses of LSTM and GRU, focusing on their ability to capture temporal dependencies and manage vanishing gradient issues.

The study emphasizes that while LSTMs have a more complex structure with multiple gates, GRUs simplify this with fewer parameters, potentially leading to faster training times and similar performance in certain contexts. Empirical results demonstrate that GRUs can match or exceed LSTM performance in specific tasks, suggesting that the choice between the two may depend on the nature of the data and the specific requirements of the task.

The paper concludes that both LSTM and GRU networks are effective for learning symbolic sequences, and the decision on which to use should consider factors such as model complexity, training efficiency, and the specific characteristics of the dataset being analyzed.
