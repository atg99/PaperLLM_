This paper presents a comparison between Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks and Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) networks in learning symbolic sequences. It investigates the impact of various training parameters, particularly focusing on training time, learning rates, and network architectures.

The study reveals that a learning rate of 0.01 is optimal across different stopping criteria. In terms of network structure, the research examines the effect of the number of layers on the networks' memorization capabilities. It trains both LSTM and GRU networks with layer configurations of 1 to 3 layers, maintaining a consistent total number of units. The results indicate that a single hidden layer network yields the best performance in terms of both training time and forecast accuracy, with only marginal improvements noted with additional layers.

A total of 3,239 recurrent neural networks (RNNs) were trained across various parameters and seed strings to avoid bias. The findings suggest that the learning rate and the number of hidden units are critical hyper-parameters influencing the effectiveness of the networks, aligning with previous research. Following these initial tests, the study plans to utilize single-layer RNNs and explore a narrower range of learning rates in further tests, focusing on seed strings with both low and high LZW complexities.
