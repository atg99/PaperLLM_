The paper discusses the effectiveness of hybrid forecasting methods that integrate recurrent neural networks (RNNs) with exponential smoothing, highlighting comparisons between LSTM and GRU networks on numerical time series data. It emphasizes the importance of data pre-processing in enhancing model performance, noting that techniques like data discretization can significantly improve neural network forecasting. The study also critiques common evaluation metrics such as mean squared error for their sensitivity to noise, particularly in high-dimensional time series, and suggests that symbolic time series representations can help mitigate these issues.

Key findings from the research reveal that the learning rate is a critical hyper-parameter for training RNNs, with optimal values around 0.01 leading to improved training time and forecast accuracy. The study indicates that a single-layer RNN with approximately 100 units often suffices for the tasks at hand, and that GRUs tend to outperform LSTMs on simpler sequences, while the opposite is true for more complex sequences.

The authors introduce a Python library, slearn, designed to assist the community in exploring machine learning models on symbolic sequences by generating synthetic sequences of varying complexities. Additionally, the paper contrasts their approach with global forecasting models (GFMs), noting that while GFMs are beneficial for generalization and training efficiency, they introduce greater complexity and challenges in hyper-parameter selection. By focusing on training individual RNN models, the study aims to provide clearer insights into the relationship between model architecture and the complexity of the symbolic sequences being learned.
