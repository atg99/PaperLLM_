The paper discusses the key dimensions of AI ethics, focusing on the conceptual, substantive, and procedural aspects. The conceptual dimension highlights how the definitions and notions surrounding AI shape normative intuitions and determine the scope of AI ethics. A critical examination of the term "artificial intelligence" reveals that it is a broad term encompassing various complex technologies, such as machine learning and neural networks, often used differently by computer scientists compared to public discourse.

The author emphasizes that current AI systems, while capable of processing vast amounts of data and performing specialized tasks, lack general intelligence akin to humans. This limitation suggests that AI is not truly "intelligent" or entirely "artificial," as these systems depend heavily on natural resources and extensive human labor. The paper argues for a conceptual critique as foundational to a normative critique of AI ethics, underscoring the need for clearer definitions and understandings in ethical discussions surrounding AI technologies.
