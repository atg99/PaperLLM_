The paper discusses the potential advantages of low-tech or no-tech solutions in addressing problems often tackled by AI technologies. It argues that considering the full costs of AI, including negative externalities, may render simpler solutions more ethically preferable. 

Two main procedural implications for AI ethics emerge from these discussions. Firstly, ethical debates should include the voices of all stakeholders affected by AI technologies, not just influential economic or political actors. This inclusion is framed as a matter of justice and is supported by concepts like community-oriented design ethics and design justice. The paper advocates for broader participation in discussions around the development, regulation, and impact of AI, emphasizing that ethical technology development cannot be confined to corporate settings alone. A cultural shift towards prioritizing social benefits over profits is deemed necessary for the responsible advancement of AI.

Secondly, the paper calls for integrating ethical knowledge and experiences into AI ethics discussions, which often lack input from trained ethicists. It emphasizes the need for a combined approach to technical and ethical issues from the outset, recommending that philosophical ethicists improve their communication with practitioners and policymakers. Additionally, it suggests that professionals in computer science, engineering, and politics receive substantial training in ethics and justice to enhance the quality of AI ethics discourse. Overall, the pursuit of ethically sound AI is presented as a long-term project that requires collective effort and a shift in corporate practices.
