The paper discusses the inequities embedded within the global infrastructure that supports AI applications, highlighting the systemic injustices that lead to unequal distribution of benefits and risks among various groups. It emphasizes the necessity of including diverse voices affected by AI technologies in discussions about their development, use, and disposal, as the implications extend beyond the conventional debates.

A significant ethical challenge identified is "technosolutionism," which questions whether AI is the appropriate means to address societal issues. The author argues that a preference for technological solutions can result in overlooking more suitable non-technological alternatives. This mindset, termed technological solutionism, is prevalent beyond AI, as illustrated by examples such as apps designed to address social problems and the medicalization of behavioral issues.

The paper also notes two dynamics that reinforce the tendency to resort to technological solutions. The first is the allure of powerful tools that prompt their use, potentially leading to the misapplication of technologies for problems they are ill-suited to solve. Furthermore, it discusses relational injustice, where dominant groups impose technological solutions that may not align with the needs or perspectives of those most affected by the issues in question. Overall, the paper calls for a more critical examination of the role of technology in problem-solving, advocating for a broader understanding of the ethical implications of AI.
