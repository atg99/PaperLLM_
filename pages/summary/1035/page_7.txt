The paper critiques the narrow focus on AI technologies in discussions of AI ethics, arguing that a broader examination of the social, political, and economic contexts is essential to understand the moral implications of AI. It emphasizes the need to expand the ethical discourse surrounding AI technologies by considering their material and infrastructural requirements, as well as the ethical issues stemming from their existence and use.

Two substantive criticisms are highlighted: firstly, AI technologies necessitate significant material resources and human labor, which raise ethical concerns often overlooked in current AI ethics literature. Secondly, the paper questions the reliance on AI to solve various problems, suggesting that this tendency may not be ethically justified.

Crawford and Joler's analysis of the 'Anatomy of an AI System' illustrates these points by examining the lifecycle of Amazon's Echo device. Their findings reveal the substantial human labor, data, and planetary resources required to create, operate, and dispose of such technologies, challenging the perception of AI as a clean, cloud-based intelligence. This analysis underscores the environmental and human costs associated with AI systems, calling for greater awareness and ethical consideration in the development and deployment of AI technologies. The paper advocates for a rethinking of AI ethics to incorporate these critical dimensions.
