The paper discusses the necessity for a more composed and balanced debate surrounding AI ethics, particularly in light of the disproportionate funding allocated to this field compared to other pressing ethical challenges such as climate change, pandemics, and social justice issues. It argues that AI ethics must broaden its scope to encompass the structural conditions that influence the development and application of AI technologies, rather than solely focusing on the moral implications of specific actions.

The author emphasizes the importance of considering the social, cultural, economic, and political contexts in which AI technologies operate. This involves recognizing the unequal power dynamics and privilege that shape the ethical landscape of technology. By doing so, discussions about AI can incorporate the entire lifecycle of technologies, including resource use and the distribution of benefits and costs, and consider who benefits from these technologies and at what expense.

Furthermore, the paper warns against limiting the search for solutions within AI ethics to merely technological strategies. Instead, it advocates for a broader understanding of the ethical implications of AI that includes structural factors and the wider societal impact of technology. This approach aims to correct the often narrow focus of current debates and foster a more comprehensive ethical discourse in the field.
