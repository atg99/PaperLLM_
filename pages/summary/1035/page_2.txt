The paper critically examines the optimistic claims surrounding Artificial Intelligence (AI) as a tool for enhancing human flourishing and societal well-being, as articulated by the High-Level Expert Group on Artificial Intelligence to the European Commission. It acknowledges the potential of AI to contribute to the UN's Sustainable Development Goals but aims to scrutinize the practice of AI ethics that guides its development and application.

The analysis unfolds in three sections. The first section introduces the dominant normative and methodological frameworks currently prevalent in AI ethics. The second section presents a critical evaluation of these frameworks, identifying conceptual, substantive, and procedural challenges that impact the ethical practice surrounding AI. The third section offers constructive recommendations for the future development of AI ethics.

Central to the paper's argument is the ideal of relational equality, which posits that all human beings possess equal moral worth. This perspective emphasizes the importance of equitable interactions and seeks to address the asymmetrical power dynamics that often result in unjust distributions of resources and opportunities. By applying a relational egalitarian framework to AI ethics, the paper advocates for a broader consideration of the social, economic, political, and environmental contexts in which AI operates. This approach aims to illuminate existing ethical and social issues, encouraging a more comprehensive understanding of the implications of AI technologies.
