The paper discusses the ethical reflections and arguments found in scholarly publications, policy documents, and tech industry guidelines concerning artificial intelligence (AI). It identifies three main approaches to AI ethics that correspond to the normative theories in Western moral philosophy: consequentialism, deontology, and virtue ethics. 

Consequentialism evaluates the risks and opportunities of AI technologies, weighing their potential benefits against harmful consequences. Deontology focuses on establishing moral principles that dictate what should be done regarding AI, akin to a duty-based framework. Virtue ethics emphasizes the ideals and virtues that should guide individuals in the development and use of AI, advocating for a vision of trustworthy and explainable AI.

The paper argues that these approaches are not mutually exclusive and can be employed simultaneously in ethical discussions. It highlights that the ethical evaluation of AI involves a diverse debate with various perspectives centering around key questions and normative tools for analysis and decision-making.

However, the author raises critical meta-questions about the current state of AI ethics, addressing conceptual clarity, underlying normative assumptions, and ethical decision-making procedures. The goal is to reflect on the ethical discourse surrounding AI, aiming to establish a more robust ethics of AI ethics itself.
