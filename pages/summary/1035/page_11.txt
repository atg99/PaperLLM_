The paper argues that addressing societal unfairness requires solutions at the social level rather than relying solely on technological advancements. While technology may offer superficial improvements, it often fails to tackle the root causes of social issues, leading to significant opportunity costs when resources are diverted to technological solutions. Each decision to pursue a technological fix must be justified by demonstrating that it is the most effective approach.

In discussing the procedural dimension of AI ethics, the paper critiques the institutional practices surrounding ethical assessments of AI technologies. It highlights that committees tasked with establishing ethical standards often lack the independence and critical perspective necessary for objective evaluations, resulting in biased ethical assessments. The focus is on the European AI landscape, though similar issues are noted globally.

The paper also points out that multinational corporations may prioritize their interests over independent ethical evaluations, using corporate ethics initiatives primarily to mitigate reputational risks and comply with existing standards rather than to genuinely engage in ethical scrutiny. This trend reflects a diminishing commitment to independent ethics as conflicts with corporate interests become more apparent.
