This paper aims to enhance current AI ethics practices by addressing key ethical issues that arise from AI's significant impact on human life and societal interactions. It identifies core questions concerning the understanding and control of complex AI systems, accountability for harm caused by AI, the prevention of discrimination and bias in AI outputs, the protection of individual privacy, the safeguarding of autonomous decision-making against AI influence, and the risk of malicious superintelligence prioritizing its own objectives over human welfare.

The discourse on AI ethics has attracted diverse contributions from academia, corporations, and political institutions, reflecting the economic, geopolitical, and social implications of AI technologies. These contributions manifest in various forms, including reports from policy bodies, corporate guidelines, design checklists, and scholarly publications across multiple disciplines. The paper emphasizes the importance of a comprehensive and inclusive ethical framework that incorporates insights from various stakeholders, including engaged citizens and civil society organizations, to effectively address the complex ethical landscape of AI.
