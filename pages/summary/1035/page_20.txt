This collection of references encompasses a range of topics related to artificial intelligence, ethics, justice, and social responsibility. Key contributions include McKeown's exploration of structural injustice, Mills' work on personalized nudging, and the analysis of responsibility gaps in AI by Santoni de Sio and Mecacci. Morozov critiques technological solutionism, while Zuboff discusses the implications of surveillance capitalism. The ethical considerations surrounding AI are further elaborated by Ryan, Roberts et al., and Vallor, who emphasize the importance of trust, accountability, and the philosophical foundations of technology. The works of Rawls and Young provide a theoretical backdrop on justice and oppression, highlighting the social dimensions of technological advancement. This compilation serves as a critical resource for understanding the multifaceted ethical landscape of AI and its societal impacts.
