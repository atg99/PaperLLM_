The paper discusses the conceptual challenges associated with the term "AI" and its implications for AI ethics. It argues that the term can mislead perceptions of the technology, which may distort ethical considerations. The author compares AI to electricity, emphasizing its pervasive role across various sectors such as healthcare, communication, and warfare. This analogy highlights AI's status as a fundamental infrastructure rather than a distinct ethical topic. 

The paper critiques the notion of a separate AI ethics, suggesting that such a distinction may be unwarranted given that AI technologies are increasingly integral to everyday life. By treating AI as a universal tool, the author warns against the risks of mislabeling and narrowing the focus of ethical discussions. This could lead to misguided expectations and concerns that obscure broader societal issues influenced by AI. Overall, the paper calls for a more integrated approach to ethical assessments that considers AI as part of larger societal structures rather than as an isolated phenomenon.
