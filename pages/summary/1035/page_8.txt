The paper discusses the unequal distribution of financial gains from AI systems, exemplified by the stark contrast between the wealth of corporate leaders like Amazon's CEO and the plight of underpaid laborers in hazardous conditions, such as those in cobalt mines. It highlights the environmental harm caused by toxic waste from unused devices, framing these issues as examples of distributive injustice rooted in power asymmetries that enable exploitation.

The author addresses potential objections to the relevance of these issues in AI ethics, arguing that such concerns are indeed pertinent. They note that fields like medical ethics are increasingly recognizing the connections between service provision and environmental impact. The paper contends that ethical assessments must consider the broader social structures that underpin actions, as these structures can influence the moral implications of seemingly benign behaviors.

Expanding the focus of ethical inquiry to include these structural injustices is essential for a comprehensive understanding of AI ethics. The author suggests that integrating these considerations aligns with critical theory, emphasizing that the financial disparities among contributors to AI systems reflect deeper relational injustices, where some individuals benefit disproportionately at the expense of others, further perpetuating cycles of domination and disadvantage.
