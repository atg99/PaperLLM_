The paper discusses the prevalent tendency in modern societies to favor technological solutions, particularly digital technologies, for addressing various problems. This inclination often leads to a narrow focus on the technological aspects of issues, overshadowing their broader social, political, and economic dimensions. While acknowledging the effectiveness and desirability of AI and other advanced technologies in solving certain problems, the author critiques the concept of technosolutionism. 

The critique is not against the use of technology per se but emphasizes the need for ethical considerations in determining whether a problem should be framed as a technological issue at all. Many problems, especially those of a social or political nature, may require solutions that do not involve technology, highlighting the importance of considering low-tech or no-tech approaches.

The paper further illustrates this point with the example of algorithmic bias, arguing that such biases stem from deeper social issues rather than being purely technological problems. Thus, effectively addressing algorithmic bias requires a comprehensive understanding that goes beyond technological fixes to include the underlying social dynamics at play. The author advocates for an ethical framework in AI that broadens its focus to encompass all dimensions of perceived problems, thereby avoiding a technosolutionist mindset.
