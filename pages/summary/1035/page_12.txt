The paper discusses the challenges of corporate ethics in the context of artificial intelligence (AI) and the role of ethics committees. It highlights the difficulty of maintaining independent ethical assessments while being affiliated with corporate interests. The focus shifts to an ethics expert advisory group established by the European Commission, aimed at providing independent ethical guidance on AI technologies.

The document, "Ethics Guidelines for Trustworthy AI," emphasizes the importance of addressing various ethical issues related to AI and advocates for a systemic approach involving all stakeholders throughout the AI lifecycle. The paper contrasts the European strategy with AI development in the United States and China, noting different motivations and approaches to ethics in technology.

Critically, the composition of the Independent High-Level Expert Group, primarily composed of industry representatives with few ethics experts, raises concerns about the group's ability to provide comprehensive ethical guidance. This imbalance suggests a potential alignment with the European Commission's economic interests rather than a genuine commitment to establishing robust ethical standards. The paper argues for greater inclusion of trained ethicists and civil society representatives to ensure a more thorough ethical framework for AI development.
