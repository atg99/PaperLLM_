This paper identifies six key challenges in AI ethics, highlighting issues such as misleading intuitions about artificial intelligence, a lack of recognition of AI's pervasive role in society, and the influence of commercial and political interests on ethical discussions. It categorizes these challenges into conceptual, substantive, and procedural issues, all of which point to ethical shortcomings in the current discourse.

To enhance the ethical framework of AI ethics, the author proposes a broadened perspective that integrates social, economic, political, and environmental contexts, advocating for a shift from AI ethics to AI justice. The paper emphasizes the need for a clear conceptual specification of AI ethics to improve ethical discussions. This includes an honest assessment of AI's capabilities and limitations, as well as a recognition of its integration into societal infrastructure. 

The author suggests that such clarifications will help ground ethical debates, reduce misconceptions about AI, and promote a more nuanced understanding of its implications, ultimately leading to more constructive ethical discussions.
