This paper presents the training algorithm for CogLTX, which operates in both unsupervised and supervised modes. The algorithm begins by initializing relevance labels for the training set using methods such as Bm25 or Glove when applicable. The training process runs for a specified number of epochs and involves extracting random spans from the input data and computing loss values through cross-entropy for both relevant and irrelevant blocks. The judge model is updated based on the aggregated losses.

In unsupervised mode, the algorithm infers relevance labels by testing the necessity and sufficiency of blocks through loss comparisons after removing certain blocks. This allows the model to dynamically adjust labels to improve training efficiency. A buffer zone is established to prevent frequent label changes, ensuring stability in the training process.

The paper also draws connections to latent variable models, highlighting that the unsupervised CogLTX can be considered a generalization of these models. It emphasizes the unique challenges posed by the discrete distribution of the latent variable z, which requires a more efficient point estimation method rather than traditional sampling approaches used in Bayesian frameworks. The approach is likened to K-means clustering, suggesting that the method optimizes z through local searches rather than relying solely on probabilistic inference.

Overall, the algorithm effectively addresses the challenges of training with limited relevance labels while maintaining a focus on optimizing the judge and reasoner models to improve performance in tasks such as text classification.
