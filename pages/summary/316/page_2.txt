The paper introduces the CogLTX framework, which aims to enhance the reasoning capabilities of BERT (Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers) for processing long texts. Traditional BERT faces limitations due to its maximum position embedding of 512 tokens, leading to challenges in memory consumption and long-distance attention when handling lengthy documents. The authors draw an analogy between BERT and human working memory, highlighting how humans manage information through processes like rehearsal and retrieval competition.

CogLTX addresses these limitations by implementing a MemRecall mechanism, which identifies and extracts relevant text blocks, akin to episodic memories, to facilitate multi-step reasoning. This approach allows for reasoning over concatenated key sentences, reducing the burden on memory while maintaining performance across various tasks. The framework introduces a secondary BERT model, termed "judge," which evaluates the relevance of the text blocks and is jointly trained with the original BERT reasoner.

Through experiments, the authors demonstrate that CogLTX achieves or surpasses state-of-the-art results in tasks like NewsQA, HotpotQA, and others, while maintaining consistent memory usage regardless of text length. The paper emphasizes the importance of bridging the gap between machine and human reasoning processes in the context of long text comprehension.
