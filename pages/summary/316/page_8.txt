The paper discusses the performance of CogLTX in text classification tasks, specifically focusing on the 20NewsGroups and Alibaba datasets. It highlights the negative impact of data distribution discrepancies during training and testing, which resulted in a 2.3% decrease in Joint F1 score when blocks were randomly selected for training. 

In the 20NewsGroups experiment, CogLTX achieved an accuracy of 87.0%, outperforming various baseline models, including Bi-LSTM and MS-CNN. The study emphasizes that the Glove initialization for relevance labels significantly enhances performance, although the absence of adjustments through interventions led to a 2.2% accuracy drop. Additionally, the Bm25 initialization method was less effective due to its limited applicability to training samples.

For the Alibaba dataset, which involves multi-label classification, CogLTX also demonstrated superior performance, achieving an accuracy of 98.2% with the large model. This outperformed strong baselines like TextCNN and Bi-LSTM, with the tiny version of CogLTX surpassing TextCNN despite having significantly fewer parameters. The paper suggests that the max-pooling approach used in RoBERTa-large may not effectively handle long texts, further underscoring the advantages of CogLTX.

On memory and time consumption, CogLTX exhibits constant memory use during training, which is more efficient than the O(L2) complexity of standard BERT models. In contrast, the longformer model shows an O(L) complexity under certain conditions, indicating CogLTX's efficiency in handling longer texts. Overall, the results indicate that CogLTX is a robust model for text classification tasks, demonstrating both high accuracy and efficient resource usage.
