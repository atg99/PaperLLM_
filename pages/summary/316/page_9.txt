The paper introduces CogLTX, a cognition-inspired framework designed to enhance the application of BERT for processing long texts. It emphasizes the efficiency of CogLTX in training, where scores of blocks are cached, allowing each epoch to require only twice the time of single-BERT training. The framework demonstrates competitive performance across four large datasets, with a focus on maintaining fixed memory usage during training and facilitating attention between distant sentences. 

CogLTX's time complexity is O(n), making it faster than traditional BERT models once the text length exceeds 2,048 and approaching the speed of sliding window methods as text length increases. However, it faces challenges with extremely complex sequence-level tasks and may overlook antecedents near block boundaries. This limitation can be partially mitigated by incorporating entity names in sentence structures and could be addressed through future advancements in position-aware retrieval and coreference resolution.

The authors acknowledge the support from various funding bodies and express gratitude to collaborators and reviewers for their contributions to the research. Overall, CogLTX is positioned as a promising baseline for complex natural language processing tasks.
