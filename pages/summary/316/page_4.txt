MemRecall is a method designed to enhance the extraction of relevant information from long texts by simulating the working memory process of the brain. It utilizes an initial key text, z+, in conjunction with the input text x to facilitate the retrieval of important blocks of information. The model employs a judge, based on BERT, to score the relevance of these blocks, allowing for a dynamic interaction between the input text and the working memory representation.

The process begins with a retrieval competition where blocks of text are scored for relevance and the top-scoring blocks are retained in z+. This method allows for a more nuanced understanding of relevance, as it incorporates iterative retrieval and fine-tuning of scores based on interactions between blocks. The iterative nature of MemRecall is particularly beneficial in multi-step reasoning tasks, where previous blocks can also be discarded if they are deemed irrelevant based on new information.

Training protocols for the judge and reasoner are tailored to address the challenges posed by diverse downstream tasks. The judge is trained in a supervised manner using labeled relevant and irrelevant blocks, ensuring it learns to accurately assess relevance. The reasoner’s training focuses on maintaining consistency with the data distributions encountered during inference, which is crucial for effective performance.

Overall, MemRecall represents a significant advancement in information retrieval techniques, leveraging principles of working memory to improve the extraction and relevance scoring of textual information in various tasks.
