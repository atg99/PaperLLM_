The paper "CogLTX: Applying BERT to Long Texts" addresses the challenges posed by BERT's limitations in processing long texts due to its quadratic memory and time complexity. Traditional methods, such as using a sliding window to slice text, fail to maintain long-range attention between distant tokens, which is critical for understanding complex information. The authors draw inspiration from cognitive theories, particularly Baddeley's model of working memory, to propose a new framework called CogLTX. This framework identifies key sentences using a trained judge model and facilitates multi-step reasoning through processes akin to rehearsal and decay.

The authors acknowledge the lack of relevance annotations in many datasets and suggest using interventions to generate supervision for training. CogLTX is presented as a general algorithm that not only overcomes the limitations of BERT but also achieves competitive results against state-of-the-art models across various downstream tasks, with memory usage that remains consistent regardless of text length. The paper emphasizes the potential of CogLTX to enhance the effectiveness of pre-trained language models in handling longer texts, which is increasingly relevant in real-world applications.
