This paper discusses three prominent convolutional neural network (CNN) architectures: AlexNet, VGG, and ResNet, each of which has made significant contributions to the field of deep learning, particularly in image classification tasks.

AlexNet, developed by Alex Krizhevsky et al. in 2012, consists of five convolutional layers followed by three fully connected layers. Notable features include the use of larger filters, local response normalization, and overlapping pooling. The architecture is structured with layers that progressively increase the number of filters and includes ReLU activation in the fully connected layers.

VGG, introduced by Karen Simonyan and Andrew Zisserman in 2014, emphasizes the use of small 3x3 convolutional filters across multiple layers, complemented by max pooling. This architecture also incorporates dropout regularization in the fully connected layers to mitigate overfitting, maintaining ReLU activation throughout.

ResNet, created by Kaiming He et al. in 2015, addresses the issue of vanishing gradients in deep networks through the introduction of residual connections. Each residual block consists of multiple convolutional layers along with shortcut connections that link the input directly to the output, thereby enhancing the network's ability to learn. 

Overall, these architectures have set benchmarks in performance on the ImageNet dataset and have influenced subsequent advancements in deep learning methodologies.
