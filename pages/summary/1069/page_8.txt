The paper discusses the significance of activation functions (Activ_Funcs) in convolutional neural networks (CNNs), emphasizing their role in introducing non-linearity, which is essential for accurately modeling complex real-world phenomena. Key Activ_Funcs mentioned include the rectified linear unit (ReLU), sigmoid function, and hyperbolic tangent (tanh) function. 

ReLU is highlighted as the most prevalent due to its simplicity and effectiveness in mitigating the vanishing gradient problem, defined mathematically as f(x) = max(0, x). This function allows CNNs to better learn intricate features by deactivating negative values. 

The sigmoid function, defined as f(x) = 1 / (1 + e^(-x)), is useful for binary classification but can hinder training in deep networks due to its saturating nature, leading to the vanishing gradient problem. Similarly, the tanh function, which maps values to a range between -1 and 1, also faces this issue. 

Additionally, the softmax function is introduced for multi-class classification tasks, converting the output into a probability distribution. The choice of activation functions significantly influences the model's performance and convergence, making it crucial to select the appropriate function for specific tasks in CNN design.
