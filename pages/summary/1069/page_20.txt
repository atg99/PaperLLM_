CNNs excel in tasks involving visual data analysis, such as image classification and object detection, due to their ability to learn hierarchical representations. They are also applicable in sequential data analysis, like speech recognition and natural language processing, where they can capture phonetic and semantic features. 

However, the performance of CNNs is contingent on the availability of large, high-quality datasets, as they have millions of parameters that necessitate significant variation in the training data to prevent overfitting. Computational demands for training and inference are high, but advancements in hardware, such as GPUs and TPUs, have facilitated the training of large models. Techniques like model pruning, quantization, and the use of pre-trained models can further mitigate computational costs while maintaining performance. Overall, CNNs are a robust choice for complex data analysis tasks, provided that appropriate resources and data are available.
