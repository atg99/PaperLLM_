The paper discusses various optimization algorithms used for training neural networks, focusing on their role in updating weights and biases to minimize the loss function. 

Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD) is identified as the most common algorithm, which makes small updates to parameters based on the gradient of the loss function. Additionally, several other algorithms are highlighted:

1. **Momentum**: This method incorporates a momentum term to smooth out updates and enhance convergence speed. The update rule combines past momentum and the current gradient.

2. **AdaGrad**: This algorithm adjusts the learning rate for each weight based on the magnitude of past gradients, leading to faster convergence in flat regions and slower in steep areas.

3. **RMSProp**: By using a moving average of squared gradients, RMSProp adapts learning rates to prevent them from becoming excessively large.

4. **Adam**: This algorithm merges concepts from both momentum and AdaGrad, employing adaptive learning rates that perform effectively across various neural network architectures.

The update rules for these algorithms are mathematically defined, illustrating how each method modifies weights based on gradients and other parameters. These optimizations are crucial for improving the efficiency and effectiveness of neural network training.
