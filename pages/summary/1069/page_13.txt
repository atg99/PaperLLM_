The paper discusses various regularization techniques aimed at preventing overfitting in neural networks, particularly in convolutional neural networks (CNNs). Key methods highlighted include:

1. **Dropout Regularization**: This technique involves randomly setting a proportion of neurons to zero during training with a probability p. The output is adjusted accordingly during testing to account for the dropout effect.

2. **Early Stopping**: This method involves halting the training process based on the performance of a validation set, monitoring metrics like validation loss or accuracy to avoid overfitting.

A summary table outlines the different regularization techniques:

- **L1 Regularization**: Introduces a penalty based on the absolute values of weights, promoting sparsity and feature selection.
- **L2 Regularization**: Adds a penalty proportional to the square of weights, encouraging smaller weights to mitigate overfitting.
- **Dropout**: Randomly omits neurons during training to reduce reliance on specific features and enhance generalization.
- **Early Stopping**: Stops training early based on validation performance metrics to prevent overfitting.

Additionally, the paper employs a confusion matrix as an evaluation metric to assess the CNN model's performance in image categorization. This matrix allows for the comparison of predicted outcomes against actual values, providing insights into both accurate and inaccurate predictions, and facilitating the identification of specific error types.
