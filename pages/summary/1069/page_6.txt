The paper outlines the various layers of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) and their respective functions. Key layers include the convolutional layer, which extracts features; the pooling layer, which reduces spatial dimensions; the activation layer, which introduces non-linearity; and the batch normalization layer, which normalizes outputs for improved convergence. Additionally, dropout layers are utilized to mitigate overfitting by randomly omitting neurons during training, while fully connected layers serve to connect all neurons for final output generation. Specialized layers, such as recurrent and LSTM layers, capture temporal dependencies in sequential data.

Overall, the convolution operation is a pivotal mathematical process in CNNs, enabling effective feature extraction through the manipulation of input signals, thereby enhancing various applications in computer vision.
