The paper discusses the optimization of the U-Net architecture for brain tumor segmentation, particularly through the development of the SegResNetVAE model, which integrates a variational autoencoder branch for input reconstruction. The comparative performance is evaluated based on averaged Dice scores across multiple folds for three models: baseline U-Net, UNETR, and SegResNetVAE. The results indicate that the baseline U-Net achieved the highest mean Dice score of 0.9130, while SegResNetVAE, despite having similar performance, required significantly longer training time due to its additional complexity. Consequently, the authors opted to further explore the U-Net architecture.

Subsequent experiments examined various modifications to the U-Net, including decoder attention, deep supervision, residual connections, and drop block techniques. The introduction of a modified loss function, Focal loss combined with Dice loss, was also tested. Among these modifications, deep supervision notably improved the mean Dice score to 0.9149, marking it as the only significant enhancement over the baseline.

Finally, the researchers experimented with a deeper U-Net encoder, transitioning from a depth of 6 to 7 and adjusting the convolution channels accordingly. They also incorporated one-hot encoding for foreground voxels to assess its impact on segmentation performance. These findings contribute to the ongoing refinement of U-Net architectures for more effective brain tumor segmentation.
