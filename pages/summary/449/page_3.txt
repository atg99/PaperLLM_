2.3 Model Architecture
The proposed U-Net architecture was optimized for brain tumor segmentation tasks. The model consists of an encoder-decoder structure with skip connections that facilitate the recovery of spatial information lost during downsampling. The encoder captures high-level features while the decoder reconstructs the segmentation map. Modifications included the incorporation of attention mechanisms to enhance the focus on relevant features, as well as the use of deeper layers and batch normalization to stabilize training.

2.4 Training Strategy
The model was trained using a combination of cross-entropy loss and dice loss to handle class imbalance effectively. The Adam optimizer was employed to minimize the loss function. A learning rate schedule was implemented to adjust the learning rate dynamically based on validation performance. Early stopping was also utilized to prevent overfitting.

2.5 Evaluation Metrics
The performance of the model was evaluated using several metrics, including Dice coefficient, sensitivity, specificity, and Hausdorff distance, to provide a comprehensive assessment of segmentation accuracy. The results were compared against baseline models to demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed optimizations.

In conclusion, the optimized U-Net architecture shows promise for improving brain tumor segmentation, leveraging multi-modal MRI data and advanced training techniques to achieve higher accuracy and robustness in clinical applications.
