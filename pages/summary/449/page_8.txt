The paper discusses the Residual U-Net model, which incorporates residual connections inspired by ResNet to enhance gradient flow during the training of deep neural networks. The key distinction between the basic U-Net and Residual U-Net lies in the computations within the convolutional blocks. However, experimental results indicate that the basic U-Net outperforms the Residual U-Net, leading to its selection for further analysis.

The authors focused on optimizing the encoder depth and selecting the appropriate number of convolution channels. They started with the default U-Net architecture from the nnU-Net framework, which has a depth of six and specific channel configurations. Their findings reveal that increasing the encoder depth to seven and adjusting the channel counts significantly improves performance.

In terms of the loss function, the paper adopts a strategy from previous work on brain tumor segmentation, converting class labels into three partially overlapping regions: whole tumor, tumor core, and enhancing tumor. The contest leaderboard is based on these regions rather than individual classes, prompting the authors to design their output feature map with three channels corresponding to each region. The loss function combines binary cross-entropy or Focal loss with Dice loss, optimizing each region separately and employing a batched variant of Dice loss for enhanced performance.
