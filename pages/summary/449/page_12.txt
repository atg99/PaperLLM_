The paper presents an evaluation of various modifications to a U-Net model for medical image segmentation, specifically targeting the segmentation of enhancing tumors in brain scans. The authors conducted a 5-fold cross-validation study to compare the performance of different model configurations, including deep supervision, deeper U-Net encoders, a modified number of convolution channels, and the addition of one-hot encoding for foreground voxels.

Table 3 summarizes the averaged Dice scores across the folds, revealing that individual modifications provide slight improvements over the baseline U-Net with deep supervision, which achieved a mean Dice score of 0.9149. When all modifications are applied collectively, the score improves to 0.9156.

Additionally, the authors explored a post-processing strategy aimed at enhancing the final scores by removing small regions of enhanced tumors. This approach involved identifying connected components of enhancing tumors and either reclassifying small components or replacing all enhancing tumor voxels with non-enhancing tumor voxels based on specific criteria. The results in Table 4 show that post-processing further improved the mean Dice score from 0.9156 to 0.9163.

The final model, which included the deeper U-Net architecture, deep supervision, modified convolution channels, and one-hot encoding, excelled in the challenge validation phase, as indicated by the scores in Table 5, which detail the normalized statistical rankings for the BraTS21 validation phase.
