The paper discusses an optimized U-Net architecture for brain tumor segmentation, emphasizing the use of deep supervision to enhance gradient flow. This is achieved by introducing two additional output heads, allowing for the computation of loss at different decoder levels. The loss function integrates contributions from these outputs with varying weights, facilitating better training.

During inference, the model accommodates input volumes of arbitrary sizes through a sliding window approach. The window size matches the training patch size, with overlapping regions to ensure comprehensive coverage. Predictions from these overlapping areas are averaged using Gaussian importance weighting to prioritize center voxels.

To bolster prediction robustness, test-time augmentations are applied, generating eight variations of the input volume through flips along multiple axes. The predictions are then transformed back to align with the original volume configuration, and the outputs are averaged.

For classification, a strategy is employed to convert overlapping region probabilities back to original tumor classes, with specific thresholds determining class assignments. Additionally, a post-processing method addresses potential inaccuracies by modifying small connected components and ensuring that isolated enhancing tumor predictions are adjusted to avoid false positives. This approach effectively enhances the model's final performance metrics, particularly the Dice score.
