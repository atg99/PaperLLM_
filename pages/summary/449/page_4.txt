The paper discusses a methodology for processing volumetric data for neural network training, focusing on data normalization and augmentation techniques to enhance model robustness and mitigate overfitting. Initially, redundant background voxels with a value of zero are cropped from the borders of each volume. The remaining voxels are normalized by subtracting the mean and dividing by the standard deviation, while maintaining the background voxels at zero. An additional input channel is created using one-hot encoding to differentiate background from normalized voxels.

The authors implemented several data augmentation techniques during the training phase to artificially expand the dataset and improve the model's performance. These techniques include:

1. Biased crop: Randomly cropping a patch from the input volume, ensuring the presence of foreground voxels with a 40% probability.
2. Zoom: Resizing the image with a random scale factor, applying cubic interpolation for the image and nearest neighbor interpolation for the ground truth.
3. Flips: Randomly flipping the volume along each axis with a 50% probability.
4. Gaussian Noise: Adding random Gaussian noise to the input volume with a mean of zero and a standard deviation sampled uniformly.
5. Gaussian Blur: Applying Gaussian blurring to the input volume with a randomly sampled standard deviation.
6. Brightness: Adjusting the brightness of the input volume by multiplying it with a randomly sampled value.
7. Contrast: Modifying the contrast of the input volume similarly and clipping values to the original range.

Additionally, the paper outlines the model architecture selection process, highlighting various neural network models evaluated through ablation studies, including U-Net, Attention U-Net, Residual U-Net, SegResNetVAE, and UNETR, with brief descriptions provided for each model.
