The paper discusses two advanced architectures for medical image segmentation: SegResNetVAE and UNETR. 

SegResNetVAE is a modified U-Net that incorporates a variational autoencoder (VAE) to enhance regularization. It features an encoder using ResNet-like blocks, which consist of two convolutions with group normalization and ReLU activation, combined with additive identity skip connections. The decoder mirrors the encoder's structure but employs a single block at each spatial level, reducing channel numbers and increasing spatial dimensions. In the VAE branch, the bottleneck feature map is compressed into a low-dimensional space, from which samples are drawn to reconstruct the input image dimensions.

UNETR, on the other hand, extends the Vision Transformer (ViT) to 3D convolutions by replacing traditional 3D convolutions in the encoder with multi-head self-attention. It processes 3D input volumes by dividing them into uniform patches, projecting these into a higher-dimensional embedding space, and enhancing them with positional embeddings before applying multi-head self-attention.

Both architectures aim to improve the efficacy of medical image segmentation through innovative structural modifications.
