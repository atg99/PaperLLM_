The paper discusses an optimized U-Net architecture specifically designed for brain tumor segmentation. The U-Net model consists of two main parts: the encoder and the decoder. The encoder operates through a contracting path that transforms the input volume into a lower dimensional space using a modular structure of repeating convolution blocks. Each block reduces the spatial dimensions of the input feature map through a 3x3x3 convolutional layer with a stride of 2x2x2, followed by instance normalization and Leaky ReLU activation. A second transformation with a stride of 1x1x1 is also applied.

Once the feature map dimensions are reduced to 2x2x2, the decoder phase begins. The decoder aims to increase the spatial dimensions by utilizing a modular structure consisting of three smaller blocks. It starts with transposed convolution to double the spatial dimensions, then concatenates the upsampled feature map with the corresponding encoder feature map before applying two additional convolution blocks.

The architecture also incorporates deep supervision, allowing for the computation of loss functions from outputs at lower decoder levels, thereby enhancing the model's performance in segmentation tasks. The use of additional output heads for deep supervision is indicated in the architecture diagram.
