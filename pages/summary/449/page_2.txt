The paper discusses the Tumor Segmentation Challenge (BraTS), which provides a substantial dataset of multi-modal MRI brain scans with segmentation masks. The authors highlight the prevalence of U-Net and its variants in brain tumor segmentation tasks, noting that these models have consistently performed well in competitions such as BraTS. They reference specific advancements in U-Net architectures over the years, including modifications by Myronenko et al., Jiang et al., and Isensee et al., which have contributed to their success.

The authors emphasize the challenge of selecting an optimal U-Net variant and designing an effective training schedule due to the multitude of available architectures and the critical components involved, such as loss functions and data augmentation strategies. The limited size of typical medical imaging datasets complicates performance comparisons; however, the BraTS21 dataset, with 2,040 examples, offers a unique opportunity for robust evaluation.

To address these challenges, the authors conducted extensive ablation studies to identify the best U-Net variant and training schedule for the BraTS21 challenge. They tested several U-Net architectures, including Attention U-Net, Residual U-Net, SegResNetVAE, and UNETR, while also experimenting with techniques like Deep Supervision and Drop-Block, along with various loss function combinations. Additionally, they optimized their model by increasing encoder depth, adding one-hot-encoded channels for foreground voxels, and increasing convolutional filter numbers.
