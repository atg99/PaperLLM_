The paper presents the Inception Recurrent Convolutional Neural Network (IRCNN), a novel architecture aimed at improving the accuracy of deep convolutional neural networks (DCNNs) for visual recognition tasks. It builds upon existing structures like Inception Nets and Recurrent Convolutional Neural Networks (RCNNs), emphasizing the importance of context modeling in input samples. The IRCNN architecture features several components including general convolution layers, IRCNN blocks, transaction blocks, and a softmax layer for classification.

A key innovation of the IRCNN is the integration of recurrence within the Inception module, allowing for recurrent convolution operations that utilize multiple convolutional kernels of varying sizes. This design not only reduces computational parameters but also enhances recognition accuracy by accumulating feature maps across time steps, thereby strengthening the extraction of target features.

The paper also references previous works, such as the RCNN structure proposed by Liang and Hu and the Long-term Recurrent Convolutional Network (LRCN) developed by Donahue et al., which combined CNNs with LSTM for better visual recognition and description. Through these advancements, the IRCNN aims to achieve superior performance in visual recognition tasks, leveraging the strengths of both Inception and recurrent architectures.
