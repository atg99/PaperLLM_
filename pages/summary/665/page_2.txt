This paper introduces the Inception Recurrent Convolutional Neural Network (IRCNN), which merges concepts from Inception and recurrent models like the RCNN. The IRCNN aims to enhance recognition accuracy while using fewer computational resources compared to existing DCNN architectures.

The study highlights that human visual processing, characterized by recurrent synaptic connectivity, plays a crucial role in context modeling for visual tasks. Despite the success of various DCNN models, such as AlexNet and GoogleNet, the integration of recurrence is limited in most architectures. The IRCNN seeks to fill this gap, leveraging the strengths of Inception and residual networks while addressing the challenges posed by increasing model complexity.

The paper outlines three primary contributions: the proposal of the IRCNN model, experimental evaluations against multiple benchmark datasets (including MNIST, CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and SVHN), and an exploration of the recurrent layer's impact within the Inception framework. By refining the architecture and emphasizing recurrent properties, the IRCNN demonstrates potential improvements in both training efficiency and recognition performance within the deep learning domain.
