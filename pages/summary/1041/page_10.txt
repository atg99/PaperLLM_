The paper posits that a more expansive understanding of ethics is necessary in the tech industry, moving beyond traditional casuist and principle-based frameworks. It begins with the premise that ethics revolves around the inquiry of what one should do, a question rooted in the history of moral philosophy dating back to Aristotle. This dominant Western perspective assumes individuals can always ask and answer ethical questions based on external authorities.

However, thinkers such as Nietzsche challenge this notion by suggesting that the ethical decision-making process does not merely hinge on free choice but is influenced by various human interests and limitations. Consequently, the capacity of individuals to engage with ethical questions varies significantly; for some, ethical considerations may be a low priority due to their circumstances.

The paper also critiques the idea that philosophers or casuists are best suited to address ethical dilemmas, advocating instead for consultation with experts in relevant fields. This perspective aligns with insights from danah boyd and Solon Baracas, who argue that criticism of tech experts often overlooks the complexities of technology's practical implications. Ultimately, the paper raises the pivotal question of who should undertake ethical considerations in the tech industry, highlighting the need for a more inclusive approach to ethics.
