The paper critiques Luciano Floridi's rejection of solution-less ethics, arguing that such a stance restricts human practices in confronting technology's influence. It emphasizes that accepting narrow definitions of digital ethics limits critique and imposes undue burdens on critics. The author proposes that ethical considerations in technology should be reframed as political actions, which are inherently collective and contentious. This shift allows for broader participation in discussions about AI politics beyond academic expertise. 

The author advocates for a stronger focus on 'data politics' over traditional ethics, suggesting that researchers should transition from a universalist perspective to a more specific analysis of power dynamics and ethical practices. This transformation involves rebranding ethicists as political analysts who engage with practical systems rather than abstract principles. 

In conclusion, the essay underscores the importance of reflexivity in ethical-political judgments, stressing how the creation of theories and guidelines by ethicists is influenced by their historical and contextual foundations. This reflexivity is crucial for understanding the implications of their work in the academic and practical realms of technology.
