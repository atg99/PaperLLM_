The paper discusses the importance of engaging with the social-political context when evaluating and critiquing political practices, emphasizing a collective approach over individualistic perspectives. It argues that understanding the nuances of a political practice leads to three significant advantages in critique: 

1. Improved epistemological evaluation by grasping the cultural context and how concepts of 'good' and 'bad' are shaped within practices.
2. Enhanced normative critique by studying the practice's history and legitimizing mechanisms, allowing for a moral evaluation relevant to the specific context.
3. Increased practical effectiveness of interventions by better organizing critiques that consider the broader implications of actions within global structures.

The paper uses the example of criticizing Amazon for environmental damage to illustrate the complexities of critique, highlighting that individual actions may not suffice in addressing systemic issues. It emphasizes the need to consider the nature of interventions—whether they should be individual or collective, internal or external, and local or global. The author critiques traditional research methodologies that prioritize a linear approach and advocates for a more nuanced understanding that incorporates diverse disciplinary perspectives based on the context of the practice.
