The paper discusses the importance of understanding sociotechnical assemblages through their practical functioning rather than relying solely on theoretical frameworks. It critiques the reduction of technology development to mere translations of moral principles, echoing concerns raised by philosopher Bernard Williams regarding the limitations of such approaches. 

The author emphasizes the value of using examples, or "exemplars," in ethical analysis, as suggested by Wittgenstein. These exemplars serve as practical tools that provide insight into moral issues by reflecting historical contexts rather than solely offering new guidelines. The study of past and present examples contributes to collective memories that shape communities and ethical considerations.

Furthermore, the paper highlights a distinction made by David Owen regarding political philosophy. Owen argues that while principles dictate what agents should do, exemplars illustrate what it means to embody those principles in practice. The paper underscores the need to focus on real-world applications of ethical norms rather than abstract ideals, reinforcing the idea that understanding the actualities of ethical behavior is crucial for effective governance and criticism of sociotechnical systems.
