The paper discusses the relationship between data ethics and the regulation of artificial intelligence (AI), distinguishing between 'hard ethics,' which focus on values and responsibilities related to laws, and 'soft ethics,' which provide guidance beyond existing regulations. It highlights the importance of translating ethical principles into practical applications to bridge the gaps between theory and practice. Successful translations can facilitate internal organizational changes and promote ethical leadership.

However, the effectiveness of ethical guidelines is debated, with some studies indicating a lack of impact on behavior, particularly among software engineers. The paper argues that the production of ethical guidelines by commercial actors often overshadows their enforcement, leading to concerns that these guidelines may serve as mere tokens rather than genuine efforts to improve practices. It emphasizes the responsibility of both tech developers and guideline producers to ensure ethical practices are upheld, as both have significant societal influence.

The authors also note that ethical frameworks can contribute to the discourse surrounding ethics and design, shaping the legitimacy of certain practices and guiding decision-making processes. The paper suggests that understanding these dynamics is crucial for assessing the role of ethics in technology and its implications for society.
