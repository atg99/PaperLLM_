The paper critiques the prevailing approaches to data ethics and AI ethics, contrasting them with a more robust conceptual framework. It references Jonsen and Toulmin’s analysis to highlight the shortcomings of casuistic computer ethics, which they argue lacks depth and leads to simplistic answers to complex ethical issues. Instead, the authors advocate for a more mature digital ethics that not only evaluates moral problems related to data and algorithms but also supports the making of morally sound decisions.

The paper emphasizes the proliferation of guidelines and frameworks in the field, suggesting that this focus on moral principles often overshadows the practical application of ethics in real-world contexts. It draws on philosopher Raymond Geuss to further complicate the discourse on ethics, arguing against the dominant view that seeks definitive answers to ethical questions. The author suggests that this perspective limits the broader understanding of ethics and morality in human life, advocating for a revival of moral practices grounded in professional judgment within specific contexts.

Overall, the paper calls for a shift from abstract discussions of ethics in AI to a more nuanced understanding that incorporates structural and social factors, thereby addressing the underlying issues within both AI ethics and broader ethical practices in Western academia.
