The cited works reflect a growing discourse on the intersection of technology, ethics, and society, particularly in the context of artificial intelligence. Key themes include the necessity for ethical frameworks in AI development, the challenges of implementing ethical principles in practice, and the role of various stakeholders in shaping technology's moral landscape. 

Jonsen and Toulmin's historical analysis of moral reasoning emphasizes the importance of context in ethical decision-making. Keay and Kinstler highlight the need for tech companies to not only discuss ethics but to actively engage in ethical practices. The works of Morley et al. and Mittelstadt argue that principles alone are insufficient for ensuring ethical AI, calling for practical tools and methods to translate these principles into actionable practices.

Additionally, the concept of power dynamics in decision-making regarding technology is explored by Lee et al., while Marres advocates for an experimental approach to political ontology to enhance public participation in technological discourse. The contributions of the arts and humanities to ethical AI are also emphasized by Powell, suggesting a broader interdisciplinary approach to addressing ethical concerns.

Overall, these texts collectively argue for a more robust and practical engagement with ethics in technology, urging a shift from theoretical discussions to concrete actions that address the ethical implications of AI and digital society.
