The paper discusses the ethical considerations surrounding techno-political practices, critiquing the prevalent deontological approach that emphasizes the creation of ethical guidelines. This method assumes that establishing moral principles will enhance legislation and promote good behavior. However, the author argues that this individualistic perspective is limited, as it overlooks the broader context of decision-making and the appropriateness of focusing solely on individual actions.

Instead, the paper advocates for a practice-oriented approach, emphasizing the need to engage with techno-political practices directly. The author introduces four questions to guide this empirical investigation, which seeks to understand how practices are self-organizing and self-investigating. This methodological shift is supported by various scholars who call for a focus on practice over theory, highlighting the importance of understanding how technologies reshape political and democratic practices.

The work of Marres is particularly influential, as it encourages analysts to examine how technologies influence the norms and practices of politics and democracy, rather than merely assessing their impacts. The paper ultimately calls for a more nuanced understanding of ethical considerations in the context of technology, advocating for empirical research that reflects the complexities of real-world interactions.
