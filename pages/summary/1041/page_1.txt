In this paper, Gijs van Maanen critiques the phenomenon of "ethics washing" in the tech sector, where companies publish ethics guidelines primarily to mitigate criticism rather than to effect genuine ethical practices. The author argues that conventional ethical theories, which focus on principles and dilemmas, are inadequate in resisting the misuse of ethics by powerful entities, resulting in a failure to address the ethical implications of technology effectively.

Van Maanen proposes a shift toward a question-based ethical framework, drawing from casuistry and the political philosophy of Raymond Geuss. This approach emphasizes understanding the social and political contexts surrounding technological development rather than relying solely on abstract principles. Additionally, the author highlights the importance of "exemplars" or narratives that provide real-world context and lessons from past, present, or future scenarios. By incorporating these stories, a more nuanced and effective repoliticization of data ethics can emerge, fostering a deeper understanding of the ethical challenges posed by technology.
