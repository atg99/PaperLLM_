This paper explores the politicization of data science and ethics, focusing on a specific type of ethics primarily practiced by academics, though increasingly adopted by industry. The author argues that the distinction between academic ethics and industry ethics is blurred, prompting a call for recognition of the contextual nature of ethical research and its potential misuse. The essay limits its discussion to the genealogy of AI ethics and its vulnerability to industry influence, while acknowledging other factors like funding structures and lobbying that are not extensively analyzed.

The ethical question at the core of the discussion revolves around what individuals should do or how they should live, with three primary ethical approaches identified: deontological, teleological, and virtue ethics. Deontological ethics emphasize rules and duties, often linking ethics to policy and regulation. Teleological ethics focus on assessing the consequences of technology, while virtue ethics concern how ethical norms are integrated into the design process, exemplified by Value Sensitive Design. The paper highlights the significant differences among these approaches in terms of theory, practice, and popularity, all while maintaining a commitment to addressing the ethical question.
