The paper discusses the role of narratives in shaping our understanding of technology's impact on society. It emphasizes that stories from the past, present, or future can inspire change and provide valuable lessons. Instead of merely following rules, these narratives encourage a deeper engagement with ethical concerns surrounding technology. The authors highlight the work of Floridi and Cowls, who successfully communicated principles for tech companies, and reference the book "Data Feminism" by D’Ignazio and Klein, which challenges conventional concepts of ethics and highlights stories of individuals and collectives confronting oppressive technological structures. The paper argues for an ethical approach that transcends theoretical discussions, advocating for an empirical analysis of techno-political practices. This perspective recognizes ethics as a collective endeavor that must confront power dynamics within political work. The author aligns their argument with a critique of traditional AI ethics, suggesting that it cannot adequately address issues of power.
