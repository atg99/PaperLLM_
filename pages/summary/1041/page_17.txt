The paper discusses the need for a more politicized and activist approach to ethics within the tech sector, highlighting the limitations of existing ethical frameworks. It critiques the dominant focus on principles and guidelines, arguing instead for a historically informed reformulation of ethics that prioritizes questioning, empirical analysis, and exemplars over rigid ethical theories. The author posits that the term 'ethics' may not be suitable for the proposed 'repoliticized ethics' due to the complexities it entails.

The paper references scholars such as Mouffe, Williams, and Geuss, who emphasize the importance of agonistic elements in ethical and political judgments. It asserts that differences and tensions among individuals and societies cannot be entirely resolved through consensus-building and that there is value in supporting dissenting views. The author argues for a broader understanding of the relationship between academic ethics and political practice, suggesting that it should encompass contentious alternatives rather than solely focusing on support and consensus.

The paper also critiques Terzis' existentialist approach to AI ethics, expressing concern that its focus on a singular overarching value may lead to similar issues faced by other principle-based methods. Furthermore, it highlights the risk of ethical clarity overshadowing important inquiries related to the epistemological and methodological challenges posed by new technologies, as noted by Marres. Overall, the paper calls for a reevaluation of how ethics is approached in the context of technology and AI, advocating for a more nuanced and politically aware framework.
