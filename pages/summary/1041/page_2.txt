The essay contends that the focus on abstract principles in ethical discussions in tech has led to a disconnect from real-world implications and practices. This disconnect allows companies to engage in "ethics washing," where they superficially adopt ethical stances while continuing harmful practices.

The introduction sets the stage for a critical examination of the role of ethics in technology, arguing that a shift towards a practice-oriented ethical framework could enhance the ability of ethicists to challenge dominant narratives and behaviors in the tech industry. The author suggests that a more grounded approach, emphasizing empirical research and narrative examples, could lead to a deeper understanding of the socio-political dimensions of technology, ultimately fostering a more effective ethical discourse.

In summary, the paper advocates for a reevaluation of how ethics is conceptualized and applied in the tech sector, arguing for a transition from principle-based ethics to a practice-oriented approach that prioritizes real-world implications and accountability.
