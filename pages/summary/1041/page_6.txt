The paper discusses the disconnect between universal ethical codes and the concrete ethical challenges faced in practice, highlighting how such codes can lead to a focus on infrastructure management rather than genuine ethical commitments. It references Metcalf's critique of philosophical ethics, which can result in routinized obedience that detracts from addressing real-world ethical dilemmas. To counteract these issues, various alternative approaches have been proposed, including Science and Technology Studies (STS), global data justice, and human rights frameworks. 

Despite these alternatives, the paper raises concerns about the effectiveness of ethics in combating "ethics washing" in the tech industry, noting a lack of clarity regarding ethics' role in this context. The author examines criticisms from political philosophers regarding dominant moral and political philosophies, suggesting these critiques can illuminate the shortcomings of ethical guidelines in technology.

The discussion transitions to the limitations of top-down ethical frameworks, labeled as "ethics first" approaches by Geuss, which prioritize theoretical constructs over practical applications. It references the work of Jonsen and Toulmin, who advocate for a revival of casuistry, an ethical practice focused on case-based reasoning, reflecting on their historical context and its relevance today. The paper ultimately emphasizes the need for a more grounded and practical approach to ethics in addressing the complexities of technological and political structures.
