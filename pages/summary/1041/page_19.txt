The paper argues for a diversification of ethical approaches in academic work, particularly in the context of technology. The author emphasizes the importance of understanding the specific components of ethical-political judgments, such as who presents arguments, to whom, and in what context. This understanding aids in evaluating the contributions of ethicists and encourages empirical scrutiny of their work.

The primary aim of the argument is to highlight the limitations of dominant ethical frameworks that are theory-oriented and regulation-focused. By illustrating that these approaches are relatively recent in human history, the author advocates for the inclusion of collective, contextual, and political forms of ethics in technology practices. The paper suggests that while current tech development may not reflect this broader ethical perspective, it is important to challenge the prevailing individualistic and abstract practices of ethics.

The author calls for reflection among ethicists regarding their roles, the implications of their academic work, and their relationship with the tech industry. It is posited that proximity to the industry may hinder meaningful change, and a critical distance could be more beneficial for those seeking to influence ethical practices. The argument concludes by advocating for the expansion of ethical repertoires to allow for more practice-oriented and contentious approaches, thereby fostering the potential for reform or even revolutionary change in the field.
