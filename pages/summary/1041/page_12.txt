The paper discusses the limitations of traditional ethical approaches in political philosophy, particularly those that prioritize theoretical formulations over empirical realities. It references Geuss, who advocates for a 'realist' political theory that begins with human practices and emphasizes the importance of context in political action. Instead of focusing on fixed moral principles, Geuss suggests that political philosophers should ask practical questions to navigate complex social-political situations. He proposes three key questions: who is acting and for whose benefit, when is the appropriate time to act, and what legitimatory mechanisms are involved in the proposed actions. These questions encourage a nuanced understanding of the socio-political landscape, especially in the context of ambiguous concepts like AI and algorithms. The paper also highlights the need for a careful consideration of the interplay between technology, norms, and human actions in ethical discussions, adding a fourth question related to the ethical implications of these interrelations.
