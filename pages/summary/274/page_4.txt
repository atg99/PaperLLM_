This paper presents ablation studies on video action recognition using various configurations of attention layers and positional embedding methods in the Longformer architecture. The experiments assess the impact of different numbers of attention layers, types of positional embeddings, temporal footprint sizes, and fine-tuning strategies on model performance, measured by top-1 and top-5 accuracy on the Kinetics-400 dataset.

Key findings include:
1. The number of attention layers showed minimal variance in accuracy, with the best performance achieved using three layers.
2. Different positional embedding methods influenced results, particularly with learned embeddings providing slight advantages over fixed ones.
3. The temporal footprint, defined by the number of frames processed, demonstrated that larger temporal footprints did not consistently yield better accuracy.
4. Fine-tuning the ViT backbone significantly improved performance compared to training without fine-tuning.

The paper emphasizes the limitations of traditional inference approaches that rely on short clips, advocating for full video context utilization to improve action recognition accuracy. Additionally, it discusses the integration of various spatial backbones, including ViT and ResNet models, to evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed Video Transformer Network (VTN).

Overall, the research highlights the importance of architectural choices and training strategies in enhancing the performance of video action recognition systems.
