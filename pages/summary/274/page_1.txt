This paper introduces the Video Transformer Network (VTN), a novel transformer-based framework for video recognition that moves away from traditional 3D ConvNets. VTN classifies actions by leveraging the entire video sequence information through an attention mechanism, enhancing the analysis of temporal dynamics. The proposed method is versatile, compatible with any existing 2D spatial network, and demonstrates significant efficiency, training 16.1 times faster and running 5.1 times faster during inference while requiring 1.5 times fewer GFLOPs. 

The authors report competitive performance on Kinetics-400 and Moments in Time benchmarks and conduct an ablation study to explore the properties of VTN and its balance between accuracy and inference speed. The motivation behind the approach lies in the limitations of current methods that rely heavily on short video clips, which are inadequate for longer video sequences. By representing videos as sequences of frames, similar to language modeling, VTN aims to address these challenges and establish a new baseline for future research in video recognition. Code and models are made available for further exploration.
