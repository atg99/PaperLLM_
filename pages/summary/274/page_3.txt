This paper presents the Video Transformer Network (VTN), a framework designed for video recognition tasks, particularly action recognition. The VTN architecture consists of three key components: a spatial backbone for feature extraction, a temporal attention-based encoder, and a classification MLP head. The spatial backbone can utilize various 2D image networks, which can be pre-trained or trained during the learning process.

A notable aspect of VTN is its use of Longformer, an efficient variant of the self-attention mechanism, which allows for the processing of long video sequences with linear computational complexity. This is crucial for video data, as traditional Transformers face challenges with long input sequences due to their O(n²) complexity in self-attention. Longformer addresses this by employing a sliding window approach for local context and global attention on selected tokens, facilitating the integration of information across long sequences.

During inference, VTN offers several processing methods, including end-to-end video processing and chunk-based processing. The classification MLP head processes the final representation of the video, derived from the features associated with a special classification token, to predict the action category.

The paper also includes performance metrics for various backbone models tested on the Kinetics-400 validation set, highlighting their top-1 and top-5 accuracy on ImageNet. The results demonstrate the effectiveness of VTN in leveraging Transformer architectures for video recognition, showcasing its potential in the field.
