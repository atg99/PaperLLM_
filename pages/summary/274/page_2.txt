The paper discusses the development of a video temporal network (VTN) that enhances video analysis by effectively processing long sequences while maintaining accuracy and speed. The authors argue that analyzing full video context is more intuitive than evaluating isolated clips, as it reduces the risk of false predictions. VTN utilizes a Longformer model, which allows for global attention across lengthy sequences, addressing the limitations of current networks that focus on short clips.

The framework achieves a significant balance between speed and accuracy, demonstrating a 16.1 times faster training process compared to state-of-the-art networks while maintaining similar inference accuracy. Its modular structure permits flexibility in integrating different 2D spatial backbones, stacking attention layers, and adapting the classification head for various video tasks.

The paper also reviews related work in spatial-temporal networks, highlighting approaches such as 3D ConvNets, two-stream architectures, and the SlowFast network. It emphasizes the limitations of existing methods, particularly their inability to efficiently handle long-term temporal context. The authors note that while Transformers have been successful in NLP, their application in computer vision is emerging, with VTN being a notable advancement in video analysis.
