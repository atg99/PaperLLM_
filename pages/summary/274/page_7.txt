The paper presents a comparative analysis of various video inference models, focusing on their performance, computational efficiency, and the impact of different configurations on accuracy. The models evaluated include NL I3D, SlowFast, and ViT-B-VTN, with metrics such as frames per inference, crop size, GFLOPs, and top-1 accuracy reported for both multi-view and full-video approaches.

Key findings include:

1. **Performance Metrics**: ViT-B-VTN demonstrates a competitive top-1 accuracy of 78.6% with reduced GFLOPs compared to SlowFast-16X8-R101, which achieved 78.9% but with significantly higher computational cost.

2. **Effect of Input Frame Shuffling**: An unexpected result emerged when evaluating the models with shuffled input frames, leading to improved accuracy in some cases, suggesting that Kinetics-400 may prioritize static frame classification over motion dynamics.

3. **Temporal Footprint and Clip Length**: The study examined the influence of clip duration and frame count on model performance, finding that the accuracy remained consistent across varying temporal settings, indicating that VTN is agnostic to these hyperparameters.

4. **Backbone Training**: Fine-tuning the 2D spatial backbone significantly enhanced the model's performance, with a noted 7% increase in top-1 accuracy when the backbone was trained alongside the VTN.

5. **Attention Mechanism**: The importance of the attention mechanism was highlighted, showing that learned attention outperformed a uniform attention distribution, which struggled to generalize during validation.

6. **Training Efficiency**: Despite a larger number of parameters, the proposed models converged faster, achieving optimal performance more quickly during training and validation compared to the SlowFast model.

In conclusion, the paper emphasizes the effectiveness of the ViT-B-VTN model, particularly in terms of computational efficiency and performance, while also exploring the nuances of attention mechanisms and input configurations in video classification tasks.
