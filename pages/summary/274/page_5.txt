The paper discusses the implementation and evaluation of a video action recognition model utilizing attention mechanisms. In Figure 3, the attention weights of the [CLS] token across various video frames are illustrated, showing that segments relevant to the abseiling category, such as shackle and rope, have higher attention weights compared to irrelevant segments like shoes and people. The model successfully predicts the video's action as abseiling.

Figure 4 evaluates the influence of attention in a training and validation context using Kinetics-400. The model employs a ViT-B-VTN architecture with three Longformer layers, comparing learned attention heads to fixed uniform ones. The training setup utilizes an 8-V100-GPU machine, and input clips are processed by stacking frames to facilitate efficient training.

The Longformer configuration includes an effective attention window of size 32, with specific hidden and inner hidden sizes derived from the spatial backbone. Attention Dropout is implemented with a probability of 0.1, and the impact of the number of Longformer layers is analyzed.

Three positional embedding strategies are explored: learned positional embedding based on frame indices, fixed absolute encoding for the temporal axis, and no positional embedding while maintaining the global position for the [CLS] token.

For inference, two approaches are used: a multi-view method that samples clips from the video and a full video inference method that processes all frames. The multi-view approach generates 30 views per video, averaging softmax scores for predictions. The Kinetics-400 dataset consists of 246,535 training videos and 19,761 validation videos, with some data loss resulting in a slight performance drop. Overall, the experiments aim to assess the effectiveness of the proposed model and its components in recognizing human actions from video data.
