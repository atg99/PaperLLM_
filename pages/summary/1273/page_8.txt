This paper discusses various dimensionality reduction (DR) techniques and their corresponding quality metrics, aimed at evaluating the effectiveness of these methods in preserving data structure. It begins by categorizing DR techniques into three groups: nonlinear, linear and local, and others that utilize labeled data. Notable methods include Generalized Discriminant Analysis, Least Square Projection, Autoencoders, and Large-Margin Nearest Neighbor Metric Learning. The paper emphasizes that using labeled data can enhance class separation but acknowledges the difficulty in comparing these methods against unlabeled ones.

The second part of the paper focuses on quality metrics used to assess projection methods on selected datasets. It highlights the importance of multiple metrics for evaluating DR techniques due to the ill-posed nature of DR. Scalar metrics are particularly emphasized, with four main metrics detailed: Trustworthiness, Continuity, Normalized Stress, and Neighborhood Hit. Each metric measures different aspects of projection quality, such as the preservation of local structures and the separability of labeled data.

The paper also considers additional metrics that were excluded from the benchmark due to their complexity or less clear interpretation for typical users. Visual separation metrics are mentioned as a specialized category aimed at assessing the perceived separability of clusters in scatterplots, with Distance Consistency identified as a promising metric for this purpose.

Overall, the paper provides a comprehensive overview of DR techniques and the metrics used to evaluate their performance, contributing valuable insights to the field of visualization and computer graphics.
