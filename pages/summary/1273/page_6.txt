The paper discusses the challenges of selecting datasets and projection techniques for evaluating visualization methods in high-dimensional data spaces. It highlights the difficulty of sampling from trait bins due to the exponential number of possible datasets, which would be impractical to evaluate. To address this, the authors manually collected a smaller set of 18 datasets that encompass a range of trait values relevant to their analysis.

The datasets include a variety of applications, such as bank marketing, image classification, human activity recognition, and sentiment analysis, each with specific attributes that make them suitable for evaluating different projection techniques.

In the subsequent section, the authors outline their approach to sampling the space of projection techniques, which is informed by existing taxonomies but tailored to reflect traits that non-specialist users consider important. These traits include linearity, input type, neighborhood preservation, ease of use, computational complexity, out-of-sample capability, inverse transform ability, and determinism. Each of these traits plays a critical role in determining the appropriateness of a projection technique for specific datasets and user needs.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of carefully selecting both datasets and projection techniques to ensure effective evaluation and visualization of high-dimensional data.
