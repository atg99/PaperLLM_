This paper presents a comprehensive evaluation of projection techniques used for dimensionality reduction, highlighting their performance across various datasets. The authors introduce a benchmark workflow designed to quantitatively assess projection methods, focusing on five significant traits of high-dimensional data. They utilize 18 diverse real-world datasets with varying characteristics, alongside 44 projection techniques, including well-established algorithms in the field.

The evaluation is based on five quality metrics, revealing that t-SNE and PBC consistently deliver superior results in terms of low average errors, effective clustering of similar data points, and a minimal occurrence of high-error points. The study also emphasizes the importance of inspecting error distributions across different techniques and datasets to establish trust in the projections.

Additionally, the authors discuss the typical approach in projection method surveys, which often provide limited qualitative and quantitative comparisons. In contrast, their work aims to cover a broader spectrum of practical goals, offering a more detailed and rigorous evaluation. The entire benchmark, including datasets and measurement tools, is publicly available, marking it as a significant contribution to the dimensionality reduction community. The implementation of this workflow is conducted in Python, with specific projection details provided in the paper.
