This paper presents a comprehensive overview of dimensionality reduction (DR) techniques, categorizing them based on linearity and neighborhood considerations. A selection of 44 DR methods is highlighted, chosen for their relevance to end-users and availability in literature. The datasets used for evaluation are detailed, showcasing various traits such as size, dimensionality, intrinsic sparsity, and type. 

The paper emphasizes a user-centered approach to evaluating DR techniques, avoiding deep theoretical discussions in favor of practical usability. The selected techniques are grouped into four categories: 

1. **Linear and Global**: Techniques like PCA and its variants focus on linear transformations across all samples.
2. **Nonlinear and Local**: Methods like Isomap and t-SNE preserve local neighborhood structures while employing nonlinear functions.
3. **Nonlinear and Global**: Techniques such as MDS and K-PCA consider all samples and utilize nonlinear transformations.
4. **Hybrid Approaches**: Techniques that blend characteristics of the aforementioned categories are also discussed.

The authors aim to guide non-specialist users in selecting appropriate DR techniques based on their specific needs, ultimately contributing to the understanding and application of these methods in practical scenarios.
