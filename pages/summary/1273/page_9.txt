In this paper, various dimensionality reduction (DR) techniques are evaluated based on selected traits, including projection linearity, input type, neighborhood considerations, free parameters, complexity, out-of-sample capability, inverse transform availability, and deterministic implementation. The techniques range from linear methods like Principal Component Analysis (PCA) to nonlinear approaches such as t-Distributed Stochastic Neighbor Embedding (t-SNE) and Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection (UMAP). Each technique is characterized by its computational complexity, with some requiring cubic or quadratic time complexity based on the number of samples and dimensions.

The authors argue for the importance of evaluating DR techniques not just on human perception but based on how well the scatterplot captures the underlying n-dimensional data structure. They highlight that stability, while relevant, is complex to define and quantify, leading to its exclusion from this study. The paper emphasizes the need for more nuanced metrics, such as point-pair metrics, which consider the preservation of distances between individual points rather than relying solely on average metrics, thus providing deeper insights into the effectiveness of different projection methods.

Overall, this study contributes to the understanding of how various DR techniques perform in terms of capturing the structure of high-dimensional data, while also addressing the limitations and challenges in evaluating these techniques.
