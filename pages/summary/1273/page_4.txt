The paper discusses the limitations of current surveys in dimensionality reduction (DR) techniques, particularly in their evaluation of performance metrics across various datasets. It aims to address these gaps by providing a more comprehensive analysis that includes a greater variety of DR techniques, datasets, and quality metrics. The authors propose a systematic approach to categorize datasets based on five traits: type, size, dimensionality, intrinsic dimensionality ratio, and sparsity ratio. These traits are designed to be easily understandable for non-specialist users, allowing them to better assess DR methods in relation to their own data.

The study emphasizes the importance of these traits in evaluating projection techniques and aims to explore how these techniques perform across different dataset types and characteristics. The research intends to answer critical questions about the influence of dataset type, size, intrinsic dimensionality, and sparsity on the effectiveness of various projection techniques. By creating a representative collection of datasets, the authors seek to enhance the practical understanding of how different DR methods operate in real-world applications.
