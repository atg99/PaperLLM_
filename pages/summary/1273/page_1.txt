The paper addresses the challenges faced by practitioners in selecting appropriate dimensionality reduction techniques for high-dimensional data exploration. It emphasizes the importance of considering functional and non-functional requirements, such as pattern preservation, computational scalability, robustness, and ease of use. The authors present a comprehensive survey that includes taxonomies of datasets, projection techniques, and quality metrics, ultimately establishing a benchmark for evaluating these techniques.

The methodology involves sampling a diverse set of datasets, techniques, and metrics to ensure comprehensive coverage while maintaining manageable effort. The results are made publicly available, allowing stakeholders to examine and extend the benchmarking process. The findings highlight the dependencies and correlations between various metrics and projection techniques, facilitating a better understanding of their respective strengths and weaknesses.

In summary, this work contributes to the field of dimensionality reduction by providing a structured approach to evaluating projection techniques, offering practical guidance for practitioners in selecting the most suitable methods for their specific contexts.
