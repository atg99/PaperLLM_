The paper discusses the implementation of the YOLO (You Only Look Once) model for vehicle counting in a GPU environment using OpenCV, cuDNN, and CUDA. Proper installation of CUDA is essential for successful application, and its effectiveness can be verified by running a sample video. The study utilized a specific hardware configuration, including an Intel Core i7-8750 CPU and an NVIDIA GeForce GTX 1060 GPU. The processing time for one hour of video on this setup ranged from one hour forty minutes to one hour fifty minutes, indicating the necessity for high-performance hardware, with recommendations for a 2060 GPU with more than 6 GB of memory for improved processing speed.

Additionally, a benefit-cost analysis was performed to assess the advantages of automated vehicle counting. This analysis considered the time taken for manual counting of video data collected using different camera types, including MioVision and CountCam2. It was found that manual counting requires approximately 21 minutes for each hour of video, which was translated into actual working hours and converted to monetary value based on local electricity costs in East Baton Rouge Parish, LA. This analysis underscores the potential efficiency gains and cost savings of automated counting methods compared to manual processes.
