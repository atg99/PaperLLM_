The paper presents an analysis of the accuracy of automated vehicle counting across various sites over two days. The results, summarized in Table 3, indicate the number of vehicles counted manually and automatically, along with the corresponding accuracy percentages for each site. The accuracy ranged from 78.87% to 94.61% for entry counts and from 83.22% to 95.61% for exit counts, highlighting variability in performance across sites.

To assess the differences between manual and automated counts, a two-tailed paired t-test was employed. This statistical method was chosen to compare two related groups (manual and automated counts) across ten sites, under the assumption of no significant outliers and a normal distribution of differences. The null hypothesis (H0) posited that the automated counts were equal to the manual counts, while the alternative hypothesis (HA) suggested a difference.

The t-test was conducted separately for day 1 and day 2, focusing on both raw differences and adjusted differences to account for potential undercounting bias. For day 1, the results showed a mean difference of 44.10, with a standard deviation of 18.91 and a t-statistic of 7.37, indicating a significant difference between the automated and manual counts. The 95% confidence interval ranged from 30.12 to 59.27, further supporting the conclusion that automated counts differed from manual counts in a statistically significant manner. 

Overall, the study emphasizes the effectiveness of automated vehicle counting while also identifying areas for improvement, particularly in sites with lower accuracy.
