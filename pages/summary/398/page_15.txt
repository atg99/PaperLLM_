The paper discusses the development and evaluation of neural network algorithms for automated vehicle counting in various traffic scenes, achieving an accuracy of over 90%. In contrast, previous efforts by Pereira et al. reported lower accuracy rates of 60% to 70%, attributed to low-resolution video quality. The developed algorithms present a cost-effective alternative to traditional methods like pneumatic road tubes and inductive loops, which, while more accurate, are prohibitively expensive for smaller projects.

However, the program has limitations. It struggles to detect fast-moving vehicles due to their brief appearance in footage, particularly evident at high-speed entrances like those of strip malls. Conversely, it performs well when vehicles exit more slowly. The effectiveness of the counting is further compromised by the camera angle; if positioned too close to the entrance, vehicles can be partially out of frame, leading to inaccuracies. Environmental factors such as rain can obscure the camera lens and diminish video quality, while low visibility conditions hinder counting efficiency. Additionally, the program is unable to detect overlapping vehicles, resulting in undercounting when two vehicles pass simultaneously. Lastly, the counting speed is influenced by the hardware used, with better configurations enhancing performance.
