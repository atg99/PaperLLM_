This paper presents a cost analysis comparing manual and automated counting methods for traffic measurement. The calculations reveal that the cost for conducting manual counts (USD 1120) significantly exceeds the operating cost for automated counts (USD 160). Energy consumption costs are also lower for manual counts (USD 2.68) compared to automated counts (USD 11.18), but the overall benefit-cost (B/C) ratio for the automated method is estimated at 1.54. This indicates that the automated counting method provides a 54 percent greater benefit than traditional manual methods.

The paper further compares the accuracy of various automated counting methods. Pneumatic road tube counting and piezoelectric sensors both demonstrate high accuracy at 99 percent. Inductive loops also show similar reliability, while computer algorithms for automated counting yield an accuracy of 90 percent. The study highlights that while pneumatic road tube counters generally perform well, their reliability in measuring traffic volumes can be questioned due to significant errors in speed and classification.

Overall, the findings suggest that the proposed automated counting method is not only more cost-effective but also competitive in accuracy compared to other established methods.
