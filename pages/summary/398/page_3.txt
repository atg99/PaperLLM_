The paper discusses the development and evaluation of an automated vehicle counting method using the YOLO object detection model. YOLO, created by Redmon and Farhadi, operates by processing an image in one pass through the network, achieving superior accuracy and speed compared to other neural-network-based object detectors. It employs non-maximal suppression and divides images into a grid, predicting bounding boxes for potential objects, with detections validated against a probability threshold.

The study highlights that while YOLO excels in counting larger objects, its accuracy diminishes with smaller ones, which is not a concern for vehicle counting in this research. The motivation for this study arises from the high costs associated with traditional in situ traffic counting technologies, such as piezoelectric sensors and pneumatic tubes, prompting a search for more affordable automated alternatives.

Various existing automated traffic detection methods are reviewed, noting limitations such as accuracy, speed, and flexibility in counting intervals. In contrast, the proposed method utilizing YOLO is presented as a cost-effective solution that meets the demand for flexible interval counting and directional vehicle counts, which is essential for transportation planning.

The research involved collecting video data from ten strip mall locations in Baton Rouge, Louisiana, over two days, which was then analyzed using the developed counting method. The study aims to provide valuable outcomes for transportation planners, engineers, and policymakers, along with a benefit-cost analysis to assess the advantages of the new automated counting approach.
