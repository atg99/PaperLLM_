The paper discusses a cost analysis comparing manual and automated counting methods in video data collection, focusing on the benefit-cost (B/C) ratio of the automated approach. The analysis includes several cost factors: field data collection, manual counting expenses, and energy consumption for both methods. The automated counting process utilizes computer algorithms, requiring an individual to preprocess and upload videos, which incurs labor costs based on time taken for these tasks.

Energy consumption is highlighted as a significant factor, particularly when using deep learning models, which involve substantial computational costs. The study quantifies these costs by estimating the hours and kilowatt hours needed for both configuring the computer and running automated counts, converting these into monetary values using a set electricity rate.

The B/C ratio is calculated using a specific equation that incorporates the working hours, kilowatt hours, and costs associated with each component of the counting methods. This comprehensive approach aims to provide a clearer understanding of the economic implications of adopting automated counting in video data collection.
