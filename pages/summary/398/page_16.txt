The study highlights that traditional methods, such as pneumatic road tubes and inductive loops, while more accurate, are considerably more expensive, making the proposed algorithms a suitable choice for smaller projects.

However, the model does have limitations, particularly when vehicles pass at high speeds, leading to detection failures. The camera angle also plays a critical role in accuracy; improper positioning can result in significant portions of vehicles being out of frame. The automated vehicle counting model is based on the YOLO object detection framework and utilizes OpenCV for processing pre-recorded videos. The evaluation of the model indicates a consistent performance with a significant reduction in counting errors compared to manual methods.

The model is designed for various real-world applications, including traffic management and safety analysis, and it has demonstrated flexibility in providing directional counts. Despite its advantages, challenges such as sensitivity to video quality and camera installation must be addressed to enhance performance. Recommendations include using high-resolution cameras and optimizing camera placement to minimize errors.

A benefit-cost analysis shows that the proposed method has a substantial economic advantage over traditional counting methods, with a B/C ratio greater than 1.54, indicating a 54 percent greater benefit. In conclusion, the automated counting method offers a practical solution for vehicle counting, particularly in scenarios where traditional counting technologies are financially unfeasible.
