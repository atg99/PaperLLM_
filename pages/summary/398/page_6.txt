The paper presents a detailed methodology for processing video frames to detect and track objects, specifically vehicles. The process begins with receiving the input video file's name and path, which triggers the `detect_start` function to initialize detection. The first frame is captured and displayed using OpenCV, allowing the user to establish a reference line, referred to as ‘mid_line’, through mouse clicks. This line assists in drawing two parallel lines automatically on either side based on geometric algorithms.

Subsequently, the `detect_image` function processes each video frame, resizing it to 128x128 pixels for compatibility with a YOLO weight file utilized in TensorFlow for object detection. This function identifies potential objects, encasing them in rectangular bounding boxes and assigning confidence scores ranging from 0 to 1. A threshold of 0.2 is set, allowing only those boxes with higher scores to be considered for further analysis.

The paper further discusses the vehicle tracking mechanism, which employs the `KalmanBoxTracker` to compare frames and track objects based on pixel variance. Each bounding box is numbered for identification, and rectangles are drawn around detected vehicles, facilitating a visual representation that aids in vehicle counting. The tracking process iteratively updates the bounding boxes as the video progresses, ensuring accurate movement direction determination. Overall, the algorithms developed in this study provide a systematic approach to video analysis for vehicle detection and tracking.
