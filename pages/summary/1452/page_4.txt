The paper discusses a multi-task deep neural network (MT-DNN) designed for natural language inference (NLI) and relevance ranking tasks, exemplified through the QNLI dataset. The NLI task aims to determine the logical relationship between a premise and a hypothesis by employing a sophisticated reasoning approach inspired by the stochastic answer network (SAN). The SAN's answer module utilizes multi-step reasoning, where the model iteratively refines its predictions based on a working memory created from the contextual embeddings of the premise and hypothesis.

The reasoning process begins with an initial state derived from the hypothesis's memory. The model then performs K-step reasoning, updating its state at each step and utilizing a classifier to predict the relationship. The final relation prediction is obtained by averaging scores from all K outputs, enhancing robustness through stochastic prediction dropout during training.

For relevance ranking, the model computes relevance scores for candidate answers to a given question based on their contextual embeddings. The training procedure consists of two stages: pretraining, which follows the BERT model's methodology using unsupervised tasks like masked language modeling, and multi-task learning, where the model is updated using mini-batch stochastic gradient descent across various tasks.

The classification tasks employ cross-entropy loss, while text similarity tasks use mean squared error. Relevance ranking tasks follow a pairwise learning-to-rank approach, ensuring effective optimization across multiple objectives. The study leverages pre-trained BERT models to enhance performance in these tasks.
