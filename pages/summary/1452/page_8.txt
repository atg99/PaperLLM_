This paper presents the MT-DNN model, which integrates multi-task learning with language model pre-training to enhance language representation learning. The model is evaluated on domain adaptation tasks using the SNLI and SciTail datasets, where it consistently outperforms the BERT baseline, particularly with limited training data. For instance, with only 0.1% of SNLI training data, MT-DNN achieves an accuracy of 82.1%, compared to BERT's 52.5%. As training data increases, MT-DNN maintains superior performance, demonstrating its effectiveness in adapting to new tasks and domains.

In comprehensive evaluations, MT-DNN achieves state-of-the-art results on both datasets, with improvements over previous benchmarks, reaching 91.6% on SNLI and 95.0% on SciTail. These results underscore MT-DNN's exceptional performance and generalization capabilities across multiple natural language understanding tasks, as it achieves new highs on ten NLU tasks across three prominent benchmarks: SNLI, SciTail, and GLUE. The findings highlight the model's potential for practical applications where collecting large labeled datasets is challenging.
