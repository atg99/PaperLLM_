The paper discusses the construction of decision trees using the rpart() function on the Vac18 dataset, which contains 42 observations. The default tree, illustrated in Fig. 2.13, features only three leaves due to the limited number of observations and the rapid separation of classes LIPO5 and NS. The first split effectively isolates all LIPO5 instances in the left child node.

In contrast, the maximal tree, shown in Fig. 2.14, consists of six leaves achieved through five splits, demonstrating perfect class separation. This example highlights the restrictive nature of relying solely on variables present in the tree's splits, as only five out of 1,000 variables are utilized.

To further analyze the data, a leave-one-out cross-validation approach is suggested for estimating error, which can be configured by adjusting the xval parameter in the rpart.control() function. This method is particularly relevant given the small sample size of the dataset.
