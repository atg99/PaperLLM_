The section discusses the construction of maximal trees using the CART (Classification and Regression Trees) methodology. It begins by illustrating the relationship between a classification tree and its corresponding partition of the explanatory variable space, represented as a unit square. The focus is on the splitting rule applied to continuous explanatory variables in the input space Rp, where p denotes the number of variables.

The process initiates at the root node, encompassing all observations from the learning sample. The first step involves optimally splitting this root into two child nodes, a procedure that is recursively repeated. A split is defined as the union of conditions {Xj≤d} and {Xj>d}, where j represents a variable index and d is a threshold value. Observations are allocated to the left child node if they satisfy the left condition and to the right child node otherwise.

To determine the best split, the method aims to minimize a specific cost function. In regression tasks, this involves minimizing the within-group variance when dividing a node into two child nodes. The variance for a node is calculated based on the average of the observations and their counts within that node. Consequently, the objective becomes maximizing the reduction in variance achieved through the split.
