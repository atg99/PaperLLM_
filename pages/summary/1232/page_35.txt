The section discusses the importance index provided by CART trees, focusing on the concept of surrogate splits as outlined by Breiman et al. in 1984. The importance of a variable, denoted as Xj, is measured by summing the heterogeneity reductions from splits involving Xj and those from surrogate splits when Xj does not define the split. The surrogate split's contribution is weighted based on the relative agreement beyond the majority routing rule, which ensures a nuanced evaluation of variable importance.

Despite its interesting approach, this variable importance index has fallen out of favor due to its lack of intuitiveness, instability reliant on specific trees, and lower relevance compared to permutation-based importance measures in random forests. The text also notes that a similar measure exists for random forests, which tends to favor nominal variables with many possible values.

In the context of spam detection, the CART variable importance indices yield reasonable and interpretable results, highlighting key variables such as charExclamation, capitalLong, charDollar, free, and remove, along with a secondary group of less significant variables like capitalAve and num000.
