The paper discusses a pruning algorithm for decision trees, focusing on optimizing tree complexity while minimizing error in regression tasks. It highlights that building a complete sequence of trees with k leaves is theoretically possible but impractical due to exponential growth in model complexity. Instead, the pruning algorithm allows for an effective implicit enumeration of nested subtrees, denoted as T1 to TK, derived from a maximum tree Tmax. 

The algorithm minimizes a penalized criterion that balances model fit and complexity, defined as critα(T) = err(T) + α|T|. Here, err(T) quantifies the training error of the subtree T, and |T| represents the number of leaves, with α controlling the penalty for complexity. A higher α value emphasizes penalization of complex models.

The paper references the foundational work of Breiman et al. (1984), establishing that for a sequence of increasing α parameters, the nested models Tk are the optimal subtrees minimizing the penalized criterion. This approach effectively reduces algorithmic complexity while ensuring model performance, applicable to both regression and classification tasks.
