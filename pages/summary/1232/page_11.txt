Chapter 1 of the book "Random Forests with R" provides an introduction to two significant algorithms proposed by Leo Breiman: CART (Classification And Regression Trees) and random forests. CART was introduced in the mid-1980s, while random forests emerged in the early 2000s. The chapter begins with a historical overview of these algorithms, highlighting their contributions to the fields of statistics and statistical learning.

Key statistical objectives such as classification, regression, prediction, and variable selection are defined, alongside the introduction of relevant notations. The chapter also lists three R packages utilized throughout the book and mentions some competing methods. To illustrate the application of these techniques, four datasets are presented: a spam dataset, a genomic dataset, and two pollution datasets focused on ozone and dust.

CART trees serve as the foundational tool for various tree-based ensemble methods. Despite their simplicity and interpretability, decision trees faced criticism prior to the 1980s. CART addressed these concerns by providing a framework for automatic model selection, enhancing their theoretical foundation and applicability. However, the instability of decision trees remained a challenge. Random forests tackle this issue by introducing randomness in the selection of both individuals and variables during tree construction, leading to a more robust final prediction by combining multiple trees.
