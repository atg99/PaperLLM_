The paper discusses the classification and regression tasks within the CART (Classification and Regression Trees) framework, focusing on how to determine optimal splits at each node. In classification, the Gini index quantifies node impurity, defined as Φ(t) = ∑ C c=1 p̂c t (1 - p̂c t), where p̂c t represents the proportion of observations for class c in node t. The goal is to maximize the decrease in impurity through splits, expressed as Φ(t) - (|tL|/|t|Φ(tL) + |tR|/|t|Φ(tR)). 

The search for the best split involves evaluating all variables, allowing a variable to be utilized multiple times or not at all. For regression tasks, the focus shifts to reducing variance in resulting nodes. While the Gini index is a common measure for node homogeneity, alternative measures like the misclassification rate may not ensure a unique optimum for splits, which is beneficial for computational efficiency.

For nominal variables, splits are defined by partitioning the possible values into two groups. The paper also addresses handling underrepresented classes through prior probabilities or oversampling strategies to mitigate classification errors. The tree construction continues recursively until a stopping criterion is met, typically avoiding splits on pure nodes or those with insufficient observations, resulting in terminal nodes referred to as leaves.
