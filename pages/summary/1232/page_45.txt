In Section 3.1, the paper discusses the concept of bootstrap sampling and its effect on the construction of CART trees using the spam dataset. Two bootstrap samples, spamBoot1 and spamBoot2, are created from the original learning sample spamApp, with each sample generated by randomly selecting observations with replacement. The differing structures of the resulting trees, treeBoot1 and treeBoot2, are highlighted, illustrating that variations in the initial splits significantly influence the entire tree's development due to the recursive nature of CART. 

The trees exhibit notable differences, particularly in their initial splits, indicating that the bootstrap samples can lead to vastly different models. However, the tree built on spamBoot2 shows some similarity to the original tree constructed from the entire dataset. The analysis suggests that the variable charDollar is likely to reappear in the root node split due to its previous importance in the tree-building process.

Finally, a comparison of predictions from both trees on a test sample reveals a mean prediction discrepancy of approximately 0.088, indicating some level of agreement between the two models despite their structural differences. This section underscores the variability introduced by bootstrap sampling in predictive modeling.
