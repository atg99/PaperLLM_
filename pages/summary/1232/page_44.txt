Random forests are an ensemble learning method that combines multiple decision trees to improve predictive performance in both regression and classification tasks. The prediction of a random forest in regression is the average of individual tree predictions, while in classification, it is determined by the majority vote among tree predictions. This definition, although slightly different from Breiman's original, emphasizes that the randomness introduced by the model parameters is independent of the learning sample, aligning with common random forest variants.

To achieve high predictive accuracy, a random forest must consist of diverse trees, as similar predictors will not enhance the model's performance. Additionally, each tree should possess a reasonable level of predictive capacity; otherwise, the aggregation of poor predictions will not yield reliable results.

The chapter also highlights the instability of CART trees, indicating that even minor changes to the training data can lead to significantly different tree structures. This property underscores the importance of utilizing multiple trees within the random forest framework to achieve robustness and stability in predictions.
