The analysis compares the variable selection at the root node of decision trees created using two different ensemble methods: Bagging and Random Forests (RF). In the Bagging approach with stumps, the charExclamation and charDollar variables are predominantly chosen for splits, indicating a lack of diversity in the selected splitting variables. Specifically, charExclamation is chosen 70 times and charDollar 29 times, with free appearing only once.

In contrast, the Random Forest model, which allows for a greater variety of variables to be considered at each split, exhibits a broader range of splitting variables. While charExclamation and charDollar remain among the most frequently selected, the RF model includes a wider array of variables such as free, remove, and others, indicating a more diverse set of optimal splits. This difference highlights the increased diversity and robustness provided by the Random Forest method compared to Bagging, even though the study focuses solely on root node splits.
