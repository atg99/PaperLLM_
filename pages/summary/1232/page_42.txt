The paper presents an analysis of a decision tree model applied to the Vac18 data set, focusing on the optimal pruning of the tree for improved predictive performance. It highlights that the optimal tree identified through leave-one-out cross-validation consists of four leaves, contrasting with the 1-SE tree, which retains the default structure of three leaves. The methodology for obtaining the optimal tree is detailed through a series of R commands that involve selecting the minimum complexity parameter from the tree's complexity table, followed by pruning the tree accordingly. The final visualization of the optimal tree is achieved through plotting and annotating the tree structure. This approach underscores the importance of model optimization in enhancing prediction accuracy.
