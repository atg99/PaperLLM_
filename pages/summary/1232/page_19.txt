Chapter 2 focuses on Classiﬁcation And Regression Trees (CART), a statistical method introduced by Breiman et al. in 1984 for creating tree predictors for regression and classification tasks. The chapter details the construction of CART trees, which involves two main steps: the maximal tree growing algorithm that generates a broad range of models and the pruning algorithm that selects the most suitable final model. The process is exemplified using the spam dataset with the rpart package.

CART trees are structured with the root at the top and leaves at the bottom. Leaves represent outcomes or class labels, while nonterminal nodes pose conditions that guide the decision-making process. The chapter illustrates how predictions are made by tracing a path from the root to a leaf based on the conditions specified at each node. For example, an email is classified as spam if certain character proportions exceed specified thresholds.

Additionally, the chapter addresses interpretability of CART trees, the use of competing and surrogate splits, and applies the method to predict ozone concentration and analyze genomic data. Overall, CART provides a clear and effective framework for decision-making in both classification and regression scenarios.
