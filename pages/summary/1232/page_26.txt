The paper discusses the process of model selection and pruning in classification and regression trees (CART), emphasizing the role of the rpart package in R. It begins by noting that the penalized criterion crit α/k minimizes the model complexity, where α/k is calculated as the square root of the product of α and the number of leaves. The chosen model is Tˆk, determined by the minimum cross-validated criterion among a set of trees.

The authors highlight that while a tree with k leaves is optimal within the examined sequence, this sequence does not include all potential best trees; rather, it only contains those that are competitive based on the penalized criterion. Trees that do not meet this competitiveness threshold are not computed, as they typically incur higher costs without providing better performance. 

Furthermore, the paper stresses the necessity of pruning CART trees to prevent overfitting, which can lead to poor generalization on new data. The rpart package implements the CART methodology, providing functions for tree construction, control, and pruning. It also effectively manages missing data during both the learning and prediction phases.

Additional packages in R that facilitate decision tree analysis are mentioned, including tree, which offers validation sample pruning, rpart.plot for enhanced graphics, and party, which provides alternative splitting criteria. The paper concludes with an example of using rpart() and prune() functions for spam detection, illustrating how to build a tree with default parameters in R.
