The section discusses the construction of a maximal tree, denoted as Tmax, in the context of regression and classification tasks. Each node in the tree is assigned a value, either representing the output for regression or the majority class label for classification. This creates a piecewise constant function that reflects the partitioning defined by the tree's leaves. The maximal tree's structure, illustrated with spam data, shows that the edges connecting nodes vary in length, with the height of an edge indicating the reduction in heterogeneity achieved through splitting. Greater homogeneity gains are observed near the root, while splits closer to the leaves yield minimal gains.

The pruning process is introduced as the second step of the CART algorithm. It aims to identify the best pruned subtree from the maximal tree by minimizing generalization error. This process seeks a balance between the high variance and low bias of the maximal tree and the low variance but high bias of a tree that consists solely of the root. Pruning functions as a model selection method, focusing on binary subtrees of Tmax that share the same root.
