The paper by Genuer et al. (2010b) introduces a variable selection method that operates automatically without requiring prior specification of the number of variables to select. This method is implemented in the VSURF package. The selection process consists of two main steps: 

1. A coarse, descending step that eliminates a large number of irrelevant variables based on their importance.
2. A finer, ascending step that sequentially introduces variables into random forest models.

The method distinguishes between two objectives for variable selection: interpretation and prediction. For interpretation, the aim is to select all variables strongly related to the response variable, even if they are correlated with each other. In contrast, the prediction objective seeks a parsimonious subset of variables that can effectively predict the response, typically resulting in fewer variables that are weakly correlated.

The paper highlights a practical example in high-dimensional classification, such as fMRI data, where many variables may be uninformative, and groups of highly correlated predictors may exist. In this context, selecting all important variables aids in interpretation, while finding a small number of predictive variables focuses on discrimination.

The procedure's initial step is common to both objectives, while the subsequent step varies based on the selected goal. Further details on the procedure will be provided in the following sections.
