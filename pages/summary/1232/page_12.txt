The paper provides an introduction to Random Forests (RF), a nonparametric statistical learning method developed by Breiman and others, which has gained popularity across various fields such as genomics, ecology, and pollution prediction. The method is noted for its excellent predictive performance, as evidenced by comparative evaluations highlighting its superiority over other algorithms like CART. RF is versatile, capable of handling high-dimensional data and suitable for both classification and regression tasks, accommodating a mix of qualitative and quantitative variables.

One of the key features of RF is its ability to quantify the relative importance of explanatory variables, a concept that is often overlooked in traditional statistical approaches. This capability not only aids in variable selection, allowing practitioners to identify the most relevant factors influencing the response variable, but also enhances model interpretability and predictive efficiency by reducing the number of irrelevant variables. The authors emphasize the practical value of RF as both a powerful predictive tool and a means for effective variable selection, thus streamlining the learning process. The notation introduced in the paper assumes the availability of a learning sample represented as pairs of observations and responses.
