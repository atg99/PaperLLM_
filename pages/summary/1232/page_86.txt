Chapter 5 focuses on variable selection using random forests, introducing an automatic three-step procedure. This process begins with a coarse elimination of irrelevant variables, followed by a more refined and sequential introduction of variables into random forest models for interpretation and prediction. The chapter details the principles and methods of the VSURF package, utilizing the spam dataset to illustrate the approach. It further examines the selection of appropriate VSURF parameters.

The chapter highlights the shift in data analysis from traditional statistical problems, characterized by a small number of variables and a larger number of observations, to high-dimensional data scenarios. In contemporary applications, it is common to encounter datasets with a vast number of variables compared to a limited number of observations, particularly in fields like biomedicine. This high-dimensional nature presents unique challenges, as traditional statistical techniques struggle to effectively manage such data.

Random forests are presented as a powerful tool for addressing both conventional and high-dimensional data problems. The authors reference Biau (2012), who demonstrates that under certain sparsity conditions, the random forest predictor is reliant solely on active variables, reinforcing the method's suitability for high-dimensional contexts. The chapter concludes with practical applications of the variable selection procedure in predicting ozone concentration and analyzing genomic data.
