In this section, the paper discusses the concepts of competing and surrogate splits in the context of decision trees, specifically within the CART framework. Competing splits refer to alternative splits that may be optimal or nearly optimal, taking into account factors such as uncertainty, measurement ease, and interpretability.

Surrogate splits address the practical challenge of missing values when predicting outcomes. When an input variable is missing for a given observation, it presents a problem only if the prediction path relies on that variable for splitting. In such cases, CART allows for the use of alternative variables, known as surrogate splits, to minimize routing errors. These surrogate splits are determined by their ability to approximate the routing decisions made by the optimal split, providing a localized and efficient strategy for handling missing data instead of relying on broader imputation methods.

The section illustrates these concepts through an example involving a decision tree model created with the R package rpart, highlighting the model's structure, variable importance, and the predicted outcomes based on the training data.
