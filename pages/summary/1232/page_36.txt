The paper discusses the application of CART (Classification and Regression Trees) methodology in analyzing various datasets, with a specific focus on predicting ozone concentration. It highlights the use of the rpart package in R for building decision trees. The example provided involves loading the Ozone dataset and constructing a decision tree with the default parameters of the rpart function. The response variable is identified as column 4 of the dataset, denoted as V4.

The analysis of the generated tree reveals that the first splits are determined by variables V8 and V10, followed by V1 and V2. This is significant as ozone is categorized as a secondary pollutant, formed through the chemical transformation of primary precursor gases emitted from sources such as vehicle exhaust. The paper emphasizes that while the dataset consists of real data, the results are interpretable and contribute to our understanding of ozone concentration dynamics in relation to its precursors.
