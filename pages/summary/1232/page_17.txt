The paper discusses various datasets relevant for different research studies, emphasizing their applications and characteristics.

The "Ozone" dataset, part of the mlbench package, can be accessed in R using the command >data("Ozone", package = "mlbench"). 

The vac18 dataset, derived from an HIV prophylactic vaccine trial, includes gene expression data for 42 observations from 12 negative HIV participants exposed to four stimuli: the candidate vaccine LIPO5, a Gag peptide vaccine (GAG), a non-Gag peptide vaccine (GAG-), and a non-stimulation condition (NS). The goal is to classify the type of stimulation based on gene expression, marking it as a 4-class high-dimensional classification problem. This classification is a preliminary step aimed at identifying the most significant genes for distinguishing between the vaccines. The vac18 data is available in the mixOmics package and can be loaded with >data("vac18", package = "mixOmics").

Lastly, the paper describes a dataset on dust pollution published by Jollois et al. in 2009, focusing on PM10 airborne particles measured by Air Normand in Upper Normandy. The dataset includes readings from six monitoring stations across various environments, including industrial and urban areas, as well as a rural location. The European regulation limit for PM10 concentration is set at 50 µg/m³ as a daily average, which should not be exceeded more than 35 days a year.
