The paper discusses the CART (Classification and Regression Trees) methodology for building decision trees, highlighting its relevance and comparison with earlier methods like CHAID and C4.5. It emphasizes the ongoing interest in tree methods in fields like computer science and statistics. The authors focus on the commonly used variant of CART introduced by Breiman et al. in 1984, noting its accessibility and interpretability as reasons for its success.

CART operates through a two-step process: first, it constructs a maximal tree by recursively partitioning the input space, and then it prunes this tree to derive optimal subtrees. The partitioning process is described as a binary tree structure, where each node represents a partition of the input space. This systematic approach allows for effective prediction based on the partitioned data. The paper also references a concise presentation of the regression CART method in a French PhD thesis by Gey.
