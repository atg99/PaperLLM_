The methodology employed involves using deterministic models to create a pollution map that does not rely on direct measurements but instead on emissions inventories, which can introduce significant uncertainties. To mitigate these uncertainties, statistical tools, particularly Random Forest (RF) models, are utilized to compute variable importance (VI) for different monitoring stations categorized by their proximity to traffic, urban, industrial, or rural areas.

The analysis reveals that meteorological variables exhibit similar importance across stations, with values close to 20, except for specific cases like PA at GUI, which reaches 32. In contrast, the importance of pollutants varies more widely, ranging from 28 to 49, particularly noted in urban stations for SO2. The traffic monitoring stations show consistent NO2 importance, while the industrial station presents an intermediate SO2 importance.

The findings emphasize the utility of measuring diffuse pollution from the AIL station, which serves as a regional indicator unaffected by local sources, facilitating a clearer understanding of the overall pollution dynamics in the region. This structured approach to variable importance not only aids in the assessment of local contributions but also informs adaptive strategies for regional pollution management.
