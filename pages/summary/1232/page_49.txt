The section discusses the principles of Bagging and the Random Forests-Random Inputs (RF-RI) algorithm. Bagging addresses the instability of Classification and Regression Trees (CART) by creating multiple trees from different bootstrap samples, which are then aggregated to form a stable predictor. This method is particularly effective with unstable predictors like trees, while stable predictors, such as logistic regression, do not benefit significantly from Bagging.

The RF-RI algorithm, introduced by Breiman in 2001, is highlighted as a reference method in random forests. The term "Random Inputs" refers to the random selection of input variables in building the trees. In RF-RI, each tree, termed an RI tree, is constructed using a bootstrap sample and incorporates a unique approach where a subset of variables is randomly chosen at each node for splitting, and the trees are not pruned. This method enhances the diversity and performance of the tree predictors. A diagram summarizing the RF-RI algorithm illustrates the bootstrap and random variable selection processes.
