The paper discusses the application of prompt-based learning for CR, outlining the structure of prompts and their roles in facilitating the task. It highlights the distinction between the static prefix, which describes the CR task, and the dynamic unfilled prompt, which varies with each input. The study references the WSC dataset, demonstrating that even without supervised training on CR tasks, models can achieve significant accuracy, indicating some inherent knowledge of CR.

The paper categorizes existing CR models into unsupervised and supervised types, detailing the Multi-Pass Sieve model as a notable unsupervised approach and the e2e-coref model as a pioneering supervised end-to-end neural model. It notes that while supervised models show considerable advancements, they are computationally expensive.

The methodology section elaborates on a three-step process for CR: detecting mentions, determining coreference between mention pairs, and clustering mentions. The authors define the input-output framework for their model, emphasizing the importance of few-shot learning to enhance the model's task comprehension through examples from the training dataset. The construction of the final prompt is crucial for generating predictions without altering the parameters of the pre-trained language model (LM).

In summary, the paper presents a novel prompt-based learning approach for CR, leveraging pre-trained models and few-shot learning to yield effective predictions while addressing the challenges of output validity.
