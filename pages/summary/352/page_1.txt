In the paper "What GPT Knows About Who is Who," the authors Xiaohan Yang, Eduardo Peynetti, Vasco Meerman, and Chris Tanner explore the capabilities of large language models (LLMs) in the task of coreference resolution (CR), which is essential for understanding natural language. They highlight that while CR systems typically rely on supervised labels that are costly and challenging to annotate, there is potential for employing prompt engineering to leverage pre-trained models.

The authors introduce a question-answering (QA)-based prompt-engineering method to assess the performance of GPT-2 and GPT-Neo in identifying coreferent mentions. Their experiments reveal that these models can produce valid answers, but their ability to accurately identify coreferent mentions is inconsistent and highly dependent on the prompts used.

The introduction discusses the significance of CR in natural language understanding and the limitations of existing datasets like ECB+, which contains a small number of documents and mentions, posing challenges for state-of-the-art models. The paper emphasizes the emerging trend of using prompt engineering for low-resource tasks, contrasting it with traditional fine-tuning methods.

The related work section notes the growing interest in prompt-based learning across various NLP tasks, with over 120 relevant papers published since 2019. However, it points out that there has been limited research applying prompt-based approaches specifically to coreference resolution.

Overall, the paper aims to evaluate whether pre-trained LMs can effectively resolve coreferences and provides a foundation for future research in this area.
