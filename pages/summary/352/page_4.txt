The analysis of model performance in coreference resolution reveals that e2e-coref outperforms GPT-based models in precision, recall, F1, and AUC metrics, indicating a more reliable classification of positive examples. While GPT-2 shows sensitivity to prompts and inconsistency across repetitions, GPT-Neo's predictions are largely inaccurate, resembling random guesses. The experiments conducted on the imbalanced ECB+ dataset highlight the poor performance of GPT models, attributed to their extremely low precision and inability to handle the dataset's characteristics effectively. 

Further investigations into part-of-speech (POS) tags and named-entity types suggest that GPT models may perform slightly better when the second mention is a pronoun, yet e2e-coref consistently outperforms them. The analysis of mention similarity demonstrates a general improvement in F1 scores as semantic similarity increases, although the GPT models struggle with low similarity pairs. Overall, classical coreference resolution models like multi-pass sieve and e2e-coref achieve higher precision across various mention types compared to the GPT models, underscoring their limitations in coreference resolution tasks.
