The paper discusses the implementation and evaluation of various prompt formulas for text generation models, particularly focusing on the ECB+ dataset. It outlines the challenges faced during data preprocessing, specifically the difficulty in converting XML tokenized data into a readable format that aligns with the training of Generative Pre-trained Transformers (GPTs). The authors emphasize the need for proper formatting to ensure that elements like URLs and dates are correctly represented in the input text.

In the experimental section, the authors present results from their few-shot learning experiments. They find that a 4-shot learning approach yields the best accuracy for both GPT-2 and GPT-NEO. However, increasing the number of examples to 10 does not improve performance, with the model showing a tendency to predict "yes" more frequently. This behavior may be attributed to the imbalance in the dataset, which contains only 8% positive samples.

The results are summarized in two tables: Table 3 presents the n-shot performance across different metrics, while Table 4 compares average results from various datasets used in the experiments. Notably, the ECB+ dataset prefix performs slightly better in terms of AUC, likely due to its alignment with the evaluation set. The WSC dataset, however, produces perfect recall but poor accuracy, indicating that it prompts the model to respond affirmatively regardless of context. This reinforces the notion that GPT-2 is highly sensitive to prompt design.
