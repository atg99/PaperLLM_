The paper discusses a method for improving predictions in coreference resolution by averaging the outputs from multiple prompt formulas to reduce bias from any specific formula. The experimental setup utilizes the ECB+ dataset, which includes coreference information across documents, and involves preprocessing to create a development set of 172 documents. The authors experiment with three data sources to generate prefixes for their model, including the WSC dataset and a manually created dataset. 

The evaluation focuses on pairs of mentions within the same or successive sentences, resulting in 17,832 candidate mention pairs, with 7.86% being positive samples. To benchmark their approach, the authors compare their method against three traditional coreference resolution models: Multi-Pass Sieve, e2e-coref, and a Streamlining model. They implement these models using established toolkits and train the Streamlining model from scratch due to the lack of a pre-trained version.

For their predictions, the authors utilize GPT-2 and GPT-Neo-125M as pretrained language models, adjusting parameters such as repetition and temperature to enhance output robustness. They conduct few-shot learning experiments, with a focus on the 4-shot setting for optimal accuracy, ensuring balanced samples in each prefix.

Results indicate that GPT-2 can produce valid Yes/No predictions, achieving 93.7% accuracy with at least two filled prefixes, although some predictions may lack accuracy. The analysis reveals that traditional coreference resolution models tend to perform better in distinguishing positive examples from negative ones, as evidenced by their prediction distributions.
