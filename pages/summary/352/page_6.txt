The cited works encompass significant advancements in natural language processing (NLP) and machine learning, focusing on various methodologies and challenges. Notable contributions include the development of end-to-end neural coreference resolution by Lee et al., the introduction of the Winograd Schema Challenge by Levesque et al., and a comprehensive survey of prompting methods in NLP by Liu et al. 

The RoBERTa model, presented by Liu et al., showcases an optimized pretraining approach for BERT, while Manning et al. detail the Stanford CoreNLP toolkit, highlighting its utility in NLP tasks. Research on few-shot learning by Perez et al. and the exploration of transfer learning limits by Raffel et al. demonstrate the evolving landscape of model capabilities. 

Sanh et al. discuss multitask prompted training, facilitating zero-shot task generalization, and improvements in pattern exploiting training are presented by Tam et al. Triantafillou et al. approach few-shot learning from an information retrieval perspective, while Tsimpoukelli et al. explore multimodal few-shot learning with frozen language models. Finally, Wei et al. emphasize the potential of fine-tuned language models as zero-shot learners, underscoring the transformative impact of these methodologies in the field.
