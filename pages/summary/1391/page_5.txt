The paper discusses a hierarchical segmentation model called HSSN, which utilizes a tunable focusing parameter to control the down-weighting of easy classes in the segmentation process. The model's performance is compared to existing methods, demonstrating that HSSN achieves more precise results. The authors introduce a pixel-wise hierarchical representation learning strategy that leverages the semantic relationships within a class hierarchy to improve pixel embeddings and segmentation accuracy. 

The hierarchical structure is represented as a tree, where the distance between labels reflects their semantic similarity. A tree-triplet loss is employed to ensure that pixel embeddings are well-separated according to their semantic relationships. This loss is defined on pixel triplets consisting of anchor, positive, and negative samples, and aims to maintain a specified margin between embeddings of similar and dissimilar classes.

The HSSN framework is designed to be versatile, applicable to various segmentation architectures, and is implemented with popular backbones like ResNet and HRNet. The training objective combines the focal tree-min loss and the tree-triplet loss to optimize segmentation performance. The model is trained end-to-end, with specific training strategies and a projection function to refine pixel embeddings. Inference is conducted by assigning labels to each pixel based on learned representations. Overall, the proposed method enhances segmentation by leveraging hierarchical semantic structures effectively.
