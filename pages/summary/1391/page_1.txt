In the paper "Deep Hierarchical Semantic Segmentation," the authors, Liulei Li et al., address the limitations of current semantic segmentation methods, which typically treat target classes as disjoint and fail to capture the structured relationships inherent in visual scenes. They introduce a novel approach called Hierarchical Semantic Segmentation Network (HSSN) that aims to provide a structured, pixel-wise description of visual observations based on a class hierarchy.

The HSSN framework tackles two key challenges: adapting existing segmentation networks for hierarchical semantic segmentation and leveraging hierarchy information to enhance network learning. To adapt networks, HSSN reformulates the task as a pixel-wise multi-label classification problem, requiring minimal changes to existing architectures. For leveraging hierarchy information, HSSN incorporates inherent properties of the class hierarchy into the training objective, ensuring that segmentation predictions align with the hierarchical structure. Additionally, it employs hierarchy-induced margin constraints to reshape the pixel embedding space, resulting in improved pixel representations and segmentation outcomes.

The authors validate the effectiveness of HSSN through experiments on four semantic segmentation datasets, including Mapillary Vistas, Cityscapes, LIP, and PASCAL-Person-Part, demonstrating its generalization capabilities and superiority over existing methods. The paper emphasizes the importance of hierarchical reasoning in semantic segmentation and presents HSSN as a significant advancement in the field.
