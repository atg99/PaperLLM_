The paper discusses a novel approach for pixel-wise hierarchical segmentation learning that incorporates class hierarchy constraints to improve segmentation accuracy. It introduces two key properties, the Positive T-Property and the Negative T-Property, which ensure that if a pixel is labeled positive for a certain class, all its ancestor classes must also be positive, and if a pixel is negative, all its subclasses must also be negative. 

To implement these properties, the authors propose a hierarchy-aware segmentation learning scheme that includes a pixel-wise hierarchical segmentation learning strategy and a pixel-wise hierarchical representation learning strategy. The segmentation learning strategy defines two hierarchy constraints: the Positive T-Constraint and the Negative T-Constraint, which help maintain consistency in pixel labeling according to the hierarchy.

The proposed method replaces the traditional binary cross-entropy loss with a Tree-Min Loss (LTM) that aligns pixel predictions with the established hierarchy. This loss function is designed to ensure that the score distributions of pixel predictions adhere to the hierarchy constraints, thus penalizing any violations of these properties.

Additionally, a Focal Tree-Min Loss (LFTM) is introduced, which incorporates a modulating factor to focus on difficult-to-classify pixels, reducing the loss for well-classified samples. The results indicate that this approach significantly enhances the performance of hierarchical segmentation models by ensuring coherent and accurate class predictions.
