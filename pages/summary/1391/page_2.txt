This paper introduces a novel approach for hierarchical semantic segmentation (HSS) termed HSSN, which organizes classes in a tree-shaped hierarchy instead of a flat structure. This hierarchical organization allows for a more nuanced pixel classification, where each pixel is linked to a root-to-leaf path in the class hierarchy, capturing general-to-specific relationships. HSSN addresses two significant aspects of HSS that have not been previously explored. 

Firstly, it reformulates HSS as a pixel-wise multi-label classification task, enabling the integration of existing segmentation models into the HSS framework without complex network redesign. Secondly, it leverages the class hierarchy during network learning by implementing two constraints: a pixel belonging to a class must also belong to its ancestors, and a pixel not belonging to a class must not belong to its descendants. This enforces hierarchical coherence in pixel predictions.

HSSN enhances pixel representation learning by reshaping the embedding space according to the class hierarchy, facilitating the grouping of semantically similar pixels and distancing dissimilar ones. This method improves learning efficiency by recognizing common patterns and allows for different levels of mistakes, which is crucial in applications like autonomous driving.

The proposed method is adaptable to various existing hierarchy-agnostic segmentation networks with minimal modifications. Extensive experiments on four public benchmarks demonstrate the generalization and effectiveness of HSSN across multiple segmentation architectures and backbones. The work advances the field of hierarchical segmentation by integrating structured class relationships, reflecting the hierarchical reasoning of human visual perception.
