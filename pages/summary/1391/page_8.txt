The paper introduces H SSN, a structured method for semantic segmentation that leverages taxonomic semantic relationships for improved scene parsing. It enhances existing hierarchy-agnostic segmentation networks by incorporating hierarchical properties as optimization criteria, which allows for explicit penalties on hierarchical violations in segmentation predictions. The method utilizes hierarchy-induced margin separation to produce more effective pixel representations.

The experimental results demonstrate that H SSN outperforms several existing segmentation models across four well-known datasets. Key analyses include the performance of various loss functions, such as categorical cross-entropy, binary cross-entropy, focal loss, and the proposed focal tree-min loss (LFTM). Results indicate that LFTM significantly improves performance compared to other loss functions. Additionally, the paper examines the tree-triplet loss (LTT) and shows that its hierarchy-aware design yields better results than traditional triplet loss approaches.

The findings emphasize the importance of hierarchical representation learning in semantic segmentation, suggesting that combining LFTM and LTT enhances overall performance. The paper concludes by highlighting the potential of H SSN to advance research in hierarchical semantic segmentation, supported by grants from various institutions.
