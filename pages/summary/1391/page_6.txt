The paper presents an experimental evaluation of hierarchical semantic segmentation methods on urban street scene datasets, specifically Mapillary Vistas 2.0 and Cityscapes, as well as human body parsing datasets like PASCAL-Person-Part and LIP. The datasets utilized have varying class hierarchies, with Mapillary Vistas 2.0 containing 144 semantic concepts organized into three levels and Cityscapes featuring 19 fine-grained concepts grouped into six super-classes.

The training setup includes hyper-parameter configurations for both CNN-based and Transformer-based models, with specific optimizers and learning rates. Data augmentation techniques such as horizontal flipping and random scaling were employed. The number of training iterations varied by dataset, with significant iterations allocated for both Mapillary Vistas 2.0 and Cityscapes.

Testing followed a multi-scale inference approach, and evaluation was performed using the mean intersection-over-union (mIoU) metric, providing detailed results for different hierarchy levels. The results indicate that the proposed H SSN method outperforms several leading segmentation techniques across the datasets, showcasing its effectiveness in hierarchical semantic segmentation.

Tables included in the paper display quantitative results, highlighting the performance of various methods and backbones, with H SSN achieving competitive mIoU scores across different scenarios. This suggests that the proposed approach is a strong contender in the field of semantic segmentation.
