The paper discusses the current state of myoelectric pattern recognition (PR) control methods for hand prostheses, emphasizing the need for real-time evaluation and application. It identifies key areas for improvement in myoelectric prosthetics, including intuitive control of multiple degrees of freedom, two-way communication with the peripheral nervous system, and enhanced learning capabilities. Despite advancements in pattern recognition techniques, many remain confined to clinical settings and face challenges such as electrode positioning, muscle fatigue, and cross-talk.

The paper is structured as a review, with Section 2 detailing the research methodology, which focuses on real-time EMG-PR control. It notes that while classification accuracy in offline settings exceeds 95%, real-time applications do not achieve the same performance. The review highlights existing classifications, feature extraction methods, and factors affecting performance, asserting its contribution to the limited literature on real-time applications.

Subsequent sections explore key techniques in myoelectric PR, contrasting pattern recognition systems with simpler non-pattern recognition controls, which are less flexible. The paper concludes with a discussion on future research directions aimed at enhancing the functionality and usability of myoelectric prostheses based on critical analysis of the current advancements in the field.
