This paper discusses various advancements in the classification of myoelectric signals for enhancing the control of multifunction prostheses. It begins with a study by Karlik et al. in 2003, which compared fuzzy clustering neural networks (FCCN) with multi-layer perceptrons (MLP). The FCCN demonstrated superior accuracy at 98% with reduced training time. Following this, Chan and Englehart in 2005 introduced a hidden Markov model (HMM) for data classification, achieving a performance accuracy of 94.63% with improved robustness compared to MLP.

Al-Timemy et al. focused on classifying finger motions for precise control of myoelectric prostheses, employing TD-AR features from six below-elbow amputees. They utilized PCA and an orthogonal fuzzy neighborhood discriminative approach (OFNDA) alongside classifiers like LDA and SVM, finding that feature reduction was more critical than the choice of classifier in achieving high accuracy.

In 2013, Pan et al. developed a method for predicting finger joint angles in partial hand amputees using EMG signals from eight muscles. Their approach achieved a classification error rate of 6.18%, indicating effective classification of wrist positions and finger movements.

The paper also highlights earlier work from 1993 using the Hudgins feature set with an ANN classifier. Riillo et al. later proposed an optimization methodology for hand gesture classification based on sEMG, employing preprocessing techniques like PCA and CSP.

Overall, the studies emphasize the importance of advanced signal processing techniques and feature reduction in enhancing the functionality and accuracy of myoelectric control systems.
