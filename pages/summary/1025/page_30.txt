The paper discusses various studies and advancements in brain-computer interfaces (BCIs) and related technologies. Key findings include the exploration of combined acoustic-visual feedback in BCIs, highlighting its potential benefits in enhancing user experience. The research also evaluates the accessibility of EEG-based BCIs, revealing that a significant number of individuals can successfully operate these systems. Additionally, the implementation of ultrasound imaging as a human-machine interface for upper-limb amputees is examined, showcasing a realistic approach to improving functionality for this demographic. The paper concludes with a presentation of the IMES, an innovative implantable myoelectric sensor, which represents a significant advancement in the field of neuroengineering.
