The paper discusses various challenges associated with real-time electromyography-based control of hand prostheses. Key challenges include:

1. **Comfort**: Inadequate socket fitting can lead to pain, sores, and discomfort, as well as issues like heat and chafing.
   
2. **Appearance**: Many prostheses lack a natural look and can be uncomfortable for users, complicating control of multiple degrees of freedom.

3. **Functionality**: While prostheses can perform many daily tasks, accurately interpreting the signals from residual muscles (primarily biceps and triceps) for hand movements remains difficult.

4. **Durability**: Many devices are heavy and have limited battery life.

5. **Cost**: The high price of around $50,000 makes them unaffordable for many amputees.

6. **Technology**: Current prosthetic devices struggle with intuitiveness and reliability, requiring extensive training for users.

7. **Processing Delay**: A delay in the embedded processor (approximately 3 seconds) disrupts real-time EMG acquisition.

8. **EMG Interferences**: Variations in EMG signals due to external factors, muscle fatigue, and electrode issues can hinder device performance and user adoption.

9. **Electrode Displacement**: Movements during prosthesis use can shift electrodes, complicating the EMG signal interpretation.

10. **Amputee Movement**: EMG signals are typically recorded in static positions, but real-world use involves dynamic movements that affect classification performance.

11. **Muscle Contraction Forces**: The variability in muscle contraction during different activities leads to inconsistencies in myoelectric signal patterns.

12. **Limb Position Variation**: Changes in limb position and gravitational effects during various actions can alter EMG signal patterns, impacting control performance.

These challenges highlight the complexities involved in improving EMG-based control systems for prosthetic devices.
