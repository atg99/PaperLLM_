This review paper focuses on the development and challenges of real-time electromyography (EMG) based pattern recognition control systems for hand prostheses. It emphasizes the limitations faced by amputees in daily activities due to upper limb loss and highlights the potential of myoelectric prostheses, which utilize signals from residual muscles to restore functionality. However, the complexity of acquiring myosignals and the computational demands for processing these signals present significant hurdles.

The authors point out that variations among amputees, such as differences in mobility, muscle contraction, limb position, and electrode placement, necessitate adaptive solutions for effective prosthetic control. Modified machine learning approaches are suggested as a means to improve the accuracy of EMG pattern recognition while minimizing the impact of user movement and muscle contractions.

The review systematically examines existing upper limb prosthesis technologies from a technical control perspective, with a strong emphasis on real-world applications and the effectiveness of pattern recognition systems in practical use. It concludes by discussing the challenges that remain in the field and offering recommendations for future research to enhance the functionality and accessibility of myoelectric prostheses. Key terms include myo-prosthesis, EMG, pattern recognition, and myosignals.
