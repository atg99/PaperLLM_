The paper discusses the complexities involved in replicating the coordinated control of biological hands in prosthetic devices. It highlights the necessity of synchronizing the nervous and musculoskeletal systems to perform daily activities, emphasizing the role of various joints in achieving a wide range of motions. The paper identifies three main components of prosthetic hands: an input signal acquisition unit, a processing and control unit, and an end effector, with surface electromyography (sEMG) signals being the primary method for controlling end effectors. 

The authors note that while advances in sensor technology have improved the performance of prosthetic devices, traditional methods of acquiring myosignals have remained largely unchanged for decades. They point out the evolution of myoelectric hands to address the limitations of earlier models, particularly through the development of pattern recognition (PR) techniques that enhance dexterity compared to conventional control methods. 

The PR approach extracts multiple features from EMG signals, allowing for more intuitive control of prosthetic devices. This method facilitates independent control of multiple degrees of freedom, making it easier for users to manage their prosthetic limbs in a manner that mimics natural arm functions. The paper concludes that integrating PR technology with machine learning algorithms significantly enhances the accuracy of limb movement decoding, ultimately improving the usability of myoelectric prostheses.
