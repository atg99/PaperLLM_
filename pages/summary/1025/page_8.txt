The paper discusses various methods for controlling myoelectric prostheses, focusing on regression techniques, artificial neural networks (ANN), and non-negative matrix factorization. It highlights the challenges faced in real-time applications, particularly the transition from virtual environments to real-world usability. Key obstacles include the need for robust portable embedded systems, effective designs for dexterous prosthetic hands, and efficient multichannel electrode placements. 

Research shows that while offline classification of electromyography (EMG) signals offers promising accuracy, real-time performance often falls short due to arm position variations. Various classification techniques have been proposed to mitigate these effects. The paper reviews studies that evaluate the performance of different classification algorithms, noting that fuzzy clustering neural networks (FCCN) outperformed multi-layer perceptron (MLP) models in terms of accuracy and training time. Additionally, a hidden Markov model (HMM) was highlighted for its effectiveness in handling sequential variations in myoelectric signals, achieving higher accuracy compared to MLP in certain applications.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the need for ongoing development in both hardware and algorithms to enhance the functionality and usability of myoelectric prostheses in real-world settings.
