The paper discusses advancements in prosthetic hand control through the integration of surface electromyography (sEMG) and inertial measurement (IM) systems. Using twelve electrodes at a sampling frequency of 2 kHz, key features such as Mean Absolute Value (MAV), Waveform Length (WL), Auto-Regressive coefficients (4AR), and logarithmic variance (logVar) were extracted via a sliding window technique. Experiments were conducted in both offline and real-time settings, utilizing Touch Bionics' robotic hand, demonstrating improved classification performance and reduced sensor requirements when combining IM and sEMG.

Further exploration by Yanjuan et al. highlighted the importance of both offline motion classification accuracy and real-time motion completion rates in evaluating EMG-based prosthetic control. Trongmun et al. introduced a signal processing method that successfully classified 17 spontaneous motion classes using six EMG sensors, achieving an overall classification rate of 83% and 92% for nine movements.

The introduction of targeted muscle reinnervation (TMR) has marked a significant breakthrough in enhancing control over prosthetic hands. A study involving four TMR patients showed that a generic electrode grid outperformed traditional electrode placements in both real-time and offline classifications. Although TMR holds promise for advanced myoelectric control, challenges remain in implementing it across multiple degrees of freedom (DOFs).

Todd et al. evaluated the real-time performance of TMR-based multifunction prostheses, achieving a mean classification accuracy of 88% for TMR patients and 97% for control participants. The findings underscore the potential of integrating advanced signal processing techniques and surgical innovations to improve prosthetic hand usability and control. A summary of related studies utilizing virtual reality environments is also included in the paper.
