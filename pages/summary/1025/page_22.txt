The potential of EEG and ECog as alternatives to EMG for controlling prostheses is acknowledged, but their current limitations, such as invasiveness, signal stability, and the need for extensive training, suggest that they may be more suitable for individuals with spinal cord injuries rather than general amputees. 

To improve the effectiveness of prosthetic devices, the paper advocates for the development of low-cost materials, advanced pattern-recognition techniques, and intelligent adaptive systems that can accurately represent data streams in real-time. Additionally, addressing challenges in feature extraction through deep learning methods could enhance the usability of EMG-PR systems, making them more intuitive and responsive for users.

In conclusion, while significant progress has been made in understanding EMG-PR techniques and their application in prosthetic control, ongoing research is essential to overcome existing challenges and improve real-time functionality for a wider range of amputees. This includes exploring multiple degrees of freedom and refining the interaction between neural signals and prosthetic devices to ensure a more natural and effective user experience.
