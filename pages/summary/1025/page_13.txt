The paper discusses advancements in real-time assistive devices for individuals with limb disabilities, particularly focusing on the use of electromyographic (EMG) signals for controlling prosthetic devices. A notable development from 2007 involved a multi-layer neural network that classified EMG signals from lower arm muscles during wrist movements to control a cursor, although it lacked detailed control implementation. Anbin et al. introduced a method combining EMG and inertial measurement unit (IMU) signals for mouse control, achieving 88% accuracy.

Bitar et al. detailed a portable Musical Instrument Digital Interface (MIDI) system using continuous wavelet transform (CWT) and support vector machine (SVM) for classifying EMG signals, achieving 91% accuracy. Ke et al. advanced this field with an embedded system for real-time EMG decoding to control a six-degree-of-freedom prosthetic hand, showing promising results for daily applications.

Xiaorong et al. presented a cumulative sum algorithm for real-time self-recovery classification that effectively reduced misclassification due to motion artifacts. In subsequent work, Ann et al. compared adaptive and non-adaptive control methods for a trans-humeral prosthesis, finding that adaptive control improved performance during continuous use.

The paper also highlights user experiences with commercially available EMG-PR controlled prosthetics, such as the DEKA arm, which indicated a positive reception among users. Finally, Mastinu et al. explored real-time pattern recognition implementations on dysmelia subjects using the iLimb-ultra, assessing classification accuracy across various hand motions. Overall, these studies illustrate significant progress in EMG-based control systems for prosthetics, emphasizing the importance of user experience and real-time functionality.
