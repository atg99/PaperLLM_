The study highlights the effectiveness of real-time pattern recognition in motion tests, showing that accuracy in recognizing patterns surpassed both classification and execution accuracy. Hargrove et al. demonstrated the successful outcomes of using commercially available prosthetics in subjects who underwent targeted muscle reinnervation (TMR) while performing household tasks. The research compared direct methods with pattern recognition methods, revealing that users performed better with devices incorporating pattern recognition, with seven out of eight participants preferring this control method. Additionally, the paper discusses various classifiers used in real-time embedded systems, noting that Linear Discriminant Analysis (LDA) is particularly favored due to its simplicity in implementation. Despite advancements in developing prosthetics using EMG-PR control, achieving a natural and reliable limb control remains a significant challenge.
