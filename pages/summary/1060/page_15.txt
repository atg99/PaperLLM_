The paper discusses the applications of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) in medical imaging, highlighting their effectiveness in lesion classification, abnormality detection, image registration, and other tasks. It notes that using a lesion-specific GAN class yields better performance compared to a unified GAN approach. The authors reference Finlayson et al. (2018), who suggest that GAN-generated images can enhance data augmentation in medium-data regimes but may not be beneficial in high or low-data scenarios.

In the detection of abnormalities, GANs' discriminators can identify lesions by learning the distribution of normal images. Studies, such as those by Schlegl et al. (2017) and Alex et al. (2017), illustrate how GANs can be employed for anomaly detection in optical coherence tomography (OCT) and MRI images, respectively. Additionally, the paper discusses how CycleGAN can be utilized for image reconstruction, with the potential to exploit the distribution matching effect for abnormality detection.

Regarding image registration, the paper describes how conditional GANs (cGANs) can facilitate both multi-modal and uni-modal image registration by generating transformation parameters or directly producing transformed images. Various studies, including those by Yan et al. (2018b) and Mahapatra et al. (2018), demonstrate the effectiveness of GANs in aligning images from different modalities, achieving performance comparable to traditional methods.

The authors also mention other applications of GANs, such as modeling patient-specific motion, highlighting disease-affected regions, and treatment planning in radiotherapy. They observe a significant increase in GAN-related studies, particularly in MR imaging, attributed to the time-intensive nature of MR sequence acquisition and the availability of extensive MR datasets.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the growing interest in GANs for medical imaging, particularly for their potential to improve efficiency and accuracy in various imaging tasks.
