The paper discusses the application of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) in various domains, particularly in medical imaging. It presents a categorization of GAN-related publications based on canonical tasks and imaging modalities. The analysis shows that synthesis and segmentation are the most common tasks associated with GANs, with contributions from diverse imaging techniques such as MR, CT, and X-ray. The paper also highlights the growth in publications from 2014 to 2018, indicating a rising interest in GAN applications.

The foundational concept of the vanilla GAN is introduced, detailing its architecture comprising a generator (G) and a discriminator (D). G generates samples from random noise, aiming to produce outputs that resemble real data, while D evaluates whether the input is real or generated. The training process involves G trying to produce convincing fakes to deceive D, which is trained to distinguish between real and fake samples. The objectives of both networks are mathematically formulated, emphasizing the competitive nature of the GAN framework, where G seeks to minimize the likelihood of D detecting fakes, and D aims to maximize its accuracy in classification.

Overall, the paper underscores the significance of GANs in generating realistic medical images and the evolving landscape of research in this area.
