This paper presents a review of the application of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) in medical imaging, highlighting their potential and recent advancements. GANs have gained prominence in the computer vision field due to their ability to generate data without explicit probability modeling. The adversarial training mechanism, which involves a generator and a discriminator, allows for the effective use of unlabeled data and enhances consistency in generated outputs. This has led to various applications in medical imaging, including image reconstruction, segmentation, detection, classification, and cross-modality synthesis.

The review is motivated by the increasing adoption of deep learning methods in medical imaging, which has seen a significant rise in publications since 2012. The authors conducted a comprehensive literature search, focusing on works published up to January 2019, to compile relevant studies on GANs in medical imaging. They categorize the applications of GANs into several tasks such as reconstruction, image synthesis, segmentation, classification, and registration.

The paper is structured to first introduce the principles of GANs and their structural variants, followed by a detailed review of their applications in various medical imaging domains, including radiology and dermatology. The authors conclude by summarizing the findings, discussing future applications, and identifying challenges that need to be addressed for the broader implementation of GANs in medical imaging tasks.
