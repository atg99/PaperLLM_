The paper discusses various applications of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) in medical imaging, focusing on their generative and discriminative capabilities. It highlights three popular conditional GANs: pix2pix, CycleGAN, and UNIT, and refers readers for more detailed evaluations of these models.

In medical imaging, GANs are employed primarily in two ways: generative methods that create new images from existing data, addressing issues like data scarcity and patient privacy, and discriminative approaches that utilize the discriminator component to identify normal versus abnormal images. The paper categorizes applications into tasks such as reconstruction, image synthesis, segmentation, classification, detection, and registration.

Reconstruction methods are emphasized, detailing a shift from traditional analytic techniques to machine learning-based approaches. These methods aim to enhance image quality by mitigating noise and artifacts, often using GANs to formulate the problem as image-to-image translation. The paper reviews various studies employing the pix2pix framework for tasks like low-dose CT denoising and MR reconstruction, incorporating techniques such as perceptual similarity and saliency mapping to improve outcomes.

Challenges in medical image reconstruction, such as the hallucination of unseen structures and the need for reliable performance metrics, are discussed. The authors note that while adversarial losses enhance visual appeal, they may introduce inaccuracies. They call for the development of standardized datasets for better evaluation of GAN-based reconstruction methods and highlight the necessity for rigorous validation before these reconstructed images are used for clinical diagnosis. Overall, the paper underscores the potential of GANs in medical imaging while identifying areas for further research and development.
