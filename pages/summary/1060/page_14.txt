The table summarizes various publications focused on segmentation across different imaging modalities, including CT, MR, retinal fundus imaging, X-ray, histopathology, fluorescent microscopy, dermoscopy, mammography, and ultrasound. Each entry includes the authors, the dataset used, and notable remarks regarding their methodologies.

In CT imaging, notable works include Yang et al., who utilized a U-net generator with deep supervision, and Dou et al., who ensured indistinguishable feature distributions between MR and CT images. Rezaei et al. introduced additional refinement networks to maintain temporal consistency.

For MR imaging, Xue et al. employed a multi-scale L1 loss in their discriminator for feature comparison, while Rezaei et al. utilized heterogeneous MR scans for segmentation tasks. Other contributions involved joint learning approaches and recurrent networks to enhance spatial-temporal processing.

In retinal fundus imaging, Son et al. demonstrated the effectiveness of deep architectures in reducing false positives, while Zhang et al. integrated both annotated and unannotated images in their segmentation pipeline.

X-ray segmentation by Dai et al. highlighted the utility of adversarial loss in correcting shape inconsistencies. In histopathology, Wang et al. focused on basal membrane segmentation.

Fluorescent microscopy work by Li and Shen employed a pix2pix model with an auxiliary classifier to enhance segmentation. Izadi et al. applied adversarial training in dermoscopy to refine boundary precision. Zhu et al. emphasized network invariance in mammography to mitigate overfitting, and Tuysuzoglu et al. combined landmark localization with prostate contour segmentation in ultrasound imaging.

Overall, the publications reflect diverse strategies and innovations in segmentation across various medical imaging fields, emphasizing the importance of adversarial training, joint learning, and domain adaptation techniques.
