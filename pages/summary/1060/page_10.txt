This paper reviews various unconditional medical image synthesis publications, categorizing them by imaging modalities such as CT, MR, X-ray, mammography, histopathology, retinal fundus imaging, and dermoscopy. Key publications are summarized in Table 4, highlighting the methods used, datasets, quantitative measures, and remarks regarding each study.

In CT imaging, methods like DCGAN and ACGAN were employed for generating specific lesion classes, while PGGAN facilitated joint learning of images and segmentation maps. MR imaging studies utilized approaches such as LAPGAN and Semi-Coupled-GAN, demonstrating innovations like coupling multiple generators with a single discriminator.

X-ray studies predominantly used DCGAN, with findings showing that semi-supervised methods can match the performance of traditional supervised CNNs with significantly fewer labeled samples. In histopathology, a combination of WGAN and InfoGAN was used for cell-level representation learning, while PGGAN and DCGAN were applied in retinal fundus imaging.

Dermoscopy studies explored various GAN architectures, including LAPGAN and PGGAN, focusing on feature representation learning for skin lesions. The paper emphasizes the effectiveness of adversarial training in enhancing segmentation accuracy and classification tasks, illustrating how GANs can serve both as feature extractors and classifiers.

Overall, the paper highlights advancements in medical image synthesis and classification through the application of GANs, showcasing their potential to improve diagnostic processes in medical imaging.
