The paper discusses the impact of data augmentation and transfer learning on improving the performance of cell shape detection using the YOLOv2 model applied to a bright-field time-lapse dataset. It illustrates that data augmentation, which included techniques like mirroring and 90° rotations, enhances prediction performance, as shown in the comparison of raw images and model predictions after 30 training epochs. The mean average precision (mAP) is analyzed in relation to the number of epochs for varying levels of data augmentation.

Additionally, the paper emphasizes the effectiveness of transfer learning with a pretrained model, demonstrating high-quality segmentation results from the StarDist model after only 5 epochs. However, it also highlights potential pitfalls of transfer learning, where models trained on large datasets may yield inappropriate results. The performance metrics, including intersection over union (IoU) and F1 scores, are presented through boxplots that depict the median and interquartile range, indicating the variability of results across different training conditions. The findings underscore the importance of both data augmentation and transfer learning in optimizing model performance in image analysis tasks.
