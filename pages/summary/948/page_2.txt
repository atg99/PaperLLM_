The paper discusses the rapid growth in the volume and complexity of bioimages over the past decade, necessitating advanced image analysis techniques. To address this challenge, deep learning (DL), a subset of artificial intelligence, has been increasingly utilized for tasks such as object detection, image segmentation, and restoration. While DL outperforms traditional methods, its adoption has been limited among researchers lacking computer science expertise due to the complexity and computational demands of training DL networks.

The authors highlight two main training approaches: supervised, which requires paired input and output images, and self-supervised, which identifies patterns without such pairings. Training these networks typically involves significant computational resources, often necessitating expensive hardware or cloud services. While pretrained models offer a solution, they may not perform well on datasets that differ significantly from the training data.

To bridge this gap, the authors present ZeroCostDL4Mic, a user-friendly, cloud-based platform that simplifies DL deployment for microscopy applications. It features Jupyter Notebooks with a graphical user interface, allowing users to conduct DL tasks without coding skills or local computational resources. The platform runs on Google Colaboratory, providing free access to necessary computational power.

ZeroCostDL4Mic enables users to train, validate, and apply various DL models for bioimage analysis, including image segmentation, denoising, and super-resolution microscopy. It aims to democratize access to DL tools in biomedical research, fostering a deeper understanding of these methods while minimizing the barriers to entry for researchers.
