The paper discusses the generation and culture of various cell lines for research purposes, employing techniques such as CRISPR/Cas9 and lentiviral transduction. Glioma cells expressing endogenous paxillin-GFP were created, and MCF10 DCIS.COM cells were cultured using a specific media formulation. HeLa cells and A2780 ovarian carcinoma cells were also cultured under controlled conditions, with detailed protocols for fixation and staining to preserve cellular structures.

The study emphasizes the use of advanced imaging techniques, including spinning-disk confocal microscopy, to capture live-cell dynamics and cellular morphology. Specific datasets were generated for various training purposes in image analysis, utilizing platforms like Jupyter Notebooks and Google Colab, with essential Python packages such as Keras and TensorFlow. 

Training datasets for U-Net segmentation included publicly available electron microscopy datasets, while StarDist training involved manual labeling of nuclei in live-cell images. The paper also outlines methodologies for automatic cell tracking using StarDist and Fiji's TrackMate plugin.

Furthermore, it discusses the generation of datasets for Noise2Void and CARE algorithms, highlighting the acquisition of high and low signal-to-noise images for improved image processing. The research also covers training for label-free predictions and Deep-STORM analysis, detailing the preparation of datasets and imaging techniques used to capture cellular components, particularly focusing on mitochondrial markers and actin filaments.

Overall, the paper provides a comprehensive overview of the cell culture methods, imaging techniques, and data generation protocols used to advance cellular imaging and analysis in biological research.
