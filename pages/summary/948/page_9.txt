The paper discusses the use of image-to-image translation networks, specifically focusing on pix2pix and CycleGAN, for converting fluorescence images from one label to another in migrating DCIS.COM cells. Pix2pix requires paired 2D images for training, demonstrated by converting actin images to nuclear images. In contrast, CycleGAN can learn the relationships between different image domains without paired examples, which is illustrated by its ability to predict microtubule images from spinning-disk confocal (SDC) images processed with super-resolution radial fluctuations (SRRF) and structured illumination microscopy (SIM). The paper includes quality control metrics for the models, indicating the effectiveness of both methods in translating images while highlighting the flexibility of CycleGAN in handling unpaired datasets. Overall, the study showcases the capabilities of these networks in enhancing image analysis in biological research.
