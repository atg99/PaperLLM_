The paper details the methodology for training models for super-resolution imaging and cell tracking using various imaging techniques and datasets. The study employs binning techniques to create datasets with 14,975 frames (BIN4) and 5,990 frames (BIN10) from simulated images of a 64 × 64 pixel field-of-view, matching experimental parameters. Drift correction was performed using ThunderSTORM software, with DNA-PAINT imaging conducted on U2OS cells labeled with anti-β-tubulin antibodies and excited with a 647-nm laser.

For the YOLOv2 dataset, live-cell movies of breast cancer cells (MDA-MB-231) were captured, and images were annotated using an online platform for object detection training. CycleGAN training involved U2OS cells fixed and stained with anti-tubulin antibodies, with images acquired via a spinning disk microscope. The fluctuation-based super-resolution dataset was generated from SDC images processed with NanoJ-SRRF, while the SIM dataset was obtained using a DeltaVision OMX system.

The pix2pix dataset consisted of DCIS.COM lifeact-RFP cells imaged live over 14 hours. The authors note that all experiments were conducted once, focusing on algorithm performance rather than biological variability. Ethical compliance for animal research was confirmed, and datasets along with code are publicly available through Zenodo and GitHub, facilitating reproducibility and further research. 

The paper concludes by providing a table of simulation parameters for training data generation, which includes details on field of view size, pixel size, read-out noise, emitter density, and other relevant metrics for the datasets utilized.
