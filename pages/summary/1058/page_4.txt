This paper provides a comprehensive overview of techniques and operations used to enhance explainability in machine learning, particularly within natural language processing (NLP). The document categorizes these techniques into local and global explainability, each further divided into post-hoc and self-explanatory methods. 

Local post-hoc techniques include feature importance methods like first derivative saliency and example-driven saliency, with notable contributions from various studies, such as those by Wallace et al. and Ross et al. Surrogate model approaches also fall under this category, employing techniques like layer-wise relevance propagation and input perturbations. 

Local self-explanatory methods highlight feature importance through attention mechanisms and explainability-aware architectures, with significant research from Mullenbach et al. and Ghaeini et al. Induction methods, such as rule induction and provenance representation, are also discussed. 

Global post-hoc methods include techniques like class activation mapping and taxonomy induction, while global self-explanatory approaches focus on reinforcement learning for declarative representations. 

The paper emphasizes the prevalence of feature importance and surrogate model-based approaches in the literature, accounting for a majority of the reviewed papers. The complexity of NLP systems often leads them to utilize multiple explainability techniques simultaneously. 

Finally, the paper discusses operations that enable explainability, such as first-derivative saliency, which estimates input contributions by calculating output derivatives concerning inputs, linking it to the established concept of sensitivity.
