The paper discusses the importance of explainability in NLP, particularly in the context of MathQA tasks where step-by-step explanations are critical for understanding how answers are derived. It highlights the work of Amini et al. and Ling et al., noting that while Amini et al. focuses on the sequence of mathematical operations, it lacks comprehensive context from the input text. Conversely, Ling et al. provide a richer explanation by integrating textual thought processes with mathematical formulas.

The authors argue that the effectiveness of explanations depends on the techniques used and the target audience, emphasizing that higher coverage in explanations can be beneficial but may not always be necessary for all users. The paper also reviews recent advances in explainable AI (XAI) in NLP, categorizing explanations into local vs. global and self-explaining vs. post-hoc types, and discusses various techniques for generating explanations.

Future research directions include the need for clearer terminology in explainability, improvements in evaluation processes and metrics for human evaluation, and a deeper exploration of fidelity in explanations. The paper notes a surprising scarcity of global explanations in the literature, suggesting that the focus has been primarily on black box models. It posits that white box models, while less emphasized, could serve as valuable resources for developing and evaluating explanation techniques. The authors advocate for a more balanced approach in the field, encouraging research that bridges existing gaps in XAI for NLP.
