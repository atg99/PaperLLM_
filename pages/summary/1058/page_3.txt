Counterfactuals. This technique involves presenting alternative scenarios that illustrate how changes in input data would lead to different predictions. Counterfactual explanations provide insight into the decision boundaries of models, making it easier for users to understand the factors influencing a specific prediction.

The paper categorizes explanations into local and global types, as well as self-explaining and post-hoc categories, highlighting their respective methodologies. Local explanations focus on individual predictions, utilizing either the model itself or additional operations after the prediction is made. Global explanations address the overall reasoning of the model, either through direct interpretability or additional operations for clarification.

Furthermore, the paper emphasizes the importance of both the derivation and presentation of explanations. The derivation involves mathematical justifications produced by AI researchers, while the presentation focuses on how these justifications are communicated to end users, often utilizing various visualization techniques.

In summary, the paper outlines key aspects of explainability in machine learning, including various techniques for deriving explanations and the importance of effective presentation to enhance user understanding. It identifies five major techniques—feature importance, surrogate models, example-driven approaches, provenance-based explanations, and counterfactuals—each with unique methodologies for generating insights into model predictions.
