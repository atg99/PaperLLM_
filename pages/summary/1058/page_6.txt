Saliency heatmaps and highlighting are prevalent in the literature, indicating their dominance in explaining model behavior across AI domains like computer vision and speech. Raw declarative representations, which include logic rules and reasoning trees, are designed for advanced users familiar with these formats. 

Natural language explanations aim to make model outputs comprehensible to a broader audience, utilizing deep learning models or template-based approaches to convert technical outputs into human-readable language. This adaptability enhances accessibility and understanding for lay users.

The paper also addresses the quality of explanations in explainable artificial intelligence (XAI). It emphasizes that a model's effectiveness should be assessed not only by its accuracy but also by the clarity of its explanations. Currently, there is a lack of consensus on evaluation methods, with many studies either lacking standardized evaluations or employing informal methods. A few have utilized formal evaluations, including ground truth data and human assessments, highlighting the ongoing challenges in establishing robust metrics for explanation quality.
