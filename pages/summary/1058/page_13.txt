The paper presents a survey focused on recent advancements in Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI) within the Natural Language Processing (NLP) field, specifically from 2013 to 2019. The authors aimed to highlight significant contributions rather than compile an exhaustive list of all XAI-related works. They identified relevant papers from major NLP conferences, such as ACL, NAACL, EMNLP, and COLING, filtering for titles that included keywords associated with explainability or interpretability. This method yielded 107 papers, which were then reviewed to ensure their alignment with the survey's focus on explainability as a means of understanding model outcomes.

After a thorough review process, which involved multiple reviewers to confirm the appropriateness of each paper, 50 papers were selected for inclusion in the survey. The analysis revealed the top three NLP topics represented in these papers: Question Answering, Computational Social Science & Social Media, and Syntax (Tagging, Chunking & Parsing). The leading conferences where these papers were published were EMNLP, ACL, and NAACL.

The methodology emphasizes the importance of keywords in assessing the relevance of papers to the XAI domain, positing that authors who prioritize explainability are likely to reflect this in their titles. This approach underscores the growth of interest and research in interpretability within the NLP community.
