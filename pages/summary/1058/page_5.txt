This paper discusses various techniques for interpreting neural network models, particularly in the context of natural language processing (NLP). It highlights several methods for feature importance explainability, including:

1. **First-Derivative Saliency**: This method utilizes auto-differentiation to compute the importance of features, particularly effective for word/token-level features.

2. **Layer-Wise Relevance Propagation**: This technique attributes relevance to features in intermediate layers of neural networks, applicable to various layer types, and has been used for both feature importance and example-driven explainability.

3. **Input Perturbations**: Introduced by LIME, this approach generates random perturbations of input data to train interpretable surrogate models, typically linear ones.

4. **Attention Mechanisms**: Attention layers enhance model explainability by indicating which parts of the input the model focuses on, though their effectiveness as an explanation tool remains debated.

5. **LSTM Gating Signals**: In recurrent networks like LSTMs, the outputs of gating signals can also provide insights into feature importance, complementing other interpretation methods.

6. **Explainability-Aware Architecture Design**: This approach involves creating neural network architectures that reflect human reasoning processes, facilitating better interpretability and the generation of human-readable solutions.

The paper also notes that various visualization techniques are crucial for conveying explanations effectively. Saliency-based visualizations, such as heatmaps, are highlighted for their user-friendly presentation of feature importance, which aligns well with the techniques used for generating explanations.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the need for diverse methods and visualization strategies to enhance the interpretability of neural network models in NLP applications.
