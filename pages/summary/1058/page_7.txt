The evaluation techniques discussed in this section highlight various methods used to assess the quality of explanations in machine learning models.

The major categories of evaluation techniques include:

1. **Informal Comparison to Human Examination**: This technique involves high-level discussions and assessments of how generated explanations align with human intuition. It can involve examining explanations in isolation or comparing them against other reference methods, such as LIME.

2. **Ground Truth Evaluation**: In this approach, generated explanations are compared to established ground truth data to measure performance. Metrics like precision, recall, F1 score, perplexity, and BLEU are commonly used. However, care must be taken to ensure the quality of ground truth data and to account for varying valid explanations through multiple annotators and inter-annotator agreement metrics.

3. **Human Evaluation**: This method directly involves human judgment to assess the quality of explanations. It offers the advantage of recognizing that there may not be a singular best explanation. Variability in the number of evaluators and the tasks they perform, such as rating single explanations or comparing multiple techniques, is noted.

4. **Operation-Specific Techniques**: With the prevalence of attention mechanisms in natural language processing, specific evaluation techniques have emerged, such as counterfactuals or erasure tests. These tests evaluate whether altering attention weights affects model predictions, thus assessing the explanatory power of attention layers.

The paper emphasizes that explanations are multi-faceted and can be evaluated on various aspects, including fidelity and comprehensibility. Understanding what is being evaluated is essential for interpreting results. While many studies do not specify their evaluation targets, some have outlined clear goals for explanations, such as revealing model behavior and justifying predictions.

Overall, the evaluation of explainability approaches is complex and requires careful consideration of both the techniques employed and the aspects of explanations being assessed. The paper encourages further exploration of these dimensions to enhance the understanding and quality of explanations in machine learning.
