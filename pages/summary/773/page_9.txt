The paper discusses the effectiveness of PestNet in multi-class pest detection, particularly in comparison to other methods like Faster RCNN and SSD. PestNet utilizes various backbone CNNs, achieving higher precision and recall, which in turn reduces false positive and misdetection rates. The analysis reveals that certain classes, such as class #2, are more challenging to detect, as indicated by the distance of their precision-recall (PR) curves from the ideal point (1,1). Notably, PestNet demonstrates superior performance on class #16, achieving a high integral of the PR curve.

Figures illustrate the feature maps generated by PestNet and the mean PR curves for various classes, highlighting the comparative performance of PestNet against other architectures. The results in Table 2 show that SSD underperforms with a mean Average Precision (mAP) of 51.34% and 62.88% across 16 pest categories, attributed to its limitations in feature extraction. In contrast, PestNet outperforms Faster RCNN by approximately 9.19% in mAP, with significant improvements also noted when using deeper networks like ResNet50 and ResNet101.

The paper emphasizes that the advantages of PestNet stem from its ability to effectively leverage channel and spatial information through its architecture, which enhances feature learning. Interestingly, while PestNet with ResNet101 achieves the highest detection precision at 75.46% mAP, the performance difference between it and ResNet50 is minimal, suggesting that increased depth may have diminishing returns when sufficient depth is already present. The results also highlight disparities among classes, with pest #8 being notably difficult to detect due to its smaller size compared to the more easily localized pest #15.
