This module enhances the feature maps by assigning weights to different channels based on their importance, allowing the model to focus on the most relevant features for pest detection.

The PestNet architecture comprises three main stages: pest feature extraction, pest region search, and pest prediction. Initially, images are processed through a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) to extract feature maps. The CNN consists of convolutional layers, activation functions, and pooling layers, facilitating automatic feature extraction without the need for hand-crafted features. The convolutional layers utilize filters to transform the input images into abstract representations, while the ReLU activation function ensures non-linearity and promotes faster training. Max-pooling layers are employed to reduce feature dimensions and achieve spatial translational invariance.

Following feature extraction, the model integrates a Region Proposal Network (RPN) and a Pest Spatial Sampling Module (PSSM) to identify pest regions and provide predictions. During the prediction phase, Contextual Regions of Interest (RoIs) are utilized to incorporate contextual information, thereby improving detection accuracy.

The dataset used for training and testing consists of various pest classes, with detailed statistics on the number of images and objects identified in each class. The comprehensive nature of this dataset, along with the sophisticated architecture of PestNet, aims to enhance pest detection efficiency and accuracy, ultimately contributing to better pest management strategies.
