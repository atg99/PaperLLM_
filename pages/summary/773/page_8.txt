The study emphasizes the importance of utilizing a larger dataset through data augmentation to enhance the model's ability to learn invariant features, effectively addressing the overfitting issue. 

The experiments involved training four CNN architectures, specifically ZF, VGG16, ResNet50, and ResNet101, integrated with a CSA module for feature extraction. The evaluation of the models was primarily based on mean Average Precision (mAP), calculated from the area under the Precision-Recall curve, which is critical for assessing object detection accuracy.

Training loss analysis indicated that the optimal training iteration was set at 120k, as this point marked the convergence of the model with deep architectures exhibiting lower and more stable training losses compared to shallow networks. Feature visualization further illustrated the learning capacity of PestNet, showing that deeper layers effectively filter out background noise and enhance the detection of pests.

Overall, the results indicate that PestNet is capable of progressively learning and distinguishing pests more effectively than traditional methods, underscoring the advantages of deep learning approaches in pest detection tasks.
