The paper discusses the evaluation metrics used in machine learning and deep learning for malware detection, specifically focusing on precision, recall, and F1 score. Precision measures the accuracy of positive predictions, while recall assesses the fraction of actual positives correctly identified. The F1 score serves as a balance between precision and recall, indicating the classifier's overall performance.

The results compare the proposed DenseNet-based malware detection method against various machine learning techniques (KNN, LR, NB, SVM, DT, RF, and Adaboost) and deep learning models (CNN and its variants). The proposed model demonstrates superior performance across three datasets: achieving an accuracy of 98.23% for Malimg, 98.46% for BIG 2015, and 98.21% for MaleVis. The results highlight that the proposed model outperforms both traditional machine learning and advanced deep learning methods, as evidenced by the metrics presented in Tables 2 and 3.
