The paper discusses the implementation and evaluation of a proposed malware detection system using deep learning techniques. The dataset was split into 70% for training and 30% for validation, utilizing a total of 10,343 cleanware samples and various malware datasets. The training and testing samples were distributed across three datasets: Malimg, BIG 2015, and MaleVis, with a significant number of samples used for each.

The experiments were conducted on a Linux system with specified hardware, and the model was trained with hyperparameters set at 100 epochs, a learning rate of 0.0001, and a batch size of 32. Notably, image dimensions of 64x64 yielded better predictive accuracy compared to 32x32.

To assess the model's performance, four classification metrics were defined: True Positive (TP), True Negative (TN), False Positive (FP), and False Negative (FN). These metrics were organized in a confusion matrix to evaluate the classification results effectively. The paper defines how to calculate each metric and introduces additional performance measures including Accuracy (Acc), Precision (Pr), Recall (Re), and F1 score. Accuracy is specifically calculated as the ratio of correct predictions to total predictions.

Overall, the study demonstrates the effectiveness of the proposed deep neural network model in malware detection, highlighting the significance of input image size and the importance of a comprehensive evaluation framework.
