The paper evaluates the generalization ability of a proposed DenseNet model for malware detection using an unseen dataset, the Malicia dataset, which contains different classes from the trained malware datasets. The model achieved an accuracy of 89.48% on the Malicia dataset, which is lower than the performance of various machine learning (ML) and deep learning (DL) methods on their respective trained datasets. A comparison table (Table 4) highlights the performance of several ML and DL models, with the proposed method demonstrating superior accuracy and performance metrics.

In terms of computational efficiency, the proposed model also exhibits reduced training and testing times compared to other deep learning-based malware detection systems, as shown in Table 5. Specifically, the proposed model requires significantly less time for both training (1941 seconds) and testing (4.36 seconds) when compared to other models.

Further comparisons (Table 6) illustrate that the proposed model outperforms existing detection methods across four malware datasets, achieving an overall accuracy of 98.23%. This performance is slightly lower than the highest recorded accuracy of 98.65% by Roseline et al. on the Malimg dataset but surpasses the results of other methods on the BIG 2015, MaleVis, and Malicia datasets. The findings suggest that the proposed DenseNet-based approach is both effective and efficient for malware detection.
