The paper presents a DenseNet-based malware detection model evaluated on three datasets: Malimg, BIG2015, and MaleVis. The results indicate high training and test accuracy along with low loss across all datasets, as depicted in the training and test accuracy figures. Confusion matrices demonstrate that the model correctly classifies the majority of samples, with particularly high accuracy evident in the Malimg dataset, which contains 26 classes. The diagonal elements of the confusion matrices reflect the number of correctly classified samples, while off-diagonal elements indicate misclassifications. 

Furthermore, the Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) curves for each dataset illustrate the model's performance, showing a high True Positive Rate (TPR) and a low False Positive Rate (FPR) across all classes. The area under the ROC curves for the Malimg and BIG2015 datasets is greater than that of the MaleVis dataset, highlighting the superior efficiency of the proposed model. Overall, the results affirm the model's effectiveness in malware detection.
