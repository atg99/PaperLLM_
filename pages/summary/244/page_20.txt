The proposed malware detection system demonstrates robust performance across all tested datasets, with precision and recall metrics also indicating strong results. The precision values are 0.9743 for Malimg, 0.9756 for BIG2015, and 0.9650 for MaleVis, while the recall values are 0.9750, 0.9748, and 0.9681 respectively. The F-scores, which reflect the balance between precision and recall, are similarly high across the datasets, with scores of 0.9746, 0.9752, and 0.9665.

Despite the system's effectiveness in detecting known malware, it faces challenges with zero-day malware attacks, which are previously unseen and may evade detection. In such cases, the model's performance could decline if these attacks become prevalent. However, the model can be retrained with new samples to adapt to evolving malware tactics, ensuring ongoing effectiveness in malware detection. Overall, the DenseNet-based model exhibits a promising capability for malware classification, showcasing its potential for real-world applications in cybersecurity.
