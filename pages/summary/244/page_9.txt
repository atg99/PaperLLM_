The paper discusses the implementation of the Adaptive Learning Rate Optimization Algorithm, known as Adam, for updating weights in a neural network trained on malware data. Adam determines individual learning rates for parameters by evaluating the first and second moments of the gradient, utilizing moving averages based on the current mini-batch gradient. The equations for the moving averages are provided, indicating the influence of decay rates on these calculations.

The paper also describes the use of cross-entropy loss (CE) and its variations for assessing classification efficiency. The CE loss increases when the predicted probability diverges from the actual class label, with the formula provided. Categorical Cross-Entropy (CCE) loss is highlighted as a combination of the softmax activation function and CE loss for multiclass classification. Additionally, Class Balanced Cross-Entropy (CBCE) loss is introduced to address class imbalance in training samples.

For experimental evaluation, the model was tested on four malware datasets: Malimg, Microsoft’s BIG 2015, MaleVis, and Malicia, with the latter used for testing. The experiments included 1043 cleanware samples, and the Malimg dataset contained 9339 malicious samples categorized into 25 malware classes. The paper emphasizes the variety and number of malware samples across different datasets to validate the proposed malware detection method.
