The paper presents a model designed for malware classification using a convolutional neural network (CNN). The model architecture includes an initial convolutional layer, a max-pooling layer, followed by four Dense Convolution (Dense Conv) blocks and four transition layers combining 1x1 convolutions and 2x2 average pooling. Each Dense Conv block consists of 1x1 and 3x3 convolutional layers, with repetitions of these convolutions being 6, 12, 48, and 32 times across the blocks. After processing through these layers, the output feature maps are passed to a Global Average Pooling (GAP) layer, which is then followed by a fully connected (FC) layer that classifies the malware samples into distinct classes.

In terms of preprocessing, the Portable Executable (PE) binary files are converted into a one-dimensional vector of 8-bit unsigned integers, representing pixel intensity levels. These values are then organized into a two-dimensional array, creating grayscale images for further analysis. The paper highlights the importance of consistent image sizes for CNN input, as varying resolutions can disrupt the classification process.
