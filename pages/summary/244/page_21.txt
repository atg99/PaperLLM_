The paper presents an advanced malware detection and classification method that integrates malware visualization with a pretrained DenseNet model, employing a reweighted categorical cross-entropy loss criterion. The proposed approach was tested on four malware datasets, demonstrating superior classification accuracy compared to other models. Specifically, it achieved accuracies of 98.23% on the Malimg dataset, 98.46% on the BIG 2015 dataset, and 98.21% on the MaleVis dataset. The model also successfully identified 89.48% accuracy on an unseen dataset, showcasing its effectiveness against obfuscated malware samples without requiring execution or unpacking of packed executables.

The results indicate that the proposed technique can effectively classify malware samples even in the presence of an imbalanced training set, achieving high accuracy and performance comparable to traditional machine learning solutions while eliminating the need for manual feature engineering. Future work will focus on reducing false negatives for optimal detection.
