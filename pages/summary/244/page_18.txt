The paper discusses a proposed malware detection system that demonstrates effective performance across multiple datasets, including the MaleVis dataset. The results, presented in Table 7, indicate high accuracy rates, with the Malimg, BIG2015, and MaleVis datasets achieving accuracy percentages of 97.55, 97.72, and 96.81, respectively. The precision and recall metrics further reinforce the model's efficacy, highlighting values close to 0.97 for most datasets.

The model's generalization property enables it to detect new malware samples that resemble previously encountered families with similar accuracy. However, the paper acknowledges a limitation in detecting completely unseen malware, referred to as zero-day attacks. In instances where such attacks accumulate, the model's performance may decline. To address this, the system can be retrained with new malware samples, effectively updating its training set to adapt to evolving malware threats and anti-malware evasion techniques. This retraining process is crucial for maintaining the model's effectiveness over time.
