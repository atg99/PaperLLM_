The paper discusses the methodology employed in training a convolutional neural network (CNN) for classifying COVID-19 images. It begins with an overview of the distribution of patients in the COVIDx dataset, emphasizing the importance of data representation in training and testing phases.

Data augmentation techniques were applied to increase the size of the training dataset. Transformations included vertical flips, random rotations up to 15 degrees, and alterations in lighting conditions, which collectively enhance model generalization. Importantly, only training data underwent augmentation.

For the network architecture, the authors utilized ResNet50, a deep residual network known for its efficiency and performance in classification tasks. This architecture allows for flexibility in image sizes during training, which is crucial for effective learning. The network, termed COVID-ResNet, incorporates pretrained weights from the ImageNet dataset for better initialization.

Training followed a progressive resizing approach, initially using smaller images and gradually increasing their size. This method leverages the independence of features learned by different CNN layers from image size. The training was organized into three stages, each with distinct image dimensions and learning rate strategies, including the Cyclical Learning Rate technique to optimize learning rates dynamically.

In the first stage, images were resized to 128x128 pixels, with a two-step tuning process for the newly added model head and the entire network. The second stage involved fine-tuning with 224x224 pixel images, while the final stage focused on 299x299 pixel images and employed discriminative learning rates for various layers.

The authors argue that this multi-stage training with progressive resizing promotes better generalization and stability in weight updates as training progresses, showcasing the effectiveness of transfer learning across different image sizes.
