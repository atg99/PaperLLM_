This paper builds upon the research presented in a previous study, focusing on the development of open-source techniques to combat the COVID-19 pandemic. It utilizes the COVIDx dataset, which includes chest radiography images classified into four categories: Normal, Bacterial pneumonia, Viral pneumonia (non-COVID-19), and COVID-19. The authors of the original study reported an overall accuracy of 83.5%, with the highest positive predictive value for Normal cases (95.1%) and the lowest for non-COVID-19 pneumonia (67.0%).

The current work aims to enhance the model's performance by achieving the following objectives: improving overall accuracy for all classes with positive predictive values above 90, using a neural network architecture that is less parameter-intensive and computationally efficient, and employing training techniques that reduce the number of required epochs for faster training. 

State-of-the-art methodologies are applied, with an emphasis on the benefits of human intervention during training to improve model performance and reduce training time. Techniques such as progressive image resizing, automatic learning rate finding, and discriminative learning rates are utilized to attain superior results.

The paper is structured as follows: Section II details the methods, including information on the COVIDx dataset and the proposed architecture; Section III presents the results; Section IV provides a discussion; and Section V concludes the findings. The COVIDx dataset comprises 5941 chest radiography images from 2839 patients, with a notable imbalance in class distribution, particularly fewer COVID-19 cases compared to other categories.
