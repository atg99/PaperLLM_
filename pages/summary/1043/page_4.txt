Local Interpretable Model-agnostic Explanations (LIME) is a technique designed to elucidate predictions made by complex models, such as deep neural networks. LIME operates by creating a simpler, interpretable surrogate model that approximates the target model's predictions in the local vicinity of the input of interest. This process involves generating samples around the input, evaluating them with the complex model, and fitting a linear function to explain the model's behavior. While LIME has been successfully applied across various domains, its effectiveness hinges on the quality of the surrogate model, which can lead to high computational costs and introduce uncertainty, resulting in non-deterministic explanations.

Anchors, another model-agnostic explanation method, seeks to provide clarity for individual predictions by establishing decision rules that "anchor" the prediction. These rules are expressed as IF-THEN statements that fix the classification within defined regions of the feature space, ensuring consistent predictions regardless of changes to other feature values. Effective anchors are characterized by high precision, indicating that a large proportion of data points within the rule’s region share the same class, and high coverage, which reflects the number of data points the rule applies to. Anchors are generated using reinforcement learning techniques combined with a modified beam search algorithm, allowing them to be applicable to any prediction model without requiring insights into the model's internal workings.
