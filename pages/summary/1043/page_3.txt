The paper provides an overview of explainable AI (XAI) methods, highlighting the evolution and development of various explanatory techniques for complex machine learning models. It emphasizes early methods like Layer-wise Relevance Propagation (LRP) and Local Interpretable Model-agnostic Explanations (LIME) as foundational approaches. The chronology of method development is illustrated, indicating a progression from model-centric analyses to approaches that leverage structural information and variable relationships.

Additionally, the paper mentions important tools and datasets that enhance the evaluation and comparison of XAI methods, such as Quantus, which offers a comprehensive suite of evaluation metrics, and CLEVR-XAI, a benchmark dataset for assessing neural network explanations. The flexibility and applicability of model-agnostic methods, particularly LIME, are underscored, as they do not rely on internal model details, making them versatile in practice. The paper acknowledges earlier techniques like gradient-based sensitivity analysis but notes their limitations, thereby justifying the focus on more recent methods.
