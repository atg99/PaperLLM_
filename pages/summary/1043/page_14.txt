The paper discusses the use of SHAP values, which are grounded in game theory, to interpret model predictions by attributing contributions from individual variables. A key advantage of SHAP is its ability to assign zero attribution to features that do not influence predictions, thereby adhering to important axioms. However, a significant limitation is the high computational complexity when applied to modern models like deep neural networks, making exact computation impractical. There are model-specific implementations for tree-based methods, but estimation versions such as KernelSHAP can be slow.

The paper also highlights the potential pitfalls of SHAP, particularly in cases of feature dependence that can lead to extrapolation in low-data regions. While SHAP provides non-sparse explanations by attributing non-zero values to all influencing features, counterfactual explanations may offer more succinct interpretations when sparsity is desired.

Furthermore, the paper introduces Asymmetric Shapley Values (ASV), which address the symmetry limitation of SHAP values. ASV allows for the incorporation of causal relationships between variables, enabling a redistribution of attribution where source variables receive greater credit for their influence on dependent variables and model predictions. This method calculates ASV by considering only variable orders consistent with the causal graph, resulting in a more accurate reflection of the causal effects in the model.
