The paper discusses the application and implications of Layer-wise Relevance Propagation (LRP) and Deep Taylor Decomposition (DTD) in neural network interpretation. LRP has been effectively utilized across various domains, although it requires careful adaptation of redistribution rules for new model architectures. Existing literature outlines recommended rules for popular layer types. Beyond mere visualization, LRP explanations can enhance models by discovering artifacts in image datasets and improving model performance through loss augmentation or training data adaptation. Additionally, LRP can identify relevant elements of a neural network, enabling optimal pruning and quantization to enhance coding efficiency and computational speed.

The DTD method is introduced as a propagation-based explanation technique that utilizes Taylor expansion to explain neural network decisions. It redistributes output values to input variables in a layer-by-layer manner, closely related to LRP. The relevance of a neuron at a given layer is modeled as a function of lower-layer activations, with the process iteratively redistributing relevance back to the input layer. The DTD framework supports approximations at intermediate layers, allowing for a structured understanding of how relevance is assigned throughout the network.
