The paper discusses Integrated Gradients, a technique for explaining deep neural networks and other differentiable models. It highlights its theoretical soundness based on sensitivity and implementation invariance, while also noting its computational efficiency and reliance on gradient information at selected alpha points. However, it identifies three main drawbacks: the need for a baseline observation which influences attributions, its limitation to differentiable models, and the potential issues with gradient integration paths that may not align with data topology. Extensions to address these drawbacks are proposed.

Additionally, the paper addresses causal models, specifically referencing the work of Madumal et al. on structural causal models within reinforcement learning (RL). These models utilize counterfactuals to analyze potential outcomes of different actions taken by the RL agent, aiming to maximize long-term rewards. The explanations generated focus on minimally complete causal chains, omitting some intermediate nodes to meet explanation satisfaction criteria. This approach allows users to understand the reasoning behind the agent's decisions, offering insights into both chosen and unchosen actions. The explanations are designed to be simple, ethical, and personalized, marking a significant advancement towards causal explanations in model-free reinforcement learning.
