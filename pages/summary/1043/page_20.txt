The paper discusses the development of the EXplainable Neural-Symbolic Learning (X-NeSyL) method, which integrates symbolic knowledge from domain experts into neural network explanations. X-NeSyL aims to enhance the interpretability of neural network outputs by aligning feature importance assignments with human-like classification methods. Utilizing state-of-the-art convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and SHAP values, the method constructs a knowledge graph (KG) that reflects constraints and relations derived from feature importances. This graph is compared with a KG provided by human experts, and a designated loss function is employed to minimize discrepancies, thereby improving both explainability and performance.

The approach is characterized as explainability-by-design, ensuring interpretability at every stage of training. However, it relies heavily on domain-specific knowledge, which can be challenging to obtain and may lead to conflicting perspectives among experts. The study evaluates the method across various datasets and CNN architectures to assess its effectiveness in incorporating human insight into AI processes.

Looking forward, the paper anticipates advancements in explainable AI (xAI) that will focus on capturing causal dependencies and measuring the quality of explanations. Future xAI models will likely necessitate new human-AI interfaces to facilitate contextual understanding and allow domain experts to engage with the AI through counterfactual questioning. This human-in-the-loop approach aims to enhance AI processes by integrating human experience and conceptual knowledge.
