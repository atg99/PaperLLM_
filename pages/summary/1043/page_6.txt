The paper discusses two methods for explaining predictions in Graph Neural Networks (GNNs): GraphLIME and Layer-wise Relevance Propagation (LRP). 

GraphLIME extends the LIME method by using HSIC estimation to learn correlations between features and prediction distributions. It was compared with GNNExplainer based on three criteria: detection of useless features, assessment of prediction trustworthiness, and identification of superior GNN classifiers. GraphLIME outperformed GNNExplainer significantly in the latter two, indicating that models with fewer untrustworthy features tend to have better classification performance. Additionally, GraphLIME is noted for its computational efficiency and its application in analyzing backdoor attacks on GNNs by revealing relevant node features. Future work could involve identifying important graph substructures and comparisons with other methods like PGExplainer and GNN-LRP.

LRP, on the other hand, is a propagation-based method that requires access to a model’s internal structure. It explains predictions by iteratively redistributing relevance from the output back to the input layers, allowing for a more efficient explanation process. This approach is characterized by a layer-by-layer decomposition of the total evidence contributing to a prediction, thus simplifying the explanation problem compared to model-agnostic methods. 

Both methods enhance the interpretability of GNNs, addressing the need for transparency in machine learning models.
