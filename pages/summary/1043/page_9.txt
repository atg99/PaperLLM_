This paper provides an overview of various explainable artificial intelligence (xAI) methods, focusing on Layer-wise Relevance Propagation (LRP) and Prediction Difference Analysis (PDA). 

LRP involves redistributing relevance scores across different layers of a neural network, depending on the type of layer (e.g., convolutional or fully connected). The method uses root points to guide this redistribution and can be adapted to different model architectures, although applying it to novel architectures may be challenging.

PDA, introduced by Zintgraf et al. at the 2017 ICLR conference, estimates the relevance of input features by analyzing changes in predictions when certain features are made unknown. It generates a relevance vector that reflects the importance of each feature based on the difference in prediction probabilities. A significant change indicates a feature's importance in classification, while negligible changes suggest lesser relevance. Positive values in the relevance vector imply that a feature supports the predicted class, whereas negative values indicate that the feature detracts from the classification confidence.

Both methods aim to enhance the interpretability of neural networks, which is crucial for their application in sensitive fields such as medicine. The paper emphasizes the need for effective visualization techniques to foster trust and understanding in black-box classifiers.
