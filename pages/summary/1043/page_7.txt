The paper provides an overview of Layer-wise Relevance Propagation (LRP), an explainable artificial intelligence (XAI) method that assigns relevance scores to input elements and neurons in neural networks. R(0) denotes the relevance of the ith input element, while R(l)j represents the relevance of the jth neuron at the lth layer. LRP conservatively redistributes relevance, ensuring that no relevance is added or lost, akin to energy conservation principles. This allows for signed explanations, where positive relevance indicates supporting evidence for predictions, and negative relevance points to evidence against them.

The theoretical foundation of LRP is based on the Deep Taylor Decomposition (DTD), which differentiates it from heuristic-based XAI techniques. Initially developed for convolutional neural networks and bag-of-words models, LRP has been extended to various architectures, including LSTM, GNN, and Transformer models. The "neuralization trick" further enables the application of LRP to classical machine learning algorithms like k-means clustering and one-class SVM.

Meta-analysis methods, such as spectral relevance analysis (SpRAy), have been introduced to systematically cluster and analyze LRP explanations, aiding in the detection of dataset artifacts and model biases. The Zennit toolbox and CoRelAy toolbox offer implementations of LRP and other analysis methods in Python, while the iNNvestigate library provides a common interface for various XAI methods.

LRP is recognized for its computational efficiency, requiring only one backward pass, and its strong theoretical basis, making it a reliable explanation method. Its widespread application across domains such as computer vision, natural language processing, and EEG analysis underscores its significance in the field of XAI.
