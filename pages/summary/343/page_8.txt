In their paper, Floridi and Chiriatti examine the limitations of GPT-3 through three specific tests focused on mathematical, semantic, and ethical reasoning. They highlight that while GPT-3 can provide correct answers to basic mathematical problems, it struggles with more complex calculations, demonstrating a lack of true understanding and reliance on statistical patterns. For instance, when asked to solve simple equations, GPT-3 can produce accurate results, but its performance declines with larger numbers, indicating potential confusion for users who might misuse the tool for mathematical tasks. 

The authors also address the ineffectiveness of GPT-3 in passing the Turing Test, emphasizing that its responses lack genuine comprehension of semantics and context, as it operates solely on syntactic patterns. This inability to distinguish between human and artificial intelligence can lead to misunderstandings and significant implications. Overall, the paper underscores the need for caution when interpreting the outputs of AI systems like GPT-3, as they do not possess true intelligence or understanding.
