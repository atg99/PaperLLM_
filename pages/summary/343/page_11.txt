The paper discusses the transformative impact of GPT-3 on the writing process, highlighting its potential to significantly reduce the workload for writers. It notes that industries like journalism are already utilizing automated systems to produce real-time content, leading to job losses in traditional writing roles. However, the paper argues that writers will need to adapt by developing new skills to effectively use tools like GPT-3, emphasizing the importance of crafting prompts and intelligently collating generated results.

The author suggests that while GPT-3 can assist in completing texts or creating new content, human intelligence remains crucial in the writing process. Readers may find themselves unable to distinguish between human and AI-generated content, potentially benefiting from improved quality in written materials. However, there is concern that AI may also inherit and perpetuate human linguistic flaws.

The paper posits that future literary works may be categorized based on their authorship—whether solely human, collaborative with AI, or entirely AI-generated. This shift may prompt a reevaluation of literary awards and copyright regulations, as demonstrated by the recognition of AI-generated music by organizations like SACEM. Overall, the paper outlines the profound implications of AI on writing, creativity, and authorship.
