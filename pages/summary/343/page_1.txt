In this commentary, the authors Luciano Floridi and Massimo Chiriatti examine the distinctions between reversible and irreversible questions, which can help identify the sources of their answers. They introduce GPT-3, a third-generation autoregressive language model known for generating human-like text through deep learning. The commentary applies the reversible and irreversible question framework to analyze GPT-3, presenting three tests based on mathematical, semantic (including the Turing Test), and ethical questions, ultimately concluding that GPT-3 is not designed to pass any of these tests.

The authors emphasize that GPT-3's capabilities should not be misconstrued as evidence of the emergence of general artificial intelligence; such interpretations are seen as misinformed. The discussion also highlights the implications of mass production of semantic artifacts through automation, urging caution in how AI advancements are perceived and applied. The commentary underscores the limitations of GPT-3 and calls for a more nuanced understanding of its nature and potential consequences in the field of artificial intelligence.
