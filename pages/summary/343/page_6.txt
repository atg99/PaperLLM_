In this paper, the authors explore the intersection of artificial intelligence and creative expression, specifically through the lens of literary generation. They reference a poetic excerpt to illustrate the emotional depth and aesthetic qualities of human artistry, contrasting it with the capabilities of machines. Turing's assertion from 1950 is invoked, emphasizing that true equivalence between machine and human creativity hinges on a machine's ability to produce art not merely through random arrangement of symbols, but as a result of genuine thoughts and emotions. The authors present an example of text generated by GPT-3 in response to a literary prompt, highlighting the complexities and nuances involved in machine-generated creativity. This discussion raises critical questions about the nature of creativity and the potential limitations of AI in replicating the human experience of art.
