The paper by L. Floridi and M. Chiriatti discusses the implications of widely available automated writing tools like GPT-3 on content production and society. As these tools become common, the volume of text generated will increase significantly, leading to a proliferation of low-quality content, often referred to as "semantic garbage." This phenomenon will be exacerbated by online advertising strategies that leverage automated text generation for clickbait and targeted marketing.

The authors highlight the potential negative consequences, including the spread of fake news and disinformation, as automated systems can produce credible-looking false narratives. This could intensify opinion polarization and create "filter bubbles," as content becomes tailored to individual preferences and cognitive abilities. The paper warns that individuals may increasingly rely on automated text generators for information, similar to how they currently use search engines for existential queries.

Conversely, the authors note that advancements in AI, like GPT-3, could enhance intelligence and analytics systems, improving consumer-business relationships through sophisticated chatbots and knowledge management systems. To address the challenges posed by automation, the authors argue for a need for critical thinking and a reassessment of business models, particularly those reliant on advertising. They advocate for clearer distinctions in content production, akin to marked restorations of historical artifacts, and suggest the need for new accountability mechanisms in the digital landscape. Ultimately, the paper calls for a better digital culture to prepare current and future citizens for navigating the complexities of the evolving infosphere.
