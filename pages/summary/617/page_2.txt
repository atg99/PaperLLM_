The paper discusses the significance of artificial neural networks (ANNs) in modeling non-linear relationships across various scientific and technical fields. Among different ANN architectures, Radial Basis Function Neural Networks (RBFNNs) are highlighted for their effectiveness in non-linear system modeling due to their universal approximation capabilities. The challenges associated with traditional learning methods, particularly error back-propagation (BP), are noted, including issues with global search efficiency and convergence rates. 

To address these challenges, alternative methods such as Recursive Least Squares (RLS) and adaptive growth and pruning techniques are explored. The use of evolutionary algorithms (EAs), particularly Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO), is examined for enhancing RBFNN performance, demonstrating improvements in convergence speed and solution optimization. 

The proposed hybrid PSO combined with a spiral-shaped mechanism (HPSO-SSM) aims to refine RBFNN design by balancing exploration and exploitation in parameter optimization while leveraging existing optimal solution data to navigate the search space effectively. This approach is expected to significantly advance the modeling and prediction capabilities of RBFNNs in complex non-linear systems.
