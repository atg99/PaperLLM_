The paper discusses the development and evaluation of the HPSO-SSM-RBFNN algorithm for predicting total phosphorus (TP) values in wastewater treatment plants (WWTP). The algorithm demonstrated rapid convergence and achieved a stable error rate of approximately 0.0029, indicating its effectiveness in approximating desired accuracy.

The model was trained using various input variables such as pH, dissolved oxygen concentration, temperature, total suspended solids, and influent TP, with training and testing datasets obtained from a WWTP in Beijing. After data cleaning, 250 instances were used for training and 150 for testing. Results showed that the HPSO-SSM-RBFNN algorithm provided accurate one-step-ahead predictions, with errors confined within the range of -0.03 to 0.03, indicating superior performance compared to other models.

In evaluating TP prognosis, the algorithm used eight hidden neurons, achieving the smallest root mean square error (RMSE) during training. Comparison of prediction values revealed that HPSO-SSM-RBFNN outperformed existing algorithms, yielding the lowest testing RMSE of 0.103 for TP prediction, thus confirming its efficacy in handling wastewater treatment challenges. The findings illustrate the algorithm's robustness in modeling complex systems and its potential for real-time application in wastewater management.
