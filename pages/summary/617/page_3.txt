The paper presents a novel hybrid optimization algorithm, HPSO-SSM, designed to enhance the performance of Radial Basis Function Neural Networks (RBFNNs) by optimizing their parameters and size. The study addresses the limitations of traditional Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO), such as slow convergence and local minima, by incorporating three significant improvements: 

1. A logistic map sequence is utilized to adjust the inertial weight, promoting diversity in the search process and helping avoid suboptimal solutions.
2. An improved update equation is proposed to better integrate exploration and exploitation in generating new positions.
3. A spiral-shaped mechanism (SSM) is introduced as a local search strategy to refine the search around known optimal solutions.

The effectiveness of the HPSO-SSM algorithm is demonstrated through its application to three non-linear benchmark functions: the Lorenz time-series, non-linear system identification, and total phosphorus modeling in wastewater treatment processes. The results show that RBFNNs trained with HPSO-SSM exhibit lower root mean squared error (RMSE) and superior solution precision compared to other RBFNN models.

The paper is structured into sections detailing the principles of RBFNN, the implementation of PSO and HPSO-SSM, and an evaluation of various optimization techniques against benchmark datasets. Additionally, the study introduces a master feature vector (MFV) strategy to address issues related to data inconsistencies, missing values, and class imbalance, ultimately improving predictive performance.

The primary aim of this research is to enhance data quality and algorithm performance in machine learning applications by providing innovative methods for addressing common challenges in neural network training.
