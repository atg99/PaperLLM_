The paper presents a novel 1xN pruning pattern aimed at enhancing the efficiency of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) while maintaining model accuracy and achieving significant speedups on general CPUs. The 1xN pattern groups consecutive N output kernels with the same input channel index into blocks, which are then pruned if deemed unimportant. This approach allows for a more effective pruning granularity compared to traditional methods.

The authors outline a workflow for filter rearrangement that optimizes the weight matrix in the output channel dimension to identify influential blocks, followed by a similar rearrangement for the next-layer weights to preserve the integrity of convolutional operations. This method facilitates output computation through a parallelized block-wise vectorized operation, leading to notable performance improvements.

Experimental results, particularly on the ILSVRC-2012 dataset, demonstrate the efficacy of the proposed pruning pattern. For instance, with a pruning rate of 50% and N set to 4, the method achieves a 3.0% increase in top-1 accuracy for MobileNet-V2 when compared to filter pruning, alongside a reduction in inference time on a Cortex-A7 CPU.

The study situates the 1xN pruning pattern within the broader context of network pruning techniques, contrasting it with weight and filter pruning, which have limitations in speed and efficiency, particularly on CPU platforms. The authors emphasize the importance of their approach for practical deployment of CNNs in resource-constrained environments. The project is publicly available for further exploration.
