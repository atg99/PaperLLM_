The paper discusses a novel network pruning technique termed 1-N pruning, aimed at enhancing both accuracy and computational efficiency for mobile and embedded devices. Traditional pruning methods, including weight and filter pruning, often face challenges in maintaining performance under high sparsity rates. The authors highlight that while weight pruning can significantly reduce parameters without sacrificing accuracy, filter pruning tends to degrade performance when applied aggressively.

The proposed 1-N pruning method offers a middle ground between fine-grained weight pruning and coarse-grained filter pruning. It focuses on removing consecutive output kernels with smaller L1 norms, which are considered less important. The process follows a three-step pipeline: training the network, pruning the kernels, and fine-tuning the resulting sparse model. A key innovation is the filter rearrangement workflow, which organizes weights based on their importance to improve accuracy.

Experimental results demonstrate that the 1-N pruning outperforms both filter and weight pruning, providing notable accuracy improvements and inference speedups. For instance, with a 50% pruning rate and N=4, the method achieved a 3.0% increase in top-1 accuracy on MobileNet-V2 while also saving significant inference time on a Cortex-A7 CPU.

The contributions of the paper include the introduction of the 1-N pruning pattern, the filter rearrangement workflow for enhanced accuracy, and the successful simultaneous maintenance of high accuracy and CPU acceleration. The paper also reviews related work in traditional network pruning methods, setting the stage for the proposed advancements. The authors conclude by discussing limitations and future research directions for their 1-N pruning approach.
