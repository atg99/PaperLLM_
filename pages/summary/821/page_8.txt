This paper presents a novel 1xN pruning technique aimed at enhancing the efficiency of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) while maintaining their accuracy. The authors compare their method with traditional weight pruning and filter pruning, demonstrating that their approach significantly reduces latency without compromising performance. Specifically, they highlight that weight pruning does not effectively utilize hardware capabilities, leading to minimal speed improvements, while filter pruning, although faster, results in notable performance degradation. In contrast, the 1xN pruning achieves substantial latency reductions, such as a 56.04 ms inference time savings on a Cortex-A7 CPU at a 50% pruning rate, while preserving comparable accuracy.

The paper discusses several limitations of their method, including the potential retention of lower-magnitude kernels due to their filter rearrangement strategy, which relies on l1 norms. Additionally, while initially designed for CNNs, the method shows limited effectiveness for recurrent neural networks (RNNs). The authors also acknowledge the lack of comparison with various studies that explore similar intermediate pruning strategies and the absence of a new pruning criterion, as they utilize existing measures to assess kernel importance.

In conclusion, the authors assert that their 1xN pruning pattern offers a promising solution for achieving both model accuracy and significant speedups across general CPU platforms, as evidenced by experiments on MobileNets and ResNet-50. They plan to further investigate the limitations and potential generalization of their method in future work.
