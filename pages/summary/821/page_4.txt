The paper discusses a novel approach to network pruning called Block Pruning, which focuses on preserving the most influential kernels in convolutional neural networks to enhance accuracy while maintaining sparsity. The method involves a three-step process: training the network, pruning kernels with smaller `1 norms, and fine-tuning the resulting sparse network.

A key innovation of this approach is the filter rearrangement workflow. This process rearranges the weight matrix in the output channel dimension based on the `1 norm of each filter, allowing for the preservation of kernels with higher magnitudes during the pruning phase. The rearrangement helps mitigate the loss of important filters that would otherwise be discarded. Once the weight matrix is rearranged, the 1:N pruning is applied, leading to a more effective pruning outcome.

The authors emphasize that their method of filter rearrangement differs from previous channel permutation techniques. While both aim to preserve high-magnitude values, their approach focuses on maintaining the positions of filters rather than individual weights, using the `1 norm of each filter as a basis for rearrangement.

The effectiveness of this method is validated through experiments on MobileNet-V2, demonstrating improvements in accuracy. The paper also addresses the computational efficiency of encoding and decoding the input and output tensors, ensuring that the proposed method does not incur additional runtime costs during inference.

Overall, the paper presents a structured approach to filter rearrangement and kernel pruning that enhances model performance while maintaining operational efficiency.
