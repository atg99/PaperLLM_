The paper discusses various Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) variants for motion planning in robotics, specifically focusing on their effectiveness in navigating obstacle-dense environments. The PSO methods evaluated include Fix-PSO (with Fixed Inertia Weight and Acceleration Coefficients), Rand-PSO (with Random Inertia Weight and Acceleration Coefficients), TVAC-PSO (with Linearly Decreasing Inertia Weight and Time Varying Acceleration Coefficient), LDIWPSO (with Linearly Decreasing Inertia Weight and Fixed Acceleration Coefficients), and CPSO (Constricted Particle Swarm Optimization). These methods were selected based on their successful applications in robotics as reported in prior research.

The study involves two sets of experiments that assess the feasibility of these motion planning methods in complex scenarios. The experiments simulate a robot navigating from a starting point to a destination in a maze-like environment filled with obstacles. Two layout types are used, resembling maze and indoor navigation scenarios. Notably, classical and meta-heuristic approaches, except for the Dynamic A* (DA) method, operate without prior knowledge of obstacle locations or environmental layouts.

Additionally, the paper includes pseudo code for various classical path planning algorithms (such as Potential Field, Dijkstra's Algorithm, Rapidly exploring Random Tree, and Probabilistic Road Map) and meta-heuristic algorithms (including Genetic Algorithm, Differential Evolution Algorithm, Particle Swarm Optimization, and Cuckoo Search Algorithm) for local path planning, which are provided in the supplementary section. The first experiment focuses on a maze layout characterized by a complex arrangement of obstacles that challenge the robot's navigation.
