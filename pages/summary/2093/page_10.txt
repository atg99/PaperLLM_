The paper discusses the Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) algorithm, focusing on its velocity update equations and parameter adjustment mechanisms. The velocity of a particle at iteration t is determined by its previous velocity, cognitive component, and social component, as expressed in the equation Vi;j(t) = wVi;j(t-1) + Ci;j + Si;j. Here, w is the inertia weight, c1 and c2 are acceleration coefficients for cognitive and social influences, and r1;j and r2;j are random values.

The performance of PSO is significantly influenced by how its parameters are adjusted and controlled. Various mechanisms for tuning these parameters have been proposed since the algorithm's inception in 1995. The paper examines several of these methods, including Fixed Inertia Weight (FIW), Linearly Decreasing Inertia Weight (LDIW), Time Varying Acceleration Coefficient (TVAC), Random Inertia Weight (RANDIW), Random Acceleration Coefficients (RANDAC), and Fixed Acceleration Coefficients (FAC).

Equations 2 and 3 provide formulations for LDIW and TVAC, detailing how inertia weights and acceleration coefficients can be adjusted over iterations. The LDIW adjusts the inertia weight linearly from an initial value to a final value over the course of iterations, while TVAC modifies cognitive and social coefficients similarly based on the current iteration and predefined initial and final values. The study aims to evaluate these parameter adjustment methods to enhance PSO's overall optimization performance.
