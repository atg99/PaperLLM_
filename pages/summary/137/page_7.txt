The paper discusses the Kullback-Leibler divergence (DKL) between the variational posterior distribution q(z|xi) and the prior p(z). The derivation includes mathematical expressions showing how DKL can be rewritten in terms of expectations and variances of the distributions. 

The key equation presented is:

DKL(q(z|xi) || p(z)) = log(q) - 1/2 * (σ²_q + (μ_q - μ_p)²)

This equation is evaluated under specific conditions, leading to a simplified form when parameters σ_p and μ_p are set to 1 and 0, respectively. The result shows that DKL can be expressed in terms of the variances and the log of the variance ratio.

The paper further connects this result to the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO), indicating that the contribution of a data point xi towards maximizing the objective function is represented by:

1/2 * (1 + log(σ²_j) - σ²_j + (μ_j - μ_p)²) + E_q[log p(xi|z)]

For a batch of data, the objective function G is formulated to include contributions from all dimensions of the latent vector z and multiple stochastic samples, highlighting the importance of the re-parametrization trick in variational inference.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the relationship between variational inference, KL divergence, and the maximization of the ELBO in the context of probabilistic modeling.
