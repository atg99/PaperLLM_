The Variational Autoencoder (VAE) framework is discussed, highlighting its components and the mathematical formulation of the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence between the approximate posterior distribution and the true posterior. The decoder of the VAE is represented by a neural network, denoted as pθ(x|z), where z is the latent variable sampled from a distribution parameterized by the encoder.

The KL divergence is expressed mathematically, showing its dependence on Bayes' theorem. The equation is broken down using logarithmic laws, leading to a clearer understanding of the components involved. The derivation demonstrates how the KL divergence can be separated into two integrals: one related to the log of the decoder's output and the prior distribution, and the other involving the log of the observed data.

Notably, the second integral simplifies due to the properties of probability distributions, resulting in a final expression that relates the log probability of the observed data to the expected value of the log of the decoder's output and the prior distribution, providing insights into the learning objectives of the VAE.
