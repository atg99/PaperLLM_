The Kullback-Leibler divergence is termed a 'divergence' rather than a 'metric' due to its lack of symmetry, which is a requirement for metrics. Recent research has introduced various symmetrization techniques for the KL divergence that enhance its generative fidelity. The KL divergence is inherently non-negative, as demonstrated by the integral formulation, which shows that DKL(q(x) || p(x)) is always greater than or equal to zero. This non-negativity stems from the properties of the logarithm function, confirming that the divergence measures the difference between two probability distributions without yielding negative values.

In the context of variational autoencoders (VAEs), the encoder generates an approximate posterior distribution q(z|x), which is parameterized by a neural network. This formulation allows for effective applications, such as the detailed characterization of diseases. The integration of these concepts highlights the significance of the KL divergence in variational inference and its role in enhancing the performance of generative models like VAEs.
