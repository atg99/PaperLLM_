In this tutorial, Stephen G. Odaibo presents a detailed derivation of the loss function for the standard Variational Autoencoder (VAE), emphasizing its reliance on variational inference to manage computational challenges in Bayesian machine learning. The tutorial focuses on maximizing the evidence lower bound on the log likelihood of data during training, particularly under the assumption of a Gaussian latent prior and Gaussian approximate posterior. This specific scenario allows for a straightforward closed-form solution for the Kullback-Leibler divergence term within the variational lower bound.

The paper begins with a foundational explanation of Bayes' theorem, which serves as the cornerstone for updating beliefs in light of new evidence. It articulates the relationship between the hypothesis and data probabilities, illustrating the theorem's derivation from the principles of conditional probability. The tutorial provides a comprehensive overview of the necessary concepts, including a thorough explanation of Bayes' theorem and the joint probability framework, which are essential for understanding the derivation of the VAE loss function. Overall, the tutorial aims to equip readers with the theoretical tools needed to grasp the mechanics behind VAEs and their loss function derivation.
