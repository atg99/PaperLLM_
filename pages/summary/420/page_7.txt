The paper presents quantitative and qualitative results of an object detection method in various scenarios, focusing on its performance across different vehicle categories including cars, trucks, buses, and bicycles. The results are evaluated using mean Average Precision (mAP) metrics in both Source and Unsupervised Domain Adaptation (UDA) settings. The proposed method shows improvements over existing approaches like ADDA and DTf+FT, particularly in the ability to detect smaller objects and provide tighter bounding boxes.

Qualitative analysis reveals that the adapted model enhances localization accuracy and confidence in detecting challenging objects, outperforming the source model in identifying small instances. An ablation study examines the impact of pairing mechanisms in the split pooling module and the number of sample images and annotated bounding boxes, confirming that effective pairing significantly boosts adaptation performance.

The study also highlights the benefits of parameter sharing among discriminators across different scales, leading to superior results compared to independent discriminator architectures. Overall, the findings underscore the importance of effective adaptation techniques in improving object detection performance in diverse scenarios.
