The paper presents a quantitative analysis of a proposed method for car detection across various scenarios, focusing on few-shot domain adaptation (FDA) and unsupervised domain adaptation (UDA) settings. The results demonstrate the effectiveness of the method in improving average precision (AP) scores compared to baseline models.

In the FDA setting, the approach utilizes scaled image-level adaptation modules, which lead to significant improvements. Combining these modules yields a 2.7 AP gain on average. Additionally, an object instance-level adaptation component contributes a 3.1 AP improvement. The combination of image-level and instance-level adaptations results in a further enhancement of 1.6 AP over instance-level only and 2.0 AP over image-level only, showcasing the complementary benefits of these adaptations. While fine-tuning with limited target samples offers minor improvements, it is orthogonal to the adversarial adaptation modules. Overall, the proposed method achieves a 5.2 AP boost over the raw source model, outperforming state-of-the-art methods in the UDA setting.

In the UDA setting, with a larger number of target images, the proposed split pooling adaptation significantly outperforms other methods, showing a 6.4 AP gain over the baseline. The results across various scenarios consistently indicate that the proposed method excels in both FDA and UDA settings, achieving state-of-the-art performance through effective domain shift mitigation.

The study highlights the limitations of baseline methods, which fail to effectively capture the domain shift with limited target data. The results suggest that the proposed method is robust and effective in various scenarios, reinforcing its potential application in real-world car detection tasks.
