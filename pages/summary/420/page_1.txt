In the paper "Few-shot Adaptive Faster R-CNN," the authors address the problem of performance degradation in object detection models due to domain shifts when adapting to new environments with limited target domain images and annotations. They identify key challenges: insufficient target data, the complexity of simultaneous localization and classification in object detection, and risks of over-adaptation leading to degraded performance.

To tackle these issues, the authors propose a novel framework called Few-shot Adaptive Faster R-CNN (FAFRCNN). This framework incorporates a pairing mechanism that aligns source and target features to mitigate the lack of target samples. The adaptation is executed through a bi-level module: the image-level adaptation module employs split pooling to extract and align local patch features at varying scales and aspect ratios, while the instance-level adaptation module semantically aligns object features to avoid confusion between classes. Additionally, a source model feature regularization (SMFR) is applied to stabilize the adaptation process.

The authors demonstrate the efficacy of FAFRCNN through experiments on multiple datasets, achieving state-of-the-art performance in both few-shot domain adaptation (FDA) and unsupervised domain adaptation (UDA) settings. Their approach shows that it is feasible to adapt object detectors to new domains using only a few annotated samples, reflecting key domain characteristics without the noise from larger datasets.
