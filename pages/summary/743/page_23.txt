The paper discusses the Single Timescale Actor-Critic Linear Quadratic Regulator (LQR) and focuses on the stationary distribution of the state variables and control actions. It begins with the characterization of the stationary distribution for the state variable \( x \) and control \( u \), establishing that \( x \) converges to a normal distribution as \( N \) approaches infinity, given certain conditions on the system matrices. The derivation shows that the joint distribution of the state and control actions can be expressed as a multivariate normal distribution with specified covariance matrices.

Key results include the relationship between the covariance matrices \( D(N_0) \) and \( D \) as well as the bounds on the difference between the expected values of the functions evaluated at the two distributions. The paper further explores the implications of these distributions on performance metrics by evaluating integrals that represent the expected costs associated with the control strategies.

The analysis indicates that the difference in expected costs between the two distributions decreases geometrically as \( N_0 \) increases, which is beneficial for understanding the convergence properties of the actor-critic framework. The paper concludes with bounding the integrals involved, demonstrating that the differences in determinants of the covariance matrices remain manageable, ensuring the stability and effectiveness of the proposed LQR approach in the context of actor-critic methods.
