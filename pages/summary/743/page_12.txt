In this section of the paper by Zhou and Lu, the authors analyze the critic part of their algorithm, establishing several lemmas that support their findings. 

Lemma 1 indicates that under Assumption 1, the matrices DKt, PKt, and Kt are bounded between specific constants, which depend on various parameters such as A, B, D, Q, and R. The lemma also notes that the inequality holds when DKt is replaced by DK*.

Lemma 2 discusses the strong convexity of the critic loss, stating that the function LK(θ) is strongly convex under certain conditions. The authors emphasize that the use of a stochastic policy is crucial for ensuring this strong convexity, aiding in the robustness of their approach.

Lemma 3 quantifies the accuracy of the critic gradient sampling. It shows that the sampled gradient drLt(θt) approximates the true gradient rLt(θt) within a specified error bound, which is crucial for the effectiveness of the algorithm. The results depend on small values of ε and provide bounds on the Frobenius norm of the sampled gradient.

Remark 4 outlines the implications of applying this lemma later in the analysis, particularly focusing on the relationship between step sizes and the error bounds established.

Finally, Lemma 4 presents a Lipschitz property relating the actor parameters K and K0, showing that the difference in the critic parameters is bounded by a constant multiple of the difference in actor parameters, given certain conditions on the parameters.

Together, these lemmas provide a foundational understanding of the critic's behavior in the algorithm, leading to conclusions about the improvement gained through the critic update mechanism.
