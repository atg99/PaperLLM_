This paper presents a single timescale actor-critic algorithm for solving the Linear Quadratic Regulator (LQR) problem, achieving a sample complexity of O(ε^(-1) log(ε^(-1))^2), which is an improvement over existing methods. The authors contrast their work with previous studies, such as Zeng et al. (2021), which utilized a two timescale approach with a sample complexity of O(ε^(-3/2)) under strong convexity assumptions on the higher-level loss function. Additionally, they reference works by Dean et al. (2020) and Mania et al. (2019), which explored model-based solutions to LQR with sample complexities of O(ε^(-2)).

The paper outlines the theoretical foundations of the LQR problem, including the discrete-time Markov process formulation and the goal of minimizing an infinite horizon cost functional. The optimal control law is established as linear in the state, derived from the solution to the Riccati equation, which is accessible when the model parameters are known. However, the authors focus on a model-free setting, employing a stochastic policy parameterized by a normal distribution.

The structure of the paper is organized into sections: Section 2 provides theoretical background on LQR, Section 3 discusses the algorithm and parameter choices, and Section 4 outlines the convergence proof, with detailed proofs and numerical examples relegated to the appendix. This organization reinforces the clarity and rigor of the proposed algorithm in addressing the LQR problem.
