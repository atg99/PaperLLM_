In the paper by Zhou and Lu, the authors discuss the role of exploration in reinforcement learning and its impact on optimal policy parameters. They establish that while the addition of exploration does not alter the optimal policy parameters, it aids convergence in algorithm performance. The authors introduce a state trajectory equation that incorporates exploration, leading to a modified state process with a stationary distribution described by a Lyapunov equation. 

They denote the spectral radius of a matrix and assert that for stability, the condition that the spectral radius of (A - BK) must be less than one is essential. The paper further defines the state-action value function (Q function) and the state value function, which are critical for reinforcement learning. These functions are governed by the Bellman equation, enabling the assessment of expected costs under specific policies.

Additionally, the authors define a matrix PK that satisfies a matrix-valued equation, interpreting it as the second-order adjoint state, which serves as the shadow price for the system. The properties of PK are highlighted, underscoring its significance in the analysis presented in the paper. The proofs for these properties are provided in the appendix, reinforcing the theoretical foundation of their findings.
