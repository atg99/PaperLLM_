The paper presents a theoretical analysis of a single timescale actor-critic algorithm for Linear Quadratic Regulator (LQR) problems. It begins with Lemma 5, which establishes conditions under which the expected squared error between subsequent actor parameters converges, given a small step size and certain assumptions. This convergence is influenced by the cost functional associated with the actor's performance, as highlighted in Lemma 6. It shows that the cost functional is approximately quadratic in the gradient of the actor parameters.

Further, Lemma 7 discusses improvements in the actor update process, indicating that the update yields a reduction in the cost functional when specific conditions are met. This lemma extends previous results by incorporating stochastic estimates of the critic, which introduces additional terms in the improvement analysis.

The paper concludes with numerical examples that validate the theoretical findings. Two specific LQR problems are analyzed, detailing the matrices involved for different dimensions, demonstrating the practical applicability of the developed theoretical framework. The code used for these examples is made available for further exploration.
