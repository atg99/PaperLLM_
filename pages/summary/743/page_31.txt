The paper discusses a Single Timescale Actor-Critic approach within Linear Quadratic Regulator (LQR) frameworks. It establishes inequalities relating to the Lyapunov function, which is crucial for ensuring stability in control systems. The analysis is structured around three main cases that consider different conditions on the expected values of the state and cost functions.

In the first case, when the expected distance between the current and target policies is less than a threshold, a contraction rate for the Lyapunov function is derived, indicating that the expected loss at the next time step is bounded by a function of the current expected loss. This leads to a contraction form that implies stability over time.

In the second case, when the expected difference between the cost function values is below a certain threshold, similar conclusions about the contraction of the Lyapunov function are drawn. Both cases indicate that the expected loss diminishes over time, reinforcing the stability of the system.

The third case considers simultaneous conditions where both expected distances are below their respective thresholds. The analysis shows that the expected loss remains bounded and converges to a stable point.

Overall, the findings provide a mathematical foundation for the stability and convergence properties of the actor-critic methods in the context of LQR, emphasizing the importance of managing expected values of policy differences and cost functions to achieve optimal control outcomes.
