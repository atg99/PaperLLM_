The paper discusses the derivation of upper and lower bounds in the context of a single timescale actor-critic linear quadratic regulator (LQR) framework. It begins by establishing a general formula for the difference in performance measures, J(K) - J(K0), which does not depend on the optimality of the feedback gain K0. This formula is critical for later proofs and is expressed in terms of the trace of matrix products involving the gain matrices and system dynamics.

The authors set K0 to a specific form that satisfies the equality condition, allowing them to leverage the optimality of the feedback gain K* and derive further inequalities. They show that the performance difference can be bounded using the minimum eigenvalue of certain matrices, leading to a relationship that incorporates terms related to the system's dynamics and control inputs.

In the proof of Lemma 7, the authors analyze the change in the performance measure from time step t to t+1, utilizing trace inequalities and properties of the matrices involved. They confirm that the relationship holds under certain conditions on the matrices D and P, which are linked to the system's state and control dynamics.

Overall, the paper provides a rigorous mathematical foundation for understanding the behavior of actor-critic methods in LQR settings, emphasizing the importance of the chosen feedback gains and their impact on system performance.
