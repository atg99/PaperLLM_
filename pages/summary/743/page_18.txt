The paper discusses the expected extra cost associated with initiating an action within a specified policy framework. The function QK(x;u) represents this expected cost, defined through a combination of cost functions and a policy's value function. The key components of this function include the cost incurred from the state-action pair, the value function under the policy at the initial state, and several trace terms related to matrices representing system dynamics and costs.

The analysis also extends to the Fisher information matrix, which quantifies the amount of information that an observable random variable carries about unknown parameters. The paper derives this matrix based on the distribution of the policy, which is assumed to follow a normal distribution characterized by specific parameters. The derivation involves integrating over the state space and leveraging properties of the normal distribution, ultimately leading to a formulation that incorporates the dynamics of the system and the cost structure.

In summary, the paper provides a detailed mathematical framework for assessing the expected costs and information dynamics in a controlled system, emphasizing the interplay between the chosen actions, state transitions, and the underlying statistical properties of the policy.
