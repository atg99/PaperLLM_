In the paper by Zhou and Lu, the authors present a single timescale actor-critic algorithm aimed at solving Linear Quadratic Regulator (LQR) problems. They establish a convergence theorem (Theorem 1) that specifies the conditions under which their algorithm achieves desired accuracy in terms of sample complexity and terminal error.

The initial Lyapunov function is defined as \( L_0 = O(1) \), and a positive constant \( c_4 = O(1) \) is introduced, which helps in determining the one-step decay ratio of the Lyapunov function. The parameters for the algorithm are set to \( N = O(1) \), \( N_0 = O(\log(1/\epsilon)) \), and \( N_1 = O(1) \), ensuring that the sample of the critic gradient meets accuracy requirements. The notation \( \beta_t = O(1) \) signifies the single timescale nature of the algorithm, where the actor and critic are interdependent, complicating the analysis.

The algorithm is summarized in Algorithm 1, which iteratively updates the critic and actor parameters over \( T \) training steps. The main result indicates that under certain assumptions, the algorithm achieves a sample complexity of \( O(1/\epsilon \log(1/\epsilon)^2) \), with the terminal error bounded by \( E[\| \theta_T - \theta^* \|_F] \leq \epsilon \) and \( E[J(K_T) - J(K^*)] \leq \epsilon \).

The authors note that this complexity is nearly optimal, especially when compared to stochastic gradient descent algorithms, which require \( O(\epsilon^{-1}) \) samples for achieving an \( \epsilon \)-optimal solution. They also suggest that additional assumptions, such as strong convexity of the cost function, may be needed for tighter error estimates.

Finally, a sketch of the proof for Theorem 1 is provided, with further details and supporting lemmas to be included in the appendix. The numerical examples presented in the paper corroborate the theoretical convergence rate established.
