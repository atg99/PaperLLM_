The paper presents a framework for the single timescale actor-critic Linear Quadratic Regulator (LQR) problem. It establishes two key propositions regarding the policy defined by a specific matrix K when the condition ρ(A-BK) < 1 holds true.

Proposition 1 introduces the explicit expressions for the cost function J(K) and its gradient with respect to K. The cost function is expressed as J(K) = Tr(DKPK) + ϵ²Tr(R), while the gradient is given by rKJ(K) = 2h(R + B>PKB)K - B>PKAi DK. The remark highlights that if DK is positive definite, the critical point for J(K) where rKJ(K) = 0 leads to K being defined by the formula K = (R + B>PKB)⁻¹B>PKA, which aligns with the Riccati equation.

Proposition 2 provides the explicit expressions for value functions VK(x) and QK(x,u). VK(x) is expressed as x>PKx - Tr(DKPK), and QK(x,u) includes terms involving state x and control u, along with the Riccati-related expressions. The dynamics of the process are reformulated in terms of a concatenated vector z consisting of state and control variables, leading to a simplified representation of the system dynamics.

The paper also discusses the conditions for ergodicity, stating that the dynamics are ergodic if ρ(A-BK) = ρ(E) < 1. The stationary distribution for the concatenated variable z is characterized by a normal distribution, with the corresponding covariance structure provided.

Overall, the paper effectively outlines the mathematical foundations and implications of the single timescale actor-critic LQR approach, emphasizing the relationships between the policy, cost function, value functions, and system dynamics.
