The paper discusses a method for improving the sample complexity and efficiency of reinforcement learning algorithms, specifically focusing on a linear state-action setting. The one-step sample complexity is noted as O(N0N1N), with the proposed Linear State-Action Dynamic Programming (LSTD) algorithm being similar to the TD(0) algorithm but utilizing multiple trajectories.

Key components of the method include the critic update mechanism, where the critic parameter at step t is updated using the gradient of the loss function. The update rule is defined as:

θ_{t+1} = θ_{t} - β_{t} drL_{t}(θ_{t}),

where β_{t} is the step size.

For the actor part of the algorithm, ideas from existing literature are leveraged, particularly those from Fazel et al. (2018), Yang et al. (2019), and Zeng et al. (2021). The policy improvement is framed as a gradient ascent problem, with the gradient represented as:

G_{K} = (R + B^{T} P_{K} B)K - B^{T} P_{K} A.

A common variant of the policy gradient is utilized, specifically the natural policy gradient method, which incorporates the inverse Fisher information matrix for gradient preconditioning. The Fisher information matrix at each state is defined, and the average Fisher information matrix is computed.

The steepest descent direction of the performance measure J(K) is derived, leading to a simplified expression for the actor update:

K_{t+1} = K_{t} - ϵ_{t} bG_{K_{t}},

where bG_{K_{t}} is a modified gradient term that incorporates the true parameters related to the policy.

The algorithm ensures that the updates remain measurable with respect to the filtration generated by the training process, thereby maintaining the integrity of the learning dynamics.
