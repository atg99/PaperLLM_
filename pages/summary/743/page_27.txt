This paper discusses the Single Timescale Actor-Critic Linear Quadratic Regulator (LQR) method, focusing on the mathematical foundations and proofs related to the performance guarantees of the actor and critic components in reinforcement learning. The analysis begins with the application of the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality to derive expectations involving the norms of the difference between current and target policies, resulting in an expression that bounds the expected squared error.

Key inequalities are established, demonstrating how the performance of the policy can be controlled through parameters defined in the context of the learning algorithm. Specifically, the paper shows that the expected difference in policy performance can be expressed in terms of various factors, including the optimal control gain and the properties of the associated matrices.

The proofs for the actor's performance involve deriving upper bounds on the differences between the value functions associated with different policies. The relationship between the control matrices and the expected outcomes is explored, leading to a series representation for the policy's performance.

Overall, the paper provides a rigorous mathematical framework for understanding the convergence and effectiveness of the Single Timescale Actor-Critic LQR method, highlighting the interplay between the actor and critic components in optimizing control policies.
