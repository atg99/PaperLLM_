In this section, Zhou and Lu detail the design of the actor-critic algorithm, emphasizing the use of the Least-Squares Temporal Difference (LSTD) method for policy evaluation (critic) and a natural policy gradient method for policy improvement (actor). They denote the filtration generated during training as Gt and specify that a quantity O(a) is bounded by a constant times a, dependent solely on the problem parameters and not on target accuracy or training trajectory.

For policy evaluation under a fixed policy 𝜋K, the authors define the state-action value function QK, parameterized by 𝜃. The Bellman residual with respect to the critic parameter 𝜃 is expressed as BR𝜃(x; u), which captures the difference between the immediate cost and the expected future rewards. They introduce a feature matrix 𝜑(x; u) and parametrize the Q function accordingly. The loss function for the critic is defined as the expectation of the squared Bellman residual, LK(𝜃), which aims to minimize the discrepancy between the estimated and actual values.

The authors note that the scalar parameter 𝜃0 does not influence the training process, thus focusing on 𝜃 as the primary critic parameter. They highlight that the unique minimizer of the loss function is the true value function, reinforcing the theoretical foundation of their approach.
