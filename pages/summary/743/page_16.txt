In this paper, Zhou and Lu derive the total cost function J(K) and its gradient with respect to K. The total cost function is expressed as a limit involving expected values and traces, ultimately leading to a formula that incorporates terms related to matrices Q, R, and K. 

The authors show that the expression for J(K) can be simplified to include the trace of the product of matrices, specifically involving DK, which is defined as a sum of two components. The gradient of J(K), denoted as rKJ(K), is derived using properties of matrix derivatives, leading to a formula that highlights the relationship between K, R, and the terms involving the matrix B and the state-action matrices A and K.

Key results include the recursive application of matrix derivatives to obtain a closed form for rKTr[DKQ0], demonstrating the relationship between the components of the system. The final expression for the gradient, rKJ(K), reveals the structure of the optimization problem, linking K to the matrices involved and ensuring convergence under specific assumptions about the system parameters. 

Overall, the paper provides a thorough mathematical framework for analyzing the cost function and its sensitivity to changes in control parameters, which is crucial for optimal control and estimation problems.
