The paper by Zhou and Lu focuses on the mathematical properties of certain matrix norms and their relationships, particularly in the context of convex optimization. It establishes that the norm of the difference between two matrices, denoted as K and K0, can be bounded by a polynomially growing constant c1 that depends on other matrix norms and parameters. Specifically, it shows that the Frobenius norm of the difference kK - K0kF can be expressed in terms of constants c, cK, and cP, alongside the norms of related matrices.

The proof provided for Lemma 5 illustrates the derivation of upper bounds on the Frobenius norm of the difference between two iterates, denoted as t and K, incorporating terms that account for the strong convexity of the function Lt. This results in a series of inequalities that cumulatively show how the evolution of the iterates is controlled by the properties of the underlying matrices and their interactions.

The key takeaway from the paper is the establishment of a relationship between these norms, which grows polynomially with the dimensions of the matrices involved, highlighting the interplay between matrix properties and optimization behaviors in the analyzed framework.
