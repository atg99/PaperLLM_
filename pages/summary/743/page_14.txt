In the paper by Zhou and Lu, the authors present a detailed analysis of learning curves for two examples, focusing on the performance of actor-critic algorithms. The study evaluates the errors associated with these algorithms over multiple steps, specifically examining the average errors from ten independent runs while also considering standard deviations.

The learning curves, depicted in Figure 1, illustrate the progression of critic and actor errors as the number of steps increases, with a step size of 0.001. Initially, the error curves show a linear trend, consistent with earlier one-step improvement analyses. However, as the algorithm reaches its capacity, the errors stabilize.

To assess convergence rates, the authors conduct additional tests with varying step sizes, maintaining a constant ratio between step size and total steps. The results, shown in Figure 2, reveal that the final errors follow a linear regression with a slope of approximately -1.0, supporting the theoretical predictions made previously.

Furthermore, the authors evaluate the norm in Assumption 1, with maximum values for various metrics provided for both examples. These findings reinforce the validity of the assumptions made in the study.
