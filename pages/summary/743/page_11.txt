The proof sketch of Theorem 1 demonstrates the convergence of the single timescale actor-critic algorithm in a Linear Quadratic Regulator (LQR) setting. It begins with Lemma 2, which establishes that the critic loss is strongly convex. Following this, Lemma 3 shows that the gradient samples can be obtained with a small bias. 

Using these results, Lemma 5 indicates that the critic error improves with each iteration, quantifying this improvement through an inequality involving the critic's parameter error and the actor's update. The expectation of the actor's performance improvement is formulated in Lemma 7, which provides a bound on the change in the objective function.

To establish convergence, a Lyapunov function is defined as the sum of the critic and actor errors. The proof combines the results from Lemmas 5 and 7 to demonstrate a decay estimate for the Lyapunov function. This decay indicates that if both the critic and actor errors are sufficiently small, the Lyapunov function remains bounded, ultimately leading to convergence.

The crux of the proof lies in bounding the positive terms related to the critic's improvement with the negative terms associated with the actor's improvement, thereby establishing a contraction rate for the Lyapunov function. This approach ensures that both components of the algorithm work synergistically towards convergence. The proof concludes with the anticipation of further analysis on the actor and critic components.
