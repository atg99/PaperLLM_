The paper presents the convergence rates of a single timescale actor-critic algorithm in a linear quadratic regulator (LQR) context, examining two examples with varying numbers of steps from 125 to 4000. The results indicate that both the final critic and actor errors exhibit consistent convergence rates, with slopes approximately equal to -1.0 across different step sizes. The convergence behavior is illustrated through error curves averaged over ten independent runs.

In the appendix, fundamental linear algebra properties are utilized to support the proofs presented in Sections 2 and 3. Specifically, the paper leverages the properties of positive semi-definite matrices and their implications on trace and operator norms. Proposition 1 is proven using the condition that the eigenvalues of (A - BK) are less than one, leading to an expression for the cost in terms of a series expansion. The expected one-step cost is derived, showing how it is influenced by the state and control inputs.

Overall, the findings affirm the effectiveness of the single timescale actor-critic method in achieving convergence in the LQR framework, with rigorous mathematical proofs supporting the theoretical claims.
