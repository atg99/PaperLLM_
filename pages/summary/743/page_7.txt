The paper discusses a method for minimizing a loss function in the context of a Single Timescale Actor-Critic Linear Quadratic Regulator (LQR) framework. It begins by defining the gradient and Hessian of the loss function with respect to parameter \( \theta \). The gradient is expressed as an expected value involving the cost function and a term related to the dynamics of the system, indicating that the loss function exhibits strong convexity.

To minimize the loss, the authors employ a stochastic gradient descent approach, requiring an accurate sample estimate of the gradient for given parameters \( K \) and \( \theta \). The function \( f(x, u) \), which combines the cost and the dynamics, is introduced for simplifying the notation. The sampling process is broken down into three steps, beginning with the use of Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) methods to sample trajectories based on the policy \( \pi_K \).

The first step involves generating independent trajectories, from which unbiased samples are drawn. The second step calculates the mean of these samples to obtain an unbiased estimate for \( f(x, u) \). The authors emphasize that the unbiased samples for covariance and expectation contribute to the overall estimate of the gradient.

Finally, the paper concludes with the formulation of the sample gradient, which averages the estimates obtained from multiple sampled trajectories. The methodology outlined indicates that the approach is not purely online due to the requirement of a certain sample size for accuracy. Overall, the proposed framework aims to enhance the efficiency of the Actor-Critic method in LQR settings.
