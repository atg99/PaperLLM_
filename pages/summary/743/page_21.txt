The paper discusses the single timescale actor-critic Linear Quadratic Regulator (LQR) framework, emphasizing the mathematical foundation behind its performance metrics. It begins by defining key variables and parameters, including symmetric matrices and their relationships through various equations.

The analysis involves calculating expectations of quadratic forms, specifically focusing on the Frobenius norm and operator norms of matrices involved in the system dynamics. The paper derives equations that express the expectations of these norms in terms of traces of matrices, revealing how the interplay between the matrices influences the overall system behavior.

Key results show that the Frobenius norm of certain matrices can be bounded by the operator norm, establishing a polynomial dependence on dimensions. This is crucial for proving stability and performance guarantees in the LQR framework.

The derivations culminate in expressions that relate the expected quadratic forms to the traces of the matrices involved, illustrating the systematic approach to analyzing the actor-critic method in a single timescale setting. The implications of these results are significant for understanding the efficiency and robustness of the actor-critic algorithms in control tasks.
