In this paper, Zhou and Lu introduce a single timescale actor-critic algorithm designed to address the linear quadratic regulator (LQR) problem, which is a fundamental issue in reinforcement learning and optimal control. The proposed method employs a least squares temporal difference (LSTD) approach for the critic and a natural policy gradient method for the actor. The authors provide a proof of convergence for their algorithm, demonstrating a sample complexity of O(ε⁻¹ log(ε⁻¹)²). This convergence proof is also applicable to broader single timescale bilevel optimization challenges.

The introduction outlines the significance of reinforcement learning as a semi-supervised model that maximizes expected rewards through action and environmental interaction. The LQR problem is highlighted as a particularly well-structured case for theoretical analysis and practical applications. The paper contrasts traditional model-based methods, which rely on known dynamics, with more contemporary model-free approaches that utilize observed states and rewards.

The actor-critic framework, which alternates updates between the actor (responsible for policy improvement) and the critic (which evaluates the value function), is also discussed as a key methodology for solving RL and optimal control problems. Overall, the research contributes to the understanding and application of reinforcement learning techniques in the context of LQR, providing both theoretical insights and numerical validation of the proposed algorithm.
