The paper introduces the Action Transformer model, which is designed for recognizing and localizing human actions within video clips. This model leverages a Transformer architecture to aggregate features from the spatiotemporal context surrounding individuals whose actions are being analyzed. The authors highlight that the model learns to track individual people and discern semantic context from the actions of others by utilizing high-resolution, person-specific, class-agnostic queries. The attention mechanism of the model focuses on critical areas such as hands and faces, which are essential for action discrimination, all done without explicit supervision beyond bounding boxes and class labels.

The Action Transformer is trained and tested on the Atomic Visual Actions (AVA) dataset, achieving significant improvements over state-of-the-art methods using only raw RGB frames as input. The paper emphasizes the importance of contextual information in action recognition, noting that understanding a person's action often requires knowledge of the people and objects around them. The proposed architecture combines a spatiotemporal I3D model for feature extraction and a region proposal network for localizing individuals, feeding this information into the Transformer to contextualize actions effectively.

The findings suggest that the Action Transformer can successfully learn to track individuals and contextualize their actions relative to others, demonstrating its capability in recognizing complex human interactions in video content.
