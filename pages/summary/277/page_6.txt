The paper presents a detailed evaluation of the localization performance of different heads for person detection, focusing on the I3D and Action Transformer (Tx) models. The results indicate that the I3D head outperforms the Tx head in localization tasks, achieving a mean Average Precision (mAP) of 92.9% at an Intersection over Union (IoU) threshold of 0.5. The use of high-resolution query transformations significantly enhances the performance of the Tx head, narrowing the gap between the two models.

In the overall performance assessment, the Tx head with high-resolution preprocessing and 300 proposals results in notable improvements, surpassing the I3D head. The combination of the I3D head for regression and the Tx head for classification yields the best results, highlighting their complementary strengths.

The comparison with existing state-of-the-art models reveals that the proposed Tx+I3D head achieves superior performance, exceeding the previous best by over 7.5% on the validation set and 3.5% on the test set, while utilizing only a single model and raw RGB frames as input. This contrasts with prior approaches that often employed multiple modalities and ensembles.

Additionally, an ablation study confirms the significance of class-agnostic regression, data augmentation, and pre-training on the Kinetics dataset, all of which are critical for achieving strong performance in action detection tasks. The findings emphasize the efficacy of the proposed methods and suggest avenues for future research in improving localization accuracy in action recognition systems.
