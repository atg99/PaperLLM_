The paper presents a detailed methodology for a chicken swarm optimization algorithm, which is structured in several steps. The algorithm begins by defining key variables related to the chicken population, including the total number of individuals, roosters, hens, chicks, and hens with offspring. It then determines the maximum number of iterations and the number of subgroups.

In the optimization process, the fitness value of the chicken swarm is calculated, and individual optimization positions are initialized. A hierarchy is established based on fitness values, with random relationships assigned between hens and chickens. The positions of roosters, hens, and chicks are updated through specific formulas incorporating Gaussian distribution and random selection among the swarm.

The fitness value is recalculated after each position update, allowing for potential updates if the new fitness is superior. The iteration count is monitored to determine if the maximum has been reached, prompting either a continuation or termination of the process.

While the chicken swarm optimization algorithm is noted for its fast convergence speed and high efficiency, it is criticized for low solution precision and weak local search capabilities. Its applications span various fields such as image processing, sensor communication, production scheduling, and power systems.

Additionally, the paper compares different swarm intelligence optimization algorithms, highlighting their advantages and drawbacks. The findings indicate that while some algorithms, like the Ant Colony Algorithm (ACA), offer robustness and high accuracy, they also struggle with local optima and slow convergence. Other algorithms, such as Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) and Artificial Fish Swarm Algorithm (AFSA), exhibit their own strengths and weaknesses, particularly regarding search speed and accuracy. Tables summarizing these comparisons provide a clear overview of the performance metrics of each algorithm.
