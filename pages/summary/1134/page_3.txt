This paper discusses the Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) algorithm and the Artificial Fish Swarm (AFS) algorithm, both of which are swarm intelligence optimization techniques. 

The PSO algorithm utilizes an updated formula for particle velocity and position, where the velocity is influenced by individual and global best positions, along with random factors. The key equations are:

1. vk+1_id = vk_id + C1 * randk1 * (pk_id - xk_id) + C2 * randk2 * (pk_gd - xk_id)
2. xk+1_id = xk_id + vk+1_id

The parameters C1 and C2 are non-negative learning factors, and the velocity is capped at a maximum value. While the PSO algorithm is known for its computational simplicity and rapid convergence, it has limitations such as a tendency to get trapped in local minima and low accuracy in discrete problems.

In contrast, the AFS algorithm, proposed by Li et al. in 2002, is inspired by fish behavior, specifically foraging, clustering, and rear-ending. The algorithm operates through two main behaviors:

1. Foraging Behavior: The artificial fish evaluates food concentration in its current state and decides whether to move towards a randomly selected state based on the food concentration.
2. Clustering Behavior: The algorithm assesses the number of visible partners and calculates a central position. If the central position indicates a safe environment, the fish may move towards it; otherwise, it reverts to foraging behavior.

The AFS algorithm emphasizes collaboration among agents and adaptability to their environment, showcasing a different approach to optimization compared to PSO. Overall, both algorithms exhibit unique strengths and weaknesses in solving optimization problems.
