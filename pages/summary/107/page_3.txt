The paper discusses the VERTPOM research project, which aims to create positive energy territories through the development of smart electricity grids. The project focuses on optimizing the balance between energy production and consumption by utilizing algorithms for forecasting and simulating power levels across various distribution systems. It emphasizes the use of artificial intelligence, particularly machine and deep learning, to simulate scenarios that ensure a positive production-consumption balance.

Key objectives include anticipating power requirements based on diverse parameters such as climate, scheduled events, and consumption patterns. This information is intended to guide decisions on energy consumption priorities, balancing renewable and conventional energy sources, optimizing peak consumption, and facilitating intelligent interaction with consumers.

The methodology involves a framework utilizing three neural network models for predicting energy consumption. Data analysis and preprocessing are critical steps, as they significantly impact forecasting accuracy. The study uses average power consumption data from Péronne city, collected via measurement tools at a 30-minute sampling rate, reflecting various consumer types and factors like climate and calendar considerations. The overall goal is to develop digital tools for effective power management and ensure the safety of electrical infrastructures within the smart grid context.
