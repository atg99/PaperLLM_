The results indicate that while all models demonstrate effective forecasting capabilities, the Drop-GRU model outperforms both the GRU and LSTM in terms of accuracy and speed. Specifically, the GRU excels at predicting high consumption values but is slower than Drop-GRU. 

The analysis reveals that as the prediction horizon increases, both the uncertainty in the predictions and execution times rise for all models. The evaluation metrics RMSE and MAE show lower values for the GRU and Drop-GRU compared to LSTM, with correlation values for Drop-GRU often surpassing those of GRU. The findings suggest that the Drop-GRU architecture achieves reliable and precise forecasting results, making it a robust choice for short to medium-term power load forecasting.

In conclusion, the paper highlights the effectiveness of the proposed deep learning models for energy management strategies, with GRU being a preferable option for short-term predictions due to its efficiency and potential for improvement through hybridization techniques.
