the paper discusses the application of advanced forecasting techniques for power consumption, focusing on the use of deep learning models, particularly Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks. The study contrasts traditional forecasting methods like autoregressive integrated moving average (ARIMA) with machine learning approaches such as Decision Trees, Support Vector Machines, and Artificial Neural Networks. It highlights the advantages of LSTM networks over standard Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs), particularly in managing long sequences of data and mitigating issues like gradient vanishing.

The authors propose a dynamic model utilizing LSTM to forecast power consumption, detect peaks, and manage load shedding, aiming to maintain balance in the electrical grid. The paper also introduces additional models, including Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) and Drop-GRU, to enhance prediction accuracy by capturing dynamic changes in consumption data and reducing overfitting through dropout techniques.

The methodology section outlines the framework and implementation of these predictive models, detailing data processing, parameter selection, and evaluation metrics. Experimental results demonstrate the performance of the LSTM model compared to GRU and Drop-GRU, showcasing its effectiveness in reducing error rates.

The context of the research aligns with the development of energy management systems aimed at improving efficiency in public infrastructure and optimizing electrical networks. The paper concludes with an overview of findings and implications for future work.
