This section of the paper discusses the methodologies utilized to enhance the performance of a deep learning model, particularly focusing on dropout regularization, data partitioning, and performance evaluation metrics.

The authors highlight the importance of dropout as a regularization technique to combat overfitting in deep learning neural networks. By randomly deactivating a proportion of hidden units during training, dropout reduces the complexity of the network, promoting better generalization of learned features.

The dataset is divided into three segments: training, validation, and testing. The training set, comprising 80% of the total data, is employed to adjust model parameters and optimize forecasting. The validation set helps tune hyperparameters and avoid overfitting, while the remaining 20% serves as the test set for model evaluation. The authors detail their experimentation with various train/test splits and the rationale behind selecting an 80:20 ratio based on performance outcomes.

Moreover, the study employs different algorithms, including LSTM and GRU, with varying numbers of hidden units for different forecasting horizons, and utilizes the Adam optimizer with a learning rate of 0.01. The performance of the forecasting model is assessed using three key metrics: RMSE (Root Mean Square Error), MAE (Mean Absolute Error), and the correlation coefficient (R), which collectively provide a comprehensive evaluation of forecasting accuracy.
