The paper discusses a methodology for predicting power consumption over various time horizons—namely one day, three days, one week, and two weeks—using three neural network models: Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM), Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU), and Drop-GRU. The models are designed with fixed internal architectures that take as input the current power consumption and previous values. Each model is tailored to its specific prediction horizon, allowing for accurate consumption forecasts after 30 minutes, which can be extended to the entire desired period through parameter adjustments.

The experimental setup involves constructing four neural networks corresponding to different prediction horizons, with training data set at four times the length of the prediction horizon. Key parameters for each network, including the number of inputs, neurons in hidden layers, and training iterations, are determined through training on real data. The validation process for the machine learning models shows that the predicted curves closely align with the original data, indicating strong interpolation capabilities and minimal errors, thus demonstrating the effectiveness of the proposed methodologies.
