The paper discusses the architecture and functioning of Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks, particularly focusing on their application in maintenance and information update of cell statuses. It outlines the computational process involved in LSTM cells, detailing the roles of various components such as forget, input, and output gates, as well as the memory cell state. The mathematical formulations for these processes are provided, including the sigmoid activation function.

The LSTM model is structured into three main parts: the input layer for data preprocessing, the hidden layer for parameter optimization and training, and the output layer for data prediction. Additionally, the complexity of the network is defined by its trainable parameters, referred to as trainable weights, which are connected across the input, hidden, and output layers. The formula for calculating the Number of Trainable Weights (NTW) is presented, emphasizing the relationships between the network's inputs, outputs, and LSTM cells.
