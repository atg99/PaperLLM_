The paper discusses the prediction of power consumption using three different models: Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM), Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU), and Drop-GRU. The results of these models are evaluated based on their ability to forecast power consumption over various timeframes, specifically focusing on one-day and three-day predictions.

In the first experiment, the one-day prediction results demonstrate that all three models closely align with actual power consumption values, indicating their effectiveness in short-term forecasting. The graphical representation shows that the prediction curves of LSTM, GRU, and Drop-GRU closely mimic the true data.

The second experiment extends the prediction horizon to three days. The results reveal that the models not only maintain the shape of the actual data curve but also successfully forecast the peaks in power consumption. The findings suggest that the proposed algorithms are reliable for short-term power consumption forecasting, with the visual data supporting the accuracy of the predictions. 

Overall, the experiments illustrate the potential of LSTM, GRU, and Drop-GRU in effectively predicting power consumption patterns.
