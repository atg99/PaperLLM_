This paper presents the design of a real-time object detection prototype system utilizing YOLOv3, a prominent method in computer vision known for its speed and accuracy. The study emphasizes the significance of object detection, which involves identifying and classifying objects within images or videos, providing essential semantic understanding for various applications, including image classification.

The YOLO method operates by predicting bounding boxes and class probabilities from the entire image using a single neural network, making it faster than many other algorithms. However, it does face challenges such as localization errors and relatively slow training speeds. The research aims to create a system capable of detecting random objects in real-time, assessing its accuracy and performance using data from the researcher’s surroundings.

The system is designed using Unified Modeling Language (UML) diagrams and implemented in Python, leveraging its high-level programming capabilities and ease of learning. The results of the object detection process will include the object's name, bounding box, and accuracy percentage, facilitating recognition of stationary and moving objects.

Overall, this study seeks to enhance the understanding of YOLOv3's effectiveness in object detection and aims to contribute to the field by providing data on its performance in real-world scenarios.
