This collection of references encompasses various studies in machine learning, particularly focusing on semi-supervised learning, domain adaptation, and representation learning. Key contributions include:

1. **Back-Translation**: Edunov et al. investigate the scalability of back-translation for improving translation models.
2. **Infinite Variational Autoencoders**: Abbasnejad et al. propose a method for semi-supervised learning using infinite variational autoencoders.
3. **Neural Simpletrons**: Forster et al. introduce directed generative networks to learn with minimal labeled data.
4. **Label Noise Classification**: Frénay and Verleysen provide a comprehensive survey on the impact of label noise in classification tasks.
5. **Self-Ensembling**: French et al. present a self-ensembling approach for visual domain adaptation.
6. **Label Propagation**: Fujiwara and Irie discuss efficient label propagation techniques.
7. **Unsupervised Domain Adaptation**: Ganin and Lempitsky propose a method for domain adaptation through backpropagation.
8. **Consistency-Based Active Learning**: Gao et al. focus on minimizing labeling costs through a semi-supervised active learning framework.
9. **Generative Adversarial Networks**: Goodfellow et al. introduce GANs, a significant advancement in generative modeling.
10. **Node2Vec**: Grover and Leskovec present a scalable feature learning method for networks.

These studies contribute to advancements in understanding and improving learning algorithms in the presence of limited labeled data, noise, and domain shifts, highlighting the importance of efficient representation learning and label propagation techniques.
