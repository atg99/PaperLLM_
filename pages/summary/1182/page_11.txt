The paper discusses a dual loss function combining supervised and unsupervised losses for model training. The total loss L consists of a supervised loss Ls and an unsupervised loss Lu, where hyperparameters control the weight of each loss term. A key aspect of the training process involves ensuring one student model's predictions align with its counterpart, updating weights based on model stability. If both models are stable, the one with greater prediction variation is adjusted.

The authors introduce Fast-SWA, an enhancement of Stochastic Weight Averaging (SWA), which addresses the observation that models continue to explore weight space during late training stages. Fast-SWA averages model weights more frequently within cycles, improving performance by stabilizing the training trajectory and yielding better generalization. The method is motivated by the premise that averaging is more beneficial when the distance between averaged points is significant, thus facilitating a more efficient ensembling process.
