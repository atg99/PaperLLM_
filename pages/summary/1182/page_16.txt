The paper discusses various methods in semi-supervised learning (SSL), focusing on entropy minimization and proxy-label methods. 

Entropy minimization aims to reduce the uncertainty of model predictions by minimizing the entropy of the output distribution. While effective in theory, high-capacity models like neural networks can overfit to low-confidence data, leading to overly confident predictions that do not generalize well. Thus, combining entropy minimization with other techniques can yield better results.

Proxy-label methods generate pseudo-labels for unlabeled data using the model's predictions. These labels, although potentially noisy, are used alongside labeled data to enhance training. The methods are categorized into self-training, where the model assigns labels to unlabeled data, and multi-view learning, which uses different model perspectives to create labels.

In self-training, an initial model is trained on a small labeled dataset, then used to label the unlabeled dataset based on a confidence threshold. The process iterates, refining the model with the newly labeled data until it can no longer produce confident predictions. While self-training can improve model performance, it shares limitations with entropy minimization, as it may not effectively correct errors in the labeling process.
