The paper discusses the concept of Temporal Ensembling and its implementation in a Mean Teacher framework for training neural networks. Temporal Ensembling utilizes an exponential moving average (EMA) of predictions from previous training epochs, giving more weight to recent predictions. This approach enhances the stability of targets during training, leading to improved results and faster training since only a single forward pass is needed to obtain predictions. However, it also requires significant memory to store aggregated predictions, especially with large datasets.

The Mean Teacher model builds on Temporal Ensembling by separating the roles of the teacher and the student. The teacher generates targets from its predictions, which the student uses to learn through a consistency loss. This separation helps address the issue of confirmation bias, where the model may become stuck in its predictions if unsupervised loss dominates supervised loss. To improve the quality of targets, the Mean Teacher framework suggests careful selection of perturbations and teacher models instead of relying on the student model for both roles. Overall, the Mean Teacher approach facilitates faster learning and better target quality, overcoming limitations of previous methods.
