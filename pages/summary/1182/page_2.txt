The paper discusses semi-supervised learning (SSL), which combines aspects of supervised and unsupervised learning by utilizing both labeled and unlabeled data. In SSL, a small portion of the dataset consists of labeled examples, while the majority remains unlabeled. The primary objective is to leverage the unlabeled data to enhance model performance beyond what is achievable with labeled data alone. This involves estimating the data distribution and refining decision boundaries.

SSL techniques originated with self-training, where a model is initially trained on labeled data, and then iteratively annotates unlabeled data to improve training. The paper categorizes SSL methods into various approaches, including consistency regularization, which relies on the principle that predictions should remain stable under realistic perturbations of unlabeled data. This method aims to ensure consistent predictions for both original and perturbed examples, thereby improving the model's robustness and accuracy.
