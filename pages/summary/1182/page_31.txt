The paper discusses the computational aspects of updating weights in sparse graphs and label propagation methods. Sparse graphs are characterized by limited connections among nodes, typically using approaches like k-nearest neighbors (kNN) or epsilon-nearest neighbors (εNN). The edge weights can be computed using a Gaussian kernel, which incorporates a hyperparameter for distance measurement.

Label propagation operates under the premise that data points within the same manifold likely share semantic labels. It propagates labels from labeled to unlabeled data points based on the graph's structure and node similarity. The labeling scores are optimized to minimize a loss function related to the graph Laplacian, which penalizes differing predictions between connected similar nodes.

The loss function is defined as a quadratic form involving the graph Laplacian matrix, which captures smoothness across the graph. The optimal solution involves matrix inversion, which is computationally intensive for large graphs. An iterative approach is suggested as a more efficient alternative, where labels are progressively propagated until convergence is achieved. Additionally, a variant proposed by Zhou et al. introduces a small contribution from neighboring nodes to enhance label propagation.
