The collection of papers listed primarily focuses on advancements in semi-supervised and self-supervised learning techniques, particularly in the context of image classification and natural language processing. Key contributions include:

1. **Self-training and Noisy Student Methods**: Xie et al. (2019) demonstrate that self-training with a noisy student model enhances ImageNet classification performance, indicating the effectiveness of leveraging unlabeled data.

2. **Billion-scale Learning**: Yalniz et al. (2019) explore semi-supervised learning on a large scale, addressing challenges in image classification with vast datasets.

3. **Graph-based Approaches**: Yang et al. (2016) revisit semi-supervised learning through graph embeddings, highlighting the importance of relational data in improving model accuracy.

4. **Unsupervised Learning Techniques**: Yarowsky (1995) presents methods for word sense disambiguation that rival supervised approaches, emphasizing the potential of unsupervised learning.

5. **Noise Correction**: Yi and Wu (2019) propose a probabilistic approach for correcting noisy labels, which is crucial for training robust models in real-world scenarios.

6. **Self-supervised and Consistency Regularization**: Zhai et al. (2019) introduce self-supervised semi-supervised learning, while Zhang et al. (2019) focus on consistency regularization within generative adversarial networks (GANs).

7. **Mixup Technique**: Zhang et al. (2017) present the Mixup method, which extends empirical risk minimization by combining training examples to enhance model generalization.

8. **Graph Neural Networks**: Zhou et al. (2018) provide a comprehensive review of graph neural networks, discussing their methods and applications across various domains.

9. **Weakly Supervised Learning**: Zhou (2018) gives an overview of weakly supervised learning, which utilizes limited labeled data effectively.

10. **Tri-training and Label Propagation**: Zhou and Li (2005) introduce tri-training to exploit unlabeled data using multiple classifiers, while Zhu et al. (2003) explore label propagation methods for semi-supervised learning.

Overall, the papers collectively contribute to the understanding and development of learning paradigms that effectively utilize both labeled and unlabeled data, addressing challenges in model training and data efficiency.
