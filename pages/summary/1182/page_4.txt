Active learning (AL) is introduced as a method that enables a learning algorithm to selectively request labels for specific examples from a large pool of unlabeled data, aiming to improve accuracy while minimizing labeling costs. Key criteria in AL include informativeness, which assesses how much an unlabeled instance can reduce uncertainty, and representativeness, which evaluates how well an instance reflects the overall data structure.

The paper also explores the connection between active learning and semi-supervised learning (SSL), noting that both approaches seek to enhance learning with limited labeled data. Various studies demonstrate the effectiveness of combining AL and SSL across different applications.

Transfer learning is defined as a technique to improve performance on a target domain by leveraging knowledge from a related source domain. Domain adaptation, a subset of transfer learning, focuses on scenarios where the task remains the same but the data distributions differ. The paper distinguishes between unsupervised, semi-supervised, and supervised domain adaptation based on the availability of labeled data in the target domain.

Overall, the paper highlights the importance of leveraging unlabeled data and the interplay between different learning strategies to enhance model performance in challenging data scenarios.
