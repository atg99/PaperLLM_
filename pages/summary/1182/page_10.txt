The paper discusses a method called Mean Teacher, which employs a teacher model that is an exponential moving average (EMA) of a student model to generate targets for consistency training. The student model is trained to minimize both supervised loss on labeled examples and consistency loss on unlabeled examples. The teacher model is updated at each iteration to reflect the latest information from the student model, with noise injected during evaluation.

A key distinction of the Mean Teacher approach is that it utilizes the same model for both student and teacher roles, contrasting with methods like Temporal Ensembling that average predictions from multiple models. The loss function combines supervised and unsupervised losses, where the teacher model provides targets for the unsupervised loss based on its predictions.

However, the Mean Teacher method has a limitation: over many training iterations, the teacher model's weights can converge to those of the student model, potentially propagating biased predictions. To address this, Ke et al. propose the Dual Students approach, which involves training two student models with different initializations simultaneously. One model generates targets for the other based on stability criteria, which include consistent predictions for clean and perturbed inputs and confidence levels above a specified threshold.

In summary, the paper highlights the Mean Teacher method's mechanics and its limitations, while introducing the Dual Students approach as a solution to maintain model prediction stability during training.
