The paper discusses the Pi-Model, a simplified version of the Ladder Networks' ϕ-Model, which utilizes a single network for predictions on both corrupted and uncorrupted inputs. The key objective is to minimize the distance between two predictions made on the same input, leveraging the stochastic nature of neural networks enhanced by techniques like dropout and data augmentation. The loss function combines weighted unsupervised loss, computed as the mean squared error between the two outputs, with supervised loss from labeled examples.

Additionally, the paper introduces Temporal Ensembling, a refinement of the Pi-Model that addresses the instability of using single evaluations as targets for unsupervised loss. Instead, it aggregates previous predictions into a stable target through an exponentially moving average, improving training stability and efficiency. This approach allows for faster training by requiring only one forward pass to obtain current predictions and aggregated targets, thus enhancing the model's ability to extract training signals from unlabeled data.
