The paper discusses two dropout techniques: Element-wise Adversarial Dropout (EAdD) and Channel-wise Adversarial Dropout (CAdD). EAdD drops individual activations without considering spatial correlations, while CAdD drops entire feature maps, making it more appropriate for convolutional layers. The mask for CAdD is constrained to be similar to the mask of EAdD to ensure spatial consistency.

Additionally, the paper introduces Interpolation Consistency Training (ICT), a method aimed at improving semi-supervised learning (SSL) efficiency. ICT utilizes a MixUp operation to generate interpolated inputs and trains the model to produce consistent predictions across these interpolations. This approach addresses the inefficiencies of traditional adversarial perturbation methods, which require extensive computations to maximize prediction changes.
