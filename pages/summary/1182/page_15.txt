The paper discusses methods for semi-supervised learning (SSL) using consistency training and unsupervised data augmentation. It introduces a framework where a teacher model generates targets for a student model, aiming for consistency in predictions between mixed inputs of unlabeled data points. The objective is to minimize the mean squared error between the student model's predictions and the teacher model's outputs.

The paper highlights the advantages of using mixup as a perturbation method, suggesting that it can effectively navigate low-density regions in the data distribution, which is beneficial for moving decision boundaries.

Unsupervised Data Augmentation techniques, such as RandAugment and Back Translation, are proposed to enhance the diversity and realism of augmented examples. These methods aim to improve sample efficiency and provide necessary inductive biases for various tasks.

Additionally, the paper discusses entropy minimization as a strategy to encourage the network to make confident predictions on unlabeled data. This approach reinforces the cluster assumption by discouraging the decision boundary from passing near low-confidence predictions, ultimately enhancing the model's performance in classifying unlabeled samples.
