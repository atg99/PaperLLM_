The paper discusses context aggregation in graph neural networks, illustrating a three-step process where the context of a node is influenced by its neighbors and their neighbors. It highlights the limitations of traditional methods like DNGR and SDNE, which rely on deep autoencoders for node representation but face challenges with scalability and transductive learning.

To address these issues, recent node embedding techniques focus on local neighborhood information rather than the entire graph. Graph neural networks utilize node features, akin to convolutional neural networks, to generate embeddings. The training involves initializing hidden states with node features and iteratively updating them through aggregation of neighboring hidden states using specific functions.

The process includes three main steps: aggregating neighbor hidden states, applying a non-linear transformation, and normalizing the results. Various approaches like GCN, GraphSAGE, and GAT implement this framework but differ in their aggregation and combination methods. GraphSAGE, for instance, employs concatenation and tests different aggregation functions such as mean and max-pooling.
