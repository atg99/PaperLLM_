The paper discusses the framework of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) and their application in semi-supervised learning (SSL). In a GAN, a generator network creates samples from a latent variable, while a discriminator network distinguishes between real and generated samples. The discriminator aims to classify real samples as real and generated samples as fake, with the generator attempting to produce realistic samples to fool the discriminator.

The paper also introduces a modification to traditional Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) called Infinite Variational Autoencoder (Infinite VAE). This approach addresses the limitations of fixed latent space dimensions in standard VAEs by allowing for an infinite mixture of autoencoders. This flexibility enables the model to adapt to the complexity of the data, improving the accuracy of distribution capture for unlabeled data. The generative model from Infinite VAE can be combined with labeled data to train a discriminative model, enhancing its performance with fewer samples.

In summary, the paper highlights the integration of generative models, like GANs and Infinite VAEs, into SSL frameworks, emphasizing their capability to improve classification tasks by leveraging both labeled and unlabeled data effectively.
