The paper discusses the training objectives for Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), specifically focusing on the discriminator (D) and generator (G). The objective functions for D and G are defined to maximize and minimize specific log probabilities, respectively. The standard normal distribution is commonly used for the latent variable z. Various enhancements to the training process are mentioned, including hinge-loss formulations and Wasserstein GANs (WGAN), which have been further refined through techniques like spectral normalization and consistency regularization.

A specific type of GAN, called Categorical Generative Adversarial Networks (CatGAN), is introduced. CatGAN integrates generative and discriminative training by having the discriminator act as a classifier that maximizes mutual information between input data and predicted labels across multiple classes. The discriminator must be confident in classifying real samples while remaining uncertain about generated samples. This approach helps the model generalize better to unseen data and mitigates overfitting.

The CatGAN framework establishes specific requirements for both the discriminator and generator. The discriminator must ensure certainty in class assignments for real samples, uncertainty for generated samples, and equal distribution across classes. The generator must produce samples that are confidently assigned to classes and also maintain an equal distribution across classes. The objectives for both D and G are formulated to minimize and maximize the entropy of their respective outputs, ensuring the desired class distribution and uncertainty levels are achieved.
