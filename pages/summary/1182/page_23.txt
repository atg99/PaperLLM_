The paper discusses the FixMatch model, which utilizes weakly augmented inputs to generate proxy labels for training. If the maximum class probability from a weakly augmented input exceeds a defined threshold, it is used as a target label for training on strongly augmented versions of the input. The unsupervised loss is formulated based on these proxy labels and the strong augmentations generated.

Weak augmentations involve simple transformations like flipping and shifting images, while strong augmentations utilize techniques like RandAugment and CTAugment, which apply random transformations to each sample. Key components of FixMatch include the Adam optimizer, weight decay regularization, and a cosine learning rate schedule that adjusts the learning rate throughout the training process.

Additionally, the paper contrasts unsupervised and supervised learning, explaining that the goal of unsupervised learning is to estimate the density of an unknown data distribution, while supervised learning focuses on estimating the relationship between inputs and labels. Semi-supervised learning with generative models combines elements of both approaches, leveraging both labeled and unlabeled data to enhance learning. The section concludes with an exploration of generative methods in deep semi-supervised learning.
