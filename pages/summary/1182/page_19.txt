The paper discusses various techniques for improving image classification through co-training and tri-training methods. Qiao et al. propose using adversarial perturbations to generate new views for deep co-training, where models are encouraged to align in predictions on labeled data while differing in errors under adversarial attacks. 

Democratic Co-training extends this idea by employing multiple models with different architectures that label data based on majority consensus from their predictions. Tri-training addresses data scarcity by leveraging three independently trained models to validate predictions, requiring agreement before adding unlabeled data to the training set. This method is applicable without needing multiple views or unique learning algorithms, although it can be computationally intensive.

To enhance efficiency, Ruder et al. suggest sampling a limited number of unlabeled data points per epoch, gradually increasing the pool size. Multi-task tri-training further optimizes this by sharing a feature extractor among the models while maintaining distinct classification layers, introducing constraints to prevent similarity among models.

Cross-View Training innovates by using a shared encoder and auxiliary prediction modules, separating roles into primary teacher and student modules. This approach aims for consistent predictions across varied input views, with the teacher module generating pseudo-labels from full input views while students learn to match these predictions from limited views. The training objective balances labeled and unlabeled data loss to enhance model performance.
