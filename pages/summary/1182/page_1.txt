This paper provides a comprehensive overview of deep semi-supervised learning (SSL), highlighting its significance in addressing the challenges of training deep neural networks with limited labeled data. The authors, Yassine Ouali, Céline Hudelot, and Myriam Tami from Université Paris-Saclay, emphasize that while deep learning has achieved remarkable results in various supervised tasks, the creation of large labeled datasets is often resource-intensive and impractical in many scenarios.

The paper begins with an introduction to SSL, which is particularly relevant in real-world applications where unlabeled data is abundant, but labeled examples are scarce and costly to obtain. SSL methods aim to enhance classifier performance by leveraging the available unlabeled data, adhering to certain assumptions, such as the notion that decision boundaries should avoid high-density regions.

The authors outline key SSL methods and frameworks applicable to deep learning, including concepts such as consistency training, entropy minimization, proxy labeling, generative models, and graph neural networks. They argue that SSL is effective in improving classification performance when the model assumptions align well with the problem structure.

The paper serves as a resource for understanding the current landscape of deep SSL, summarizing dominant approaches and providing insights into the potential of these methods to make deep learning more data-efficient. For further exploration, the authors reference the Semi-Supervised Learning Book as a detailed resource on the topic.
