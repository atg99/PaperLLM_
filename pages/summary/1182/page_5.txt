The paper discusses various learning paradigms, particularly focusing on semi-supervised learning (SSL), weakly-supervised learning, and learning with noisy labels. In SSL, both labeled and unlabeled data arise from the same distribution, contrasting with unsupervised domain adaptation where target and source distributions differ. Techniques such as adversarial distribution alignment and consistency regularization are highlighted for enhancing classification performance with limited labeled data.

Weakly-supervised learning addresses the challenge of acquiring large, high-quality labeled datasets by utilizing lower-quality annotations from various sources, such as crowd workers or heuristic rules. This approach allows for the substitution of expensive pixel-level labels with simpler forms like image labels or bounding boxes. SSL methods can further improve performance when a few strongly labeled examples are available alongside weak annotations.

The paper also addresses the issue of learning from noisy labels, which can degrade model performance. Strategies to mitigate this include correcting the loss function, relabeling noisy examples, and reweighting training samples to differentiate between clean and noisy data. SSL methods can be employed to regularize training by treating noisy examples as unlabeled data.

For evaluating SSL approaches, the paper outlines a conventional methodology involving dataset selection, creation of labeled and unlabeled sets, and training a model with an SSL technique. To enhance this evaluation, it suggests implementing a shared architecture for fair comparison and establishing a high-quality supervised baseline to benchmark SSL performance against traditional supervised learning.
