The paper discusses two main approaches to label propagation and graph embedding in the context of semi-supervised learning (SSL). 

In label propagation, the authors present a loss function that combines smoothness and fitting constraints, allowing for adjustments to initial noisy labels. The optimal solution for minimizing this loss is expressed mathematically, with the propagation formula provided for an iterative approach that is computationally cheaper but may not yield identical results to the optimal solution.

The section on graph embedding focuses on representing nodes in a graph as low-dimensional vectors for SSL tasks like node classification. The goal is to encode nodes in a way that reflects their local neighborhood structure. Various methods are discussed, including factorization-based approaches like Laplacian Eigenmaps and scalable techniques such as LINE and HOPE, which aim to preserve high-order proximities. Additionally, random walk methods are highlighted, where embeddings are optimized based on the co-occurrence of nodes in random walks, leveraging algorithms like word2vec to enhance node representations.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of effective label propagation and graph embedding techniques for improving SSL performance in graph-based data.
