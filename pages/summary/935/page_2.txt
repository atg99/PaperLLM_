This report marks the beginning of a century-long series of studies aimed at exploring artificial intelligence (AI) and its implications for government and industry. Chaired by Professor Peter Stone from the University of Texas at Austin, the inaugural Study Panel consists of seventeen experts from various fields, including AI, law, political science, and economics. The panel was formed in response to the Standing Committee's recognition of the need to assess both recent AI advancements and their societal impacts across multiple domains such as jobs, healthcare, and public safety.

The committee opted for a thematic focus on "AI and Life in 2030," acknowledging that AI's effects are interconnected with broader societal and technological changes. By concentrating on a typical North American city, the study aims to provide insights that are globally relevant while recognizing the diversity of urban environments. Future studies are planned to expand the geographic scope and enhance the understanding of AI's role worldwide.

The report's structure includes an executive summary, an overview of AI, domain-specific applications, and recommendations for AI public policy, along with a historical context of AI development. Each section addresses critical areas where AI is expected to influence life in urban settings, emphasizing the importance of thoughtful policy development in response to these advancements.
