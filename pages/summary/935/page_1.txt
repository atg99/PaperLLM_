The One Hundred Year Study on Artificial Intelligence, initiated in 2014, is a comprehensive investigation into the advancements and societal impacts of AI. This ongoing study, overseen by a Standing Committee, assembles a Study Panel every five years to evaluate the progress of AI, anticipate future developments, and identify associated technical and societal challenges. Key areas of focus include ethics, economics, and the design of AI systems that align with human cognition.

The study aims to produce synthesized assessments that guide AI research, development, and policy, ensuring that AI systems benefit individuals and society as a whole. It draws inspiration from the AAAI Asilomar Study, which convened experts to discuss near-term developments and ethical considerations in AI. The success and influence of the Asilomar meeting highlighted the need for a long-term study of AI's evolving role in everyday life, leading to the establishment of the One Hundred Year Study at a university. The overarching goal is to provide a cohesive understanding of AI's trajectory and its implications for humanity.
