Cities are increasingly employing AI methods to enhance transportation services, including optimizing bus and subway schedules, dynamically adjusting traffic conditions, and implementing smart pricing on highways and bridges. The integration of sensors and cameras allows for improved traffic light timing and automated enforcement, aimed at maximizing resource efficiency within the transportation network. This shift from static to dynamic pricing strategies, which emerged after the 2000s, raises concerns over equitable access to public goods, particularly during peak demand.

The advent of large-scale data has made transportation a suitable field for machine learning applications, leading to the widespread use of routing services like Google Maps and Bing Maps since 2006. These applications provide real-time traffic information and facilitate optimal routing for vehicles and pedestrians. However, the adoption of sensing and optimization techniques at the city infrastructure level has lagged behind their application for individual users. Factors such as high infrastructure costs, varying priorities among cities, coordination challenges, and public privacy concerns have hindered progress.

Despite these challenges, the potential for AI and data-driven approaches in urban transportation remains significant. Ethical dilemmas also arise, particularly regarding the programming of autonomous vehicles in scenarios where human injury is unavoidable, raising questions about decision-making in life-threatening situations.
