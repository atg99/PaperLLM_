The paper outlines several traditional sub-areas of artificial intelligence (AI), noting that while some areas are currently more prominent, others hold historical significance and may regain relevance in the future. Key sub-areas include:

- **Search and Planning**: Focuses on reasoning for goal-directed behavior, exemplified by chess programs like Deep Blue that decide optimal moves to win.
  
- **Knowledge Representation and Reasoning**: Involves structuring large amounts of information for efficient retrieval, as demonstrated by IBM's Watson, which won Jeopardy by organizing and indexing vast data sources.

- **Machine Learning**: A paradigm that allows systems to improve performance through data observation, significantly contributing to the recent AI surge across various applications such as speech recognition and fraud detection.

- **Multi-Agent Systems**: Studies the interactions among intelligent systems, increasingly relevant in online marketplaces and transportation.

- **Robotics**: Investigates the integration of sensing and acting in physical systems, with a growing focus on Human-Robot Interaction.

- **Machine Perception**: Central to AI, particularly in robotics, with significant studies in Computer Vision and Natural Language Processing.

- **Social Network Analysis and Crowdsourcing**: Emerging focus areas due to the Internet's growth, exploring community behavior and collective problem-solving techniques.

The paper emphasizes that while these sub-fields allow for specialized advancements, achieving comprehensive intelligence integration requires a synthesis of multiple ideas, illustrated by the AlphaGo program, which utilized various machine learning algorithms and sophisticated search methods to defeat a human champion in Go.
