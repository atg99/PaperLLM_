The paper discusses the integration of AI technologies in education, particularly through the use of online resources and learning analytics. It highlights various assessment techniques such as vocabulary tests, WH questions, and multiple-choice questions that utilize platforms like WordNet and Wikipedia. The expansion of online courses has created opportunities for data collection, enhancing the field of learning analytics. Organizations like the Society for Learning Analytics Research and conferences such as Learning Analytics and Knowledge Conference have emerged, focusing on analyzing student engagement and outcomes using AI methods.

Current projects aim to address common misconceptions among students, predict risks of failure, and provide immediate feedback linked to learning outcomes. There is also an emphasis on understanding cognitive processes related to learning, which informs the development of educational technologies.

Despite the potential of AI in education, its adoption in institutions has been limited due to financial constraints and insufficient data on effectiveness. This slow progress is being addressed through initiatives by private foundations and teacher training programs. The paper notes that previous overestimations of MOOCs' capabilities have led to more realistic evaluations of new educational technologies, as seen in the experiences of institutions like San Jose State University with Udacity.
