The evolution of healthcare robotics has shifted dramatically from its early days, where companies like Robodoc faced commercial challenges despite technological advancements in orthopedic surgery. The introduction of the da Vinci surgical system by Intuitive Surgical in 2000 marked a significant turning point, establishing a new standard for minimally invasive procedures, particularly in prostate cancer treatment. The da Vinci system, now in its fourth generation, offers enhanced 3D visualization and ergonomic designs, facilitating nearly three-quarters of a million surgeries annually. This platform has not only improved surgical processes but also fostered an innovation ecosystem encompassing new instruments and data analytics.

On the other hand, intelligent automation in hospital operations has not seen similar success. Early attempts, such as HelpMate's delivery robots, failed commercially, while more recent initiatives like Aethon's TUG Robots have seen limited adoption in hospitals. However, the success of robotics in other industries suggests potential for future innovations in healthcare.

Looking forward, many healthcare tasks may benefit from robotic augmentation rather than full automation. For instance, robots could assist in delivering items within hospitals but would still require human intervention for final placements. The complexity of medical judgment, governed by strict regulations, remains a significant challenge, emphasizing the need for human expertise in conjunction with robotic assistance.
