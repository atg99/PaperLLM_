The paper discusses the transformative impact of artificial intelligence (AI) on labor markets and economic structures. It highlights that while AI can lower the cost of goods and services, thereby increasing overall wealth, the immediate concern for many individuals is job loss, which is often framed negatively in political discourse. The fear of AI replacing all human jobs, particularly cognitive roles, is described as unlikely to occur suddenly, but a gradual shift towards AI integration in various sectors is anticipated.

The economic implications of AI on cognitive jobs are compared to the effects of automation in manufacturing, which have already displaced many middle-aged workers from stable, well-paying jobs. As AI progresses, there is a risk that a significant portion of the workforce may lose access to adequate living standards due to the diminishing value of labor compared to intellectual capital ownership. This shift necessitates a political response to develop social safety nets to support those affected, rather than relying solely on economic solutions.

Short-term responses may include education and retraining, while long-term considerations could involve evolving social services, such as healthcare, education, or even a guaranteed basic income. The paper suggests that countries like Switzerland and Finland have begun exploring these options. It posits that as AI becomes a new form of wealth creation, society must engage in discussions about equitable distribution of its benefits. Ultimately, the paper advocates for a societal framework where AI, likened to a new generation of children, supports the older generation that contributed to its development.
