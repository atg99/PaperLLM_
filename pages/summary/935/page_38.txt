The paper discusses the potential impacts of AI technologies on employment and workplace trends in North American cities, highlighting the challenges in assessing current effects due to past economic shifts driven by recession, globalization, and advancements in non-AI digital technologies. Despite productivity and GDP growth since the 1990s, median income has stagnated, and employment ratios have declined.

Digital technologies have significantly affected various industries, particularly those involving skilled middle positions, while the scope of tasks performed by AI is expanding, even into high-skilled professions. The paper emphasizes that AI is likely to replace tasks rather than entire jobs in the short term, leading to the emergence of new job categories that are difficult to predict.

It suggests that as AI automates functions traditionally requiring human labor, the size and structure of organizations may change, potentially leading to smaller, more efficient enterprises. AI will create new jobs by elevating the importance of certain tasks and enabling novel forms of interaction. Overall, the transition to AI in the workplace is expected to be gradual, with a mix of replacement and augmentation effects across various job sectors.
