The paper discusses the evolving landscape of personalized medicine through the integration of automated data capture from wearable devices and extensive lifetime clinical records of individuals. It highlights the commercial viability of engaging large populations to generate population-scale data for individualized analytics. However, the paper points out significant barriers to innovation, particularly the slow approval process by the FDA for diagnostic software and the restrictive HIPAA regulations that hinder the flow of patient data necessary for AI technologies. 

The potential for routine detection of adverse drug effects is noted, yet mobile applications may be restricted in accessing critical patient information. The lack of standardized privacy protection methods further complicates the advancement of AI in healthcare. The FDA's cautious approach stems from unclear cost-benefit analyses of new systems, which, if addressed, could facilitate quicker approvals for new treatments.

The paper also examines the challenges in automated image interpretation in healthcare, contrasting it with rapid advancements in other fields. While medical imaging has become predominantly digital, the nuances of medical judgment require expert radiologist input, making the current value proposition for full automation less compelling. Additionally, stringent regulations limit data sharing across institutions, leaving only large integrated care organizations to tackle these challenges.

Despite these hurdles, there is a growing momentum for automated and augmented image interpretation. The future may see initial implementations of image triage and secondary checks that enhance the efficiency of medical imaging. The coupling of electronic patient record systems with machine learning techniques could harness vast archives of patient scans, leading to promising developments in automated radiological assessments. However, the EHR market remains dominated by a few companies with substandard user interfaces, which may hinder widespread adoption and usability for healthcare professionals.
