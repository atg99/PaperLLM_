The paper discusses the evolving landscape of artificial intelligence (AI) research, highlighting key areas driven by economic incentives and the need for human-aware intelligent systems. It outlines several prominent research domains:

1. **Large-scale Machine Learning**: Focuses on designing algorithms that can efficiently process vast data sets.
2. **Deep Learning**: A subset of machine learning that has significantly advanced tasks such as object recognition and natural language processing.
3. **Reinforcement Learning**: Emphasizes experience-driven decision-making, enabling AI to take actions in real-world scenarios.
4. **Robotics**: Aims to train robots for reliable interaction with their environment and people, necessitating advancements in computer vision and machine perception.
5. **Computer Vision**: Transformed by deep learning, it allows computers to outperform humans in certain visual tasks, with ongoing research in automatic image and video captioning.
6. **Natural Language Processing**: Progressing towards systems that can engage in dialogue and improve machine translation, enhancing real-time communication across languages.
7. **Collaborative Systems**: Investigates models for autonomous systems that can work alongside humans and other systems.
8. **Crowdsourcing and Human Computation**: Explores methods to integrate human expertise into problem-solving for challenges beyond computer capabilities.
9. **Algorithmic Game Theory**: Examines the economic and social aspects of AI, addressing incentive misalignments among human participants and AI agents.
10. **Internet of Things (IoT)**: Focuses on interconnecting devices to leverage sensory data for intelligent applications.
11. **Neuromorphic Computing**: Aims to replicate biological neural networks to enhance computing efficiency and robustness.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the shift towards creating collaborative and interactive AI systems that can effectively learn from and work with humans.
