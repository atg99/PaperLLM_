This section explores the definition of Artificial Intelligence (AI) and the thriving areas of research and application within the field. It highlights the absence of a universally accepted definition, which has paradoxically contributed to the rapid growth and advancement of AI. Nils J. Nilsson's definition is presented, describing AI as the activity aimed at making machines intelligent, with intelligence being the ability to function appropriately and with foresight in an environment.

The discussion emphasizes that AI exists on a multi-dimensional spectrum, where distinctions between simple devices like calculators and complex systems like human brains are based on scale, speed, autonomy, and generality. While calculators demonstrate a form of intelligence, they do not reflect the capabilities of modern AI, which has evolved significantly and is now focused on enhancing the intelligence found in smartphones and other advanced technologies.

The text also addresses the "AI effect," where technologies initially classified as AI become standard and lose their AI designation as they become commonplace. This indicates that AI advancements occur incrementally rather than as sudden breakthroughs. Overall, the complexity of defining AI poses challenges in accurately representing its capabilities, which often contrasts with popular perceptions.
