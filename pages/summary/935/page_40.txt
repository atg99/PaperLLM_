The paper discusses the profound impact of the internet and artificial intelligence on entertainment and social interaction over the past fifteen years. It highlights the rise of user-generated content and social networks, like Facebook, which serve as personalized platforms for social interaction, sometimes at the expense of face-to-face communication. Applications such as WhatsApp and Snapchat facilitate constant connectivity among users, while virtual worlds like Second Life and games like World of Warcraft provide alternative existences.

The advent of specialized devices, such as the Kindle, has transformed traditional pastimes like reading, making it more convenient to access and store books. Various trusted platforms now facilitate the sharing of blogs, videos, and other user-generated content, relying on advanced techniques in natural language processing, image processing, and machine learning to enhance user experience through personalized recommendations.

Additionally, traditional entertainment sectors have adopted AI technologies to improve their offerings, as seen in the sports industry with data analytics and in music composition. AI also plays a role in historical research and arts analysis, exemplifying its broad applicability.

Despite the enthusiasm for AI-driven entertainment, there are concerns regarding its potential to diminish real-life interactions, especially among children who may prefer digital engagement over outdoor play. The paper argues for further research to maximize the benefits of interactive and personalized entertainment while considering its societal implications.
