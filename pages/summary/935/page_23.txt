The paper discusses the growing influence of AI on city infrastructure, particularly in transportation systems. With enhanced data availability, predictive models may accurately forecast individual movements, preferences, and goals, which raises ethical concerns outlined in Section III. The U.S. Department of Transportation's 2016 initiative aims to invest forty million dollars in medium-sized cities to innovate smart transportation infrastructure. A key vision includes a network of connected vehicles that communicate with one another to improve safety and efficiency, potentially transforming transportation through advances in multi-agent coordination.

The paper also highlights the role of robots and drones in transportation, with companies like Amazon testing drone delivery systems, though safety regulations remain a concern. The integration of advanced sensing capabilities and connected infrastructure raises privacy issues that must be addressed proactively by the industry or through legal measures, as discussed in Section III.

On-demand transportation services like Uber and Lyft exemplify the application of AI and connectivity in urban mobility. These platforms utilize algorithms for driver-passenger matching and implement dynamic pricing to manage demand and encourage driver participation. However, they pose regulatory challenges, including competition with traditional taxis and safety concerns. The potential for carpooling and ridesharing is acknowledged, though large-scale adoption has been limited.

The study panel concludes that while prototypes for versatile drones exist, such as flying quadcopters, their widespread use as a transportation method is unlikely by 2030.
