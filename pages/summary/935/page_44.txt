The paper emphasizes the need for interdisciplinary research on the societal implications of AI technologies, highlighting the importance of addressing various critical questions surrounding AI's safety, privacy, fairness, and accountability. Key inquiries include determining responsibility in incidents involving self-driving cars or intelligent medical devices, preventing unlawful discrimination by AI applications, and deciding who benefits from AI-driven efficiencies while ensuring protections for those whose skills may become obsolete.

As AI becomes increasingly integrated into various sectors, existing regulatory frameworks must adapt to accommodate these innovations. In the U.S., regulation has been sector-specific, with agencies like the FDA overseeing medical applications, the FAA regulating drones, and the FTC managing consumer-facing AI systems. The concept of "critical infrastructure" is also relevant, as it pertains to the end-user applications of AI rather than the technology itself.

The paper notes that current federal policy is inclined towards regulating sixteen specific sectors, and emphasizes that there is a significant gap in investment for research into the societal impacts of AI. It calls for increased funding from both private and public sectors to support interdisciplinary teams that can analyze AI's multifaceted implications effectively.
