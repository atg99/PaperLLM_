The paper discusses the potential of artificial intelligence (AI) in leveraging social networks for public health interventions and enhancing public safety. It highlights the challenges faced in targeting homeless youth in Los Angeles for health-related information dissemination, suggesting that AI can help identify effective peer leaders within their social networks to spread crucial messages, such as HIV prevention. The paper emphasizes the need for careful AI deployment to avoid reinforcing discriminatory practices, while also noting that AI systems may offer greater auditability compared to human decision-making.

In the realm of public safety and security, the paper outlines the increasing reliance on AI technologies in North American cities by 2030, including surveillance cameras, drones, and predictive policing. It acknowledges the dual nature of AI's impact—while there are concerns regarding overbearing policing, AI can also facilitate more targeted interventions. AI's role in detecting white-collar crime and enhancing cybersecurity is noted, alongside its potential use in crime scene management and resource allocation during emergencies, although full automation remains a future goal.

The paper concludes that while current surveillance technologies primarily aid in solving crimes post-factum, advancements in AI could improve crime prevention through better event classification and anomaly detection in video feeds. Overall, the paper advocates for the potential benefits of AI in these domains while cautioning against the risks associated with its implementation.
