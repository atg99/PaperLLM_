The paper discusses the lobbying efforts of major tech companies like Google, Facebook, and Amazon to avoid being classified as critical to the economy, fearing that such a designation could lead to burdensome regulations that hinder their innovation and product development. Despite this resistance, there is an increasing interest in regulating AI, particularly as it relates to critical infrastructure. Current regulatory frameworks, such as those by the FDA for high-stakes medical software, require specific engineering practices, but modern software often comprises various components from multiple vendors, complicating the feasibility of applying stringent standards universally.

The paper emphasizes the challenging balance between fostering innovation and ensuring safety in AI applications. It suggests that regulatory bodies will need heightened expertise to navigate the implications of the standards set by academia, industry, and government.

Policy and legal considerations surrounding AI are also highlighted. The paper notes that AI could challenge existing legal frameworks across various jurisdictions due to its transformative nature. American law encompasses a blend of common law, statutes, and regulations, which could all be impacted by AI. For instance, Nevada has enacted laws for autonomous vehicles, illustrating how legislation is adapting to technological advancements.

The paper further explores the implications of AI in different sectors, such as tax advice, stock market trading, and medical diagnostics, where the legal effects will depend on the specific regulatory environment. It warns that without adequate technical expertise, officials may either reject promising AI applications or approve those that lack thorough safety evaluations, potentially leading to public safety concerns. Overall, the paper underscores the need for a nuanced approach to regulation that considers both innovation and safety in the context of AI development and deployment.
