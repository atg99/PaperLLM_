This appendix provides a brief history of Artificial Intelligence (AI), primarily based on Nilsson's work and reflecting a contemporary focus on data-intensive methods. AI was officially established in 1956 at a workshop organized by John McCarthy at Dartmouth, where the term "artificial intelligence" was first used. The workshop fostered a dedicated research community that included key figures like Marvin Minsky and Herbert Simon, who would lead significant AI projects.

While the Dartmouth workshop unified the field, many foundational ideas had existed earlier. Thomas Bayes contributed a framework for probabilistic reasoning in the 18th century, and George Boole formalized logical reasoning in the 19th century. By the early 20th century, advancements in statistics enabled rigorous data inferences. The technological landscape matured with the development of electronic computers and primitive autonomous robots.

Alan Turing's contributions were pivotal, particularly his formal model of computing and his essay "Computing Machinery and Intelligence," which explored concepts of simulating intelligence and machine learning. Despite his groundbreaking ideas, Turing lacked the computing resources to implement them fully.

From the 1950s to the 1970s, several focal areas in AI research began to emerge, setting the stage for future developments in the field.
