The paper discusses the responsibility of companies regarding privacy, emphasizing that broad legal mandates have prompted them to establish dedicated staff and processes for enforcing privacy controls. This has led to increased transparency, allowing civil society and media to play a role in holding corporations accountable, thereby making privacy a significant concern for corporate boards. 

In the context of artificial intelligence (AI), regulators are encouraged to foster a cycle of accountability and transparency rather than mere compliance. As AI becomes more integrated into urban environments, it poses challenges to existing privacy and accountability protections. The paper highlights the dual potential of AI for both beneficial and harmful uses, underscoring the need for informed discussions on how to navigate its development for societal enrichment while promoting creativity.

The report advocates for policies that ensure equitable access to AI's benefits and warns against the concentration of power among a select few. Given the unpredictable nature of future AI technologies, it calls for continuous evaluation of policies in light of societal challenges and real-world evidence.

Recent advancements in AI, spurred by large data sets, enhanced sensory technologies, and deep learning, have already impacted North American cities significantly over the past fifteen years, with more substantial changes anticipated in the next decade. As new AI applications emerge, especially in transportation and healthcare, it is crucial that they are introduced in ways that foster trust and uphold human and civil rights. Policies must balance innovation with ethical, privacy, and security considerations to ensure that the benefits of AI are widely and fairly distributed, which is essential for a positive impact on urban life by 2030 and beyond.
