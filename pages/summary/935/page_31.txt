The past fifteen years have witnessed significant advancements in artificial intelligence (AI) within the education sector. AI applications are increasingly utilized by educators and learners across K-12 and university settings, enhancing the educational experience through personalized learning at scale. Despite the need for human engagement in teaching, integrating AI technologies effectively with traditional learning remains a challenge.

Historically, robots have served as educational tools, beginning with the Lego Mindstorms kits from the MIT Media Lab in the 1980s. Intelligent Tutoring Systems (ITS) have evolved to provide interactive tutoring in various subjects, leveraging Natural Language Processing combined with machine learning to facilitate online learning and cater to individual learning styles. This growth in online learning has been supported by extensive data sets, fueling advancements in learning analytics.

However, the adoption of AI in schools and universities has been sluggish, primarily due to funding constraints and insufficient evidence of its effectiveness in achieving learning goals. Over the next fifteen years, the integration of intelligent tutors and AI technologies in classrooms is expected to grow significantly, alongside the use of virtual reality in learning, though human teachers will still play an essential role.

In K-12 education, advanced robotics kits are now available, such as Ozobot, Cubelets, and Wonder Workshop's Dash and Dot, which engage students in coding and logical thinking. Despite their potential, widespread adoption of these tools hinges on demonstrating their impact on academic performance.

Overall, while ITS have transitioned from experimental research to practical applications, the challenge remains to substantiate their effectiveness in enhancing educational outcomes.
