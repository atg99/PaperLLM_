The paper discusses the current and future landscape of AI policy, emphasizing that the success of AI applications should be measured by the value they create for human lives. It advocates for the design of AI systems that promote understanding, participation, and trust among users, while highlighting the need for public policies that facilitate societal adaptation to AI. The study panel recommends that all levels of government enhance their technical expertise in AI and encourages research on fairness, security, privacy, and societal implications by increasing funding and removing barriers.

In the U.S., numerous agencies regulate AI-related sectors, necessitating the development of new legal and policy frameworks to address issues such as accountability in accidents involving AI systems and the mitigation of biases that could lead to discrimination. As AI becomes more integrated into various domains, it is crucial to disseminate best practices and adapt regulatory frameworks accordingly.

The panel acknowledges that while AI systems are unlikely to autonomously cause harm, they can be misused, and ensuring unbiased data remains a significant challenge. The need for regulation is anticipated, but it warns against poorly informed regulations that could hinder innovation or drive it elsewhere. Instead, it advocates for a balanced regulatory approach that encourages accountability and transparency similar to existing privacy regulations.

The paper concludes with a call for informed discussions on steering AI development in ways that benefit society, acknowledging the risk of widening inequalities if access to AI technologies is unevenly distributed. It stresses that misunderstandings about AI could lead to opposition against beneficial technologies, highlighting the importance of thoughtful regulation that fosters innovation rather than stifling it.
