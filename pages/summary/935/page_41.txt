The paper discusses the evolving landscape of entertainment and predicts significant changes over the next fifteen years. It highlights that entertainment is shaped by individual and social preferences, which are diverse and fluctuate over time. Current trends indicate a shift towards more advanced hardware innovations alongside the ongoing software revolution, with virtual reality and haptic technologies expected to become commonplace in homes. The development of more human-like interactions with robots and entertainment systems, enhanced by Automatic Speech Recognition, is anticipated.

Furthermore, the paper emphasizes the democratization of content creation, where advancements in tools and applications will simplify the production of high-quality media. Technologies like ASR, dubbing, and Machine Translation will facilitate the customization of content for various audiences, complicating predictions about evolving entertainment tastes.

As content delivery becomes increasingly digital, media companies will leverage extensive data on consumer preferences to tailor content for specialized audiences, raising concerns about potential monopolistic practices. The paper suggests that this could lead to the emergence of media conglomerates acting as "Big Brothers," controlling individual exposure to ideas and experiences. The implications of such developments, particularly regarding AI-related policies, are explored in further sections of the paper.
