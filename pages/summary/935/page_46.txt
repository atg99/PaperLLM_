The paper discusses the implications of artificial intelligence (AI) on various legal and policy issues within the structure of American administrative law. Key areas of concern include privacy, innovation policy, and civil liability.

In the realm of privacy, AI's ability to reveal private information through predictions based on historical data raises significant challenges. Companies utilize machine learning for purposes such as assessing credit risk and predicting recidivism, which necessitates careful consideration to avoid bias related to factors like race and sexual orientation. Furthermore, anthropomorphic interfaces associated with AI present unique privacy issues, as humans may respond to these technologies as if they were interacting with actual individuals, potentially compromising their sense of solitude.

Regarding innovation policy, the paper contrasts the early legal frameworks that facilitated the growth of the Internet with the current software industry's shift towards aggressive intellectual property protections. This shift has created patent "thickets," complicating the balance between fostering AI innovation and protecting against third-party harms.

The topic of civil liability is highlighted, as AI's unpredictable behavior challenges traditional tort law assumptions about foreseeability of harm. Courts may face dilemmas in assigning liability, as they might hold human actors accountable for AI-caused injuries, even when the harm could not have been anticipated. This raises concerns about fairness, efficiency, and the potential for victims to bear the brunt of AI-related injuries.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the need for thoughtful legal frameworks to address the complex issues AI presents, particularly concerning privacy, innovation, and liability.
