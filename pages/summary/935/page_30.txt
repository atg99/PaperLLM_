The elderly population in the United States is projected to increase by over 50% in the next fifteen years, leading to a significant rise in demand for home health aides, which are expected to grow by 38% over the next decade. Despite the opportunities presented by various technologies aimed at improving elderly care, progress has been slow over the past fifteen years. However, the generational shift in technology acceptance among the elderly suggests a growing interest in existing technologies that support health and well-being.

Key innovations anticipated in elder care include:

1. **Life Quality and Independence**: 
   - Automated transportation will enhance independence and social engagement.
   - Information sharing technologies will keep families connected, with predictive analytics promoting positive interactions.
   - Smart home devices will assist with daily activities such as cooking and personal care.

2. **Health and Wellness**:
   - Mobile applications will monitor activities and suggest ways to maintain mental and physical health.
   - In-home health monitoring will alert caregivers to changes in mood or behavior.
   - Personalized health management will address the complexities of managing multiple health conditions.

3. **Treatments and Devices**:
   - Improved hearing aids and visual assistive devices will enhance safety and social connections.
   - Personalized rehabilitation and therapy will decrease reliance on hospital stays.
   - Advanced physical assistive devices will expand the capabilities of individuals with mobility challenges.

The study panel anticipates a surge in low-cost sensing technologies that can greatly benefit elderly individuals at home. The integration of various AI technologies, including Natural Language Processing and robotics, will be essential to create effective systems for elderly care. Additionally, these advancements will raise important privacy concerns among families and caregivers, as the elderly population becomes increasingly active and engaged well into their later years.
