This paper provides a comprehensive review of recent deep metric learning methods specifically for few-shot image classification. It emphasizes the advantages of metric learning, such as the ability to avoid overfitting when dealing with limited labeled samples, as metric learning does not require additional parameter training for new classes once the metric is established. The authors decouple metric learning into three stages: learning feature embeddings, learning class representations, and learning distance measures. This framework promotes collaboration between the fields of few-shot classification and deep metric learning.

The review contrasts with previous surveys on few-shot learning (FSL) by providing deeper insights into metric learning methods, which have not been fully explored in earlier works. It highlights the evolution of these methods and their applicability in real-world scenarios, noting the rapid advancements in FSL since earlier surveys. The authors discuss various classical and recent metric learning algorithms published between 2018 and 2022, organizing them according to the aforementioned learning stages.

The paper also identifies challenges in current methods and suggests directions for future research. The review is structured to first define few-shot classification, introduce evaluation procedures, and outline commonly used datasets, followed by an analysis of classical and contemporary metric learning approaches, and concluding with a discussion of remaining challenges. Overall, it aims to provide an updated perspective on deep metric learning for few-shot image classification.
