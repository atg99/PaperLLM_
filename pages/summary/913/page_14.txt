Conditional Neural Adaptive Processes (CNAPS) utilize Mahalanobis distance for classification, estimating task-specific covariance matrices as convex combinations of sample covariance matrices, regularized toward an identity matrix. Transductive Episodic-wise Adaptive Metric (TEAM) learns a task-specific metric from support and query samples through three modules: a feature extractor, a task-specific metric module, and a similarity computation module, optimizing the Mahalanobis distance to refine the classification process.

Nguyen et al. introduced SEN, a dissimilarity measure that combines Euclidean distance and L2-norm differences to enhance feature normalization. DeepEMD employs Earth Mover’s Distance combined with convolutional feature embedding for few-shot learning, training parameters in an end-to-end manner. Xie et al. developed the Brownian Distance Covariance (BDC) metric, which offers a closed-form expression for discrete feature vectors, facilitating efficient computation and integration with few-shot classification methods.

The Relation Network was the first to use a neural network for modeling similarity in few-shot learning, comprising an embedding module and a relation module that computes similarity between support and query images. Li et al. proposed the Bi-Similarity Network (BSNet) to enhance feature discrimination for fine-grained classification using dual similarity measures. Zhang et al. introduced Absolute-relative Learning (ArL) to incorporate continuous-valued relations based on image attributes, addressing the limitations of binary relations.

Semantic Alignment Metric Learning (SAML) employs an MLP network for similarity scoring, utilizing an attention mechanism to compute a relation matrix from local features. Furthermore, recent advances in using Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) for few-shot learning emphasize the importance of relational modeling in improving classification outcomes.
