The paper outlines an evaluation procedure for C-way K-shot classification, detailing the steps involved in the algorithm. The input consists of a database of base classes and novel classes, along with a specified number of episodes. The evaluation process involves randomly selecting classes and samples to create support and query sets, predicting labels, and computing accuracy for each episode until the specified number of episodes is reached. The final output is the mean accuracy across all episodes.

Additionally, the paper discusses benchmark datasets used for few-shot image classification, including Omniglot, Mini-ImageNet, Tiered-ImageNet, CIFAR-FS, FC100, and Stanford Dogs. Each dataset is characterized by its class distribution, number of images, and the division into base, validation, and novel classes. Omniglot is noted for its high accuracy rates in recent studies, while Mini-ImageNet and Tiered-ImageNet are highlighted for their structured class distributions. CIFAR-FS and FC100 are derived from CIFAR-100, and Stanford Dogs focuses on fine-grained classification of dog breeds. The datasets are essential for evaluating and benchmarking few-shot learning algorithms.
