The references listed cover various advancements in few-shot learning, emphasizing different methodologies and approaches to improve image classification with limited data. Key contributions include:

1. **Rectifying Shortcut Learning**: Luo et al. address the issue of shortcut learning in background contexts, proposing solutions to enhance few-shot learning performance.

2. **Hierarchical Prototype Refinement**: Zhou et al. introduce a method that focuses on refining prototypes hierarchically, optimizing inter-categorical discrimination for better classification accuracy.

3. **Amortized Bayesian Prototype Meta-Learning**: Sun et al. present a probabilistic approach to few-shot learning that utilizes Bayesian methods for effective prototype management in image classification tasks.

4. **Variational Methods**: Zhang et al. and Chen et al. explore variational techniques in few-shot learning, providing insights into metric-based meta-learning and scaling.

5. **Adaptive Subspaces and Negative Margin**: Simon et al. and Liu et al. discuss the importance of adaptive learning subspaces and the role of margin in classifying few-shot instances.

6. **Temperature Networks and Metric Scaling**: Zhu et al. and Qiao et al. propose innovative metrics and temperature networks to enhance the learning process under few-shot conditions.

7. **Feature Normalization and Dissimilarity Measures**: Nguyen et al. and Zheng et al. investigate novel normalization techniques and dissimilarity measures that improve prototypical networks and face recognition tasks.

8. **DeepEMD and Joint Distribution**: Zhang et al. and Xie et al. focus on advanced methodologies like differentiable earth mover’s distance and deep Brownian distance covariance, which enhance classification accuracy in few-shot scenarios.

9. **Bi-Similarity Networks**: Li et al. introduce BSNet, which leverages dual similarity metrics to tackle the challenges of fine-grained image classification in few-shot learning contexts.

Collectively, these works contribute to a deeper understanding of the mechanisms and strategies that can be employed to improve few-shot learning, focusing on prototype management, metric optimization, and feature normalization.
