In the realm of few-shot metric learning, early methods like Siamese Network and Matching Network primarily rely on measuring distances between query samples and support samples for classification. However, due to the limited number of support samples, these approaches struggle to represent novel classes effectively. To address this limitation, researchers have introduced class prototypes as reference vectors for each class. These prototypes can be derived through simple or weighted averages of feature embeddings or learned in an end-to-end manner to enhance their representation capabilities. Some studies also explore class distributions or subspaces for representation.

The Prototypical Network exemplifies a foundational approach, classifying samples by calculating Euclidean distances to class prototypes formed as the mean of support sample embeddings. Its effectiveness hinges on the assumption that a well-defined embedding space exists where instances cluster around their class prototypes. Variations, such as using covariance matrices for prototype representation and similarity measurement, have also been explored.

Advancements include the semi-supervised Prototypical Network, which integrates labeled and unlabeled samples through soft k-means to compute weighted prototypes. The Infinite Mixture Prototypes (IMP) model addresses multi-modality by representing each class with multiple clusters modeled as Gaussian distributions, allowing for dynamic adjustment of cluster variance to optimize performance.

Further developments involve query-dependent prototypes, where weights for support samples are determined based on their relevance to the query, enhancing robustness to outliers. Techniques like CrossTransformers (CTX) extend this idea by computing attentive prototypes for specific local regions within query images, coupled with self-supervised training episodes.

Lastly, the Robust attentive profile Networks (RapNets) focus on improving prototype resilience against outliers and label noise by transforming raw feature embeddings into correlation features, leveraging bidirectional LSTM and fully-connected networks to generate attention scores for weighted combinations of support images. These innovations represent significant strides in enhancing few-shot learning methodologies by improving class representation and robustness against data challenges.
