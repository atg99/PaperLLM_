XtarNet comprises three meta-learners: the MetaCNN module that adapts feature embeddings for each task, the MergeNet module that generates weights for blending pre-trained and meta-learned features, and the TconNet module that enhances class prototypes for better discriminability. Rahman et al. proposed a unified approach for zero-shot, generalized zero-shot, and few-shot learning, which classifies query images based on the similarity between their semantic representations and the textual features of each class. This semantic representation integrates a linear combination of base samples' semantic features and a linear mapping derived from support images.

In the domain of feature learning, data augmentation serves as a valuable strategy to artificially expand the support set while preserving labels, particularly beneficial when support samples are scarce. Common methods include deformation techniques like cropping, padding, and horizontal flipping, alongside generating additional training samples and pseudo labels. In few-shot learning, some approaches embed a generator within the model to produce augmented data, enhancing the diversity of training data. For instance, Wang et al. introduced an end-to-end method where training data flows through two streams: one directly to the classifier and the other through a 'hallucination' network to create augmented data. Zhang et al. proposed a saliency-based strategy leveraging foregrounds and backgrounds for image hallucination. Another simpler feature synthesis method perturbs semantic representations and projects them into visual feature space, using competitive learning to refine synthesized samples.

Additionally, multi-task feature learning has emerged as a technique to leverage auxiliary information, promoting a regularization effect that aids in learning discriminative features. For example, TADAM incorporates an auxiliary task of training a global classifier on the base dataset, sampled probabilistically during training. Gidaris et al. combined few-shot learning with self-supervised learning by artificially rotating support samples, while Zhu et al. connected base and novel classes through visual attributes, employing attribute learning as an auxiliary task. Other works have enhanced this approach by predicting both common and differing attributes between image pairs, and neural architecture search has been introduced to optimize operations in few-shot learning.
