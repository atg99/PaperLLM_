This paper reviews recent developments in deep metric learning for few-shot image classification, focusing on approaches from 2018 to 2022. Few-shot classification aims to recognize new concepts with only a few training images, contrasting with traditional models that require extensive datasets. The primary challenge is the high risk of overfitting due to limited labeled samples. 

Deep metric learning has emerged as a key solution, enabling classification of unseen samples based on their distances to a few seen samples within an embedding space generated by deep neural networks. This method enhances performance while mitigating overfitting. The authors categorize the reviewed methods into three stages: learning feature embeddings, learning class representations, and learning distance measures. 

The review identifies the unique contributions of various methods and the challenges they encounter. Additionally, it discusses ongoing issues in the field and suggests future research directions. Overall, the paper highlights the potential of deep metric learning to improve few-shot image classification, aligning more closely with human learning capabilities.
