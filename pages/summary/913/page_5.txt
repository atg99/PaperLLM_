This paper discusses various benchmark datasets used for few-shot image classification, including CUB-200-2011, Mini-ImageNet, Fewshot-CIFAR100, Stanford Dogs, and Omniglot. 

CUB-200-2011 is a fine-grained bird classification dataset comprising 200 classes and 11,788 images, typically split into 100 base classes, 50 validation classes, and 50 novel classes. Mini-ImageNet serves as a base dataset for cross-domain few-shot classification, where 50 classes from CUB-200-2011 are used for validation and the remaining 50 as novel classes.

The Meta-Dataset is introduced as a large-scale dataset for evaluating few-shot classification methods, particularly in cross-domain contexts. It includes 10 diverse image datasets, such as ImageNet and MS COCO, and has two training procedures: multi-domain learning, where methods are trained on the first eight datasets and evaluated on both in-domain and out-of-domain datasets; and single-domain learning, which focuses on training with a version of ImageNet and evaluating across other datasets.

The paper also outlines few-shot deep metric learning methods, emphasizing the objective of supervised metric learning to develop a distance metric that effectively distinguishes between semantically similar and dissimilar instances. In few-shot classification, the metric is learned from the base dataset, allowing for the classification of query images from novel classes based on their distances to support images using distance-based classifiers like k-nearest neighbors.
