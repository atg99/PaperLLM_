Few-shot classification methods have been enhanced by incorporating noisy data and introducing a new evaluation metric to assess their robustness. A geometric interpretation of the Prototypical Network has been provided by Ma et al., conceptualizing it as a Voronoi diagram. They further advanced this idea with the Cluster-to-Cluster Voronoi Diagram (CCVD), which enables the ensemble of models trained on various data augmentations, utilizing either single or multiple feature transformations, and employing linear or nearest neighbor classifiers.

Ravichaandran et al. proposed an implicit method for learning class representations, treating prototypes as learnable functions of feature embeddings from labeled samples. This strategy accommodates unbalanced sample sizes in novel data. Another approach by Das and Lee involves a two-stage process for prototype generation, first obtaining coarse prototypes from mean embeddings and then refining novel prototypes through a meta-learnable function.

TapNet explicitly models class prototypes as learnable parameters, training them alongside feature embeddings in a way that makes them task-specific through a linear projection matrix. Luo et al. introduced a method to learn base and novel class prototypes concurrently, enhancing prototype diversity through sample synthesis. Chen et al. proposed the Knowledge Graph Transfer Network (KGTN), utilizing a gated graph neural network to leverage semantic correlations among classes for better prototype learning.

Additional methods include the Shared Object Concentrator (SOC) algorithm, which derives multiple prototypes from local features of support images, and Zhou et al.'s Progressive method, which also contributes to the evolving landscape of few-shot classification techniques. Overall, these advancements reflect a trend towards more sophisticated and adaptable approaches in few-shot learning, particularly in handling class prototypes and leveraging correlations among classes.
