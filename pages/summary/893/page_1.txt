The paper "Attention-aware Multi-stroke Style Transfer" by Yuan Yao et al. addresses limitations in existing neural style transfer methods, particularly their inability to coordinate spatial visual attention between content and stylized images and to render varying levels of detail through different brush strokes. The authors propose an attention-aware multi-stroke style transfer model that incorporates a self-attention mechanism into a style-agnostic reconstruction autoencoder framework, enabling the derivation of attention maps from content images.

The model employs multi-scale style swapping on content and style features to produce diverse feature maps that reflect various stroke patterns. A flexible fusion strategy is introduced to integrate characteristics from the attention map, ensuring that multiple stroke patterns harmoniously occupy different spatial regions of the output image. The paper demonstrates that this approach effectively generates stylized images comparable to state-of-the-art methods while maintaining attention consistency with the content image.

The introduction outlines the evolution of neural style transfer techniques, starting from Gatys et al.'s foundational work, and highlights the challenges faced by current methods, including the restricted binding to specific styles and the indiscriminate treatment of diverse image regions. The proposed method aims to overcome these challenges by achieving faithful style transfer while maintaining attention consistency, thus enhancing the quality and effectiveness of stylized image generation.
