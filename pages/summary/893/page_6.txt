The paper presents a comparative analysis of various style transfer methods, specifically focusing on the performance of different approaches such as AdaIN, WCT, Avatar-Net, and the proposed method. Results are evaluated across diverse content images, including portraits, animals, and landscapes, highlighting the effectiveness of each technique.

The findings indicate that while Avatar-Net successfully bridges the gap between content and style features, it struggles with attention-aware feature adaptation, leading to distortions in semantically important areas, such as facial features. In contrast, the proposed method demonstrates superior visual plausibility and spatial consistency in stylized images. The attention map plays a crucial role in maintaining focus on salient regions of the content images, ensuring that important attributes like eyes and key objects are preserved in the stylization process.

Quantitative evaluations further validate the effectiveness of the proposed method, with metrics showing significant improvements in attention consistency when compared to a baseline version without self-attention. The self-attention module captures essential characteristics of the content image, enabling better integration of multiple stroke patterns through the multi-scale style swap and multi-stroke modules.

Overall, the study emphasizes the importance of attention mechanisms in enhancing the quality of style transfer, illustrating that the proposed method outperforms existing techniques by maintaining visual coherence and detail consistency across stylized outputs.
