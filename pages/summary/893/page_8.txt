The paper presents an attention-aware multi-stroke style transfer model designed to enhance the stylization of images while maintaining visual consistency with their content. The authors utilize a self-attention mechanism to generate attention maps, enabling the integration of multiple stroke patterns through a multi-scale style swap and flexible stroke fusion strategy.

In terms of runtime performance, the proposed method shows competitive efficiency compared to existing feed-forward methods, as demonstrated in Table 2. The model performs well on a 12G Tesla M40 GPU, with average processing times for three strokes being 0.80 seconds for 256x256 images and 0.94 seconds for 512x512 images. The results indicate a manageable increase in runtime with additional strokes, highlighting a balance between efficiency and detail diversity.

The paper introduces two key features: multi-stroke fusion control and automatic spatial stroke size control. The former allows for the integration of different stroke sizes and levels of detail, enhancing the variety of patterns in the stylized output. The latter automates the use of strokes of varying sizes based on the saliency of image regions, allowing finer strokes for more salient areas and coarser strokes for less important ones. This automatic approach contrasts with previous methods that relied on handcrafted masks for region-specific stylization.

The experimental results affirm the model's effectiveness in achieving favorable stylization effects while maintaining visual coherence with the content images. Future work may explore the relationship between stroke assignment and aesthetic factors to further improve the stylization process.
