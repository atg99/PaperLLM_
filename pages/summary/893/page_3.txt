The paper presents a novel approach to style transfer using a self-attention autoencoder designed to reconstruct images in a style-agnostic manner. The architecture integrates three main modules: multi-scale style swap, multi-stroke fusion, and a self-attention module, which work together to manipulate features effectively. 

The network takes a content image (Ic) and a style image (Is) as inputs. During the testing phase, the self-attention module generates a feature map (Ac) that captures salient characteristics of the content image. The multi-scale style swap module synthesizes features from multiple stroke sizes, allowing for a flexible style transfer while simultaneously applying style swaps across K style features. The multi-stroke fusion module then blends these features with the content feature, guided by the attention map, to create a synthetic feature (fcs). 

After processing through these modules, the synthetic feature is fed into the decoder to produce the final stylized image (Io) in a single feed-forward pass. The use of skip connections enhances the stylization effects by incorporating multiple levels of synthetic and style features. The paper emphasizes the effectiveness of the self-attention mechanism in capturing relationships across spatial regions, thus improving the quality of the style transfer process. Overall, the proposed method aims for efficient and high-quality image stylization.
