The paper presents an attention-aware multi-stroke (AAMS) model aimed at enhancing arbitrary style transfer in images by addressing limitations in previous methods. Traditional approaches, such as the StrokePyramid, struggled with local content awareness and flexibility, leading to a lack of detail and uniform stroke patterns in the stylized output.

The AAMS model enhances visual attention consistency between the content and stylized images by employing a self-attention mechanism within an autoencoder framework. This mechanism enables the model to capture long-range dependencies and salient features across the image, producing an attention map that highlights critical areas and maintains consistent attention for different regions. Furthermore, the model introduces a multi-scale style swap module that facilitates the blending of various stroke patterns by correlating receptive fields with stroke sizes.

Key contributions of the paper include:
1. The introduction of a self-attention mechanism to capture essential characteristics and relationships in the input image.
2. The development of a multi-scale style swap to overcome fixed receptive field limitations, generating diverse stroke patterns.
3. A flexible fusion strategy that harmoniously integrates multiple stroke patterns into specific spatial regions, ensuring attention consistency between the content and stylized images.

In comparison to existing methods, the AAMS model efficiently handles the integration of multi-stroke patterns and demonstrates superior performance in generating stylized images with varied stroke characteristics. The paper also discusses related works in neural style transfer and attention models, highlighting the advancements made by the proposed approach.
