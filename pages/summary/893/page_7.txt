The paper discusses an advanced method for artistic style transfer using an autoencoder architecture enhanced by a self-attention module and a multi-stroke fusion strategy. The authors first evaluate the performance of their approach, referred to as AAMS, against a baseline method, AAMS(-SA), which lacks the self-attention mechanism. The results indicate that AAMS outperforms AAMS(-SA) by providing a more visually appealing output through two key improvements: the emphasis on salient regions using fine-grained strokes and the effective integration of diverse stroke patterns without losing the overall coherence of the image.

The paper also highlights the role of a multi-scale style swap module, which enables the generation of multiple stroke patterns, enhancing the richness and variability of the stylized results. The stroke size can be adjusted to control the level of detail in the final output.

To quantitatively assess the proposed method, the authors conducted user studies comparing AAMS with other competing methods including AdaIN, WCT, and Avatar-Net. Participants evaluated the stylized images based on how well they transferred style characteristics and preserved content fidelity. The results, which included a total of 600 votes from 30 subjects, showed that AAMS was preferred for both stylization effects and content preservation.

Additionally, the authors employed saliency metrics to further measure the effectiveness of their method in maintaining attention consistency between the original and stylized images. Overall, the findings demonstrate that the integration of self-attention and multi-stroke fusion significantly enhances the quality of artistic style transfer.
