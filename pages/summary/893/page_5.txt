This paper presents a method for enhancing regional homogeneity in feature representation through a Gaussian kernel convolution layer, which helps to create an attention map that highlights salient regions in content images. The attention map is normalized to a range of [0,1], effectively amplifying the influence of salient regions while maintaining correlations among distant areas. The authors introduce two types of features: swapped features from a multi-scale style swap and a whitened content feature, leading to a total of K+1 features for multi-stroke fusion.

To facilitate a scalable integration of arbitrary strokes, the authors propose a flexible fusion strategy. This involves clustering the attention map into multiple segments using a k-means approach to minimize the Euclidean distance between cluster centers and their elements. The multi-stroke fusion is then formulated to combine content features with the swapped features guided by the attention map.

The paper details the implementation of a self-attention autoencoder trained on the MS-COCO dataset, with specific architectural choices such as using layers from the VGG-19 model for feature extraction. The training process is elaborated, including the use of perceptual loss and various hyperparameters to balance different loss components.

In qualitative evaluations, the authors compare their method against four existing state-of-the-art techniques for arbitrary style transfer. They highlight the limitations of these methods, such as Style-Swap's reliance on patch similarity leading to poor style transfer and AdaIN's tendency to produce similar textures across different images. The authors argue that their approach offers a more nuanced and effective stylization process, as demonstrated in the results presented.
