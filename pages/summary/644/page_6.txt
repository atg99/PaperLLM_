In this section, the paper categorizes graph convolutional neural networks into spectral-based and spatial-based methods, focusing on spectral methods. The initial significant spectral-based model, introduced by Bruna et al., constructs spectral convolutional layers that process input feature maps to yield output maps. However, this method faces several challenges, including high computational complexity due to the need for eigenvalue decomposition of the graph Laplacian, which scales as O(n^3) for large graphs. Even with pre-computed eigenvectors, the complexity remains O(n^2), and the model requires O(n) parameters per layer.

To mitigate these issues, Bruna et al. suggest using a rank-r approximation of the eigenvalue decomposition, which retains only the first r eigenvectors and reduces the number of parameters significantly. This approach may also provide some localization if the graph has clustering structures. Building on this work, Henaff et al. propose using input smoothing kernels to improve spatial localization, but computational complexities persist.

Defferrard et al. introduce ChebNet, which employs K-polynomial filters for better localization within the vertex domain, integrating features from K-hop neighborhoods. This reduces the number of trainable parameters to O(K) and addresses computational efficiency through Chebyshev polynomial approximations. These approximations allow for recursive calculations of spectral graph convolutions while normalizing the filters to keep scaled eigenvalues within a manageable range.

Overall, the paper highlights the evolution of spectral graph convolutional models, addressing their limitations and proposing innovative solutions to enhance efficiency and localization.
