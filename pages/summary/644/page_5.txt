The paper discusses graph filtering, a process that allows localized operations on graph signals, drawing an analogy to traditional signal processing. It begins by defining the Fourier transform of a graph signal, which is expressed in the spectral domain. The inverse graph Fourier transform is also introduced, highlighting the relationship between vertex and spectral domains.

The authors outline two main types of graph filtering: frequency filtering and vertex filtering. 

1. **Frequency Filtering**: This is likened to convolution in classic signal processing. In this context, the convolution in the time domain corresponds to the multiplication of spectral representations in the spectral domain. The mathematical representation of this frequency filtering on a graph signal is provided, indicating how it can be expressed through spectral convolution.

2. **Vertex Filtering**: This approach involves a linear combination of signal components from a node's neighborhood. The formula for vertex filtering at a specific node incorporates the K-hop neighborhood and weights for the combination. The paper indicates that frequency filtering can be interpreted through the vertex filtering lens using K-polynomial filters.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the complexities of graph signal processing due to the irregular structure of graphs, while establishing foundational equations for both frequency and vertex filtering.
