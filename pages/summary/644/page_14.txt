The paper discusses the challenges of encoding intrinsic graph structures in specific learning tasks and contrasts this with the effectiveness of graph convolutional networks (GCNs) in computer vision applications. It categorizes these applications based on data types: 

1. **Images**: Image classification is essential in various applications. GCNs can process unstructured images by converting them into structured graph data using methods like kNN similarity graphs. Significant contributions include models for visual question answering that leverage multiple image facts, enhancing the accuracy of responses. Visual relationships among objects in images are also explored, with GCNs being utilized for tasks such as visual relationship detection and image captioning. Additionally, attentional models have been proposed to focus on reliable visual relationships while diminishing the influence of unreliable ones.

2. **Videos**: Action recognition in videos is highlighted as a crucial application for understanding video content. Various models, including spatial-temporal GCNs, have been developed to improve expressive power without relying on hand-crafted part assignments. Other approaches involve skeleton-based methods and space-time region graphs to facilitate action recognition.

3. **Point Clouds**: Point clouds are used for flexible geometric representation in computer graphics and vision. Following the advancements of PointNet, state-of-the-art deep neural networks have emerged that focus on local features of point clouds.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the transformative potential of GCNs across different media types, improving various tasks within computer vision.
