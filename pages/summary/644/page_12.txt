The paper discusses advancements in graph convolutional networks (GCNs) and their variants, particularly addressing the challenges associated with deeper models. It notes that while minibatch training can be facilitated by uniformly sampling neighboring nodes, deeper GCNs often suffer from degraded performance in node representation learning. Empirical evidence suggests that a two-layer GCN typically yields optimal results, as more layers lead to less distinguishable representations among nodes from different clusters due to Laplacian smoothing.

Xu et al. provide insights into the differing propagation behaviors of nodes in various graph structures. They highlight that nodes in dense regions spread features more rapidly than those in tree-like structures, resulting in indistinguishable representations. To address these issues, they propose the Jumping Knowledge Network, which incorporates skip connections from residual networks to allow adaptive selection of intermediate layer aggregations. This model can utilize various aggregators such as concatenation, max-pooling, and LSTM-attention, and is compatible with existing GCN frameworks.

The paper also places GCNs within the broader context of graph neural networks, noting that they are a specific type characterized by convolutional aggregations. It briefly mentions other variants, such as gated graph neural networks and graph attention networks. An early model in this domain is referenced, which defines parametric local transition and output functions for node representations. The paper critiques the inefficiency of iterative state updates in traditional methods, contrasting it with the Stochastic Steady State Estimation approach that seeks to learn node representations more effectively.
