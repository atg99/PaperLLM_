The paper systematically reviews graph convolutional networks (GCNs), categorizing existing models into spectral-based and spatial-based methods. It emphasizes the importance of understanding the underlying graph structure and signal representation in GCNs. The authors discuss the mathematical foundations of GCNs, including notations related to graphs, adjacency matrices, and the Laplacian matrix, which are essential for comprehending the operations performed by GCNs.

The survey also outlines various applications of GCNs, showcasing their utility across different domains. It identifies key challenges within current models, such as scalability and interpretability, and suggests promising future research directions to address these challenges. The concluding remarks encapsulate the survey's findings, reinforcing the significance of GCNs in advancing machine learning on graph-structured data.
