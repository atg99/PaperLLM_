Graph convolutional networks (GCNs) are increasingly utilized for various natural language processing (NLP) tasks, particularly in relation extraction and event extraction. Marcheggiani et al. introduced a syntactic GCN model that leverages syntactic dependency trees, proving effective for semantic role labeling and neural machine translation. These networks can enhance semantic machine translation by incorporating semantic biases into sentence encoders, leading to performance improvements. Additionally, a dilated iterated GCN model has been developed for dependency parsing.

In the realm of science, GCNs have significant applications in particle physics, where they are used to classify jets arising from energetic hadrons. Recent advancements include message-passing neural networks that distinguish between quantum chromodynamics-based and W-boson-based jets. ParticleNet, utilizing edge convolutions, is another specialized architecture for jet tagging. GCNs have also been applied for IceCube signal classification and in predicting physical dynamics through hierarchical graph representations.

Within chemistry, biology, and materials science, GCNs are employed for molecular fingerprint prediction and drug discovery, such as in the DeepChemStable model for predicting chemical stability. By integrating protein-protein and drug-protein interactions into multimodal graphs, GCNs can forecast polypharmacy side effects. Moreover, they are instrumental in predicting molecular properties through frameworks like Message-Passing Neural Networks and PotentialNet, which focus on learning features from chemical bonds and spatial distances. In protein interface prediction, GCNs construct graphs from protein residues, utilizing convolution layers to enhance prediction accuracy, which is crucial for drug discovery.
