The paper by Zhang et al. discusses the evolution and significance of graph convolutional networks (GCNs) within the broader context of graph neural networks. GCNs are highlighted as powerful neural network architectures capable of leveraging graph structures to aggregate information from node neighborhoods. This approach has proven effective across various tasks and applications, showcasing their expressive power in learning graph representations.

The authors note the emergence of multiple types of graph neural networks, including graph auto-encoders, generative models, attention models, and recurrent neural networks. They reference existing surveys that cover mathematical foundations and early approaches to geometric deep learning, alongside specific focuses on various types of graph neural networks.

However, the authors argue that many of these surveys may not encompass the latest advancements in GCNs due to the rapid development in this research area. Thus, the paper aims to fill this gap by reviewing contemporary literature on GCNs and outlining recent progress.

The main contributions of the survey include the introduction of two taxonomies for categorizing GCN models: one based on the type of convolution (spectral-based and spatial-based) and another based on application domains. The authors motivate each taxonomy through a comprehensive discussion of current GCN models, providing an organized overview of the field.
