The paper discusses various approaches to graph convolutional networks (GCNs), focusing on learnable kernel functions and the patch operator. The patch operator is defined as a summation over neighboring nodes, allowing for flexibility in the design of GCN models, particularly in MoNet, which generalizes many existing models. SplineCNN is mentioned as a related framework that employs B-spline convolution kernels.

For graphs with edge attributes, an edge-conditioned convolution (ECC) is introduced, which utilizes a filter-generating network to produce edge-specific weight matrices. This allows the convolution operation to adapt based on edge attributes, enhancing the model's flexibility.

Additionally, GraphSAGE is presented as an aggregation-based inductive representation learning model. It operates by aggregating representations from a node's neighbors, concatenating this aggregated representation with the node's own representation, and passing it through a fully connected layer. Various aggregator functions are available, including mean, LSTM, and pooling aggregators, with the mean aggregator simplifying the process to resemble traditional GCN models. The mathematical formulations for these operations are provided, illustrating the intricacies of representation learning in graph neural networks.
