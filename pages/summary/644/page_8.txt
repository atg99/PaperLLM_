The paper discusses advancements in Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) aimed at improving training efficiency and model performance. Zhang et al. introduce a top-down approach to construct GCN layers, complemented by a layer-wise sampler to mitigate neighborhood over-expansion and reduce variance through explicit importance sampling.

In addition to Chebyshev polynomial-based models, alternative localized polynomial filters have emerged. Levie et al. propose the CayleyNet model, which uses Cayley polynomials to enhance filter approximation, particularly in community-structured graphs where eigenvalues tend to cluster in narrow bands. The Cayley filters allow for specialized frequency band detection through a spectral zoom parameter and can achieve localization and linear complexity with Jacobi approximation.

LanczosNet is another notable model that addresses multi-scale characteristics in graphs, utilizing the Lanczos algorithm for low-rank matrix approximation to simplify computations involving the graph Laplacian. The spectral filter in LanczosNet incorporates both short-scale and long-scale parameters to capture diverse scale information.

Furthermore, Xu et al. advocate for the use of spectral wavelet transforms on graphs to accommodate varying scales. Recognizing that conventional graph structures may not optimally support all learning tasks, Li et al. propose a spectral graph convolution layer capable of learning the graph Laplacian concurrently, enhancing the adaptability of GCNs for specific applications.
