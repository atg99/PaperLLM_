The paper discusses various advancements in graph convolutional networks (GCNs) applied to point cloud segmentation and natural language processing (NLP). In the context of point clouds, traditional methods often overlook geometric relationships, while EdgeConv effectively captures local geometric structures, outperforming other techniques in segmentation tasks. A regularized GCN model updates the graph Laplacian dynamically to enhance feature connectivity, and FeaStNet adapts filter weights based on graph neighborhoods. Additionally, Wang et al. introduce a local spectral GCN for classification and segmentation, and Valsesia et al. develop a generative model for 3D point cloud creation.

For mesh applications, the paper highlights shape correspondence, with several GCN approaches emerging beyond classic CNN methods. Litany et al. combine GCNs with variational auto-encoders for shape completion.

In NLP, the paper outlines the use of GCNs for text classification by modeling documents as nodes in a citation network. Various models have been proposed for node classification, while others focus on graph-level classification. TextGCN integrates word and document embeddings in a heterogeneous graph for improved classification. Gao et al. enhance performance by incorporating node ordering through hybrid convolutions. The paper also addresses information extraction, noting that GraphIE employs recurrent neural networks to create context-aware representations and captures non-local dependencies for tasks like named entity extraction.
