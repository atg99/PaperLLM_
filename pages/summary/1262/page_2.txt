The paper discusses advancements in neural network (NN) ensemble methods aimed at mitigating forgetting phenomena and enhancing performance by decomposing complex tasks into smaller subtasks. Researchers advocate for smaller NN architectures that lead to shorter training times and improved generalization abilities, as illustrated in the ensemble structure where multiple NNs are trained on the same dataset and their outputs are combined. 

Key challenges include designing appropriate learning algorithms for NN ensembles, which depend on factors such as initial weights, NN topology, and training datasets. The paper highlights the limitations of existing algorithms, such as manual design and predefined parameters, which can hinder flexibility and effectiveness. 

The authors distinguish between ensemble and modular approaches, noting that while ensembles combine outputs from various trained NNs for a reliable result, modular approaches assign distinct tasks to individual NNs. Nonetheless, determining optimal NN sizes and topologies remains complex and often relies on expert knowledge or empirical methods.

The research aims to create an NN ensemble that automatically determines the architecture, size of individual NNs, and training examples to enhance learning. The effectiveness and generalization ability of the proposed ensemble are tested on real-world classification problems. The paper is structured to cover related works, the description of the proposed DEL algorithm, dataset details, experimental results, and concluding discussions.
