The paper discusses advancements in neural network (NN) ensembles, particularly focusing on the Dynamic Ensemble Learning (DEL) approach. Traditional methods typically fix the number of hidden neurons and train individual networks either independently or sequentially, which limits interaction and diversity among the networks. In contrast, DEL introduces a dynamic methodology that allows for the adaptive determination of both the number and architecture of individual networks within the ensemble, promoting enhanced diversity.

The DEL approach employs a constructive strategy for the automatic selection of the number of networks and a hybrid constructive-pruning strategy for architecture determination. This innovative framework addresses the need for diversity by encouraging individual networks to explore different regions of the data space through the use of varied training sets. However, it is noted that if networks attempt to learn different areas with inadequate architectures, it can lead to insufficient learning outcomes.

The paper includes empirical results showcasing the effectiveness of DEL, emphasizing improved diversity and performance in ensemble training, as illustrated in various datasets such as cancer and glass datasets. Correlation metrics among networks are also presented, demonstrating the relationships and diversity achieved through this dynamic approach. Overall, DEL represents a significant shift in ensemble design, aiming to optimize the interaction and performance of neural networks.
