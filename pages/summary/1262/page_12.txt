The paper compares the performance of the DEL algorithm against several established ensemble learning methods, including the empirical study by Opitz and Maclin, the semi-supervised algorithm SemiNCL by Chen et al., and the fully semi-supervised approaches CBoost-Sup and CBoost-Semi by Soares et al. Opitz and Maclin's study involved various networks and ensemble techniques like Bagging and Boosting, achieving a 95% confidence level with ensemble methods over single classifiers. However, they did not include certain datasets (thyroid, gene, horse, mushroom), resulting in unavailable comparison results. Similarly, Chen et al. and Soares et al. did not utilize datasets such as cancer, diabetes, and heart, leading to gaps in comparative data.

The discussion highlights that many existing ensemble learning methods rely on trial-and-error to determine the number and structure of neural networks (NNs) in the ensemble. This process typically involves a two-stage design: first creating individual NNs and then combining them. The paper illustrates training errors for NNs with different hidden layer configurations and the impact of hidden node addition on training performance across various datasets. Overall, the findings emphasize the need for more systematic approaches in designing ensemble networks.
