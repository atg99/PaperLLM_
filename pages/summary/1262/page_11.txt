The paper discusses the training dynamics of individual neural networks (NNs) through pruning and constructive strategies, particularly focusing on their application to cancer datasets. The initial architectures of the networks are noted as (9-12-2-2) and (9-12-2), which evolve to (9-10-2-2) and (9-10-2) after training. Figures illustrate the impact of hidden node deletion and the addition of networks during the training process, indicating that ensemble performance improves with the addition of networks, ultimately resulting in four networks in the ensemble.

The paper presents correlations among individual networks using correlation strength parameters k = 0.2 and k = 1.0. Tables show that with k = 0.2, correlations are generally positive, while with k = 1.0, negative correlations dominate. This analysis highlights the variability in network relationships based on the chosen correlation strength, emphasizing the importance of these dynamics in the performance of ensemble methods.

Figures also depict the training error profiles for various datasets, showcasing the performance differences between ensembles and individual networks across different architectures, thereby illustrating the effectiveness of the constructive algorithm in enhancing training outcomes.
