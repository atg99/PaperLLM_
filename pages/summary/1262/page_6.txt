The paper presents an experimental analysis of the DEL algorithm, evaluating its effectiveness and performance on real-world benchmark problems sourced from the UCI machine learning repository. The analysis involved various tests with different parameter settings, particularly focusing on the correlation strength parameter, k. When k is nonzero, DEL operates as outlined in the methodology, while a zero value indicates independent training of individual neural networks using standard backpropagation.

Key parameters such as the learning rate and correlation strength were set within specific ranges, and initial weights for the neural networks were randomly generated. The study employed a winner-takes-all classification method, utilizing both majority voting and simple averaging methods to assess the generalization ability of DEL.

The experimental dataset included medical datasets, which presented unique challenges such as reliance on expert input attributes for diagnosis, small sample sizes, and the presence of missing or noisy data. Four medical datasets were specifically analyzed:

1. **Breast Cancer Dataset**: Comprises 699 examples (458 benign, 241 malignant) with 9 attributes related to tumor characteristics, aimed at classifying tumors.
2. **Diabetes Dataset**: Contains 768 examples (500 class 1, 268 class 2) with 8 attributes, focusing on the classification of diabetes status in female patients of Pima Indian heritage.
3. **Heart Disease Dataset**: Includes 920 examples sourced from costly medical tests, featuring 35 attributes to classify the presence of heart disease.
4. **Thyroid Dataset**: Encompasses 7200 examples from clinical tests, with 21 attributes to classify into three functional categories.

The summary of benchmark datasets is presented in a table, detailing the number of examples, attributes, classes, and the split between training and test sets for each dataset. Overall, the analysis highlights the complexities of medical data classification and the application of DEL in addressing these challenges.
