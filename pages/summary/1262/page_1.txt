This paper introduces a dynamic ensemble learning (DEL) algorithm designed for constructing ensembles of neural networks (NNs). The DEL algorithm automates the ensemble design process by determining the appropriate size of the ensemble, the number of individual NNs, the number of hidden nodes in each NN, and the training samples used for learning. To enhance diversity among the NNs, the paper incorporates negative correlation learning and varied training samples, which collectively improve learning outcomes. 

Key advantages of the DEL approach over traditional ensemble algorithms include the automatic design of the ensemble, simultaneous maintenance of accuracy and diversity, and a reduced number of user-defined parameters. The algorithm is evaluated across various real-world classification problems, including datasets related to cancer, diabetes, heart disease, and others, demonstrating its ability to create dynamic NN ensembles with effective architectures and diversity, leading to good generalization performance.

The introduction outlines the limitations of monolithic NN structures, such as scalability issues and problems with incremental learning, notably catastrophic forgetting. The paper discusses various strategies previously proposed to address these challenges, emphasizing the potential benefits of using ensemble methods. The DEL algorithm represents a significant advancement in overcoming the limitations associated with traditional NN approaches, offering a more flexible and robust solution for various applications.
