The paper discusses the design of neural network (NN) ensembles, emphasizing the importance of accuracy and diversity in improving ensemble performance. It highlights that accurate and diverse NNs can effectively distribute errors across various regions of the input space. Rosen's proposed algorithm trains individual NNs sequentially, minimizing training errors while decorrelating them from previous errors, although this can lead to a lack of correlation among individual NN errors.

The paper critiques the mixtures-of-experts (ME) approach for requiring a separate gating NN and lacking balance in the bias-variance-covariance tradeoff. It points out that traditional two-stage designs—where individual NNs are first created and then combined—often result in some NNs not contributing significantly to the ensemble. To address this, a one-stage design process with a penalty term in the error function is proposed, allowing for simultaneous and interactive training of NNs, promoting specialization and cooperation.

Evolutionary computing is suggested as a means to ensure interaction and simultaneous learning among NNs. Liu et al. used an evolutionary algorithm to optimize the number of NNs and their combinations, while Chen and Yao employed a multi-objective genetic algorithm for regularized negative correlation learning (NCL) to enhance both accuracy and diversity.

The paper also introduces two approaches: constructive NN ensemble (CNNE), which incrementally determines the number of NNs and their hidden neurons, and pruning NN ensemble (PNNE), which employs a competitive decay approach to manage overfitting and ensure diversity. Incremental learning algorithms like NegBagg and NegBoost are discussed, showcasing their constructive methods for fixing the number of hidden neurons in the ensemble.

Additionally, the dynamic ensemble of ensembles (DE2) and a two-phase dynamic ensemble selection (DES) framework are presented as advanced methods for ensemble learning. Chen and Yao further propose regularized NCL (RNCL) to improve performance by incorporating regularization into the training process.

The paper concludes with a discussion on semi-supervised learning, illustrating its effectiveness in utilizing both labeled and unlabeled data. Chen and Wang's semi-supervised boosting framework is highlighted for its constant performance improvement. The balance between accuracy and diversity in ensembles is crucial, and the proposed semi-supervised NCL (SemiNCL) aims to address this by incorporating a correlation penalty in the cost function.
