The paper discusses the limitations of semi-supervised learning, particularly its sensitivity to incorrectly labeled data. To address this, Soares et al. introduced a cluster-based boosting method called CBoost, which enhances generalization ability through cluster regularization and semi-supervised optimization. The authors also reference recent advancements in dynamic classifier selection and ensemble methods.

Dynamic Ensemble Learning (DEL) is presented as a novel approach that differs from fixed ensemble architectures by automatically determining the number of base neural networks (NNs) and their configurations during the training phase. The DEL algorithm follows an eight-step process:

1. An initial ensemble of two NNs is created with a basic architecture, employing a dynamic node creation method for the first NN and a pruning algorithm for the second.
2. Separate training examples are generated for each NN by randomly selecting subsets from the main training set, with one NN learning in a forward order and the other in reverse.
3. The NNs are trained partially for a user-defined number of epochs using Negative Correlation Learning (NCL), regardless of convergence.
4. The training error for each NN is computed using a specific formula that combines elements from previous research, ensuring it is independent of training set size.
5. The ensemble error is calculated as the average of the individual NNs' errors. If this error is deemed acceptable, the final ensemble is produced; if not, the architecture may be adjusted.

Overall, the DEL approach is designed to improve the adaptability and generalization capability of ensemble learning methods.
