The paper discusses the integration of deep learning (DL) techniques with physics-based approaches to enhance materials science applications. It emphasizes the significance of physics-based regularization in improving model performance and addresses the role of DL, particularly through graph neural networks and symbolic regression, in discovering symbolic equations that describe physical phenomena.

Key aspects for successful DL applications in materials science include the need for large, diverse datasets, the selection of appropriate DL methodologies, and relevant performance metrics. The paper categorizes materials science applications based on input data types: atomistic, stoichiometric, spectral, image, and text, summarizing the impact of DL in these areas.

In atomistic representations, the paper highlights various DL methods for addressing challenges posed by the complexity of atomic structures. It reviews approaches such as force-field development, property predictions, and materials screening, noting the advantages of DL methods over traditional machine learning in terms of efficiency and accuracy. The use of databases and software libraries for atomistic DL models is also discussed, underscoring the evolution of computational methods.

The development of DL-based force fields is noted for its speed and efficiency in making predictions, although it requires substantial datasets from computationally intensive methods. Various neural network architectures, such as Behler-Parrinello and graph neural networks, are employed to create interatomic potentials applicable across different conditions.

Additionally, DL methods are used to establish structure-property relationships, achieving high accuracy in predicting various material properties based on atomic configurations. Models like SchNet and ALIGNN are highlighted for their performance in predicting properties such as formation energies and electronic characteristics. The ongoing efforts to refine these models and their applications in predicting catalytic behavior are also mentioned, indicating a significant advancement in materials design through DL methodologies.
