The paper discusses the development and curation of extensive databases for X-ray absorption spectroscopy (XAS), specifically focusing on K-edge and L-edge XANES spectra. The Materials Project hosts over 400,000 K-edge and 90,000 L-edge site-wise spectra, while experimental XAS data remains limited, numbering in the hundreds. Collaborative efforts aim to compile data from various sources, including the RRUFF and SOP spectra libraries, but consistency is a concern. The MPContribs platform allows contributors to share experimental data, supported by the US Department of Energy, ensuring some degree of data persistence.

Advancements in technology have enabled high-throughput measurements, leading to significant databases such as HTEM and UV-Vis, which contain tens of thousands of optical absorption spectra. The emergence of cloud-based platforms facilitates data sharing and analysis, enhancing collaboration within the materials science community.

Applications of deep learning (DL) models in analyzing XRD spectra are highlighted, where phase identification is approached as a classification or clustering task. Despite challenges in accessing comprehensive experimental datasets, simulated XRD patterns from the Inorganic Crystal Structure Database (ICSD) have been successfully utilized. CNN models have demonstrated high accuracy in classifying structural information based on these simulated data.

The paper emphasizes the importance of filtering duplicate entries in the ICSD and addresses normalization challenges due to uneven representation of structures across space groups. A CNN model developed by Lee et al. showcases the capability to identify phases and predict compound fractions from complex mixtures relevant to battery materials.

Overall, the paper illustrates the integration of extensive databases, advanced measurement techniques, and deep learning methodologies to enhance the analysis and understanding of material structures through XRD and XAS data.
