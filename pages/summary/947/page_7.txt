The paper discusses advancements in property prediction models for bio-materials, particularly proteins, and their applications in materials science using deep learning (DL) techniques. It highlights various methods like deep learning autoencoders and reinforcement learning for inverse materials design, which complements traditional forward prediction models. The limitations of density functional theory (DFT) in screening materials are addressed, with DL-based models providing a faster alternative for pre-screening chemical compounds. Specific examples include the use of CGCNN and iCGCNN models for screening stable materials and predicting thermoelectric properties. 

The paper also notes the challenges of obtaining precise atomic positions for deep learning models, leading to the exploration of atomic position-independent descriptors. Techniques such as the Bayesian optimization method combined with the MEGNet energy model have been employed to discover new materials without requiring precise initial structures. Additionally, it discusses the use of descriptors like SMILES for molecules and elemental fractions for solids, which lack explicit atomic structure information but serve as effective representations for DL applications. 

Overall, the paper emphasizes the potential of DL methods in accelerating materials discovery and property prediction, particularly in the context of complex materials systems.
