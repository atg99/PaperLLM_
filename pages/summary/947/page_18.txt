The paper discusses the advancements in the field of thermoelectric materials and the importance of uncertainty quantification (UQ) in deep learning (DL) models used for material predictions. It highlights that previous predictions of thermoelectric compositions have been validated through computational and experimental methods. However, the challenge of directly predicting materials remains.

UQ is emphasized as a crucial aspect of evaluating the reliability of DL models, which often suffer from issues related to robustness and interpretability. The paper outlines that while current research primarily focuses on enhancing algorithm accuracy, there is a growing interest in UQ methodologies. The existing UQ implementations are limited as they often require retraining or modifications to models, which can be resource-intensive. Bayesian neural networks are noted as an exception, though they also demand significant adjustments and computational resources.

The authors point out that accurately estimating uncertainties in DL predictions can enhance model reliability, guide data collection, and help identify necessary training datasets. The paper distinguishes between two types of uncertainties: epistemic uncertainty, linked to insufficient training data, and aleatory uncertainty, which arises from unmodeled parameters. Common methods for evaluating uncertainty in DL include Dropout methods, Deep Ensemble methods, Quantile regression, and Gaussian Processes.

The paper also features a schematic representation of the application of a skip-gram variant of Word2vec in predicting context words, illustrating how word embeddings can be useful in various applications, including materials science. Overall, the work underscores the need for effective UQ strategies to improve the reliability of DL models in material discovery.
