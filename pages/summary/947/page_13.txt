The paper discusses the application of deep learning (DL) techniques to enhance the analysis of electron backscatter diffraction (EBSD) data and other materials science challenges. Liu et al. introduced a DL-based method for EBSD indexing that predicts three Euler angles from EBSD images, although they treated the angles independently. Jha et al. improved this by developing a single DL model that minimizes the misorientation angle, resulting in a 16% reduction in disorientation error compared to traditional methods. Kaufman et al. further advanced this field by using a CNN to identify space groups, enabling phase identification in unknown samples more efficiently than X-ray or neutron diffraction.

Recent advancements include using DL to learn crystal plasticity from strain profile images. One study integrated domain knowledge to enhance predictive accuracy, achieving 92.48% accuracy in identifying deformation history, significantly outperforming traditional methods. The capability to predict stress-strain curves was also demonstrated.

Pixelwise learning has emerged as a significant focus in materials science, allowing for detailed analysis of image features. Azimi et al. used a fully convolutional neural network to accurately segment various phases in carbon steel SEM images, achieving 94% accuracy. Other studies applied different models, such as PixelNet, to segment microstructural constituents with high precision.

The paper also mentions a variety of databases and software packages available for applying DL methods in image analysis, facilitating further research and development in this field. Overall, the studies indicate the promising potential of DL to improve EBSD experiments and materials characterization methodologies.
