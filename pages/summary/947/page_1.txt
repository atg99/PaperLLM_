This review article explores the recent advances and applications of deep learning (DL) methods in materials science, emphasizing their role in analyzing diverse data types including atomistic, image-based, spectral, and textual data. The authors highlight how the growth of large materials databases has spurred the use of DL, particularly in atomistic predictions, while advancements in image and spectral data have benefited from high-quality synthetic data and generative unsupervised DL techniques.

The article provides an overview of DL methodologies and discusses their applications in atomistic simulation, materials imaging, spectral analysis, and natural language processing. It covers both theoretical and experimental data, outlining typical modeling approaches along with their strengths and limitations, and points to relevant publicly available software and datasets.

The review also addresses the challenges of linking processing, structure, property, and performance in materials science, noting the complexity introduced by varying length and time scales. It emphasizes the necessity of automated analysis due to the rapid expansion of materials datasets, facilitated by the Materials Genome Initiative and adherence to FAIR principles.

The authors argue that DL serves as a complementary approach to traditional physics-based methods, offering significant advantages in speed and efficiency. They discuss the potential of DL to enhance materials design, traditionally reliant on trial and error, and suggest that techniques like transfer learning and active learning can make DL applicable even with small datasets.

In conclusion, the article highlights ongoing challenges and limitations in the field, while identifying opportunities for further growth and integration of DL methods in materials science, particularly in uncertainty quantification and the exploration of new methodologies.
