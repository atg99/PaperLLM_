The paper discusses the challenges and advancements in accessing and processing scientific literature, particularly in the fields of materials science and chemistry. Access to full-text articles often necessitates strict agreements with publishers, while preprint servers and patents typically offer less restrictive access but are often in PDF format, which is difficult to parse accurately. The need for tools that can convert PDFs into structured HTML format is emphasized as a significant opportunity for improvement.

The application of Natural Language Processing (NLP) to scientific literature involves multiple steps, including data retrieval, pre-processing, and machine learning for information extraction. Although general-purpose NLP libraries exist, the specialized nature of chemistry and materials science texts requires domain-specific tools to accurately process complex chemical formulas and scientific notation. These specialized tools enhance the reliability of data extraction and improve the performance of machine learning models.

NLP methods have been effectively applied for information extraction and materials discovery, automating the extraction of datasets previously requiring labor-intensive manual entry. Tools like ChemDataExtractor enable the collection of important data such as magnetic transition temperatures and battery properties. Recent advancements in deep learning, particularly using LSTMs and transformer models, have further enhanced the extraction of materials synthesis information.

The paper also highlights successful applications of NLP in discovering new materials with desirable properties, such as the identification of candidate dyes for solar cells through automated text mining. This approach underscores the potential of literature-based extraction to uncover materials that may not have been previously tested for specific applications.

Additionally, the possibility of directly predicting materials applications using word embeddings trained on materials science literature is explored. This innovative method could yield predictions about materials applications without the need for intermediary models, demonstrating the transformative potential of NLP in materials science.

In conclusion, the paper illustrates the critical role of NLP in enhancing access to and analysis of scientific literature, ultimately facilitating advancements in materials discovery and application.
