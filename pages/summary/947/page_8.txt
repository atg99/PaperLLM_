This paper discusses the use of Simplified Molecular Input Line Entry System (SMILES) and chemical formula representations for machine learning (ML) and deep learning (DL) applications in molecular design and screening. SMILES allows for a compact representation of molecular structures, including information on chirality and bonding types, which can be transformed into a SMILES feature matrix for DL applications. Key tasks in this domain include designing novel molecules using techniques like Variational Autoencoders (VAE), Generative Adversarial Networks (GAN), and Recurrent Neural Networks (RNN), as well as predicting molecular toxicity. While traditional SMILES models may struggle to generate physically valid molecules, fragment-based models like DeepFrag have been developed to improve accuracy by focusing on suitable fragment substitutions.

Chemical formula representations can also be employed in ML/DL models, ranging from simple elemental fractions to more complex descriptors that incorporate chemical knowledge. Notably, ElemNet, a 17-layer neural network, demonstrated superior performance over traditional ML methods by learning chemistry patterns directly from raw elemental data, even without periodic table information. This highlights the potential of DL for effective representation learning. To overcome challenges like the vanishing gradient problem, models such as IRNet have been introduced, allowing for deeper learning and improved accuracy across various datasets.

Graph-based methods, like Roost, have emerged as powerful alternatives, successfully applied to diverse datasets for property prediction tasks. The paper emphasizes the versatility of these approaches in predicting various material properties and their utility in transfer learning scenarios where large datasets may not be available. Additionally, a comprehensive list of software tools for applying DL to chemical representations is provided, demonstrating the growing accessibility of these technologies in computational materials science.
