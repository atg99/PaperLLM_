The paper discusses the growing significance of deep learning (DL) methods in materials science and engineering (MSE), highlighting their potential to achieve accuracy levels comparable to traditional physics-based models. It emphasizes the accessibility of DL research due to open data and computational resources, as well as well-documented libraries that facilitate entry for newcomers. Despite the advantages, the paper notes the challenges posed by the black-box nature of DL models, which complicates interpretability and the extraction of scientific insights. 

The authors provide an overview of machine learning (ML) concepts, positioning DL as a subset of ML that employs multilayer neural networks. They outline common ML techniques and the difficulties associated with applying them to unstructured data. The paper introduces neural networks, starting with the perceptron as a fundamental component, and mentions popular software packages for training neural networks, such as PyTorch and TensorFlow. Additionally, a GitHub repository is provided to keep resources updated as the field evolves.

Overall, the article aims to motivate materials scientists to explore DL applications by presenting basic concepts, terminology, and relevant databases to facilitate their research endeavors.
