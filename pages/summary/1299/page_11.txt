The paper discusses the evaluation metrics used in a recommendation system model, focusing on Root Mean Square Error (RMSE), precision, and recall. RMSE is emphasized for its ability to highlight larger absolute errors, indicating that a lower RMSE reflects a more accurate prediction of user reviews. Precision is defined as the ratio of relevant recommended items to the total recommended items, while recall measures the ratio of relevant items that are recommended to all relevant items available. 

In the results section, the model's performance is assessed using the ML-1M and ML-10M datasets. The loss and validation loss metrics show a decreasing trend over epochs, stabilizing without intersecting due to the dropout layer's effect, which prevents overfitting. The ML-10M dataset exhibits lower loss values compared to ML-1M. Additionally, the precision and recall values improve over epochs, with a threshold of 0.6 set for both metrics.
