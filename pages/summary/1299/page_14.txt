The paper discusses the challenges retail organizations face in adapting to rapidly changing consumer demands and the importance of innovation and personalization in attracting customers. It highlights the potential of recommendation systems as a strategy to enhance sales and customer loyalty. With the rise of Generation Y, characterized by their tech-savviness, there is a pressing need for continuous improvement in these systems.

The study specifically explores the effectiveness of autoencoders in generating product recommendations and compares it with the widely used Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) technique. While SVD demonstrated faster execution times, the autoencoder achieved lower Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) values, particularly benefiting from larger datasets. The research also emphasizes the necessity of dropout techniques in neural networks to prevent overfitting.

The paper acknowledges the limitations of collaborative filtering, particularly its dependency on sufficient data and user interaction for accurate recommendations. To address these limitations, future research could explore hybrid filtering methods and test the model with new datasets. Additionally, the authors suggest expanding the scope of recommendations to include discount coupons based on user interests, which could further enhance sales and customer satisfaction, ultimately providing a competitive edge for companies.
