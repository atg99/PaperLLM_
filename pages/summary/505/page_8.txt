The authors emphasize the significance of selecting appropriate pretext tasks for self-supervised learning, particularly in the context of computer vision and natural language processing (NLP). They note that certain transformations like scaling and aspect ratio changes may introduce visual artifacts that hinder performance, pointing out that rotation-based tasks can be ineffective when dealing with datasets characterized by color textures, as illustrated with the DTD dataset.

In the realm of NLP, self-supervised learning has evolved through various pretext tasks designed to generate labels for unsupervised problems, allowing models to be pretrained. The paper highlights two major pretext tasks: 

1. **Center and Neighbor Word Prediction**: Introduced by Word2Vec, this approach includes two models. The continuous bag-of-words model utilizes "center word prediction," where a word is predicted from its context, while the skip-gram model employs "neighbor word prediction," predicting neighboring words from a given word. These tasks enable the model to learn effective word representations applicable to downstream tasks.

2. **Next and Neighbor Sentence Prediction**: In this task, models like BERT predict whether two sentences can be consecutive. Positive samples are consecutive sentences, while negative samples come from unrelated documents. This method has significantly enhanced performance in tasks requiring understanding of sentence relationships, such as question answering and language inference. 

Overall, the paper underscores the critical role of pretext tasks in advancing self-supervised learning methodologies in both computer vision and NLP.
