The memory bank approach mitigates the challenges associated with large mini-batch optimization by allowing the model to utilize a vast number of negative samples accumulated over time, rather than being restricted to the limited samples available in a single batch. This method involves creating a dictionary that regularly updates the embeddings of samples based on recent training epochs, creating a dynamic and extensive repository of feature representations.

In summary, the paper discusses the use of contrastive loss in training encoders to generate distinct representations of samples, highlighting the effectiveness of end-to-end models like SimCLR and the challenges posed by batch size limitations. It introduces the concept of a memory bank as a solution to these challenges, allowing for the accumulation of a larger set of negative samples to improve model training and performance.
