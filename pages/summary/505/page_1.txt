This paper provides a comprehensive review of contrastive self-supervised learning, a method gaining traction for its ability to circumvent the challenges of annotating large datasets. Contrastive learning focuses on embedding augmented versions of the same sample closely while distancing embeddings from different samples, making it a key technique in self-supervised learning across various domains, including computer vision and natural language processing.

The authors discuss commonly used pretext tasks within the contrastive framework and outline various architectures that have emerged. They also compare the performance of different methods across several downstream tasks, such as image classification, object detection, and action recognition. The paper concludes by addressing the limitations of current methods and suggesting areas for future research to enhance the effectiveness and applicability of contrastive self-supervised learning.

Overall, this review highlights the significance of self-supervised learning in overcoming the limitations of traditional supervised approaches, which often rely on extensive labeled data and face issues like generalization error and adversarial attacks. By leveraging unlabeled data through innovative pretext tasks and contrastive strategies, the field can continue to advance.
