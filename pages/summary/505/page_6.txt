The paper discusses various techniques for improving the learning of visual representations in video data through temporal coherence and contrastive learning. One approach involves using semantically related frames, where nearby frames in time are treated as positive samples while others serve as negative samples. This method aims to train models to recover the temporal order of shuffled video frames.

Additional strategies include sampling clips from longer videos and applying spatial augmentations, all governed by a contrastive loss function. This function ensures that similar clips from the same video are closer in the embedding space, while clips from different videos are pushed apart. The framework proposed by Qian et al. emphasizes contrasting positive samples—augmented clips from the same video—against negative samples to create distinct regions in the encoded representation space.

The paper also highlights future prediction tasks, which aim to forecast high-level information based on previous data points, commonly used in sequential data like videos and audio. Techniques involve compressing high-dimensional data into a lower-dimensional latent space while preserving mutual information, using autoregressive models to create context representations.

Furthermore, view prediction tasks are addressed for scenarios with multiple perspectives of the same scene. This approach encourages the model to bring together images taken from similar viewpoints while differentiating them from images captured at different times. The model learns to recognize similar features across frames and discern differences in sequences, enhancing its predictive capabilities across modalities.
