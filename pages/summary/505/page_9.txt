In the neighbor sentence prediction task, models are tasked with predicting both the preceding and following sentences based on a given sentence. This methodology, akin to the skip-gram approach, is exemplified in the Skip-Thought Vectors paper. Autoregressive language modeling involves predicting the subsequent word based on preceding words, a technique utilized in various n-gram models and neural networks, including the GPT series. 

The BART model introduces a sentence permutation task, where a continuous text span is divided into sentences, which are then randomly reordered. The model's objective is to ascertain the original sequence of these sentences.

Contrastive learning architectures are categorized based on how negative samples are collected relative to a positive data point. The four main architectures include End-to-End learning, Memory Bank, Momentum Encoder, and Clustering. 

End-to-End learning employs a gradient-based system with differentiable modules and large batch sizes to maximize negative sample acquisition. This architecture utilizes two encoders, a Query encoder and a Key encoder, which can differ and are updated through backpropagation during training. The overall goal is to refine the model's capacity to effectively differentiate between positive and negative representations.
