This method emphasizes the organization of feature spaces by grouping similar samples together, which enhances the model's ability to differentiate between various classes. By utilizing clustering techniques, this architecture enables the model to learn more robust representations without the need for additional memory management or the computational overhead associated with maintaining a memory bank.

In summary, the paper discusses various strategies for improving contrastive learning in visual representation tasks. It highlights the use of memory banks and momentum encoders to manage sample representations effectively while addressing the computational challenges associated with these methods. Ultimately, the introduction of a clustering-based approach offers a promising alternative that simplifies the training process and enhances representation learning through better organization of feature spaces.
