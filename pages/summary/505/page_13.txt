The paper discusses advancements in feature extraction and training methodologies for deep learning models, particularly focusing on contrastive learning. It highlights the use of ResNet architectures, specifically ResNet-50, for extracting features from images. Misra et al. demonstrated that later residual blocks (res5) provided superior representations compared to earlier blocks (res2, res3, res4). Similarly, Chen et al. utilized a traditional ResNet with features extracted from the average pooling layer, further processing them with a shallow MLP to apply contrastive loss for action recognition tasks.

Training these encoders involves a pretext task where contrastive loss is employed to differentiate between similar and dissimilar instances. The paper details the use of cosine similarity as a common metric for measuring the closeness of embeddings, which is foundational in various contrastive loss functions. Noise Contrastive Estimation (NCE) is introduced as a method to facilitate this learning, with a focus on distinguishing between actual samples and artificially generated noise.

The paper also outlines the InfoNCE variant for scenarios with multiple negative samples, emphasizing the importance of the temperature coefficient and l2 normalization in improving model performance. Finally, it notes the application of Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD) as a prevalent optimization algorithm in contrastive learning, with learning rate being a critical hyperparameter for effective training.
