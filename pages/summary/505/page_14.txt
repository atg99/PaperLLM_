The paper discusses various optimization methods used in deep learning, particularly focusing on the Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD) and its improved version with momentum, which is widely adopted in most deep learning approaches. It highlights the Adam optimizer, which incorporates momentum as an estimate of the first-order moment, and includes bias corrections for both first and second-order moments. 

The paper addresses challenges that arise when training with large batch sizes using standard SGD-based optimizers, noting that this can lead to instability. To counteract this, it introduces the Layer-wise Adaptive Rate Scaling (LARS) optimizer combined with a cosine learning rate strategy. LARS differentiates itself by utilizing unique learning rates for each layer and adjusting updates based on weight norms, enhancing training stability and control.

Furthermore, the paper outlines the concept of downstream tasks in computer vision that leverage knowledge gained from self-supervised learning through pretext tasks. These downstream tasks, such as classification, detection, and segmentation, benefit from the pretrained models, showcasing the generalization capabilities of learned features. The effectiveness of these features in downstream applications is evaluated using techniques like kernel visualization and nearest-neighbor approaches, which also assess the pretext task's contribution to model training.
