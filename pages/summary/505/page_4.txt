The paper discusses the role of pretext tasks in contrastive learning, specifically focusing on how original images serve as anchors while their augmented versions act as positive samples, with other images in the batch serving as negative samples. It categorizes the commonly used pretext tasks into four main types: color transformation, geometric transformation, context-based tasks, and cross-modal-based tasks, each applicable to various problem-solving scenarios.

In the section on color transformation, the paper explains that this involves basic color adjustments such as blurring and color distortions, which help the network learn to recognize similar images regardless of their color variations. An example from the ImageNet dataset illustrates this concept.

The geometric transformation section defines spatial modifications that alter the image's geometry without changing pixel information. Transformations like scaling, random cropping, and flipping are highlighted, with the original image representing the global view and the transformed image representing the local view. This approach is aimed at feature learning during the pretext task, as demonstrated by Chen et al.
