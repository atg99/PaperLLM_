The paper discusses a contrastive learning framework that employs both inter-sampling and intra-sampling techniques to enhance the learning of video frame sequences. Inter-sampling is achieved through multi-view representations of the same sample, while intra-sampling focuses on learning temporal relationships among frames using methods like frame repetition and shuffling, which serve as negative samples.

A critical aspect of the framework is the identification of the appropriate pre-text task, which is essential for the model's performance. The right pre-text task helps the model become invariant to specific transformations while maintaining the ability to discriminate between different data points. However, the introduction of biases through augmentations can lead to mixed outcomes. For example, while rotation can improve aerial image recognition, it may hinder performance on tasks that require orientation detection in photographs. Similarly, colorization tasks may not be effective for fine-grained classification, underscoring the necessity of selecting the right pre-text tasks for varied applications.
