This paper, presented at ICLR 2021, explores the effectiveness of learned views in self-supervised learning for speech data, particularly in the context of contrastive learning. The authors adapt the SimCLR framework to train models on 2D log mel spectrograms derived from large unlabeled speech datasets like LibriSpeech. They investigate various data augmentation techniques, including time-domain and spectral view functions, to enhance model robustness.

The results, summarized in Table 2, indicate that the learned views significantly outperform existing augmentation methods across several speech classification tasks, including Fluent Speech Commands and Google Speech Commands. Notably, the ResNet-18 model with a distortion budget of 0.05 achieves an average improvement of 9 percentage points over the best expert views. The study highlights that while the models perform well on most datasets, there are anomalies, such as in LibriSpeech speaker identification, where potential dataset-specific artifacts may influence the outcomes.

The findings suggest that learned views present a promising direction for advancing self-supervised learning approaches in speech applications, indicating a need for further research into their broader applicability across different modalities.
