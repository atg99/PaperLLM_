The paper presents additional experimental details for comparing SimCLR and the instance discrimination method (InstDisc) across image and speech tasks. 

In the image experiments, a modified ResNet-18 encoder is employed for smaller inputs like CIFAR-10, with an embedding dimension of 128 and no projection head. The SimCLR objective uses a temperature of 0.07, while InstDisc utilizes 4096 negative samples from a memory bank, optimized with SGD. The viewmaker adapts a style transfer network, simplifying it to three residual blocks and incorporating stochasticity through random noise. Preliminary tests with a U-Net architecture were less effective. For transfer evaluations, features from the last convolutional layer are utilized, following specific preprocessing and training protocols.

In the speech experiments, the setup mirrors that of the image experiments, with distinctions in data preprocessing. Pretraining is conducted on two LibriSpeech datasets, with raw waveforms truncated for consistency. Log mel spectrograms are generated as input, and expert views are derived from both waveforms and spectrograms. 

Overall, the experiments aim to investigate the performance of different architectures and methods in transfer learning across distinct domains.
