This paper presents a novel approach to learning diverse and useful views for contrastive learning, addressing the limitations of manually designed views for various modalities. Previous methods for image classification have relied on expert-designed views, which are not easily transferable to new domains or modalities. The authors introduce "viewmaker networks," a modality-agnostic generative model that learns to produce views by training jointly with an encoder network. This adversarial training approach generates stochastic perturbations added to inputs, effectively controlling the strength of the views while reducing mutual information between them.

Key contributions include:
1. The introduction of viewmaker networks for unsupervised representation learning.
2. Demonstration of comparable transfer performance to state-of-the-art contrastive methods on image data, with increased robustness to corruptions.
3. Significant improvements in speech recognition tasks over existing human-defined views.
4. Enhanced performance in human activity recognition tasks using time-series data from wearable sensors.

The paper also reviews related work on unsupervised representation learning, the role of views in contrastive learning, and the exploration of data augmentation strategies across different domains. Overall, the proposed method aims to democratize the process of view generation across various modalities, streamlining the development of effective representations without requiring extensive domain expertise.
