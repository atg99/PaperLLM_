The paper discusses methods for augmenting audio data using the NLPAUG library, specifically focusing on techniques such as random cropping, Gaussian noise addition, and masking in both frequency and time dimensions. The authors detail the preprocessing steps that lead to the creation of tensors suitable for training models, including the conversion of waveforms to normalized spectrograms. Two distinct datasets are utilized: a 100-hour dataset processed with a ResNet-18 model and a 960-hour dataset with a ResNet-50 encoder. The paper also outlines the normalization process for various transfer datasets, including AudioMNIST and Google Speech Commands.

In addition to audio data, the authors describe experiments with wearable sensor data, where they generate spectrograms for multiple sensors and normalize them accordingly. The training process is conducted on NVIDIA Titan XP GPUs, noting an increase in training time and memory utilization when using a viewmaker network.

The frameworks employed for the experiments include PyTorch and its extensions. The authors also present visualizations of augmented views for CIFAR-10, highlighting the complexity and diversity of the generated views. They explore the potential of generating perturbations in the frequency domain, suggesting further avenues for research in data augmentation techniques.
