This paper presents a novel method for learning representations from wearable sensor data, specifically for human activity recognition (HAR) tasks. The authors utilize the Pamap2 dataset, which includes time-series data from multiple sensors across various activities performed by different participants. They focus on the effectiveness of their learned views compared to existing view functions, particularly in the context of self-supervised learning.

The study demonstrates that their method significantly outperforms traditional spectral masking approaches in terms of accuracy. For instance, the linear evaluation accuracy of ResNet18 models trained on the Pamap2 dataset shows improvements of 12.8 percentage points with minimal distortion and up to 16.7 percentage points with a larger distortion budget. These results highlight the robustness of the learned views across varying distortion levels, while also noting that excessively strong distortions hinder learning.

Furthermore, the paper explores semi-supervised scenarios where labeled data is limited, showing that their approach can still yield strong performance using labels from just one participant. Overall, the findings suggest that the proposed method for learning views is broadly applicable and effective for representation learning in diverse domains, particularly for wearable sensor data.
