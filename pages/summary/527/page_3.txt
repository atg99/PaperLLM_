This paper, presented at ICLR 2021, discusses a novel approach to generating adversarial views for contrastive learning, emphasizing its independence from human-defined view functions and specific downstream tasks. The proposed method utilizes a viewmaker network that produces stochastic adversarial views confined to an `1 sphere around the input, enhancing the robustness of the model.

The authors draw inspiration from existing adversarial methods, highlighting their connections to the study of `p balls in adversarial robustness and the principle of vicinal risk minimization. They note that previous adversarial view-based pretraining relied on adding noise to handcrafted views or required specific tasks, whereas their method is more generalized and can create perturbations beyond typical variations in the training data.

The paper primarily focuses on contrastive learning, particularly the SimCLR algorithm, while also noting compatibility with other view-based algorithms. The SimCLR loss function is outlined, which aims to bring embeddings of positive views closer together while distancing those of negative views. The viewmaker network is trained alongside the main encoder network to facilitate the generation of effective perturbations, which are critical for producing robust representations.

Overall, the proposed method offers significant advantages in terms of flexibility and robustness, setting it apart from traditional adversarial view methods.
