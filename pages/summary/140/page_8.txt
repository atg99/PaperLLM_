The paper presents a comparative analysis of neural architecture search (NAS) and Bayesian network structure learning using two models: D-VAE and S-VAE. The results indicate that D-VAE significantly outperforms S-VAE in both RMSE and Pearson's correlation, suggesting that D-VAE's encoding of computations leads to more informative latent embeddings. This is contrasted with adjacency-matrix-based methods and graph-based approaches that primarily capture local graph structures without modeling computations on directed acyclic graph (DAG) structures.

Bayesian optimization (BO) was conducted using D-VAE and S-VAE, with the former demonstrating superior performance in identifying high-accuracy neural architectures. The top five architectures found by D-VAE achieved a maximum test accuracy of 94.80%, compared to S-VAE's best accuracy of 92.79%. The findings emphasize D-VAE's ability to stably discover high-performance architectures, as all top architectures surpassed 94% accuracy.

For Bayesian networks, D-VAE also yielded better results, with its best network achieving a BIC score of -11125.75, indicating a significantly higher probability of explaining the data compared to the best training network. Although the true network was not exactly found, D-VAE's results suggest a promising approach for Bayesian network structure learning through embedding space exploration.

The paper concludes with a discussion on the latent space visualization of the VAE models, aiming to assess their smoothness and effectiveness in capturing the underlying structures of the data.
