This paper presents a novel encoding procedure for neural architectures, specifically focusing on directed acyclic graphs (DAGs). It describes a method for computing the hidden states of nodes in a DAG through an iterative message-passing process. Each node's hidden state is derived from the hidden states of its predecessors, simulating the flow of input signals through computations. The final output of this encoding process is based on the hidden state of the terminal node, which is then used to derive parameters for a posterior approximation.

Two key theorems are introduced to support the framework. Theorem 1 establishes that the D-VAE encoder is invariant to permutations of nodes in the input DAG, given that the aggregation function is order-invariant. This implies that isomorphic DAGs will consistently produce the same encoded output, allowing for flexibility in the message-passing order. Theorem 2 asserts that the D-VAE can injectively encode computations represented by DAGs, ensuring that each unique computation corresponds to a distinct embedding in the latent space. This injective property facilitates optimization in a continuous latent space, which simplifies the process of searching for high-performance configurations.

The paper emphasizes that the focus is on encoding computations rather than the graph structures themselves, as different structures representing the same computation should yield identical encodings. This characteristic is pivotal for tackling the graph isomorphism problem, although the primary goal is to optimize computational processes. The authors propose using neural networks to model the injective functions required for the encoding and updating processes, leveraging techniques such as gated sums and gated recurrent units (GRUs) to achieve the desired properties. 

Overall, the D-VAE framework offers a robust approach to encoding and optimizing computations on DAGs, positioning itself as a valuable tool in the field of machine learning and optimization.
