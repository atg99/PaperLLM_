This paper evaluates the performance of various models in reconstructing directed acyclic graphs (DAGs) and their predictive capabilities using Variational Autoencoders (VAEs). The evaluation criteria include reconstruction accuracy, prior validity, uniqueness, and novelty, which measure how well the models can reconstruct input examples, generate valid new examples, and produce diverse outputs.

According to the results presented in Table 1, D-VAE and S-VAE outperform other models across most metrics, achieving near-perfect reconstruction accuracy and prior validity while also demonstrating higher uniqueness compared to GraphRNN. The GCN and DeepGMG models show lower reconstruction accuracies due to their focus on local structures, which may hinder the encoding of global topology necessary for effective reconstruction. Despite DeepGMG generating more unique graphs, the paper emphasizes that reconstruction accuracy is more critical for the intended applications.

In a subsequent experiment, the authors assess the predictive performance of the learned latent representations. This involves predicting the performance of unseen DAGs using a sparse Gaussian process model trained on the embeddings from the training data. The results in Table 2 indicate that D-VAE and S-VAE provide the best predictive performance, evidenced by low root mean square error (RMSE) values and high Pearson correlation coefficients, suggesting a strong relationship between predicted and actual performances.

Overall, the findings highlight the effectiveness of D-VAE and S-VAE in both reconstruction and predictive tasks, while also revealing the limitations of other models in accurately capturing the complexities of neural architectures and Bayesian networks.
