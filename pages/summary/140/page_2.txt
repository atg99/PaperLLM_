This paper introduces a novel approach to training a variational autoencoder specifically for directed acyclic graphs (DAGs), termed D-VAE. The authors note that traditional graph neural networks (GNNs) treat nodes symmetrically and focus on local structures, which may not be optimal for DAGs that have an inherent ordering based on dependency structures. To address this, the paper proposes an asynchronous message passing scheme that respects the computation dependencies within a DAG. In this scheme, a node's feature learning is contingent upon the completion of its predecessors' feature learning, allowing for a more structured approach to encoding computations.

D-VAE is demonstrated to possess a strong theoretical property, as it can injectively encode computations on DAGs, enabling a mapping from a discrete space to a continuous latent space. This property justifies the use of optimization techniques in the latent space for DAG structures. The contributions of the paper include the introduction of D-VAE with its unique message passing scheme, a new framework for DAG optimization through Bayesian optimization in a latent space, and applications of D-VAE to neural architecture search and Bayesian network structure learning. Experimental results indicate that D-VAE effectively generates valid DAGs and learns smooth latent spaces, enhancing the optimization of DAG structures.

The paper also discusses related work in the fields of variational autoencoders and deep graph generative models, highlighting various approaches to graph generation, including token-based, adjacency-matrix-based, and graph-based models. It touches on the advancements in neural architecture search and categorizes existing methods, setting the context for the significance of D-VAE in optimizing neural network architectures and learning Bayesian networks.
