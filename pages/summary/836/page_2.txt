This paper presented at ICLR 2020 discusses the quantization of neural networks, emphasizing its importance for deploying models on specialized low-precision integer hardware. Quantization reduces the memory footprint and computational intensity of networks by converting high-precision floating-point representations into lower bitwidth formats. The authors compare various quantization methods, highlighting their characteristics in Table 1, including parameters such as all-layer quantization (AL), batch normalization (BN), linear-symmetric quantization (LS), activation function support (AF), and structure intactness (SI).

The paper argues that effective quantization must consider all parameters involved in neural network operations, including weights, biases, activations, and scaling factors, which are crucial for low-precision representations like binary or ternary formats. Many existing quantization techniques have either left certain parameters unquantized or used high-precision accumulators, which are not feasible for very low bitwidths. The authors advocate for a quantization approach that utilizes fixed-point numbers for bias, ensuring compatibility with integer-only arithmetic and minimizing accuracy loss while avoiding the necessity of high bitwidth accumulators.

The findings emphasize that quantization is not merely a method for reducing memory usage but a critical step for enabling efficient inference on low-precision hardware accelerators, which are increasingly relevant for mobile and embedded applications.
