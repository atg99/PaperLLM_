The paper presents a novel low-bit quantization scheme for deep learning models, specifically designed for efficient hardware implementation. It emphasizes the importance of utilizing pre-trained weights to enhance convergence and accuracy during the training of quantized networks. The proposed method employs a unified learned linear symmetric quantizer to quantize weights and activations, focusing on a single scaling factor, which minimizes additional resource consumption while achieving high accuracy across various network architectures.

Key methodologies include channel-wise quantization for convolutional layers and layer-wise quantization for fully connected layers, utilizing symmetric linear quantization to compress values into low-bit representations, such as 4-bit integers. The quantization process is mathematically described, with emphasis on clamping activation values to ensure non-negativity when using ReLU activation functions.

The paper also incorporates batch normalization (BN) fusion techniques to facilitate faster inference on hardware accelerators. It highlights the benefits of starting with pre-trained parameters, particularly for object detection tasks, where high-resolution classification networks significantly enhance performance metrics.

Overall, the proposed quantization approach aims to balance resource efficiency and model performance, making it suitable for deployment on integer accelerators while maintaining state-of-the-art accuracy.
