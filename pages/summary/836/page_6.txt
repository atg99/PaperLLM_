This paper presents a comparison of two methods for optimizing quantization in neural networks: Simulated Gradient (SG) and Exponential Moving Average (EMA). The results indicate that SG generally outperforms EMA across various network architectures, as shown in Table 2, where models trained on Cifar10 and ImageNet demonstrate different performance metrics.

The authors discuss the challenges associated with finding the optimal quantization parameter, denoted as 𝛼, which changes dynamically during the retraining phase. They propose a method to determine a near-optimal value of 𝛼 through a search strategy during training iterations, which enhances computational efficiency compared to brute-force methods.

Furthermore, the paper addresses the fusion of Batch Normalization (BN) layers with convolutional layers to reduce inference latency. This fusion allows for the quantized representation of activations and weights while maintaining the integrity of learned parameters. The mathematical formulations provided illustrate how to merge BN layers into the quantization process effectively.

In summary, the study emphasizes the effectiveness of SG over EMA in optimizing quantization and outlines a method for efficiently adjusting the quantization parameter during training, alongside a strategy for improving inference speed through BN layer fusion.
