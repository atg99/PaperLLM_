The paper presents a learned linear symmetric quantization (LLSQ) method aimed at optimizing low-precision neural network accelerators. The authors address several challenges associated with quantization in convolutional neural networks (CNNs), particularly focusing on maintaining high accuracy while reducing computational and memory costs.

Key contributions include:

1. **Quantization of the Entire Network**: Unlike conventional methods that often exclude the first and last layers, this approach quantizes the complete network, including biases and scaling factors, which supports low bitwidth integer arithmetic units.

2. **Batch Normalization (BN) Fusion**: The paper highlights the importance of merging BN layers with convolutional layers to reduce computation. Two strategies are discussed: "BN-folded training," which increases training time, and symmetric linear quantization, which avoids additional computational overhead, making it more efficient.

3. **Linear Quantization Preference**: The authors argue for the necessity of linear quantization for state-of-the-art accelerators. They explain that while non-linear quantization methods may offer better accuracy, they complicate operations and require additional transformations. In contrast, linear quantization is more compatible with existing hardware designs.

4. **Compatibility with Various Activation Functions**: The proposed method is adaptable to different activation functions, such as Leaky ReLU, addressing a gap in existing quantization research that primarily focuses on ReLU.

5. **Maintaining Network Structure**: The authors emphasize the importance of keeping the network architecture unchanged to avoid increased computational and memory overhead, which is crucial for energy-efficient implementations.

The paper concludes by validating the LLSQ method on a specialized low-precision CNN inference accelerator, demonstrating its efficiency and effectiveness for edge and embedded applications, which prioritize small footprint, high throughput, and low power consumption.
