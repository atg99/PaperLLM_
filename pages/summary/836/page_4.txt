The paper discusses the growing importance of specialized neural network processors for efficient inference in mobile and embedded systems. It highlights various architectures such as DianNao, ShiDianNao, DeepBurning, Eyeriss, and EIE, which focus on low power consumption and optimized data movement. These processors typically utilize fixed-point arithmetic to minimize the energy and area overhead associated with floating-point operations. 

For cloud scenarios, architectures like TPU and FPGA-based accelerators are replacing traditional GPUs and CPUs, with a preference for fixed-point processing due to their superior throughput relative to power and area constraints. However, the transition from floating-point to fixed-point formats necessitates quantization, which is crucial for maintaining accuracy post-conversion. 

The paper critiques existing quantization methods that often overlook the specific architecture and operation of integer neural network processors, leading to accuracy losses or performance penalties. It emphasizes the challenges faced by low-bitwidth networks and the incompatibility of certain quantization techniques with hardware designed for low-precision computing.

In response, the authors introduce their Learned Linear Symmetric Quantization (LLSQ) method, which is designed to accommodate the constraints of specialized integer neural network processors. They detail the quantization process, including low-precision representation, quantized network training, and deployment on their custom CNN accelerator tailored for wearable applications. The proposed scheme aims to enhance the model quantization flow while maintaining hardware compatibility, ultimately achieving fast inference with low latency and energy consumption.
