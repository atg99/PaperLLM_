The paper discusses a method for low-bit integer-only arithmetic in neural networks, focusing on bias and scaling factor quantization. The authors propose using a scaling factor for biases and a bit-shift operation for multipliers to avoid multiplications in hardware. They highlight that small scaling factors can introduce significant quantization noise, necessitating parameter re-training. The re-training employs Straight-Through Estimator (STE) to handle non-differentiable quantization functions for weights, biases, and activations.

The experimental section evaluates the proposed learned linear symmetric quantization (LLSQ) across three datasets: Cifar10, ImageNet, and Pascal VOC. The experiments first apply LLSQ to weights and activations while keeping the first and last layers at full precision. Then, the entire network, including these layers, is quantized as LLSQF, and finally, biases and scaling factors are quantized. The LLSQ method is implemented in PyTorch, with various neural network architectures tested, including VGG-Small, AlexNet, ResNet, and MobileNet.

Specifically, for the VGG-Small architecture on Cifar10, the model is trained for 400 epochs, with quantization training using a warmup learning rate strategy over 10 epochs. The results show that with 3-bit weights and activations, the quantization maintains competitive accuracy, demonstrating the effectiveness of the proposed method in reducing model size while preserving performance.
