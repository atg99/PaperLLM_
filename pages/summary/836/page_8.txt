This paper presents a novel low-bit quantization method, LLSQ, which is evaluated on various datasets including CIFAR-10 and ImageNet. The comparison is made against state-of-the-art methods such as LQ-Nets and RQ across different bitwidth configurations for weights, activations, biases, and scaling factors.

In CIFAR-10 experiments, LLSQ demonstrates superior performance with higher accuracy and minimal degradation compared to existing methods. Specifically, it achieves an accuracy of 94.34% with 4-bit quantization, showing a degradation of only 1.00%, which is an improvement over LQ-Nets. The method maintains near baseline performance even when the first and last layers are quantized.

For the ImageNet dataset, LLSQ is applied to various architectures including AlexNet, ResNet18, ResNet34, and MobileNetv2. The results indicate that the accuracy drop is consistently less than 0.4% when quantizing the entire network, particularly excelling with 4-bit quantization. LLSQ outperforms non-linear quantization methods and shows significant advantages over RQ, especially for MobileNetv2.

In the context of object detection on the Pascal VOC dataset, LLSQ is implemented in YOLOv2, resulting in minor mean Average Precision (mAP) losses across different bitwidths. In contrast, when using PACT for quantization, there are substantial mAP losses. The training for the quantized YOLOv2 model is conducted for 170 epochs, yielding promising results that highlight the effectiveness of the proposed quantization approach. Overall, the findings suggest that LLSQ provides a robust solution for low-bit quantization while preserving model accuracy across various tasks.
