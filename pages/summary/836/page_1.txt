This paper presents a learned linear symmetric quantization method for neural networks, aimed at optimizing performance on low-precision integer hardware. The authors highlight the existing gap between software quantization methods and low-precision hardware implementations, which can reduce efficiency. Their proposed quantization technique quantizes both weights and activations into low-bit integers while enhancing hardware inference through batch normalization fusion and the use of low-precision accumulators and multipliers.

The method demonstrates improved performance over previous approaches across various neural network architectures, including AlexNet, ResNet, and MobileNet, while being compatible with accelerator designs. The authors further apply their quantization method to object detection with YOLO-v2, achieving high performance and accuracy. They validate their approach by deploying quantized models on a specialized integer-only DNN accelerator, showing that linear symmetric quantization can outperform asymmetric and non-linear methods in 4-bit networks. Notably, their quantizer results in less than a 0.4% accuracy drop in models like ResNet18, ResNet34, and AlexNet when quantizing the entire network, demonstrating its effectiveness in addressing the challenges of deploying neural networks on resource-constrained devices.
