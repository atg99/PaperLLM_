This paper presents advancements in the quantization of deep neural networks, particularly focusing on the Low-Latency Symmetric Quantization (LLSQ) method. It compares the performance of LLSQ and LLSQF against various state-of-the-art models such as ResNet and VGG. The results indicate that LLSQ outperforms baseline models while maintaining a minor performance penalty for LLSQF after quantizing certain layers.

The study further demonstrates the application of LLSQ on the YOLOv2 detector, providing detailed mAP scores across various classes at different bitwidths. It highlights the impact of quantizing biases and scaling factors, revealing that these components, when poorly managed, can lead to overflows that adversely affect network performance. The authors address this by implementing an 8-bit quantization for biases and scaling factors, followed by a brief fine-tuning process which restores the model's performance.

Additionally, the paper discusses the deployment of these quantized models on specialized integer-only hardware, utilizing a 2D systolic array architecture tailored for low-precision operations. The findings reinforce the feasibility of deploying quantized networks in practical settings without significant accuracy loss, thereby enhancing the efficiency and speed of deep learning applications on constrained hardware.
