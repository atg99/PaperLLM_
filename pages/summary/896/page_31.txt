The paper by Shorten and Khoshgoftaar presents an experiment comparing their proposed Neural Augmentation technique with traditional image augmentation methods and a style transfer approach. The dataset used is limited to 800 images across two classes, specifically using Tiny-imagenet-200 and MNIST images. Traditional augmentation methods included transformations such as cropping, rotation, and flipping, expanding the dataset size from N to 2N. The GAN-based style transfer utilized six artistic styles for image transformation. 

Neural Augmentation was tested with three levels based on different loss functions, using a convolutional network architecture for all experiments. Results indicated that Neural Augmentation outperformed traditional methods in distinguishing between dogs and goldfish, with slightly reduced performance on the dogs versus cats classification, and no impact on the MNIST dataset. The authors recommend integrating traditional and Neural Augmentation techniques for optimal results.

Additionally, the paper introduces Smart Augmentation, which relies on learned parameters from a CNN rather than the Neural Style Transfer algorithm. This method involves two networks, where Network-A generates augmented images to train Network-B, allowing for meta-learning of augmentations.
