The paper discusses the utility of autoencoders for feature space augmentations in data processing. Autoencoders consist of an encoder that transforms images into low-dimensional vector representations and a decoder that reconstructs these vectors back into the original images. This encoded data is valuable for feature space augmentation. DeVries and Taylor tested their method by extrapolating between the three nearest neighbors per sample and compared it to input space extrapolation and affine transformations. 

Feature space augmentation can also be achieved by using vector representations from a convolutional neural network (CNN) by truncating its output layer. These vector representations can then be used to train various machine learning models, though the effectiveness of this approach remains to be explored. A notable drawback is the challenge of interpreting the vector data, and reconstructing vectors into images using autoencoders can lead to complex and time-consuming training processes. Wong et al. found that data-space augmentation often outperforms feature space augmentation when image transformations are viable.

Additionally, the paper introduces adversarial training as a method for exploring augmentation possibilities. This involves using multiple networks with opposing objectives in their loss functions. Adversarial training can generate image augmentations that misclassify inputs in a rival classification network, demonstrating unexpected success despite contradicting conventional understanding of image representation. Performance results from experiments comparing feature space and input space extrapolation on datasets like MNIST and CIFAR-10 are also presented, highlighting the efficacy of different augmentation techniques.
