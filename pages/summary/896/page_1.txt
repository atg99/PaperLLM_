The paper by Connor Shorten and Taghi M. Khoshgoftaar presents a survey on Image Data Augmentation for Deep Learning, emphasizing its importance in enhancing the performance of deep convolutional neural networks (CNNs) in computer vision tasks. While CNNs have achieved significant success, their performance is heavily dependent on large datasets to mitigate the risk of overfitting. Overfitting occurs when a model learns overly complex patterns that do not generalize well to new data, which is a common challenge in fields with limited data availability, such as medical imaging.

The survey outlines various data augmentation techniques aimed at increasing the size and quality of training datasets. These techniques include geometric transformations, color space augmentations, kernel filters, image mixing, random erasing, feature space augmentation, adversarial training, and generative adversarial networks (GANs). The authors place particular emphasis on GAN-based augmentation methods. Additionally, the paper discusses related aspects of data augmentation, such as test-time augmentation, the impact of resolution, final dataset size, and the concept of curriculum learning.

By reviewing existing methods and highlighting promising developments in data augmentation, the paper aims to provide insights into how such techniques can enhance model performance and effectively leverage limited datasets, thereby capitalizing on the advantages of big data.
