The paper discusses the impact of partitioning training data on model performance, specifically comparing training with original and mixed images for the first 100 epochs and original images only for the last 50 epochs. This approach is linked to curriculum learning and further explored in the context of a meta-learning technique by Lemley et al. that optimizes image mixing.

Random erasing is highlighted as a notable data augmentation technique inspired by dropout regularization, aimed at addressing image recognition challenges caused by occlusion, where parts of objects are obscured. This method forces models to learn more descriptive features, preventing overfitting to specific visual elements. By randomly masking patches of an image with various values, random erasing has been shown to significantly reduce error rates on the CIFAR-10 dataset. The most effective masking method was found to be random values, with mask size and fill method being the primary parameters for implementation.

Random erasing not only mitigates overfitting but can also be combined with other augmentation techniques, achieving high accuracy on the CIFAR-10 dataset. The paper also references related work, such as Cutout Regularization, which similarly masks regions of images, and proposes combining random erasing with GANs for image inpainting.
