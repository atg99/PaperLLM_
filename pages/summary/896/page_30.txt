The paper by Shorten and Khoshgoftaar discusses the effectiveness of evolutionary algorithms for architecture search in machine learning, comparing them to Reinforcement Learning and random search methods. It highlights the evolution of Deep Learning from feature engineering techniques to advanced architecture designs, indicating that meta-architecture design could represent a significant advancement in the field. Neural Architecture Search (NAS) employs recurrent networks trained with Reinforcement Learning to optimize architectures, achieving a notable error rate of 3.65 on the CIFAR-10 dataset.

The paper also introduces three experiments utilizing meta-learning for Data Augmentation, employing a neural network to enhance data through image mixing, Neural Style Transfer, and geometric transformations. One significant method discussed is Neural Augmentation, which utilizes a CNN to combine two images from the same class and applies Neural Style Transfer through CycleGAN. This approach is designed to optimize the weights for style and content loss based on classification errors, allowing the network to refine its augmentation strategies. The algorithm was tested on the MNIST and Tiny-imagenet-200 datasets, illustrating its application in scenarios with limited data.
