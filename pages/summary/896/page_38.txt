The study by Shorten and Khoshgoftaar suggests a training strategy for deep learning models where initial training is conducted solely with original data, followed by a combination of original and augmented data, although opinions vary on this approach. The SamplePairing method involves training on ImageNet for one epoch and 100 epochs on other datasets before integrating mixed image data. Once SamplePairing data is included, training alternates between 8 epochs with SamplePairing and 2 without. Jaderberg et al. focus on synthetic data for natural scene text recognition, generating training images through various fonts and augmentations, accumulating training sets of 50k and 90k lexicons. Mikolajczyk and Grochowski argue that using augmented data to initialize deep convolutional network weights mirrors transfer learning from datasets like ImageNet, with subsequent fine-tuning on original data.

Curriculum learning, highlighted by Schroff et al. in the context of One-Shot Learning systems like FaceNet, emphasizes the importance of training on similar examples to enhance the learned distance function. The concept of curriculum learning, introduced by Bengio et al., applies broadly across deep learning models, not just those with limited data, and tracking training accuracy over time may reveal patterns that expedite training. Data augmentation techniques, such as flipping, translating, and erasing, can significantly enhance the training dataset.

The paper also discusses the impact of image resolution on training deep convolutional networks (CNNs). Higher resolutions, like HD and 4K, demand more computational resources and memory, yet next-generation models are expected to utilize them. Current practices often involve downsampling images to manage computational feasibility, which can lead to information loss and hinder recognition accuracy. Wu et al. examine the balance between accuracy and processing speed in relation to image resolution across several open-source datasets, highlighting the trade-offs involved.
