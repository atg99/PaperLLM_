The paper discusses the challenge of improving generalization in deep convolutional networks for Computer Vision tasks. Generalization refers to the model's performance on unseen data compared to training data, with poor generalization indicating overfitting. A common method to identify overfitting is by plotting training and validation accuracy over epochs. For effective deep learning models, validation error should decrease alongside training error, which can be achieved through Data Augmentation. This technique creates a broader range of data points, minimizing discrepancies between training, validation, and testing datasets.

While Data Augmentation is the focus of this survey, the paper also introduces other strategies to mitigate overfitting, including architectural advancements from AlexNet to DenseNet, and functional solutions like dropout regularization, batch normalization, transfer learning, and pretraining. Dropout, for instance, randomly zeros out neuron activations during training to promote robust feature learning. The paper emphasizes the importance of understanding these overfitting solutions to contextualize Data Augmentation within the broader landscape of deep learning techniques.
