The paper discusses the effectiveness of using variability in training data styles for simulating real-world environments in Computer Vision models. Shorten and Khoshgoftaar found that diverse styles were more beneficial than striving for hyper-realism in simulations, contrasting with Shrivastava et al.'s approach of using GANs for realistic data generation. The work of Richter et al. highlights the high labor costs associated with manual annotation of pixel-level datasets like CamVid and Cityscapes, underscoring the need for synthetic datasets. Neural Style Transfer is introduced as a method to enhance the generalization of simulated datasets, although it faces challenges related to style selection and computational demands. The original algorithm by Gatys is noted for its slow processing time, while Johnson's faster method is limited to pre-trained styles. Additionally, the concept of meta-learning in Deep Learning is mentioned, emphasizing its growing popularity in optimizing neural networks.
