The vanilla GAN architecture utilizes multilayer perceptrons for both the generator and discriminator, which works well for simple datasets like MNIST but struggles with more complex, higher-resolution datasets. MNIST images are small, at just 28 × 28 pixels, while datasets such as ImageNet feature images of 256 × 256 pixels, representing a significant increase in complexity. To address these limitations, various modifications to the GAN framework have been proposed, including changes in network architecture and loss functions, leading to improved sample quality. Notable architectures that enhance GAN capabilities include DCGANs, Progressively Growing GANs, CycleGANs, and Conditional GANs, particularly useful for Data Augmentation.

The DCGAN architecture specifically employs convolutional neural networks instead of multilayer perceptrons, allowing for the generation of images with greater complexity. Tested on the LSUN interior bedroom dataset, which consists of 64 × 64 pixel images, DCGAN increases the generator's complexity by projecting inputs into high-dimensional tensors and using deconvolutional layers to enhance spatial dimensions, thereby enabling the production of higher resolution images.
