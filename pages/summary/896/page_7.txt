The paper discusses various image data augmentation techniques that enhance the effectiveness of image recognition tasks. Early examples include simple transformations like horizontal flipping, color space adjustments, and random cropping. The survey categorizes augmentations into types such as geometric transformations, color space transformations, kernel filters, mixing images, random erasing, feature space augmentation, adversarial training, GAN-based augmentation, neural style transfer, and meta-learning schemes. 

A key focus is on geometric transformations, which are easy to implement and crucial for understanding more complex augmentation techniques. The "safety" of these transformations—defined as their ability to preserve labels post-transformation—is highlighted as a critical consideration. For example, while rotations and flips are generally safe for certain tasks like distinguishing between cats and dogs, they may not be safe for digit recognition, potentially leading to mislabeling. This lack of safety complicates the construction of refined labels necessary for training robust models.

The paper emphasizes that all image processing functions can alter labels depending on the distortion applied, making the development of generalizable augmentation policies challenging. Flipping, particularly horizontal flipping, is noted for its ease of implementation and effectiveness on datasets like CIFAR-10 and ImageNet, but it may not preserve labels in text recognition datasets like MNIST or SVHN.
