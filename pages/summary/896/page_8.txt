In the paper, Shorten and Khoshgoftaar discuss various techniques for image data augmentation to enhance the performance of convolutional neural networks (CNNs). 

Color space manipulation involves encoding digital images as tensors and performing augmentations in the color channels. Simple methods include isolating a single color channel and manipulating RGB values to adjust brightness. More complex techniques involve altering color histograms to change lighting, akin to photo editing.

Cropping is highlighted as a practical method for handling images of varying dimensions. Central and random cropping can simulate translation effects, although cropping reduces image size, potentially impacting label preservation.

Rotation augmentations involve rotating images by degrees between 1° and 359°. Minor rotations can be effective for tasks like digit recognition, but larger rotations risk losing the original labels.

Translation, or shifting images in any direction, helps mitigate positional bias, especially in datasets where images are centered. This method preserves spatial dimensions by filling empty spaces with constant or random values.

Noise injection adds random Gaussian values to images, aiding CNNs in learning robust features. Overall, geometric transformations are effective in addressing positional biases in training data.
