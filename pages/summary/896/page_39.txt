The study by Shorten and Khoshgoftaar investigates the effects of downsampling images to various resolutions on model performance. They found that an ensemble of models trained on both high (256 × 256) and low (512 × 512) resolution images achieved a combined top-5 error rate of 6.97%, outperforming the individual models with 7.96% and 7.42% error rates, respectively. This suggests that using images at different resolutions can serve as an effective Data Augmentation technique.

The paper also discusses the potential benefits of upsampling images using Super-Resolution Convolutional Neural Networks and Super-Resolution Generative Adversarial Networks, which could enhance image quality and improve classifier robustness. Moreover, it highlights challenges in generating high-resolution outputs from GANs due to training stability and mode collapse, while newer architectures like StackGAN and Progressively-Growing GANs aim to address these issues. 

Finally, the authors emphasize the importance of determining the final dataset size in Data Augmentation, noting that techniques such as horizontal flipping can double the dataset size. They also mention the need to balance memory and computational constraints, suggesting the use of data generators for on-the-fly transformations versus pre-processed data.
