Their findings, summarized in Table 9, indicate that performance improves when both test and training images are augmented. Matsunaga et al. further demonstrate the benefits of test-time augmentation specifically for skin lesion classification through geometric transformations. 

The effectiveness of a classifier can also be evaluated based on its robustness, defined by the consistency of predictions across different augmentations. For instance, Minh et al. explore the use of Reinforcement Learning to assess robustness by applying distortions to test images, revealing a performance drop from 74.61% to 66.87% with augmented images.

Moreover, the need for speed in certain classification models suggests potential for methods that gradually enhance prediction confidence through incremental test-time augmentations. Different computer vision tasks present unique challenges in aggregating predictions from augmented images, particularly in object detection and semantic segmentation.

In addition to data augmentation, researchers are exploring strategies for selecting training data that outperform random selection, focusing on the relationship between original and augmented data over training epochs.
