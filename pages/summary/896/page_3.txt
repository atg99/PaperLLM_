The paper discusses various techniques to mitigate overfitting in deep learning models. 

1. **Spatial Dropout** is introduced as a method that drops entire feature maps instead of individual neurons, enhancing regularization.
2. **Batch Normalization** normalizes activations within a layer by adjusting for the batch mean and standard deviation, which is a common preprocessing step for pixel values.
3. **Transfer Learning** allows networks to leverage pre-trained weights from large datasets like ImageNet for new tasks, particularly effective due to shared low-level spatial characteristics in image datasets.
4. **Pretraining** is similar to transfer learning but focuses on initializing weights from large datasets while allowing for different network architectures.
5. **One-shot and Zero-shot learning** techniques address model training with limited data. One-shot learning, often used in facial recognition, employs siamese networks or memory-augmented networks, while zero-shot learning classifies images based on descriptive attribute embeddings.
6. **Data Augmentation** targets the training dataset by artificially increasing its size through techniques like data warping and oversampling. Data warping retains labels while transforming images, whereas oversampling creates synthetic instances. These methods can be combined for more effective dataset enhancement.

The paper emphasizes the importance of these approaches in improving model performance and reducing overfitting by expanding the training dataset's information content.
