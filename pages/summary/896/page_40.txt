The paper discusses the challenges and strategies surrounding data augmentation in the context of machine learning, particularly in large datasets. It distinguishes between online and offline data augmentation, with online referring to real-time transformations and offline involving pre-processed data stored on disk. The authors highlight a method by Chilimbi et al. that involves pre-augmenting images to enhance the efficiency of distributed training systems by enabling pre-caching of training batches. 

Additionally, the paper emphasizes the importance of selecting a subset of augmented data that can achieve performance comparable to the entire dataset, drawing parallels to curriculum learning and optimal data ordering.

A significant issue addressed is class imbalance, where datasets predominantly feature one class, leading to biased model predictions and misleading accuracy metrics. The authors reference studies that investigate the effects of imbalanced data in convolutional neural networks and outline various data-level and algorithm-level solutions, with data augmentation being a key strategy.

While simple oversampling techniques using geometric transformations can be employed, they risk overfitting on the minority class. The paper suggests more sophisticated deep learning-based oversampling methods, such as adversarial training and Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), as effective alternatives that can generate new images and improve model robustness.
