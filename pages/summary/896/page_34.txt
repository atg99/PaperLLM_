The paper discusses the performance improvements achieved with the Smart Augmentation meta-learning strategy, as illustrated in Figure 31. AutoAugment, developed by Cubuk et al., represents a distinct meta-learning approach from Neural and Smart Augmentation. It utilizes a Reinforcement Learning algorithm to identify an optimal augmentation policy from a defined set of geometric transformations with varying distortion levels. Each policy comprises several sub-policies, each consisting of an image transformation and its magnitude. The AutoAugment method successfully achieved a 1.48% error rate on CIFAR-10 and 83.54% Top-1 accuracy on the ImageNet dataset. Notably, the policies learned for ImageNet were transferable, resulting in reduced error rates of 1.16% and 1.76% on the Stanford Cars and FGVC Aircraft tasks, respectively.

Geng et al. expanded on AutoAugment by substituting the Reinforcement Learning search with Augmented Random Search (ARS), addressing the limitations of the discrete search space. This modification led to lower error rates across CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet. Similarly, Minh et al. explored Reinforcement Learning for data augmentations, focusing on learning transformations for individual instances rather than the whole dataset, resulting in improved classification accuracy. They also examined the robustness of classifiers concerning test-time augmentation, highlighting significant accuracy differences on CIFAR-10 and the classification of dogs versus cats.
