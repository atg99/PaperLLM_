In their study, Shorten and Khoshgoftaar argue that filters are more effectively integrated as a network layer rather than through Data Augmentation. They discuss a novel Data Augmentation technique called SamplePairing, which involves mixing images by averaging their pixel values. Despite appearing counterintuitive, this method has shown effectiveness in enhancing classification models. Ionue's experiments demonstrated that by randomly cropping and flipping images, and then averaging their pixel values, the error rate on the CIFAR-10 dataset decreased from 8.22% to 6.93%. Furthermore, when applied to a smaller dataset of 1000 samples, the error rate improved from 43.1% to 31.0%, highlighting its utility in limited data scenarios. The study also found that mixing images from the entire training set yielded better results than mixing within the same class. SamplePairing effectively expands the dataset size and can be combined with other augmentation techniques, enhancing overall model performance.
