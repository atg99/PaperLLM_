The paper discusses the effectiveness of oversampling techniques, particularly using Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), to increase the size of minority classes in datasets while maintaining their distribution. It suggests that using the entire minority class as real examples or subsets as inputs to GANs could enhance class sampling, with evolutionary sampling as a promising area for future research.

The discussion categorizes image data augmentation into two main strategies: data warping and oversampling. While some augmentation methods like horizontal flipping and random cropping are easily justified, others, such as mixing pixels or images (e.g., PatchShuffle and SamplePairing), lack clear explanations for their effectiveness. The paper highlights the challenge of interpreting the learned representations of neural networks in GAN-based augmentation, variational autoencoders, and meta-learning, emphasizing the need for a deeper understanding of convolutional networks.

It notes that traditional augmentation techniques are being enhanced through advanced methods like GANs, Neural Style Transfer, and meta-learning algorithms. Neural Style Transfer, which separates style from content and applies different styles to images, is presented as a more powerful augmentation method compared to traditional techniques.

The paper also points out the combinatory nature of these augmentation methods, where techniques like random erasing can be layered with others. The recursive property of GANs allows for the generation of additional samples through traditional augmentations, significantly expanding the dataset size. Test-time augmentation is compared to ensemble learning, where predictions from augmented images are aggregated to improve performance, suggesting a viable approach for achieving high performance in predictive systems.
