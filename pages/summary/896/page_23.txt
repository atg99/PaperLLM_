Frid-Adar et al. tested the effectiveness of Deep Convolutional Generative Adversarial Networks (DCGANs) for generating liver lesion medical images, producing 64 × 64 × 1 size images from an original dataset of 182 CT scans (53 cysts, 64 metastases, and 65 hemangiomas). Their results showed an increase in sensitivity from 78.6% to 85.7% and specificity from 88.4% to 92.4% after incorporating DCGAN-generated samples alongside classical augmentations.

Another architecture discussed is Progressively Growing GANs, which trains networks with increasing resolution from 4 × 4 to 1024 × 1024. This method has yielded impressive results, particularly in facial image generation.

CycleGAN, proposed by Zhu et al., introduces a Cycle-Consistency loss function to stabilize GAN training, facilitating image-to-image translation between different domains, such as translating horses to zebras. This process involves a generator mapping images to another domain and a discriminator verifying their authenticity. Zhu et al. also applied CycleGANs in emotion classification using the FER2013 dataset, recognizing seven emotions by leveraging CycleGAN for intelligent oversampling to address class imbalance. The CycleGAN effectively learned unpaired image-to-image translation, exemplified by transforming neutral images into those depicting disgust.
