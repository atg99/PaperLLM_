The paper discusses the application of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) and Neural Style Transfer in addressing class imbalance and enhancing data augmentation, respectively. GANs can be employed as an oversampling technique to generate rare normal samples, thereby reducing false positive rates in unsupervised anomaly detection, as demonstrated by Lim et al. However, challenges such as achieving high-resolution outputs and the necessity for substantial training data limit their practicality. Salimans et al. provide an in-depth examination of GAN training issues.

Neural Style Transfer, initially recognized for artistic purposes, manipulates CNN-generated image representations to transfer styles while preserving content. This technique serves as an effective data augmentation tool. The advancement known as Fast Style Transfer improves efficiency by utilizing a perceptual loss function, leading to faster stylization processes. Ulyanov et al. suggest that instance normalization enhances fast stylization results. This method allows for diverse texture and style encoding, presenting practitioners with the challenge of selecting appropriate styles for generating new images.
