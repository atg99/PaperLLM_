The paper discusses advancements in classification boundaries achieved through training with both real and GAN-generated data. It highlights studies that utilize visual Turing tests to assess the quality of GAN outputs, specifically in medical imaging tasks such as skin lesion classification and liver cancer detection. Results from these tests indicate that experts struggled to accurately identify GAN-generated liver lesion images, with only 62.5% and 58.6% accuracy for the first and second experts, respectively. 

Tables included present the findings from visual Turing tests on liver lesion and brain tumor MR images generated by DCGAN and WGAN models. For example, the DCGAN model achieved varying accuracy rates across different types of images, with the highest being 77% for concatenated images at 128x128 resolution. These results underscore the effectiveness of GANs in medical image analysis while also highlighting challenges in distinguishing between real and synthetic images.
