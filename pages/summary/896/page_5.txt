The paper discusses the challenges in medical imaging, including privacy concerns, the need for expert labeling, and the high costs associated with manual imaging processes. These challenges have prompted research into image Data Augmentation, particularly using GAN-based oversampling for medical image classification. 

It highlights the use of popular academic datasets like MNIST, CIFAR-10/100, and ImageNet for benchmarking Data Augmentation techniques. While many studies focus on these datasets, the paper emphasizes the importance of addressing limited datasets and class imbalance, proposing Data Augmentation as a solution for class-balancing oversampling.

The analogy between human learning and neural networks is presented to illustrate the concept of transfer learning, suggesting that models trained on larger datasets can perform better on smaller ones. Data Augmentation is likened to human imagination, as it allows models to generate variations of images for enhanced understanding.

The structure of the paper is outlined: it begins with a "Background" section on Data Augmentation and Deep Learning, followed by a detailed examination of various augmentation techniques, design considerations, and concludes with discussions on future work and conclusions. The history of image augmentation is traced back to early CNN applications like LeNet-5, and the technique of Random Oversampling is introduced as a method to address class imbalance.
