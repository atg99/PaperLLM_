The paper by Bazdaric et al. discusses the fundamentals of linear regression analysis, emphasizing its importance for authors and editors in scientific research. Simple linear regression, which predicts the value of one variable based on another, is defined by the equation Y’ = A + BX, where Y’ is the predicted outcome, A is the intercept, and B is the slope. The authors explain that regression provides an approximation of values, leading to errors of prediction known as residuals. The best-fitting regression line minimizes the sum of squared errors, and the coefficient of determination (R²) indicates the proportion of variance in the outcome explained by the model.

To illustrate these concepts, the authors analyze a dataset involving 974 patients with atrial fibrillation to predict renal function quality using estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) as a dependent variable. They highlight the need for both variables to be numerical and have a linear relationship, assessed through scatter plots. The authors utilize MedCalc software for their analysis, which is tailored for biomedical research, and confirm the assumptions of linear regression by evaluating the normal distribution and random dispersion of residuals.

Additionally, the paper discusses the inclusion of categorical variables in regression analysis, explaining how they can be incorporated as dummy variables. Overall, the paper serves as a comprehensive guide for understanding and applying linear regression in scientific studies.
