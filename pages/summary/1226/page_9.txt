The paper by Bazdaric et al. discusses the fundamental principles of linear regression analysis, particularly focusing on the interpretation of regression results, including the coefficient of determination (R²) and beta coefficients. It highlights the issue of P-hacking, where authors may manipulate their choice of variables or modeling approaches to achieve statistically significant results without real interpretative value. 

The authors emphasize the importance of reporting R², particularly adjusted for sample size, to assess the explanatory power of the model. A low R² value, such as 0.10, suggests that only a small percentage of variance in the dependent variable can be explained by the predictors, raising questions about clinical significance. When determining the individual contributions of predictors, it is crucial to recognize that only statistically significant variables should be interpreted as independent contributors, as collinearity may obscure the effects of other predictors.

The paper cautions against making claims of independent associations without considering the potential influence of collinearity and the adequacy of the sample size. It argues that conclusions drawn from stepwise regression models should be carefully scrutinized, as they may not reflect true independent contributions.

In conclusion, the authors advocate for clearer guidance from journal editors regarding statistical methods and suggest that statistical peer review should be integrated into the publication process to enhance research quality in biomedical literature.
