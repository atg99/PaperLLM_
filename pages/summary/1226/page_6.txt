The paper by Bazdaric et al. discusses the fundamentals of linear regression analysis, particularly focusing on its application in predicting eGFR (estimated glomerular filtration rate) using various clinical variables. The authors highlight the importance of standardizing variables to achieve a mean of 0 and a standard deviation of 1, which aids in comparing effect sizes and understanding individual contributions of predictors through squared semi-partial coefficients of correlation.

In their example, they analyzed the relationship between eGFR and predictors such as haemoglobin, body mass index, left ventricular ejection fraction, age, and presence of coronary artery disease. While these variables are associated with renal function, the authors emphasize that causal relationships cannot be definitively established. The study found that the model explained 21.2% of the variance in eGFR, indicating that while the adjusted R² is modest, the findings still hold clinical significance.

The regression analysis revealed that age and haemoglobin were the most significant predictors of eGFR, with higher levels of haemoglobin and left ventricular ejection fraction correlating positively with eGFR, while higher body mass index and older age correlated negatively. The authors constructed a regression equation to predict eGFR based on input variables, demonstrating its practical application.

The paper also discusses the considerations required when including variables in the model, particularly regarding multicollinearity and the significance of predictors. It suggests that exploratory analyses could be conducted to investigate interactions between independent variables and the dependent variable, albeit acknowledging the limitations of the current study in addressing this complexity.

Overall, the authors advocate for a careful interpretation of linear regression results, highlighting the necessity of understanding both individual contributions of predictors and the broader clinical implications of the findings.
