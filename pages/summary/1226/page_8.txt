The paper by Bazdaric et al. discusses essential considerations for authors and editors regarding linear regression analysis in scientific writing. It emphasizes the importance of using appropriate terminology when titling articles that employ regression, advising against terms like 'influence' or 'cause' which imply causation, and recommending the use of 'correlation' or 'association' instead. The authors clarify the distinction between multivariate and multivariable analyses, highlighting that the former involves multiple outcomes and the latter involves one outcome with multiple predictors.

The paper stresses the necessity of confirming that assumptions for multiple regression are met, as violating these assumptions can lead to unreliable results. It acknowledges that real-world data often do not meet all assumptions and suggests that authors should either address these limitations or consider alternative statistical methods. The authors also provide guidance on transforming variables to improve model fit, cautioning against simplistic transformations that do not adequately address assumption violations.

Furthermore, the authors highlight the importance of including clinically relevant predictors in regression models, advocating for the inclusion of recognized predictors from the literature and variables with meaningful biological relationships. They also note the significance of interpreting the results of regression models accurately, particularly focusing on the strength of evidence indicated by p-values, with lower values suggesting stronger evidence against the null hypothesis.

Overall, the paper serves as a guide for effectively using linear regression analysis in research, ensuring clarity and accuracy in the presentation of statistical findings.
