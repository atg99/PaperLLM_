The paper by Bazdaric et al. discusses the fundamentals of linear regression analysis, a statistical method prevalent across various fields such as natural sciences, biomedicine, engineering, and social sciences. The authors highlight that linear regression estimates the relationship between continuous variables, typically using correlation coefficients like Pearson's or Spearman's. They emphasize the importance of understanding the distinction between dependent and independent variables in building prediction models.

The motivation for the paper arose from the authors' observations of statistical errors in the presentation and interpretation of regression results in public health literature. These errors could have been avoided if authors, reviewers, and editors adhered to established guidelines on statistical reporting. The paper aims to provide guidance on the correct application of simple and multiple linear regressions, along with strategies for effectively reporting results.

In addition to explaining both simple and multiple linear regression techniques, the authors also address common pitfalls in presenting regression results, such as misleading titles, causal language, inappropriate conclusions, and misinterpretation of data. The paper serves as a resource for researchers to improve their understanding and application of regression analysis in their work.
