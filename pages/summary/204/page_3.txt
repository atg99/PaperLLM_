The paper discusses the application of reinforcement learning (RL) in the context of autonomous vehicles, highlighting the relationship between optimal control and RL. It begins by outlining traditional control methods such as PID and Model Predictive Control (MPC), which aim to stabilize vehicle behavior and track specified paths. The connection between optimal control and RL is emphasized, where optimal control can be seen as a model-based RL problem, leveraging well-defined differential equations to model vehicle dynamics.

Reinforcement learning is introduced as a branch of machine learning where an agent learns to enhance its performance through interaction with its environment, rather than being explicitly instructed by an expert. The agent is driven by a reward function that evaluates its actions, with the goal of maximizing cumulative rewards over time. The paper identifies key challenges in RL, such as sample complexity, exploration versus exploitation trade-offs, and the simulator-to-real gap.

The exploration-exploitation dilemma is particularly emphasized, where agents must balance leveraging known actions that yield high rewards with the need to explore new actions that might provide even greater rewards. Strategies like ε-greedy and softmax are discussed as methods to manage this trade-off, with an emphasis on increasing exploitation as the agent gains more experience during training. The paper also encourages further reading on the stochastic control domain and ongoing research in exploration strategies for RL agents.
