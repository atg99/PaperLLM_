The paper discusses various approaches to autonomous driving tasks utilizing deep reinforcement learning (DRL) techniques. It highlights the use of different architectures and algorithms for specific driving scenarios such as lane keeping, lane changing, ramp merging, overtaking, negotiating intersections, and motion planning.

1. **Lane Keeping**: Two studies are referenced. One employs a DRL system combining discrete (DQN) and continuous actions (DDAC), concluding that continuous actions yield smoother trajectories but result in slower convergence. Another study emphasizes the benefits of removing memory replay in DQNs for faster learning.

2. **Lane Changing**: A Q-learning approach is proposed that offers a more robust solution for lane changes compared to traditional fixed waypoint methods.

3. **Ramp Merging**: The use of Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks is suggested to model long-term dependencies, improving the robustness of merging into highways.

4. **Overtaking**: A multi-goal RL policy using Q-learning is introduced, which enhances speed and collision avoidance during overtaking maneuvers.

5. **Intersections**: A DQN is utilized to evaluate Q-values for maneuvering through intersections safely, employing actions defined as Creep-Go.

6. **Motion Planning**: An improved A* algorithm leveraging deep neural networks for heuristic learning is proposed, resulting in smoother vehicle control compared to traditional methods.

The paper also addresses the simulation-to-reality gap, emphasizing the importance of transfer learning. Techniques such as domain adaptation and imitation learning are discussed, showcasing how simulated training can effectively translate to real-world performance with fewer real-world samples.

Lastly, the issue of sample efficiency in reinforcement learning is raised, noting that while animals learn efficiently through prior knowledge, RL methods often require extensive samples, making it a significant challenge in developing effective autonomous driving systems.
