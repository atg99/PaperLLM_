The paper discusses the distinction between value-based and policy-based methods in reinforcement learning, emphasizing that value-based methods prioritize evaluating optimal cumulative rewards, while policy-based methods directly estimate the optimal policy. Policy-based methods typically utilize parameterized neural networks and employ gradient descent to optimize the policy for maximum expected reward. This can result in either stochastic or deterministic policies, particularly relevant in continuous action spaces, which are addressed by Deterministic Policy Gradient (DPG) algorithms.

The DPG approach simplifies the integration process by focusing only on state space, which is beneficial for problems with large action spaces. Techniques like REINFORCE calculate discounted cumulative rewards over entire episodes, updating policy parameters based on performance gradients. Trust Region Policy Optimization (TRPO) and Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) are introduced as methods to ensure stable policy updates by limiting deviations from existing policies, with PPO being noted for its simplicity and superior sample efficiency.

Actor-critic methods blend policy-based and value-based approaches, where the actor selects actions and the critic evaluates their effectiveness. The paper outlines how the Direct Policy Gradient algorithm represents actions as parameterized functions, with the policy gradient step being influenced by a baseline to reduce variance in learning. Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (DDPG) extends these concepts to off-policy learning in continuous action spaces, utilizing techniques like noise addition for exploration and replay buffers for stabilizing learning.

Asynchronous Advantage Actor Critic (A3C) is highlighted as a robust alternative that employs asynchronous gradient descent across parallel environments, eliminating the need for experience replay and enhancing the training process. A3C demonstrates significant performance improvements in challenging domains, such as Atari games, while reducing training time.
