This paper provides a comprehensive survey of deep reinforcement learning (DRL) in the context of autonomous driving. It highlights the evolution of DRL algorithms and their applicability in various automated driving tasks, as well as the computational challenges faced in real-world implementations. The authors discuss the significance of related fields such as behavior cloning, imitation learning, and inverse reinforcement learning, differentiating them from traditional RL methods.

The introduction outlines the complexity of autonomous driving systems, which rely heavily on deep learning for perception and involve tasks that cannot be effectively addressed by classical supervised learning. Key challenges include the need for agents to adapt to changing environments, make optimal decisions in high-dimensional spaces, and learn from stochastic cost functions. The review emphasizes the sequential decision-making process inherent in RL, where the agent must develop an optimal policy.

The contributions of the paper are threefold: it provides an accessible overview of RL for the automotive community, offers a detailed literature review on the application of RL in autonomous driving, and discusses the challenges and opportunities associated with deploying RL in real-world scenarios.

Subsequent sections of the paper cover the components of autonomous driving systems, an introduction to reinforcement learning concepts, advanced extensions of RL, applications of RL in various driving problems, and the challenges of implementing RL in practical autonomous driving systems. The paper concludes with final thoughts on the future of DRL in autonomous vehicles, emphasizing the emergent nature of this field and the ongoing challenges that remain.
