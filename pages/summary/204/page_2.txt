The paper discusses the architecture and components of modern autonomous driving systems, highlighting the critical modules involved in ensuring safe and effective operation. 

The perception module is essential for creating an intermediate representation of the vehicle's environment, which includes identifying obstacles, lane positions, and traffic signals. This information is crucial for the decision-making system that formulates the driving policy. To enhance safety, the perception module employs redundant sources and various tasks such as semantic segmentation and motion estimation, which can be integrated into a multi-task model.

Scene understanding is a pivotal module that connects the mid-level perception data to high-level decision-making. It integrates data from diverse sensors to create a coherent representation of the environment, accounting for sensor noise and uncertainties. This fusion enables more robust decision-making capabilities.

Localization and mapping are fundamental for automated driving, allowing the vehicle to determine its position within a mapped area. Advanced mapping techniques, combined with semantic object detection, enhance reliability in localization.

The planning and driving policy module focuses on generating motion commands based on route-level plans. It addresses the complexities of motion planning by considering the vehicle's dynamics and constraints, employing algorithms like Rapidly-exploring Random Trees (RRT) that are suitable for non-holonomic vehicles.

Lastly, the control module manages the vehicle's speed, steering, and braking based on the trajectory generated from the map. It utilizes optimal control theory to minimize a cost function across various states and control actions, ensuring precise trajectory tracking throughout the driving process. 

Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of integrating various tasks and technologies to achieve reliable and safe autonomous driving.
