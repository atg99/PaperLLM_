The paper discusses the application of reinforcement learning (RL) in autonomous driving, highlighting various challenges and strategies for improvement. It begins by addressing the limitations of imitation learning and proposes an end-to-end deep neural network (DNN) approach that utilizes raw images and vehicle locations to predict ego-vehicle actions and neighboring vehicle trajectories. 

A significant focus is placed on intrinsic reward functions, which are crucial in real-world scenarios where explicit rewards may not be available. Techniques such as reward shaping and intrinsic motivation are explored, with curiosity defined as the prediction error of an agent's actions. This allows agents to assess the value of their behavior even in the absence of external rewards.

The paper also emphasizes the importance of safety in deep reinforcement learning (DRL) for autonomous vehicles. It presents methods like Safe DAgger, which incorporates a safety policy to predict potential deviations from a reference policy. Additionally, it discusses strategies for managing safety in multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL), addressing the interactions between various agents in driving environments, including pedestrians and other vehicles.

Finally, the paper concludes that while RL shows promise in autonomous driving applications, significant challenges remain, including the lack of large-scale datasets, the simulation-reality gap, and the need for effective reward functions. Future research directions are suggested, emphasizing the necessity of validating RL systems, enhancing sample efficiency, and integrating safety into decision-making processes.
