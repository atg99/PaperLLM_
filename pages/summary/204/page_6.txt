The paper discusses the advantages of using advantage estimates in reinforcement learning, particularly in the context of policy gradient methods. By defining the advantage function, agents can better evaluate their actions relative to expectations, leading to improved convergence and lower variance in training. The A3C and A2C models demonstrate comparable performance in various environments, emphasizing the importance of balancing exploration and exploitation to gather valuable information about uncertain states.

Moreover, the distinction between model-based and model-free approaches is highlighted. Model-based methods, such as Dyna-Q, leverage learned models of the environment to minimize costly interactions, while model-free approaches directly sample from the environment to estimate value functions. The discussion also touches on the on-policy versus off-policy method classification, explaining how different policies can be utilized during the learning process.

The paper further addresses the challenges associated with tabular representations in reinforcement learning, particularly the "curse of dimensionality." It advocates for function approximation techniques, including tile coding and neural networks, to manage continuous state and action spaces. The emergence of deep reinforcement learning (DRL) is noted, with examples illustrating its success in complex tasks, such as playing Atari games and Go, through the use of deep neural networks to derive control policies from raw input data.
