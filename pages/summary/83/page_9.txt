The paper discusses the mathematical foundations of recurrent neural networks (RNNs) and long short-term memory (LSTM) networks, focusing on the derivation of gradients through the chain rule. It introduces an ordered system of vectors, where each vector is dependent on all previous vectors. The chain rule for ordered derivatives is presented, which allows for the expression of full derivatives in terms of previous vectors.

The recursive application of this chain rule is detailed, emphasizing that each component in the gradient can be traced back through specific paths from one vector to another. This process ensures that each gradient component corresponds to a unique path, culminating in a product of partial derivatives along the path.

Moreover, the paper addresses the vanishing gradient problem, a significant issue in training RNNs. It highlights the challenges encountered when calculating gradients with respect to the RNN parameters, particularly in relation to a loss function that is derived from the final hidden state of the network. The Jacobian is expressed in terms of the gradients with respect to the final hidden state and the parameters of the network.

Overall, the paper provides a rigorous mathematical framework for understanding how gradients are computed in RNNs and LSTMs, while also acknowledging the complexities and limitations associated with these computations.
