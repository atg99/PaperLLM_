The section discusses the use of recurrent neural networks (RNNs) as discriminative sequence models in medical image computing and computer-assisted interventions. It highlights the modeling of the conditional probability of a sequence of output labels given a sequence of input measurements, specifically illustrating applications such as surgical activity recognition during robot-assisted surgery.

In discriminative modeling, the outputs are assumed to be conditionally independent given the inputs. The paper outlines two settings for RNN applications: the online setting, where predictions are made using only past and current inputs, and the offline setting, which allows for the inclusion of future inputs through bidirectional RNNs.

In the online setting, RNNs are used to model the probability of an output at time t based on past inputs, employing recursive functions to update hidden states. In contrast, the offline setting utilizes both forward and backward RNNs to concatenate hidden states from both directions for improved prediction accuracy. The section concludes with formulations for both scenarios, emphasizing the flexibility and effectiveness of RNNs in handling sequential data in medical contexts.
