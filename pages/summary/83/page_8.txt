The paper discusses the use of discriminative models in the context of recurrent neural networks (RNNs) for modeling sequences. It suggests relaxing independence assumptions over output sequences by using a factorization approach that conditions each output on the previous outputs and the input sequence. Specifically, it employs an encoder-decoder architecture, where one RNN processes the input sequence to produce a final hidden state, which is then used by a second RNN to model the joint distribution of the outputs. This method was initially applied in machine translation and has been extended to other applications, such as predicting future motion based on past motion.

The training of RNNs typically involves gradient-based optimization, using backpropagation. The text emphasizes the terminology surrounding backpropagation through time, clarifying that it refers to the application of error backpropagation in RNNs rather than a unique algorithm. It introduces the concept of ordered derivatives and the chain rule to aid in understanding the training process, addressing the vanishing gradient problem that often arises in RNN training. The paper also outlines common strategies employed to mitigate this issue in practice, highlighting the complexities and nuances involved in training RNNs effectively.
