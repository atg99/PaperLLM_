The paper discusses the application of deep learning techniques, specifically Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks, in predicting the probability of malignancy based on a sequence of measurement vectors from patient examinations. 

The naive solution proposed involves stacking measurement vectors into a single input, which is problematic as it fails to capture the inductive biases necessary for effective modeling of temporal dynamics. This approach also struggles with varying numbers of examinations per patient, leading to the need for heuristics such as discarding past information or padding inputs.

To address these issues, the paper introduces a modified simple RNN architecture that retains latent information over time steps. The model explicitly incorporates past hidden states into its calculations, allowing it to learn representations specific to the task. The initial hidden state is set to zero, indicating no prior information, and the subsequent hidden states are computed using both the current input and the previous hidden state. This formulation allows the model to accommodate patients with varying numbers of examinations without losing critical temporal information.

Overall, the proposed RNN model enhances the ability to capture local dynamics in the data and effectively processes sequences of varying lengths, making it a suitable approach for the problem at hand.
