In teacher forcing, during the training phase, the model is fed the true output from the previous time step as input for the current time step, rather than using its own predictions. This approach allows the model to learn more effectively by reducing the error propagation from previous predictions, enabling it to converge faster.

In scenarios where the model might generate outputs that deviate significantly from the expected results, teacher forcing mitigates the risk of compounding errors. However, it is important to note that this method can lead to a discrepancy between training and inference phases, as the model may become overly reliant on the ground truth inputs provided during training. This challenge is often referred to as exposure bias.

Overall, the paper discusses critical concepts in training recurrent neural networks (RNNs), particularly the use of long short-term memory (LSTM) units to address issues such as exploding gradients and the computational demands during backpropagation. Techniques like truncated backpropagation through time (TBTT) and teacher forcing are highlighted as effective strategies to optimize training processes and improve model performance in generating sequential data.
