The paper discusses the mathematical framework for analyzing the gradients in recurrent neural networks (RNNs), particularly focusing on the gradients of hidden states over time. It starts by applying the chain rule to express the gradient of the final hidden state with respect to previous hidden states, emphasizing the importance of temporal components in this calculation. The relationship between the spectral norm of the gradients and their impact on learning from earlier time steps is highlighted. Specifically, if the spectral norm is small, the influence of earlier states on the final gradient diminishes, complicating the learning process.

The analysis notes that in simple RNNs, a single path exists from earlier hidden states to the final state, leading to a specific gradient component that can be bounded by the product of Jacobians. This results in an exponential decay of the gradient contributions from earlier time steps, depending on the spectral norm of the Jacobians involved. The paper acknowledges the challenges posed by this vanishing gradient problem and reviews various strategies that have been proposed to mitigate it, including regularization techniques and architectural modifications. Ultimately, it cites long short-term memory (LSTM) networks as a successful architecture that provides more stable gradient propagation through the introduction of additional connections between non-adjacent hidden states.
