This section of the Handbook of Medical Image Computing and Computer Assisted Intervention discusses truncated backpropagation through time (BPTT) in recurrent neural networks (RNNs), specifically illustrating its application with a sequence of time steps. The method involves performing updates every two time steps, which conserves memory by caching hidden states on a per-group basis during backpropagation. This approach significantly reduces memory usage to one-fifth compared to standard backpropagation while increasing the number of gradient updates.

The text also addresses the challenges of training RNN-based generative models, particularly the disconnect between training and inference. During training, a technique called teacher forcing is employed, where the model uses observed data instead of sampling from the model itself. 

Furthermore, the paper introduces Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks and Gated Recurrent Units (GRUs), both designed to mitigate the vanishing gradient problem that can hinder the training of RNNs. LSTMs and GRUs have gained popularity due to their effectiveness, and while there are numerous variants of these architectures, the core mechanisms for addressing the vanishing gradient issue are similar. The section emphasizes the importance of understanding these architectures and their implementations in deep learning frameworks to avoid confusion in result reproducibility.
