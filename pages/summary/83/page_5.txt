The paper discusses the capabilities and limitations of recurrent neural networks (RNNs) and long short-term memory (LSTM) networks. It highlights that, under certain assumptions, simple RNNs can simulate a Turing machine with a limited number of units, making them powerful for representation. However, the ability to learn these representations from data in a reasonable timeframe is a significant concern. A major limitation of simple RNNs is their difficulty in learning long-term dependencies, which LSTMs were specifically designed to address.

The paper outlines the general structure of RNNs, where they map sequences of inputs to sequences of hidden states through a set of parameters. The function used in this mapping is more complex than simple activation functions, as seen in LSTMs, which involve a combination of gates and nonlinearities. Additionally, RNNs can be layered to create deep RNNs, allowing for richer model architectures that can be trained end-to-end.

The modeling capabilities of RNNs are exemplified through a case study where they map sequences of measurement vectors to the probability of malignancy, illustrating their use in discriminative modeling. The section also indicates that RNNs are versatile and applicable to a variety of modeling scenarios beyond the initial example, setting the stage for further exploration of their applications in the literature.
