The paper discusses a methodology for improving diabetic retinopathy classification using a capsule network (CapsNet) architecture. It emphasizes the importance of preprocessing retinal images to enhance the quality of input data for model training. The preprocessing steps involve resizing all images to 512×512 pixels and enhancing contrast by focusing on the green channel of RGB images, as it provides better visibility for microaneurysms. Contrast limited adaptive histogram equalization (CLAHE) is applied to further improve image contrast, followed by noise reduction using Gaussian filtering.

The paper contrasts traditional convolutional neural networks (CNNs) with capsule networks, highlighting limitations of CNNs such as information loss due to pooling layers and the need for large datasets. Capsule networks are introduced as a solution that retains spatial information and significant features, allowing for better handling of image deformations and varying viewing conditions. CapsNet architecture includes layers for convolution, primary capsules, and class capsules, utilizing matrix multiplication, scalar weighting, dynamic routing, and a squashing function to process features.

The proposed CapsNet architecture aims to effectively identify and classify diabetic retinopathy from fundus images, leveraging the strengths of capsule networks to improve classification accuracy.
