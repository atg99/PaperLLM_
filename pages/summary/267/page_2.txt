The paper discusses the significant prevalence of diabetes, particularly in India, where the number of individuals affected has risen sharply over the years. The International Diabetes Federation reported that in 2020, 463 million people globally had diabetes, with 88 million in Southeast Asia and 77 million in India alone. Most cases are type-2 diabetes, leading to complications such as diabetic retinopathy, cataracts, and glaucoma. Diabetic retinopathy, a serious condition affecting the retina due to high blood sugar levels, can result in vision loss if not detected early.

The paper highlights the stages of diabetic retinopathy, starting from mild non-proliferative retinopathy, where microaneurysms form, to proliferative retinopathy, where abnormal blood vessels develop. The prevalence of diabetic retinopathy is notably high among individuals over 50, with urban areas showing higher rates. The National Program for Prevention and Control of Cancer, Diabetes, Cardiovascular Diseases, and Stroke was initiated in India to facilitate early detection through health camps.

The review of literature emphasizes the necessity of early diagnosis and regular monitoring of diabetic retinopathy to prevent vision loss. Various algorithms and automated systems have been developed for the diagnosis of diabetic retinopathy, focusing on analyzing fundus images for signs of the disease. Deep learning technologies are increasingly being applied to enhance the automation and accuracy of disease detection in the medical field. Overall, the paper advocates for improved detection methodologies to mitigate the impact of diabetic retinopathy among diabetic patients.
