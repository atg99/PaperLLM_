The paper presents a study on the performance of a reformed Capsule Network (CapsNet) applied to the Messidor dataset, which consists of fundus images classified into four grades of diabetic retinopathy (DR). The model was trained and evaluated using a system equipped with an AMD Ryzen processor and NVIDIA GPU. The dataset was split, with 75% allocated for training and 25% for testing, further dividing the training data into training and validation sets.

The results are summarized in a confusion matrix, showing the model's predictions against actual classifications. Notably, the CapsNet achieved high accuracy across all grades: 97.98% for grade 0 (healthy retina), 97.65% for grade 1 (DR stage 1), 97.65% for grade 2 (DR stage 2), and 98.64% for grade 3 (DR stage 3). Performance measures such as precision, recall, and F1 score were calculated, with grade 3 achieving a perfect recall of 100%.

The findings indicate that while achieving a perfect classification score is unrealistic, the model demonstrates strong performance with high precision and recall values, suggesting its potential effectiveness for classifying diabetic retinopathy in medical imaging contexts. The study emphasizes the importance of these metrics in evaluating classification models, particularly in medical applications where misclassification can have significant consequences.
