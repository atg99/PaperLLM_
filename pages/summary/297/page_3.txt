The paper explores spatial attention mechanisms in deep learning, particularly in the contexts of neural machine translation and computer vision. It highlights the influence of attention modeling, including variants like deformable and dynamic convolution, which function similarly to attention mechanisms by utilizing different attention weight functions. A specific focus is placed on channel-wise feature attention, which captures correlations among different semantic concepts encoded by feature channels.

Despite the prevalence of spatial attention mechanisms, there has been limited analysis of their effectiveness across various tasks. Previous studies primarily focused on visualizing learned attention weights in NLP tasks, often equating attention weight assignment to traditional word alignment. However, recent findings challenge this assumption, indicating that attention weights do not reliably correlate with feature importance or impact predictions.

This paper presents a comprehensive empirical study on spatial attention modules across NLP and computer vision tasks, aiming to disentangle different attention factors and weight assignment functions. The authors develop a generalized attention formulation to represent various module designs and facilitate ablation studies. The study investigates how multi-head attention aggregates key contents based on the compatibility between query and key elements, demonstrating the role of self-attention in capturing intra-relationships among elements.

In the context of Transformer attention, the paper details the computation of attention weights based on multiple terms sensitive to query content, emphasizing the compatibility of query and key contents as a driving factor in the attention mechanism. The findings aim to deepen the understanding of spatial attention mechanisms and their implications for improving model performance across diverse applications.
