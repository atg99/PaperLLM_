This paper introduces an architecture called "Attended Residual Block" that integrates various attention mechanisms into residual blocks for enhanced performance in different tasks, including image object detection, semantic segmentation, and neural machine translation (NMT). The architecture comprises three attention mechanisms: Transformer attention, dynamic convolution, and deformable convolution, which are depicted in various configurations in Figure 2.

In the context of neural machine translation, the model is based on the Transformer architecture with relative position encoding. The deformable convolution unit is incorporated before the Transformer attention module to form a "Transformer + Deformable" architecture.

The experiments are structured around three main tasks:

1. **Image Object Detection**: The models are trained on the COCO 2017 dataset, utilizing Faster R-CNN with Feature Pyramid Networks as the baseline. The attended residual blocks are applied to the last two stages of a ResNet-50 backbone, with accuracy measured by mean Average Precision (mAP).

2. **Image Semantic Segmentation**: Models are trained on the Cityscapes dataset, where CCNet is used for semantic segmentation. The attended residual blocks are again applied similarly to the object detection task, and an additional Transformer attention/dynamic convolution module enhances performance.

3. **Neural Machine Translation (NMT)**: Training is conducted on the WMT 2014 English-German dataset, with evaluation based on BLEU scores. The Transformer architecture serves as the backbone, with hyperparameters set according to previous studies.

The paper also explores the disentanglement of various terms within the Transformer attention module to analyze their individual effects on performance. This comprehensive study demonstrates the potential of attention mechanisms in improving model performance across multiple domains. Further details and hyperparameter settings are provided in the appendix.
