This paper investigates spatial attention mechanisms, particularly within Transformer attention, deformable convolution, and dynamic convolution. It aims to unify these mechanisms and understand their operational differences. The authors present an empirical study that dissects various attention factors—specifically query content, key content, and relative position—across applications like neural machine translation, semantic segmentation, and object detection.

Key findings include: 
1. In Transformer self-attention, the query and key content terms are less significant, while they are crucial in encoder-decoder attention. 
2. Deformable convolution, relying on query content and relative position, outperforms Transformer attention in image recognition tasks. 
3. The most impactful factors in self-attention are query content combined with relative position and key content alone. A blend of deformable convolution with key content enhances accuracy with reduced computational cost compared to traditional Transformer attention.

These results challenge the prevailing view that query-sensitive attention, particularly the query and key content terms, is the primary driver of success in spatial attention modules. The paper suggests that the design of attention mechanisms may need reevaluation, as modules that omit query-sensitive terms can perform comparably to those that include them. This work paves the way for future research into optimizing spatial attention in deep networks. 

The paper also contextualizes its findings within the broader landscape of attention mechanism development in both natural language processing and computer vision, highlighting the evolution and variations of attention factors utilized in these fields.
