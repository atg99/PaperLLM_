The paper compares various attention mechanisms, focusing on their spatial properties, complexity, and the role of query and key content. The attention mechanisms discussed include Transformer attention, regular convolution, deformable convolution, and dynamic convolution. 

Transformer attention is characterized as dense and global, with a complexity of O(N²sC + NsC²). It emphasizes the importance of query-sensitive prioritization, particularly the compatibility between query and key content. The mechanism involves several terms, with E1 being crucial as it directly relates to query content and key content. Other terms like E2, E3, and E4 address relative position and key content, but may not directly involve the query.

Regular convolution is described as a sparse and local mechanism, sampling a fixed number of key elements (e.g., 3x3) around a query, with a complexity of O(NsC²Nk). It lacks learnable parameters for content adaptation, focusing solely on relative position. Deformable convolution builds on this by introducing learnable offsets to adjust sampling positions dynamically based on query content, allowing it to capture spatial transformations while maintaining sparse complexity.

Dynamic convolution, a more recent approach, aims to replace Transformer attention in self-attention mechanisms. It introduces a new paradigm where attention factors are determined dynamically, enhancing flexibility and adaptability.

Overall, the paper highlights the trade-offs between different attention mechanisms in terms of density, locality, and adaptability, emphasizing the significance of query and key content interactions in achieving effective attention.
