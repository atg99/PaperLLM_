This paper investigates the accuracy-efficiency tradeoffs associated with various configurations of Transformer attention, specifically focusing on self-attention mechanisms in image object detection, semantic segmentation, and neural machine translation tasks. The authors analyze different configurations denoted by binary codes, where each bit represents the activation of specific attention terms.

Key findings include:

1. The activation of query-sensitive terms (E1 and E2) has a minimal impact on overall accuracy compared to query-irrelevant terms (E3 and E4). This is particularly evident in image recognition tasks, where query and key content terms contribute significantly to computational load but provide little accuracy improvement.

2. The paper presents empirical results demonstrating that configurations utilizing the full Transformer attention (denoted as "1111") yield substantial accuracy gains over configurations without it ("w/o"). However, the greatest improvements come from activating query-irrelevant terms, underscoring their importance in achieving optimal performance.

3. The recommended configuration for achieving a favorable balance between accuracy and computational efficiency is "0010 + deformable," which utilizes deformable convolution alongside the selected attention terms.

The analysis highlights that while the Transformer attention mechanism enhances performance, careful consideration of which terms to activate can lead to significant gains in efficiency without sacrificing accuracy.
