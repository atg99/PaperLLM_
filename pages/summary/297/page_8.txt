The paper investigates the performance of dynamic convolution and deformable convolution in comparison to the E2 term from Transformer attention across different tasks: dynamic object detection, semantic segmentation, and neural machine translation (NMT). The analysis focuses on how variations in query and key content terms impact accuracy and computational efficiency.

Key findings include:

1. In dynamic object detection and semantic segmentation, the introduction of the query and key content term (controlled by βTrans1) shows negligible performance gains, allowing for reduced computational overhead. However, its removal in encoder-decoder attention significantly affects accuracy, highlighting the necessity of these terms for aligning source and target words in NMT.

2. For self-attention scenarios, the configuration utilizing only key content and relative position factors maintains accuracy close to the full model while saving computational resources. The key content term improves performance with minimal overhead.

3. Deformable convolution outperforms the E2 term in object detection and semantic segmentation but achieves comparable results in NMT. Its efficiency stems from not needing to traverse all key elements, which is particularly beneficial in image processing.

4. The combination of deformable convolution with only the key content term offers the best accuracy-efficiency balance, reaffirming the importance of these components in self-attention mechanisms.

5. In dynamic convolution, while it matches the accuracy of the E2 term in NMT, it underperforms in image recognition tasks. The study suggests that design choices in dynamic convolution may not be optimal for image tasks, as its original design is tailored for NMT.

Overall, the research challenges the assumption that query-sensitive terms are essential for Transformer attention, indicating their critical role primarily in encoder-decoder settings while being less impactful in self-attention contexts.
