This model demonstrated superior performance compared to other algorithms, achieving an accuracy of 98.1%, along with high AUC, sensitivity, and specificity metrics. The computational efficiency of LightGBM also stands out, with the fastest testing time and relatively low training time among the better-performing models.

Despite its strengths, the study acknowledges certain limitations, including the unavailability of key diabetes indicators such as 2-hour postload plasma glucose and HbA1c due to the high cost of testing. Additionally, the reliance on invasive indicators and the focus on Ethiopian data may restrict the model's applicability across diverse populations.

In conclusion, the LightGBM model represents a significant advancement in the classification of medical data for diabetes diagnosis, highlighting the potential of machine learning techniques in healthcare analytics. The findings underscore the importance of utilizing comprehensive datasets to enhance the accuracy and generalizability of predictive models in diabetes detection.
