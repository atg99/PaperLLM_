The paper discusses the collection and analysis of a diabetes dataset specific to Ethiopia, referred to as the Zewditu Memorial Hospital diabetes dataset (ZMHDD). This dataset includes physical examination data from 1030 individuals with diabetes mellitus (DM) and 1079 nondiabetic individuals, collected from Zewditu Memorial Hospital, with ethical approval obtained. The dataset contains 23 indicators, but many were found to have weak correlations with diabetes. To enhance the relevance of the data, consultations with Ethiopian diabetologists were conducted to identify significant diabetes indicators, focusing primarily on type 2 diabetes, as type 1 and gestational diabetes cases were excluded from the study.

The ZMHDD comprises 2109 instances, 10 indicators, and 2 classes (diabetic and nondiabetic). During data preprocessing, missing values were addressed using the median of corresponding features, which is preferable in smaller datasets. Furthermore, feature values were normalized using Min-Max Normalization to ensure consistency across different scales, with values bounded between 0 and 1.

For model evaluation, various performance metrics—including accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, and the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC)—were utilized. The k-fold cross-validation method was employed to assess model performance, where the dataset was partitioned into k subsets for training and testing, allowing for a robust evaluation of the model's effectiveness.
