The paper discusses the development of a Light Gradient Boosting Machine (LightGBM) model aimed at effectively classifying diabetic patients for diabetes mellitus (DM) diagnosis. Various LightGBM models were constructed, modifying the number and complexity of trees in the ensemble, followed by evaluation through 10-fold cross-validation to determine the optimal model. This model was then compared to six reference models—K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN), Support Vector Machine (SVM), Naive Bayes (NB), Bagging, Random Forest (RF), and XGBoost—assessing accuracy, sensitivity, and specificity. The results indicated that the LightGBM model outperformed all reference techniques in DM screening, highlighting its potential as a valuable diagnostic tool for physicians.

Looking ahead, the authors plan to implement the LightGBM model in real-time diabetes diagnosis systems and explore its application across other medical datasets to validate its effectiveness and generalizability. They also suggest focusing more on noninvasive indicators for diabetes detection in the general population.
