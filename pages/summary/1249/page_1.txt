The paper presents a method for diagnosing diabetes mellitus using the Light Gradient Boosting Machine (LightGBM) to enhance early detection, particularly in regions with limited medical resources. Diabetes is a prevalent chronic condition, with many individuals unaware of their status, heightening the need for effective diagnostic tools. The study utilizes data from Zewditu Memorial Hospital in Addis Ababa, Ethiopia, highlighting the suitability of LightGBM due to its low computational complexity.

The findings indicate that the LightGBM model achieved high performance metrics, including an accuracy of 98.1%, AUC of 98.1%, sensitivity of 99.9%, and specificity of 96.3%, surpassing other models like KNN, SVM, and Random Forest when applied to the dataset. This demonstrates the potential of advanced data mining techniques in medical diagnostics, particularly for diabetes detection, thereby addressing a significant global health challenge. The paper emphasizes the importance of early diagnosis to prevent complications associated with diabetes, especially in low and middle-income countries where the incidence is rapidly increasing.
