The paper discusses various attributes associated with diabetes mellitus and their correlations to diabetes status, as well as the effectiveness of different machine learning algorithms in predicting diabetes. Key attributes include age, gender, insulin levels, blood pressure (both systolic and diastolic), body mass index (BMI), total cholesterol, low-density lipoprotein (LDL), pulse rate, and fasting blood sugar (FBS), with FBS showing the highest correlation (0.37) to diabetes status.

Faruque et al. conducted a study utilizing machine learning techniques on data from 200 patients, identifying 15 diabetes indicators. They compared four algorithms: Support Vector Machine (SVM), Naive Bayes (NB), K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN), and C4.5 Decision Tree. The C4.5 decision tree achieved the highest accuracy of 73.5%.

Xu and Wang proposed a prediction model for type 2 diabetes using an ensemble learning approach based on the UCI Pima Indian diabetes dataset, which includes several attributes like pregnancy history, plasma glucose levels, and BMI. Their work emphasizes the importance of these indicators in diabetes risk assessment and the potential of machine learning in enhancing predictive accuracy in medical diagnostics.
