In the paper, the authors evaluate the performance of a proposed model using the ZMHDD dataset through a structured approach involving model training and testing. The evaluation process comprises two phases, starting with a 10-fold cross-validation applied during a grid search over three hyperparameters of the LightGBM algorithm. This method helps identify the optimal hyperparameter set and model architecture, as visualized in Figure 4.

The optimum configuration determined includes a number of trees (nest) set at 150, maximum depth (depth max) at 3, and number of leaves (nleaves) at 4, achieving a test accuracy of 0.9815 with a training time of 0.624 seconds. The experimental analysis emphasizes the importance of hyperparameter tuning to enhance model performance, particularly in managing the number of trees, maximum tree depth to prevent overfitting, and the complexity of the tree model through the number of leaves.

The results indicate that careful optimization of these parameters is crucial for achieving high accuracy and efficient training times, especially when working with smaller datasets like ZMHDD. The findings will be further discussed in subsequent sections of the paper.
