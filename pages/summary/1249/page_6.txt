The paper discusses a methodology for predicting diabetes using machine learning techniques. The process begins with the conversion of collected diabetes data into a tabular format suitable for analysis. Data preprocessing involves visualizing patterns through box-plots and correlation heat-maps, followed by the removal and replacement of irrelevant data elements. To identify important features, the correlation coefficients of input variables with the dependent variable (diabetes status) are calculated. The authors highlight a potential issue where machine learning algorithms may prioritize certain attributes based on their numerical ranges, such as fast blood sugar (FBS) over gender. To address this, Min-Max normalization is applied to standardize attribute values.

The core predictive model employed is the Light Gradient Boosting Machine (LightGBM), which is optimized through 10-fold cross-validation to determine the best hyperparameters. Additionally, other classifiers such as KNN, SVM, Naive Bayes, Bagging, Random Forest, and XGBoost are developed and their performance compared to the optimized LightGBM model.
