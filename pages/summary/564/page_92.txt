The section discusses hyperparameter optimization, which is akin to model selection but involves distinct challenges, such as dealing with continuous and high-dimensional hyperparameter spaces. It highlights that the hyperparameter space is a subset of the Cartesian product of individual hyperparameter domains, often with strict subsets due to dependencies among hyperparameters. For instance, certain parameters of a deep belief network's third layer become irrelevant if the network's depth is limited to one or two layers. 

The concept of conditional hyperparameters is introduced, where a hyperparameter is active only if another hyperparameter is set within a specific range, forming a hierarchical structure similar to a tree or directed acyclic graph. The optimization problem is formally defined as minimizing a loss function over the hyperparameter space.

Furthermore, the paper introduces the Combined Algorithm Selection and Hyperparameter Optimization (CASH) problem, which involves selecting the best algorithm and its corresponding hyperparameters from a set of algorithms. This can be reframed as a single hierarchical optimization problem that incorporates a new root-level hyperparameter to facilitate algorithm selection while conditioning the parameters of each algorithm's hyperparameter space on this selection.
