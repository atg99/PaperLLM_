It points out that constraints can be binary (success or failure) or unknown, with examples drawn from AutoML contexts. 

The simplest method to handle constraint violations involves assigning a penalty value for failed runs. More sophisticated approaches model the likelihood of constraint violations and seek configurations that minimize loss while avoiding such violations. Additionally, Bayesian optimization frameworks that utilize information-theoretic acquisition functions can separate the evaluation of target functions and constraints, allowing for efficient resource allocation during the optimization process. This is particularly beneficial when different evaluations require significantly varying amounts of time.
