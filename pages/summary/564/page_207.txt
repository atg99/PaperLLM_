The paper discusses challenges in evaluating modeling difficulty in machine learning, particularly underfitting and the selection of predictive models. It highlights that underfitting can be diagnosed by comparing the variance or entropy of predictions against target values. Given the uncertainty about the model class, data scientists often resort to generic models, which range from simple, highly biased models to complex, versatile models capable of learning intricate functions. 

To assess modeling difficulty indirectly, the authors compared the performance of the challenge winner's method against basic untuned models and Selective Naive Bayes (SNB), a regularized model. They analyzed datasets from the 2015/2016 challenge and noted that while some datasets appeared easy, others, such as MADELINE and DILBERT, presented significant modeling challenges despite low intrinsic difficulty. The authors observed that datasets introduced later showed high intrinsic difficulty, which could be misleading due to the novelty of these datasets to the machine learning community.

Furthermore, the authors aimed to predict intrinsic difficulty using meta features from OpenML, employing a Random Forest classifier. They identified three key meta features that correlate strongly with dataset difficulty: the performance of a decision tree classifier, the performance of a nearest neighbor classifier, and the minimum skewness of features, which indicates the symmetry of feature distributions. The findings contribute to understanding the complexities of modeling difficulty in machine learning.
