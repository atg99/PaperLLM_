The paper discusses the concept of full model selection in the context of hyper-models, which are composite models built from simpler components for tasks like classification. These hyper-models can integrate various techniques, including nearest neighbors, linear models, and neural networks, along with preprocessing and feature selection modules.

A predictive model is defined as y=f(x;α), where α represents model parameters, and a learning algorithm optimizes these parameters using training data. The trained model, referred to as a predictor, produces an output based on the input. The model's performance is assessed through an objective function J(f).

The hypothesis space for model selection is characterized by a vector θ of hyper-parameters, which includes both model-switching parameters and choices related to preprocessing and modeling techniques. This scenario is often referred to as full model selection or the CASH problem, which involves combined algorithm selection and hyperparameter optimization. The paper formalizes hyper-models as y=f(x;θ)=f(x;α(θ),θ), indicating that the model parameters α depend on the hyper-parameters θ, determined through a trainer using specific training data.

Model selection is framed as a bi-level optimization problem, with a lower objective J1 for training model parameters and an upper objective J2 for training hyper-parameters. Both objectives must be optimized concurrently. The paper also highlights that model selection involves multiple testing, where the performance prediction error increases with the complexity of the hyper-model. A crucial aspect of AutoML is to regularize the upper-level objective J2 to prevent overfitting, similar to how lower-level objectives J1 are regularized.
