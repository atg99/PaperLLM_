The paper analyzes the AutoML Challenge Series from 2015 to 2018, focusing on the relationship between modeling difficulty and intrinsic difficulty of datasets. In the analysis, modeling difficulty is assessed through the performance of the best untuned model across various algorithms such as KNN, NaiveBayes, RandomForest, and SGD, as well as through a Selective Naive Bayes model. Higher scores indicate better performance, with negative or NaN scores adjusted to zero. The findings are visually represented, highlighting that datasets with low intrinsic difficulty but high modeling difficulty are optimal for benchmarking.

Additionally, the paper examines meta-features that predict dataset intrinsic difficulty based on data from the 2015/2016 challenges. The GINI importances of these meta-features are derived using a random forest regressor, which predicts the highest leaderboard scores based on dataset characteristics. The correlations between these meta-features and score medians are depicted, with blue indicating positive correlations and red indicating negative correlations. This analysis provides insights into the factors that influence model performance and dataset complexity in the context of AutoML challenges.
