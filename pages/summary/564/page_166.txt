Chapter 9 of the paper discusses the Automatic Statistician project, which aims to automate data science processes, enabling the generation of predictions and human-readable reports from raw datasets with minimal human involvement. The reports produced include basic graphs and statistics, along with curated high-level insights derived from three main components: automated model construction, model comparison, and a software component that translates results into natural language.

The introduction highlights the close relationship between Machine Learning (ML) and data science, emphasizing their role in advancing artificial intelligence (AI). However, it points out significant limitations in current ML approaches, particularly their black-box nature, which complicates interpretation, understanding, and trust. This opacity poses challenges in critical applications such as healthcare, criminal justice, and autonomous vehicles, where explainability is essential. The chapter notes ongoing efforts to develop explainable AI systems that prioritize interpretability, trust, and transparency in machine learning applications.
