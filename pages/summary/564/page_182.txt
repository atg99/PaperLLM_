The paper presents an analysis of the AutoML Challenge Series conducted from 2015 to 2018, focusing on medium-sized datasets under 200 MBytes and constrained computational resources, specifically targeting execution times of under 20 minutes on a standard machine configuration. The authors excluded certain learning methodologies such as unsupervised and active learning due to their distinct evaluation settings, which complicate comparisons. However, they retained various data modalities like video and text, provided these were preprocessed into feature representations to simplify the challenge.

The challenge included a range of complexities, encompassing diverse data distributions, tasks (regression, binary and multi-class classification), and scoring metrics (AUC, BAC, MSE, F1). It also considered factors such as class balance, sparsity, missing values, categorical variables, irrelevant features, and the size of training examples and variables. Participants faced numerous modeling and hyper-parameter decisions within this framework.

While the challenge addressed significant aspects of automating machine learning, several critical areas remain for future exploration. These include data ingestion and formatting, pre-processing, feature learning, handling of biased data, and the management of large datasets. The paper highlights the need for further research into matching algorithms to specific problems, active learning, and the generation of meaningful reports, emphasizing the ongoing evolution and challenges in the field of automated machine learning.
