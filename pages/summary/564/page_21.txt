Bayesian optimization is a leading framework for the global optimization of expensive blackbox functions, particularly gaining prominence in hyperparameter optimization (HPO) for deep learning applications like image classification, speech recognition, and neural language modeling. This section provides an overview of Bayesian optimization, including its surrogate models, extensions for conditional and constrained spaces, and its applications in HPO.

Recent advancements in Bayesian optimization have shifted from treating HPO as a blackbox to incorporating techniques such as multi-fidelity HPO, meta-learning, and pipeline structures. Additionally, developments not specifically aimed at HPO, such as new acquisition functions and parallelization methods, can still be effectively applied to HPO scenarios.

At its core, Bayesian optimization involves an iterative process with two main components: a probabilistic surrogate model and an acquisition function that guides the selection of the next evaluation point. The surrogate model is updated with observations of the target function, and the acquisition function, which evaluates the utility of candidate points, balances exploration and exploitation. A commonly used acquisition function is expected improvement (EI), which can be computed in closed form under certain conditions, allowing for efficient evaluations compared to the costly blackbox function.

Overall, Bayesian optimization presents a robust approach for optimizing hyperparameters in various complex settings, leveraging its ability to model uncertainty and make informed decisions about where to sample next.
