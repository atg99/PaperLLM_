The paper discusses the evolution of the AutoML challenge series, beginning with the NIPS 2006 "model selection game." Participants utilized a machine learning toolbox to construct models through various modules, focusing on model selection rather than developing new algorithms. The challenge highlighted the importance of cross-validation techniques and innovative search methods.

In the 2015/2016 AutoML challenge, a new structure was introduced where each dataset came with a specific scoring metric and a fixed time budget of 20 minutes for model optimization. This change aimed to enhance time management for participants. Additionally, participants were allowed to submit any Linux executable, facilitated by the Codalab platform, and provided with a Python starting kit based on scikit-learn. This led to the creation of wrappers around scikit-learn, notably the winning entry, "auto-sklearn."

Subsequently, a "beat auto-sklearn" competition was held, where participants attempted to manually adjust hyper-parameters to outperform auto-sklearn, but none succeeded. The competition allowed for direct comparisons of search methods using the same set of hyper-models.

The AutoML challenge was integrated into major conferences like IJCNN and featured numerous related events, including workshops and bootcamps. The design details of the AutoML challenge were documented in associated publications.
