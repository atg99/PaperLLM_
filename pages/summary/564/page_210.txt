The paper presents an analysis of the AutoML Challenge Series from 2015 to 2018, focusing on the application of linear discriminant analysis (LDA). It illustrates a scatter plot of datasets represented in principal axes, where the LDA was trained using meta features to predict the winning model among four basic models: Naive Bayes (NB), Stochastic Gradient Descent with a linear kernel (SGD-linear), K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN), and Random Forest (RF). The performance of these models was evaluated using standard metrics with default hyper-parameters. The scatter plot visualizes datasets as points in the space defined by the first two LDA components, with colors indicating the winning model and opacity representing the performance scores of the winning model. Additionally, the paper discusses the importance of meta features, computed as scaling factors for each LDA component, highlighting their role in model selection.
