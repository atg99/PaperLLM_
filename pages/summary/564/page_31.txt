The paper discusses the evolution of HPO methods, starting from grid search and early adaptive techniques like greedy depth-first search and pattern search, which improved upon default configurations. It highlights the introduction of genetic algorithms in 2004 for tuning hyperparameters of support vector machines (SVMs) and neural networks, showcasing their efficiency over grid search.

The use of Covariance Matrix Adaptation Evolution Strategy (CMA-ES) for HPO was first documented in 2005, demonstrating its effectiveness in optimizing multiple hyperparameters of SVMs and later deep neural networks, especially in parallel settings. The expansion of HPO to Full Model Selection was introduced in 2009 by Escalante et al., who developed the particle swarm model selection (PSMS) method, combining various algorithms into a unified pipeline without needing domain expertise.

Bayesian optimization emerged as a powerful tool for HPO around the same time, with early applications demonstrating significant speed improvements over grid search. Notably, Bergstra et al. in 2011 leveraged Bayesian optimization for deep neural networks, showing superior results compared to manual and random searches, thus establishing a new standard for tuning complex models. The paper underscores the continuous advancements in HPO methodologies and their impact on machine learning performance across diverse applications.
