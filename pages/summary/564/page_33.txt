This section discusses open problems and future research directions in hyperparameter optimization (HPO). It emphasizes the need for a standardized set of benchmarks to evaluate the strengths and weaknesses of various HPO methods. Current benchmarks, such as the COCO platform and HPOlib, have not achieved widespread acceptance comparable to COCO. The importance of clearly defined metrics is highlighted, noting that different studies often report performance using varying criteria, such as validation versus test set performance or based on function evaluations versus time. These differences can significantly impact the assessment of HPO methods.

The paper stresses the importance of reproducibility and the necessity for new benchmarks to be accompanied by strong baseline comparisons and implementations. It suggests that without a common software library, akin to those available in deep learning, the community may struggle with consistent evaluations and comparisons in HPO research. Overall, the section calls for collaborative efforts to establish benchmarks and metrics that can evolve with the field, ensuring fair comparisons and advancing the effectiveness of HPO methodologies.
