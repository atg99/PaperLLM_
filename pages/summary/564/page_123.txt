The paper discusses Auto-sklearn, which is an automated machine learning framework that utilizes a combination of meta-learning and Bayesian optimization to enhance the efficiency and robustness of machine learning tasks. The meta-learning component relies on meta-features—characteristics of datasets that can be computed quickly—to identify suitable algorithms for new datasets. This approach helps to rapidly suggest configurations that may perform well, while Bayesian optimization refines these configurations over time, albeit at a slower pace.

In detail, the authors describe an offline phase where they evaluated 140 datasets from the OpenML repository, employing Bayesian optimization to find effective instantiations of the ML framework for each dataset. They ran a process called SMAC for 24 hours, using 10-fold cross-validation to determine the best-performing framework instantiation for each dataset. For a new dataset, they calculate its meta-features, rank existing datasets based on their similarity to the new dataset, and select the top 25 nearest datasets to inform the Bayesian optimization process.

The paper outlines the implementation of 38 meta-features, which include various statistical measures about the datasets. However, it notes the exclusion of landmarking meta-features due to their high computational cost. The authors emphasize that the effectiveness of this meta-learning approach is bolstered by the growing repository of datasets, particularly with initiatives like OpenML, which is expected to expand the dataset availability and enhance the capabilities of automated machine learning.
