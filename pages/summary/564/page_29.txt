The section discusses Hyperparameter Optimization (HPO), focusing on the HyperBand strategy and its advancements through the BOHB approach. HyperBand addresses the challenge of selecting from random configurations by dividing the total budget into various combinations of configurations and corresponding budgets. It employs a technique called successive halving, which allows it to perform competitively against vanilla random search, particularly benefiting from low-fidelity evaluations. HyperBand has demonstrated superior performance over traditional methods in tuning deep neural networks and other algorithms.

However, HyperBand's limitation lies in its static configuration proposal strategy, which does not adapt based on function evaluations. To address this, BOHB merges Bayesian optimization with HyperBand, leveraging the strengths of both approaches. BOHB offers quick initial improvements through low-fidelity evaluations while ensuring strong overall performance by integrating Bayesian optimization in place of random search. It effectively utilizes parallel resources and accommodates various hyperparameter domains, employing multidimensional kernel density estimators for its Bayesian component.

Additionally, alternative strategies for combining multiple fidelity evaluations with HPO are discussed. These include conducting HPO on smaller data subsets to identify promising configurations for further tuning on larger datasets, as well as iteratively removing underperforming algorithms from the configuration space to expedite solutions to the Configuration-Aware Search (CASH) problem. The section also suggests the potential for adaptive fidelity selection, moving beyond predefined schedules to actively choose evaluation fidelities based on prior observations.
