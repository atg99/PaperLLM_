This chapter by J. Vanschoren explores the concept of meta-learning, which involves leveraging prior experience from similar tasks to improve the performance of machine learning models on new tasks. The chapter is structured around different meta-learning approaches, emphasizing the importance of task similarity in effectively utilizing prior meta-data. 

Meta-learning is defined as learning that draws from experiences with previous tasks, and the challenge lies in determining task similarity. When tasks are unrelated, prior knowledge may not be beneficial; however, real-world scenarios often present opportunities for learning from past experiences.

The chapter categorizes meta-learning techniques based on the type of meta-data utilized. In Section 2.2, the focus is on learning from model evaluations, where the goal is to recommend useful configurations and explore configuration search spaces. This involves using evaluations from prior tasks to inform the selection of configurations for new tasks.

Section 2.3 discusses characterizing tasks to better express task similarity, allowing for the development of meta-models that understand the relationship between data characteristics and learning performance. Section 2.4 addresses the transfer of trained model parameters between similar tasks, facilitating transfer learning and few-shot learning.

The chapter clarifies that while multi-task learning and ensemble learning can complement meta-learning, they do not inherently involve learning from prior experiences on other tasks. The insights presented are based on a recent survey article, indicating a comprehensive examination of the field.
