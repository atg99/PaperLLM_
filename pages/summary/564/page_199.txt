2018, a single round of an AutoML competition was organized, consisting of two phases. The first phase allowed participants to practice on five datasets and submit either code or results, with immediate feedback provided via a leaderboard. The final submission from this phase automatically transitioned to the second phase, a "blind test" where participants' code was evaluated on five new datasets, which were kept undisclosed. Only submissions that included code capable of automated training and testing were eligible for ranking and prizes.

Participants used a starting kit similar to that of the previous 2015/2016 challenge, with additional access to the winning code from that challenge. The results section outlines the outcomes of both challenges, detailing the methods used by participants and their innovative elements, along with analyses of post-challenge experiments focused on the efficacy of model search techniques.

The 2015/2016 challenge spanned 18 months and concluded with practical, open-sourced solutions from the winners. Results from both the AutoML blind testing and final phases were presented in a table, highlighting top-ranking participants. Graphical comparisons showed minimal overfitting to the validation set, except for a few outliers. Performance patterns indicated a decline in participation as tasks became more challenging, with some participants achieving significantly better results in manual tweaking phases compared to AutoML performances. The findings suggest that there remains potential for improvement through manual adjustments and increased computational resources, as indicated by notable performance discrepancies between the Tweakathon and AutoML results.
