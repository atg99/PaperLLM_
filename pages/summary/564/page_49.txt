This paper discusses alternative methods for optimizing hyperparameter settings in machine learning algorithms. One approach involves learning an optimal default setting for hyperparameters, determining their importance based on the performance improvement gained from tuning them compared to their default values. This method was evaluated using a substantial dataset from OpenML experiments involving various algorithms and datasets. The default values are identified through training surrogate models and sampling configurations to minimize average risk across tasks.

Another method presented involves learning defaults independently, based on configurations that frequently appear in the top-performing configurations for each task. It also considers meta-features that could influence optimal settings and uses statistical tests to assess whether a hyperparameter can remain at its default without significant performance loss.

The paper also introduces the concept of configuration transfer, which provides recommendations for new tasks by evaluating how similar they are to previous tasks. This involves assessing performance similarities and using this information to train a meta-learner that predicts optimal configurations for new tasks. The notion of relative landmarks, which measures performance differences between configurations on specific tasks, is employed in an active testing framework. This method starts with a globally best configuration and iteratively selects competing configurations that outperform it on similar tasks, enhancing the optimization process.
