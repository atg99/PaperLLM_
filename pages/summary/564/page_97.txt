The paper discusses Auto-WEKA, a framework for automatic model selection and hyperparameter tuning in machine learning. It presents a table listing various datasets, detailing the number of discrete and continuous attributes, classes, and the sizes of training and test sets. The datasets vary in complexity and type, including examples like Dexter, GermanCredit, and CIFAR-10.

The authors note an issue observed in early experiments where the Sequential Model-Based Optimization (SMBO) method in Auto-WEKA selected hyperparameters that excelled in training but failed to generalize effectively, indicating a risk of overfitting. To address this, the training set was split into 70% for the SMBO method and 30% for validation, allowing for better detection of overfitting.

Additionally, the paper outlines baseline methods for comparison, highlighting a straightforward approach for non-expert users: performing 10-fold cross-validation on the training set using default hyperparameters for each classifier. This method, referred to as Ex-Def, serves as a benchmark for assessing the performance of Auto-WEKA against conventional practices.
