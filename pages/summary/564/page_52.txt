The section discusses the importance of learning curves and task properties in meta-learning. Learning curves provide insights into how model performance evolves as more training data is introduced. By dividing training into steps and measuring performance at each step, we can generate a learning curve for a given configuration on a specific task. This information can be leveraged to enhance hyperparameter optimization and to predict performance on new tasks by utilizing partially observed learning curves.

The paper introduces a method to assess task similarity based on the shapes of partial learning curves, allowing for the prediction of a configuration's effectiveness on an unseen task by referencing the complete learning curves of similar tasks. This approach can expedite the search for optimal configurations and has shown success when combined with active testing. However, it notes that existing methods predicting learning curves during neural architecture searches have not yet utilized past learning curves from other tasks.

Additionally, the paper highlights the significance of task properties, described through meta-features that characterize each task. By representing tasks as vectors of these meta-features, the similarity between tasks can be quantified, facilitating the transfer of knowledge from similar tasks to new ones. This information, along with prior evaluations, can be used to train a meta-learner capable of forecasting the performance of configurations on new tasks.
