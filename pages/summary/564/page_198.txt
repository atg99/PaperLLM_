The paper discusses the structure and phases of the AutoML Challenge Series from 2015 to 2018, highlighting the organization and participation dynamics across different rounds. Each round consisted of distinct phases, including the AutoML phase, Tweakathon, and Final phase, each with specific goals, durations, and submission guidelines. Notably, the introduction of a GPU track in Round 4 aimed to encourage the use of advanced deep learning techniques.

Participants had to submit code or results during the Tweakathon phase, with the option to submit both to enhance scoring. Immediate feedback was available through a leaderboard based on validation data, while final rankings were determined by test performance. The challenge provided baseline software using the scikit-learn library, emphasizing the importance of a solid starting point, albeit with the risk of biasing participants towards specific methods.

The paper argues for a shift from long challenges to annual events, suggesting that this format could alleviate concerns of new participants feeling disadvantaged while fostering a balance between competition and collaboration. The changes aim to enhance engagement and inclusivity in future AutoML challenges.
