The paper discusses metrics for evaluating predictive models, specifically focusing on the F1 score and Probabilistic Accuracy (PAC). The F1 score is adjusted based on the expected value for random predictions, while PAC is derived from cross-entropy and log loss. The formulas presented normalize these metrics against a baseline score, R, which is calculated based on the predictions made from the positive class examples.

The paper also details the structure of the 2015/2016 challenge, which was organized into six rounds, including a preparation round and five rounds of increasing difficulty. Each round, except the first and last, consisted of three phases alternating between AutoML contests and Tweakathons. Participants submitted their code only during the Tweakathon phases, and results were tracked on a leaderboard. New participants could join at any time, and prizes were awarded during specific phases, ensuring that the challenge remained accessible and competitive throughout its duration.
