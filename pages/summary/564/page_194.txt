The paper analyzes the AutoML Challenge Series from 2015 to 2018, focusing on the structure and rules of the challenges designed to foster the development of fully automated machine learning solutions. During the challenges, datasets were kept confidential to eliminate the influence of domain knowledge on participants. The 2015/2016 challenge featured a two-phase approach, alternating between development (Tweakathon) and blind testing (AutoML phases). Participants could submit either results or code during development but were required to submit code for rankings in the blind test phase. 

In contrast, the 2018 challenge simplified the protocol to just one round, comprising a development phase with practice datasets and a final blind test phase with new datasets. The challenges were hosted on the Codalab platform, which provided shared computational resources. In the earlier challenges, participants used up to 10 compute workers with substantial memory, while in 2018, resources were reduced to encourage more efficient solutions. Each submission was evaluated within a time budget, typically set at 1200 seconds per dataset, although participants were not informed of this limit beforehand. Participants had the option to submit results without code, which could provide advantages for certain entries. However, code submissions were required for participation in the blind test phases, where result submissions were not permitted.
