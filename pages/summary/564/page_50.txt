Meta-learning is explored through various methods to assess task similarities and transfer knowledge effectively. The first method involves evaluating task similarities based on the performance of configurations across tasks. This method, however, is limited as it relies on configurations that have been evaluated on multiple prior tasks.

A more flexible approach utilizes surrogate models, which predict outcomes for prior tasks based on available performance data. Task similarity is defined by the predictive accuracy of these surrogate models. For instance, Wistuba et al. employ Gaussian Processes to create surrogate models for each prior task and for new tasks, combining their predictions using a weighted average based on task similarity, determined by relative landmarks. This method enhances the influence of tasks that are more similar to the new task.

Feurer et al. advance this concept by integrating predictive distributions from individual Gaussian processes, maintaining the model's Gaussian nature while weighting according to generalization performance. Additionally, meta-data can be incorporated into the acquisition function rather than solely the surrogate model, allowing for a weighted average of expected improvements from both new and prior tasks.

Lastly, warm-started multi-task learning is discussed, where joint task representations are developed through task-specific surrogate models, with a feedforward Neural Network learning suitable configurations. This collective approach aims to leverage prior evaluations to enhance performance on new tasks.
