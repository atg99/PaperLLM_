Chapter 5 of the paper discusses Hyperopt-Sklearn, a software project aimed at automating the algorithm configuration process for the Scikit-learn machine learning library. Building on the Auto-Weka framework, the authors argue that the selection of classifiers and preprocessing modules should be treated as a unified hyperparameter optimization problem. Hyperopt is employed to define a comprehensive search space that includes various standard components, such as Support Vector Machines (SVM), Random Forests (RF), K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN), Principal Component Analysis (PCA), and Term Frequency-Inverse Document Frequency (TFIDF).

The chapter presents evidence that navigating this search space is both practical and effective through the use of Hyperopt's search algorithms applied to benchmark datasets like MNIST, 20-Newsgroups, and Convex Shapes. Notably, the authors report improvements over previously established best-known scores for MNIST and Convex Shapes at the time of publication.

In the introduction, the authors highlight that while traditional algorithms like SVMs and RFs have relatively few hyperparameters, manual tuning and basic search methods can yield satisfactory results. However, they advocate for a broader perspective where the choice of classifier itself is treated as a hyperparameter. This approach extends to the configuration of preprocessing components, framing the entire process as a model selection and hyperparameter optimization challenge.
