The paper discusses the challenges of selecting machine learning algorithms and tuning their hyperparameters, a process that can significantly impact performance. Users often rely on algorithm reputation or default settings, which can lead to suboptimal outcomes. To address this, the authors introduce the combined algorithm selection and hyperparameter optimization (CASH) problem, which aims to automate the simultaneous selection of algorithms and tuning of hyperparameters to enhance empirical performance.

Despite previous research on model selection and hyperparameter optimization, the authors note a lack of comprehensive methods addressing the CASH problem, particularly due to the complexity of navigating the high-dimensional, noisy response space that includes both categorical and continuous variables. The paper highlights meta-learning strategies that utilize past dataset characteristics to inform algorithm choices but emphasizes that their proposed CASH approach starts anew for each dataset.

The authors propose a framework where algorithm selection is treated as a hyperparameter, allowing for the application of advanced Bayesian optimization techniques to efficiently explore the algorithm-hyperparameter space. They define the CASH problem and demonstrate its practical application using the WEKA package. Their findings indicate that leveraging this combined approach often leads to superior model performance compared to traditional methods, particularly on larger datasets, with specific Bayesian optimization methods like TPE and SMAC showing promising results.
