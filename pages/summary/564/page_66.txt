The section on meta-learning presents a compilation of key studies and advancements in the field, focusing on various methodologies and applications. 

Feurer et al. (2018) explore scalable meta-learning techniques for Bayesian optimization, while their earlier work (2015) discusses automated machine learning that is both efficient and robust. They further investigate the use of ranking-weighted Gaussian process ensembles for Bayesian optimization in their 2018 AutoML workshop contribution. In a 2014 conference paper, Feurer et al. highlight the role of meta-learning in initializing hyperparameter optimization.

Finn et al. (2017) introduce model-agnostic meta-learning, which enables rapid adaptation of deep networks, and later (2018) discuss the universality of deep representations and gradient descent in approximating learning algorithms. Other notable contributions include Fusi et al. (2018) who apply probabilistic matrix factorization to automated machine learning and Gil et al. (2018) who propose a phased performance-based pipeline planner for automated machine learning.

Giraud-Carrier (2008) provides a tutorial on meta-learning, while Giraud-Carrier and Provost (2005) assess the implications of the no free lunch theorem on meta-learning. Golovin et al. (2017) present Google Vizier, a service for black-box optimization, and Gomes et al. (2012) combine meta-learning with search techniques for parameter selection in support vector machines.

The section also references foundational concepts in machine learning, such as the complexity measures of supervised classification (Ho and Basu, 2002) and the long short-term memory architecture (Hochreiter and Schmidhuber, 1997).

Overall, the references provide a broad overview of meta-learning, emphasizing its importance in optimizing machine learning processes and enhancing model performance through innovative techniques.
