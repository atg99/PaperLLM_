In this chapter, the authors discuss model selection as an optimization problem in machine learning, specifically using the Hyperopt optimization library alongside Scikit-Learn. Model selection involves identifying the best-performing machine learning model from a potentially infinite set of options, with the optimization task framed around hyperparameters that influence model performance. The objective function, which is typically a measure of success like accuracy or F1-Score, is often approached as a minimization problem using techniques such as cross-validation to enhance robustness.

The authors highlight the range of algorithms available in Scikit-Learn for both classification and regression, along with preprocessing methods that transform data into suitable formats for these algorithms. A key feature of Scikit-Learn is its pipeline data structure, which allows users to combine preprocessing steps and classifiers into a single component. While Hyperopt-sklearn does not formally utilize this pipeline object, it offers similar functionalities by parameterizing a search space that includes various combinations of preprocessing methods and classifiers.

The search space at the time of writing encompasses 24 classifiers, 12 regressors, and 7 preprocessing methods, comprising 65 hyperparameters in total. Despite the large number of hyperparameters, individual models typically involve a smaller subset of active hyperparameters. The Hyperopt description language is employed to distinguish between conditional and non-conditional hyperparameters, thereby enhancing the efficiency of the model selection process. The authors suggest that the open-source nature of this project will lead to future expansions of the search space as contributions from users increase.
