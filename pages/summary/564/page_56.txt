The paper discusses various approaches in meta-learning, specifically focusing on surrogate models for Bayesian optimization in hyperparameter tuning. Schilling et al. propose a modified multilayer perceptron that incorporates meta-features and binary indications to represent task similarities, although it lacks uncertainty representation. To address this, they use an ensemble of 100 multilayer perceptrons for better predictive performance.

Yogatama and Mann introduce a scalable Bayesian surrogate model that considers only tasks similar to the new task, defined by the Euclidean distance between meta-feature vectors. They standardize the values to manage different scales. Feurer et al. enhance scalability by sorting prior tasks based on a broader set of meta-features and employing warm-starting techniques to optimize the surrogate model further.

Collaborative filtering is also explored as a means to recommend promising configurations, where tasks serve as ratings for configurations. This approach faces the cold start problem, addressed by Yang et al. through D-optimal experimental design to sample initial evaluations. Misir and Sebag utilize meta-features to mitigate this issue, while Fusi et al. adopt a probabilistic matrix factorization method that integrates Bayesian optimization for effective pipeline configuration optimization. Overall, these methods illustrate the evolving strategies in leveraging meta-learning for efficient hyperparameter tuning.
