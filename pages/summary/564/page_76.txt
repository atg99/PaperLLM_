In this section, the authors discuss the concept of a cell search space in neural architecture design, highlighting the work of Zoph et al. and Zhong et al. who propose the use of smaller architectural motifs called cells or blocks instead of designing entire architectures from scratch. These cells include normal cells that maintain input dimensionality and reduction cells that decrease spatial dimensions. The key advantages of this approach are twofold: it significantly reduces the search space size, allowing for faster optimization—Zoph et al. report a seven-times speed-up over previous methods while achieving improved performance—and it facilitates the transfer of optimized cells across different datasets, as demonstrated by their successful application from CIFAR-10 to ImageNet. This cell-based methodology has influenced numerous subsequent studies, indicating its effectiveness in neural architecture search. However, the authors note that this approach introduces new design choices that must be addressed.
