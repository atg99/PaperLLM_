In this section, the paper discusses the concept of meta-models, which are designed to learn the complex relationships between a task's meta-features and the effectiveness of specific configurations. A meta-model, denoted as L, can recommend the most suitable configurations for a new task based on its meta-features.

The paper references a substantial amount of prior research on meta-models for algorithm selection and hyperparameter recommendation, indicating that methods such as boosted and bagged trees often yield optimal predictions, although results vary with different meta-features.

The section further explores the ranking capabilities of meta-models, noting that a k-nearest neighbor (kNN) meta-model can identify similar tasks and rank the best configurations accordingly. It highlights specific ranking-focused meta-models, including predictive clustering trees and Approximate Ranking Tree Forests (ART Forests), which excel in meta-feature selection and robustness, especially with limited prior tasks. Additionally, it mentions the use of autoBagging and other methods to rank configurations based on past datasets.

Performance prediction is another key function of meta-models, allowing for the estimation of a configuration's performance metrics, such as accuracy or training time, based on its meta-features. Early approaches employed linear regression or rule-based regressors for performance ranking, while more recent methods include SVM meta-regressors that predict accuracy or optimized performance based on a new task's meta-features. The section concludes by noting that meta-regressors can also be utilized to estimate algorithm training and prediction times.
