The paper discusses a methodology for optimizing kernel models using the conjugate-gradient method and evaluating them through the Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC). BIC balances model complexity against data fit, with the model's likelihood being integrated with latent Gaussian Processes. The best-performing model from each iteration is used to propose new models, either by expanding kernels with grammar production rules or mutating them by changing base kernels. The iterative search continues until no new proposals outperform the best existing model or a predefined search depth is reached.

While this greedy search approach does not guarantee finding the globally optimal model, it aims to identify a good and interpretable model efficiently. Alternative search methods are mentioned, such as Bayesian optimization and particle filtering.

Upon completion of the search, the top kernel expressions are scored, and the one with the highest score is translated into natural language. This conversion involves transforming the kernel into a canonical form, which includes flattening nested sums and products and simplifying certain kernel products into base kernels with adjusted parameters.
