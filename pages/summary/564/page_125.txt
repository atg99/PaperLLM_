The paper discusses the development of Auto-sklearn, an automated machine learning (AutoML) system built on the widely-used scikit-learn library. This choice is due to scikit-learn's robust implementation of a variety of machine learning algorithms, making it accessible for both novice and expert users. Auto-sklearn resembles Auto-WEKA but is distinct in its approach, focusing solely on base classifiers rather than including meta-models and ensembles that complicate hyperparameter tuning.

Auto-sklearn's architecture includes 15 classification algorithms, 14 preprocessing methods, and 4 data preprocessing techniques, resulting in a configuration space of 110 hyperparameters. Many of these hyperparameters are conditional, activating only when their corresponding components are selected, a feature effectively managed by the SMAC algorithm used in Auto-sklearn.

The classification algorithms span various categories, including linear models, support vector machines, decision trees, and ensembles. The design choice to limit the number of hyperparameters allows Auto-sklearn to be more data-efficient than Auto-WEKA, which has a significantly larger hyperparameter space due to its inclusion of complex ensembles. This efficiency is enhanced by Auto-sklearn's post-hoc method for constructing ensembles, allowing for effective evaluation without the overhead of evaluating multiple component classifiers simultaneously.
