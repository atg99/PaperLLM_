The section discusses the functionalities and design elements of Automatic Statistician systems, highlighting their capability to generate human-readable reports and perform various data-driven tasks. These systems can provide raw predictions or make decisions based on data, extending beyond traditional Gaussian processes or grammars to include regression and classification tasks.

A key aspect of these systems is the selection, evaluation, and comparison of models, which are interdependent processes. The selection strategy component is crucial in determining which models to evaluate, influenced by the dataset's variable types, such as distinguishing between continuous and discrete data. 

Model evaluation involves training selected models on part of the dataset and assessing their performance on held-out data, with the ability to parallelize evaluations across multiple CPUs or computers, enhancing efficiency. 

The report curator component is responsible for compiling the final report, deciding which results to include based on evaluation outcomes. This may involve detailing the best-fitting models, providing extrapolations, and incorporating additional sections like model criticism or recommendations. In some cases, the output may consist of raw predictions or model source code instead of a traditional report.
