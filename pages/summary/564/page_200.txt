The paper analyzes the results of the AutoML Challenge Series from 2015 to 2018, focusing on the performance of participants across different rounds. Key metrics include average rank (R) and average score (S) over five datasets, along with the percent increase (UP) in performance from the AutoML phase to the Final phase.

In the 2015/2016 challenge, notable winners and their ranks and scores are presented. For instance, the team "ideal" achieved the highest average rank in the first round, while "aad" consistently performed well throughout multiple rounds. The data reveals significant performance increases between phases, with some teams showing substantial improvements, such as "djaj," which had an impressive UP of 481% from the AutoML to the Final phase in the third round.

The paper also discusses participant performance in the context of overfitting and the benefits of manual tuning in Tweakathons compared to AutoML. It shows that most participants did not overfit their models based on validation set performances visible during the competition. Additionally, a comparative analysis highlights the performance gap between AutoML and Tweakathons, illustrating that manual adjustments and additional resources can lead to improved outcomes for participants. Overall, the findings provide insights into the effectiveness of various strategies in automated machine learning competitions.
