The paper discusses various evaluation metrics used in the AutoML Challenge Series from 2015 to 2018, focusing on Mean Absolute Error (MAE), Mean Absolute Deviation (MAD), Balanced Accuracy (BAC), Area Under the Curve (AUC), and the F1 score. 

MAE and MAD are defined as the absolute differences between actual and predicted values, with MAE providing a straightforward measure of prediction error. BAC is introduced as a crucial metric for classification problems, calculated as the average of class-wise accuracy and the average of sensitivity and specificity for binary classifications. For binary cases, it is computed using true positives and true negatives, while for multi-class problems, it requires averaging across all classes.

The paper also explains the normalization process for BAC, which adjusts the metric based on expected values for random predictions. AUC, which measures the area under the ROC curve, is highlighted as important for ranking and binary classifications, with calculations performed separately for each class before averaging.

Additionally, the F1 score is discussed as the harmonic mean of precision and recall, with definitions provided for both precision and recall. Similar to BAC, the F1 score involves handling prediction thresholding and class averaging, followed by normalization.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of these metrics in assessing the performance of models in the AutoML challenges, ensuring a comprehensive understanding of evaluation standards in machine learning competitions.
