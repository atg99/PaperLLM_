The paper analyzes the AutoML Challenge Series from 2015 to 2018, focusing on the varying difficulty of tasks presented in different rounds. In round 4, two regression problems were introduced, but regression did not prove significantly harder than multiclass classification. Post round 3, dataset median scores stabilized around the overall median, suggesting that participants adapted to the challenges. Rounds 2 and 4 were noted as more difficult compared to round 3.

The 2018 challenge datasets highlighted issues such as extreme class imbalance, the presence of categorical variables, and high dimensionality as factors contributing to task difficulty. In contrast, the datasets from 2015 and 2016 presented challenges that were less predictable, with dataset size emerging as a critical factor pushing participants to enhance their computational efficiency.

The paper posits that binary and multi-label classification problems are generally easier than multiclass problems, which helps explain the higher median performances in rounds dominated by these types. To quantify task difficulty, the authors differentiate between intrinsic difficulty, related to noise levels, and modeling difficulty, linked to the bias and variance of estimators. They suggest that evaluating intrinsic difficulty is challenging without knowledge of the true function generating the data, thus using the winners' performance as a proxy for the best achievable outcomes, acknowledging potential biases and variances in this estimation.
