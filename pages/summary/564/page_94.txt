The paper discusses Sequential Model-Based Algorithm Configuration (SMAC), which utilizes various models, including random forests, to understand the relationship between hyperparameters and the loss function. Random forests are chosen for their effectiveness with discrete and high-dimensional data. SMAC addresses conditional parameters by assigning default values to inactive ones, allowing decision trees to focus on active hyperparameters.

The approach involves estimating the predictive mean and variance of the loss function using frequentist methods, which are then modeled as a Gaussian distribution. SMAC employs an expected improvement criterion to guide the search for better hyperparameter configurations, taking into account the performance of the best-known configuration.

Designed for robust optimization amidst noisy evaluations, SMAC keeps track of its best configuration and builds confidence in its estimates. The method progressively improves estimates by evaluating loss terms individually, balancing accuracy with computational efficiency. A configuration must outperform the current best in multiple comparisons before it can be considered a new incumbent, with the evaluation increasing in folds over time. Additionally, SMAC incorporates a diversification mechanism by randomly selecting every second configuration, allowing exploration of the search space while minimizing computational overhead.
