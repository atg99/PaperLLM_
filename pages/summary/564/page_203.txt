The paper discusses the difficulty of tasks in the 2015/2016 challenge, evaluating two indicators of task difficulty: intrinsic difficulty, determined by the performance of the winners, and modeling difficulty, indicated by the difference between the winner's performance and a baseline method, Selective Naive Bayes (SNB). Ideal tasks would exhibit low intrinsic difficulty and high modeling difficulty, aiding in participant differentiation.

Key observations include the challenges posed by datasets with a large number of classes, exemplified by DIONIS, which has 355 classes and is noted for its complexity due to size and class imbalance. In contrast, HELENA, with 100 classes, did not prove particularly difficult. Additionally, multi-class problems were generally found to be more challenging than binary classification tasks.

The paper also highlights the limited number of regression problems in the challenge, with only four datasets being used. Sparse data presented another significant challenge, particularly in datasets like ALEXIS, WALLIS, and GRIGORIS, which resulted in memory issues during the competition. Adjustments were made to increase server memory to mitigate these difficulties. Lastly, the authors anticipated that the feature-to-example ratio would contribute to the challenges faced, although this aspect is not fully elaborated in the excerpt.
