The paper discusses challenges encountered in machine learning competitions, particularly focusing on issues related to overfitting, dataset size, irrelevant features, evaluation metrics, time constraints, and class imbalance. It highlights that while modern algorithms can handle overfitting, the primary difficulty arose from handling large datasets, particularly those exceeding 20 million entries, such as ALBERT and DIONIS. Participants often struggled with memory management, leading to performance issues or program failures.

The presence of irrelevant features, termed "probes," was common in two-thirds of the datasets, complicating the task. The paper outlines six different metrics used for evaluation, noting that their application varied based on the type of task. A consistent time budget of 1200 seconds was enforced across datasets, which presented challenges for larger datasets.

Class imbalance emerged as a significant difficulty in the 2018 competition, with some datasets exhibiting extreme ratios, such as 1–1000, negatively impacting team performance. The paper includes a figure illustrating the distribution of participant performance across rounds, indicating improvements in median performance from the AutoML to Tweakathon phases, despite notable failures during certain rounds due to the introduction of larger datasets and sparse matrices. Overall, the paper emphasizes the need for careful consideration of dataset characteristics and challenges in future competitions.
