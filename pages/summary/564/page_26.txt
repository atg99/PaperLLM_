This section of the paper discusses multi-fidelity optimization in the context of hyperparameter optimization (HPO), addressing the challenges posed by large dataset sizes and complex models that can prolong blackbox performance evaluations. Training a single hyperparameter configuration on expansive datasets can take hours to days, prompting the need for more efficient tuning techniques.

Multi-fidelity methods are introduced as a solution to expedite the optimization process by using low fidelity approximations of the actual loss function. These methods allow for training on smaller data subsets, fewer iterations, or reduced feature sets, ultimately balancing the tradeoff between optimization performance and runtime. The authors highlight that despite the approximation error, the speed improvements often justify their use.

The discussion is structured into three main areas: first, methods that model an algorithm's learning curve to determine whether to continue training; second, selection methods that choose from a set of algorithms or hyperparameter configurations; and third, multi-fidelity approaches that decide which level of fidelity will yield the most information for optimizing hyperparameters.

The section also focuses on learning curve-based prediction for early stopping, where models assess performance across different dataset subsets or iterations. Learning curve extrapolation plays a critical role, allowing for the prediction of a configuration's performance based on partially observed data. This predictive approach aims to halt training when further resources are unlikely to improve results, utilizing a weighted combination of parametric functions to fit the learning curves effectively. The parameters and weights are optimized via Markov chain Monte Carlo methods, resulting in a predictive distribution that aids in decision-making during HPO.
