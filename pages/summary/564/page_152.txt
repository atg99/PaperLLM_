In this paper, the authors analyze and compare the performance of various versions of Auto-Net, specifically Auto-Net 1.0, ensembles of Auto-Net 1.0 and Auto-sklearn, as well as Auto-Net 2.0 with different worker configurations. The evaluation is based on error metrics across different datasets, specifically the medium-sized newsgroups dataset and the smaller dorothea dataset. Results indicate that Auto-Net 2.0 significantly outperforms Auto-Net 1.0 on the newsgroups dataset, particularly with four workers, making it competitive with ensembles of Auto-sklearn and Auto-Net 1.0. 

However, on the dorothea dataset, while Auto-Net 2.0 initially outperformed Auto-Net 1.0, the latter eventually surpassed it after sufficient time, which the authors attribute to the absence of ensembling in Auto-Net 2.0 and its larger search space. The paper concludes by emphasizing the advantages of combining Auto-Net with Auto-sklearn, leading to enhanced performance compared to using either tool independently. Additionally, early results from Auto-Net 2.0 suggest that a broader search space and the use of BOHB optimization yield promising outcomes, indicating potential for further improvement in automated neural network tuning.
