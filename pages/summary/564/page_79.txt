This paper discusses the evolution of neural architecture search (NAS) methods, particularly focusing on the integration of gradient-based optimization techniques with evolutionary algorithms for optimizing neural architectures. While traditional weight optimization methods like stochastic gradient descent (SGD) excel in supervised learning tasks, recent neuro-evolutionary methods leverage SGD for weight optimization and use evolutionary algorithms solely for architectural optimization.

In the evolutionary process, a population of models is evolved through mutations, which can include adding or removing layers, adjusting hyperparameters, or modifying training parameters. The fitness of offspring networks is evaluated based on their performance on validation sets and they are incorporated into the population.

The paper highlights various strategies for parent sampling and offspring generation in neuro-evolutionary methods. For instance, some methods utilize tournament selection for sampling parents, while others employ multi-objective Pareto front sampling. Additionally, different approaches exist regarding population updates and offspring initialization, with some methods allowing knowledge transfer from parent networks.

A comparative study within the paper reveals that both reinforcement learning and evolutionary methods perform similarly in test accuracy, although evolutionary methods demonstrate better anytime performance and smaller model sizes. Random search (RS) is shown to lag behind both methods, achieving slightly higher test errors. Overall, while evolutionary methods are competitive, gradient-based optimization remains the dominant approach for supervised learning tasks.
