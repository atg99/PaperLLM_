This chapter expands on the original Auto-sklearn framework introduced in a 2015 NeurIPS paper, focusing on its efficient and robust automated machine learning capabilities. It begins by framing AutoML as a Combined Algorithm Selection and Hyperparameter optimization (CASH) problem, referencing the foundational work of Auto-WEKA.

Two primary challenges in AutoML are identified: the variability in performance of different machine learning methods across datasets and the dependence of certain algorithms on hyperparameter tuning. Bayesian optimization is highlighted as a key technique to address hyperparameter optimization, which is essential since algorithm performance can significantly vary based on hyperparameter settings.

The CASH problem is formally defined, where a set of algorithms is assessed based on their hyperparameters and performance on a training dataset split into cross-validation folds. The objective is to jointly optimize the choice of algorithm and hyperparameters to minimize loss. This problem was previously addressed by the Auto-WEKA system, which utilized tree-based Bayesian optimization methods to effectively explore the hyperparameter space.

Bayesian optimization works by creating a probabilistic model to understand the relationship between hyperparameters and performance, balancing exploration and exploitation. While Gaussian process models excel in low-dimensional spaces, tree-based models are noted for their effectiveness in high-dimensional and structured scenarios, making them suitable for the complexities involved in AutoML.
