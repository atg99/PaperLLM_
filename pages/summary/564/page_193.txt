The paper discusses the datasets used in the 2018 AutoML challenge, which consisted solely of binary classification problems with a uniform evaluation metric of Area Under the Curve (AUC) and a time budget of 1200 seconds for each task. The challenge was divided into two phases: Phase 1 served as a development phase, while Phase 2 was a final blind test phase. 

The datasets varied in characteristics such as class balance, sparsity, missing values, category presence, and the ratio of training instances to features. Phase 1 included datasets like D, ARCENE, GINA, GUILLERMO, and a variant of L, while Phase 2 featured datasets labeled M, H, I, RICCARDO, and another variant of M. The challenge highlighted issues such as extreme class imbalance, the presence of categorical features, and temporal dependencies among instances, which posed additional complexities compared to previous challenges.

The design of the challenge emphasized thoroughness and fairness, focusing on supervised learning without human intervention. Notably, some datasets contained chronological sorting of instances, providing participants with an opportunity to leverage this information in developing their methods. Overall, the challenge aimed to push the boundaries of automated machine learning by introducing new datasets with varying levels of difficulty and data complexity.
