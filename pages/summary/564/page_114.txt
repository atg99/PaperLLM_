The paper discusses the performance of hyperopt-sklearn in hyperparameter optimization across three datasets: MNIST, 20 Newsgroups, and Convex Shapes. The results indicate that hyperopt-sklearn ranks among the top methods for MNIST without relying on image-specific knowledge, achieving an accuracy of 98.7%. In the 20 Newsgroups dataset, it attained a weighted-average F1 score of 0.856, which is competitive compared to other methods that used macro-average F1 scores. On the Convex Shapes dataset, hyperopt-sklearn outperformed both previous automated approaches and manual tuning, achieving an accuracy of 88.7%.

The paper presents a comparison of several approaches, highlighting the performance of hyperopt-sklearn relative to established methods. For instance, while the Committee of ConvNets achieved the highest accuracy on MNIST (99.8%), hyperopt-sklearn closely followed with 98.7%. In 20 Newsgroups, the Class-Feature-Centroid (CFC) model showed strong performance with an F1 score of 0.928, albeit typically inferior to SVMs due to limitations in centroid positioning.

The authors note that searching the full configuration space generally yields performance on par with more restricted searches, with SVC frequently identified as the best classifier across datasets, though optimal parameters varied significantly. The CFC model's unique centroid-based approach is mentioned, but it is not implemented in hyperopt-sklearn. Overall, the findings emphasize hyperopt-sklearn's effectiveness in automated model selection and parameter tuning across diverse datasets.
