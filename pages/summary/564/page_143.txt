This chapter discusses the Auto-Net framework, specifically focusing on its two versions, Auto-Net 1.0 and Auto-Net 2.0. The introduction emphasizes the relevance of automating deep learning processes, noting that Auto-Net is among various tools like Auto-Keras and Google’s Cloud AutoML that aim to achieve this goal. 

In Section 7.2, Auto-Net 1.0 is detailed, highlighting its implementation as an extension of Auto-sklearn. This version is designed to handle fully-connected feed-forward neural networks, which are applicable to various datasets, while the exploration of other network types is reserved for future iterations. The framework utilizes the Lasagne library, which is based on Theano, although the approach remains generally independent of specific neural network implementations.

Auto-Net 1.0 optimizes a total of 63 hyperparameters, distinguishing between layer-independent and per-layer hyperparameters. The configuration space is consistent across various supervised learning tasks, including binary and multiclass classification and regression. To manage computational efficiency, the number of layers in the network is limited to between one and six, balancing model complexity and training time. The optimization of neural network weights is achieved through stochastic gradient descent (SGD), which relies on backpropagation for partial derivative calculations. The design considers practical constraints, aiming to evaluate multiple configurations within a two-day timeframe on a single CPU.
