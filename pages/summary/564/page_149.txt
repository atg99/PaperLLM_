The paper discusses the development and evaluation of Auto-Net 1.0, an automated deep learning framework, in conjunction with Auto-sklearn. The validation and test sets are defined for performance measurement, and the framework is implemented on both CPU and GPU platforms, with the latter demonstrating significantly faster performance due to the matrix operations inherent in neural networks.

In the baseline evaluation, different configurations of Auto-Net 1.0 were tested across five datasets from the AutoML challenge. The experiments included comparisons between CPU and GPU versions, as well as the integration of neural networks with Auto-sklearn models. Each method underwent ten one-day runs, incorporating five-fold cross-validation within a fixed compute budget. Results indicated that the GPU-based Auto-Net was approximately ten times faster than its CPU counterpart. While the CPU version generally underperformed, it still provided improvements over Auto-sklearn in three out of five datasets when combined with Auto-sklearn.

The paper also highlights the successful use of the combined Auto-sklearn and GPU-based Auto-Net in the later phases of the AutoML competition, demonstrating the framework's effectiveness in automated model selection and tuning.
