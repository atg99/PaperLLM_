The study evaluates the performance of Auto-WEKA in optimizing machine learning algorithms compared to traditional methods such as grid search and random search. The authors found that even with limited computation time, Auto-WEKA outperformed grid search in 14 out of 21 datasets, while random search surpassed grid search in 9 cases. Notably, Auto-WEKA demonstrated significant improvements in cross-validation loss, achieving reductions exceeding 10% in 6 out of 21 instances.

To assess generalization, Auto-WEKA's selected models were trained on the entire training set and evaluated on a separate test set. Results showed that Auto-WEKA continued to outperform baseline methods, with performance differences being less pronounced than in cross-validation. Specifically, grid search was better than Ex-Def in 15 out of 21 cases, and random search outperformed grid search in 7 cases. Auto-WEKA excelled in 15 out of 21 cases, particularly on larger datasets, where the risk of overfitting was lower. In some cases, Auto-WEKA achieved over a 16% reduction in test misclassification rates.

The methodology involved using 70% of the training set for optimization and reserving 30% for evaluating overfitting risks. Throughout the process, Auto-WEKA tracked the best-performing hyperparameter configurations, allowing for a robust assessment of generalization performance on the withheld validation data.
