The paper discusses the performance of Auto-Net, an automated neural network tuning framework, particularly in the context of the tania dataset. It highlights the results from experiments where Auto-Net was run on both GPUs and CPUs, demonstrating that the GPU-based version outperformed Auto-sklearn. Specifically, Auto-Net achieved first place in the competition by constructing an ensemble of various neural network architectures and a logistic regression model after extensive training over 48 hours on eight GPUs.

The paper further compares Auto-Net versions 1.0 and 2.0, noting that Auto-Net 2.0 features a more comprehensive search space, which is expected to enhance performance on larger datasets. Despite the increased complexity of searching this larger space, Auto-Net 2.0 employs a multi-fidelity optimizer, BOHB, to efficiently manage resources by terminating underperforming models early. This design choice is anticipated to yield strong performance even when time is limited. Overall, the findings underscore the effectiveness of automated tuning in deep neural networks, particularly with the advancements in Auto-Net 2.0.
