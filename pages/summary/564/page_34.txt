This paper discusses the optimization of hyperparameters (HPO) in machine learning models, focusing on different methodologies. It highlights the importance of using solid implementations when comparing various optimization techniques, particularly emphasizing that simpler versions of Bayesian optimization can yield inferior performance. 

The paper also explores gradient-based optimization, which is applicable in scenarios where the gradient of the model selection criterion can be obtained with respect to hyperparameters, such as in least-squares support vector machines and neural networks. Unlike black-box HPO methods that yield a single float value per evaluation, gradient-based methods provide a hypergradient vector, enabling faster optimization.

Maclaurin et al. presented a method for calculating exact gradients of validation performance concerning continuous hyperparameters by backpropagating through the training process of stochastic gradient descent. This approach allows for efficient handling of multiple hyperparameters, leading to flexibility in model parametrization. They demonstrated its effectiveness across various high-dimensional HPO problems, such as optimizing learning rates and weight initialization scales.

However, this method increases the time complexity of training. To mitigate this, subsequent research has proposed performing hyperparameter updates using a separate validation set during training. Recent applications of gradient-based optimization for tuning model hyperparameters have shown promising results, often outperforming traditional Bayesian optimization methods. The ability to tune hundreds of hyperparameters indicates a potential for significant advancements in HPO.
