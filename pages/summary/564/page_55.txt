In this section, the paper discusses various landmarking methods and their rationale for estimating task performance based on certain meta-features. The methods include different variants such as Elite 1NN, Stump, RandomTree, and more, each focusing on specific characteristics like data sparsity, separability, and feature independence. These models are trained using a Siamese network architecture, allowing for the comparison of similar tasks by mapping them to the same regions in the latent space. This approach is beneficial for warm-starting Bayesian hyperparameter optimization and neural architecture search.

The concept of warm-starting optimization from similar tasks leverages meta-features to gauge task similarity, enhancing the efficiency of optimization processes. For instance, a genetic search algorithm can be initiated in regions containing promising solutions. The paper cites Gomes et al. and Reif et al. as examples of methods that utilize distances between task meta-features to identify the most similar prior tasks, subsequently using their best configurations to initialize optimization algorithms like Particle Swarm Optimization and genetic algorithms.

Model-based optimization also stands to gain from starting with a set of effective configurations. The SCoT method exemplifies this by using a surrogate model that predicts the ranking of configurations based on a small set of meta-features. Overall, the discussion highlights the importance of task similarity and meta-features in efficiently navigating the optimization landscape.
