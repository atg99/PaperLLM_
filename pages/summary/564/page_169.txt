The paper discusses the development of Automatic Statistician systems tailored for various objectives, particularly in the context of time series data. It highlights the significant advancements in automating machine learning methods, referencing earlier works such as statistical expert systems and the Robot Scientist, which integrates machine learning with experimental platforms. Notable projects like Auto-WEKA and Auto-sklearn are also mentioned for their use of Bayesian optimization in automating classifier learning.

The focus then shifts to a specific system, Automatic Bayesian Covariance Discovery (ABCD), developed for one-dimensional regression tasks. ABCD employs a flexible language of Gaussian process models structured through a compositional grammar over kernels. This approach allows for the creation of function distributions with distinct properties, such as linearity or periodicity, by utilizing various kernels.

The grammar over kernels is emphasized for its systematic and interpretable nature, enabling the representation of complex function properties in a way that can be easily understood and described in human language. The paper sets the foundation for discussing a broader taxonomy of Automatic Statistician systems, noting common design elements and architectural considerations.
