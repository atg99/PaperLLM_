The section discusses two key challenges in hyperparameter optimization (HPO): scalability and overfitting. 

In terms of scalability, it highlights the limitations of current HPO methods in addressing large-scale machine learning problems, particularly in the context of deep neural networks applied to the ImageNet dataset. The high costs of training models on such extensive datasets have hindered progress. The text suggests that innovative methods, such as multi-fidelity approaches, gradient-based techniques, and meta-learning, may provide solutions. Additionally, while parallel Bayesian optimization has been explored, there is a lack of methods that scale effectively to numerous workers, with most existing population-based approaches not applicable to larger datasets.

Regarding overfitting, the paper notes that HPO can suffer from overfitting to the limited validation data available, which can compromise generalization to unseen data. It points out that a simple strategy to mitigate this issue is to vary the train-validation splits for each evaluation, which has shown to enhance generalization in support vector machine tuning.

Overall, the section underscores the need for more advanced methods to overcome the scalability limitations and to improve generalization in hyperparameter optimization.
