The challenges highlighted the distinction between fixed time learning and anytime learning, emphasizing their respective practical applications. The 2015/2016 challenge featured a progressive difficulty in transfer learning, while the 2018 challenge simplified this aspect. 

The starting kit provided to participants led to a predominant reliance on ensemble learning, raising questions about potential bias in the approaches taken. The challenge protocol also revealed the difficulties of maintaining team focus over extended periods, suggesting a preference for shorter, more frequent events combined with workshops to foster collaboration and recognition. The success of the second challenge, which attracted a large number of participants in a shorter timeframe, supports this idea. Overall, the design choices made in the AutoML challenges contributed significantly to the fairness, reproducibility, and engagement of participants.
