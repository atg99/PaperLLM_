The paper discusses advancements in hyperparameter optimization (HPO) using Bayesian optimization techniques. It highlights the effectiveness of various approaches, such as Tree-structured Parzen Estimator (TPE) and random forests, in optimizing neural architectures and hyperparameters. A significant contribution by Snoek et al. in 2012, through the Spearmint system, introduced practical strategies for Gaussian process-based HPO, achieving state-of-the-art results for deep neural networks.

The paper also addresses the Combined Algorithm Selection and Hyperparameter Optimization (CASH) problem introduced by Auto-WEKA, which models algorithm selection and hyperparameters jointly using a random-forest based system called SMAC. 

Recently, multi-fidelity methods have gained traction, particularly in deep learning. Hyperband, which utilizes low-fidelity approximations, was shown to outperform standard Bayesian optimization methods. The 2018 BOHB paper by Falkner et al. combined Bayesian optimization with Hyperband, demonstrating improved performance across various models, including support vector machines and neural networks.

For practical HPO applications, the authors recommend the following tools:
- For scenarios with multiple fidelities, BOHB is suggested as a robust, efficient, and parallelizable method.
- Without multiple fidelities:
  - For a few dozen evaluations with real-valued hyperparameters, a Gaussian process-based tool like Spearmint is recommended.
  - For large and conditional configuration spaces, SMAC or TPE are advised based on their performance.
  - For purely real-valued spaces with cheap objective functions allowing for hundreds of evaluations, CMA-ES is recommended.
