Part III of the paper addresses the challenges associated with Automated Machine Learning (AutoML). It highlights several key areas of concern that researchers and practitioners face when implementing AutoML systems. 

One major challenge is the complexity of model selection and hyperparameter tuning, which can be computationally intensive and time-consuming. The paper discusses the need for efficient algorithms that can navigate the vast search space while minimizing resource consumption.

Another challenge is the issue of data quality and preprocessing. AutoML systems must effectively handle noisy, incomplete, or unstructured data, and the paper emphasizes the importance of robust data cleaning and transformation techniques.

The paper also points out the difficulty of achieving generalizability across different domains. AutoML solutions often struggle to adapt to diverse datasets and specific application requirements, which can limit their effectiveness.

Additionally, the ethical implications of AutoML are considered, particularly regarding bias in data and decision-making processes. The need for transparency and accountability in automated systems is underscored.

Finally, the paper discusses the integration of human expertise into AutoML processes, suggesting that collaboration between automated systems and human insight can enhance the overall performance and reliability of machine learning applications. 

Overall, the section outlines the multifaceted challenges facing AutoML and calls for ongoing research to address these issues effectively.
