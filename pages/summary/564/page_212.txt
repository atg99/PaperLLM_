The analysis of the AutoML Challenge Series from 2015 to 2018 highlights various strategies employed in model ensembling and evaluation. One key approach involves incorporating predictors from all folds directly into the ensemble, which enhances computational efficiency and can produce robust solutions, although it may introduce bias due to smaller sample sizes. Different methods weigh the contributions of predictors differently; for instance, bagging methods like Random Forest assign equal weights, while boosting methods adjust weights during learning.

A notable method discussed is that used by the aad_freiburg team, which cycles through trained models and includes the one that most enhances ensemble performance in each cycle. This approach allows for rapid recomputation of model weights using saved cross-validated predictions and facilitates optimization for specific performance metrics.

Model evaluation methods such as cross-validation, especially K-fold cross-validation, are emphasized as essential for accurate predictive assessment. Many participants utilized "cheap" evaluations on single folds for quick model space exploration, while others employed strategies like the "freeze-thaw" method to manage training based on learning curves.

The paper also delves into the debate between searching large versus small model spaces. While most participants explored a broad range of models from the scikit-learn library, successful results were achieved by teams using simpler, homogeneous models, such as boosted decision trees. This raises questions about the necessity of diverse models, as the rate of convergence and the efficiency of learning curve progression are critical factors influencing model selection.
