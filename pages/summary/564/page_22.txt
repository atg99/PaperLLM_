The section discusses the use of surrogate models, specifically Gaussian processes, in Bayesian optimization. Gaussian processes are favored for their expressiveness and ability to provide smooth, well-calibrated predictions of the target function. An illustration is provided to demonstrate the optimization process on a one-dimensional function. The goal is to minimize the target function (represented by the dashed line) using a Gaussian process surrogate, which predicts values (shown as a black line) and accounts for uncertainty (indicated by a blue tube). The acquisition function, depicted by the lower orange curve, guides the selection of new observation points. It is noted that the acquisition value is low near existing observations and peaks at points where the predicted function value is low but uncertainty is high. The section illustrates the decision-making process for selecting new observation points based on predictive uncertainty and expected improvement over previously evaluated points.
