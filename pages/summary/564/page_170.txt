The paper discusses the Automatic Statistician, a system designed for generating reports from time-series data. The process begins with data input, followed by a search over a predefined grammar of models to find the best interpretation using Bayesian inference for model scoring. The components of the identified models are then translated into English phrases, culminating in a comprehensive report that includes text, figures, tables, and a section on model validation.

The grammar comprises base kernels such as constant, linear, squared exponential, periodic, and white noise, along with operators for addition, multiplication, and change points. These kernels can be combined to create new kernels, enabling a vast range of distributions over functions to be explored and described automatically.

The search and evaluation procedure employs a greedy algorithm to navigate the model space defined by the grammar. Each proposed model's kernel parameters are optimized to enhance the report's accuracy and relevance. This approach builds on prior work in matrix factorization and Gaussian Process models, demonstrating the system's advancement in automated statistical analysis.
