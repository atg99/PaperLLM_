The paper discusses the concept of hyperparameter optimization (HPO) and introduces the successive halving algorithm as a method for selecting the best algorithm configurations. Initially proposed by Karnin et al., this strategy evaluates all algorithms based on a predetermined budget and iteratively eliminates the worst-performing half, subsequently doubling the budget for the remaining algorithms. This process continues until only one configuration remains.

Jamieson and Talwalkar benchmarked several bandit methods and found that successive halving is effective in minimizing the number of iterations and computation time required. It outperforms a uniform budget allocation strategy, particularly when algorithms converge favorably, and is preferable to other well-known strategies like UCB and EXP3.

However, the method does face challenges regarding the trade-off between budget allocation and the number of configurations tested. Users must pre-determine whether to explore many configurations with a small budget or a few configurations with a larger budget. An inadequate budget may prematurely terminate promising configurations, while an excessive budget could extend the evaluation of poor configurations, leading to inefficient use of resources. The paper also notes that the hyperparameter for the fraction of algorithms to drop was changed from 2 to 3 with the introduction of HyperBand.
