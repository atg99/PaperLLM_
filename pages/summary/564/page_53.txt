This section of the paper discusses the concept of meta-features in the context of model performance evaluation. Meta-features are characteristics derived from datasets that can help predict how well a model will perform on a given task. The paper presents a table summarizing commonly used meta-features along with their computation formulas and justifications for their significance. It notes that the optimal set of meta-features varies by application and emphasizes the importance of aggregating single features or combinations of features using summary statistics or histograms. 

To compute task similarity effectively, normalization, feature selection, and dimensionality reduction techniques like PCA are necessary. The text also highlights the existence of specialized meta-features for different data types, such as streaming data, time series, and unsupervised problems, which can leverage domain-specific information.

In the second part, the paper explores the possibility of learning meta-features instead of defining them manually. One method involves creating meta-models that form a new representation based on existing task meta-features and performance data. This method includes evaluating various configurations to determine which performs better in pairwise comparisons. Additionally, the paper mentions using feedforward neural networks and deep metric learning techniques, such as Siamese networks, to learn joint representations of meta-features, particularly when tasks share similar input spaces.
