The paper discusses advancements in hyper-parameter optimization techniques used in the AutoML Challenge Series from 2015 to 2018. It emphasizes that traditional optimization methods, such as grid search, sample parameters in a regular scheme, which can lead to an exponential increase in possibilities as the dimension of parameters grows. K-fold cross-validation is introduced as a method for assessing model performance, where the dataset is divided into k folds to provide a more reliable estimate of test scores. However, there is a noted lack of guidelines on selecting the number of grid points and the value of k.

The paper reviews various approaches to optimize continuous hyper-parameters, including bilevel optimization methods that utilize either k-fold cross-validation or leave-one-out estimators. These methods have been enhanced by regularization techniques and gradient descent for efficiency. Despite advancements, limitations remain in the applicability of these methods to specific models and hyper-parameters.

Early attempts at model selection, such as the pattern search method and Particle Swarm Optimization, are highlighted, with the latter achieving successful model retrieval in a significant percentage of cases. Overfitting concerns are addressed through heuristic methods, although no optimal data splitting strategy has been proposed.

In contrast, Bayesian modeling has long incorporated regularization through hyper-priors, with recent developments in Bayesian hyper-parameter optimization showing promising results, particularly with Gaussian processes. However, Tree-structured Parzen Estimator approaches have been found to outperform Gaussian processes in structured optimization problems involving multiple hyper-parameters. The central focus of these methods is to model the performance function to reduce variance and enhance estimation accuracy.
