The paper discusses the concept of an Automatic Statistician, a system designed to automate the process of statistical analysis and report generation. It operates through a flow diagram that outlines the stages of data modeling, evaluation, and reporting. The system constructs probabilistic models from an open-ended language, evaluates them against data, and selects the best models for reporting. This approach allows for extrapolation and predictions based on the data, as well as the ability to critique models based on their assumptions.

The core philosophy of the Automatic Statistician is rooted in model-based machine learning, where probabilistic models serve as explanations for data patterns. This Bayesian framework effectively balances model complexity and data complexity, aiming to prevent issues of overfitting and underfitting. The system integrates essential components such as an expressive modeling language, an efficient search procedure for model exploration, a methodical evaluation process, and a mechanism to explain models in an understandable manner.

Overall, the Automatic Statistician aims to provide a comprehensive and interpretable statistical analysis, making it accessible to both experts and non-experts alike. The paper emphasizes the potential for further development of such systems to enhance statistical reporting and analysis.
