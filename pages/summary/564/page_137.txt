The paper discusses Auto-sklearn, an automated machine learning framework that enhances efficiency and robustness through a combination of techniques. It begins with a fixed portfolio of 16 machine learning pipeline configurations, derived from a greedy submodular function maximization approach applied to 421 datasets. This initial step employs successive halving, and if time allows, it progresses to a more sophisticated method combining Bayesian optimization and successive halving, inspired by the BOHB framework.

The authors highlight that Auto-sklearn not only optimizes hyperparameters for user-defined models but also has its own hyperparameters affecting its performance, such as time limits and resampling strategies. They suggest that these choices can be viewed as a meta-learning problem, which they aim to expand upon. The paper acknowledges advancements in meta-learning since the original writing, indicating potential for improved optimization through newer methods.

Moreover, the authors express concerns regarding overfitting due to the automated testing of numerous hyperparameter configurations. To mitigate this risk, they propose integrating Auto-sklearn with techniques aimed at improving generalization, including differential privacy methods.

In conclusion, Auto-sklearn outperforms previous AutoML systems, and future work will focus on refining its design choices and optimization strategies to enhance its robustness and efficiency.
