Neuro-evolutionary approaches offer a complementary perspective to reinforcement learning in neural architecture search (NAS), as they do not rely on extensive computational resources and can explore the architecture space through biologically inspired mechanisms. This can lead to diverse architectures and potentially discover novel designs that may not be found through traditional gradient-based methods.

In summary, NAS has evolved significantly since its inception, with various strategies emerging to optimize neural architectures more efficiently. The two primary approaches discussed are reinforcement learning, which models the architecture generation as a sequential decision-making process, and neuro-evolutionary methods, which leverage genetic algorithms to optimize both architecture and weights. Both methodologies aim to reduce computational costs while improving performance, highlighting the growing interest and advancements in the field of automated machine learning.
