Bayesian Optimization (BO) is a widely used method for hyperparameter optimization but has seen limited application in Neural Architecture Search (NAS) due to its reliance on Gaussian processes, which are typically suited for low-dimensional continuous optimization. Some researchers have attempted to adapt BO for architecture search using specialized kernel functions, but have not achieved significant advancements in performance. In contrast, tree-based models, such as treed Parzen estimators and random forests, have demonstrated superior capabilities in navigating high-dimensional conditional spaces, enabling joint optimization of neural architectures and hyperparameters, and often outperforming evolutionary algorithms.

Hierarchical approaches to architectural search have also been explored, combining methods like evolution and sequential model-based optimization. Some studies have leveraged the tree structure of search spaces using techniques like Monte Carlo Tree Search, while others, like Elsken et al., have proposed simpler hill climbing algorithms that effectively discover high-quality architectures through greedy optimization.

Additionally, Liu et al. introduced a continuous relaxation of the search space to facilitate gradient-based optimization by allowing a convex combination of operations at each layer. This method enables simultaneous optimization of network weights and architecture parameters through alternating gradient descent. Other works have focused on optimizing specific aspects of architecture, such as layer hyperparameters or connectivity patterns.

Lastly, to evaluate the performance of various architectures during the search process, strategies typically require training each architecture and assessing its performance on validation data. However, training can be computationally intensive, often requiring thousands of GPU days, which poses significant challenges for NAS.
