The paper discusses the Automatic Statistician project, which aims to automate data science processes, encompassing data pre-processing, modeling, evaluation, and result generation. It highlights the challenges posed by the rapid growth of data, which currently exceeds our analytical capabilities, primarily relying on human expertise. The project advocates for automating aspects of machine learning, with a focus on intelligent resource management to enhance usability and efficiency.

The authors emphasize the importance of considering resource constraints when selecting and evaluating models, suggesting that intelligent systems should allocate resources based on the potential of models to deliver valuable insights. They describe the implementation of gradual evaluation techniques, such as incremental training on larger data subsets, using methods like Freeze-thaw Bayesian optimization.

Despite the ambitious goals of the project, the authors acknowledge the progress made in developing various Automatic Statistician systems, each designed with a shared philosophy to make data analysis accessible to a wider audience. Overall, the paper suggests that such automated tools can empower society by facilitating better utilization of data resources.
