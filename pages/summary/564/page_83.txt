The paper discusses the limitations and potential directions for Neural Architecture Search (NAS), emphasizing that current methodologies may not yield architectures that significantly surpass existing ones due to their inherent design constraints. It advocates for extending NAS applications beyond image classification to areas like language modeling, music modeling, image restoration, network compression, and various others such as reinforcement learning and generative adversarial networks.

The paper also highlights the need for NAS methods tailored for multi-task and multi-objective problems, incorporating resource efficiency as a performance metric alongside predictive accuracy. It suggests that reinforcement learning and bandit approaches could be adapted to account for task-specific requirements, as demonstrated by prior work that generates architectures dynamically based on the task at hand. Additionally, exploring NAS for architectures resilient to adversarial examples presents an intriguing avenue.

A critical issue is the rigidity of current search spaces, which often rely on human-designed structures that do not easily transfer to different domains. The authors propose the development of more flexible search spaces that can accommodate broader hierarchical structures and facilitate the discovery of novel building blocks in architecture design.

Furthermore, the paper points out the challenges in comparing different NAS methods due to variability in performance influenced by factors beyond the architecture itself, such as search space configurations and training methodologies. It stresses the importance of establishing standardized benchmarks for fair evaluation, exemplified by a proposed benchmark for joint architecture and hyperparameter optimization in fully connected neural networks.
