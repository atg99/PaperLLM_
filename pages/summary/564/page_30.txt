The paper discusses the concept of multi-task Bayesian optimization, which employs a multi-task Gaussian process to optimize related tasks by learning their correlations. This approach enables the dynamic switching between low-fidelity, cost-effective tasks and high-fidelity, expensive target tasks, thereby reducing the time needed for hyperparameter optimization (HPO) by approximately half. It highlights the importance of exploring the configuration space through cheaper tasks before transitioning to more costly ones. 

However, the method requires prior specification of a limited number of fidelities, which can lead to suboptimal results due to potential misestimation. To address this, the paper suggests treating fidelity as a continuous variable, allowing for more nuanced evaluations based on data subsets. This adaptation can significantly enhance performance, achieving speedups of 10 to 100 times compared to traditional black-box Bayesian optimization.

The discussion also includes the extension of Bayesian optimization using the Upper Confidence Bound (UCB) acquisition function to multiple fidelities, noting the evolution from the MF-GP-UCB approach, which required upfront fidelity definitions, to the more flexible BOCA algorithm. The latter allows for optimization across multiple continuous fidelities, indicating a trend towards greater interest in this area.

The authors caution that while adaptive fidelity selection methods are powerful, they depend heavily on strong models to make effective fidelity choices. Weak models may lead to excessive evaluations at higher fidelities, suggesting that fixed budget schedules may perform better under certain constraints.

The section concludes with a historical overview of significant hyperparameter optimization systems and their applications in automated machine learning, setting the stage for further exploration in this field.
