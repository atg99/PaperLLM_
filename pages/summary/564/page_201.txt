The 2018 AutoML challenge faced significant challenges, particularly in Round 3, where nearly all participants struggled with sparse datasets during blind testing. However, by the end of the competition, several teams managed to submit effective solutions across all datasets. Despite this progress, there remains a notable performance gap of 15-35% between the AutoML phases involving blind testing with computational constraints and the Tweakathon phases, which allowed for human intervention and additional computational resources.

In Round 4, a GPU track was introduced, enabling participants to explore Deep Learning methods. While these methods showed competitiveness with the best CPU track solutions when additional computing power was available, they did not perform well under the limited constraints typical of the CPU track.

The challenge, which ran from November 30, 2017, to March 31, 2018, yielded slightly lower performance compared to previous editions due to the increased difficulty of the tasks and the inclusion of misleading datasets in the feedback phase. Despite these challenges, several teams achieved results above chance levels. The winning team was AAD Freiburg, which built upon its previous success in the 2015/2016 challenge. The second-place team offered a similar approach, while a three-way tie emerged for third place. The results from both the AutoML and feedback phases are documented, highlighting the performance of the top teams across five datasets.
