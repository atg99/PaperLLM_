The paper discusses the performance of various machine learning algorithms when selecting default learners and optimizing their hyperparameters. It highlights that there is a significant disparity in performance between the best and worst learners, as illustrated by the misclassification rates on the Dorothea dataset, which ranged from 4.93% to 99.24%. This underscores the necessity of algorithm selection for improving model performance.

A baseline approach introduced is grid search, which optimally selects learners and their hyperparameters through an exhaustive search over a predefined hyperparameter grid. While grid search can enhance performance—showing a 13% error reduction in the CIFAR-10 small task compared to default settings—it is computationally expensive, requiring over 10,000 CPU hours for each dataset, making it impractical for most applications.

The paper also contrasts grid search with random search, which samples algorithms and hyperparameters randomly and has been shown to outperform grid search in terms of efficiency while holding time budgets constant. The results indicate that grid search outperformed default settings in 17 out of 21 cases, emphasizing the importance of not only selecting the right algorithm but also tuning its hyperparameters effectively. However, the extensive time requirements for grid search highlight its impracticality in real-world scenarios.
