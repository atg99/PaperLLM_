The AutoML Challenge Series from 2015 to 2018 focused on evaluating automated machine learning solutions, emphasizing the computational viability of submissions. Winners were required to release their code under an open-source license, fostering software publications as a result of the challenges. The 2018 challenge introduced Docker, facilitating consistent reproduction of results across different environments. However, varying hardware still posed challenges, though cloud computing is expected to mitigate this issue over time.

The series is expanding, with upcoming challenges such as the NIPS 2018 Life Long Machine Learning challenge, which will examine data distribution changes over time, and a focus on automated machine learning transfer between similar domains.

Acknowledgements highlight support from Microsoft, various academic institutions, and contributions from numerous individuals who provided feedback, resources, and assistance in developing the challenge platform. The datasets used were curated from 72 contributions by co-authors and collaborators. Initial analyses of challenge results were also conducted by various researchers involved in the project.
