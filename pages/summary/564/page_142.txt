The paper discusses the development of Auto-Net, a system designed to enhance automated machine learning (AutoML) by integrating neural networks into the existing frameworks. AutoML aims to simplify the process of selecting algorithms, preprocessing methods, and hyperparameters, alleviating the burden on both experts and non-experts. The problem is framed as a combined algorithm selection and hyperparameter optimization (CASH) task, which seeks to identify the optimal combination of algorithm components based on their cross-validation performance.

Bayesian optimization is highlighted as a powerful technique for addressing the CASH problem, though traditional Gaussian processes struggle with its high dimensionality and mixed hyperparameter types. As a solution, tree-based models have shown superior performance in this context. Auto-Net is inspired by two established AutoML systems, Auto-WEKA and Auto-sklearn, both of which utilize random forest-based Bayesian optimization methods to find optimal classifier configurations. Auto-sklearn enhances performance through meta-learning and ensemble selection.

The paper introduces two iterations of Auto-Net: Auto-Net 1.0, based on Theano, and Auto-Net 2.0, implemented in PyTorch. Auto-Net 1.0 employs SMAC for neural network configuration, while Auto-Net 2.0 utilizes a more sophisticated approach combining Bayesian Optimization with HyperBand for efficiency. Notably, Auto-Net 1.0 achieved top performance in the ChaLearn AutoML Challenge, marking a significant milestone as the first fully automated neural network to outperform human experts. Auto-Net 2.0 further advances these capabilities for larger datasets, reflecting ongoing progress in the field of automated neural network tuning.
