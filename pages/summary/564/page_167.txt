The paper discusses the challenges faced in the development of machine learning (ML) systems, highlighting the reliance on human experts and manual tuning, which can lead to sub-optimal solutions. This dependency is ironic given that ML aims to create systems that learn autonomously from data. The disparity between the demand for data science solutions and the limited supply of experts may result in missed societal opportunities.

The authors propose the vision of the Automatic Statistician, an AI system designed to automate various aspects of data analysis, model discovery, and explanation. This system should be capable of automating feature selection, handling messy data, searching for effective models, avoiding overfitting and underfitting, and explaining findings to users in an accessible manner, all while being efficient and robust under resource constraints.

The Automatic Statistician aims to differentiate itself from traditional ML literature, which often focuses on improving performance in pattern recognition. Instead, it emphasizes building interpretable models and effectively representing uncertainty. This approach is intended to be applicable to a wide range of fields, addressing both large and small datasets. Progress has been made in achieving these goals, particularly in discovering plausible models and providing clear explanations.
