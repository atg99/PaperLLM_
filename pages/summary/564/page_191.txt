In this paper, the authors discuss advancements in machine learning algorithms, particularly in the context of model selection and the efficiency of search methods. They highlight how some embedded methods can compute leave-one-out solutions in a closed form, thus optimizing model training on all available data. However, many researchers have primarily focused on improving search efficiency while neglecting the risk of overfitting the second-level objective, often defined by k-fold cross-validation with arbitrary k values.

The authors mention Bayesian methods that help mitigate overfitting through hyper-priors, albeit with assumptions regarding data generation and without performance guarantees. They argue that previous approaches to full model selection have not adequately addressed the dual optimization of both modeling choices and data splitting, suggesting a need for further exploration of statistical and computational challenges in this area.

The paper also describes the AutoML challenge series, which serves as a benchmark for comparing methodologies in this field, free from bias. It details the creation of a dataset pool for the 2015/2016 challenge, consisting of 30 diverse datasets across various applications, including biology, ecology, and social media. Data preprocessing was conducted to standardize feature representations, with specific tasks like text and video processing being included without their native variable-length formats.

For the 2018 challenge, additional datasets were introduced alongside some from the initial pool, all reformatted to maintain anonymity except for final rounds where new data was utilized. The paper notes the progressive increase in data difficulty across challenge rounds, beginning with simpler classification tasks in the initial round.
