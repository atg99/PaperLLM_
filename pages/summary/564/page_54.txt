The section provides an overview of commonly used meta-features in meta-learning, categorized into six groups: simple, statistical, information-theoretic, complexity, model-based, and landmarkers. Key meta-features are defined along with their associated formulas and rationales. 

The meta-features include metrics such as the number of instances (n), features (p), classes (c), missing values (m), and outliers (o), which assess data scalability, imputation effects, and data noisiness. Additionally, skewness and kurtosis are used to evaluate feature normality, while correlation and covariance measure feature interdependence. 

Other important metrics include the coefficient of variation for target variation, PCA components for variance and intrinsic dimensionality, and class probabilities and entropies for distribution and imbalance analysis. 

Metrics like mutual information and uncertainty coefficients are highlighted for assessing feature importance, while the noise-signal ratio indicates data noisiness. Fisher's discriminant and volume of overlap evaluate class separability, while concept variation and data consistency measure task complexity and data quality. 

The summary encapsulates key concepts essential for understanding the role of meta-features in meta-learning, emphasizing their importance in assessing data characteristics and model performance.
