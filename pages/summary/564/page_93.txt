The paper discusses Auto-WEKA, which utilizes Bayesian Optimization, specifically Sequential Model-Based Optimization (SMBO), for automatic model selection and hyperparameter tuning. SMBO operates by first establishing a model that captures the relationship between hyperparameter settings and the loss function. It then iteratively selects candidate hyperparameters to evaluate based on this model, assesses their performance, and updates the model with new data points.

The algorithm initializes a model and continues until the time budget for optimization is exhausted. It selects hyperparameter configurations, computes the associated loss, and updates the model with the results. To determine the next hyperparameter configuration, SMBO employs an acquisition function that quantifies the potential benefit of evaluating specific configurations. The aim is to balance between exploiting known good hyperparameters and exploring less tested ones to prevent premature convergence.

The paper highlights the maximization of the expected improvement function, which quantifies the potential gain over the current best loss, allowing SMBO to effectively navigate the hyperparameter space. Overall, the SMBO method is positioned as a robust framework for optimizing hyperparameters within the Auto-WEKA system.
