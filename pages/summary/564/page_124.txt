The paper discusses an innovative approach to improving predictions in machine learning by utilizing the models generated during Bayesian hyperparameter optimization. Although this optimization method is effective in identifying the best hyperparameter settings, it often discards many trained models that could perform nearly as well as the top model. The authors propose storing these models and employing a post-processing method for automated ensemble construction, enhancing robustness and reducing the risk of overfitting compared to relying on a single model.

The paper emphasizes that ensembles typically yield better performance than individual models, especially when the contributing models are strong and make uncorrelated errors. To create effective ensembles, the authors found that simply averaging model weights is inadequate; instead, they adjusted these weights based on model predictions on a hold-out set. Through experimentation, they evaluated various methods for optimizing these weights, including stacking, numerical optimization, and ensemble selection. They determined that ensemble selection, a method that iteratively adds models to minimize validation loss, was the most efficient and robust approach.

In their experiments, the authors constructed ensembles consisting of 50 models using selection with replacement and calculated ensemble loss based on the same validation set used for Bayesian optimization. They acknowledge that while previous works have also applied ensemble methods in AutoML systems, their approach uniquely adapts ensemble construction based on individual model performance, illustrating a novel contribution to the field.
