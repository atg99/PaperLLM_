The section discusses the challenges and strategies involved in Neural Architecture Search (NAS), particularly focusing on the selection of meta-architectures and search spaces. It highlights the importance of determining how many cells to use and their connectivity within the model. For instance, Zoph et al. construct a sequential model where each cell receives inputs from the two preceding cells, while Cai et al. integrate cells into established architectures like DenseNet. The text underscores the necessity of optimizing the meta-architecture as part of NAS to avoid oversimplifying the cell search process.

A hierarchical search space proposed by Liu et al. is introduced, consisting of multiple levels of motifs, with the cell-based search space being a specific instance where three levels are defined. The optimization problem's complexity is influenced significantly by the search space, remaining non-continuous and high-dimensional even with a fixed meta-architecture. The architectures can be represented as fixed-length vectors, allowing for the use of constrained search spaces.

The section transitions to search strategies suitable for exploring neural architecture spaces, mentioning various methods such as random search, Bayesian optimization, evolutionary methods, reinforcement learning, and gradient-based approaches. Evolutionary algorithms have historical significance in NAS, while Bayesian optimization has gained traction since 2013, achieving notable successes in developing advanced vision architectures and improving performance on datasets like CIFAR-10.
