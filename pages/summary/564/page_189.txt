This paper discusses various approaches for hyper-parameter optimization in machine learning, particularly emphasizing Bayesian optimization, reinforcement learning, and evolutionary algorithms. It highlights the effectiveness of the random-forest-based SMAC algorithm for optimizing hyper-parameters in high-dimensional and discrete spaces, and notes the integration of Bayesian methods with meta-learning and ensemble techniques to enhance performance under time constraints.

The paper also explores the use of reinforcement learning to devise optimal neural network architectures, framing the hyper-parameter optimization as a reinforcement learning problem where states represent hyper-parameter settings, actions involve modifying the architecture, and rewards correspond to validation accuracy. Additionally, evolutionary algorithms are presented as a method for optimizing hyper-parameters through a population-based approach, where settings are iteratively modified based on their performance.

Despite their success, these advanced methods are noted to require significant computational resources, and scaling challenges remain, particularly for evolutionary algorithms. A recent proposal by Pham et al. introduces weight sharing among models to improve efficiency while maintaining performance.

The paper concludes by mentioning the potential for multi-level parameter optimization, which adds complexity but can enhance model performance. An example is provided to illustrate a frequentist approach to multi-level parameter optimization, using kernel ridge regression and neural networks, demonstrating the recursive validation of models.
