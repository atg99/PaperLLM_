The paper discusses various models and techniques used in Bayesian optimization for hyperparameter tuning in machine learning. Factorization machines can incorporate information from previous tasks and have been adapted to address the Configuration-Aware Hyperparameter Search (CASH) problem. Random forests serve as an alternative to Gaussian Processes (GPs), particularly excelling in larger, categorical, and conditional configuration spaces where GPs may struggle. The computational efficiency of random forests is notable, with fitting and variance prediction scaling as O(n log n) and O(log n), respectively, compared to the cubic and quadratic scaling of GPs.

The SMAC framework employs random forests for Bayesian optimization, contributing to the development of prominent AutoML tools like Auto-WEKA and Auto-sklearn. Additionally, the Tree Parzen Estimator (TPE) approaches the problem by modeling density functions for observations based on their performance, using a percentile to separate good and bad observations. TPE is particularly effective for structured hyperparameter optimization tasks and is integrated into the Hyperopt-sklearn framework.

The paper also addresses the configuration space description, noting that traditional Bayesian optimization is designed for box-constrained, real-valued functions. Many hyperparameters require optimization of their exponent, and input warping techniques can learn these transformations during optimization. To enhance flexibility, methods to dynamically expand the configuration space and algorithms like TPE that accommodate infinite spaces with Gaussian priors are also discussed.
