The paper discusses the significance and evolution of hyperparameter optimization (HPO) in machine learning. HPO enhances the performance of algorithms tailored to specific problems, leading to state-of-the-art results in various benchmarks. It also promotes reproducibility and fairness in scientific studies, as automated HPO ensures consistent tuning across methods.

The history of HPO dates back to the 1990s, with insights revealing that different hyperparameter configurations yield varying effectiveness across datasets. Recently, it has been recognized that HPO can adapt general-purpose machine learning pipelines to specific domains, and tuned hyperparameters consistently outperform default settings.

The growing application of machine learning in commercial contexts has heightened interest in HPO, which is now integral to internal tools, cloud services, and standalone offerings. However, HPO presents challenges such as costly function evaluations, complex and high-dimensional configuration spaces, lack of access to gradients of loss functions, and limitations in optimizing generalization performance due to small training datasets.

The chapter outlines the structure of the discussion, beginning with a formal definition of HPO and its variants, followed by an exploration of blackbox optimization algorithms and modern multi-fidelity methods that address the high costs associated with model evaluations. It concludes with an overview of key HPO systems and applications in automated machine learning (AutoML), alongside a discussion of remaining open problems in the field.
