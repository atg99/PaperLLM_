The paper discusses Neural Architecture Search (NAS), a critical process in automating architecture engineering within machine learning. As neural architectures become more complex, NAS serves as a subfield of AutoML, closely related to hyperparameter optimization and meta-learning. The authors categorize NAS methods into three key dimensions: 

1. **Search Space**: This defines the potential architectures that can be represented. Incorporating prior knowledge can streamline the search but may also introduce bias, limiting the discovery of innovative architectures.

2. **Search Strategy**: This outlines how to navigate the search space, balancing the need for rapid identification of high-performing architectures with the risk of prematurely settling on suboptimal solutions.

3. **Performance Estimation Strategy**: The main goal of NAS is to identify architectures that perform well on unseen data. Estimating performance can be computationally intensive, prompting research into methods that reduce this cost.

The chapter is organized around these dimensions, beginning with search spaces, followed by search strategies, and concluding with performance estimation approaches. It also references a recent survey article for further context.
