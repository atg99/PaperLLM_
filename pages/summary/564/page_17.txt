The section on hyperparameter optimization outlines the challenge of optimizing a machine learning algorithm characterized by N hyperparameters, each with its respective domain. The overall hyperparameter configuration space is represented as a Cartesian product of these individual domains. Hyperparameters can be classified as real-valued, integer-valued, binary, or categorical, often with bounded domains for practical purposes.

The text highlights the existence of conditional hyperparameters, where the relevance of one hyperparameter depends on the value of another, forming directed acyclic graphs. This scenario is common in automated machine learning pipeline tuning and neural network architecture optimization.

The goal is to identify the optimal hyperparameter vector λ∗ that minimizes the expected loss of a model, as measured by a validation protocol on training and validation datasets. Common validation methods include holdout and cross-validation, with strategies suggested for reducing evaluation time, such as testing on subsets of data or using auxiliary tasks to gather information without fully training the model.
