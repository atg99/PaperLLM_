The paper discusses the challenges of hyper-parameter tuning within time constraints and large datasets, as highlighted by the performances of various scoring metrics in machine learning tasks. It is noted that basic methods typically do not allow for metric optimization, which can lead to suboptimal performance compared to more advanced techniques like auto-sklearn. When using common metrics such as balanced accuracy (BAC) and R2, the winning methods from challenges may not consistently outperform basic methods. However, when challenge-specific metrics are utilized, a significant performance gap often appears between basic methods and the winners, although Random Forest with optimized hyper-parameters (RF-auto) sometimes shows comparable or superior performance.

The paper also explores the concept of meta-learning, specifically the potential to predict the effectiveness of classifiers on future datasets based on their past performances. The authors examined this possibility using auto-sklearn's meta-learning features while ensuring data integrity by excluding certain features that could lead to data leakage. The results indicate that no single basic method consistently outperforms others across all metrics, although RF-auto and linear models like SGD-def show competitive results under different conditions. Ultimately, the techniques used by challenge winners demonstrate their effectiveness, emphasizing the importance of metric selection in evaluating model performance.
