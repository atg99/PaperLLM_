The paper discusses various techniques for optimizing neural network configurations through surrogate modeling and multi-task learning. It highlights the use of linear surrogate models that are pre-trained on OpenML metadata to enhance the optimization process. Previous research has focused on transferring knowledge between similar tasks using joint Gaussian Processes (GP) for Bayesian optimization, although this method can be less scalable compared to individual GP models for each task. Alternative approaches include using Bayesian Neural Networks to learn task relationships during optimization and employing multi-armed bandits to identify source tasks most related to new tasks.

Additionally, the paper explores defining task similarity through Thompson Sampling to measure the KL-divergence between distributions of task optima, which aids in constructing an acquisition function for predicting promising configurations. Another method suggested involves recommending configurations to avoid by analyzing the performance predictions from surrogate models. This is done by identifying task similarities using rank correlation coefficients to exclude regions in the configuration space that are likely to yield poor performance, thereby accelerating the search for optimal configurations.
