The paper discusses the effectiveness of various predictive models for estimating oil production from newly developed wells, focusing on the performance of GRU_RF compared to RNN, LSTM, and GRU. The results indicate that GRU_RF significantly outperforms the other models, achieving a mean RMSE of 10.67 and a mean MAE of 7.55, while the other methods show higher error rates. The study emphasizes that traditional decline curve analysis (DCA) and conventional time-series methods are inadequate for wells with limited production data, as they rely on declining trends or linear statistical models, which do not suit early production phases characterized by rising trends.

The proposed model offers several advantages: it can predict production for wells with over two weeks of data, enables batch forecasting, and operates faster without the need for extensive hyperparameter tuning. Additionally, it incorporates external features such as completion strategy (CS) and stimulation intensity (SI), which traditional models overlook. The study highlights the importance of geological and operational factors in refining prediction accuracy and notes that the model minimizes subjectivity in predictions by providing consistent outputs for the same inputs.

Furthermore, the paper underscores the dynamic nature of production, influenced by various factors such as fracture propagation and oilfield operations. Continuous learning from updated production data is advocated to improve model accuracy over time. Overall, the findings suggest that the GRU_RF model is a robust tool for predicting oil production, particularly for new wells with limited initial data.
