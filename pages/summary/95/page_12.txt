The study evaluates the performance of a Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) model in predicting oil production rates from various wells, highlighting its strengths and limitations. The model effectively captures gradual changes in production but struggles with sudden fluctuations, which lead to increased prediction errors. This issue is noted particularly for specific wells during periods of abrupt change, a factor often overlooked in prior research.

The GRU model incorporates external variables such as choke size (CS) and shut-in (SI) operations, demonstrating that larger choke sizes correlate with higher production rates. Additionally, prolonged shut-in periods result in increased oil production due to pressure buildup, aligning with field observations.

The research also introduces an iterative approach for long-term production forecasting, allowing predictions to be made sequentially based on prior actual and predicted rates. This method facilitates the estimation of production rates over extended time frames, beginning with limited historical data.

Overall, the findings suggest that while the GRU model is capable of handling complex production dynamics, future improvements could enhance its accuracy in forecasting sudden changes. Potential strategies include smoothing abrupt variations and extending the prediction time frame from days to months.
