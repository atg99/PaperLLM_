The paper discusses the challenges in predicting production rates in oilfields, particularly focusing on the limitations of the Iterative approach compared to the SingleStep scheme. It highlights that while the SingleStep method yields smaller errors, the Iterative method accumulates errors, especially when there are sharp variances in operations such as choke size changes and shut-in periods. For example, Well 32 shows significant discrepancies post shut-in, while Well 35 exhibits a consistent underestimation of actual production due to varying geological conditions. The mean Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) is significantly higher for the Iterative approach, indicating the sensitivity to numerous iterations and operational variances.

To address these accuracy issues, the authors propose a GRU_RF model that combines a Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) model with a Random Forest (RF) model. The RF model is designed to fit the errors between the predicted and actual production rates, utilizing both dynamic and static features related to production and operational conditions. The workflow involves training the GRU model with a structured dataset, followed by error correction using the RF model. This dual approach aims to enhance prediction accuracy by integrating the strengths of both modeling techniques.
