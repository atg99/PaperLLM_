This paper presents a forecasting model for predicting oil production using a combination of a Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) model and a Random Forest (RF) model, termed GRU_RF. The approach consists of several steps:

1. **Preliminary Forecasting**: The GRU model is initially employed to predict production based on historical data through continuous iterations.
2. **Training the RF Model**: The RF model is trained to correct errors from the GRU predictions by using the discrepancies between predicted and actual production as outputs. Both models utilize the same training wells.
3. **Second Forecasting**: The RF model predicts correction errors using inputs related to production, operations, and geological parameters.
4. **Final Forecasting**: For new test wells, the GRU_RF model combines outputs from the GRU and the RF models to provide long-term production predictions without the need for repeated training.

The RF model takes various input features, including predicted production from the GRU, cumulative production over specified days, operational parameters, fracturing details, and formation characteristics. The output is the prediction error between the GRU's forecasted and actual production rates.

To evaluate the performance of the GRU_RF model, its accuracy is compared to that of other neural network architectures, including fully connected RNNs and LSTMs. All models are trained with the same parameters and data treatment. Results indicate that the GRU_RF model achieves lower mean Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) and Mean Absolute Error (MAE) compared to the other models, demonstrating superior generalizability and predictive capability. Specifically, the GRU_RF model has a mean RMSE of 10.67 and a mean MAE of 7.55, outperforming RNN, LSTM, and GRU models, which have higher error metrics. Overall, the findings suggest that the GRU_RF model is more effective in forecasting oil production than its counterparts.
