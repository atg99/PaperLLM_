The paper discusses the architecture and prediction schemes of Gated Recurrent Units (GRUs) for dynamic production forecasting in oilfields. It introduces the concept of a gating signal that regulates the balance between memorization and forgetting within the memory cell state. The gating signal, denoted as zT, influences how much information is retained or discarded, thereby controlling the output hT.

Three distinct prediction schemes are proposed: "SingleStep," "Iterative," and "PartIterative." The SingleStep approach is designed for model evaluation by eliminating cumulative error influences, allowing for a clear assessment of performance based on actual production data. In this scheme, predictions are made using true values without replacing them with previously predicted outputs, thus focusing on single-step error evaluation.

The Iterative scheme, in contrast, utilizes previously predicted values, leading to potential error accumulation over time. This method is suitable for long-term production forecasts when new data is not updated daily. 

To leverage the strengths of both approaches, the PartIterative scheme is introduced. This combines elements of the SingleStep and Iterative methods, allowing for iterative predictions while periodically updating with actual production data. This adaptive system assesses whether retraining is necessary based on the accuracy of predictions compared to actual outcomes. Overall, the paper emphasizes the flexibility of GRU architecture in facilitating various predictive strategies tailored to the dynamic nature of oilfield data.
