The paper discusses the implementation of a Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) model and Random Forest (RF) algorithm for predictive modeling. The GRU model uses Xavier initialization for its matrices, ReLU activation function, and Adam optimizer, which combines first and second moment estimations to minimize the loss function. To prevent overfitting, the model incorporates L2 regularization and dropout techniques. Key parameters include 32 neurons in the GRU layer, a batch size of 50, and a learning rate of 0.001, with the model coded in Python 3.7.

The RF algorithm, an ensemble method of decision trees, is noted for its accuracy and robustness against noise and outliers. It uses mean decrease impurity to rank feature importance, aiding in the understanding of fracturing stimulation mechanisms. The implementation details reference Scikit-learn and highlight the process of maximizing information gain through feature splits.

Evaluation indices for model performance include mean square error (MSE), root mean square error (RMSE), and mean absolute error (MAE). MSE assesses the overall performance by comparing actual and predicted values, while RMSE provides insight into the average error per sample, enhancing understanding of the model's predictive capabilities.
