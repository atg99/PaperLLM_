In this section of the paper, the authors discuss the application of the Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) model using an iterative approach to predict long-term production rates from limited historical data. The iterative method involves predicting production rates day by day, starting with data from the first 14 days of a well's production history to forecast the 15th day. This prediction is then used to refine the forecast for subsequent days.

The study compares two models: a one-feature model using only the initial production rate (q o) and a four-feature model that incorporates additional variables such as total production (TP), saturation index (SI), and completion strategy (CS). The analysis is based on two training wells, Well 11 and Well 36. Results indicate that the one-feature model is limited in its ability to adapt to operational changes, leading to significant errors, particularly in wells with fluctuating production patterns. In contrast, the four-feature model demonstrates improved predictive accuracy, especially for Well 11, where its predictions align closely with actual production trends. Overall, the findings highlight the importance of incorporating multiple features for more accurate production forecasting in oil fields.
