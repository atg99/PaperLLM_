Decline curve analysis (DCA) is a commonly used method in the oilfield for predicting production declines based on empirical data. While it is straightforward and requires minimal data, DCA has limitations, including its applicability only after a well has been producing for several months and its inability to account for operational changes. Additionally, the subjective selection of fitting data can significantly affect results.

Machine learning, particularly deep learning, has emerged as a promising alternative for production analysis in the oil and gas industry. Traditional time-series prediction methods like ARMA and ARIMA have been effective in predicting production in unconventional reservoirs. However, deep learning models, such as fully connected neural networks, LSTM, and GRU, have shown superior performance in various applications, including production forecasting.

Previous studies have primarily focused on the relationship between geological factors and cumulative production without addressing time-series rate prediction. RNNs, especially LSTM and GRU, have been developed to overcome limitations in predicting long sequences due to issues like gradient vanishing. Research indicates that LSTM outperforms DCA and traditional methods in terms of trend accuracy and error reduction.

This paper introduces GRU models incorporating multiple external features for oil rate prediction in the M conglomerate oilfield in China. The goal is to create a production prediction model that accommodates batch predictions with limited production data. The paper details a comprehensive data preprocessing approach essential for successful predictions.
