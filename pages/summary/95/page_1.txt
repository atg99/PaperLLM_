The paper discusses the application of a Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) neural network for predicting batch production in conglomerate reservoirs, addressing the challenges of traditional forecasting methods constrained by limited historical data, complex fracture behaviors, and operational changes. The authors propose a model trained on four key features: production rate, tubing pressure, choke size, and shut-in period, using data from 70 multistage hydraulic fractured horizontal wells.

The study emphasizes a thorough data preprocessing stage, which includes excluding unfit wells, data screening, feature selection, and z-score normalization. A comparison between the four-feature model and a model that considers production rate alone reveals that the former captures production rate variances more effectively amidst frequent operational changes. Additionally, the authors employ Random Forest (RF) to enhance the prediction accuracy of the GRU model.

The performance of the proposed GRU model is evaluated against simpler Recurrent Neural Network (RNN) and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) models, demonstrating superior prediction accuracy and generalization capabilities. The authors highlight that the GRU model can be continuously updated with new data, enhancing its predictive power as more wells are developed. The findings underscore the model's potential in improving production forecasting in unconventional reservoirs, thereby aiding decision-making and operational planning.
