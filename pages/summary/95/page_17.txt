The paper discusses a novel predictive modeling approach using a combined GRU_RF model for forecasting long-term production in newly developed wells with limited production data. Traditional decline curve analysis (DCA) and conventional prediction methods are often ineffective for new wells due to insufficient data. The proposed GRU_RF model integrates a Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) model and a Random Forest (RF) model to enhance prediction accuracy.

The modeling process consists of several steps: 

1. **Training the GRU Model**: Data preprocessing is performed, and the dataset is divided into training and validation sets.
2. **Preliminary Forecasting**: The GRU model generates time-series predictions based on historical production data through iterative iterations.
3. **Training the RF Model**: This model corrects errors from the GRU model by training on the discrepancies between predicted and true production values.
4. **Second Forecasting with RF**: The RF model predicts correction errors using production and operational parameters.
5. **Final Forecasting with GRU_RF**: The GRU_RF model is applied to predict long-term production for test wells by integrating the outputs of the GRU and RF models.

The performance of the GRU_RF model is compared with that of traditional RNN and LSTM models. Results show that GRU_RF outperforms these models, achieving lower mean root mean square error (RMSE) and mean absolute error (MAE). Specifically, the GRU_RF model achieved a mean RMSE of 10.67 and mean MAE of 7.55, compared to higher values for RNN, LSTM, and GRU.

Overall, the GRU_RF model demonstrates significant advantages over traditional prediction methods by effectively utilizing limited data from new wells, providing a promising approach for long-term production forecasting in the oil and gas industry.
