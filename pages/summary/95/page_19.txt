The paper discusses the implementation of continuous learning in production prediction for oil wells. Continuous learning allows for adaptive learning and the incremental development of complex skills and knowledge throughout the production period. This process involves updating the history window and models based on incoming production data. For individual wells, the PartIterative scheme is used, where the history window is continuously updated to optimize production data utilization. In the case of multiple wells, models are updated as new data becomes available, with old data serving as the training set and new data as the test set.

The study introduces a novel hybrid model, GRU_RF, designed to predict long-term production while incorporating multiple external factors. The methodology includes thorough data preprocessing and modeling procedures that can be applied to other reservoirs. The GRU_RF model demonstrates strong performance in predicting the production of new wells, showing satisfactory generalization across various datasets.

Key findings include:
1. The GRU_RF model effectively predicts long-term production with initial data, outperforming traditional methods such as DCA and simple RNNs.
2. The model's inclusion of significant external factors enhances its predictive capabilities, capturing complex variance patterns.
3. Both formation and fracturing parameters significantly influence production predictions, highlighting the importance of combining static and dynamic features in time-series predictions.
4. Continuous learning enables real-time predictions and batch processing, facilitating ongoing updates to the model.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the potential of continuous learning and the GRU_RF model for enhancing production prediction accuracy in oilfields.
