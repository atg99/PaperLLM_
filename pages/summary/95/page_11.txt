The paper presents a study on the application of the Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) model for predicting production rates in a conglomerate reservoir, marking its first use in this context. The research demonstrates that once the model is trained using data from existing wells, it can be applied to new wells without incurring additional computational costs. Key performance metrics such as the Root Mean Square Error (RMSE), Mean Squared Error (MSE), and Mean Absolute Error (MAE) are employed to assess model accuracy.

The results indicate that the RMSE for the model across training, validation, and test wells generally falls within the range of 4 to 6, with mean values of 4.89, 4.98, and 4.91, respectively, suggesting a strong generalization capability of the model. The RMSE distribution for test wells aligns closely with that of training wells.

Additionally, the SingleStep prediction approach is highlighted as a method to minimize the impact of cumulative errors from iterative predictions. This approach yields predictions that are closely aligned with actual production values, particularly during stable oil rate phases. However, significant deviations occur during periods of rapid fluctuation, underscoring the model's limitations under extreme conditions. Overall, the findings validate the effectiveness of the GRU model for production rate forecasting in the studied reservoir context.
