The paper discusses a systematic approach to data preprocessing and model analysis for predicting oil production rates from newly developed wells. It emphasizes the importance of selecting an appropriate history window (HW) for data analysis, settling on 14 days to balance sample size and information retention. Unfit wells with production times shorter than HW are excluded, along with five wells after initial screening.

Data screening involves removing periods devoid of oil production to reduce computational load, while zero production periods from shut-in operations are retained as they provide valuable external context. The paper identifies relevant input features from an oilfield database, discarding unchangeable features like production management (PM) and strokes since all wells are gusher wells. It highlights the significance of choke size (CS) and shut-in (SI) operations on production fluctuations, supported by a Pearson correlation analysis that reveals positive correlations between CS and tubing pressure (TP) with output (qo), and a negative correlation with SI.

A total of 65 wells are selected for dataset partitioning into training, validation, and test sets, with 52 wells allocated for training (80% of the dataset). The validation set comprises 7 wells, while the test set includes 6 wells. The paper also details the standardization of data using z-score normalization to enhance model efficiency, ensuring that normalization for validation and test sets is based solely on the training set's mean and standard deviation. Overall, the structured methodology aims to optimize prediction accuracy in oil production modeling.
