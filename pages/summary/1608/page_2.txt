The paper discusses the relationship between morality and law, exploring how they intersect and conflict. It introduces the concepts of moral tenets (M) and laws (L) as overlapping sets, with their intersection (O) representing shared instances. The distinction is made between instances of agreement (A) and disagreement (D) within O, highlighting that A poses no conflict while D necessitates conflict resolution.

The paper proposes that an Artificial Moral Agent and an Artificial Legal Reasoner should work in tandem within an AI system for social good. In cases of conflict (D), the system must engage in a conflict resolution process, with D further categorized into hard cases (D*) and non-hard cases (D°).

Conflict resolution approaches are outlined, starting with the notion that morality may prevail over law, as suggested by Hart and Rao. The paper emphasizes the complexity of resolving conflicts between moral and legal reasoning, citing the challenge posed when law and morality contradict each other. Overall, it advocates for a systematic approach to integrate moral and legal reasoning within AI frameworks.
