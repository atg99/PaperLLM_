The paper discusses four conflict resolution approaches for Artificial Moral Agents and Artificial Legal Reasoners in the context of AI for Social Good. 

1. **Approach C1** asserts that in class D, the moral agent (M) prevails over legal considerations (D° or D*), reducing the need for legal reasoning and runtime effort, as issues in class L' still require legal analysis.

2. **Approach C2** states that legal reasoning prevails over morality, meaning decisions in class D are determined solely by the Artificial Legal Reasoner, aligning with the Rule of Law, regardless of moral implications.

3. **Approach C3** addresses situations where conflicts arise between the moral and legal agents without clear resolutions from C1 or C2. It suggests various algorithmic methods, such as weighting schemes or dialogue-based argumentation, to determine which agent's decision should prevail. The importance of timely conflict resolution is emphasized, particularly in critical situations like autonomous driving, where delays can have severe consequences.

4. **Approach C4** explores scenarios where neither agent's decision prevails. Options include random selection or methods based on ethical theories like consequentialism or deontology. Engaging end-users for resolution is also considered, though it poses risks of unintended consequences.

The paper highlights the significance of the moral-legal dualism in AI systems and calls for further research in this emerging field, stressing its importance as AI becomes more integrated into society.
