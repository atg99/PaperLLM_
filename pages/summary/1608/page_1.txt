This paper discusses the neglected dualism in AI for Social Good, specifically the interplay between artificial moral agency and artificial legal reasoning. While AI researchers have focused on developing Artificial Moral Agents to ensure AI systems make appropriate moral decisions, they have largely overlooked the integration of Artificial Legal Reasoners, which is essential for achieving comprehensive moral and legal outcomes. The author highlights that morality and law are intrinsically linked, and their simultaneous consideration is crucial for effective AI deployment in real-world applications.

The paper emphasizes the historical tension between morality and law, referencing various scholarly perspectives, including natural law and legal positivism. It suggests that without incorporating both moral and legal reasoning capabilities, AI systems may struggle to navigate complex ethical and legal dilemmas. The author proposes a coordinated approach, where both Artificial Moral Agents and Artificial Legal Reasoners operate together, to enhance the decision-making process of AI systems.

The remainder of the paper outlines the challenges faced in achieving this dualism and presents a research agenda aimed at addressing these issues, ultimately seeking to improve the moral and legal reliability of AI systems in social contexts.
