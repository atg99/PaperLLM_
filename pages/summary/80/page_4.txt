The paper explores the performance of conventional Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks and their variants on three benchmark datasets: TIMIT, IAM Online, and JSB Chorales. It finds that while standard LSTM performs satisfactorily, none of the eight examined variants, including Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU), significantly enhance performance.

A key focus of the research is on the concept of random connectivity within LSTM architectures, leading to the development of a new model termed Random Connectivity LSTM (RCLSTM). Unlike traditional LSTMs, which feature fully connected neurons in each memory block, RCLSTM introduces randomness in the connections among neurons. This approach is inspired by the observation that certain neural circuits in the brain benefit from a random topology, which can facilitate specific functional connectivity.

In RCLSTM, the connections between neurons are determined by random processes, allowing for a more flexible architecture. The study highlights that the trainable parameters in LSTM primarily exist between the input and functional layers, and the random connectivity can enhance the model's performance and efficiency. The paper includes a visual representation comparing standard LSTM and RCLSTM structures, indicating how connections can be selectively included or omitted. This novel approach aims to leverage the potential advantages of randomness in neural connectivity to improve the LSTM framework.
