The paper introduces the structure and forward propagation mechanism of LSTM, highlighting its potential in time series prediction. To tackle the high computational costs associated with LSTM, the authors propose a novel approach called Random Connectivity LSTM (RCLSTM). This model employs stochastic connectivity between neurons, resulting in a sparse architecture that significantly reduces computational complexity while maintaining prediction accuracy.

The effectiveness of RCLSTM is evaluated through experiments on traffic and user mobility predictions in telecommunication networks. The results indicate that RCLSTM achieves comparable accuracy to conventional LSTM models across varying training sample sizes and input sequence lengths, making it suitable for latency-sensitive applications.

The importance of accurate time series prediction in telecommunications is underscored, as it enables network operators to efficiently allocate resources and respond to dynamic changes in user behavior and network traffic. The paper emphasizes that while traditional methods like ARIMA have been widely used, they often struggle with long-term dependencies, positioning RCLSTM as a promising alternative for improving prediction performance in complex and dynamic environments.
