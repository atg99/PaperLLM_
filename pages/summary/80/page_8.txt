In this paper, the authors introduce a novel model called RCLSTM, designed to enhance time series prediction, particularly in traffic and user mobility forecasting. This model is a reimagining of the conventional Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) architecture, utilizing a random sparse graph to establish neural connections. The study demonstrates that while the RCLSTM model exhibits approximately 25% lower prediction accuracy compared to standard LSTM models, it significantly reduces computational complexity by 30% when utilizing only 1% of neural connections. 

The results of simulations reveal that RCLSTM can match LSTM's prediction accuracy across various input sequence lengths for different users, with User A showing stable accuracy regardless of input length, while User E's accuracy improves with longer sequences due to more irregular mobility patterns. Overall, the RCLSTM model stands out for its ability to maintain satisfactory performance while minimizing the number of parameters, making it a promising tool for future applications in intelligent telecommunication networks. The findings underscore the potential of deep learning techniques in optimizing time series predictions.
