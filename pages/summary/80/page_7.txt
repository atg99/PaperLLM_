The paper presents a detailed analysis of traffic prediction using the RCLSTM (Recurrent Convolutional Long Short-Term Memory) network. The results indicate that RCLSTM models exhibit greater variability compared to traditional LSTM models, particularly in their susceptibility to the length of input sequences. The study compares RCLSTM with established prediction techniques such as SVR (Support Vector Regression), ARIMA (AutoRegressive Integrated Moving Average), and FFNN (Feedforward Neural Network), detailing the hyper-parameters for each method. The findings highlight that the LSTM with a memory cell size of 300 outperforms the other models, including RCLSTM, which also shows effective performance despite its smaller memory cell size.

In the section on human mobility prediction, the RCLSTM model's performance is assessed through user-specific predictions. The results reveal varying prediction accuracies among users, influenced by their behavioral patterns. For instance, users with similar lifestyles showed higher predictability, while those with less structured schedules exhibited lower predictability. Overall, the paper underscores the effectiveness of the RCLSTM model in both traffic and human mobility predictions, emphasizing its advantages over conventional models.
