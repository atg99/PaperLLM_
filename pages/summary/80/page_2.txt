This paper discusses the advancements in time series prediction, particularly focusing on Support Vector Regression (SVR) and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks. While SVR has been effective, it lacks structured methods for parameter determination. LSTMs have emerged as a solution due to their ability to manage sequential data through recurrent connections. However, traditional LSTM architectures face challenges with exponential decay or growth of input effects over time.

To address these limitations, the authors introduce a novel architecture called Random Connectivity Long Short-Term Memory (RCLSTM). This model incorporates sparse neural connections, inspired by the performance of Feed Forward Neural Networks (FFNNs) with sparse connections. The RCLSTM model is designed to reduce computational costs while maintaining predictive accuracy.

The study utilizes two datasets: traffic data from GÉANT networks and realistic user-trajectory data. This choice is motivated by the sensitivity of ANN-based models to data variations and the significance of both traffic and user mobility predictions in telecommunications. Simulation results demonstrate that RCLSTM outperforms traditional LSTM in traffic prediction and user-location forecasting with significantly fewer neural connections. Specifically, RCLSTM with only 1% of the neural connections surpasses ARIMA, SVR, and FFNN models while decreasing computing time by approximately 30%. Notably, as the length of input sequences increases, RCLSTM maintains consistent prediction error, unlike LSTM, which experiences a rise in error.

The paper provides a comprehensive overview of artificial neural networks (ANNs), categorizing them into FFNNs and RNNs, and highlighting their structural differences and applications. The findings emphasize the potential of RCLSTM in enhancing prediction accuracy and efficiency in time series analysis.
