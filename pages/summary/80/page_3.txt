The paper discusses the architecture and functionality of Feedforward Neural Networks (FFNN), Recurrent Neural Networks (RNN), and Long Short-Term Memory networks (LSTM). It begins with an illustration of these models, highlighting that RNNs utilize both the current input and the previous hidden state to generate outputs. This allows RNNs to theoretically manage long-range dependencies in input data. However, practical challenges in learning these dependencies have been identified, particularly by Hochreiter and Schmidhuber.

LSTMs are introduced as a specialized type of RNN designed to effectively learn long-term dependencies. The core enhancement in LSTMs is the memory block, which comprises a memory cell and several gates that regulate information flow. Specifically, input gates determine how much new information is added to the memory cell, forget gates manage the retention of information, and output gates control the information used for output computation.

The paper outlines the activation functions commonly used in artificial neural networks, specifically the sigmoid and tanh functions, and explains the operational steps of LSTMs. These steps include deciding what information to forget, what new information to add, and what to output, all through a series of operations involving the gates and memory cell.

The authors note that since the introduction of LSTMs, various enhancements to the original architecture have been proposed, with Greff et al. evaluating these conventional LSTM improvements. This discussion highlights the evolution and ongoing refinement of LSTM networks in the context of deep learning and time series prediction.
