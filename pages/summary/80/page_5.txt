The paper discusses the Randomly Connected Long Short-Term Memory (RCLSTM) model, highlighting its differences from the traditional LSTM model. The key innovation of RCLSTM lies in its implementation strategy of randomly connecting neurons based on assigned probability values, which can follow various statistical distributions. In the simulations, a uniform distribution is chosen for computational efficiency. This approach allows for the creation of sparsity within the network, significantly reducing the number of parameters to be trained and the overall computational load.

The paper then focuses on the application of the RCLSTM model for traffic and mobility prediction. A three-layer RNN is constructed, consisting of RCLSTM memory blocks, to predict future values based on historical data. The input data spans a sequence of time steps, and the output is the predicted value for the next time step. The performance of the RCLSTM network is evaluated against other algorithms, with variations in training data samples and input sequence lengths examined to assess their impact on prediction accuracy.

Data for the traffic prediction task is sourced from the GÉANT backbone networks, with 10,772 traffic data points collected over a four-month period. Due to the uneven numerical range of the raw data, a logarithmic transformation and normalization process are applied. This ensures the data is constrained between 0 and 1, facilitating faster convergence during the training of the ANN-based models. A sliding window technique is introduced for real-time prediction, utilizing a fixed number of previous time slots for learning and forecasting current data traffic. The processed data is eventually divided into training and test sets to evaluate the RCLSTM network's prediction accuracy.
