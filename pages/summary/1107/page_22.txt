This paper discusses the comparative advantages and challenges of Petri Nets (PNs) and Bayesian Networks (BNs) in model-to-model transformation approaches. Modularization has been highlighted as a successful technique to enhance performance in PN methodologies, indicating potential for future research. When the state space of a PN is extensive, simulation can be a viable alternative to traditional analysis.

The accuracy of BN and PN models, particularly when transformed from other artifacts like fault trees, relies heavily on the correctness of the input models. However, translating large models into PNs can be complex and may lead to difficulties in understanding due to intricate graphical representations. Additionally, the state space explosion problem often arises during analysis of large models.

In contrast, BN approaches offer greater flexibility and produce more comprehensible models. They circumvent the state space explosion issue by leveraging local dependencies among variables, which simplifies modeling complex behaviors. Unlike PNs, where only firing rates of timed transitions need to be set, BNs require the configuration of Conditional Probability Tables (CPTs) for each variable, which can be more cumbersome due to the number of variable combinations.

A practical comparison is illustrated through a dynamic fault tree (DFT) example, revealing that the PN model is significantly more complex than the BN model in terms of nodes and arcs. The parameter setting for the PN model involves managing multiple timed transitions, whereas the BN model, while requiring extensive probability table entries, benefits from automatic generation of deterministic values.

Both models produced similar estimates for the top event probability of the DFT, demonstrating coherence in their results. Furthermore, the paper distinguishes the time modeling capabilities of PNs and BNs, noting that PNs utilize a continuous time model, whereas BNs can operate with both discrete and continuous time models. The choice of time discretization granularity in BNs impacts the balance between computational efficiency and result precision.

Recent advancements, such as a generalized continuous-time BN approach, aim to merge beneficial aspects of both BNs and PNs, though this may also encounter state space explosion challenges. The paper suggests further exploration of approximate algorithms, such as the Boyen-Koller algorithm for dynamic BNs, as a means to address these complexities in analysis.
