The referenced papers collectively explore various methodologies for reliability analysis and fault detection in systems, particularly focusing on the integration of Bayesian networks and Petri nets. 

Bobbio et al. (2003) introduce a parametric fault tree approach for analyzing redundant systems, emphasizing the high-level Petri net semantics for reliability assessment. They also highlight improvements in dependable system analysis through the conversion of fault trees into Bayesian networks (Bobbio et al., 2001). Boudali and Dugan (2005) contribute a discrete-time Bayesian network framework for reliability modeling, extending this to dynamic fault trees and continuous-time models in subsequent works (Boudali & Dugan, 2006).

The application of Bayesian belief networks is further demonstrated by Bouissou et al. (1999, 2003), who assess safety-critical systems and evaluate performance in multistate systems. Boyen and Koller (1998) provide insights into tractable inference methods for complex stochastic processes.

Several studies focus on specific applications, such as Brito and Griffiths (2016), who predict risks associated with autonomous underwater vehicles, and Cai et al. (2012, 2015) who utilize Bayesian networks for reliability evaluation in subsea blowout preventer control systems.

Petri nets are prominently featured in the literature, with various authors discussing their application in diagnosing discrete event systems and modeling safety-critical systems (Cabasino et al., 2009, 2010; Buchacker, 1999). Chiola et al. (1991, 1993) explore stochastic well-formed colored nets for multiprocessor modeling, while Cheng et al. (2015) present a fault diagnosis method considering service features.

Overall, the body of work illustrates the evolving methodologies in reliability engineering, emphasizing the synergy between Bayesian networks and Petri nets for enhanced system analysis and fault management.
