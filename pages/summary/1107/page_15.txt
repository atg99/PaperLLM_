This paper discusses the application of Petri nets (PNs) in the reliability and safety analysis of various systems, particularly in power and manufacturing sectors. It highlights several studies where different types of PNs, including Generalized Stochastic Petri Nets (GSPN) and fuzzy PNs, have been employed for modeling reliability in nuclear power plants, phasor measurement units, and other critical systems.

Ionescu et al. utilized GSPN for medium voltage distribution systems, while Ghosh et al. applied it to analyze PMUs. Codetta-Raiteri and Bobbio addressed dynamic reliability using GSPN and FSPN. In another approach, Garg integrated PNs with intuitionistic fuzzy sets to handle uncertain information in repairable systems by representing failure and repair times with fuzzy numbers.

Singh and Rajput focused on safety analysis in computer-based systems using PNs, proposing methods that consider properties like deadlock and stability. Chung and Chang's hierarchical model for the MOCVD process demonstrated the effects of component failures, while hybrid PNs were used by Ghasemieh et al. for wastewater treatment facility modeling.

In fault detection and diagnosis, PNs were utilized by Sun et al. to create models for electric power systems, leveraging fuzzy set theory to handle incomplete alarm data. Other studies combined fuzzy reasoning with PNs for enhanced fault diagnosis capabilities.

Additionally, the paper mentions the integration of case-based reasoning with PNs for diagnosing induction motor faults and a state-based approach for fault detection under partial observation. Overall, the research illustrates the versatility of PNs in addressing reliability and safety challenges across various domains.
