The paper discusses the limitations of classical approaches to system analysis, which often rely on unrealistic assumptions that can lead to incorrect estimations of system dependability. To address these shortcomings, the paper highlights the effectiveness of Bayesian Networks (BNs) and Petri Nets (PNs) in providing more accurate analyses by modeling systems under realistic conditions. The increasing popularity of BNs is attributed to their flexible structure and ability to reason under uncertainty, making them particularly useful for diagnostic analysis in practical applications. Conversely, PNs are better suited for analyzing the behavior of complex and distributed systems, although their application in safety and reliability assessments remains limited.

Despite the advantages of BNs and PNs, the paper identifies ongoing challenges that need to be addressed to enhance their applicability in various contexts. Future research should focus on overcoming these challenges to expand the use of these advanced analysis methods. The paper concludes by acknowledging the support from the DEIS H2020 project.
