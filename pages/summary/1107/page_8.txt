The paper discusses the handling of uncertainties in systems through various contributions from researchers, particularly focusing on Bayesian Networks (BNs). A summary of key contributions is provided in Table 1, which outlines different aspects such as multi-state systems, event dependencies, uncertainties, human reliability, and continuous variables. 

Notable studies include Wu's integration of fuzzy set theory with BNs for reliability analysis, Huang's fuzzy set approach for imprecise failure data, and Zhang's BN application for safety analysis in construction. Human reliability analysis (HRA) is emphasized as critical for system dependability, with various BN-based methodologies developed for identifying and analyzing human errors. For example, Groth and Swiler's BN adaptation of the SPAR-H method aims to bridge research and practice gaps in the nuclear industry, while Podołlini and Dang's method enhances expert judgment integration regarding human error probabilities. Additionally, Musharraf et al. employed BNs to model human factor interdependencies during emergencies, utilizing multi-expert knowledge to address data scarcity and uncertainty.

The paper also highlights the application of BNs in model-to-model transformation, starting with Torres-Toledano and Sucar's work in 1998, which transformed reliability block diagrams into BN models for probabilistic evaluations. Further advancements in this area are referenced, indicating ongoing research and applications of BNs in reliability and human error analysis.
