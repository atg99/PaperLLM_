The paper discusses advancements in risk assessment methodologies for safety-critical systems, particularly focusing on the integration of Petri nets (PNs) and Bayesian networks (BNs) within decision support systems (DSS). The DSS comprises three modules: the risk assessment module (RAM), simulation module (SM), and decision module (DM). The SM employs colored PNs to model highway network behavior, including accident scenarios and restoration processes. Previous research has leveraged colored PNs for various risk assessments, including the quantitative assessment of domino effects in industrial settings and the evaluation of risks in oil and gas transportation.

The authors highlight the limitations of classical risk assessment methods like Fault Tree Analysis (FTA), Event Tree Analysis (ETA), and Failure Mode and Effects Analysis (FMEA), which often rely on unrealistic assumptions. In contrast, BNs and PNs provide a more nuanced approach, accommodating statistical, stochastic, and temporal dependencies, as well as the repairability of system components. BNs excel in diagnostic analysis, allowing for dynamic updates of failure probabilities, while PNs offer strong semantic foundations for modeling system behavior.

Despite their advantages, both BNs and PNs face challenges, such as the need for expert knowledge in manual construction and the complexity of models. BNs lack formal semantic guidelines, leading to potential inconsistencies, while PNs can suffer from state space explosion, complicating their application in large systems. The paper suggests that further research is needed to enhance the scalability and efficacy of these methodologies, ultimately aiming for more accurate and meaningful safety assessments in complex industrial environments.
