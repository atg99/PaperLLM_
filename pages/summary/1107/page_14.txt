The paper discusses various methodologies for building and analyzing Petri Net (PN) models focused on safety and reliability analysis. It highlights the challenge of state space explosion when generating reachability graphs from PNs, proposing a solution by translating these graphs into equivalent linear logic sequents as suggested by Sadou and Demmou in 2009. 

Additionally, it reviews several model-based approaches utilizing Stochastic Petri Nets (SPNs) for dependability analysis. For instance, Portugal et al. in 2005 applied SPNs to evaluate controller area networks in the context of transient communication faults. Sachdeva et al. in 2008 explored the reliability of pulping systems in the paper industry using Stochastic Reward Nets and Monte Carlo simulations. Signoret's work in 2009 demonstrated SPNs for dependability analysis and safety integrity level allocation. Kleyner and Volovoi in 2010 presented SPNs for availability analysis in safety-critical on-demand systems, incorporating factors like fault detectability and repair duration. Zeng et al. in 2012 modeled smart grid control networks with SPNs, analyzing both transient and steady-state reliability and availability while addressing state-space explosion issues. Finally, Pinna et al. in 2013 utilized Colored Petri Nets (CPNs) for safety and reliability analysis, offering guidelines for hierarchical modeling of deterministic systems.
