This paper provides a comprehensive overview of various methodologies for reliability analysis and fault diagnosis in complex systems, particularly through the application of Petri nets and Bayesian networks. It discusses the use of Hybrid Petri nets for modeling and performance evaluation in industrial settings, such as bottle-filling plants, and emphasizes the importance of fault tree analysis combined with Petri nets for safety assessments. The research highlights automated modeling techniques for dynamic reliability block diagrams and the application of Bayesian models to predict software reliability at an architectural level.

Several studies are referenced, illustrating the effectiveness of Petri nets in reliability analysis across diverse systems, including pulping systems and gantry crane operations. The paper also explores the integration of Bayesian networks with structural reliability methods, enhancing the robustness of reliability assessments. It further addresses the significance of task dependence in human reliability analysis and the modeling of safety-critical systems.

The findings suggest that combining different analytical frameworks can lead to improved reliability predictions and safety management in complex, safety-critical environments. The references included span various international conferences and journals, showcasing the ongoing research in this field and the evolution of methodologies for system reliability and safety analysis.
