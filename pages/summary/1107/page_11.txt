The paper discusses the application of Bayesian Networks (BNs) as model-to-model transformation approaches in various dependability and reliability contexts. It highlights classical fault trees, dynamic fault trees, and bow-tie diagrams as key dependability artefacts translated into BNs for risk assessment. Key contributions from various researchers are cited, indicating a rich body of work focused on integrating BNs with traditional probabilistic risk assessment methodologies.

Bayesian inference has been employed in risk assessment across different domains, including nuclear waste disposal and patient health monitoring. The integration of BNs with traditional methods allows for a dynamic approach to risk assessment, updating failure probabilities based on real-time observations. Notably, the bow-tie model, which combines fault and event trees, is emphasized for its effectiveness in real-time risk assessment scenarios.

The paper also outlines methodologies for mapping bow-tie diagrams to BNs, aiding in dynamic risk assessment, particularly in process industries. Comparisons between fault trees and BNs are made, with emphasis on the advantages of using BNs for safety risk analysis. A novel algorithm, neutral dependency, is proposed to reduce the complexity of conditional probability tables, enhancing the efficiency of risk analysis in process industries. Overall, the paper underscores the versatility and efficacy of BNs in enhancing risk assessment practices.
