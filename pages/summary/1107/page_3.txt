This paper provides a detailed review of the application of Bayesian Networks (BNs) and Petri Nets (PNs) in the context of system safety, reliability, and risk assessments. It is structured into several sections: Section 2 introduces the fundamentals of BNs and PNs, while Sections 3 and 4 delve into their specific applications in system safety and reliability, and risk assessment, respectively. Section 5 discusses future research directions, and Section 6 offers concluding remarks.

In the background section, Bayesian Networks are defined as directed acyclic graphs that illustrate a set of variables and their conditional dependencies. A BN consists of a qualitative component, represented by a directed acyclic graph, and a quantitative component, which includes prior and conditional probabilities for the nodes. The nodes symbolize random variables, and directed arcs denote the dependencies among them. Parent nodes directly influence their child nodes, which have conditional probability distributions reflecting this influence.

The concept of conditional independence is crucial in constructing BN models, with the rules of d-separation providing a method to ascertain independence between variables. This allows the determination of the joint probability distribution of a set of random variables using a chain rule. The paper emphasizes the significance of BNs and PNs in enhancing system safety and reliability through structured probabilistic assessments.
