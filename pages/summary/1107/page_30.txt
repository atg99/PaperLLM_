The reviewed literature focuses on various applications of Bayesian networks and Petri nets in risk analysis and reliability assessment across different domains, including maritime, nuclear, and chemical industries. Key contributions highlight the use of Bayesian approaches for dynamic risk analysis, safety assessments, and the modeling of complex systems.

Kelangath et al. (2012) utilize a data-driven Bayesian approach for assessing risks associated with damaged ships. Kelly and Smith (2009) provide an overview of Bayesian inference in probabilistic risk assessment, emphasizing its current methodologies. Khakzad and colleagues explore the application of dynamic Bayesian networks for analyzing domino effects in chemical infrastructures and compare fault tree and Bayesian network approaches for safety analysis in process facilities.

Further contributions from Khakzad et al. (2013) include risk-based design methodologies and the integration of bow-tie analysis into Bayesian networks. Kim et al. (2002, 2006) advance reliability modeling through the use of reliability graphs and probabilistic methods. Other studies, such as those by Kleyner et al. (2010) and Kumar et al. (1993), illustrate the application of Petri nets in reliability prediction and evaluation.

Li et al. (2014, 2016) contribute to maritime risk analysis by employing Bayesian networks with quantitative inputs, while also addressing the assessment of catastrophic risks using domain knowledge and spatial data. Overall, the literature collectively underscores the significance of Bayesian networks and Petri nets in enhancing the understanding and management of risks in complex systems, advocating for their integration in safety and reliability frameworks.
