The paper discusses various advancements in the application of Bayesian networks (BNs) for system reliability analysis, particularly through the transformation of fault trees (FTs) into BNs. Key contributions include the introduction of reliability block diagrams with general gates (RBDGG), which extend traditional models by incorporating multi-state systems and common cause failures. The fundamental process of mapping FTs to BNs is outlined, where basic and intermediate events are represented as nodes, with prior probabilities derived from failure probabilities of the components.

Predictive and diagnostic analyses are facilitated through this BN structure, allowing for the assessment of system reliability and the identification of critical failure contributors. Despite the prevalence of precise failure probabilities in many analyses, the paper highlights the challenge of epistemic uncertainty, particularly in large systems. To address this, evidence theory is integrated with BNs, enabling the handling of both random and epistemic uncertainties.

In addition to classical FTs, dynamic fault trees (DFTs) are examined, showcasing a method for their reliability evaluation through discrete-time Bayesian networks (DTBNs). This approach not only models the intricate behaviors of system components but also mitigates the limitations associated with traditional Markov chain methods. Overall, the paper emphasizes the versatility and effectiveness of BNs in enhancing reliability assessments across various applications, including human reliability analysis in oil tanker operations and evaluations in construction and transportation systems.
