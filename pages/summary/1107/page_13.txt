The paper discusses the application of Bayesian networks (BNs) and Petri nets (PNs) in safety, reliability, and risk assessments of various systems, including Autonomous Underwater Vehicles (AUVs) and water mains. Brito and Griffiths (2016) introduced a Bayesian approach to predict the risk of AUV loss in hazardous conditions, while Kabir et al. (2015) utilized BNs to assess water main failure risks by considering factors like hydraulic capacity and structural integrity. Trucco et al. (2008) integrated Human and Organizational Factors into risk analysis using a combination of fault tree analysis and BNs, enhancing the assessment of human impacts on system safety.

The paper further explores the use of PNs for safety and reliability analysis, highlighting their application as both standalone approaches and in model-to-model transformations. For instance, Leveson and Stolzy (1987) modeled real-time safety-critical systems with Timed Petri nets, extending the model to include faults for safety analysis. Dutuit et al. (1997) focused on qualitative event sequences leading to system failures and reliability in distributed systems.

Other notable contributions include Schneeweiss (2001), who provided a tutorial on PN modeling for reliability scenarios, and Volovoi (2004), who offered a flexible framework for analyzing non-exponentially distributed data. Mura and Bondavalli (2001) introduced a technique using Markov Regenerative Stochastic Petri Nets for dynamic behavior modeling, while Wu and Wu (2015) proposed an extended object-oriented PN model for mission reliability analysis.

The paper also highlights the work of Adamyan and He, who addressed sequence-dependent failure behaviors and enhanced PN modeling by introducing inhibitor arcs to reduce computational complexity. Sadou and Demmou (2009) focused on temporal behavior analysis using PNs to identify causes of unexpected scenarios, evaluating reliability through minimal scenario sequences leading to feared states. Overall, the integration of BNs and PNs in risk assessment underscores their significance in enhancing system safety and reliability.
