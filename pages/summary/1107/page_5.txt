The paper discusses various extensions of Petri Nets (PNs) to address limitations in modeling time-dependent behavior. Stochastic Petri Nets (SPNs) allow for exponentially distributed transition delays, while Generalized Stochastic Petri Nets (GSPNs) further enable the combination of immediate and timed transitions with graphical representations. Extended Stochastic Petri Nets (ESPNs) permit arbitrary distributions for transition delays, and Fluid Stochastic Petri Nets (FSPNs) introduce discrete and fluid places for more complex system behavior modeling. The inclusion of inhibitor arcs enhances the functionality of PNs by modifying transition firing rules based on token counts in connected places, enabling non-occurrence verification of events.

The paper also explores the application of Bayesian Networks (BNs) in safety and reliability analysis. BNs can be utilized as standalone models to directly analyze system behavior or in conjunction with other dependability analysis tools, such as fault trees, which are converted into BN models for further analysis. Early applications of BNs in this context date back to 1996, with dynamic BNs providing insights into dynamic reliability assessments.
