The translation process from Dependency Fault Trees (DFTs) to Dynamic Bayesian Networks (DBNs) parallels the conversion of Fault Trees (FTs) to Bayesian Networks (BNs), with the key distinction being the incorporation of dynamic gates alongside Boolean gates in DBNs. The quantitative aspect of this translation, specifically the generation of prior and conditional probabilities, diverges from static methods as it accounts for component failures across different time points and the dynamic behavior of logic gates. The choice of temporal discretization granularity is crucial, impacting both accuracy and computational performance.

To address challenges associated with time discretization, Boudali and Dugan proposed a continuous time BN approach, which utilizes continuous variables and expresses probabilities as probability density functions, thus avoiding the need for multi-dimensional probability tables. This method allows for an exact closed-form solution for system reliability evaluation. Marquez et al. advanced this further by integrating discrete and continuous nodes within a single model, accommodating various distributions for time-to-failure.

Montani et al. introduced a systematic method for translating DFTs to DBNs for dependability analysis, culminating in the development of the RADYBAN tool for automatic conversion into two-time-slice BNs (2TBNs). While instant-based approaches like 2TBNs have faced criticism for complexity, they effectively model time horizons with only two slices. Portinale et al. enhanced this framework by introducing a repair box gate for modeling repairable systems.

Recent advancements include applying dynamic BNs for fault detection in autonomous spacecraft and translating DFTs to Generalised Continuous Time Bayesian Networks (GCTBNs), which can account for component repairability during analysis. However, the GCTBN's reliance on Generalized Stochastic Petri Nets (GSPNs) may lead to state space explosion due to numerous states in multi-state nodes.

Beyond DFTs and FTs, various artifacts, including bow-tie diagrams and Failure Mode and Effects Analysis (FMEA), have been transformed into BNs. Additionally, BNs have been utilized alongside other dependability models to enhance analysis outcomes, as seen in applications involving FMEA, fuzzy set theory, Markov chains, semi-Markovian processes, and UML diagrams for component-based software reliability evaluation. These methodologies collectively contribute to a robust framework for system safety and reliability analysis.
