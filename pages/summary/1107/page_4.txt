Bayesian Networks (BNs) offer a robust framework for reasoning under uncertainty by integrating multiple information sources for comprehensive assessments. They facilitate both forward (predictive) and backward (diagnostic) analyses. In forward analysis, the model progresses from root nodes to leaf nodes, updating beliefs about effects based on known causes. Conversely, backward analysis works from effects back to causes, utilizing evidence on unknown variables to refine beliefs about known variables, guided by Bayes' theorem.

While classical BNs excel in static domains, they have been adapted for dynamic systems through formulations such as Dynamic Bayesian Networks (DBNs) and Temporal Bayesian Networks (TBNs). Hybrid Bayesian Networks (HBNs) can incorporate both discrete and continuous variables, enhancing their applicability.

Petri Nets (PNs) serve as a formal modeling tool for complex, distributed, and concurrent systems. Represented as a bipartite directed graph, classical PNs consist of places, transitions, and arcs, with a defined capacity and initial marking. They enable the analysis of system behavior through the interaction of tokens across input and output places, driven by transition conditions. Despite their analytical simplicity, classical PNs may encounter state explosion in moderately complex systems, prompting the development of more refined models to mitigate this challenge.
