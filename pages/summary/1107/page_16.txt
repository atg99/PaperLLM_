The paper discusses various methodologies for fault diagnosis and reliability analysis in discrete event systems (DES) using Petri nets (PNs). Basile et al. (2009) introduced a diagnoser for DES that employs integer linear programming for online fault event computation. To address the need for redesigning the diagnoser with system model changes, Dotoli et al. (2008, 2009) proposed an online diagnoser using PNs, which allows for real-time fault detection. Subsequent works by Cabasino et al. (2009, 2010) and Renganathan and Bhaskar (2010, 2013) further enhanced online diagnosis methods through the use of PNs and hybrid PNs, particularly in bottling plants.

State space explosion is a challenge in PN-based approaches, leading Giua and Seatzu (2005) to propose a method involving basis markings to mitigate exhaustive state enumeration. Jiroveanu et al. (2008) expanded upon this by developing a modular approach using timed PNs, significantly improving scalability and computational efficiency for large systems.

The paper also reviews model-to-model transformation approaches where different models are translated into PNs for safety and reliability analysis. Early work by Hura and Atwood (1988) established the use of PNs in fault tree analysis, which was later complemented by methodologies like those from Malhotra and Trivedi (1995) and Liu and Chiou (1997), focusing on dependability modeling and failure analysis. Additionally, dynamic fault trees have been adapted into PNs for further reliability assessments, with methodologies proposed by Codetta-Raiteri (2005) and Zhang et al. (2009), as well as Kabir et al. (2018a, 2015b).

The paper highlights the significance of various fault tree gates, such as AND, OR, PAND, FDEP, and SPARE, in modeling complex system dependencies and event sequences, illustrating their functional behaviors in reliability analysis.
