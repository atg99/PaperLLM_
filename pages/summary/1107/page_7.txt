This paper discusses the process of building and utilizing Bayesian Networks (BNs) for safety and reliability analysis, as outlined by Sigurdsson et al. in 2001. It highlights that while many BN-based methods depend on simulation techniques for system reliability evaluation, Guan et al. (2012) introduced a more efficient analytical approach. This method employs the Laplace method, Bayesian updating, and system response estimation, avoiding reliance on simulations like Monte Carlo, thus enhancing computational efficiency for real-time applications.

Additionally, Cai et al. (2015) presented a hybrid approach that integrates classical and dynamic BNs to assess reliability in real-time. The classical BN focuses on failure diagnosis by identifying root causes, while the dynamic BN is utilized for predictive reliability analysis.

The paper notes that most BN methodologies rely on accurate lifetime data for components to estimate system reliability. However, obtaining precise failure data can be challenging, indicating a limitation in current approaches. Overall, the paper emphasizes the advancements in BN methodologies for reliability analysis and their applications in real-time scenarios.
