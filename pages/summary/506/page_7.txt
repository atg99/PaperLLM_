This approach simplifies the training process and focuses on maximizing the agreement between positive pairs while minimizing the agreement across different samples. The main objective is to learn robust representations that generalize well across various tasks.

Self-supervised learning (SSL) encompasses various applications such as image classification, object detection, semantic segmentation, and human action recognition (HAR). Each application leverages self-supervised models to enhance feature extraction and improve performance with limited labeled data. For instance, in image classification, multiple network architectures like AlexNet and ResNet are employed, while object detection focuses on recognizing object categories and their positions using datasets such as MOSOCO and OpenImage. Semantic segmentation assigns labels to image pixels, essential for fields like robotics and autonomous driving, with models like fully convolutional networks (FCN) revolutionizing this task.

HAR identifies actions in videos using both spatial and temporal features, addressing challenges associated with limited labeled data. Techniques such as view-enhanced jigsaw puzzle (VEJP) have been proposed to enhance feature robustness for action recognition.

The foundation of SSL lies in convolutional neural networks (ConvNets), with different architectures influencing the effectiveness of learned representations. SSL techniques are broadly categorized into contrastive and non-contrastive learning. Contrastive learning emphasizes the relationship between similar and dissimilar samples, utilizing data augmentation and various encoders to enhance feature extraction. In contrast, non-contrastive learning relies solely on positive sample pairs, streamlining the training process while aiming for improved representation quality across tasks.
