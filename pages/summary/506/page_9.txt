Self-supervised learning (SSL) has emerged as a significant approach across various domains, including natural language processing, healthcare, and computer vision. In the context of natural language processing, the ALBERT model was introduced by Lan et al. to reduce memory requirements while maintaining performance. This model employs factorized embedding parameterization and cross-layer parameter sharing, allowing for a substantial decrease in parameters without increasing memory usage as network depth grows.

In healthcare, SSL has been applied to diverse tasks, as discussed by Chowdhury et al. They explored several pretext tasks such as pixel to scalar and pixel to pixel transformations, alongside adversarial and contrastive learning methods. Notably, Jamaludin et al. utilized longitudinal MRI scans with a Siamese CNN to learn embeddings, effectively separating images from the same patient over time in latent space. Other studies, including those by Chen et al. and Kwasigroch et al., demonstrated the efficacy of SSL in medical image analysis and treatment, achieving promising results even with limited labeled data.

In computer vision, SSL is gaining traction for applications like object detection and image classification. The methods range from one-stage to two-stage detection techniques, with SSL facilitating improved performance. Gidaris et al. introduced a rotation recognition method using ConvNets, while Huang et al. highlighted SSL's potential in few-shot object detection, addressing the challenges of dense labeling. Liu et al. proposed Self-EMD for learning spatial-visual representations, further showcasing the adaptability of SSL across multiple image perspectives.

Overall, the reviewed literature emphasizes SSL's capability to enhance learning in various fields by leveraging unlabelled data effectively, thereby addressing the limitations of traditional supervised learning approaches.
