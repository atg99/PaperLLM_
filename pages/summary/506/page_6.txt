The paper discusses various pretext tasks in self-supervised learning (SSL) that enhance feature representation for downstream tasks such as multi-label image classification and semantic segmentation. Treneska et al. utilized a Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) for image colorization, achieving realistic results and transferring knowledge to classification and segmentation tasks based on a dataset of nearly 12,000 images. 

Key pretext tasks include predicting a missing patch, where Doersch et al. employed SSL to train models to determine the relative positions of image patches using a CNN based on AlexNet architecture. Another task involves estimating the rotation angle of images, which requires instance discrimination to identify object positions after rotation. Devgon et al. developed a model that estimates rotation between depth images.

Jigsaw puzzle solving is highlighted as a task requiring understanding of patch relationships. Li et al. proposed using a GAN model within a multitask pipeline to solve unpaired jigsaw puzzles by classifying permutations and recovering image features in the correct order.

Downstream tasks, which include classification and object detection, benefit from the feature representations learned during pretext tasks, underscoring their importance in training effective models.
