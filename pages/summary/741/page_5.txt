The paper presents a novel multi-sensor fusion system named Lvio-Fusion, designed to enhance visual-inertial odometry using data from various sensors including cameras, lidar, IMU, and GPS. The core data structure is an observation matrix that captures motion and environmental information by segmenting the image into a grid. The proposed network utilizes the Twin Delayed Deep Deterministic policy gradient algorithm (TD3) to optimize policy decisions for maximizing long-term rewards, employing six Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs) as network backbones.

The system's performance is evaluated through experiments on the KITTI and KAIST Urban datasets. The KITTI dataset, which includes synchronized data from multiple sensors, is used to compare Lvio-Fusion against existing algorithms like VINS-Fusion and LIO-SAM. Results indicate that Lvio-Fusion outperforms these methods in terms of Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE), particularly in challenging scenarios where sensor interruptions occur. The system is robust to sensor failures, demonstrating superior accuracy and lower drift.

In the KAIST Urban dataset evaluation, the complexity of urban environments poses additional challenges, yet Lvio-Fusion effectively mitigates GPS drift through visual-inertial odometry and global optimization techniques, achieving competitive ATE results compared to VINS-Fusion.

Overall, the findings suggest that Lvio-Fusion, with its self-adaptive TD3 agent and effective multi-sensor integration, not only enhances accuracy but also maintains performance under varying conditions, making it a significant contribution to the field of sensor fusion and SLAM. The implementation is available on GitHub for further exploration and application.
