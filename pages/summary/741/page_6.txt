The paper discusses the architecture and performance of the TD3 algorithm in the context of multi-sensor fusion, specifically using Lvio-Fusion for pose estimation. It presents a network architecture where multiple critics evaluate the actions of an actor, calculating Temporal Difference (TD) errors to refine the learning process. The study involves experiments using the KAIST urban dataset, where the performance of Lvio-Fusion is compared against VINS-Fusion, showcasing significant improvements in mean Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) for Lvio-Fusion.

The training phase involves dividing the dataset into environments defined by keyframes, with the TD3 agent iteratively optimizing pose estimates based on rewards received from the Lvio-Fusion output. The training results indicate that a two-layer Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) backbone yielded the highest rewards, leading to its selection for validation.

In the validation phase, the paper emphasizes the importance of Relative Pose Error (RPE) for evaluating results without global optimization. Results indicate that the self-adaptive algorithm integrated into Lvio-Fusion significantly reduces error rates, with the average adjustment time for keyframe weights being 5.56 milliseconds. Overall, the findings affirm that the lightweight self-adaptive algorithm enhances the accuracy of multi-sensor fusion techniques.
