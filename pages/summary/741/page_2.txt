The paper discusses advancements in sensor fusion methods for state estimation, focusing on vision-based and LiDAR-based techniques. It categorizes sensor fusion approaches into loosely coupled and tightly coupled systems, with the latter being more prevalent due to their integration of raw IMU measurements directly into pose estimates. Various methods are highlighted, including filtering-based, optimization-based, and deep learning-based approaches, each with its advantages and challenges. Filtering methods, like EKF and UKF, are noted for their speed but are sensitive to time synchronization issues. Optimization-based methods enhance robustness against synchronization problems by utilizing local maps and bundle adjustment for improved real-time performance. Deep learning methods, while innovative, face challenges in applicability and performance.

The proposed optimization-based framework integrates multiple sensors, defining a coordinate system with the robot's body frame coinciding with the IMU frame. The robot's state is represented as a combination of rotation, position, speed, and IMU bias. The framework formulates a maximum a posteriori (MAP) problem under a Gaussian noise model, transforming it into a nonlinear least-squares problem that minimizes residuals from inertial, visual, and LiDAR factors. 

The system architecture includes a visual frontend that extracts 3D landmarks from stereo cameras and integrates IMU measurements for initial pose estimations. It employs visual-inertial odometry for local optimization and incorporates LiDAR data for further refinement. The system also integrates GPS and loop closure constraints to enhance global pose optimization. The visual factors are derived from a combination of the KLT sparse optical flow algorithm and feature-based methods, with a local map built to maintain tracking stability even under challenging conditions.
