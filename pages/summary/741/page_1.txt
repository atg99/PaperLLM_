The paper presents Lvio-Fusion, a self-adaptive multi-sensor fusion framework designed for simultaneous localization and mapping (SLAM) in mobile robots. The framework integrates data from stereo cameras, Lidar, IMU, and GPS, utilizing graph optimization techniques to enhance state estimation in diverse environments, particularly urban traffic scenes.

Key innovations of Lvio-Fusion include a segmented global pose graph optimization approach that mitigates accumulated drifts and improves global positioning accuracy. The framework addresses the limitations of traditional visual and Lidar methods, such as sensitivity to initialization and the sparsity of Lidar data, by incorporating additional sensors like GPS to provide robust positioning.

A significant feature of Lvio-Fusion is the implementation of an actor-critic method from reinforcement learning, which dynamically adjusts the weights of various sensors based on their performance in different environments. This adaptability enhances the overall accuracy and robustness of the system.

The authors evaluate Lvio-Fusion against state-of-the-art methods using public datasets, demonstrating its superior estimation accuracy and resilience across varied conditions. The framework is presented as open-source and scalable, contributing to advancements in multi-source perception information fusion for mobile robotics.
