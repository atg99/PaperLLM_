The paper discusses the use of molecular fingerprints and structural descriptors to quantify the similarity of molecules, particularly in relation to the atypical antipsychotic drug clozapine. It highlights that traditional methods, such as calculating Tanimoto similarity, can yield misleading results, where the most similar molecules may not visually or quantitatively align. A dataset of molecules that inhibit the uptake of clozapine was analyzed, revealing that only a few, including olanzapine, consistently ranked among the top 50 similar drugs across various encoding methods in RDKit. Notably, prazosin, a potent inhibitor, showed low Tanimoto similarity despite structural similarities. This variability in results suggests the inadequacy of current fingerprint encodings and indicates a need for alternative strategies to better assess molecular similarities.
