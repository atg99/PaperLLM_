The paper discusses the potential of variational autoencoders (VAEs) in generating novel molecules, emphasizing the vast opportunities for chemical exploration even without prior knowledge of bioactivities. The authors explain that the generalization ability of VAEs is linked to their learning process, which involves understanding a "neighborhood" around training examples within a lower-dimensional manifold. They found that increasing the training set size improved generalization, but noted that their architecture might have been too complex for smaller datasets, potentially hindering generalization. 

The authors highlight the significance of navigating chemical spaces and the unique estimation of molecular similarity allowed by the VAE approach. They acknowledge the limitations of their study, particularly concerning the exhaustive analysis of hyperparameters due to training constraints. The paper concludes by affirming that their work contributes to the understanding of molecular similarity and the capabilities of VAEs in cheminformatics.
