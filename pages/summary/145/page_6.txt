The article discusses the implementation of a Variational Autoencoder (VAE) for measuring molecular similarity, specifically targeting drugs, metabolites, and natural products. The study utilizes a dataset partitioned into training, validation, and testing sets, achieving over 95% valid SMILES outputs after training. The VAE associates each molecule's SMILES with a normalized 100-dimensional vector, allowing for the calculation of Euclidean distances to measure similarity.

The authors compare the VAE's similarity metrics with traditional encodings from RDKit, specifically MACCS and Patterned encodings. The results indicate that the VAE-Sim values, derived from the Euclidean distance of the latent vectors, differ significantly from the traditional Tanimoto similarities, suggesting that the VAE captures molecular relationships more locally. Visual representations, including heatmaps and similarity comparisons between metabolite-drug pairs, highlight the distinct outputs of the VAE compared to standard fingerprint encodings. The findings emphasize the potential of VAE in providing unique insights into molecular similarity beyond conventional methods.
