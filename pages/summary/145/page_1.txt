This article presents VAE-Sim, a novel molecular similarity measure based on a variational autoencoder (VAE). The authors argue that traditional methods of molecular similarity assessment, which involve various "fingerprint" encodings, yield inconsistent similarity values despite using the same metrics. This inconsistency suggests that no single encoding method is inherently superior. 

The VAE developed in this study learns the joint distribution of input and output data, transforming molecular structures into a latent vector within a "bottleneck layer." This unique architecture allows for both encoding and decoding of molecular information, specifically the SMILES representation of molecules. The VAE was trained on a substantial dataset of over six million druglike molecules and natural products, including a final holdout set of over one million molecules.

The resulting VAE vector distances provide a rapid and effective metric for assessing molecular similarity, which can be easily calculated. The paper discusses the methodology and its application in typical cheminformatics scenarios, emphasizing its potential advantages over existing similarity measures. Overall, VAE-Sim represents a significant advancement in the field of cheminformatics, particularly in the context of quantitative-structure-activity relationship (QSAR) analyses.
