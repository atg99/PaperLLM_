The paper presents Algorithm 1, E-GAN, which incorporates evolutionary strategies into Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs). Key parameters include the batch size, the number of discriminator updates, the number of parents, and mutation steps, with default values provided for hyperparameters. The algorithm iteratively updates the discriminator and generator parameters by sampling from real and noise data, optimizing the discriminator using the Adam optimizer based on gradient calculations.

A significant feature of E-GAN is the least-squares mutation inspired by LSGAN, which penalizes the generator for failing to deceive the discriminator. This mutation approach is designed to prevent vanishing gradients when the discriminator is strong, thereby mitigating mode collapse by avoiding extreme costs for generating fake samples. The paper asserts that while LSGAN uses a different loss function, the optimal discriminator aligns with the proposed method.

Additionally, the evaluation process in the algorithm is crucial for measuring the quality of generated samples, guiding the evolutionary direction. An evaluation or fitness function is developed to facilitate individual selection in the evolutionary framework, ensuring effective optimization of the generative model.
