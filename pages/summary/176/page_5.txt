The paper discusses two types of mutation functions used in Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs): minimax mutation and heuristic mutation. 

Minimax mutation is based on the minimax objective function, aiming to minimize the Jensen-Shannon divergence (JSD) between the real and generated data distributions. Although theoretically sound, it suffers from practical issues like the vanishing gradient problem when the discriminator confidently rejects generated samples, leading to a constant JSD if the distributions are on separate manifolds. However, effective gradients are generated when there is overlap between the two distributions.

Heuristic mutation, on the other hand, seeks to maximize the log probability of the discriminator making an error. This approach avoids saturation and the vanishing gradient problem, resulting in more consistent updates for the generator. However, it can lead to training instability and fluctuations in generative quality since it effectively pushes the distributions apart.

Both methods have their strengths and weaknesses in terms of performance and stability during training.
