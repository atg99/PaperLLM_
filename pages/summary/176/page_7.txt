The paper discusses a novel approach to evaluating the performance of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) through two key properties: quality and diversity of generated samples. The quality fitness score is defined as the average output of a discriminator when fed with images produced by the generator, indicating how well the generator can deceive the discriminator. A higher quality score suggests that the generator's output closely approximates the target data distribution.

To address the common issue of mode collapse in GANs, the paper introduces a diversity fitness score, which is based on the log gradient values of the discriminator during updates. This score assesses the spread of generated samples; a higher diversity score indicates that the generator avoids collapsing to a narrow region, thus maintaining stability in training and preventing the discriminator from easily identifying fake samples.

The overall evaluation function for the proposed evolutionary algorithm combines both fitness scores, balancing generative quality and diversity to enhance training efficiency and generative performance. The findings emphasize the importance of both quality and diversity in improving GAN optimization and mitigating mode collapse.
