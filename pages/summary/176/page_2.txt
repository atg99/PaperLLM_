Traditional GANs utilize Jensen-Shannon divergence, but alternative metrics such as least-squares, absolute deviation, Kullback-Leibler divergence, and Wasserstein distance have been proposed to enhance performance. Each metric has its advantages and drawbacks; for instance, Kullback-Leibler divergence mitigates vanishing gradients but can lead to mode collapse, while Wasserstein distance improves stability but may cause non-convergent cycles.

To leverage the strengths of these metrics while mitigating their weaknesses, the authors propose an evolutionary generative adversarial network (E-GAN). This framework treats the adversarial training as an evolutionary process, where the discriminator serves as the environment providing adaptive loss functions, and a population of generators evolves through mutations to improve performance. In each iteration, the discriminator continues to distinguish between real and generated samples, while generators adapt to the discriminator's feedback. The best-performing generators are retained for further training, aligning with the principle of survival of the fittest.

The proposed E-GAN framework effectively integrates multiple adversarial training objectives, enhancing both training stability and generative quality. Experimental results across various datasets confirm the efficacy of this approach in improving image generation outcomes. The paper also reviews related works on GANs and evolutionary algorithms, highlighting prior efforts aimed at enhancing training stability and generative performance.
