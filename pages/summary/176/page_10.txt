The paper discusses the robustness of various Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) architectures, specifically focusing on the proposed E-GAN method. The authors compare E-GAN with several existing GAN methods, including DCGAN, LSGAN, WGAN, and WGAN-GP, using different network architectures on the LSUN bedroom dataset.

The study evaluates architectures that face distinct training challenges, such as a limited discriminator capability (2-Conv-1-FC LeakyReLU), a generator without batch normalization and a fixed number of filters, and a setup where both generator and discriminator lack normalization. Each architecture was trained using five methods for 200,000 iterations.

Results indicate that E-GAN maintains training stability and generates high-quality, diverse images even when other methods fail. The findings highlight E-GAN's advantage in architecture robustness, suggesting its effectiveness in producing reasonable results across various GAN configurations. Additional details and generated images are provided in the supplementary material.
