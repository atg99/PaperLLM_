The paper discusses Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) and introduces a novel evolutionary algorithm to enhance their performance. GANs consist of two networks: a generator (G) that creates data and a discriminator (D) that distinguishes between real and generated data. The original GAN formulation involves a minimax game where G aims to produce data close to the true distribution, while D seeks to differentiate between real and fake samples.

In contrast to traditional GANs, which update G and D alternately, the proposed evolutionary algorithm evolves a population of generators in the context of D. Each generator in the population represents a potential solution, and through an evolutionary process, these generators adapt to create increasingly realistic samples. The evolution consists of three stages: variation, evaluation, and selection. Variation involves generating offspring through mutations of existing generators. Evaluation assesses the quality of each offspring using a fitness function based on D's performance. Selection retains the best-performing generators for the next iteration while discarding the weaker ones.

The discriminative network D is continuously updated to improve its ability to distinguish between real and generated samples, providing adaptive losses that guide the evolution of the generators. Additionally, the paper details various mutation strategies aimed at optimizing the training objectives to minimize the gap between the generated and real data distributions. Overall, the evolutionary approach aims to enhance the capability of GANs in generating realistic data.
