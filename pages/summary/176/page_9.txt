The paper presents an evolutionary adversarial training framework, E-GAN, which is compared against conventional GANs that use individual adversarial objectives. The authors trained both methods for 50,000 iterations and found that traditional GANs exhibited varying degrees of mode collapse, while E-GAN effectively combined different objectives to enhance model performance and accurately fit target distributions. This improvement is attributed to E-GAN's evolutionary mechanism that recognizes and promotes successful updates.

In terms of sample quality and convergence speed, E-GAN outperformed other GAN models on CIFAR-10, achieving higher inception scores with fewer training steps and demonstrating stability during convergence. Conventional GANs struggled with issues such as instability and slow convergence. E-GAN's ability to integrate various objectives allowed it to overcome the limitations of individual adversarial metrics, including WGAN and its variations.

During training, the selection of objectives was monitored, revealing that heuristic and least-square mutations were favored initially, while minimax mutations gained prominence as training progressed. This shift is linked to the challenges of providing effective gradients in the minimax approach when the discriminator easily identifies generated samples. Overall, E-GAN's framework not only improves performance but also maintains a comparable convergence speed despite longer iteration times.
