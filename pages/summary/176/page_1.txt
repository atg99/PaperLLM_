This paper introduces a novel framework called evolutionary generative adversarial networks (E-GAN) to address the training challenges faced by traditional generative adversarial networks (GANs), such as instability and mode collapse. Unlike conventional GANs that rely on a fixed adversarial objective function, E-GAN employs various adversarial training objectives as mutation operations, evolving a population of generators that adapt to the discriminator. 

The framework incorporates an evaluation mechanism to assess the quality and diversity of the generated samples, ensuring that only the best-performing generators are retained for further training. This evolutionary approach allows E-GAN to overcome the limitations associated with single training objectives and enhances the overall performance of GANs.

Experimental results on multiple datasets demonstrate that E-GAN achieves superior generative performance while mitigating the common training issues found in existing GANs. The findings suggest that E-GAN could significantly improve the stability and effectiveness of generative modeling in real-world applications.
