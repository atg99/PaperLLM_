The paper presents E-GAN, an evolutionary algorithm designed to improve the training of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs). E-GAN treats the generators as an evolutionary population and the discriminator as an environment, allowing for adaptive mutations in generator objectives during training. This approach addresses common challenges in conventional GANs, such as vanishing gradients and mode collapse, by integrating various adversarial objectives to enhance performance.

Experiments were conducted on multiple datasets, including CIFAR-10, LSUN bedroom, and CelebA, using a DCGAN architecture. The methodology involved retaining only the best-performing generator in each evolutionary step, which reduced computational costs while maintaining stability and performance. Results demonstrated that E-GAN effectively mitigates mode collapse and generates high-quality images, as evidenced by improved inception scores across different iterations and wall-clock times compared to traditional GAN approaches.
