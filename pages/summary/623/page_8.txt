The paper discusses advanced techniques in cross-modal hashing, particularly focusing on cross-modal deep variational hashing and deep visual-semantic hashing. Cross-modal deep variational hashing employs a two-step learning process: first, it learns a fusion network alongside a joint binary code matrix for different modalities through alternative optimization; second, it develops modality-specific neural networks to align the top-level representations with the binary codes while managing the approximate posterior distribution relative to a KL-divergence regularized prior.

Deep visual-semantic hashing integrates CNNs for image data and LSTMs for sentence data, creating a joint embedding space and separate structures for each modality. Different loss functions guide this process, optimizing the alignment between image-sentence pairs.

The paper also highlights the use of autoencoders for multi-modal data processing. Deep multi-modal hashing with orthogonal regularization addresses redundancy in multi-modal representations by implementing an orthogonal structure, ensuring compact and accurate hash codes. Furthermore, a multi-modal deep belief network is discussed, which includes a joint Restricted Boltzmann Machine to capture high-level representations across modalities. It introduces multi-modal and cross-modal autoencoders to facilitate the reconstruction of representations from various modalities.

The overall objective function for the approach is formulated to minimize reconstruction losses while ensuring orthogonality of hash codes, preventing overfitting through regularization. The paper provides detailed illustrations of these frameworks, showcasing their architectures and learning processes.
