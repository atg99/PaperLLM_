This paper discusses various methodologies for multi-modal hashing, emphasizing the need for efficient training and scalability in handling large datasets. Key approaches include:

1. **Semantic Correlation Maximization Hashing**: This method avoids the explicit computation of pairwise similarity by utilizing a sequential hashing learning technique with a closed-form solution for each bit.

2. **Collective Matrix Factorization Hashing**: This approach learns cross-modality hash functions by jointly decomposing feature matrices from different modalities while maintaining a shared constraint.

3. **Quantized Correlation Hashing**: It integrates hash function learning with quantization, transforming multi-modal objectives into single-modal formulations.

4. **Semantics Preserving Hashing**: This technique maps an affinity matrix into a probability distribution and minimizes the Kullback-Leibler divergence between distributions derived from pairwise Hamming distances.

The paper also distinguishes between unsupervised and supervised hashing models. Unsupervised models extend spectral hashing to cross-modal scenarios, while supervised models utilize pairwise similarity information to align hash codes across different modalities.

Finally, it highlights the integration of deep neural networks into hashing frameworks, specifically mentioning deep cross-modal hashing, which employs convolutional neural networks for enhanced cross-modal similarity search. Overall, the paper presents a comprehensive overview of advancements in multi-modal hashing techniques.
