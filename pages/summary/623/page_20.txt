It emphasizes that reasoning enables effective communication and understanding, which are crucial for bridging the gap between current algorithms and human cognition. The authors argue that cross-modal intelligence reasoning, which involves integrating knowledge across different modalities, is essential for achieving human-like cognition in artificial intelligence.

The paper further explores how humans learn and retain skills over time, contrasting this with the limitations of existing machine learning algorithms that typically excel at single tasks but struggle with others. This lack of flexibility highlights the need for algorithms that can learn new tasks without losing the ability to perform previously acquired skills. The authors introduce the concept of commonsense learning, which involves understanding everyday scenarios, as a potential breakthrough for enhancing cross-modal intelligence.

Additionally, the paper touches on the idea of collective intelligence, which refers to the collaborative decision-making process that emerges from the contributions of independent individuals. The authors illustrate this concept with examples from nature, such as ant colonies, and human society, like Wikipedia, where collective input leads to more informed decisions. They propose that leveraging collective intelligence in conjunction with cross-modal information could enhance planning and decision-making processes in artificial intelligence systems. Overall, the paper advocates for a deeper integration of reasoning, commonsense knowledge, and collective intelligence to advance the field of artificial intelligence.
