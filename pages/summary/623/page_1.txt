The paper provides a comprehensive overview of multi-modal analysis in multimedia, addressing the challenges posed by the vast amounts of heterogeneous data generated by users and service providers. The authors, Wenwu Zhu, Xin Wang, and Hongzhi Li, focus on two main scientific research problems: data-driven correlational representation and knowledge-guided fusion.

To tackle these problems, the authors explore two key aspects: the multi-modal correlational representation, which involves the fusion of data from different modalities, and the fusion of multi-modal data with domain knowledge. They identify three important categories for data-driven correlational representation: multi-modal deep representation, multi-modal transfer learning, and multi-modal hashing. For knowledge-guided fusion, they discuss methods for integrating knowledge with data and present four applications that utilize different types of domain knowledge, including multi-modal visual question answering, video summarization, visual pattern mining, and recommendations.

The introduction highlights the current era of Cyber, Physical, and Human spaces, emphasizing the exponential growth of data due to advancements in technology. The authors point out that while traditional methods in multi-modal analysis have focused on feature and semantic fusion, the advent of deep neural networks has enabled more effective ways of fusing information across modalities at a hidden level, thus enhancing the analysis of multi-modal data.

In conclusion, the paper not only outlines existing methods and applications but also presents insights into future research directions, emphasizing the potential of multi-modal analysis to advance big data and artificial intelligence research.
