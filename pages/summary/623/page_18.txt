The framework demonstrates that latent space mapping is superior to traditional matrix-oriented approaches. The optimization problem focuses on minimizing the differences between user representations while ensuring a compact and sparse distribution of user attributes, constrained by L1-norm penalties and matrix size restrictions.

Several studies have contributed to addressing the cold-start problem in recommendation systems by aggregating user profiles across platforms, utilizing overlapping users and items as bridges. The XPTrans model introduces a cross-modal user behavior similarity constraint to optimize the association of representations between different media. Various methods leverage unique characteristics of different platforms to enhance collaborative applications, such as behavior prediction across platforms without relying on overlapping users, and integrating real-time social data to improve video recommendations.

There are two primary approaches in the existing literature: one group focuses on unified frameworks using matrix factorization and probabilistic models, while another adopts a two-step procedure for representation association. Despite the consensus on aligning cross-modal information, some works highlight the presence of data inconsistency, proposing methods to select consistent auxiliary data for improved cross-modal collaborative filtering. Recent studies emphasize the importance of adaptive weighting of heterogeneous data based on user-specific metrics and analyze inconsistent user behavior across different platforms to refine recommendation strategies.
