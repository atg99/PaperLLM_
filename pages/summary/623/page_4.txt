The paper discusses multi-modal fusion strategies, particularly distinguishing between feature-fusion and semantic-fusion approaches. Feature-fusion is effective for modalities with correlated features, while semantic-fusion is better suited for uncorrelated modalities. The rise of deep learning introduces intermediate-fusion, which leverages various layers of deep neural networks for flexible data fusion.

The paper categorizes multi-modal methods as discriminative, generative, or hybrid. Discriminative models learn conditional distributions based on features, while generative models focus on joint distributions. Hybrid models combine elements of both. The authors emphasize their focus on multimedia-related multi-modal scenarios, contrasting with a referenced survey on multi-modal deep learning.

The discussion also highlights two works on domain adaptation that facilitate the integration of deep representations from different modalities. The classic work by Yosinski et al. indicates that deeper layers of neural networks are more task-specific, while shallow layers capture general features. Following this principle, Tzeng et al. proposed the DCC method for adapting deeper layers but faced limitations, such as only adjusting a single layer and using a single-kernel MMD.

To address these issues, Long et al. introduced the Deep Adaptation Network (DAN), which adapts multiple layers simultaneously using a multi-kernel MMD. However, DAN matches marginal distributions rather than joint distributions, which can limit its effectiveness. The Joint Adaptation Network (JAN) was proposed to match joint distributions, enhancing performance.

The paper notes that both DAN and JAN allow for bidirectional learning between modalities, categorizing them under multi-modal deep representation rather than multi-modal transfer learning. Additionally, the authors touch on the challenges faced by existing models in adapting to new scenarios, highlighting the need for robust multi-modal transfer learning methods.
