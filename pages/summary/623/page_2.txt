The paper discusses the paradox between scalability and explainability in deep learning methods, emphasizing the importance of domain knowledge in enhancing decision-making accuracy.

The article aims to explore two main areas: the multi-modal fusion of data from various modalities and the integration of domain knowledge into this process. It identifies three significant categories of data-driven approaches—Multi-modal Deep Representation, Multi-modal Transfer Learning, and Multi-modal Hashing—focused on developing effective hidden representations that correlate information across modalities.

Moreover, the paper discusses the lack of systematic methods for incorporating domain knowledge into cross-modal learning and identifies three families of methodologies that may benefit from further investigation: Bayesian Inference, Teacher-student Networks, and Reinforcement Learning. The authors also highlight the ongoing attempts in the field to leverage domain knowledge, presenting four applications—Multi-modal Visual Question Answering, Multi-modal Video Summarization, Multi-modal Visual Pattern Mining, and Multi-modal Recommendation—to illustrate the potential of knowledge-guided multi-modal data fusion.

In conclusion, the paper reviews state-of-the-art approaches in multi-modal analysis, outlining key problems and promising research directions such as cross-modal reasoning, cognition, and collective intelligence, urging researchers to focus on these areas for future advancements in the field.
