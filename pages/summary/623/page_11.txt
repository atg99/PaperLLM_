The paper discusses advancements in Visual Question Answering (VQA) through multi-modal fusion and the incorporation of domain knowledge. It highlights the shift from traditional models that rely solely on visual and textual features to more sophisticated compositional models. These models leverage instance-specific networks built on the compositional structures of questions, enabling effective multi-modal information fusion. 

Graph-structured representations are also explored, where relationships between objects and language are modeled as graphs. Techniques such as graph convolutional networks (GCN) are employed to extract visual representations from spatial graphs, with nodes representing object detections and edges defined by an attention-based component.

Additionally, the paper emphasizes the importance of integrating external domain knowledge, as some visual questions may require more information than what is available in training datasets. It mentions pioneering works that utilize structural knowledge bases to enhance reasoning on visual concepts and introduces a Knowledge-incorporated Dynamic Memory Network (KDMN) framework, which embeds extensive domain knowledge into a semantic space for improved VQA performance.
