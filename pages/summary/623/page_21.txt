The paper presents a thorough examination of multi-modal analysis and its significance in advancing cross-media intelligence, particularly in assisting individuals with visual impairments. It identifies two key scientific challenges in multi-modal analysis for multimedia and explores methods for multi-modal fusion through both data-driven correlational representation and knowledge-guided approaches. The discussion encompasses multi-task and multi-view learning, deep representation, transfer learning, and hashing techniques, emphasizing their roles in enhancing multi-modal data integration.

The authors propose potential methodologies for effective multi-modal fusion, which can be applied to various domains such as visual question answering, video summarization, visual pattern mining, and recommendation systems. These applications showcase the necessity for diverse domain knowledge to enrich the fusion process.

Furthermore, the paper highlights promising future research directions, including cross-modal reasoning, cognition, and collective intelligence, suggesting that these areas could drive significant advancements in cross-media intelligence.

In conclusion, the authors acknowledge contributions from colleagues and stress the ongoing potential for improvement in methodologies and applications within the field of cross-media intelligence.
