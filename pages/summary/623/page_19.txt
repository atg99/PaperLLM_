Current models primarily address data fusion and correlation but lack the ability to integrate knowledge and reasoning effectively across different media. 

The paper introduces the cross-domain collaborative learning (CDCL) algorithm, which facilitates the learning of shared features across different domains, addressing issues of data inconsistency caused by media-specific disparities. A disparity-preserved deep cross-platform association model is proposed to manage these disparities by using a partially-connected multi-modal autoencoder. This model preserves both media-specific and shared latent representations, allowing for better handling of the varying granularity of data across platforms like Twitter and YouTube.

The model's architecture includes a hidden layer divided into media-shared and media-specific parts, with nonlinear mapping functions to associate cross-modal information. The loss function incorporates reconstruction error, regularization penalties, and sparsity constraints to optimize the learning process.

In summary, while significant progress has been made in multi-modal analysis and cross-modal representation, the paper emphasizes the need for continued research in cross-modal reasoning to achieve a more human-like intelligence in multimedia applications. Future directions should explore integrating knowledge from diverse domains to enhance decision-making capabilities in multimedia contexts.
