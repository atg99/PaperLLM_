The paper discusses advancements in multi-modal video summarization techniques, particularly for online videos enriched with various forms of side information such as tags, titles, and descriptions. These elements provide critical insights into the video content, enhancing the effectiveness of summarization algorithms. 

Wang et al. propose an event-driven video summarization method that integrates tag localization and key-shot mining. Their approach calculates the relevance of video shots to specific tags using a conditional probability model. The relevance score for each shot concerning an event query is derived by assessing the similarity between the query and tags, ultimately identifying key shots based on their relevance scores and the recurrence of key sub-events.

Song et al. focus on the importance of video titles, which often encapsulate the main topic of the video. They utilize video titles to retrieve related images through search engines and apply co-archetypal analysis to learn canonical visual concepts that link video frames and retrieved images. This method is framed as an optimization problem, aiming to approximate video frames and images through latent variables while satisfying geometrical constraints. The significance of each video frame is calculated based on the learned canonical concepts.

Overall, these methods highlight the potential of leveraging domain knowledge from various sources to improve video summarization outcomes in the context of the rapidly growing online video landscape.
