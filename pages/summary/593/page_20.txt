The paper discusses various strategies for training networks in the context of domain adaptation. It highlights two main approaches: staged training and joint training. In staged training, methods such as those by Tzeng et al. and Bousmalis et al. involve training components sequentially, while joint training employs adversarial techniques, alternating between training discriminators and other network parts. Notable examples include Saito et al.'s method of using pseudo-labels and Zou et al.'s self-training approach.

The paper also addresses multi-level adaptation techniques, exemplified by GraspGAN and CyCADA, which perform both pixel-level and feature-level adaptations to enhance accuracy. Tsai et al. and Zhang et al. further explore domain-invariant feature learning at multiple levels, while Chen et al. integrate consistency regularization in object detection tasks.

In terms of network architectures, the majority of methods utilize convolutional neural networks (CNNs), with some exploring attention networks and Bayesian networks. For time-series data, recurrent neural networks (RNNs) or long short-term memory networks (LSTMs) are preferred, whereas for text classification, both LSTMs and CNNs have been shown to be effective.

Finally, the paper notes that conventional hyperparameter tuning methods from supervised learning do not translate well to unsupervised domain adaptation, indicating a gap that requires further exploration.
