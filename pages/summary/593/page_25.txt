The paper presents a survey on unsupervised deep domain adaptation, focusing on various computer vision datasets utilized in this field. Key datasets discussed include:

- **MNIST**: A binary handwritten digit dataset consisting of digits 0-9, derived from NIST databases, resized to fit in a 20x20 box and centered in a 28x28 pixel image.
- **MNIST-M**: A variant of MNIST where digits are mixed with random patches from the BSDS500 dataset's color photos.
- **USPS**: A dataset of handwritten zip codes from the U.S. Postal Service, normalized to 16x16 pixels.
- **SVHN**: Contains single digits from urban house numbers in Google StreetView, resized to 32x32 pixels.
- **SYNN**: A synthetic digit dataset created using Microsoft Windows fonts, featuring various digit configurations.
- **SYNS**: A synthetic sign dataset modified from Wikipedia pictograms of traffic signs, containing 100,000 images across 43 classes.
- **GTSRB**: The German Traffic Sign Recognition Benchmark, created from videos taken in Germany, consisting of about 50,000 images and 43 classes of signs.
- **Office**: A dataset with 31 classes of objects across three domains: Amazon, DSLR, and Webcam, each with different image characteristics.

The paper also references the theoretical framework provided by Ben-David et al., which establishes bounds on target error based on source error and domain divergence. It emphasizes the focus on scenarios with one source and one target domain, aligning with common practices in the surveyed methods.
