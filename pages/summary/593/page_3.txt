The paper reviews unsupervised deep domain adaptation, focusing on its application in transfer learning where the source and target datasets are related but differ in appearance. It categorizes transfer learning methods into "domain" and "task," where a domain consists of a feature space and a marginal probability distribution, while a task involves a label space and a predictive function. The change in domain can occur through alterations in the feature space or the probability distribution, exemplified by language shifts in text classification or variations in document topics.

The authors classify transfer learning into three types: inductive, transductive, and unsupervised. Inductive transfer learning involves different source and target tasks with some labeled target data; transductive transfer learning has the same tasks but different domains, requiring both labeled source data and unlabeled target data; and unsupervised transfer learning, like inductive, differs in tasks but lacks labeled data in both domains.

The focus of the survey is on domain adaptation, a subtype of transductive transfer learning where the target task remains the same as the source but the domains differ. It distinguishes between homogeneous domain adaptation, where the feature spaces are the same, and heterogeneous domain adaptation, where they differ. The paper clarifies the terminology surrounding domain adaptation, emphasizing that it can involve various combinations of labeled and unlabeled data across both domains. The authors will refer to "unsupervised" domain adaptation when labeled source data and unlabeled target data are present and "semi-supervised" domain adaptation when both labeled and unlabeled data are used.
