The paper presents a comprehensive survey of unsupervised deep domain adaptation, focusing on various methods and their corresponding loss functions. It categorizes approaches based on their mechanisms for adapting models to new domains without labeled data. Key methods discussed include:

1. **Metric Learning Modifications**: Techniques like M-ADDA modify existing methods to maximize the margin between clusters of data embeddings. SimNet classifies embeddings based on proximity to source images, while CCN++ employs a pairwise similarity network to differentiate classes.

2. **Promoting Differences**: Several methods enhance diversity among networks by training them to learn different features. Approaches include adding norms to classifier weights, employing soft subspace orthogonality constraints, and pushing minibatch means apart.

3. **Cycle Consistency/Reconstruction**: This technique, used in models like CycleGAN, ensures that after translating images from one domain to another, they can be reconstructed back to their original form. Variants include L1 loss with transformation functions and feature-level cycle-consistency losses.

4. **Semantic Consistency**: This loss function maintains class assignments during adaptation, ensuring that the model preserves relevant semantic information across domains.

Overall, the paper highlights the evolution of unsupervised domain adaptation methods, emphasizing their innovative loss functions and the importance of maintaining feature diversity and semantic integrity during the adaptation process.
