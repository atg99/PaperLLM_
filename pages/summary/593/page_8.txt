This paper discusses various domain adaptation methods aimed at learning domain-invariant features. The authors outline how these methods differ in their alignment components during training and the extent to which feature extractors share weights across domains. The use of Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) is highlighted, where it is proven that the population MMD is zero if the two distributions are equal. The authors mention several adaptations of MMD, including the multiple kernel variant (MK-MMD) and the joint MMD (JMMD), as well as the use of adversarial objectives for better performance.

Additionally, the paper details Correlation Alignment (CORAL), which computes the distance between second-order statistics of source and target features. Various distances, such as squared matrix Frobenius norm and Euclidean distance, are employed in different adaptations of CORAL.

Contrastive Domain Discrepancy (CCD) is introduced as a method that incorporates label information by minimizing intra-class discrepancy and maximizing inter-class margin. The authors reference the Contrastive Adaptation Networks (CAN) that leverage clustering for estimating target labels, showing improved performance on the Office dataset.

Finally, the paper discusses optimal transport theory, specifically the Wasserstein distance, as a means to measure distribution distances and align features and labels. The authors present Joint Distribution Optimal Transport (JDOT) and its neural network adaptation, Deep JDOT, as effective strategies for domain adaptation.
