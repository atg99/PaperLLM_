This paper discusses the role of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) in synthetic image generation and their application in domain adaptation. GANs consist of two neural networks: a generator and a discriminator, which engage in a minimax game. The generator produces synthetic images from a noise vector, aiming to create outputs indistinguishable from real images in the training dataset. The discriminator evaluates whether samples are real or generated, striving to maximize its accuracy in classification.

The paper outlines the mathematical formulation of this adversarial process, defining a value function that captures the objectives of both networks. The generator seeks to minimize the probability that the discriminator correctly identifies its outputs as fake, while the discriminator aims to maximize this probability. This interplay continues until the generator produces images that the discriminator cannot reliably distinguish from real samples.

The application of GANs is illustrated using examples from popular datasets, such as CelebA for human faces, demonstrating the ability to generate realistic synthetic images. The research highlights the potential of GANs beyond image generation, emphasizing their emerging uses in the field of domain adaptation.
