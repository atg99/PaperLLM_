The paper discusses the mechanics of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), focusing on the roles of the generator (G) and discriminator (D) in the training process. It illustrates how G aims to create realistic images that D misclassifies as real, while D strives to accurately distinguish between real and generated images. Several challenges in GAN training are identified, including convergence difficulties, mode collapse, and vanishing gradients. Various techniques have been proposed to address these issues, encompassing architectural choices, objective modifications, and connections to reinforcement learning.

The evaluation of GANs poses additional challenges as they are often assessed through subjective visual inspections. While past generative models used log-likelihood for evaluation, this method is not feasible for GANs. Alternative methods have been developed to assess GAN performance, including techniques for detecting memorization, measuring diversity, and approximating log-likelihood. These evaluation methods can also be applied to domain adaptation tasks involving image translation. Overall, the paper emphasizes the complexity of training and evaluating GANs while noting the ongoing research aimed at overcoming these challenges.
