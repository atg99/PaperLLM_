The paper presents a survey on unsupervised deep domain adaptation strategies, focusing on various approaches for tasks such as segmentation, object detection, and person re-identification. It highlights several methods that enhance adaptation effectiveness. For segmentation, techniques include a distillation loss for location-aware alignment and class-aware constrained multiple instance loss. In object detection, dual domain classifiers are employed to ensure consistency across representations. The authors also discuss masking losses to penalize only foreground pixel differences when adapting from synthetic images.

A key strategy mentioned is low-confidence or low-relevance rejection, which improves performance by excluding data points with insufficient confidence from training. Various methods for confidence measurement are referenced, including ensemble label agreement and prediction probabilities from object detectors. 

The paper also addresses weight sharing among domains, noting that many methods completely share weights between feature extractors. However, some techniques selectively share weights, particularly in lower layers, to better adapt higher layers to the target domain. The concept of domain separation networks is introduced, which learns distinct source-specific, target-specific, and shared features, optimizing the adaptation process. Overall, the survey encapsulates the advancements and methodologies in unsupervised deep domain adaptation, emphasizing the balance between shared and specific feature representations.
