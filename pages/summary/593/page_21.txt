The paper discusses methodologies for hyperparameter tuning in unsupervised deep domain adaptation, highlighting the challenges posed by the absence of labeled data in target domains. It emphasizes the importance of selecting hyperparameters that maximize validation accuracy, typically through techniques like cross-validation. However, in unsupervised settings, the use of labeled target data for tuning can create an unrealistic performance benchmark, as real-world scenarios often involve completely unlabeled data.

One alternative approach presented is reverse validation, which leverages both labeled source data and unlabeled target data to estimate the risk of hyperparameters without using labels from the target domain. This method involves training a forward classifier on the source data, pseudo-labeling the target data, and then training a reverse classifier to evaluate performance metrics. The paper notes that initializing the reverse classifier with the forward classifier's weights and applying early stopping improves results.

Additionally, the authors mention that tuning parameters based on domain similarity can enhance performance, citing an example where augmenting MNIST data to match the characteristics of the SVHN dataset led to significant improvement.

The results section summarizes the performance of various methods across different datasets, noting that while care must be taken when comparing results across studies due to different conditions, several methods demonstrate promising outcomes. Notably, the CAN method achieved the highest performance on the Office dataset, while WDGRL excelled on the Amazon review dataset. Other methods, such as SBADA-GAN and AutoDIAL, also showed competitive results in specific tasks, with a self-ensembling approach outperforming others in certain scenarios. Overall, the paper illustrates the diversity of approaches in unsupervised domain adaptation and their varying effectiveness across tasks.
