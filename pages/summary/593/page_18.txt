The paper discusses various loss functions in domain adaptation methods, particularly focusing on cycle-consistency and semantic-consistency losses. Cycle-consistency loss minimizes the difference between the original source image and its reconstructed version after transformation, while semantic-consistency loss ensures that class assignments remain consistent between the input and mapped images. 

Task loss is essential for guiding the network to achieve the desired outcomes, such as correct classifications or semantic segmentations, typically implemented through cross-entropy loss or negative log likelihood when using a softmax output layer. However, some methods, such as SimNet and others, do not utilize traditional task losses.

Adversarial methods are highlighted for their use of a discriminator to learn domain-invariant features, generating realistic images and promoting discriminative features across domains. This adversarial loss is distinct as it is learned during training rather than predefined, utilizing gradients to adjust the feature extractor or generator.

Additionally, specific problems may lead to the development of specialized losses, such as those aimed at sharpening segmentation boundaries in semantic segmentation tasks. Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of various loss functions in enhancing the performance of domain adaptation techniques.
