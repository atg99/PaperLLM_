The paper presents a novel approach to semantic segmentation that aims to enhance inference speed and maintain competitive performance compared to existing methods. The proposed architecture, which integrates a Short-Term Dense Concatenate (STDC) module into a U-net structure, allows for scalable receptive fields and multi-scale feature representation without the need for additional time-consuming paths. This is accomplished by concatenating response maps from multiple continuous layers, which encode the input image at different scales.

The Detail Guidance module is introduced to efficiently learn spatial details from low-level layers during the decoding phase, utilizing a Detail Aggregation module to generate detail ground-truth. The model employs binary cross-entropy and dice loss for optimizing detail learning, with the side-information not needed during inference. The results demonstrate that the STDC networks achieve state-of-the-art performance on benchmarks like ImageNet, Cityscapes, and CamVid, with STDC1-Seg50 reaching 71.9% mIoU at 250.4 FPS, and STDC2-Seg75 achieving 76.8% mIoU at 97.0 FPS.

The related work section discusses efficient network designs and traditional segmentation algorithms, noting that while many existing models, such as SqueezeNet and MobileNet, focus on reducing parameters and computational cost, they require careful tuning for semantic segmentation tasks. The paper emphasizes the necessity for efficient architectures that effectively balance speed and accuracy in real-time applications.
