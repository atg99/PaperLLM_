The paper presents the Short-Term Dense Concatenate (STDC) network architecture aimed at enhancing segmentation methods through efficient design. The STDC network comprises various ConvX operations, which combine convolution, batch normalization, and ReLU activation. It introduces a lightweight backbone that allows for scalable receptive fields while utilizing a single-path decoder for detail guidance in low-level information processing.

The authors identify two mainstream approaches for efficient segmentation: lightweight backbones and multi-branch architectures. They critique existing models, such as BiSeNetV1, for their structural redundancy due to the use of classification backbones and two-stream architectures. 

The key component of the proposed network is the STDC module, which consists of multiple blocks, each with defined kernel sizes and receptive fields. The architecture is designed to efficiently concatenate feature maps from different blocks, thereby enriching feature information while maintaining computational efficiency. The STDC module's output is generated through a fusion operation that effectively combines multi-scale information. 

The advantages of the STDC module include a carefully tuned filter size that reduces computational complexity and the effective aggregation of features from various blocks, enhancing performance in semantic segmentation tasks. Overall, the proposed STDC network aims to strike a balance between speed and accuracy in segmentation applications.
