The paper presents the Short-Term Dense Concatenate network (STDC network), a novel architecture aimed at improving real-time semantic segmentation. While the BiSeNet framework has been effective, it suffers from inefficiencies due to its reliance on additional paths for spatial information encoding and the use of pretrained backbones that are not optimally designed for segmentation tasks. The STDC network addresses these issues by eliminating structural redundancy and gradually reducing the dimensions of feature maps, which enhances image representation.

In the decoder, a Detail Aggregation module integrates spatial information learning into low-level layers in a single-stream manner, enabling effective fusion of low-level and deep features to produce final segmentation results. Extensive experiments on the Cityscapes and CamVid datasets show that the STDC network achieves a significant balance between segmentation accuracy and inference speed. Specifically, it reaches a mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) of 71.9% on the Cityscapes test set at a speed of 250.4 frames per second (FPS), which is 45.2% faster than the latest methods. Additionally, at higher resolutions, it achieves 76.8% mIoU with 97.0 FPS. The findings underscore the STDC network's capabilities as a state-of-the-art solution for real-time semantic segmentation tasks, addressing the growing demands in various applications such as autonomous driving and video surveillance.
