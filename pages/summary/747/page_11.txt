The paper presents the Batch Actor-Critic Algorithm, which involves training Actor and Critic models using sequences of input and corresponding ground-truth outputs. The process starts by initializing both the Actor model (Seq2seq) and the Critic model (ValueNet). The training involves iteratively selecting batches of data, sampling action sequences from the Actor model, calculating true discounted rewards, and storing state-reward pairs for the Critic.

The Critic is trained on these pairs to minimize the loss function, while the Actor's parameters are updated based on the value estimates provided by the Critic. The algorithm emphasizes the balance between bias and variance by adjusting a parameter that influences this trade-off.

Additionally, the paper discusses an alternative approach, combining Actor-Critic methods with Q-learning. This involves maximizing the advantage function by focusing on estimating the Q-function, allowing the removal of actions from the maximization objective. Q-learning is characterized as an off-policy algorithm that learns Q-values, while the SARSA algorithm represents an on-policy approach. The paper highlights that Q-learning provides good approximations and faster training by utilizing self-estimated values rather than relying solely on ground-truth data.

The training process for the Q-function estimation involves collecting experience data during Actor training and using it to train the Q-network. However, challenges arise when using neural networks for function approximation, particularly regarding convergence guarantees. To address these issues, the paper suggests using an experience buffer to store diverse experiences and randomly sample from it for training, reducing correlation and improving efficiency. A prioritized version of the experience buffer is also mentioned, which enhances the selection process for training data.
