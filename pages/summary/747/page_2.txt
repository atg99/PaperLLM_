The decoder generates output sequences based on the current state and ground-truth outputs, utilizing context vectors to enhance generation. The model employs an RNN with parameters optimized via the teacher forcing algorithm, minimizing cross-entropy loss during training.

The training process involves encoding input sequences to obtain the last encoder state, which is then used to initialize the decoder. The output generation relies on the model's previous predictions during testing, leading to a potential mismatch due to exposure bias. This bias arises because the decoder is trained with ground-truth inputs but must rely on its own predictions during inference.

To mitigate exposure bias, the paper suggests using scheduled sampling, where the model gradually transitions from ground-truth inputs to its own predictions during training. This approach aims to create a more robust model capable of handling discrepancies between training and testing conditions. However, the random nature of scheduled sampling can lead to inconsistencies in the training process.

Overall, the paper highlights the challenges in training seq2seq models and proposes methods to improve performance and reliability during output generation.
