The paper discusses reinforcement learning methods, specifically in the context of sequence-to-sequence (seq2seq) models used for tasks like text summarization. It begins by introducing a dataset of read speech data from 630 speakers, which includes transcriptions in various dialects of American English. 

The main focus is on maximizing rewards in reinforcement learning, which can be approached through different mathematical formulations. The paper outlines various strategies, including maximizing expected rewards, discounted rewards, advantage functions, and Q-functions. Each method has distinct strengths and weaknesses, affecting the training procedures of seq2seq models.

A key algorithm discussed is the Policy Gradient (PG) method, which seeks to maximize expected rewards based on a defined policy. It employs a recurrent neural network (RNN) to generate outputs based on input sequences and evaluates performance through a reward system, often using Teacher Forcing during training. The loss function is defined as the negative expected reward of the generated sequence, with gradients calculated to optimize the model.

The paper also introduces the REINFORCE algorithm, a specific PG approach that addresses high variance in reward estimation by incorporating a baseline reward. This baseline helps stabilize the training process by reducing variance without biasing the gradient estimates. The relationship between the generated output and the ground-truth is analyzed, highlighting the importance of the baseline in improving model performance.

Overall, the paper provides a comprehensive overview of reinforcement learning techniques, their applications in seq2seq models, and the mathematical foundations that guide their implementation.
