The paper discusses the Actor-Critic (AC) model in reinforcement learning, highlighting its structure and training methodology. The AC model consists of two components: the Actor, which represents the policy, and the Critic, which estimates the value function. The Actor uses samples to compute advantage approximations and update its loss function based on the Critic's feedback. Training the Critic involves estimating expected rewards through regression, typically using a neural network and mean square error for optimization.

The paper outlines two training approaches: on-policy and off-policy. On-policy AC collects samples in real-time, leading to high variance due to correlation among samples. To mitigate this, off-policy techniques utilize a memory buffer to decouple sample correlations, enhancing training efficiency. The batch Actor-Critic algorithm is presented as a method for training the Critic with multiple state-reward pairs.

Variations of the AC approach, such as Synchronous and Asynchronous Advantage Actor-Critic (A3C), are discussed. A3C improves training by allowing multiple threads to update the model concurrently. The paper also mentions alternative off-policy methods like Retrace, UNREAL, and ACER, which blend on-policy and off-policy training strategies.

The authors propose a combined approach utilizing both Policy Gradient (PG) and AC methods to achieve low variance and unbiased estimates. They introduce Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE) as a solution to balance bias and variance, enabling improved performance in AC models. Overall, the paper emphasizes the advantages of AC models in reducing variance while acknowledging the potential biases introduced by an inaccurate Critic.
