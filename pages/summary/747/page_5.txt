The paper discusses sequence-to-sequence (seq2seq) models, which are designed to process information in the form of sequences such as words, images, or speech units. It highlights the evolution of these models, particularly the introduction of attention-based mechanisms that enhance performance across various applications including machine translation, text summarization, and speech recognition. Attention-based models improve output by integrating information from the encoder's output at each decoding step.

Despite their advantages, seq2seq models face challenges, especially in applications with large output spaces. More advanced models like pointer-generator and transformers are mentioned but not explored in detail. The paper also identifies related problems where the output is not a sequence, such as sentiment analysis, natural language inference, and pronoun resolution, emphasizing that these still qualify as seq2seq problems.

Evaluation of seq2seq models is typically conducted using cross-entropy loss during training, but various discrete measures are employed for performance assessment, tailored to specific applications. Common evaluation metrics include ROUGE, BLEU, and METEOR for text-based tasks, and CIDEr and SPICE for image captioning. Additionally, Word Error Rate (WER) is noted for speech recognition tasks.

The paper concludes with a brief overview of commonly used datasets in seq2seq models, underscoring the diversity and applicability of these models across different domains.
