The paper discusses the application of mixed training loss in various tasks such as text summarization, paraphrase generation, and dialogue generation. It highlights the limitations of policy gradient (PG) models, particularly the challenge of accumulating errors over long action sequences, which can impede convergence due to small rewards. To address this, Actor-Critic (AC) models have been proposed, where the Critic observes rewards at each decoding step, thereby stabilizing learning.

The paper references early uses of AC models in machine translation and describes techniques like temporal-difference learning for advantage function estimation. The authors emphasize the importance of a value network to enhance performance, which requires a pre-trained sequence-to-sequence (seq2seq) model. The integration of a value network during inference is compared to the AlphaGo model, combining outputs from both the seq2seq and the value network.

In addition, the paper mentions recent innovations, such as a model by Li et al. that incorporates reinforcement learning (RL) to control output length, and another by Li et al. that employs a binary classifier as the Critic to distinguish between generated and human-written summaries. The use of a mixed reward function combining various metrics is noted to improve performance in image captioning tasks.

The paper concludes by addressing current challenges in RL-based models for seq2seq tasks, such as sample efficiency and high variance. It discusses alternative approaches like RAML and SPG, which attempt to bridge the gap between maximum likelihood estimation and RL training, while also acknowledging their limitations. Overall, the paper indicates a preference for REINFORCE-based models like MIXER in addressing these seq2seq challenges.
