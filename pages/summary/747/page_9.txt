The paper discusses an attention-based pointer-generation sequence-to-sequence (seq2seq) model enhanced with a Self-Critic (SC) reward mechanism for training. The model calculates a context vector at each decoding step, combining it with the decoder output to derive an action distribution. The pointer-generation model utilizes attention distribution with action distribution via pointers to finalize actions. The training loss is refined by averaging over multiple sampled sequences, addressing the high variance often encountered in reinforcement learning.

To mitigate issues with delayed reward observation in seq2seq models, where rewards are only realized after an entire sequence is generated, the SC model employs the greedy output as a baseline for reward calculation. This approach adjusts the REINFORCE objective to enhance learning efficiency. However, challenges remain, particularly in the early training phases due to random action selection.

To improve convergence, the authors suggest pre-training the model with cross-entropy loss before transitioning to REINFORCE loss. Additionally, importance sampling is proposed as a method to further reduce variance by using sequences from an older model for loss calculation.

The paper also introduces the Actor-Critic (AC) model, which aims to create an estimator for baseline rewards. This model seeks to maximize the advantage function, leveraging neural networks as function approximators for value functions. The AC framework allows for a more refined approach to estimating expected rewards and enhances the overall learning process.
