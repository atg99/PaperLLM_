The paper provides a comprehensive overview of various applications of sequence-to-sequence (seq2seq) models that utilize reinforcement learning (RL) methods. It categorizes these applications based on the specific RL algorithms employed, including policy gradient models, self-critic models, and actor-critic models. Each application is evaluated for issues such as exposure bias, the mismatch between training and testing measures, and the observation of full rewards.

Key findings indicate that while REINFORCE-based models yield better results and are straightforward to implement, they often require extensive training time, with only marginal improvements over baseline models. This leads to their frequent use primarily for fine-tuning.

The paper also highlights significant challenges in integrating RL into seq2seq models, particularly in natural language processing (NLP) tasks where the action space is substantially larger than in simpler environments like gaming. For instance, tasks such as machine translation and image captioning face issues of reward sparsity due to the vast output vocabulary. Furthermore, reliance on standard metrics like BLEU and ROUGE for feedback can lead to misalignment between model performance and evaluation, especially in tasks like abstractive summarization.

The authors suggest that improving reward functions through inverse reinforcement learning (IRL) could address some of these challenges, although this area remains underexplored. They also reference recent advancements in RL for gaming, such as Rainbow and Quantile Nets, proposing that similar ensemble approaches could potentially enhance seq2seq tasks, which is a direction for future research.
