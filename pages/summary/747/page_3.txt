The paper addresses two significant challenges in sequence-to-sequence (seq2seq) models: exposure bias and the mismatch between training and evaluation objectives. 

Exposure bias arises when models trained with teacher forcing (using ground-truth data) are evaluated with their own outputs, leading to inconsistencies. Goyal et al. proposed a solution by introducing a differentiable approximation of the greedy search, allowing for back-propagation through the argmax operation. Additionally, adversarial generative models have been explored, where a generator creates data resembling ground-truth while a discriminator assesses its authenticity. This approach mitigates exposure bias by reducing reliance on ground-truth data, though it faces challenges like reward sparsity and mode collapse.

The second issue pertains to the evaluation measures, such as BLEU and ROUGE, which are non-differentiable and often differ from the loss function used during training. This discrepancy can result in inconsistent model performance. To align training with evaluation, reinforcement learning (RL) techniques have been suggested. The paper describes RL as a Markov Decision Process (MDP) where an agent interacts with an environment, optimizing a policy based on cumulative rewards. The Q-function and value function are defined to evaluate actions and states, respectively. The advantage function captures the importance of actions relative to the state value, allowing the seq2seq model to serve as the policy for action generation.

In summary, the paper emphasizes the integration of RL techniques to address exposure bias and evaluation mismatches in seq2seq models, setting the foundation for a combined learning approach to improve model consistency and performance.
