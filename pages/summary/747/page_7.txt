This paper discusses various datasets designed for different natural language processing and computer vision tasks. It highlights key datasets for each task, emphasizing their significance and the volume of data they encompass.

1. **Question Answering and Generation**: The CNN-Daily Mail dataset is foundational for question answering. Recent datasets like SQuAD (over 100K question-answer pairs) and TriviaQA (650K triples of questions, answers, and evidence) have advanced this area.

2. **Dialogue Generation**: Datasets such as OpenSubtitles (conversations from over 20K movies) and the Cornell Movie Dialogues Corpus (220K dialogues) are utilized for generating conversational text.

3. **Semantic Parsing**: WikiSQL, which includes 80K hand-annotated questions and SQL queries, is noted for its scale, alongside other datasets like WikiTableQuestion.

4. **Sentiment Analysis**: The Amazon product review dataset (82 million reviews) and the Stanford Sentiment Treebank (215K phrases with sentiment labels) are significant in this field.

5. **Natural Language Inference**: The Stanford Natural Language Inference (SNLI) dataset (570K sentence pairs) and MultiNLI (433K pairs) are key resources for studying entailment relations.

6. **Semantic Role Labeling**: Proposition Bank provides a comprehensive corpus for semantic annotation across multiple languages.

7. **Relation Extraction**: Freebase offers a vast collection of entity pairs and relationships, derived from the New York Times corpus.

8. **Pronoun Resolution**: The OntoNotes 5.0 dataset is commonly used, particularly its Chinese portion for pronoun resolution tasks.

9. **Image Captioning**: The COCO dataset (330K images with captions) and SBU (1 million images with user-provided descriptions) cater to image captioning needs.

10. **Video Captioning**: MSR-VTT (10K videos with captions) and YouTube2Text/MSVD (1970 videos with numerous captions) are prominent datasets for video captioning.

11. **Image Classification**: The MNIST dataset, with 60K training examples, is widely recognized in computer vision for handwritten digit recognition.

12. **Speech Recognition**: The LibriSpeech ASR Corpus (1000 hours of speech) is a primary resource, complemented by other datasets like WSJ and TIMIT, though the latter two require licenses for access.

This paper provides a comprehensive overview of these datasets, illustrating their importance in advancing research and applications in their respective fields.
