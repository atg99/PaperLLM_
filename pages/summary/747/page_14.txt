The paper discusses various reinforcement learning (RL) models, highlighting their advantages and drawbacks in a structured manner. Each model builds upon the strengths of its predecessors while inheriting their weaknesses. Additionally, it outlines the complexities involved in implementing these models in real-world applications.

A table is presented detailing the policy, action, and reward functions for different sequence-to-sequence (seq2seq) tasks, such as text summarization, machine translation, and image captioning. Each task utilizes specific models and metrics, including ROUGE and BLEU scores for evaluating performance.

The paper further explores the transition from cross-entropy (CE) loss to REINFORCE loss in training these models. It introduces the MIXER algorithm, which employs a combination of previous training methods to enhance performance. The approach involves starting with CE loss to establish a good initial policy and gradually shifting to REINFORCE loss through an annealing schedule. This method has shown success in various applications, including text summarization and image captioning.

An alternative strategy for transitioning between loss functions is also proposed, using a mixed loss function that balances CE and REINFORCE losses based on a parameter that changes throughout the training process. This approach allows for a gradual shift in reliance from CE to REINFORCE loss, ultimately optimizing the model's performance.
