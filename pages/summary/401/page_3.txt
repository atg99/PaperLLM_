The paper presents a structured detection pipeline consisting of three main components: the backbone, the encoder, and the decoder. The backbone extracts features while the encoder processes these features for detection, and the decoder is responsible for classification and regression tasks, ultimately generating prediction boxes. The authors highlight the performance of YOLOF as a strong baseline for fast and accurate detectors.

A significant focus is on the cost analysis of MiMo encoders, which, despite their success in dense object detection, introduce complexity, increased memory usage, and reduced speed compared to SiSo encoders. The paper provides quantitative data showing that MiMo encoders result in significantly higher memory consumption (134G versus 6G) and slower processing speeds (13 FPS compared to 34 FPS).

To address the drawbacks of MiMo encoders, the authors explore replacing them with SiSo encoders. However, they note that this transition results in a substantial drop in performance due to two primary issues: limited scale range of the C5 feature's receptive field and an imbalance problem with positive anchors from sparse anchors in the single-level feature setting.

The paper discusses the challenge of detecting objects at varying scales and proposes that utilizing multiple-level features is a viable solution. The authors aim to find a method to generate output features with diverse receptive fields to compensate for the limitations of SiSo encoders, thereby enhancing detection performance across different object scales.
