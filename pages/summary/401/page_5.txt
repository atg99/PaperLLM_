The paper discusses the development of a new framework called YOLOF, which aims to improve the matching of positive anchors in object detection tasks. The analysis begins with a comparison of various matching methods, particularly highlighting the limitations of Max-IoU matching, which tends to favor large ground-truth boxes, leading to imbalances across object scales. The Adaptive Training Sample Selection (ATSS) method attempts to mitigate this imbalance by adaptively sampling positive anchors. In contrast, the proposed uniform matching approach in YOLOF ensures a balanced distribution of positive anchors across different object sizes without evolving during training.

YOLOF is structured into three main components: a backbone, an encoder, and a decoder. The backbone utilizes ResNet and ResNeXt models, outputting a feature map that is then processed by the encoder to create a multi-scale feature representation. The encoder incorporates residual blocks to enhance feature extraction. The decoder, modeled after RetinaNet, includes two task-specific heads for classification and regression, with modifications to improve performance.

To address the challenge of sparse pre-defined anchors, YOLOF introduces a random shift operation to enhance the matching quality between anchors and ground-truth boxes. This operation allows for a maximum shift of 32 pixels in any direction, increasing the likelihood of better anchor matches. The experiments conducted on the MS COCO benchmark demonstrate the effectiveness of YOLOF, with comparisons made against other detection frameworks like RetinaNet and DETR. The results indicate that YOLOF provides significant improvements in anchor matching and overall detection performance.
