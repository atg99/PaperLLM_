The paper "You Only Look One-level Feature" by Qiang Chen et al. revisits the concept of feature pyramids in object detection, particularly focusing on one-stage detectors. The authors argue that the success of Feature Pyramid Networks (FPN) stems more from their optimization strategy—specifically, the divide-and-conquer approach—rather than the commonly believed benefit of multi-scale feature fusion. 

To address the detection problem, the authors propose a new method called You Only Look One-level Feature (YOLOF), which simplifies the process by utilizing only one-level features for detection. This approach is enhanced by two key components: the Dilation Encoder and Uniform Matching, which significantly improve detection performance. 

Extensive experiments conducted on the COCO benchmark demonstrate that YOLOF achieves performance comparable to that of RetinaNet, a feature pyramids model, while being 2.5 times faster. Moreover, YOLOF matches the performance of DETR using a single-level feature approach, requiring only a fraction of the training epochs. 

The authors emphasize that their findings challenge the prevailing belief regarding the necessity of multi-scale feature fusion in FPN and highlight the potential for simpler, more efficient detection models. The paper concludes by providing access to the code for YOLOF, encouraging further exploration and application of the proposed methodology.
