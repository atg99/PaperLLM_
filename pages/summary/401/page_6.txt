The paper presents a detailed comparison of various object detection models, specifically focusing on YOLOF and RetinaNet, on the COCO2017 validation set. The results indicate that YOLOF, which incorporates a single-level feature approach, achieves competitive performance with reduced computational complexity compared to RetinaNet, demonstrating a 57% reduction in GFLOPs and a 2.5x speedup. While YOLOF performs slightly worse than RetinaNet on small object detection due to its large stride, it excels in detecting larger objects, showing improved performance through the use of dilated residual blocks in the encoder.

The implementation of YOLOF involved a synchronized SGD training strategy across multiple GPUs, with specific adjustments to learning rates and warmup iterations to stabilize training. The model comparison was conducted by aligning the configurations of RetinaNet and YOLOF, including the use of generalized IoU for box loss and group normalization in the heads.

Additionally, the paper explores YOLOF's compatibility with advanced techniques such as multi-scale training and testing, resulting in improved performance metrics. The final results achieved by YOLOF include a mean Average Precision (mAP) of 47.1 and a competitive score of 31.8 mAP for small objects, demonstrating its effectiveness relative to other state-of-the-art models, including DETR, which utilizes transformer architecture.

Overall, the findings suggest that YOLOF not only maintains high detection accuracy but also enhances efficiency, making it a promising option for future research in object detection.
