The paper presents a comparative analysis of object detection models, specifically focusing on DETR and YOLOF, using the COCO2017 validation set. Key findings include:

1. **Performance Metrics**: YOLOF models, both with ResNet-50 and ResNet-101 backbones, are evaluated against DETR models, showcasing competitive average precision (AP) scores. YOLOF demonstrates superior performance on small objects, while DETR outperforms on larger objects.

2. **Training and Efficiency**: YOLOF converges significantly faster than DETR, approximately seven times quicker, making it a preferable baseline for single-level detectors.

3. **Model Components**: The study highlights the importance of two specific components in YOLOF—Dilated Encoder and Uniform Matching. The Dilated Encoder enhances performance on large objects and addresses scale limitations, while Uniform Matching is crucial for improving results on small and medium objects.

4. **Ablation Studies**: Various ablation experiments reveal that stacking more residual blocks in the encoder improves detection of large objects. Additionally, the introduction of dilated convolutions in residual blocks enhances overall performance, although benefits plateau with excessively large dilations.

5. **Conclusion**: YOLOF's architecture, which relies on local convolution layers, can achieve results comparable to global layer approaches like DETR, while maintaining a simpler design and improved efficiency in detecting small objects.

Overall, the findings emphasize YOLOF's effectiveness and efficiency as a single-level feature detector, particularly in scenarios involving varying object sizes.
