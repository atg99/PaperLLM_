The paper discusses the relationship between object scales and the scale range covered by features in object detection models. It presents a toy example illustrating three scenarios: a feature with a limited scale range, a feature that covers larger objects but misses smaller ones, and a feature that encompasses all scales through multiple receptive fields. The authors propose a Dilated Encoder, which enhances the receptive field of the C5 feature by stacking standard and dilated convolutions, allowing it to cover various object scales effectively.

The structure of the Dilated Encoder includes a Projector and Residual Blocks, where the Projector reduces channel dimensions and refines semantic contexts, while the Residual Blocks utilize different dilation rates to generate features that cover all object scales. The paper contrasts its approach with existing models like TridentNet and DetNet, highlighting the unique aspect of generating a feature with multiple receptive fields from a single-level feature.

The study also addresses the issue of imbalance in positive anchors, which arises when using Max-IoU matching in anchor-based detectors. The authors propose a Uniform Matching strategy to ensure that all ground-truth boxes are matched with an equal number of positive anchors, thus resolving the imbalance problem. This method is designed to enhance detection performance for both large and small objects.
