The paper investigates the performance of different encoder architectures in object detection, particularly focusing on the MiMo (multi-input multi-output) and SiSo (single-input single-output) encoders. It reveals that while the MiMo encoder, specifically the Feature Pyramid Network (FPN), performs comparably with less than a 1 mAP (mean Average Precision) gap, the SiSo and MiSo encoders experience a significant performance drop of 12 mAP or more. The findings suggest that the C5 feature retains sufficient contextual information for detecting objects at various scales, allowing the SiSo encoder to yield similar results to MiMo. However, the advantages of multi-scale feature fusion appear to be less significant than the divide-and-conquer approach, which effectively simplifies the complex detection problem into manageable sub-problems based on object scales.

The paper introduces a novel approach called You Only Look One-level Feature (YOLOF), which utilizes a single C5 feature with a downsample rate of 32 for object detection. YOLOF addresses the optimization challenges by designing an encoder that captures multi-scale contexts and applying a uniform matching mechanism to balance the positive anchors in the sparse anchor scenario of single features. The results demonstrate that YOLOF achieves performance comparable to RetinaNet while being 2.5 times faster and converging seven times faster than DETR (Detection Transformer).

Key contributions of the paper include:
1. Highlighting that the primary benefit of FPN lies in its divide-and-conquer solution to optimization challenges in dense object detection rather than in multi-scale feature fusion.
2. Introducing YOLOF, a simple and efficient baseline that does not rely on FPN, featuring two components: the Dilated Encoder and Uniform Matching to bridge the performance gap between encoder types.
3. Conducting extensive experiments on the COCO benchmark to validate the importance of each component and demonstrating faster performance compared to existing architectures.

The related works section outlines the evolution of multiple-level feature detectors, contrasting earlier methods that relied on image pyramids with modern approaches that utilize feature pyramids. It acknowledges the limitations of single-level feature detectors, such as those in the R-CNN series and YOLO, and emphasizes the efficiency of YOLOF in addressing the optimization problem while maintaining competitive performance.
