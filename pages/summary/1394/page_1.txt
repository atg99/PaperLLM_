This paper provides a comprehensive overview of loss functions used in semantic image segmentation, a critical area in computer vision with applications in fields like healthcare and autonomous driving. The author highlights the evolution of various objective loss functions developed over the past five years to address specific challenges such as biased data and sparse segmentation.

The paper categorizes fifteen notable loss functions into four groups: Distribution-based, Region-based, Boundary-based, and Compounded. It emphasizes the importance of selecting the appropriate loss function to enhance model convergence and performance. Additionally, a new loss function, the log-cosh dice loss, is introduced and evaluated against established loss functions using the NBFS skull-segmentation dataset. The findings are presented in terms of performance metrics such as Dice Coefficient, Sensitivity, and Specificity.

The paper concludes that certain loss functions demonstrate consistent performance across diverse datasets, making them suitable baseline choices for unknown data distributions. The implementation code is made available on GitHub, facilitating further research and application in the field.
