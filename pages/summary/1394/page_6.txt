This paper focuses on evaluation metrics for segmentation models, specifically analyzing the performance of these models using the Dice Coefficient, Sensitivity, and Specificity. The Dice Coefficient measures the overlap between the ground truth and predicted outputs, while Sensitivity emphasizes True Positives and Specificity assesses the ratio of True Negatives. The study reports achieving a Dice Coefficient of 0.98 with Focal Tversky Loss and 0.975 with Log-Cosh Dice Loss, indicating high model performance. Notably, Focal Tversky Loss excelled in Sensitivity, although Specificity remained stable across different loss functions.

The paper concludes that the choice of loss function significantly impacts model performance and that there is no one-size-fits-all solution for segmentation tasks. The effectiveness of loss functions varies based on dataset characteristics, such as distribution and skewness. Highly imbalanced datasets benefit from focus-based loss functions, while balanced datasets perform better with binary-cross entropy. The authors summarize 14 loss functions for semantic segmentation and propose a variant of the Dice Loss for improved optimization, setting the stage for future work in few-shot segmentation experiments.
