The paper discusses various loss functions used in segmentation tasks, highlighting their unique characteristics and applications. 

1. **Tversky Loss** is an extension of the Dice Loss, defined mathematically to balance false positives and false negatives using a coefficient. 

2. **Focal Tversky Loss** modifies Tversky Loss to focus on hard examples, particularly in small regions of interest, by down-weighting easier cases.

3. **Sensitivity Specificity Loss** addresses class imbalance by combining sensitivity and specificity metrics, weighted by a parameter to balance their contributions.

4. **Shape-aware Loss** incorporates geometric considerations by measuring the average Euclidean distance between predicted shapes and ground truth, influencing the cross-entropy loss.

5. **Combo Loss** integrates Dice Loss with a modified cross-entropy loss, aiming to balance class imbalance and achieve smoother predictions.

6. **Exponential Logarithmic Loss** combines Dice Loss and Cross Entropy through exponential and logarithmic transformations, enhancing decision boundaries and data distribution accuracy.

7. **Distance Map Derived Loss** utilizes distance maps to guide the network's focus on challenging segmentation areas, employing a custom penalty-based loss function.

8. **Hausdorff Distance Loss** employs the Hausdorff Distance metric to evaluate the segmentation performance by measuring the maximum distance between point sets.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of tailored loss functions in improving segmentation accuracy and handling challenges such as class imbalance and geometric fidelity.
