The paper provides a comprehensive overview of various loss functions used in semantic segmentation, particularly in the context of medical imaging, specifically skull segmentation from CT scans. The summary outlines different loss functions, their use cases, and performance metrics such as Dice Coefficient, Sensitivity, and Specificity.

Key loss functions include:

1. **Binary Cross-Entropy**: Suitable for datasets with equal class distribution.
2. **Weighted Cross-Entropy**: Effective for skewed datasets, emphasizing positive examples.
3. **Focal Loss**: Designed for highly imbalanced datasets, focusing on hard examples.
4. **Dice Loss**: Derived from the Dice Coefficient, modified for better tractability.
5. **Tversky Loss**: Addresses false positives and negatives with weighted adjustments.
6. **Log-Cosh Dice Loss**: A proposed variant that combines properties of Dice Loss with regression smoothing approaches.

The experiments utilized a 2D U-Net model on the NBFS Skull Stripping Dataset, consisting of 125 skull CT scans, with a training configuration of a batch size of 32 and Adam optimizer. The dataset was split into training, validation, and testing sets in a 60-20-20 ratio. Performance metrics were evaluated across nine selected loss functions, demonstrating the effectiveness of Log-Cosh Dice Loss, which achieved a Dice Coefficient of 0.989, alongside high Sensitivity and Specificity scores.

Overall, the study highlights the importance of selecting appropriate loss functions tailored to specific datasets and segmentation tasks, with the proposed Log-Cosh Dice Loss showing promising results in skull segmentation applications.
