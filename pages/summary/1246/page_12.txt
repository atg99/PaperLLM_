This paper presents a comparative analysis of standard gradient boosting and Adaptive Gradient Boosting (AGB) in the context of model performance as influenced by the shrinkage parameter ν. The results, illustrated through boxplots, show that as the shrinkage parameter varies, both models exhibit differences in test error and the number of selected iterations. Notably, AGB tends to overfit rapidly, leading to an increase in validation error with fixed ν, contrasting with the behavior of standard gradient boosting. The analysis is based on simulated models with correlated design, emphasizing the implications of shrinkage on model effectiveness.
