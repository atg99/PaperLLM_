The paper discusses the impact of the shrinkage parameter on test error and the number of selected iterations in gradient boosting models, specifically comparing standard gradient boosting and Adaptive Gradient Boosting (AGB). The results, illustrated through boxplots, highlight the relationship between the shrinkage parameter and model performance across various real-life datasets, indicating that sparser models are particularly advantageous for large-scale data due to reduced computational costs. Additionally, the paper provides empirical evidence of computation times for fitting trees using the rpart package, emphasizing the efficiency gains achieved by employing AGB. This analysis underscores the trade-off between model complexity and computational efficiency in machine learning applications.
