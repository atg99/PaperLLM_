The paper discusses the evolution of gradient boosting, tracing its origins to the work of Freund and Schapire on AdaBoost, which was recognized by Breiman as a gradient-descent-type algorithm. This led to the formalization of gradient boosting by Friedman, who emphasized the use of decision trees as base learners. The paper also highlights the impact of high-dimensional data on the development of accelerated first-order optimization techniques, particularly Nesterov's accelerated gradient descent. Nesterov's method, which optimally minimizes smooth convex functions at a rate of 1/t², is contrasted with traditional gradient descent methods, which achieve a slower rate of 1/t.

The authors introduce a new algorithm called Accelerated Gradient Boosting (AGB), which integrates Nesterov's approach into Friedman’s boosting framework. The paper presents extensive numerical evidence demonstrating the efficacy of AGB across various synthetic and real-world datasets, showcasing its superior performance in comparison to existing methods. The study emphasizes the significance of combining advanced optimization techniques with boosting algorithms to improve predictive accuracy in machine learning applications.
