The paper discusses the gradient boosting principle in machine learning, specifically focusing on the optimization of risk reduction using a function space approach. It begins with the formulation of a gradient of a risk function C at a function Ft, represented as ∇C(Ft). The goal is to find a new function ft+1 that minimizes the risk when added to the existing function Ft, ideally in the direction that most rapidly decreases the risk.

The proposed method suggests taking ft+1 as the negative gradient of C, but due to constraints in function space F, a least squares approximation is employed instead. This leads to the formulation of an optimization problem where ft+1 is chosen to minimize the squared difference between the negative gradient and the new function, resulting in a practical iterative algorithm.

In empirical settings, this optimization problem is expressed in terms of sample data points, allowing for the computation of the new function based on observed residuals from previous iterations. The iterative process culminates in an additive model FT, constructed from a weighted sum of the functions obtained at each step.

The paper emphasizes the importance of selecting an appropriate step size for convergence and highlights the computational challenges involved in solving the least squares problem. The gradient boosting algorithm, notably Friedman’s approach, utilizes recursive partitioning and regularization techniques to effectively minimize overfitting while achieving the desired risk reduction. The subsequent sections of the paper introduce an accelerated version of this algorithm, building on these foundational concepts.
