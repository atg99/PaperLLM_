The paper presents a comparison of different models in terms of their error rates and the number of iterations required, focusing on the impact of the shrinkage parameter ν in standard gradient boosting and Adaptive Gradient Boosting (AGB). Boxplots illustrate the test error and selected iterations as functions of ν. The findings indicate that while experimenting with shrinkage parameters is common to minimize validation error, an increase in validation error can occur rapidly after reaching the optimal number of iterations. This observation emphasizes the need to balance model complexity and performance, as the validation error can rise sharply despite initial improvements. The results underscore the importance of careful selection of parameters in machine learning models to achieve optimal performance.
