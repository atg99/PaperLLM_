The paper discusses the performance of standard gradient boosting and an accelerated gradient boosting (AGB) algorithm across various models, specifically Model 1 and Model 5. The training and validation errors are analyzed, revealing that while the validation error initially decreases and eventually increases, both methods yield comparable validation rates at the optimal number of iterations (T⋆). However, AGB demonstrates superior efficiency by producing a smaller output model, attributed to Nesterov's acceleration scheme.

Figures presented illustrate the relationship between predictive performance, the number of iterations, and the shrinkage parameter (ν). The findings indicate that both methods achieve similar predictive performances regardless of the dataset. Notably, smaller values of ν lead to improved test error, with optimal test errors occurring at ν values below 0.1. Standard boosting struggles to reach optimal T⋆ efficiently when ν is less than 0.01, often requiring up to 10,000 iterations, while AGB achieves optimal models in significantly fewer iterations. Additionally, AGB shows reduced sensitivity to the choice of ν, making it advantageous in practical applications where prior knowledge of optimal parameters may be limited.
