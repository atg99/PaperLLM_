The paper discusses the implementation of gradient boosting in machine learning, particularly through the construction of regression trees. It explains that gradient boosting sequentially builds a linear combination of trees by adding one tree at each step, employing a method akin to functional gradient descent. The focus is on minimizing a specific quantity C(F) related to the expected value of a loss function ψ over the distribution of random variables (X, Y). The empirical measure μn is defined based on the sample data, while the theoretical measure C(F) is derived from the distribution of (X1, Y1). The paper also introduces the notation for vector spaces and norms associated with measurable functions, emphasizing that the function ψ is convex and continuously differentiable for each value in Y. Overall, the goal is to minimize the functional C(F) across a subset of functions in L2(μX).
