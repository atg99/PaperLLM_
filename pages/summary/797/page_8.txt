The paper presents TT-Rec, a method for tensor train compression applied to deep learning recommendation models. TT-Rec effectively reduces model size while maintaining competitive accuracy. Key findings include that increasing the number of large embedding tables trained in the tensor train embedding (TT-Emb.) format significantly decreases model size but may lead to slight accuracy degradation. For instance, expanding from three to seven embedding tables improved model size reduction from 2.6 to 95.5 times, while validation accuracy decreased from 80.975% to 80.682%, still surpassing the uncompressed baseline of 80.45%.

The research highlights the impact of tensor train ranks on model accuracy and compression ratios. Higher TT-ranks yield more accurate models but come with a trade-off of lower compression. The weight initialization strategy also plays a critical role in model performance, with Gaussian distribution yielding the best results. 

Training time performance analysis shows that while TT-Rec achieves significant model size reductions, it incurs a modest increase in training time—12.5% for Kaggle and 11.8% for Terabyte datasets when increasing the number of embedding tables. This creates a flexible design space for optimizing memory capacity, model quality, and training time.

Furthermore, TT-Rec's implementation is more efficient than existing methods, such as T3nsor and the PyTorch EmbeddingBag operator. It provides a drastically lower memory footprint, resulting in a 10,000 times reduction compared to T3nsor, while maintaining comparable training times to the baseline. Overall, TT-Rec demonstrates a promising approach to compressing deep learning recommendation models without significantly sacrificing accuracy or efficiency.
