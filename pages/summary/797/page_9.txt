The analysis reveals that TT-Rec effectively reduces the execution time while maintaining model accuracy across various configurations. Specifically, the warm-up iterations play a crucial role in optimizing the cache structure, which captures frequently accessed embedding vectors, thus enhancing cache hit rates. An increase in warm-up iterations from 10% to 30% of total training iterations results in a 33% increase in training time for Kaggle, but as warm-up continues beyond 30%, the training time overhead decreases due to improved hit rates.

In contrast, for the larger Terabyte dataset, TT-Rec consistently reduces training time across different warm-up iterations, achieving up to a 19% reduction in end-to-end training time with minimal impact on accuracy. The results also indicate that a cache size of just 0.01% of the embedding table is sufficient for effective performance, resulting in slight accuracy improvements.

Furthermore, TT-Rec's performance is examined in the context of embedding-dominated deep learning recommendation models (DLRMs) with varying pooling factors. The findings suggest that TT-Rec maintains efficiency regardless of the pooling factor, showcasing its robustness in handling different embedding lookup demands.

Overall, the study highlights the advantages of using TT-Rec with cache support for enhancing training efficiency and accuracy in deep learning recommendation systems.
