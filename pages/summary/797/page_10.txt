The paper presents TT-Rec, a tensor train compression method specifically designed to optimize deep learning recommendation models (DLRMs). It addresses the high memory demands associated with embedding layers in these models, which can require extensive resources in industrial applications. The study reveals that as the number of embedding lookups increases, the performance of TT-Rec improves, particularly when caching strategies are employed. The results demonstrate that TT-Rec can significantly reduce memory requirements—by up to 112 times—while introducing only a modest training time overhead of 13.9%, all while maintaining model accuracy. 

The paper contrasts TT-Rec with existing model compression techniques, highlighting its unique advantages over methods like magnitude pruning, feature hashing, and quantization, which have not been as effective in the context of DLRMs. It also discusses the limitations of previous tensor methods, such as Tucker factorization and low-rank approximations, noting that TT-Rec is the first to apply tensor train decomposition specifically to DLRMs. The authors conclude that TT-Rec represents a significant advancement in managing the computational demands of modern recommendation systems, emphasizing its potential for practical implementation in online training scenarios. 

The authors acknowledge the contributions of peers and support received during the research process, signaling a collaborative effort in developing this innovative approach.
