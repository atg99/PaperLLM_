The experimental results reveal that for the Kaggle dataset, TT-Rec achieves a compression ratio of up to 221 times, reducing the model size from 2.16 GB to less than 0.5 GB, with only a minor accuracy loss of 0.03% to 0.3% and an average training time increase of 7.4%. For the Terabyte dataset, the model size is reduced by 112 times, from 12.57 GB to 0.11 GB, while improving validation accuracy by 0.2% to 0.5% compared to the uncompressed baseline, albeit with a 13.9% increase in training time.

The analysis of memory capacity illustrates that TT-Rec significantly reduces the memory requirements for the largest embedding tables, with reductions from 2.16 GB to just 18 MB for Kaggle. The method allows for efficient training of large-scale models that were previously infeasible with conventional hardware, thus enhancing performance on high-throughput training accelerators.

In terms of model accuracy, TT-Rec maintains competitive performance with varying TT ranks for different embedding configurations, indicating that the choice of TT rank plays a critical role in balancing compression and accuracy. Overall, the findings suggest that TT-Rec is an effective technique for optimizing deep learning recommendation models, providing substantial memory savings while preserving or enhancing model performance.
