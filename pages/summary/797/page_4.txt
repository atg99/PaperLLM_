The paper introduces TT-Rec, a method for compressing deep learning recommendation models using Tensor Train (TT) decomposition. Traditional uncompressed models require O(MN) space for embedding tables of size M by N. In contrast, TT-Rec focuses on learning gradients of the loss function with respect to TT-cores, significantly reducing storage needs. A key insight driving TT-Rec's design is the recognition that not all sparse features are equally important, as data samples in recommendation systems often follow a Power or Zipfian distribution. This allows for efficient data movement when utilizing GPUs for training.

TT-Rec enhances the performance of deep learning recommendation models (DLRMs) that exceed the memory capacities of training accelerators, enabling their execution on such devices. The method incorporates a caching scheme to store frequently accessed embedding vectors in an uncompressed format, optimizing computation.

To address potential accuracy loss from replacing traditional embedding lookups with tensor computations, TT-Rec employs a novel weight initialization strategy for TT-cores. The research reveals that larger TT-ranks yield lower compression ratios but may not significantly enhance model accuracy beyond a certain point. The study also explores the impact of weight initialization on model performance, finding that uniform distributions often outperform normal distributions. The objective is to approximate the uniform distribution used in DLRMs with an appropriate Gaussian distribution during initialization.

Overall, TT-Rec provides a framework that balances compression and accuracy, allowing for efficient training of recommendation models while minimizing computational overhead.
