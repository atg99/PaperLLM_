This paper addresses the challenges faced in single image super-resolution (SISR) due to the discrepancies between training and test distributions, particularly stemming from bicubic downsampling. The authors propose a novel training method that learns an inverse mapping from bicubic downsampled images to real-world low-resolution (LR) images, using cycle consistency losses in an unsupervised manner. This allows for the generation of paired LR and high-resolution (HR) images that adhere to real-world distributions, enabling the training of the super-resolution (SR) network without reliance on paired ground-truth data.

The proposed method is evaluated under two scenarios: Domain Super-Resolution (DSR), which uses a single set of natural images, and Clean Super-Resolution (CSR), which targets a distinct output distribution of high-quality images. The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of their approach through benchmarking against baseline methods and state-of-the-art techniques, showcasing qualitative results on natural images, particularly from the DPED dataset.

In the related work section, the paper outlines the evolution of SISR methods, highlighting the limitations of traditional techniques that rely on bicubic downsampling, which removes high-frequency details and fails to represent real-world scenarios accurately. The authors critique existing deep learning methods that utilize L1 or L2 loss functions, which often lead to blurry results, and discuss advancements like GANs and perceptual loss functions aimed at enhancing image quality. They also reference recent approaches to unsupervised SR that lack paired LR-HR data, noting that their framework provides direct supervision in the HR domain, resulting in improved training outcomes.

Overall, the paper presents a comprehensive approach to bridging the gap between training and testing distributions in super-resolution, proposing a method that effectively learns from real-world data without the need for complicated data collection or paired datasets.
