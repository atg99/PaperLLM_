The paper discusses the evaluation of unsupervised super-resolution (SR) approaches under real-world image degradations, specifically focusing on JPEG compression artifacts and simulated sensor noise. It compares traditional metrics like PSNR and SSIM with the learned perceptual similarity metric LPIPS, highlighting that while PSNR is based on pixel-wise differences, LPIPS accounts for perceptual quality. The results indicate that although the EDSR method achieves a higher PSNR score, the proposed method demonstrates superior perceptual quality with a 56% reduction in LPIPS distance.

The study utilizes the DF2K dataset, which combines images from DIV2K and Flickr2K, and the DPED dataset, which contains images with real-world noise. An ablation study is conducted to compare various approaches using PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS metrics, emphasizing LPIPS due to its correlation with visual perception.

The ablation results showcased that the proposed method outperforms baseline and other strategies in both DSR and CSR settings, particularly in terms of perceptual quality. The baseline method, which uses bicubic downsampling, struggled with noise and artifacts due to inappropriate training conditions. The paper also explores a cleaning input strategy to mitigate domain shift issues, which improved performance but was still outperformed by the proposed method. Overall, the findings assert the effectiveness of the proposed approach in enhancing perceptual quality in super-resolution tasks.
