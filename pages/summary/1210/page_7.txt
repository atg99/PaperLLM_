This paper presents an approach to enhance image super-resolution in the presence of sensor noise and JPEG artifacts. An ablation study demonstrates that the proposed method performs favorably compared to various baseline models, especially under challenging conditions. The results indicate that many baseline variations produce significant artifacts, while the proposed method effectively mitigates these issues, achieving better perceptual quality as evidenced by improved LPIPS and PSNR scores.

The study also emphasizes the importance of direct high-resolution (HR) supervision over low-resolution (LR) supervision. When using LR supervision, the method resulted in stronger GAN hallucinations and poorer quantitative results. In contrast, the proposed method, which utilizes HR supervision, consistently outperforms alternatives, even when compared to fully supervised models that rely on paired samples.

Comparative evaluations against state-of-the-art methods like ZSSR, EDSR, and ESRGAN show that the proposed approach closely approaches the performance of fully supervised methods, particularly in handling JPEG artifacts. The findings highlight the effectiveness of the proposed technique in maintaining image quality when faced with sensor noise and compression artifacts, achieving the best perceptual results on the DIV2K dataset across both DSR and CSR settings.
