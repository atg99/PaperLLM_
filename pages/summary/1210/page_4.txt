The paper presents a novel approach for super-resolution image generation using a two-stage training process involving domain distribution learning and super-resolution learning. The first stage involves a generator network G that learns to map bicubic downsampled images to a higher resolution domain using unpaired data from input and output distributions. This is achieved through a Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) framework that incorporates cycle consistency losses to ensure the preservation of image content despite the lack of paired samples.

In the second stage, a super-resolution network S is trained using pairs generated by G. The training of S is conducted separately to avoid overfitting and poor generalization that could arise from joint training with G. The objective for the domain distribution learning combines GAN losses for both networks and cycle consistency losses to ensure that the mappings between domains are accurate.

The architecture of the domain distribution mapping G is based on the CycleGAN design, utilizing a ResNet structure for stability and to mitigate artifacts. The training process follows the CycleGAN methodology, employing the Adam optimizer for a specified number of epochs.

For super-resolution learning, the network S is trained on generated input images and their corresponding high-resolution counterparts. The training involves pixel-wise content loss and VGG feature loss to enhance the perceptual quality of the generated images.

Overall, the proposed method effectively combines GAN-based domain learning with super-resolution techniques, demonstrating robustness in generating high-quality images from bicubic downsampled inputs.
