The method introduces the concept of domain-specific super-resolution (DSR) and clean super-resolution (CSR), where the goal is to maintain the original characteristics of images during the super-resolution process. The challenge lies in the difficulty of obtaining paired natural low-resolution (LR) and high-resolution (HR) images for training.

To tackle this, the authors propose a two-step approach where a generator network, G, learns to map bicubically downsampled images to fit the input distribution. This process is carried out in a conditional GAN framework, with a discriminator assessing the authenticity of the generated images. Additionally, a second generator, F, is employed to ensure cycle consistency by mapping input images back to the bicubically downsampled images.

The independence of training between the domain distribution network G and the super-resolution network S is emphasized, as they serve different objectives. G focuses on generating training pairs for S, while S learns to super-resolve images effectively. This framework allows for direct pixel-wise supervision in the HR domain, enhancing the performance of the super-resolution network when applied to real-world images.
