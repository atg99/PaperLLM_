The paper presents an analysis of four different approaches for real-world super-resolution, which are evaluated in an ablation study. Each method is characterized by how the super-resolution network (denoted as S) is trained and applied during inference. The methods include a baseline with domain mismatch, cleaning the input to achieve a matching domain, low resolution supervision, and a proposed approach that incorporates bicubic downsampling and various networks for super-resolution, cleaning, domain distribution, and downsampling.

The datasets utilized for the experiments include DF2K, which comprises 3450 images with an average size of 1439x1935 pixels for training, and DIV2K, containing 100 images with an average size of 1451x1979 pixels for testing. Additionally, the paper notes the generator G's ability to simulate specific image characteristics, such as Gaussian noise and blocky compression artifacts in JPEG images, particularly at sharp edges.
