The paper discusses an advanced approach to image super-resolution (SR) that enhances perceptual quality through the integration of a Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) framework, specifically employing a relativistic discriminator. The perceptual loss is computed using features extracted from a VGG network, with the features gathered at the fourth convolutional layer before the fifth maxpooling layer. The GAN discriminator estimates a relative score for real versus fake images, improving the training of the SR network.

The total loss function combines several components: the perceptual loss from the VGG network, the GAN loss, and an L1 loss, with weights applied to balance their contributions. The architecture is based on the ESRGAN model, which incorporates Residual-in-Residual Dense Blocks for enhanced training stability, and a final color adjustment layer to match the color palette of the input low-resolution image.

Training utilizes a pre-trained ESRGAN generator and discriminator, with 50,000 iterations using the ADAM optimizer. The learning rate is adjusted progressively throughout the training process.

The experimental setup introduces two testing scenarios: Domain-Specific Super-Resolution (DSR) and Clean Super-Resolution (CSR). In DSR, the input and output images share the same distribution, while in CSR, the output images are derived from a different high-quality distribution. Both scenarios involve generating synthetic training and evaluation datasets through downsampling and degradation processes, with inaccessible ground truth images used solely for evaluation.

Overall, the paper presents a comprehensive evaluation methodology for real-world SR methods, emphasizing the need for robust training strategies and the effective use of GANs to improve perceptual quality in super-resolved images.
