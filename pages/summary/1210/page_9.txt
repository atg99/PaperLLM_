The references provided encompass a range of studies and methodologies focused on image super-resolution, indicating significant advancements in this field from various perspectives. Key contributions include:

1. **Cascading Residual Networks**: Ahn et al. (2018) introduced fast, accurate, and lightweight super-resolution techniques utilizing cascading residual networks, highlighting efficiency in processing.

2. **Blind Super-Resolution**: Research by Begin and Ferrie (2004) and Gu et al. (2019) explored learning-based approaches and iterative kernel correction methods to enhance images without prior knowledge of degradation.

3. **Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs)**: Notably, Bulat et al. (2018) and Ledig et al. (2017) leveraged GANs for image super-resolution, demonstrating the effectiveness of adversarial training in producing high-fidelity images.

4. **Real-World Challenges**: The NTIRE challenges (2017-2019) organized by Timofte et al. provided benchmarks for comparing various super-resolution techniques in real-world scenarios, fostering competition and innovation.

5. **Deep Learning Approaches**: Several studies, including those by Dong et al. (2014, 2016) and Kim et al. (2016), emphasized deep learning architectures, particularly convolutional networks, for achieving state-of-the-art results in single image super-resolution.

6. **Perceptual Metrics**: Zhang et al. (2018) discussed the effectiveness of deep features as perceptual metrics, underscoring the importance of human-like evaluation in assessing super-resolution performance.

These references collectively illustrate the evolution of image super-resolution techniques, focusing on deep learning, GAN applications, and the importance of benchmarking to drive progress in the field.
