The paper discusses BiDAG, a framework for Bayesian structure learning and sampling in R, focusing on the posterior distribution given data D. Instead of identifying the highest scoring directed acyclic graph (DAG), BiDAG selects models with edges that have posterior probabilities exceeding a specified threshold. This method reduces false-positive edges compared to choosing a single maximally scoring model while minimizing the occurrence of directed cycles.

A key component of BiDAG is the Order Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) approach, which operates in the space of orders rather than directly on DAGs. This approach benefits from a smoother posterior landscape, as the likelihood for each order aggregates over multiple DAGs, leading to faster convergence compared to structure MCMC methods. 

An order is represented as a permutation of the nodes in a DAG, and a DAG is compatible with an order if the parent-child relationships align with the specified order. The scoring for an order is derived from the sum of the scores of all compatible DAGs, allowing for efficient computation of the posterior distribution. Moves within the Markov chain include local and global swaps of nodes and node relocation, with acceptance probabilities calculated using the Metropolis-Hastings algorithm.

Overall, BiDAG provides an effective mechanism for Bayesian structure learning, leveraging the properties of order MCMC to enhance sampling efficiency and accuracy.
