The paper discusses the evaluation of convergence diagnostics in Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) sampling for Directed Acyclic Graphs (DAGs) using two different algorithms: order MCMC and partition MCMC. It highlights the importance of selecting an appropriate burn-in period, which defaults to 20%, but can be adjusted as needed. The authors emphasize the need for rigorous convergence diagnostics by running MCMC multiple times with different starting points. 

Using posterior probabilities of edges computed from two chains, the results are visualized through concordance plots. The analysis reveals that the order MCMC produces consistent results, as indicated by points clustering near the diagonal in the concordance plot, suggesting good convergence. In contrast, the partition MCMC shows significant discrepancies in posterior probabilities between runs, indicating potential convergence issues. 

The paper recommends increasing the number of iterations for the partition MCMC to address these issues, though it notes that this method generally has longer runtimes compared to order MCMC. The authors stress the trade-off between runtime and the imposition of a uniform prior over structures when choosing between the two approaches. Ultimately, the order MCMC is favored for model selection in this example, while a forthcoming section will demonstrate its application with real data, where partition MCMC's convergence diagnostics appear satisfactory.
