The paper discusses the use of Bayesian structure learning and sampling in R, specifically focusing on a method for constructing joint graphs for KIRC and KIRP cohorts using a partition MCMC approach. The authors highlight the issue of false positives in the maximum a posteriori (MAP) graphs, particularly when data is noisy or scarce, as shown by Kuipers et al. (2022). To address this, they employ model averaging to derive consensus graphs by removing edges with a posterior probability below 0.5. 

Figures 11 and 12 illustrate the differences between MAP and consensus graphs, revealing that a significant number of edges in the KIRP and KIRC cohorts correspond to previously identified connections by Kuipers et al. (2018b). However, the authors note that their analysis, which utilized an updated prior and selected genes specific to the cohorts, led to the discovery of new edges, such as the connection between CCBL2 and R3HDM1, which was not part of the previous study. This demonstrates the efficacy of their method in refining the understanding of gene interactions within these cancer subtypes.
