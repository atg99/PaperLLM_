The paper discusses the BiDAG package, which facilitates Bayesian structure learning and sampling in R, specifically focusing on the analysis of mutational interactions in cancer subtypes KIRC and KIRP. The authors utilize pre-processed datasets from Kuipers et al. (2018b) and employ a prior based on the STRING protein-protein interaction database, applying penalties to edges not represented in the database for graph inference. The most recent version of STRING, 11.0, is used to ensure updated interactions.

Using iterative Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) methods, the authors identify Maximum A Posteriori Directed Acyclic Graphs (MAP DAGs) for both cancer subtypes, revealing distinct subgraphs with shared genes but no overlapping edges. They further employ partition MCMC to assess the reliability of the discovered interactions, demonstrating convergence through various diagnostic plots. The results indicate that posterior probabilities of edges stabilize after an initial burn-in period, with the MAP CPDAG edges achieving higher posterior probabilities than others. However, many edges still exhibit posterior probabilities below 0.5, highlighting varying levels of confidence in the inferred relationships based on the data.
