The paper discusses the functionalities of algorithms in Bayesian network learning, specifically focusing on the parameters and processes involved in expanding the search space for node parent sets. The functions sampleBN and learnBN are designed to halt execution if the number of potential parents exceeds a predefined limit, known as hardlimit. The parameter algorithm = "orderIter" allows for iterative expansions of the search space, with plus1it determining the number of iterations. When not specified, the search space continues to expand until no further improvements in the score of the maximally scoring directed acyclic graph (DAG) can be made.

The function sampleBN estimates an expansion graph based on a sample and a specified posterior probability threshold. The maximal parent set size K can increase during this process, but the hardlimit prevents exceeding the number of allowable parents for any single node. The parameter mergetype allows for different merging strategies between the core space and the maximally scoring graph or its equivalence classes.

Additionally, the paper details methods for Bayesian model averaging and posterior model selection using MCMC schemes. The function edgep calculates posterior probabilities for edges, while modelp constructs graphs with edges surpassing a certain probability threshold. Importantly, the conversion of DAGs to CPDAGs is recommended for more stable posterior probabilities of non-directed edges. The focus on individual edge selection in model averaging is highlighted as particularly advantageous for smaller datasets, where false-positive edges can be prevalent in estimated MAP DAGs.
