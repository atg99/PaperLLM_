The paper discusses a method for sampling Directed Acyclic Graphs (DAGs) using an Order Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) approach. The process begins with the generation of a sample of orders, where the last move is always accepted, potentially returning the current order. To derive a sample of graphs from the posterior distribution, an extra step is needed to sample DAGs based on these orders and their corresponding scores. This is feasible due to score decomposability, allowing for independent sampling of parent sets for each node.

The choice of parameter prior influences the functional form of score terms. Two options are highlighted: normal-Wishart and Dirichlet priors, which relate to BGe and BDe score functions. The default graphical prior is uniform, giving equal a priori probability to each parent set. However, the BiDAG approach allows for the imposition of a prior that penalizes additional edges, promoting the sampling of sparser DAGs. Individual penalization factors can also be applied to edges for more nuanced prior information.

For large DAGs, the paper emphasizes limiting the parent sets to a reduced search space for computational efficiency. An algorithm to define this reduced space, ensuring it captures the majority of posterior weight, is outlined. After generating a sample of DAGs, the first portion is typically discarded to account for burn-in, allowing for the approximation of the posterior probability of structural features using the remaining samples.

Additionally, the paper introduces a Partition MCMC method, which improves efficiency over traditional structure MCMC by employing a non-uniform prior that favors DAGs represented by multiple orders. A labeled partition is defined, consisting of a node ordering and a vector that segments nodes into parts. Compatibility of a DAG with a partition is determined by specific conditions related to parent-child relationships across the defined parts. BiDAG facilitates moves within this partition space, including the swapping of nodes from different parts, enhancing the sampling process.
