The paper discusses the use of BiDAG, a tool for Bayesian structure learning and sampling in R, highlighting its effectiveness in maximizing DAG scores through iterative MCMC. The authors demonstrate that, while the true positive rate (TPR) increases with each expansion of the search space, false positives (FP) also rise, leading to a less significant improvement in the structural Hamming distance (SHD). This suggests that although iterative MCMC enhances the TPR and DAG score, it may not always yield the best structural fit.

To address these challenges, the paper introduces the concept of sampling from the posterior distribution using the function sampleBN. It emphasizes the importance of including a broad range of true positives in the search space for effective sampling. The iterative MCMC method is shown to optimize this search space, allowing users to leverage it in the sampling function.

Additionally, the authors highlight the necessity of checking for convergence in MCMC sampling. They present diagnostic tools, including trace plots, to assess the stability of the sampled DAG scores, confirming that the MCMC process has reached a state where reliable conclusions can be drawn.

Overall, the paper outlines the capabilities of BiDAG for Bayesian structure learning and provides practical guidance on maximizing DAG scores and sampling from posterior distributions, while also addressing potential pitfalls in the modeling process.
