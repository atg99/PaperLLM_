The learnBN function focuses on finding the maximum a posteriori (MAP) estimate of the DAG but does not retain a list of DAGs from each MCMC iteration, returning only one maximally scoring DAG by default. On the other hand, sampleBN is designed to sample from the posterior distribution, providing a trace of the sampled DAGs, which is useful for understanding the variability in the estimates.

The parameters such as startspace and scoretable allow users to define the search space, either through a sparse or binary adjacency matrix or by providing an object of class ‘scorespace’. The search space can include arbitrary graphs without the acyclicity requirement, with the adjacency matrix indicating the presence of edges between nodes. The significance level α in the conditional independence tests influences the search space's size, where larger values may increase the risk of false positives, potentially affecting runtime.

Additionally, the paper mentions the importance of parameters like plus1 for determining the search space type and blacklist for removing specific edges from consideration. The use of a hard limit on the number of parents per node is emphasized to ensure the efficiency of the DAG construction process, particularly for larger structures. Overall, the paper provides a comprehensive guide on Bayesian structure learning and sampling techniques in R, highlighting the functionalities and considerations involved in the process.
