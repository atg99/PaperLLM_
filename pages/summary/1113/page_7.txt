This paper discusses an iterative Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) procedure designed to enhance the estimation of directed acyclic graphs (DAGs) in Bayesian networks. The procedure begins by initiating a search space (H) using the PC algorithm or an arbitrary adjacency matrix. It then employs an order MCMC scheme to find a maximally scoring DAG (Gmax) within this space. The search space is updated iteratively until no further changes occur.

The authors highlight a key issue: as the density of the true underlying DAG increases, the true positive rate (TPR) of structures estimated by the PC algorithm decreases. This limitation arises when the search space lacks edges present in the maximum a posteriori (MAP) DAG, making it impossible to identify the true structure. To mitigate this, the paper introduces an extended search space (H+) that incorporates additional potential parent sets for each node, allowing for corrections to pruning errors and yielding higher scoring DAGs.

Despite the benefits of searching in H+, the authors note that improvements may be constrained when multiple parents are missing from a node’s parent set. However, through iterative application of the MCMC procedure, more than one mistake can be rectified. Simulation studies demonstrate that this iterative approach can significantly enhance the quality of even poorly constructed search spaces, achieving a high percentage of true positive edges.

The paper also discusses the importance of defining a reasonable initial search space (H) to reduce the overall runtime of the MCMC process. In the BiDAG package, users can utilize both the PC algorithm and arbitrary adjacency matrices derived from expert input or other structure learning algorithms. Finally, the paper outlines the various functions available within the BiDAG package for structure learning, sampling, convergence diagnostics, model averaging, model comparison, and network visualization.
