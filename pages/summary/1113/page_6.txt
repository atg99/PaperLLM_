The paper discusses BiDAG, a framework for Bayesian structure learning and sampling in R, highlighting its methods for sampling and discovering maximum a posteriori (MAP) graphs. It outlines three key operations for manipulating directed acyclic graphs (DAGs): swapping nodes in adjacent parts, splitting or joining parts, and moving nodes between parts. Although partition Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) sampling is unbiased, it incurs higher complexity and slower convergence compared to order MCMC. However, research by Kuipers et al. (2022) indicates that the models derived from order MCMC are close to the true structures, mitigating concerns about bias.

For MAP graph discovery, BiDAG replaces summation with maximization in its scoring mechanism. It asserts that the non-uniform prior from order MCMC does not affect MAP searches since DAG scores remain consistent across sampling schemes. Consequently, order MCMC is preferred for both sampling and MAP estimation, while partition MCMC is reserved solely for sampling tasks.

The paper also introduces a hybrid sampling approach to manage the exponential growth of possible parent sets in larger networks. By employing a graph H to limit the maximum parent set size per node, the search space complexity is reduced. This is achieved through the constraint-based PC algorithm, which constructs a skeleton graph by deleting edges based on conditional independence tests. The resulting completed partially directed acyclic graph (CPDAG) identifies classes of equivalent DAGs. To enhance the search space H, BiDAG incorporates methods to rectify potential errors from the PC algorithm, ensuring the capture of significant posterior weight in the sampling process. Simulation studies further support the effectiveness of this approach.
