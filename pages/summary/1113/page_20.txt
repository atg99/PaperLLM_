The results indicate that the estimated Maximum A Posteriori (MAP) Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) contains a significant number of false-positive edges, which is a common issue when sample sizes are low. The ability to model selection through posterior probability estimates provides a potential solution to this problem, allowing for a more nuanced approach to determining the presence of edges in the graph. 

The performance of the models varies with the chosen threshold, with higher thresholds generally leading to fewer false positives but potentially increasing false negatives. The findings underscore the importance of selecting appropriate thresholds in Bayesian structure learning to balance sensitivity and specificity in model selection. 

Overall, the paper illustrates the utility of the BiDAG package in R for Bayesian structure learning and demonstrates how it can be applied to real-world data, providing insights into the reliability of estimated structures in the context of known ground truth.
