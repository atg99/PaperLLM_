In this paper, the authors explore the BiDAG package for Bayesian Dynamic Bayesian Networks (DBNs) and provide a detailed analysis of its functionality using simulated datasets. They focus on a specific case where the structure in the first time slice is consistent across all time slices, controlled by the parameter dbnpar. This allows for a unified approach to learning both initial and transition structures, unless specified otherwise.

Two datasets are used for demonstration: gsim100, which includes 100 observations generated from a random Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) with 100 nodes and Gaussian random variables, and DBNdata, comprising observations from five time points of a DBN with 12 dynamic and 3 static variables.

The authors first illustrate the Maximum A Posteriori (MAP) discovery process using the BiDAG package's learnBN function. They describe the setup for running the algorithms, particularly focusing on MCMC schemes and the construction of a score parameters object. They execute an order MCMC on the gsim100 dataset using the PC algorithm to define the search space, which is noted for its computational efficiency despite being susceptible to errors.

The results indicate that the score of the learned maximum DAG is lower than that of the true structure, suggesting that the estimated network has fewer true-positive edges than expected. This highlights the challenges in accurately discovering the underlying structure in Bayesian networks, emphasizing the importance of careful selection of algorithms and parameters in the learning process.
