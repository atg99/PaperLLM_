This paper discusses the use of MCMC (Markov Chain Monte Carlo) methods to estimate posterior probabilities of edges in the context of graphical models applied to KIRP and KIRC datasets. Specifically, it presents the function plotpedges, which visualizes the convergence of posterior probabilities for single edges based on MCMC chains. The analysis reveals traces of posterior probabilities for edges in the estimated MAP (Maximum A Posteriori) CPDAG (Completed Partially Directed Acyclic Graph) using an iterative approach across 1001 MCMC steps.

Additionally, the paper compares the MAP CPDAG obtained from KIRP data with a consensus model derived from averaging multiple DAGs generated through partition MCMC, focusing on edges with a posterior probability greater than 0.5. This comparison highlights common edges and unique edges present only in the KIRP MAP, illustrating the differences in network structures inferred from the two datasets. The findings emphasize the effectiveness of MCMC in capturing the underlying graphical relationships within biological data.
