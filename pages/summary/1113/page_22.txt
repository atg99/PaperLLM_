The paper discusses BiDAG, a tool for Bayesian structure learning and sampling in R, highlighting its application in dynamic Bayesian networks (DBNs). Figure 7 illustrates the transition structure of the maximum a posteriori (MAP) estimate of a 15-node DBN, revealing that the structure derived from an iterative Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) approach closely resembles the ground truth, with only two false-positive edges. This demonstrates the effectiveness of the BiDAG method in accurately identifying the relationships between variables over time.

The paper also cites the application of BiDAG in cancer research, specifically in a study by Kuipers et al. (2018b), where it was used to analyze mutation profiles across 22 cancer types from a dataset of 8198 tumor samples. The authors employed a Bayesian network-based clustering method to define tumor sample clusters, each represented by a Bayesian network. The structure learning process involved two steps, beginning with the function learnBN, which facilitates the identification of significant relationships among mutated genes. This showcases BiDAG's versatility in handling complex biological data and its potential contributions to understanding cancer genomics.
