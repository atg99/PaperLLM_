The section discusses the importance of diagnosing the convergence of Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) schemes in the context of Bayesian structure learning for directed acyclic graphs (DAGs). Convergence is crucial for both sampling from the posterior distribution and for discovering the maximum a posteriori (MAP) estimate. Although proving convergence is challenging, diagnostic plots serve as valuable tools for assessing it.

Trace plots are highlighted as fundamental for examining convergence, particularly for MCMC objects such as 'orderMCMC', 'partitionMCMC', and 'iterativeMCMC'. The function plotpedges allows visualization of changes in posterior probabilities of individual edges as new graphs are sampled. Initially, posterior probabilities may fluctuate, but they should stabilize as convergence is approached.

To gain a clearer understanding of convergence, it is recommended to analyze multiple independent MCMC runs with random starting points. If all chains converge, the DAGs should represent the posterior distribution similarly, resulting in close posterior probabilities for single edges across chains. Significant differences in these probabilities indicate non-convergence.

The function plotpcor can be used to visualize concordance between pairs of MCMC runs, allowing for inspection of edges with notable differences in posterior probabilities. Additionally, functions bidag2coda and bidag2codalist convert MCMC objects into formats compatible with the coda package, which provides standard convergence diagnostics. However, due to the unique nature of DAG samples, standard tools may not be applicable.

The BiDAG function plotpcor is recommended for diagnosing convergence in MCMC schemes for DAGs, as it is the most effective method available.
