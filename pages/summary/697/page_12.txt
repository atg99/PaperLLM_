The paper investigates the performance of the NDSPI-DQN algorithm and its decoupling version in different network scenarios, focusing on their scaling performance. The experiments utilize various network sizes, specifically scenarios 2, 3, and 4, to evaluate the algorithms' convergence by measuring mean rewards across a limited number of training episodes. Results indicate that while the NDSPI-DQN struggles to converge as the network size increases beyond 120 hosts, the decoupling version demonstrates stable convergence across all tested scenarios, with episode requirements of approximately 200, 300, and 400 for scenarios 2, 3, and 4, respectively.

Key findings include:

1. The integration of improvements enhances exploration capabilities, addressing the sparse reward challenge that arises in larger networks. This allows the agent to learn optimal policies more efficiently despite fewer positive rewards during training.

2. A reduction in action space decreases the trial-and-error cost for the agent. By structuring the action space based on the environment state, the algorithm reduces the complexity from O(M*N) to O(M+N), facilitating faster convergence in large-scale scenarios.

Overall, the decoupling NDSPI-DQN algorithm exhibits superior performance and robustness in larger network settings, driven by improved exploration strategies and a more manageable action space.
