The paper discusses the development and evaluation of a reinforcement learning agent designed to optimize attack paths in a network security context. The agent learns through exploration within episodes that conclude under specific conditions, such as gaining root access to sensitive hosts or reaching a maximum number of training steps. The experimental setup includes multiple network scenarios with varying numbers of hosts, sensitive subnets, and honeypots.

The results compare the convergence performance of three algorithms: NDSPI-DQN, decoupling NDSPI-DQN, and the baseline DQN. Experiments conducted in scenario 1 reveal that initially, the agent's mean rewards are low, and it utilizes the maximum number of steps, indicating a reliance on random action selection. Over time, both NDSPI-DQN and its decoupled variant demonstrate improved efficiency, achieving convergence towards optimal values within fewer episodes compared to DQN, which fails to converge. The findings underscore the effectiveness of the proposed algorithms in learning optimal attack strategies in network environments.
