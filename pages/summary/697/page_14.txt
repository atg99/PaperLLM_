In this paper, the authors model penetration testing as a Markov Decision Process (MDP) and employ deep reinforcement learning to enhance its effectiveness. They introduce the NDSPI-DQN algorithm, which improves robustness in autonomous penetration testing for large-scale networks. Key improvements include selecting various DQN enhancements to boost exploration capabilities and decoupling attack vectors to minimize the agent's action space, thereby reducing exploration costs. The authors conduct simulations across different network scales, demonstrating that their approach achieves better convergence in complex scenarios.

Despite these advancements, the authors acknowledge that current autonomous penetration testing remains limited to simulations due to challenges in modeling real network traffic and the high costs associated with trial-and-error methods in real-world applications. They suggest future work could explore emulation and virtualization technologies, as well as the application of advanced algorithms like multi-agent or hierarchical reinforcement learning for tackling broader cyberattack tasks based on the MITRE framework.

The paper concludes with a comprehensive author contribution section, stating that all authors have participated in various aspects of the research and manuscript preparation. The research did not receive external funding, and there are no conflicts of interest reported. Data availability is also mentioned, indicating that data can be requested from the corresponding author but is not publicly accessible due to privacy concerns.
