This paper addresses the challenges of autonomous penetration testing (pentesting) in large-scale networks by leveraging reinforcement learning (RL) techniques. While traditional methods like Partially Observable Markov Decision Processes (POMDP) have been applied, they suffer from high computational demands in extensive networks. In contrast, RL has shown significant promise in various domains, including gaming, where agents have outperformed human players. The authors propose modeling pentesting as a Markov decision process (MDP) and introduce an improved Deep Q-Network (DQN) algorithm to enhance the performance of pentesting agents.

The proposed DQN algorithm incorporates five enhancements aimed at addressing the sparse reward issue and reducing the action space associated with larger networks. By decomposing the attack vector, the algorithm can more effectively manage the challenges posed by extensive state spaces. Experiments conducted using the NASim platform demonstrate the algorithm's superior convergence performance in large-scale environments.

The paper is structured to first provide background on penetration testing and the obstacles faced in autonomous applications using RL. It then details the improvements made to the DQN algorithm, presents experimental results, and concludes with insights and suggestions for future research directions.
