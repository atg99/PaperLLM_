The paper discusses the challenges faced by hackers in acquiring sensitive data and the need for privilege escalation and anonymity. It then introduces the Markov Decision Process (MDP) and reinforcement learning as mathematical frameworks for multistage decision-making problems. An MDP is defined by the tuple <S,A,R,T>, where S is the state space, A is the action space, R is the reward function, and T is the transition function, representing the probability of state transitions. 

Reinforcement learning differentiates itself from supervised and unsupervised learning by learning through interaction with an uncertain environment. The agent observes the environment, selects actions based on a policy, and receives rewards, with the aim of maximizing cumulative rewards. The optimal policy is derived from the formula involving a discount factor that influences the importance of present versus future rewards.

Various algorithms exist for solving MDPs, with Q-learning being a prominent model-free, value-based method. The Q-learning algorithm updates the Q-function through trial-and-error, allowing the agent to derive policies based on expected rewards. However, traditional Q-learning struggles with large state spaces due to its reliance on a table to store state-action values.

To address this limitation, deep reinforcement learning (DRL) combines deep learning with reinforcement learning, enabling the handling of complex environments. The DQN algorithm, a specific DRL approach, employs deep neural networks for function approximation and uses experience replay to enhance learning efficiency. It also stabilizes learning by maintaining separate networks for predicting and targeting Q-values, with the target network being updated less frequently to improve convergence.
