The paper presents a novel approach to reinforcement learning, specifically through the implementation of the NDSPI-DQN algorithm. It outlines a learning cycle where an agent observes the current environment state and selects an action based on a learned policy. The agent receives external rewards from the environment, while an internal reward is generated by the Intrinsic Curiosity Module (ICM) based on the current state, action, and subsequent state. Both types of rewards are utilized to enhance the policy optimization.

The architecture of the NDSPI-DQN is detailed, highlighting the use of fully connected layers and dueling network structures with noisy linear layers. The input to the neural network comprises a vector representing the environment states, and the output consists of action values. Additionally, the paper discusses a decoupling mechanism within the NDSPI-DQN, where two streams share hidden layers but independently compute Q-values for different action elements. This dual-stream approach allows for the combination of selected host and operation actions into a comprehensive attack vector. 

Overall, the research emphasizes the significance of integrating internal and external rewards in reinforcement learning to improve decision-making processes in dynamic environments.
