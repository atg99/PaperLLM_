The paper discusses the performance of algorithms in various network scenarios, particularly focusing on the convergence rates of these algorithms in different host configurations. It presents results from three scenarios with varying numbers of hosts: 80, 120, and 150. Analysis of the convergence performance reveals that despite a larger scale in scenario 2 compared to scenario 1, the algorithms converge faster in scenario 2. This is attributed to the presence of honeypot hosts in scenario 1, which negatively impact the learning process when compromised, as it leads to the termination of training episodes and hinders the agent's ability to learn optimal policies. The paper emphasizes the importance of reward values in algorithm performance and suggests that the proposed method is effective in handling complex network scenarios.

Additionally, the paper provides a table listing hyperparameter values used for the algorithms, including a maximum of 2000 steps per episode, a learning rate of 0.0001, a batch size of 64, a discount factor of 0.9, a hidden layer size of 256, a replay memory size of 300,000, and a target network update frequency of 1000.
