This paper presents an innovative approach to autonomous penetration testing (APT) by employing an improved Deep Q-Network (DQN) framework. Traditional penetration testing relies heavily on skilled experts, which is often time-consuming and costly. The authors propose to model penetration testing as a Markov decision process, utilizing reinforcement learning to enhance efficiency in large-scale networks.

The proposed method, NDSPI-DQN, addresses the challenges of sparse rewards and large action spaces typically encountered in extensive scenarios. The authors enhance the DQN through five key extensions: noisy nets, soft Q-learning, dueling architectures, prioritized experience replay, and an intrinsic curiosity model. These modifications aim to improve exploration efficiency during the testing process. Additionally, the authors decouple actions and separate the estimators in the neural network to manage the action space more effectively.

Experimental results indicate that the NDSPI-DQN demonstrates superior convergence and scalability compared to traditional methods. This research contributes to making penetration testing more accessible and regular, reducing the reliance on specialized knowledge while enhancing cybersecurity measures against evolving threats.
