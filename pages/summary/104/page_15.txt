The study evaluated the performance of various deep learning models, specifically multi-LSTM and multi-GRU, in predicting water levels based on different input data characteristics. The findings indicated that while the multi-LSTM model showed consistent performance regardless of input data, the multi-GRU and LSTM-GRU models exhibited performance variations based on the input. Particularly, the multi-GRU model performed poorly when weather data was included (cases S2 and S3) compared to a simpler dataset (S1). The LSTM-GRU model also struggled with lower performance in the S1 case.

Among the tested models, S3_GRU had the worst results, with a training MSE of 0.39 and a validation MSE of 1.80, whereas S3_LSTM_GRU achieved the best outcomes, with a training MSE of 0.15 and a validation MSE of 0.19. The training and validation results were detailed in a table, highlighting the MSE values across different configurations.

For testing, the models were assessed against the highest water level observed during the data collection, with the highest recorded level being 3552.38 cm. The test results, including MSE, NSE, and MAE metrics, indicated that S1_LSTM and S3_LSTM_GRU performed well, while S3_GRU demonstrated the least effectiveness in predicting water levels. Overall, the study underscored the importance of input data characteristics in model performance for water level prediction.
