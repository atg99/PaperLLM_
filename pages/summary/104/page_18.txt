The paper discusses a study aimed at improving water level prediction models to aid in flood management, particularly in the context of increasing flood risks due to climate change. It focuses on developing an LSTM-GRU model using data from Yeojubo, Gyeonggi-do, Korea, and compares its performance against other models.

The study evaluated three scenarios (S1, S2, and S3) based on different input data configurations. The results indicated that while the GRU model performed best with S1, it was generally inferior compared to the other models. The LSTM-GRU model emerged as the most effective overall, particularly excelling in scenarios S2 and S3 with NSE values of 0.920 and 0.942, and maximum water level errors of 79.64 and 55.49, respectively. Notably, S3 yielded the best results in all evaluation metrics, including MSE, NSE, MAE, and maximum water level prediction error.

The research included experiments to optimize the LSTM-GRU model parameters, determining that 256 units in the LSTM and GRU layers and an input data size covering the past 20 hours yielded the best performance. The study also compared three datasets: water level data alone, water level data combined with AWS data, and water level data combined with ASOS data. The LSTM-GRU model demonstrated superior performance when both the water level and ASOS datasets were utilized together.

The paper emphasizes the importance of effectively predicting water levels to mitigate flood damage and suggests that the LSTM-GRU model could serve as a foundational tool for developing a flood management system capable of proactive risk assessment and evacuation planning.
