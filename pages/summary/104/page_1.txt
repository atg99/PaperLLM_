The paper by Cho et al. presents a water level prediction model utilizing a combination of Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) and Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) methods aimed at improving flood prediction accuracy. The authors emphasize the increasing damage caused by floods globally, attributed to climate change, and highlight the importance of predicting water levels to mitigate economic and human losses. 

The proposed model incorporates meteorological data, including upstream and downstream water levels, temperature, humidity, and precipitation as input variables. Experimental results demonstrate that the best performance was achieved with the LSTM-GRU model when combined with Automated Synoptic Observing System (ASOS) meteorological data. Key performance metrics indicated a mean squared error (MSE) of 3.92, a Nash–Sutcliffe coefficient of efficiency (NSE) of 0.942, and a mean absolute error (MAE) of 2.22, showcasing the model's effectiveness.

The study also notes the historical maximum water level of 3552.38 cm within the test area, with a maximum water level prediction error of 55.49 cm, the lowest recorded error in their experiments. The authors conclude that the composition of input data significantly impacts prediction performance and indicate plans for future research to develop a flood risk management system that utilizes predicted water levels for timely evacuations. 

This work contributes to the ongoing efforts in flood prediction methodologies by integrating advanced machine learning techniques with relevant meteorological data.
