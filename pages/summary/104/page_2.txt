The paper discusses the evolution of hydrological modeling techniques, focusing on the limitations of traditional hydrological models and the advancements brought by data-based intelligent models, particularly artificial neural networks (ANN) and deep neural networks (DNN). 

Traditional hydrological models analyze the characteristics of water flow using principles from fluid mechanics. While useful for flood prediction and management, these models require extensive hydrological knowledge and are affected by topographical changes over time, limiting their long-term applicability. Additionally, they struggle with input data variability and non-linearity, necessitating individual models for different watersheds, which reduces versatility.

In contrast, data-based intelligent models leverage observed data for predicting water levels and runoff, utilizing advanced data analysis and mining techniques. ANN models have shown promising results in hydrological applications, incorporating methods like harmony search and differential evolution to enhance performance by preventing overfitting and optimizing feature selection. However, they often rely on time series data and face challenges with memory limitations and parameter optimization during the learning process.

The paper highlights the emergence of DNNs, particularly Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) and Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) models, which excel at processing sequential and time series data. These advancements are expected to improve flood forecasting accuracy by addressing some of the limitations associated with traditional ANN models. The study underscores the significance of integrating machine learning approaches in hydrological modeling to enhance predictive capabilities for flood management.
