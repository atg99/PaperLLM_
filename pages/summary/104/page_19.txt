In this paper, a data-driven approach for water level prediction is presented, utilizing actual observation data from the Yeojubo region in Gyeonggi-do, South Korea. The study aims to predict water levels for the 21st hour using data from the preceding 20 hours, employing Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) and Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) models. A performance comparison was conducted with various input data configurations to identify the optimal model setup, ultimately selecting a two-layer LSTM and a two-layer GRU as the final models.

The conventional hydrological methods for flood prediction face challenges due to the complexity of factors that must be considered, necessitating advanced hydrological knowledge. In contrast, the proposed model simplifies the prediction process by relying solely on meteorological and water level datasets. The study included a performance comparison across nine cases utilizing different combinations of input data: water level data alone, water level data combined with Automatic Weather Station (AWS) data, and ASOS data from the Korea Meteorological Administration.

Results indicated that LSTM and LSTM-GRU models performed well across all data types, but the GRU model showed diminished performance with larger input dimensions. The best results were achieved using ASOS and water level data, with the LSTM-GRU model yielding a test Mean Squared Error (MSE) of 3.92, a Nash-Sutcliffe Efficiency (NSE) of 0.942, and a Mean Absolute Error (MAE) of 2.22, indicating superior predictive capability. The model is deemed particularly effective for predicting sudden water level rises due to heavy rainfall, making it a viable tool for flood management.
