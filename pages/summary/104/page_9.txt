The paper discusses the implementation and evaluation of water level prediction models based on Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) and Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) architectures. It outlines the equations governing the reset gate, update gate, hidden state candidates, and the final hidden state used in GRU, highlighting the mathematical foundation of the models.

The study emphasizes that it is inconclusive whether GRU or LSTM outperforms the other, prompting a comparative experiment involving both models. The performance of the models is assessed using three key indicators: Mean Squared Error (MSE), Nash-Sutcliffe Efficiency (NSE), and Mean Absolute Error (MAE). MSE is particularly important as it is sensitive to outliers, making it crucial for hydrological predictions where inaccuracies can lead to severe consequences. NSE provides an efficiency measure, with values closer to 1 indicating better performance, while MAE offers a straightforward interpretation of average errors.

The experimental design includes a preliminary study focusing on three main approaches: optimizing configurations for LSTM and GRU separately, determining the optimal number of units in each layer, and evaluating the impact of varying input data sizes on model performance. The findings from these preliminary experiments inform the parameters used in the final model evaluation, with MSE serving as the loss function for performance comparison. Overall, the research aims to enhance water level prediction accuracy through rigorous model evaluation and optimization.
