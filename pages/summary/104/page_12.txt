The paper evaluates the performance of various models for water level prediction, focusing on Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) and Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) architectures. The LSTM 2-layer model demonstrated the best overall performance and was chosen as the comparative model for further experiments. Among the GRU configurations, the 2-layer model also performed best, with a final learning loss of 0.34 and a validation loss of 0.18. In contrast, the GRU 1-layer model showed the lowest performance, indicating that the increase in layers positively impacts model accuracy, although the differences in performance among GRU layers were less pronounced than those observed with LSTM.

The study further explored the impact of the number of units in the LSTM and GRU models. An experimental comparison was made using various unit sizes (16, 32, 64, 128, and 256), revealing that 256 units yielded the best performance in both training and validation losses. The results indicated a proportional relationship between the number of units and learning time, with significant performance differences only observed at the lowest unit count.

Additionally, the size of input data significantly influenced model performance. Input data sizes of 5, 10, 15, 20, and 30 hours were tested, with the optimal performance achieved using 20 hours of past data. Smaller input sizes (5 and 10 hours) resulted in increased loss values due to overfitting. The findings underscored that while larger input data improved learning time, the most effective training and validation losses were observed with 20 hours of input data.

In conclusion, the optimal configurations identified in this study include the LSTM 2-layer model and GRU 2-layer model, both utilizing an input size of 20 hours and a unit count of 256 for effective water level prediction.
