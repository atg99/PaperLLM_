The paper evaluates the performance of different machine learning models, specifically Multi-LSTM, Multi-GRU, and LSTM-GRU, in predicting water levels across three distinct scenarios (S1, S2, and S3). The analysis includes comparison graphs of observed versus predicted values for each model in each scenario.

In the results, the Multi-LSTM model showed consistent performance, with the best results in the S1 case, achieving a Nash-Sutcliffe Efficiency (NSE) value that indicated superior accuracy. The maximum water level prediction error in S1 was recorded at 91.27 cm. For S2, the NSE was slightly lower at 0.834, with a maximum error of 117.87 cm.

Conversely, the Multi-GRU model exhibited the weakest performance across all scenarios. It struggled particularly with high-dimensional input data, yielding the lowest NSE values of 0.2 and 0.3 for S2 and S3, respectively, and a maximum error of 211.77 cm in S3.

The LSTM-GRU model emerged as the most effective overall, particularly excelling in S2 and S3 with NSE values of 0.920 and 0.942, respectively, and maximum errors of 79.64 cm and 55.49 cm. In S3, it achieved the best results across all evaluation metrics, suggesting that the ASOS data from this scenario is the most suitable for the test bed at Yeojubo.

Overall, the findings indicate that while the Multi-LSTM and LSTM-GRU models perform well under various conditions, the Multi-GRU model faces challenges with complex data, impacting its predictive accuracy.
