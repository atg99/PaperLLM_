The paper presents a comparison of various models used for predicting water levels, specifically focusing on their performance metrics such as Mean Squared Error (MSE), Nash-Sutcliffe Efficiency (NSE), and Mean Absolute Error (MAE). The results for different cases (S1, S2, S3) are detailed in the provided table, highlighting the performance of LSTM and GRU models, as well as a combined LSTM-GRU approach.

The S1_LSTM model demonstrated the best performance with an MSE of 5.02, NSE of 0.917, and a maximum prediction error of 91.27 cm. The S2_LSTM model followed with an MSE of 4.73, but a lower NSE of 0.834 and a higher prediction error of 117.87 cm. The S1_GRU model showed less effectiveness compared to LSTM models, with a higher MSE of 7.73 and a lower NSE of 0.835.

Figures 15 and 16 provide visual comparisons of observed versus predicted values for each model across the S1 and S2 cases. The results indicate that while the multi-LSTM model performed consistently, the NSE values varied with input data, achieving the highest efficiency with S1 case data. The study concludes that the choice of model and input data significantly impacts prediction accuracy, particularly for maximum water level predictions.
