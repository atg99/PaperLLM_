The paper investigates the impact of heavy rain in the Gyeonggi Province of South Korea, focusing on rain damage over the past 30 years using data from the Ministry of Public Administration and Security. The findings indicate that Gyeonggi Province experiences the second-highest recovery amount from rain damage and the highest frequency of rainfall events among all cities. This region, characterized by high population density due to its proximity to the capital, is particularly vulnerable to flooding, especially in downstream areas where excessive runoff occurs.

The study selects Yeojubo in Gyeonggi-do as the primary test bed due to its upstream and downstream measuring stations, which are critical for analyzing water flow and predicting flood events. The research utilizes meteorological and water level datasets from the Korea Water Management Information System, consisting of hourly measurements from October 2013 to June 2022, to develop a water level prediction model. This comprehensive analysis aims to enhance understanding and management of disaster risks associated with heavy rainfall in densely populated areas.
