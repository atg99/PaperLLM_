The paper discusses the performance of meteorological and hydrological data collection in the context of rainfall measurement, particularly during torrential summer downpours. It highlights the challenges posed by the distance between the test area, Yeojubo, and nearby weather observation stations. The nearest Automatic Weather System (AWS) is approximately 8 km away, while the Automated Synoptic Observing System (ASOS) is about 20 km distant. The authors emphasize that this distance can significantly impact model performance due to the localized nature of heavy rainfall.

To address this issue, the study plans to conduct performance comparison experiments using different types of meteorological data as input for modeling. The paper includes a table detailing the measurement variables and periods for various hydrological and meteorological stations utilized in the research.

Additionally, the paper introduces Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks, a type of recurrent neural network (RNN) adept at processing time series data, which will be employed in the modeling efforts. This approach is relevant for capturing the temporal correlations inherent in rainfall data. Overall, the research aims to improve model accuracy by carefully considering the input data sources and their proximity to the test site.
