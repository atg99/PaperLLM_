This paper presents a study on flood prediction using a Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) and Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) based model, specifically in the Da River basin of Vietnam. The research leverages runoff and precipitation datasets to evaluate the model's performance in predicting future runoff amounts for one, two, and three days ahead. The performance is assessed using the Nash-Sutcliffe efficiency (NSE) and root mean squared error (RMSE) metrics, achieving NSE values of 99.1%, 94.9%, and 87.2% for the respective days, indicating over 87.2% accuracy across all outputs.

The study highlights the effectiveness of the LSTM model for flood prediction, noting its strong performance compared to other recurrent neural network (RNN) models like GRU. It also emphasizes the role of meteorological data in enhancing model performance, revealing that the optimal results were obtained using water level data alongside Automated Synoptic Observing System (ASOS) data. The proposed LSTM-GRU model outperformed other configurations in terms of mean squared error (MSE), NSE, mean absolute error (MAE), and maximum water level prediction error.

The methodology section details the study area, training data, and deep learning techniques employed in model development. It also discusses the experimental scenarios and model design, followed by a performance comparison of the various models. The paper concludes by reviewing the findings and suggesting that utilizing appropriate meteorological data can significantly improve flood prediction models.
