The paper discusses the datasets used for water level prediction in Yeojubo, South Korea, focusing on both water level and meteorological data. The water level dataset, obtained from the Korea Water Management Information System (WAMIS), includes measurements from upstream and downstream stations taken hourly from October 2013 to June 2022, totaling 71,136 rows for each dataset. It highlights that water levels remain stable except during summer rainfall, where upstream levels increase significantly more than downstream levels.

Meteorological data from the Korea Meteorological Administration, specifically from the Automatic Weather System (AWS) and the Automated Synoptic Observing System (ASOS), were utilized as input for the predictive model. These systems measure various parameters, including air pressure, temperature, humidity, wind direction, wind speed, and precipitation, also at hourly intervals. The paper notes that the AWS data, collected automatically without human oversight, displayed lower accuracy during summer due to the region's hot and humid climate, which is prone to heavy rainfall and typhoons. The distances of the nearest AWS and ASOS stations from Yeojubo are mentioned, with the AWS being 8 km away and the ASOS 20 km away. The visualizations of the datasets are provided in figures within the paper.
