The study investigates the optimal layer configuration for water level prediction models using Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) and Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) architectures. The experiment compares models with one to three hidden layers, acknowledging that an excessive number of layers can lead to overfitting and diminished performance. 

The results indicate that the LSTM 2-layer model outperformed both the 1-layer and 3-layer models, with a training loss of 0.18 and a validation loss of 0.59. The 3-layer LSTM model exhibited overfitting, as evidenced by its higher validation loss despite having more hyperparameters and a slower learning rate. 

For the GRU models, the 2-layer configuration also achieved the best performance, with a final learning loss of 0.34 and a validation loss of 0.18. The 1-layer and 3-layer GRU models did not perform as well, confirming the 2-layer structure as the most effective for both LSTM and GRU models in this context. The findings support the use of the LSTM 2-layer model as a comparative benchmark in subsequent experiments.
