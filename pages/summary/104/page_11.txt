The study investigates the performance of various LSTM and GRU model configurations, focusing on the impact of the number of layers and the number of units on training and validation loss. The GRU 1-layer model exhibited the lowest performance, with minimal differences noted among various layer configurations, unlike the more pronounced variations seen with LSTM models. The LSTM models demonstrated a direct relationship between the number of layers and hyperparameters, with the 2-layer model achieving the best balance of training loss (0.18) and validation loss (0.59) despite higher complexity and training time compared to the 1-layer model. The 3-layer LSTM, while having a greater number of hyperparameters, faced overfitting and performed worse than the 2-layer model.

Additionally, experiments assessed the optimal number of units within LSTM and GRU models, ranging from 16 to 256 units. Results indicated that the best performance for both training and validation loss occurred with 256 units, while 32 units also showed relatively strong performance. The findings highlight the importance of model architecture and hyperparameter tuning in optimizing performance, ultimately recommending 256 units as the optimal configuration for the LSTM-GRU model.
