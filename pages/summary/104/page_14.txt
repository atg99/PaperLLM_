Each model processes input data from the past 20 hours to predict water levels for the 21st hour. The dataset spans from October 2, 2013, to June 9, 2022, with a total of 76,152 rows, where 75% of the data was allocated for training and validation, while 25% was reserved for testing.

The models were evaluated using Mean Squared Error (MSE) as the primary loss function, supplemented by Nash-Sutcliffe Efficiency (NSE) and Mean Absolute Error (MAE) for performance assessment. The Adam optimizer was employed, with both LSTM and GRU units set to 256 and 200 training epochs conducted. The selection of these parameters stemmed from preliminary experiments aimed at optimizing model performance.

Results indicated that the multi-LSTM model possessed the highest number of hyperparameters, while the multi-GRU model had the fewest. Although the GRU model exhibited the shortest training time, the relationship between hyperparameters and training duration was not linear. The loss values for all models converged towards zero, suggesting effective learning, though the potential for overfitting was acknowledged. The experiments maintained a consistent epoch count across models to ensure comparability. 

Overall, the study emphasizes the effectiveness of different recurrent neural network architectures in flood prediction based on historical water level and meteorological data.
