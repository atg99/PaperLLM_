This paper discusses the operation of Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) and Gated Recurrent Units (GRU), two advanced architectures of Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) designed to address long-term dependency issues. The LSTM architecture is detailed through a series of equations that define its gates: the forget gate, input gate, output gate, and the cell state update process. The equations illustrate how the current cell state is influenced by previous states and inputs, ultimately leading to the hidden state output.

The GRU is presented as a more streamlined alternative to LSTM, combining the forget and input gates into a single update gate, thus reducing computational complexity while maintaining performance. The GRU structure integrates the cell state and hidden state, which results in fewer parameters compared to LSTM. The functions of the reset gate and update gate in GRU are explained, highlighting their roles in determining the hidden state based on past information and current inputs.

Performance indicators for evaluating the water level prediction models using LSTM and GRU are introduced, including Mean Squared Error (MSE), Nash–Sutcliffe Efficiency (NSE), and Mean Absolute Error (MAE). MSE is particularly emphasized for its sensitivity to outliers, which is critical in hydrological modeling due to potential human consequences. NSE values range from negative infinity to one, with values closer to one indicating better model performance. MAE represents the average of absolute errors, providing another measure of accuracy.

The paper concludes by stating that no definitive performance superiority can be established between GRU and LSTM, prompting the authors to conduct comparative experiments to evaluate their effectiveness in water level prediction.
