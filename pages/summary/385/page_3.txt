The paper discusses various methodologies for detecting and classifying brain tumors from MRI images using deep learning techniques, particularly convolutional neural networks (CNNs). Hashemzehi et al. achieved a 96 percent accuracy rate in identifying three types of brain cancers using a hybrid CNN and NADE model on 3064 T1-weighted images. Diaz-Pernas et al. developed a fully automated segmentation and classification algorithm, also utilizing CNN, achieving 97 percent accuracy on the same dataset. Sultan et al. implemented a CNN with 16 layers and obtained a 96 percent accuracy rate. Abd et al. utilized a differential deep-CNN on 25,000 MRI images, achieving an impressive 99.25 percent accuracy during training. Sajja et al. used the VGG16 network on a dataset of 577 tumors, reporting an accuracy of 96.70 percent. Das et al. identified different brain tumors, achieving 94 percent accuracy through a CNN model with variable convolutional filter sizes.

The authors of the current paper propose a methodology that incorporates min-max normalization and data augmentation techniques on a dataset of 3260 brain MRI images. This dataset includes 3064 T1-weighted contrast-enhanced images sourced from Kaggle, covering meningioma, glioma, and pituitary tumors. The images were collected from 233 patients across three planes: sagittal, axial, and coronal.
