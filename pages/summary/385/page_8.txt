The paper presents a comparative analysis of various pre-trained deep learning models for brain tumor classification, focusing on their accuracy and loss metrics. The proposed dense EfficientNet model demonstrated superior performance, achieving a testing loss of 0.0645 and an accuracy of 98.78%. In contrast, other models like ResNet50, MobileNet, and MobileNetV2 showed testing losses of 0.1337, 0.1339, and 0.2452, with accuracies of 96.33%, 96.94%, and 94.80% respectively.

The evaluation of model performance employed multiple metrics including accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score, all assessed using a confusion matrix. The confusion matrix revealed that the proposed model misclassified only four tumors, while ResNet50, MobileNet, and MobileNetV2 misclassified 12, 10, and 15 tumors respectively, indicating that the proposed model had the least misclassification and, therefore, the highest accuracy. Notably, MobileNetV2 performed poorly in identifying pituitary tumors compared to others, whereas all models effectively classified meningioma tumors. The glioma class was identified as the most challenging, leading to the majority of misclassifications.

Further assessment metrics were based on true positives, true negatives, false positives, and false negatives, providing a comprehensive evaluation of each model's performance. Overall, the results highlight the effectiveness of the proposed dense EfficientNet model in brain tumor classification compared to other deep learning techniques.
