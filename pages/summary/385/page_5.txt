The paper presents a novel dense CNN model, combining pre-trained EfficientNetB0 with dense layers to enhance MRI image classification. The EfficientNetB0 architecture consists of 230 layers and 7 MBConv blocks, utilizing a dense block structure that links four layers to optimize feature map generation while minimizing convolution kernel usage. To address the limitation of a small dataset of 3260 brain images, the authors applied data augmentation techniques, including rotation, width-shift, height-shift, and zoom, which amplified the training dataset by 21 times. This approach aimed to improve the model's learning capability, reduce overfitting, and enhance generalization.

The model architecture features alternating dense and drop-out layers, with four dense layers having 720, 360, 360, and 180 neurons, respectively, and drop-out rates of 0.25, 0.25, and 0.5. A pooling layer is included to ensure smooth operation, culminating in a dense layer with four fully connected neurons and a Softmax output layer for class probability scoring.

Experimental results demonstrated the model's effectiveness, achieving 99.97% accuracy on training data and 98.78% on testing data, indicating strong performance in MRI tumor detection. The findings underscore the potential of the proposed dense EfficientNet model in medical image analysis.
