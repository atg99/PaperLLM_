The paper presents experimental evaluations of a proposed dense CNN model aimed at enhancing the accuracy of MRI image analysis. Conducted in a Python environment with GPU support, the study involved preprocessing MRI images through max-min normalization, followed by data augmentation for training purposes. The dense CNN model achieved a remarkable accuracy of 99.97% on training data and 98.78% on testing data, as illustrated in the results.

The experiments were executed over 20 epochs with a batch size of 32 and an image size of 150. Initial validation accuracy was below 0.75, but improved significantly to approximately 0.88 after the first epoch. Concurrently, the initial validation loss decreased from above 0.8 to below 0.4, demonstrating a positive trend in model performance. By the end of the training, validation accuracy approached 97.5%.

Further evaluations were performed using alternative models such as ResNet50, MobileNet, and MobileNetV2, with their results depicted in subsequent figures. Overall, the findings indicate the effectiveness of the proposed dense CNN model in enhancing MRI image analysis accuracy.
