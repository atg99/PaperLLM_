The paper discusses image pre-processing techniques for enhancing the quality of brain tumor MRI images, which often suffer from low illumination and noise. The authors propose a method that utilizes data normalization, specifically min-max normalization, alongside Gaussian and Laplacian filters. Initially, Gaussian blur is applied to the original images, and then a de-blurring process is performed by subtracting a weighted mask from the blurred image. A Laplacian filter with a 3x3 kernel is subsequently used for smoothing the images.

The MRI images, typically appearing as grayscale, require normalization to improve quality and minimize errors in further analysis. The authors reference the work of Nayak et al., who used an L membership function to normalize images within a range of 0 to 1, enhancing the images for better tumor detection.

Furthermore, the paper addresses the challenge of limited dataset size for training deep neural networks, which is crucial for achieving high performance. The dataset consists of 3260 brain images, with 80% allocated for training purposes. To augment the dataset and improve model training, techniques such as rotation, width-shift, height-shift, and zoom are applied, resulting in an increase of training data by 21 times. This data augmentation aims to enhance the model's ability to generalize and reduce overfitting.
