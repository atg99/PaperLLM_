The paper compares the performance of different object detection algorithms, specifically YOLOv2 and SSD, based on various metrics such as accuracy, speed, and architecture. 

YOLOv2, proposed by Redmon and Farhadi in 2017, utilizes the DarkNet backbone and achieves VOC07 results of 78.6 and VOC12 results of 73.5, with a speed of less than 50 FPS. It is highlighted for its high accuracy and speed, capable of detecting over 9000 object categories in real-time. However, a disadvantage noted is its poor performance in detecting small objects.

On the other hand, SSD, introduced by Liu et al. in 2016, employs a VGG16 backbone and performs better with VOC07 results of 76.8 and VOC12 results of 81.5, while maintaining a speed of under 60 FPS. It is recognized as the first accurate and efficient unified detector that effectively combines ideas from region proposal networks and YOLO, allowing for multi-scale detection. Despite its advantages, SSD also struggles with small object detection.

The paper emphasizes the importance of training data sources and provides additional details about the architecture and performance of the detectors, referencing figures and tables for further insights.
