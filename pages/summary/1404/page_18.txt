The paper discusses advancements in object detection frameworks, focusing on various models like YOLO, YOLOv2, SSD, CornerNet, and CenterNet. YOLO (You Only Look Once) innovatively bypasses the region proposal stage used in other models, predicting detections directly from the entire image divided into a grid. This global approach enables real-time performance at 45 FPS, with Fast YOLO achieving 155 FPS. However, YOLO may struggle with localization, especially for small objects, due to its coarse grid division.

YOLOv2 improves upon the original YOLO by utilizing the DarkNet19 architecture, enabling state-of-the-art performance and introducing YOLO9000, capable of detecting over 9000 object categories through joint training on ImageNet and COCO datasets.

SSD (Single Shot Detector) aims to balance speed and accuracy, outpacing YOLO and competing with region-based methods like Faster RCNN. It employs a fully convolutional network with multi-scale feature maps, achieving 74.3% mAP at 59 FPS.

CornerNet challenges the reliance on anchor boxes in detection frameworks, proposing a method that detects paired keypoints for bounding box localization. Although it outperforms previous one-stage detectors with a 42.1% AP on MS COCO, it has a slower inference speed. CenterNet further refines this approach by introducing a central keypoint, raising the AP to 47.0%.

The paper emphasizes the critical role of effective feature representations in object detection, highlighting the evolution from local descriptors to more advanced techniques for improved detection quality.
