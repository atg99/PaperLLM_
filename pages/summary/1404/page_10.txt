The paper discusses various popular databases used for object recognition in computer vision, summarizing key characteristics of each dataset in Table 2. 

1. **PASCAL VOC (2012)**: Contains 11,540 images across 20 categories, with an average of 2.4 objects per image and a resolution of 470x380 pixels. It emphasizes real-world applicability and includes significant intraclass variations and challenging samples.

2. **ImageNet**: Comprises over 14 million images categorized into 21,841 classes. It features a higher number of instances and categories per image compared to PASCAL VOC, making it more challenging and serving as a backbone for the ILSVRC challenge.

3. **MS COCO**: With over 328,000 images and 91 categories, this dataset provides richer object annotations and is designed to be closer to real-world scenarios, including object segmentation data.

4. **Places**: The largest labeled dataset for scene recognition, containing over 10 million images across 434 categories. It includes four subsets that serve as benchmarks for scene recognition tasks.

5. **Open Images**: Features over 9 million images with more than 6,000 categories. It supports large-scale object detection, instance segmentation, and visual relationship detection through comprehensive annotations.

The paper also highlights the differences between these datasets and the newly introduced OICOD, which enhances the number of classes, images, and the complexity of annotations, indicating a shift in the annotation process compared to ILSVRC and MS COCO.
