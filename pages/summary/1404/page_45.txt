The paper presents a summary of various milestone detection frameworks for generic object detection, focusing on their properties and performance. 

1. **RCNN (Girshick et al. 2014)**: Utilizes AlexNet as its backbone and operates with a fixed input image size. It achieved VOC07 results of 58.5 and VOC12 results of 53.3, but operates at a speed of less than 0.1 FPS. This framework was the first to integrate CNN with region proposal (RP) methods, leading to significant performance improvements over previous models. However, it has a multistage pipeline that makes training expensive in terms of time and space, and testing is notably slow.

2. **SPPNet (He et al. 2014)**: Employing ZFNet, this model allows arbitrary image sizes and achieved a VOC07 result of 60.9. It was notable for being the first to introduce Spatial Pyramid Pooling (SPP) into CNN architecture, which enables feature sharing and significantly accelerates evaluation without sacrificing performance. Nonetheless, it inherits the disadvantages of RCNN, such as limited training speedup and challenges in fine-tuning.

3. **Fast RCNN (Girshick 2015)**: This framework uses either AlexNet or VGG16 as backbones and supports arbitrary image sizes. It achieved VOC results of 70.0 (VGG) for 07 and 68.4 for 12, with a speed of under 1 FPS. Fast RCNN is recognized for enabling end-to-end detector training and introducing a Region of Interest (RoI) pooling layer, which makes it faster and more accurate than SPPNet. Despite these advancements, it still faces bottlenecks related to external RP computation and is not fast enough for real-time applications.

Overall, while these frameworks have made significant strides in performance and efficiency, they each carry inherent limitations that affect their practicality for real-time object detection tasks.
