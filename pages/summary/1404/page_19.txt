The paper discusses the evolution and performance of deep learning methods, particularly Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), in the field of image classification and object detection. It highlights the shift from traditional feature engineering, which requires significant domain expertise, to deep learning approaches that automatically learn feature representations directly from raw images. This transition has reduced the dependency on specific knowledge and complex procedures, placing more emphasis on the design of network architectures and training methods.

The paper reviews several leading frameworks, including RCNN, Fast RCNN, Faster RCNN, YOLO, and SSD, noting that advancements in detection accuracy and speed are largely attributed to innovations in CNN architectures. Popular CNN architectures such as AlexNet, ZFNet, VGGNet, GoogLeNet, ResNet, DenseNet, and SENet are examined, showcasing a trend toward deeper networks that enhance representational power. Notably, ResNet introduced skip connections to facilitate training of very deep networks, while DenseNet emphasized parameter efficiency through dense connectivity.

The paper also mentions recent developments in CNN architectures, including InceptionResNets and Squeeze and Excitation blocks, which have achieved significant performance improvements in competitions like ILSVRC. The ongoing research in CNN architectures continues to yield new models, indicating a vibrant and evolving field in computer vision.
