The paper discusses advancements in object detection using convolutional neural networks (CNNs) by explicitly modeling object composition and addressing challenges such as occlusions and image degradations. Early methods combined deformable part models (DPMs) with CNNs to leverage deep features, but without utilizing region proposals. Notable approaches include DeepIDNet, which introduced a deformation constrained pooling layer, DCN with deformable convolution layers, and DPFCN focusing on deformable part-based region of interest (RoI) pooling.

The handling of occlusions is emphasized, as they are prevalent in real-world scenarios leading to information loss. Techniques like deformable RoI pooling and convolution have been proposed to provide flexibility against fixed geometric structures, while adversarial networks have been suggested to generate occluded examples for training. Despite these efforts, the problem of occlusion remains largely unresolved, indicating a potential area for future research.

Image degradation, often caused by poor lighting or low-quality sensors, poses another challenge in visual recognition. The paper notes that most existing evaluation datasets focus on high-quality images, with limited research addressing the impact of degraded images on performance.

Context modeling is highlighted as crucial for object detection, with psychological evidence supporting the role of contextual information in human recognition. Three types of context are identified: semantic, spatial, and scale context. While deep learning approaches implicitly leverage contextual information, there is an opportunity to explore its explicit use in CNN-based detectors. The paper reviews recent studies that utilize global and local contexts, noting that global context can enhance detection by correlating overall scene information with object presence.

Overall, the paper underscores the importance of improving object detection through better modeling of object composition, addressing occlusions and image quality issues, and leveraging contextual information effectively.
