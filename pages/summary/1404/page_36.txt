The paper discusses advancements in object proposal algorithms leveraging Deep Convolutional Neural Networks (DCNNs). It categorizes these methods into two primary types: bounding box proposal methods and object segment proposal methods.

Bounding box proposal methods, exemplified by the Region Proposal Network (RPN) introduced by Ren et al. in 2015, enhance detection speed by predicting object proposals through a sliding window approach on feature maps. RPN integrates with Fast RCNN to form the Faster RCNN pipeline, marking a significant step in end-to-end detection systems. Subsequent methods, like the Adaptive Zoom Network (AZNet) and HyperNet, improve upon RPN by adapting anchor positions and aggregating features from multiple layers, respectively. Other notable methods include DeepProposal and ZIP, which refine proposal generation through cascaded feature processing and multi-depth feature integration.

On the other hand, object segment proposal methods, such as DeepMask and SharpMask, aim to produce more informative segment proposals that facilitate instance segmentation. These methods utilize shared convolutional layers to generate class-agnostic masks and objectness scores, employing a sliding window strategy during inference. Instance-FCN and FastMask further enhance the efficiency of segment proposal generation through fully convolutional architectures and multi-scale feature utilization.

Overall, the paper highlights the evolution and refinement of object proposal techniques in computer vision, emphasizing their significance in improving detection performance and efficiency.
