The paper provides a comparative analysis of various segment proposal methods for object detection, focusing on their performance using different backbone networks. Notable methods discussed include DeepMask, InstanceFCN, SharpMask, and FastMask, all of which utilize the VGG16 or ResNet39 architectures. 

DeepMask is recognized for being the first to generate object mask proposals using deep convolutional neural networks (DCNN), achieving a mean average precision (mAP) of 69.9% with Fast RCNN, but it suffers from slow inference times and requires segmentation annotations for training. InstanceFCN builds on the principles of both DeepMask and fully convolutional networks, introducing instance-sensitive score maps while also needing segmentation annotations. SharpMask enhances feature extraction by employing a top-down refinement module but, like its predecessors, does not share features with the detection network and requires segmentation annotations for training. 

FastMask is noted for its efficiency in generating instance segment proposals in a single shot, leveraging multiscale convolutional features and also requiring segmentation annotations. The results are evaluated based on average recall (AR) metrics and are reported for both box and segment proposals on the COCO dataset.

Overall, each method brings unique contributions to the field, though they share common challenges regarding the need for segmentation annotations and the lack of feature sharing with detection networks.
