The paper discusses various Deep Convolutional Neural Network (DCNN) architectures commonly utilized for generic object detection, highlighting their parameters, layers, test errors, and significant contributions to the field. 

1. **AlexNet** (2012) marked a pivotal shift from hand-crafted features to CNNs, achieving a top-5 error rate of 21.5% and winning the ILSVRC2012 classification competition.
2. **ZFNet** (2014) built on AlexNet's design with adjustments in convolution strides and filter sizes, reducing the error to 21.4%.
3. **OverFeat** (2014) also mirrored AlexNet but varied in convolution parameters, achieving a test error of 21.3%.
4. **VGGNet** (2015) emphasized increased depth through stacked 3x3 filters, reaching a top-5 error rate of 26.8%.
5. **GoogLeNet** (2015) introduced the Inception module with multi-branch convolutions, lowering the error rate to 6.7% and integrating bottleneck structures and global average pooling.
6. **Inception v2** (2015) improved training efficiency through batch normalization, achieving a top-5 error of 4.8%.
7. **Inception v3** (2016) included separable convolutions and spatial resolution reduction, achieving an error rate of 3.6%.
8. **YOLONet** (2016) was inspired by GoogLeNet and tailored for real-time object detection.
9. **ResNet50** (2016) utilized identity mapping to facilitate the training of deeper networks, achieving a test error of 3.6%.

These architectures illustrate the evolution and effectiveness of DCNNs in enhancing object detection capabilities in computer vision.
