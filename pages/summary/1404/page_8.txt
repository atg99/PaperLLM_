Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) are key models in deep learning that leverage fundamental properties of natural signals, such as translation invariance, local connectivity, and compositional hierarchies. A typical CNN architecture includes multiple layers that facilitate the learning of data representations with varying levels of abstraction. The core operations of a CNN consist of convolution with linear filters, application of nonlinearities (like ReLU), and pooling, which collectively reduce the dimensionality of the feature maps while preserving essential information.

The convolution operation involves a 2D kernel applied to the input feature maps, followed by a nonlinear activation function. Pooling layers contribute to downsampling, further refining the representation. A deep CNN (DCNN) architecture, exemplified by VGGNet, consists of numerous convolutional and fully connected layers, where the initial layers focus on extracting low-level features such as edges, and deeper layers capture more complex, abstract features.

DCNNs are advantageous due to their hierarchical structure, ability to learn complex functions, and capacity to automatically derive feature representations from data with minimal prior knowledge. This makes them particularly effective in various computer vision tasks.
