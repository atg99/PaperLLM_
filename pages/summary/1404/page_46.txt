The table presents a comparison of various object detection models, highlighting their key features, performance metrics, and limitations. 

1. **Faster RCNN (Ren et al. 2015)**: Utilizes Region Proposal Networks (RPN) with ZFnet and VGG backbones, achieving VOC07 results of 73.2% and VOC12 results of 70.4%. The model runs at less than 5 FPS and is published in NIPS 2015. Key highlights include the introduction of multiscale anchor boxes, unification of RPN and Fast RCNN, and a significant speed improvement over Fast RCNN. However, training complexity and inability to achieve real-time performance are noted disadvantages.

2. **RCNN (Lenc and Vedaldi 2015)**: Employs ZFNet with Spatial Pyramid Pooling (SPP) and achieves a VOC07 result of 59.7%. The model operates at less than 5 FPS and was presented at BMVC 2015. It replaces selective search with static region proposals, demonstrating that simpler and faster detectors relying solely on CNNs are feasible. However, it struggles with real-time performance and has reduced accuracy due to poor region proposals.

3. **RFCN (Dai et al. 2016)**: This model uses ResNet101 as a backbone and achieves VOC07 results of 80.5% and VOC12 results of 83.6%. Operating at under 10 FPS, it was published at NIPS 2016. RFCN is notable for being a fully convolutional detection network, utilizing position-sensitive score maps for improved accuracy while maintaining a speed advantage over Faster RCNN. Nonetheless, it faces similar training challenges and does not reach real-time speeds.

Overall, while these models show advancements in object detection, they all contend with training complexity and limitations in achieving real-time performance.
