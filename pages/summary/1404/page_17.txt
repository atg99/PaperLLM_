Unified one-stage frameworks have emerged as a solution to the computational inefficiencies of traditional region-based object detection methods, such as Faster RCNN. These unified architectures directly predict class probabilities and bounding box offsets from full images using a single feed-forward convolutional neural network (CNN), eliminating the need for separate region proposal generation and post-classification processes. This end-to-end optimization allows for improved detection performance, particularly on mobile and wearable devices with limited resources.

Early efforts like DetectorNet explored the application of CNNs to object detection, treating it as a regression problem to predict object masks. However, its reliance on multiple networks and image crops limited its scalability and speed. OverFeat, one of the first fully convolutional networks, improved efficiency by processing images of varying sizes through a single forward pass. It utilized multiscale features and offset max pooling to enhance prediction quality and robustness while maintaining speed. Despite its advantages, OverFeat's accuracy was lower than that of RCNN due to challenges in training fully convolutional networks at the time.

YOLO (You Only Look Once) further advanced the concept of unified detection by framing object detection as a regression task from image pixels to bounding boxes and class probabilities. This approach streamlined the detection process, reflecting a shift towards more efficient methods in the field. Overall, unified frameworks represent a significant step forward in the evolution of object detection, balancing speed and accuracy in an increasingly demanding computational landscape.
