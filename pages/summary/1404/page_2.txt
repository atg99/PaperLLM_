This paper discusses the advancements in generic object detection from 2016 to 2018, highlighting the increasing focus on this area within the computer vision community. The authors note that while early efforts primarily targeted single-category detection, recent trends have shifted towards creating systems capable of recognizing a wide array of object categories, akin to human capabilities.

The paper references the landmark work of Krizhevsky et al. in 2012, which introduced AlexNet, a Deep Convolutional Neural Network (DCNN) that significantly improved image classification accuracy. This marked a pivotal moment for deep learning in various computer vision tasks, including generic object detection. Despite the notable progress in the field, the authors argue that there has been a lack of comprehensive surveys focusing specifically on recent developments in generic object detection, particularly post-2012.

The authors compare their survey with previous reviews, noting that while there are numerous surveys on specific object detection types, such as pedestrian and face detection, there is a scarcity of recent surveys addressing generic object detection. They emphasize the profound impact of deep learning on the field, which has enabled models to learn complex representations and achieve breakthroughs across multiple domains.

In summary, the paper aims to provide a thorough overview of recent advancements in generic object detection, capturing the rapid progress made since the advent of deep learning methods and addressing the gap in comprehensive literature on this topic.
