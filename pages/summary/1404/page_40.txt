The paper discusses various strategies for improving object detection in deep convolutional neural networks (DCNNs), focusing on data augmentation, novel training strategies, and reducing localization errors. 

Data augmentation is highlighted as crucial for enhancing visual recognition by artificially expanding the training dataset through transformations like cropping, flipping, and adding noise. While it helps mitigate overfitting and improves generalization, it also significantly increases training time. Some methods involve synthesizing new training images, but ensuring that these images generalize well remains challenging. Researchers have proposed pasting real segmented objects into natural images to enhance datasets, emphasizing the importance of visual context for accurate placement.

Novel training strategies address the detection of small objects, which is particularly challenging due to scale variations. The paper introduces advanced data augmentation techniques such as SNIP and SNIPER, which tackle scale invariance without diminishing the number of training samples. Additionally, the MegDet framework allows for larger minibatch sizes, significantly accelerating training times and achieving success in competitions like the COCO 2017 Detection Challenge.

The paper also addresses the issue of localization error in object detection, which is assessed using Intersection Over Union (IOU) metrics. It points out that bounding boxes often include background pixels, leading to classification inaccuracies. Various methods, including iterative bounding box regression and multi-stage detection networks, aim to refine localization accuracy and reduce errors. Techniques such as Non-Maximum Suppression (NMS) are commonly used to eliminate duplicate detections, though they can sometimes suppress better-localized boxes.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of these strategies in enhancing the performance of object detection systems.
