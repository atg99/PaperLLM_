The paper discusses the training of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) for object detection, emphasizing the importance of large-scale labeled datasets with intraclass diversity. Unlike image classification, detection involves localizing multiple objects within an image. Pretraining a deep model on datasets with object-level annotations, such as ImageNet, has been shown to enhance detection performance. However, the collection of bounding box labels is costly, leading to the common practice of pretraining CNNs on large datasets with image-level labels and then applying them as feature extractors on smaller datasets.

The authors highlight the significance of fine-tuning pretrained networks on specific detection datasets, noting that different layers of the CNN yield varying detection accuracies. For instance, features from FC6 and FC7 layers of AlexNet outperform those from Pool5 in detection tasks. The relationship between source and target datasets also influences performance, as ImageNet-based features perform better for object detection than for human action recognition.

The paper outlines various methods for improving object representation in deep CNN-based detectors like RCNN, Fast RCNN, Faster RCNN, and YOLO. A major challenge in detection is handling objects at varying scales. Traditional approaches involve using image pyramids, which, while improving accuracy, increase inference time and memory usage.

To address scale variations, CNNs inherently create a multiscale feature hierarchy, but different layers present trade-offs between semantic robustness and geometric detail. Techniques such as dilated convolutions have been proposed to enhance feature resolution, albeit at higher computational costs. The authors categorize methods for multiscale object detection into three types: detecting with combined features from multiple layers, detecting at multiple layers, and combinations of both approaches. Notable methods include Hypercolumns, HyperNet, and ION, which utilize features from various layers to improve detection accuracy.
