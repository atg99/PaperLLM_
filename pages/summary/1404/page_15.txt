The paper discusses advancements in object detection frameworks, focusing on SPPNet, Fast RCNN, and Faster RCNN, highlighting their contributions and limitations.

SPPNet, introduced by He et al. in 2014, addresses the bottleneck of CNN feature extraction in the RCNN detection pipeline. By incorporating spatial pyramid pooling, SPPNet allows for the extraction of features of fixed length from region proposals of arbitrary sizes, significantly speeding up the evaluation process without sacrificing detection quality. However, it does not enhance training speed and limits fine-tuning of convolutional layers, affecting the accuracy of deep networks.

Fast RCNN, proposed by Girshick in 2015, builds on the foundations of SPPNet while improving detection speed and quality. It enables end-to-end training by simultaneously learning a softmax classifier and bounding box regression, rather than treating them separately. By introducing a Region of Interest (RoI) pooling layer, Fast RCNN efficiently shares convolutional computations across region proposals, resulting in a notable increase in efficiency—typically three times faster in training and ten times faster in testing compared to its predecessors.

Despite these improvements, Fast RCNN still relies on external region proposals, which presents a new speed bottleneck. To overcome this, Faster RCNN, developed by Ren et al. in 2015 and 2017, leverages the capability of CNNs to localize objects within convolutional layers, further enhancing the detection process. 

Overall, the paper illustrates the progression from SPPNet to Faster RCNN, emphasizing the continual enhancements in speed and detection accuracy within the object detection domain.
