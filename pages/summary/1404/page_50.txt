The paper discusses several limitations and future directions in the field of object detection, highlighting the need for improvements in various areas. Key points include:

1. **Rotation Invariance**: While rotation invariance is beneficial for certain applications, there is a lack of research on this due to the limited rotation variations in popular datasets like PASCAL VOC and COCO. Additionally, occlusion handling remains underexplored for generic object detection, despite advancements in specific areas like face and pedestrian detection.

2. **Context Reasoning**: The importance of contextual information in object detection is emphasized, noting that it can enhance detection, particularly for small or occluded objects. The paper references previous work in both pre-deep learning and deep learning eras and suggests that more effective incorporation of contextual information is necessary.

3. **Detection Proposals**: The paper highlights the significance of detection proposals in reducing search spaces and emphasizes the need for improvements in repeatability, recall, and speed. It points out that new detection proposals should be evaluated in the context of their impact on overall object detection performance.

4. **Other Factors**: Various factors influencing object detection quality are discussed, including data augmentation, training strategies, and error reduction methods. These elements are crucial for improving detection capabilities.

5. **Research Directions**: The paper identifies ongoing challenges such as open-world learning, detection under constrained conditions, and detection across different modalities. It proposes future research directions, including the development of object detection systems that can recognize a wider range of categories in open-world scenarios, and the creation of more efficient detection frameworks that can overcome current limitations.

Overall, despite significant progress in object detection, the field still lags behind human vision in addressing real-world challenges, necessitating further research and development.
