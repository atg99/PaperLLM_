The paper provides a comprehensive survey of deep learning techniques for generic object detection, emphasizing the significant advancements made in the past five years. It begins with an overview of related tutorials and surveys, highlighting the scope and limitations of existing literature in the field. The authors clarify that their focus is on generic object detection, intentionally excluding work on specific object detection types like face or pedestrian detection.

The survey aims to categorize and organize the diverse methodologies in generic object detection, based on popular datasets, evaluation metrics, and detection proposal methods. This categorization is intended to aid researchers in understanding the landscape of current research and identifying future challenges.

The structure of the paper is outlined, starting with a summary of the background and progress in the field over the last two decades, followed by an introduction to deep learning. It proceeds to discuss popular datasets and evaluation criteria, key object detection frameworks, and various sub-problems in object detection.

The paper defines generic object detection as the task of identifying instances of pre-defined object categories within an image, focusing on a wide range of natural categories rather than limited specific categories. It acknowledges the complexity of localizing a vast array of objects, with current research primarily targeting structured objects like cars and faces. The conclusion discusses state-of-the-art performance and future directions for research in object detection.
