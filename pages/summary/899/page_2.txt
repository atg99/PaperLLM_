The paper presents a comprehensive taxonomy of time series data augmentation techniques, categorizing them into basic and advanced approaches across different domains: time, frequency, and time-frequency. Basic augmentation methods include straightforward manipulations in the time domain, such as noise injection, window cropping, and flipping, which directly modify the original time series. Advanced methods are further divided into decomposition-based, model-based, and learning-based approaches, with the latter encompassing techniques like embedding space and deep generative models.

In the time domain, methods such as window cropping and warping allow for the extraction and alteration of segments of time series data. Noise injection introduces variations without altering the labels, enhancing robustness for tasks like anomaly detection. The paper also discusses label expansion as a specific technique to improve anomaly detection performance.

In the frequency domain, the study highlights the potential of using amplitude and phase spectrum perturbations to augment time series data, demonstrating that combining these with time-domain methods can lead to significant performance gains.

The authors conclude with a discussion of future research directions, emphasizing the need for further exploration of augmentation techniques in the time-frequency domain, addressing imbalanced classes, and leveraging deep generative models for more effective data augmentation strategies. Overall, the paper underscores the importance of data augmentation in enhancing the performance of various time series tasks.
