The paper discusses various strategies for augmenting time series data, particularly in the context of addressing class imbalance and enhancing classification performance. It highlights classical methods such as the Synthetic Minority Oversampling Technique (SMOTE) for oversampling minority classes, which can lead to overfitting, and cost-sensitive models that adjust loss functions to account for class imbalance and temporal dependencies. The potential for combining data augmentation with weighting strategies is identified as a promising area for future research.

The paper emphasizes the importance of selecting and combining different data augmentation methods effectively, citing studies that demonstrate improved performance through the combination of basic time-domain methods. It also references the RandAugment technique as a practical approach for combining augmentations in image classification, suggesting similar strategies could be applied to time series data.

Additionally, the paper explores the use of Gaussian Processes (GPs) for time series analysis, noting their ability to model functions with specific properties through kernel selection. Deep Gaussian Processes (DGPs) are mentioned as richer models that have not been extensively studied in this context.

The discussion extends to Deep Generative Models (DGMs), primarily focusing on Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) but also considering the potential of other models such as Deep Autoregressive Networks (DARNs), Normalizing Flows (NFs), and Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) for time series data augmentation.

In conclusion, the paper underscores the growing need for effective data augmentation techniques in the face of limited labeled data for time series tasks. It presents a comprehensive survey of existing methods, categorizes them, and highlights future research directions that could enhance the application of deep learning to time series data.
