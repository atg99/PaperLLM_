In this paper, the authors introduce the Fair-Efficient Network (FEN) as a solution for enabling agents in multi-agent systems to learn both fairness and efficiency. Traditional hierarchical reinforcement learning (RL) models fail to address the simultaneous learning of these two conflicting objectives. FEN tackles this challenge by decomposing fairness for each agent and developing a fair-efficient reward system. Each agent optimizes its own policy while interacting with a controller that manages sub-policies.

The fair-efficient reward is designed to address the common resources that agents compete for, which include CPU, memory, and network bandwidth. The utility of each agent is defined based on the average reward over time, and fairness is measured using the coefficient of variation (CV) of the utilities of all agents. A lower CV indicates a fairer system. The paper highlights that individual agents struggle to optimize CV due to its dependence on the joint policies of all agents. However, the fair-efficient reward allows agents to respond to the collective behavior of others, promoting decentralized learning.

Key components of the fair-efficient reward include a normalization constant and terms that penalize deviations from the average utility, thereby encouraging efficiency and fairness simultaneously. The authors present two propositions: the first confirms that the optimal policy set is Pareto efficient, meaning resources must be fully utilized; the second asserts that equal allocation occurs when resources are fully occupied.

Overall, the proposed FEN framework provides a structured approach to balancing efficiency and fairness in multi-agent systems through a decentralized learning process.
