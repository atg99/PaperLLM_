The paper investigates various strategies for agents to fairly and efficiently share resources in a job scheduling scenario. It compares the performance of several approaches: Avgagents maximize the average reward, Minagents focus on the worst performance, and Min+Avgagents balance both metrics using a regularized maximin fairness criterion. 

The experimental setup involves four agents in a 5x5 grid world with a single resource, where agents receive a reward of 1 for occupying the resource. The results indicate that while Independent agents achieve the highest resource utilization, they exhibit poor fairness. In contrast, FEN demonstrates the best balance, achieving low variability in utility and high minimum utility, albeit with slightly reduced resource utilization compared to Independent agents. 

The Avg approach results in high variability and lower resource utilization due to its collective nature, while Min struggles similarly. The Min+Avg method, designed to balance fairness and resource utilization, performs worse than FEN, highlighting the limitations of these objective terms. Inequity Aversion shows better performance than Independent but still falls short of FEN. 

The findings underscore the effectiveness of the FEN approach in achieving fairness without significantly sacrificing resource utilization, with hyperparameters playing a crucial role in performance outcomes.
