The paper discusses the inherent conflict between fairness and efficiency in multi-agent systems and introduces a hierarchical model to address this issue. The framework consists of a controller that optimizes a fair-efficient reward by switching between various sub-policies that interact with the environment. One sub-policy focuses on maximizing environmental rewards, while others explore diverse behaviors for fairness using information-theoretic rewards. The inclusion of average consensus enables decentralized coordination among agents, allowing them to learn and execute actions based solely on local information without a centralized controller.

Empirical evaluations across three scenarios—job scheduling, the Matthew effect, and manufacturing—demonstrate that FEN achieves both fairness and efficiency, outperforming existing methods. Ablation studies confirm that the hierarchical approach facilitates easier learning for agents. The paper also highlights the broader context of related work on fairness in multi-agent systems, existing multi-agent reinforcement learning (RL) models, and hierarchical RL techniques, noting that most prior approaches prioritize efficiency over fairness. Ultimately, the FEN framework shows promise for real-world applications by enabling fully decentralized learning and execution.
