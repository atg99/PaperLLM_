The cited works collectively explore various dimensions of cooperation, altruism, and learning in multi-agent systems and social dilemmas. Hughes et al. (2018) demonstrate that inequity aversion can enhance cooperation in intertemporal social dilemmas, suggesting that fairness considerations influence collaborative behaviors. Wang et al. (2019) investigate the evolution of intrinsic motivations that drive altruistic behavior among agents, highlighting the potential of these motivations to foster cooperation.

The concept of the Matthew effect is discussed in several studies, including Bol et al. (2018) and Perc (2014), which address how success and resources tend to accumulate with those already in advantageous positions, thereby perpetuating inequality in science funding and other domains. Jiang and Huang (2012) further illustrate this phenomenon in cooperative networked systems, while Zeng and Zuo (2019) analyze its implications in computation contests.

Hierarchical reinforcement learning is another key theme, with foundational works by Sutton et al. (1999) introducing frameworks for temporal abstraction, and later advancements by Bacon et al. (2017) and Nachum et al. (2018) refining hierarchical architectures and learning techniques. Proximal policy optimization algorithms, as detailed by Schulman et al. (2017), represent significant progress in reinforcement learning methodologies.

Overall, these studies contribute to a deeper understanding of the dynamics of cooperation, the impact of fairness and discrimination, and the development of efficient learning strategies in complex multi-agent environments.
