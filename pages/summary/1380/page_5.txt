The section on decentralized training discusses the advantages and challenges associated with centralized versus decentralized policies in multi-agent systems. While centralized policies can effectively coordinate agent behaviors, they become increasingly difficult to manage as the number of agents grows due to the curse of dimensionality. To address this, the paper introduces the Fair-Efficient Network (FEN), which operates under a decentralized framework during both training and execution.

In decentralized training, each agent focuses on its own fair-efficient reward but is still coordinated through an average consensus on utility. To determine its utility deviation, each agent needs to perceive the average utility, which becomes challenging as the number of agents increases. To overcome the communication costs in larger systems, the paper employs a gossip algorithm for distributed average consensus, allowing agents to update their average utility iteratively while requiring minimal communication with neighbors.

Algorithm 1 outlines the training process of FEN, where a controller selects sub-policies to interact with the environment. The selected sub-policy is updated based on the rewards received, and both the controller and sub-policies use Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) for training.

The experiments are designed around three scenarios: job scheduling, the Matthew effect, and a manufacturing plant. Results demonstrate that decentralized optimization of fair-efficient rewards leads to a better balance between efficiency and fairness. The hierarchical structure of the FEN facilitates the learning of both fairness and efficiency. The experiments compare FEN with various baselines, including independent agents and Inequity Aversion agents, highlighting the effectiveness of the proposed decentralized approach.
