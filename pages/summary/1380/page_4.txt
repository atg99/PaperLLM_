The paper discusses a hierarchical framework for addressing the challenges of balancing fairness and efficiency in multi-agent learning systems. It begins by establishing a contradiction related to resource allocation among agents, suggesting that resources will eventually be occupied by other agents, leading to a scenario where the initial optimality condition is violated.

To tackle the ambiguity in learning policies that must reconcile fairness and efficiency, the authors propose a hierarchical architecture consisting of a controller and multiple sub-policies. The controller selects one sub-policy based on partial observations and operates at a lower temporal resolution compared to the sub-policies, which act more frequently to interact with the environment. 

One of the sub-policies is dedicated to maximizing the environmental reward, thereby focusing on efficiency, while the others are designed to explore diverse behaviors to promote fairness through an information-theoretic objective. The paper emphasizes the importance of mutual information and entropy in guiding the learning process of sub-policies, aiming to minimize uncertainty about their differences and enhance exploration.

The overall goal of this hierarchical method is to simplify the learning process for both efficiency and fairness. The controller learns to optimize the fair-efficient reward over time, while sub-policies concentrate on their specific objectives within a defined time frame. This approach allows for a more strategic allocation of resources and decision-making in multi-agent environments.
