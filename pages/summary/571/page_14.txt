The paper discusses the selection of hyperparameters for different convolutional neural networks (CNNs) applied to EEG data, focusing on the EEGNet model. It highlights that the choice of the activation function, specifically ELU, and a learning rate of 1 were crucial for EEGNet's performance, contrasting with other CNNs that utilized leaky ReLU and a learning rate of 0.1. The study found no significant differences in training duration or loss function selection.

Classifications were evaluated for both intra-subject and inter-subject approaches, with results indicating significant differences among various classification methods for words and vowels. However, the method of hyperparameter selection did not significantly influence outcomes. The paper presents performance metrics where the FBCSP-trained rLDA outperformed SVM and RdF classifiers, achieving higher accuracies for both imagined words and vowels. The CNNs demonstrated superior performance compared to benchmark methods, with EEGNet achieving the highest mean accuracy of 24.90% for imagined words. Overall, the findings underscore the effectiveness of specific hyperparameter configurations and the advantages of CNNs in EEG classification tasks.
