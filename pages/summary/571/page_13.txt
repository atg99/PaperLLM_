The study presents a detailed analysis of hyperparameter (HP) selection for various convolutional neural networks (CNNs) using both intra-subject and inter-subject methods. For intra-subject training, ReLU and leaky ReLU were predominantly chosen as activation functions for the shallow CNN, while leaky ReLU was favored for the deep CNN. In contrast, the EEGNet primarily utilized the ELU activation function. The selection of learning rates varied significantly; the shallow CNN most commonly selected 0.01, whereas the deep CNN preferred 0.1. The EEGNet showed a distinct preference for a learning rate of 1. Regarding training epochs, both deep CNN and EEGNet showed similar preferences, with the shallow CNN often opting for more epochs. The choice of loss functions revealed minor preferences, with the deep CNN displaying no significant bias.

In the inter-subject analysis, the shallow and deep CNNs exhibited nearly identical hyperparameter selections, including leaky ReLU for activation, a learning rate of 0.1, and 60 training epochs. An exception arose with the shallow CNN using the NLL loss function during imagined word training, although this was deemed a non-critical variation due to insignificant differences found in prior statistical analysis. Despite achieving higher mean validation accuracies with a learning rate of 0.01, the optimal hyperparameter combination selected was 0.1. 

Overall, the findings highlight the distinct preferences in hyperparameter selection between different CNN architectures and training methods, providing insights for optimizing model performance in various applications.
