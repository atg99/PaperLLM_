The paper reviews the use of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) in various studies focusing on hyperparameter (HP) optimization, noting a significant lack of thorough analysis in most cases. Nine studies employed CNNs, but many only provided partial results regarding HPs, with some omitting important aspects like learning rate optimization. The review highlights the absence of statistical analysis and comparisons of HPs across studies, indicating a general trend of inadequate reporting in the literature.

In response, the current study aims to investigate three key objectives: to assess whether deep learning (DL) methods outperform traditional machine learning (ML) benchmarks, to conduct a robust statistical analysis of HP optimization to identify the most effective HPs, and to explore the feasibility of generalizing HPs across subjects without compromising performance.

The research utilizes an EEG dataset from participants performing imagined speech tasks in Spanish, comparing the performance of three specifically designed CNNs against three traditional ML classifiers. A nested cross-validation (nCV) method is applied for HP optimization, allowing for a detailed analysis of the effects of HP values on CNN performance. The study tests two HP optimization modes: an intra-subject approach with subject-specific HPs and an inter-subject approach with a uniform set of HPs for all subjects.

The methodology, results, and discussion presented in the paper aim to evaluate the effectiveness of ML and DL approaches in decoding imagined speech and underscore the significance of HP optimization across various algorithms.
