The study highlighted the challenges of small sample sizes, which may lead to overfitting and limit the generalizability of the models. It emphasized the need for larger datasets to improve CNN performance and suggested that increasing the number of EEG channels could enhance spatial resolution and decoding accuracy.

The authors acknowledged limitations in their experimental paradigm, such as the inability to analyze the differential effects of stimuli presented simultaneously and the lack of consideration for user training effects on BCI performance. They recommended that future studies incorporate multi-session protocols and neurofeedback techniques to enhance user interaction with the system and improve overall BCI outcomes.

In conclusion, the study demonstrated that proper HP selection is crucial for optimizing CNN architectures in EEG decoding tasks. The results showed that CNNs outperformed traditional classifiers, with significant interactions between HPs influencing performance. The findings underscore the importance of addressing data limitations and exploring user training strategies in the development of effective brain-computer interfaces.
