The paper discusses the architecture and optimization methods for deep convolutional neural networks (CNNs) applied to electroencephalogram (EEG) data analysis. It highlights two types of CNNs: a deep CNN that utilizes multiple convolutional layers for temporal and spatial filtering, followed by dropout, batch normalization, ELU activation, and max-pooling layers, culminating in a softmax layer for classification. The EEGNet is designed to be an effective cross-paradigm classifier, emphasizing a compact architecture suitable for limited datasets, which is common in EEG research. It employs depthwise and separable convolutions to mimic traditional EEG feature extraction techniques, complemented by regularization methods such as batch normalization and dropout, and concludes with a softmax layer for classification.

The paper also details a method for optimizing hyperparameters using a nested cross-validation (nCV) approach. Unlike typical practices in deep learning, where cross-validation is often neglected, nCV is tailored for deep learning validation to prevent overfitting, especially in the context of smaller datasets found in brain-computer interfaces (BCIs). The nCV method involves splitting the dataset into k folds, using one for testing and the others for training and validation, and iterating to determine the optimal hyperparameters based on mean validation accuracy. The final model is then trained and tested using the optimized hyperparameters, with performance reported as the mean classification accuracy across the outer folds. In this study, a 4-fold nCV was implemented, ensuring a robust assessment of model performance.
