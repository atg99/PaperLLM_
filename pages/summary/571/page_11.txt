This section of the paper discusses the impact of hyperparameters on the performance of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) when analyzing datasets of imagined words and vowels. The study evaluates different activation functions, revealing that the leaky ReLU activation function outperformed others, achieving mean inner-fold validation accuracies of 40.96% for words and 44.46% for vowels. Statistical analysis via 2-way ANOVA confirmed the significant effect of activation function on performance, with interactions between the activation function and CNN type also being significant.

Tukey post-hoc tests indicated that leaky ReLU's performance was significantly better than the other functions, particularly in comparison to the ReLU function, which yielded the lowest accuracies. The paper notes that variations in loss functions did not significantly affect performance for either dataset, leading to the conclusion that no further post-hoc analysis was necessary. Overall, the findings emphasize the importance of selecting appropriate activation functions in optimizing CNNs for specific tasks.
