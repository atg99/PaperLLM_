The paper discusses the implementation of various classification techniques for decoding EEG signals, specifically focusing on feature extraction and optimization of hyperparameters (HPs). A feature vector was constructed using the RWE of different decomposition levels, resulting in a 30-element vector for each trial. The study retained methods from an original study, including the use of linear SVM and random forest (RdF) classifiers, while employing a different approach to cross-validation and HP optimization to enhance classification performance, particularly for convolutional neural networks (CNNs).

The regularized linear discriminant analysis (rLDA) is highlighted as a classification method that addresses issues related to high-dimensional data by reducing the dispersion of eigenvalues. Additionally, the filter bank common spatial patterns (FBCSP) method is described as a prominent approach for feature extraction in brain-computer interface (BCI) applications, capable of improving the discrimination of band power features. The FBCSP method involves multiple stages, including the use of bandpass filters, spatial filtering, feature selection, and classification.

The paper also introduces a shallow CNN designed for decoding band power features from EEG data. The architecture mimics the FBCSP processing stages through temporal and spatial convolutions, followed by transformations that replicate the feature extraction processes of FBCSP. Regularization techniques, such as batch normalization and dropout, are implemented to enhance model performance. The focus on optimizing three specific HPs—number of spatial filters, mutual information quantization level, and number of features per trial—demonstrates a systematic approach to improving classification accuracy in EEG signal decoding.
