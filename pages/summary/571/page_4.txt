The study investigates the classification of imagined speech using EEG signals recorded from subjects during tasks involving both vowel and word production. Participants, averaging 25 years old, completed trials for each vowel and word 50 times, with a structure that included a stimulus presentation and resting intervals. The EEG data were obtained using a specific electrode placement system and filtered to isolate the relevant frequency range. 

Independent Components Analysis (ICA) was utilized for artifact detection, particularly focusing on eye-blink artifacts, and the data were downsampled to enhance computational efficiency. The classification process involved training six classifiers, encompassing both traditional machine learning methods and deep learning architectures, to evaluate their performance on the EEG data. 

For the machine learning classifiers, features were extracted using discrete wavelet transforms, focusing on relative wavelet energy from various decomposition levels. This approach aimed to replicate findings from previous research, ensuring a consistent methodology in analyzing imagined speech signals.
