The study investigates the impact of hyperparameter (HP) variations on the performance of multiple convolutional neural networks (CNNs) in classifying imagined words and vowels. It compares the classification accuracies of CNNs with traditional machine learning (ML) methods, specifically support vector machines (SVM), random forests (RdF), and regularized linear discriminant analysis (rLDA). The results indicate that the CNNs significantly outperformed the benchmark methods, with mean accuracies for imagined vowels ranging from 28.95% to 30.25% under both intra- and inter-subject optimization. Statistical analysis confirmed the significance of these results, particularly using Tukey post-hoc tests (p < 1 × 10–6).

The study highlights the importance of HP selection, noting that variations in HP values significantly affected performance across different CNN architectures, except for the loss function. Leaky ReLU emerged as the most effective activation function for most cases, while ELU was optimal for the EEGNet architecture. Overall, the findings underscore the potential of CNNs in enhancing classification tasks within the context of imagined speech, emphasizing the necessity for careful HP optimization.
