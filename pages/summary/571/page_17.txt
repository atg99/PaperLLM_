The study explores the application of deep learning (DL) methods, particularly convolutional neural networks (CNNs), for decoding imagined speech from EEG signals. It emphasizes the necessity of optimizing hyperparameters (HPs) to enhance model robustness, despite finding no significant differences between inter-subject and intra-subject HP selection for classifying words and vowels. The CNN architectures outperformed benchmark classifiers, achieving significant classification accuracies, with improvements ranging from 3.78% to 8.02% compared to traditional machine learning methods. 

The results indicate that different CNN architectures can effectively decode imagined speech EEG, achieving mean accuracies of 24.90% for words and 30.25% for vowels, despite variations in HP optimization and model complexity. The study highlights the computational efficiency of the compact EEGNet compared to deeper CNNs and traditional methods, supporting CNNs as viable tools for imagined speech decoding.

However, the authors caution that the current performance levels are insufficient for practical use in a direct brain-computer interface (BCI) system. They suggest that future research could focus on increasing trial numbers, enhancing recording methods, and investigating the neurolinguistic properties of speech. While the findings show potential for decoding a broader range of words and vowels, the limitations of the dataset size and classification accuracy are acknowledged, indicating that the field requires further development to reach practical application stages.
