The paper discusses the performance of various Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) in terms of inner-fold validation accuracy based on different hyperparameters, specifically learning rates and the number of epochs. 

Figure 4c illustrates that the shallow and deep CNNs achieve peak performance at a learning rate of 0.01, while the EEGNet shows a continuous increase in mean accuracy with increasing learning rates. Statistical analysis using 2-way ANOVA confirmed significant divergences in performance based on learning rates for both words and vowels, with F-values indicating strong significance. Tukey post-hoc tests revealed substantial differences among the combinations of learning rates, except for the comparison between 0.01 and 0.1.

Figure 4d presents the impact of varying the number of training epochs. The EEGNet's validation accuracy does not improve significantly beyond 20 epochs, whereas shallow and deep CNNs continue to show improvements up to 60 epochs before leveling off. The 2-way ANOVA indicated that the number of epochs significantly affects performance across both datasets, with interactions between epochs and CNN architectures also being significant. Tukey tests confirmed significant differences in performance between 20 and 60 epochs, while differences between 60 and 80 epochs were not significant for either words or vowels.

Overall, the findings highlight the critical influence of learning rate and the number of epochs on CNN performance in imagined speech datasets.
