The paper investigates the impact of various hyperparameters (HPs) on the performance of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) applied to EEG data classification. It evaluates four activation functions: deep CNN, EEGNet, squaring non-linearity from shallow CNN, ReLU, and leaky ReLU, with the latter two adding non-linearity and sparsity. The learning rate is identified as a crucial factor, with values of 0.001, 0.01, 0.1, and 1.0 tested, as it influences model convergence. The number of epochs for training (20, 40, 60, and 80) is also examined to assess differences in convergence across architectures. Two loss functions, negative log-likelihood (NLL) and cross-entropy (CE), are evaluated based on their use in prior studies.

In total, 128 HP combinations are tested across CNN architectures and datasets, with training conducted on an intra- and inter-subject basis using a batch size of 64. The softmax function is employed to convert output values into conditional probabilities, facilitating loss computation. The ADAM optimizer is uniformly applied across networks to maintain consistency. The paper also outlines a statistical analysis using repeated-measures ANOVA to assess the significance of variations in HP values on validation accuracy, allowing for a structured comparison of the CNNs. The results indicate varying numbers of trainable parameters across architectures, with EEGNet having the least.
