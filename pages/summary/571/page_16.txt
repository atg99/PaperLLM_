in this study emphasizes the significance of hyperparameter (HP) selection in the construction and training of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for classifying imagined speech EEG data. The research examined various HPs, including activation functions, learning rates, and the number of training epochs, and compared the classification accuracies achieved by CNNs against traditional machine learning methods.

Key findings reveal that the choice of HPs significantly influences performance across different CNN architectures. Notably, leaky ReLU emerged as the most effective activation function for most scenarios, while EEGNet showed optimal performance with ELU. Smaller learning rates were generally more effective for shallow and deep CNNs, whereas EEGNet performed best with a learning rate of 1. The analysis also highlighted the importance of the interaction between HPs and network architectures, as different combinations yielded varying results in validation accuracy.

The study underscores that while traditional machine learning methods exhibited less sensitivity to HP optimization, CNNs demonstrated a high level of significance in their HP interactions. The findings provide valuable insights and benchmarks for future research on classification tasks using CNNs, emphasizing the need for meticulous HP optimization to enhance model performance effectively.
