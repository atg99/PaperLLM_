The study investigates the effectiveness of deep learning (DL) approaches in decoding imagined speech from electroencephalography (EEG) signals within the context of direct speech brain-computer interfaces (DS-BCIs). The classification performance of three convolutional neural networks (CNNs) was found to be comparable and statistically insignificant. Additionally, optimizing classifiers using intra- and inter-subject methods did not yield significant effects. The implementation of nested cross-validation (nCV) was noted to enhance the robustness of the training process, particularly when working with small datasets. A significant finding was the impact of hyperparameter optimization (HP) on the results. This research contributes to the advancement of DS-BCIs by showcasing the potential of DL in decoding imagined speech and highlights the importance of HP optimization for improved outcomes. 

Supplementary materials include tables detailing trainable parameters, validation accuracies, and average precision scores for both benchmark and CNN classifiers. The authors, C.C., R.F., and D.C., contributed to various aspects of the study, including conceptualization, methodology, and writing.
