The paper discusses various convolutional neural network (CNN) architectures designed for decoding electroencephalogram (EEG) signals, specifically highlighting shallow CNNs, deep CNNs, and EEGNet. The shallow CNN processes EEG signals through a temporal convolution layer followed by a spatial convolution layer. The deep CNN builds upon this initial structure by incorporating three additional convolution blocks, allowing it to extract a broader range of features from the EEG data without being limited to specific types. It has been effective in learning spectral amplitude changes and has shown sensitivity to phase features in earlier layers and amplitude features in later layers. The deep CNN demonstrates its versatility as a decoding tool across multiple brain-computer interface (BCI) and EEG paradigms, effectively applied to both imagined and executed tasks, as well as normal and pathological EEG, where it slightly outperformed the shallow CNN. Additionally, its application in error decoding within an online BCI control paradigm has shown it to outperform benchmark classifiers. The EEGNet architecture features a 2D convolution layer and incorporates depthwise and separable convolutions, further enhancing the capability of these networks in EEG analysis.
