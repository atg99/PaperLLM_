This paper evaluates the effectiveness of hyperparameter optimization in machine learning and deep learning methods for decoding imagined speech from electroencephalography (EEG) signals. The study addresses the need for improved classification techniques in the context of direct-speech brain-computer interfaces (DS-BCIs), which allow users to communicate using brain activity alone. The authors emphasize that while deep learning has shown success in various domains, its advantages over traditional machine learning approaches for imagined speech classification remain unclear.

To investigate this, the researchers trained three convolutional neural networks (CNNs) specifically designed for EEG decoding, utilizing a nested cross-validation approach for hyperparameter optimization. They compared the performance of these CNNs with three benchmark machine learning methods: Support Vector Machine, Random Forest, and regularized Linear Discriminant Analysis. The study involved a dataset containing both words and vowels, enabling independent training on each set.

Results indicated that the CNNs achieved significantly higher accuracies than the benchmark methods for both words (24.97%) and vowels (30%), with statistical significance established (p<1×10–7). Additionally, the effects of varying hyperparameter values and their interactions with the CNNs were found to be statistically significant, highlighting the critical role of hyperparameter optimization in training CNNs for decoding imagined speech.

In summary, the findings suggest that deep learning methods, particularly when optimized for hyperparameters, can substantially enhance the classification of imagined speech EEG signals, thus advancing the development of effective DS-BCIs.
