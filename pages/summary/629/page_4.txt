The study evaluates the performance of a multimodal artificial intelligence (MMAI) prognostic model compared to the National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) model in predicting outcomes for prostate cancer patients. An ablation study was conducted to assess the contribution of different data components, revealing that the full model, which included pathology images and six clinical variables (age, Gleason primary, Gleason secondary, combined Gleason score, T-stage, and baseline PSA), provided the best results.

The MMAI model demonstrated superior discrimination across various outcomes, including 5-year distant metastasis (AUC 0.84 vs 0.74), 5-year biochemical failure (AUC 0.67 vs 0.59), 10-year prostate cancer-specific survival (AUC 0.77 vs 0.68), and 10-year overall survival (AUC 0.65 vs 0.59), with all comparisons showing p-values less than 0.001.

Further validation was performed after excluding patients with post-treatment prostate tissue, reinforcing the MMAI model's advantages (5-year distant metastasis AUC 0.83 vs 0.72, 5-year biochemical failure AUC 0.69 vs 0.61, etc.). The results indicate that the MMAI model consistently outperformed the NCCN model, suggesting its potential as a more effective tool for predicting clinical outcomes in prostate cancer.
