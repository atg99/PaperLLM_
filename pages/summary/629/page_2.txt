The paper discusses the limitations of existing prognostic models for localized prostate cancer, which lack validation in prospective randomized clinical trials and have seen minimal adoption outside the United States. These models, primarily developed from cohort data, are subject to biases and often fail to provide accurate long-term outcomes. There is a pressing need for improved tools that can personalize prostate cancer therapy.

Artificial intelligence (AI) is highlighted as a promising solution, showing potential in various medical applications, including cancer therapy. With ongoing advancements in digital histopathology, AI can analyze large datasets, making it more accessible and cost-effective compared to traditional genomic biomarkers. The study introduces a multimodal AI (MMAI) system designed to enhance prognostication in localized prostate cancer by utilizing data from five NRG Oncology phase III randomized clinical trials.

The MMAI architecture incorporates both clinical variables and digitized histopathology images, utilizing a self-supervised learning approach to optimize feature extraction and improve predictive accuracy. The system was trained on a substantial dataset, including over 16,200 histopathology slides and clinical data from 5,654 patients, to predict various outcomes such as distant metastasis and overall survival at different time points.

Results indicate that the MMAI model consistently outperformed traditional NCCN risk groups in predicting outcomes, demonstrating its potential for broader clinical application. The model's architecture involved two separate machine learning pipelines for processing clinical and image data, ultimately leading to more accurate risk assessments for patients with localized prostate cancer.
