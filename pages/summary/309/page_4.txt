The paper discusses the challenges and advancements in improving subject-specific text classification using BERT and other contextual text models. Traditional text embedding models treat each word as a fixed vector, leading to issues with ambiguity due to polysemous words that carry different meanings based on context. Additionally, the efficiency of classification often relies on neural network architectures tailored for specific subject areas, necessitating intensive manual efforts for model training.

To address these challenges, contextual models like CoVe and ELMo have been developed, which provide dynamic vector representations based on the surrounding context of each token, significantly enhancing text model efficiency. Recent research has focused on creating universal text models, such as ULM-FiT, OpenAI GPT, and BERT, pre-trained on large corpora of general texts. BERT, in particular, demonstrates superior efficiency; however, its full potential remains untapped, prompting research into methods for retraining BERT for specific tasks, including long text sequences and small text sets, as well as methodologies for unsupervised learning on unlabeled corpora.

The paper also highlights the significance of text annotation, which involves automatically selecting key phrases and sentences to represent the content of a text. While manual annotation has been effective for small data sets, the increasing volume of information necessitates automated approaches. There are two main annotation strategies: extractive, which selects existing text segments, and abstractive, which generates new summaries. Although abstractive methods are desirable for their human-like processing, they require substantial computational resources and time due to complex algorithms and deep learning models.
