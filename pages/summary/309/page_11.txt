The paper discusses the training and performance of the SciBERT model in comparison to the original BERT model and BioBERT. It highlights that training the full BERT model on a 16-core TPU takes approximately 4 days, while it could take 40-70 days on an 8-core setup. SciBERT was evaluated on various tasks related to scientific text processing, utilizing datasets from biomedicine, computer science, and interdisciplinary fields. Results showed that SciBERT outperformed both BERT and BioBERT, despite being trained on a smaller biomedical corpus. The authors emphasize the benefits of additional training on specific tasks and the use of domain-specific vocabularies for better text tokenization.

The paper also investigates BERT's robustness, noting that the model's architecture and training methodology may not be optimal. I. Liu and colleagues expanded the training corpus significantly by incorporating additional public datasets, totaling around 160 GB compared to the original 16 GB from BookCorpus and Wikipedia. Their findings indicate that the BERT model is undertrained, as improvements were observed across multiple benchmarks (GLUE, SQuAD, RACE) after this enhanced training. The study further explores the impact of training modifications, particularly the difference between static and dynamic text masking during the training process, with dynamic masking proving to enhance the model's robustness and efficiency.
