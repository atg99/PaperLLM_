The paper discusses the advancements in interlanguage representations and cross-language models, emphasizing the role of embeddings and orthogonal transformations in equalizing word distributions across multiple languages. It highlights the reduction or elimination of the need for teacher-guided training on parallel text pairs, addressing the "zero-shot" problem where models can perform tasks in unseen language pairs post-training.

A notable contribution in this area is a machine translation model utilizing a simple LSTM encoder-decoder architecture, which excels in translating rare language pairs. This model, based on extensive training with over 200 million parallel sentences, serves as a foundation for creating interlanguage textual representations.

Recent unsupervised learning approaches allow for the alignment of sentence representations without supervision. One study details an LSTM model that employs shared memory cell weights while using distinct lookup tables for different languages, yielding promising results in word translation tasks.

The paper also delves into cross-language model training, focusing on three tasks: conditional linguistic modeling, masked language modeling (MLM), and text translation modeling. These tasks involve both unsupervised and supervised learning on parallel texts, with MLM being particularly effective for improving text alignment across languages.

To evaluate the quality of interlanguage models, the XLNI dataset is utilized, encompassing texts in 15 diverse languages. The findings indicate that unsupervised multilingual learning significantly enhances the generation of reliable cross-language representations, with MLM training notably improving machine translation quality by 1.3% on average compared to existing methods.
