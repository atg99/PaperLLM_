The paper discusses the implementation of BERTScore, noting its increased accuracy in assessing text similarity compared to traditional metrics like SacreBLEU, albeit at a higher computational cost. Despite this slowdown, the impact on machine learning performance is minimal due to the nature of natural text processing datasets. It highlights ongoing improvements in BERTScore through parallelization and the potential for specialized models that enhance accuracy in specific subject areas. The differentiability of BERTScore is also emphasized, suggesting future integration into text model training methodologies.

Furthermore, the paper addresses vulnerabilities in machine learning models to adversarial examples—inputs that appear normal to humans but lead to incorrect model outputs. It underscores the need for adversarial examples in natural language processing to maintain human interpretability, semantic similarity, and linguistic correctness. Current methods for generating such examples include misspellings and phrase alterations, but there is a call for more sophisticated techniques.

The proposed method for generating adversarial examples is formalized, focusing on maintaining syntactic and semantic similarity while adhering to a black-box condition, where the model's internal workings are unknown. The algorithm TEXTFOOLER is introduced as a means to create adversarial examples for black-box models like BERT. It involves ranking the importance of words based on their influence on model predictions and transforming these crucial words while ensuring they meet the criteria of semantic similarity and syntactical appropriateness, thus enabling misclassification.
