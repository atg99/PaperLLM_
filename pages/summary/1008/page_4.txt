Spatial maps, which compile data from multiple sensors at a specific time, can be represented as 2-dimensional matrices where each entry corresponds to a specific sensor's reading. This representation is useful for visualizing spatial distributions of data, such as traffic speeds across a network.

ST raster data, encompassing measurements across various locations and time stamps, is often represented as 3-dimensional tensors. This allows for the analysis of temporal changes across the spatial landscape, facilitating complex data mining tasks.

The paper emphasizes the diverse types of ST data instances—points, trajectories, time series, spatial maps, and ST raster—and their corresponding representations, which are crucial for effective data mining and analysis using deep learning techniques. The choice of representation is influenced by the specific data mining task and the deep learning model being employed. Overall, the study aims to provide a comprehensive framework for understanding the complexities of spatiotemporal data and its applications in various domains.
