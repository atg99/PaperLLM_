This paper presents a comprehensive framework for utilizing deep learning (DL) models in spatiotemporal data mining (STDM). It outlines a general pipeline that begins with raw spatiotemporal (ST) data, which includes various forms such as event data, trajectory data, point reference data, and raster data. The process involves data preprocessing, followed by representation through sequences, matrices, tensors, or graphs.

Different DL models are selected based on the type of data being processed. Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) are predominantly used for spatial maps and raster data, while Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs), including Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks and Gated Recurrent Units (GRUs), excel in handling trajectories and time series. Hybrid models, such as ConvLSTM, combine the strengths of CNNs and RNNs to capture both spatial features and temporal correlations. The paper also mentions other models like Autoencoders (AE), Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), and deep reinforcement learning as emerging tools in STDM.

The paper categorizes various STDM tasks that these models address, including classification, predictive learning, representation learning, and anomaly detection. It highlights that predictive learning is the most prevalent focus, comprising over 70% of the related literature, as it relies heavily on effectively learned features from the data. The second most common task is representation learning, which seeks to derive features from ST data in an unsupervised or semi-supervised manner.

The section on predictive learning explains that the goal is to forecast future observations based on historical ST data, with various applications leading to different formulations of predictive problems. The paper emphasizes the adaptability of deep learning models to different types of ST data, showcasing their efficacy in solving complex data mining challenges. The paper concludes with a plan to delve deeper into specific STDM problems and the corresponding DL solutions in subsequent sections.
