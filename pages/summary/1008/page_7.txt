It consists of several key steps: constructing ST data instances from raw data collected from sensors, representing these instances in a format suitable for deep learning, selecting and designing appropriate models, and finally applying these models to tackle specific STDM challenges such as prediction and classification.

ST data preprocessing is crucial as it transforms raw data into suitable formats, including vectors, matrices, or tensors, depending on the model requirements. Different types of ST data, such as trajectory and time series data, are typically represented as sequence data, while spatial maps can be modeled as 2D matrices. The framework also notes that while certain representations are common, there are instances where alternative representations may be employed to better capture specific features, such as using CNNs on trajectory data represented as matrices.

The selection and design of deep learning models are tailored to the data representation. For sequence data, models like RNN, LSTM, and GRU are preferred due to their capability to handle temporal correlations. The Seq2Seq model is highlighted for tasks like multi-step traffic prediction. For graph data, GraphCNN is utilized, capturing spatial correlations among nodes. CNN is typically employed for single matrix inputs, while tensor data may be processed using 3D-CNN or a combination with RNN models.

In summary, the paper presents a structured framework for using deep learning in STDM, emphasizing the importance of data representation and model selection to effectively address various analytical tasks.
