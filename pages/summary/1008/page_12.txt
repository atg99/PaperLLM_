The CCNN model is highlighted for its ability to integrate diverse functional connectivity metrics, making it adaptable for various connectome-based classification and regression tasks. Additionally, 3D convolutional neural networks (3D-CNNs) are employed to directly analyze 3D structural MRI images, facilitating effective classification without the need for manual feature extraction.

The paper further examines the estimation and inference of ST data, focusing on spatial maps and trajectories. It emphasizes the challenges of inferring urban air quality due to limited monitoring stations, with the ADAIN model proposed to address this by combining feedforward and recurrent neural networks for improved feature interaction learning. The use of deep neural networks for precipitation estimation from infrared cloud images is also discussed.

For trajectory data, the paper reviews models like DEEPTRA VEL and DeepTTE, which estimate travel times using historical mobility data. Graph convolutional networks (GCNs) are utilized to infer user activity types from GPS trajectory data, while a recurrent neural network model, TULER, tackles trajectory-user linking in location-based social networks.

Anomaly detection within ST data is explored, focusing on events and spatial maps. Various studies utilize CNNs and other deep learning methods to detect non-recurring traffic congestion, traffic accidents from social media, and even predict traffic accident risk based on human mobility data. The paper concludes with the application of deep learning for detecting extreme climate events, showcasing the versatility and effectiveness of these methodologies across different domains of ST data analysis.
