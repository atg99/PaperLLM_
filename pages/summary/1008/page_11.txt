The paper highlights the use of various deep learning models, particularly convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and recurrent neural networks (RNNs), in analyzing spatiotemporal (ST) data, including taxi trajectories, traffic flow predictions, and mobility patterns in urban environments.

The authors detail the representation of ST raster data as matrices or tensors, allowing for predictions through 2D-CNN and 3D-CNN models. The research further explores how these models apply to different scenarios, such as storm tracking and vehicle flow prediction across city regions. The distinction between ST rasters and spatial maps is emphasized, noting that ST rasters incorporate multiple time slots, while spatial maps reflect a single time slot.

In representation learning, the paper discusses trajectory analysis within location-based social networks (LBSNs) and how RNNs and CNNs are utilized to derive meaningful representations from trajectory data. The integration of social network dynamics with mobility data is also addressed, showcasing models that combine these aspects for enhanced representation learning.

The classification of brain imaging data, particularly fMRI, is explored as a significant area of study within neuroscience. Various deep learning approaches, including LSTMs and CNNs, are employed for classifying conditions such as autism spectrum disorders (ASD) based on fMRI time-series data and functional connectivity matrices. The paper underscores the growing importance of combining deep learning techniques with ST data analysis for applications across various domains, including urban planning, transportation, and neuroscience research.
