This paper discusses various deep learning (DL) models applied to four types of spatio-temporal (ST) data: trajectories, time series, spatial maps, and ST raster data. The models include Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), Graph CNNs, Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) with Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) and Gated Recurrent Units (GRUs), and hybrid approaches. 

For crime prediction, models like CNNs and GRUs have been adapted to transform raw crime data into image-like heat maps and predict future incidents. Similarly, ConvLSTM has been utilized for traffic accident predictions by modeling accident counts as 3-D tensors. 

In time series analysis, several DL models have been tested for traffic flow prediction. Stacked autoencoders and Deep Belief Networks (DBNs) have been employed to forecast traffic flow based on historical data. RNNs, particularly LSTMs, are commonly used for time series predictions, integrating various contextual factors such as geographical data and social events. Additionally, LSTMs have been applied in fMRI data analysis for diagnosing conditions like autism spectrum disorders.

The paper emphasizes the challenge of incorporating spatial correlations in time series data, suggesting that while temporal models are effective, they often overlook spatial dynamics. Overall, the research highlights the versatility of deep learning in processing and predicting outcomes from complex ST data across various applications.
