In the context of semantic segmentation on ADE 20K, the authors employed AdamW as the optimizer with a specified input resolution and batch size. They conducted extensive hyperparameter tuning for different Vision Transformer (ViT) models, including ViT-B and ViT-L, adjusting learning rates, layer-wise decay rates, and drop path rates. The training process involved fine-tuning for 160K steps without multi-scale testing.

For object detection and instance segmentation on COCO, the authors utilized multi-scale training, resizing images within a defined range while maintaining a batch size of 32. They outlined the configurations for various ViT models, including specific learning rates and decay parameters. The training schedule involved 12 epochs with learning rate adjustments.

The paper discusses pretraining evaluation methods, specifically linear and attentive probing. Linear probing serves as a proxy for evaluating pretraining quality; however, the authors argue that it is insufficient for MIM methods due to the nature of patch representations. They propose a cross-attention unit that focuses on relevant patches associated with image labels, improving the evaluation of encoder performance.

Results indicate that fine-tuning performance across various methods is similar, attributed to the lack of additional knowledge during training. In linear probing, contrastive self-supervised methods outperformed MIM methods, highlighting the effectiveness of focusing on class representation in contrastive learning.

Overall, the study emphasizes the importance of tailored evaluation methods for different pretraining approaches and the impact of training configurations on model performance.
