The paper presents a Context Autoencoder (CAE) for self-supervised representation learning, focusing on its performance across various datasets and tasks. The results demonstrate that the CAE outperforms several existing methods in both supervised and self-supervised settings. Specifically, on Food-101, Clipart, and Sketch datasets, CAE achieved top-1 classification accuracies of 93.32, 81.84, and 74.65, respectively, surpassing methods like DINO and MAE.

Ablation studies reveal the importance of both the decoder and alignment constraints in enhancing performance. The addition of the decoder and alignment significantly improves downstream task results, confirming that alignment helps maintain the quality of predicted representations. The experiments also indicate that the choice of reconstruction targets, such as using RGB pixel values or DALL-E tokenizer, influences performance in linear probing and segmentation tasks.

The effect of mask ratios on performance was analyzed, with a 50% mask ratio yielding optimal results in linear and attentive probing, although a higher ratio slightly hindered semantic segmentation performance. The study also explores the impact of the number of layers in the regressor and decoder, indicating that more layers can enhance performance.

Overall, the findings emphasize the effectiveness of CAE in self-supervised learning, highlighting the critical roles of decoder architecture and alignment in improving representation quality.
