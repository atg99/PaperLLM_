The paper introduces a novel Context Autoencoder (CAE) approach aimed at enhancing the quality of encoded representations in self-supervised learning for computer vision tasks. Previous methods, including masked autoencoders (MAE), often conflate the tasks of representation learning and masked patch reconstruction, leading to limitations in representation quality. CAE addresses these issues by employing a distinct encoder-regressor-decoder architecture that separates the learning of the encoder from the reconstruction tasks.

In CAE, the encoder processes only visible patches to learn their representations, while the regressor predicts the representations of masked patches based on visible patch representations. The decoder then reconstructs the masked patches from these predicted representations, ensuring that the predictions are semantically aligned within the same representation space. This structure allows the model to focus on high-level semantics, facilitating easier predictions for masked patches and improving representation quality through empirical validation.

The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of CAE across various downstream tasks, including semantic segmentation, object detection, instance segmentation, and classification, highlighting its superior performance compared to supervised pretraining, contrastive self-supervised methods, and other masked image modeling techniques.

The paper also reviews related work in self-supervised representation learning, covering areas such as autoencoding, contrastive learning, and masked image modeling, detailing their methodologies and limitations. By positioning CAE within the context of these existing approaches, the authors underline its innovative contribution to the field of self-supervised learning in computer vision.
