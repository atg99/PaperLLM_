The paper presents a comprehensive analysis of object detection and instance segmentation using various methods, particularly focusing on the performance of the Contrastive Autoencoder (CAE) in comparison to both supervised and self-supervised approaches. Results demonstrate that CAE, especially with longer pretraining epochs, yields superior performance across different models (ViT-S, ViT-B, and ViT-L) on the COCO dataset. For instance, CAE with 1600 epochs outperforms notable methods like MAE, MoCo v3, and DeiT by significant margins in terms of average precision (AP) for both object detection and instance segmentation tasks.

The authors emphasize that the CAE captures knowledge beyond the limitations of ImageNet-1K, contributing to its enhanced performance. The results are consistent with findings from semantic segmentation tasks, indicating the robustness of the CAE approach. Additionally, the paper discusses the scaling capability of CAE, achieving a remarkable mean Average Precision (mAP) of 64.6 on the COCO test-dev dataset using a ViT-Huge backbone, surpassing previous methods that utilized larger models and more extensive training datasets.

The classification experiments conducted on datasets such as Food-101, Clipart, and Sketch further validate the effectiveness of CAE, showing improvements over existing supervised and self-supervised methods. The authors also conduct ablation studies to analyze the impact of different components in the CAE architecture, revealing that critical elements like the masked patch reconstruction task and alignment significantly enhance the model's performance.
