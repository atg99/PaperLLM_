The paper presents a Context Autoencoder (CAE) for self-supervised representation learning, focusing on the probabilistic graphical model that incorporates visible and masked patch representations. The model emphasizes the importance of object localization within images, particularly in datasets like ImageNet-1K, where objects are typically centered. The approach involves maximizing the similarity between randomly sampled image crops that contain parts of the central object, while treating crops that do not contain the object as noise.

The experiments utilize various ViT architectures (small, base, and large) to evaluate the CAE's performance. Pretraining is conducted on ImageNet-1K, employing standard data augmentation techniques and the AdamW optimizer over different training durations and configurations. The linear probing and attentive probing methods are detailed, with specific parameters for batch size, learning rate, and warmup epochs. The fine-tuning process for the models follows protocols established in prior works, ensuring layer-wise learning rate decay and appropriate weight decay settings.

Overall, the paper highlights the effectiveness of CAE in learning representations that are robust to object localization variations, substantiated by experimental results.
