In this study, the authors present a method for reconstructing masked patches in images using a cross-attention mechanism and a decoder that maps latent representations of these masked patches. The encoder processes both visible and masked patches, with the latent representations of visible patches remaining unchanged during the cross-attention computation. The decoder, structured as a series of transformer blocks, generates reconstructed masked patches without using information from visible patches directly.

The methodology employs a random block-wise masking strategy, where 98 out of 196 patches are masked for each image. The targets for reconstruction are derived from a discrete tokenizer trained on a large dataset, producing discrete tokens for each patch. The loss function for training combines a reconstruction loss for the masked patches and an alignment loss for the masked representation predictions, utilizing mean squared error and cross-entropy losses respectively.

The findings suggest that the model's predictions are effectively made in an encoded representation space, enhancing the semantic understanding of the relationships between visible and masked patches. Empirical evidence supports the importance of prediction alignment, as successful image reconstruction occurs when alignment is maintained, while misalignment leads to noisy reconstructions. This indicates that the alignment is crucial for ensuring that the representations learned by the model are coherent and effective for the task of image reconstruction.
