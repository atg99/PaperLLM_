The paper evaluates the performance of various self-supervised representation learning methods using Context Autoencoder (CAE) across different tasks, including fine-tuning, linear probing, and attentive probing, as detailed in Table 1. The results indicate that CAE demonstrates competitive performance compared to established methods like DeiT, MoCo v3, and DINO, particularly in terms of top-1 accuracy for different Vision Transformer (ViT) architectures (ViT-S, ViT-B, and ViT-L).

In the fine-tuning task, CAE achieves notable scores, particularly with 1600 epochs of training, where it outperforms several methods in the attentive probing category. The analysis suggests that Masked Image Model (MIM) methods, including CAE, extract representations that are beneficial for classification tasks, as indicated by the higher attentive probing scores compared to linear probing.

Table 2 focuses on semantic segmentation performance on the ADE20K dataset, showing that CAE* consistently achieves competitive mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) scores compared to other methods, including BEiT and MAE. Notably, CAE* trained for 1600 epochs surpasses MAE, MoCo v3, and DeiT by significant margins.

The findings imply that the choice of tokenizer, whether trained on ImageNet-1K or a larger dataset, does not significantly affect the pretraining quality or downstream task performance. Overall, CAE exhibits strong potential for self-supervised representation learning, particularly in image segmentation tasks, highlighting its effectiveness in leveraging contextual information.
