This paper introduces a novel self-supervised representation learning method called the context autoencoder (CAE) that employs masked image modeling (MIM). The CAE framework consists of an encoder-regressor-decoder architecture designed to enhance the pretraining of an encoder by predicting representations in an encoded space. The pretraining involves two primary tasks: masked representation prediction, where the model predicts representations for masked patches, and masked patch reconstruction, where the model reconstructs the masked patches from the predicted representations.

The encoder processes visible patches to generate their representations, while the regressor predicts representations for the masked patches using the representations of visible patches and their positions. The decoder then reconstructs the masked patches based on these predicted representations. This design facilitates a clear separation between learning the encoder and completing the two pretraining tasks, which empirically benefits representation learning.

The effectiveness of the CAE is demonstrated through superior performance in various downstream tasks, including semantic segmentation, object detection, instance segmentation, and classification. The authors assert that the encoder pretrained with CAE can effectively extract semantic representations, making it suitable for these tasks.

Overall, the CAE approach provides a robust framework for self-supervised representation learning, leveraging masked image modeling to improve the transferability of learned representations. The code for the CAE is made publicly available for further research and application.
