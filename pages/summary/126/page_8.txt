This paper presents a comparative analysis of attention maps generated by various self-supervised learning methods, specifically focusing on MoCo v3, MAE, and the proposed CAE model, all pretrained on ImageNet-1K. The findings reveal that MoCo v3 primarily concentrates on the object region while neglecting other areas. In contrast, both MIM-based models, CAE and MAE, exhibit a broader focus, considering nearly all patches in the images.

The authors introduce a probabilistic graphical model to articulate the relationships among the masked patches and the observed data. This model utilizes latent representations to establish conditional independence among variables, leading to a series of equations that delineate the dependencies among the encoder, latent contextual regressor, and decoder components of the CAE. The analysis emphasizes the significance of this conditional independence assumption in simplifying the model's representation and enhancing the understanding of latent representation alignment constraints. Overall, the paper underscores the advantages of the CAE approach in capturing comprehensive attention across image patches compared to traditional contrastive methods.
