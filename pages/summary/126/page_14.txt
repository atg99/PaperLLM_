This paper presents the CAE architecture for masked image modeling, focusing on predictions made from visible patches to masked patches in encoded representation space. Two pretraining tasks are employed: masked representation prediction and masked patch reconstruction. Experimental results indicate that the 4-layer model outperforms others, and a specific loss tradeoff parameter λ was tested, with λ=2 yielding the best linear probing results. The impact of different pretraining targets was also explored, revealing that the CAE architecture does not depend on specific targets for effective performance.

The advantages of the CAE approach over traditional contrastive self-supervised and supervised pretraining methods are highlighted, particularly its ability to learn representations from all image patches rather than just center patches. However, limitations were acknowledged, especially in reconstructing large contiguous masked regions within images, which poses challenges for the model to infer missing information.

The paper suggests potential extensions, such as focusing solely on masked representation prediction and exploring depth-wise convolution networks. Acknowledgments are made to contributors, and funding sources are detailed. The code and datasets used in the research are publicly accessible, ensuring transparency in the methodology and findings.
