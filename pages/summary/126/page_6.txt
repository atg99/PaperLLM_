The paper discusses various computational models for representation learning, specifically focusing on context autoencoders (CAE), BEiT, denoising autoencoders (DAE), and Masked Autoencoders (MAE). These models are compared in terms of their encoding and masked image modeling (MIM) capabilities. The authors highlight the distinct roles of encoders and decoders in these models, noting that CAE and DAE perform their tasks explicitly, while BEiT and MAE do so implicitly and simultaneously.

The authors also contrast their CAE approach with typical contrastive self-supervised learning methods such as SimCLR and MoCo. They argue that conventional methods tend to focus on the center pixels of images, leading to representations that may not capture the diversity of features outside this region. In contrast, CAE randomly samples patches from images, encouraging the model to learn comprehensive representations for all patches. This allows CAE to potentially learn more generalizable knowledge beyond the primary categories represented in the center of images, which may enhance performance on downstream tasks.

Additionally, the paper emphasizes the intuitive interpretation of CAE, likening its functioning to human cognitive abilities to infer information about masked regions based on visible ones. This insight suggests that CAE may be more effective in understanding and predicting image content compared to traditional contrastive methods.
