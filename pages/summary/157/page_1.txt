The paper "Don’t Blame the ELBO! A Linear VAE Perspective on Posterior Collapse" by James Lucas, George Tuck, Roger Grosse, and Mohammad Norouzi addresses the issue of posterior collapse in Variational Autoencoders (VAEs), where the variational posterior distribution aligns too closely with the prior for certain latent variables. The authors provide a clear explanation by analyzing linear VAEs and their relationship with Probabilistic PCA (pPCA). They demonstrate that posterior collapse can occur in pPCA due to local maxima in the log marginal likelihood. 

Contrary to common belief, the authors prove that the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) objective for linear VAEs does not create additional spurious local maxima compared to the log marginal likelihood. Their findings reveal that training a linear VAE with exact variational inference can achieve a global maximum that corresponds to the principal component directions. The authors further illustrate that their linear analysis is predictive for high-capacity, non-linear VAEs, shedding light on the interplay between observation noise, local maxima, and posterior collapse in deep Gaussian VAEs.

The paper emphasizes the need to rethink the role of the ELBO in the context of posterior collapse and suggests that the underlying mechanisms may be more complex than previously understood.
