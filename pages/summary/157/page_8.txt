The paper evaluates the performance of deep Gaussian Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) on real-valued MNIST and CELEBA datasets, focusing on the evidence lower bound (ELBO) and the phenomenon of posterior collapse. The results, averaged over five trials, show variations in ELBO as well as the percentage of latent dimensions that collapse to the prior.

Key findings include:

1. **ELBO Performance**: The ELBO values indicate that different initializations of the variance parameter influence the model's training performance. Higher initial variance values lead to closer matching of the variational distribution to the prior, which is associated with increased posterior collapse.

2. **Posterior Collapse Measurement**: The study defines posterior collapse as the proportion of latent dimensions that remain close to the prior distribution across a significant portion of training data. This is quantified using KL divergence thresholds, revealing that certain initialization strategies exacerbate this issue, particularly in nonlinear models compared to linear ones.

3. **Robustness of Measurements**: The measurements of posterior collapse are found to be moderately robust to variations in data preprocessing, indicating the reliability of the findings across different experimental conditions.

4. **Optimization Challenges**: The paper highlights that local optima in the training process may contribute to posterior collapse, suggesting that learning the variance parameter correctly is more challenging in deep VAEs.

Overall, the results underscore the complexities of training deep VAEs and the significant impact of initialization strategies on model performance and latent variable behavior.
