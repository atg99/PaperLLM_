The paper discusses how linear Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) can recover the globally optimal solution of Probabilistic PCA (pPCA). The authors present a specific VAE model defined by a Gaussian distribution for both the prior and posterior, using a diagonal covariance matrix for the data points. This model, while simpler than typical VAEs, is shown to be sufficient for achieving the global optimum of the probabilistic model.

The authors prove that the global maximum of the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) for the linear VAE is identical to the global maximum of the log marginal likelihood of pPCA. They note that the global optimum of pPCA can vary with orthogonal transformations of the weight matrix, but the linear VAE can recover this optimum when specific conditions are met. The proof highlights that the posterior distribution of pPCA is Gaussian, and the VAE model can be adjusted to ensure the posterior is accurately represented at the global optimum.

Additionally, they establish that the scaled principal components correspond to the columns of the decoder network at the global maximum of the ELBO. This result underscores the identifiability of the principal components, which is not guaranteed in other models such as the regularized linear autoencoder.

The authors also address concerns about the ELBO potentially introducing spurious local maxima due to the KL divergence term. They conclude that, for the linear VAE, the ELBO does not introduce these additional maxima, as the variational distribution can approximate the true posterior. They provide a sketch of the proof, indicating that deviations from orthogonality in the decoder columns do not create new local maxima but lead to saddle points instead.

Overall, the findings suggest that in the linear setting, issues related to posterior collapse are primarily due to the probabilistic model rather than the ELBO objective itself. The paper raises questions about the applicability of these results in non-linear settings.
