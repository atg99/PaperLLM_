This paper investigates the performance of Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) using both analytic and stochastic estimates of the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO). The results indicate that using the analytic gradient allows for faster convergence and a better final ELBO, with values of 950.7 compared to 939.3 for stochastic training. The study highlights the challenges associated with high-variance noise from stochastic ELBO estimates, which can impede optimization and lead to poorer outcomes.

The authors further explore the implications of employing nonlinear encoders with linear decoders. While the optimal variational distribution remains unchanged, the introduction of deeper networks can complicate the optimization landscape, leading to multiple stationary points. Empirical results demonstrate that linear encoders perform comparably to their nonlinear counterparts in terms of ELBO.

Additionally, the paper addresses posterior collapse in deep nonlinear VAEs trained on datasets like MNIST and CelebA. A new definition for measuring posterior collapse is introduced, focusing on the KL divergence between the variational distribution and the true posterior. The study finds that linear VAEs can experience collapse, particularly when observation noise is fixed, which can negatively impact the quality of learned representations.

Finally, the authors report that models trained with fixed observation noise exhibit significant differences in ELBO, suggesting that allowing the observation noise to be learned alongside VAE weights may yield better representations. The findings underscore the importance of optimizing both the model architecture and training parameters to enhance VAE performance.
