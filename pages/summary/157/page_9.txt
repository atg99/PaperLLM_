This paper investigates the phenomenon of posterior collapse in Variational Autoencoders (VAEs), particularly focusing on the differences between linear and deep non-linear VAEs. It presents empirical findings on the posterior collapse percentage in deep VAEs trained on the MNIST dataset, measuring the proportion of latent dimensions closely approximating the prior distribution. The study also explores the training of deep convolutional VAEs on the CelebA dataset, assessing the impact of fixed and learned observation noise on the effectiveness of the model, revealing sensitivity to initialization.

The discussion section highlights the insights gained from analyzing the relationship between linear VAEs and probabilistic Principal Component Analysis (pPCA). It identifies ill-conditioning of stationary points in the log marginal likelihood objective as a key factor contributing to posterior collapse and asserts that these optimization challenges are relevant in the context of deep non-linear VAEs as well. The authors recommend using linear VAEs as theoretical test cases for further exploration of hypotheses related to VAEs.
