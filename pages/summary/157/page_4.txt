The paper discusses the relationship between linear variational autoencoders (V AEs) and probabilistic principal component analysis (pPCA). Rolinek et al. noted that diagonal covariance in the variational distribution promotes orthogonal representations, while they modified the objective function to address posterior collapse. In contrast, the authors focus on linear V AEs without altering the objective function, demonstrating a direct link between the global solutions of linear V AEs and principal components.

Kunin et al. examined optimization issues in linear autoencoders, establishing an equivalence between pPCA and Bayesian autoencoders. They highlighted that excessive variance leads to loss of information regarding the latent code, similar to observations by Chechik et al. They also indicated that proper regularization in linear autoencoders can recover principal components, but the authors assert that linear V AEs with diagonal covariance achieve exact recovery of principal components.

The analysis section compares the loss landscapes of pPCA and linear V AEs. It begins by characterizing stationary points of pPCA, revealing that a simple linear V AE can attain the global optimum of pPCA. When the eigenvalues of data covariance are distinct, the linear V AE can identify individual principal components, whereas pPCA only captures the PCA subspace. The authors also demonstrate that the evidence lower bound (ELBO) does not add spurious maxima to the loss landscape.

The paper revisits pPCA, describing it as a fully Gaussian linear model that allows for closed-form calculations of the marginal distribution and posterior. The maximum likelihood estimate of the model's parameters is established, and the authors highlight that the stability of these estimates is influenced by the variance term, which controls the stability of stationary points in the log marginal likelihood. As the variance increases, stationary points tend to become stable local maxima, complicating the learning of additional components. Conversely, reducing the variance enhances likelihood at these stationary points, indicating that learning the variance is crucial for a comprehensive latent representation.
