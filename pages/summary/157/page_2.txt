This paper investigates the relationship between linear variational autoencoders (V AEs) and probabilistic PCA (pPCA), demonstrating that a linear V AE can effectively recover the pPCA solution. By implementing a diagonal covariance structure within the variational distribution, the authors establish that an identifiable autoencoder can be achieved, allowing the global maximum of the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) to yield the exact principal components as the decoder's weights. Crucially, it is shown that the training of linear V AEs using ELBO does not introduce additional local maxima compared to log marginal likelihood training.

The authors delve into the implications of linear V AEs on understanding posterior collapse and the challenges associated with V AE optimization. They build on the work of Tipping and Bishop, characterizing the stationary points of pPCA and highlighting that the variance in the observation model significantly affects the stability of local stationary points linked to posterior collapse. The study concludes that overcoming these sub-optimal solutions necessitates a dual approach of reducing noise while enhancing feature learning.

Key contributions of the paper include:
1. Verification that linear V AEs can accurately recover the true posterior of pPCA, and proving that the global optimum of linear V AEs retrieves the principal components, not merely their spanning subspace.
2. Demonstration that posterior collapse can arise when optimizing log marginal likelihood, even in the absence of high-capacity decoders.
3. Experimental validation that insights from the linear setting extend to high-capacity non-linear V AEs, emphasizing the role of observation noise in mitigating local maxima associated with posterior collapse.

The paper also outlines the foundational concepts of probabilistic PCA and variational autoencoders, detailing the generative models and the role of the ELBO in approximating the posterior distribution. The significance of the KL divergence in guiding the variational distribution towards the prior is discussed, alongside the architecture of V AEs which employs neural networks for encoding and decoding processes.
