The paper discusses the backpropagation process in Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), which is essential for training the model. Backpropagation calculates an error value based on the output of the previous layer, allowing for the adjustment of each neuron's weights to minimize this error. The algorithm iteratively processes inputs through activation units and computes results using an activation function, ultimately updating weights until the cost function is minimized.

A significant challenge in training CNNs is overfitting, where a model performs well on training data but poorly on unseen test data. In contrast, underfitting occurs when a model fails to capture sufficient information from the training data. A model is deemed "just fit" if it performs satisfactorily on both training and testing datasets. The paper emphasizes the importance of regularization techniques to combat overfitting. One such method is dropout, which randomly removes neurons during training sessions to encourage the model to learn diverse features and maintain balanced feature selection across the network.
