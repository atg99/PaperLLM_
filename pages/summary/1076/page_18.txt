The paper discusses the methodologies of object detection using two-stage and single-stage deep learning pipelines. Two-stage object detectors, such as R-CNN, Fast R-CNN, and Faster R-CNN, operate in two phases: first, they propose Regions of Interest (ROIs) that likely contain objects, and second, they classify these objects while filtering out less promising ROIs. In contrast, single-stage detectors like YOLO, SSD, EfficientNet, and RetinaNet perform both bounding box creation and object classification in a single step using a feed-forward neural network, resulting in faster but often less accurate predictions.

The R-CNN model, an early example of deep learning-based object detection, employed a selective search method for ROI proposals but faced challenges such as slow inference speed. Fast R-CNN improved upon this by utilizing a convolutional neural network to generate feature maps and ROI projections, while Rapid R-CNN processed the entire image for feature extraction. Faster R-CNN further optimized the process by integrating a separate network for ROI feeding into the pooling layer, enhancing prediction efficiency.

Overall, the paper highlights the evolution of object detection techniques, emphasizing the trade-off between speed and accuracy across different detection models.
