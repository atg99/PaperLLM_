The paper discusses various activation functions used in neural networks, particularly focusing on the Rectified Linear Unit (ReLU) and its alternatives. The Tanh function, similar to the sigmoid function, outputs values between -1 and 1. However, ReLU has gained popularity in Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) because it transforms all input values to a positive range, thus saving time and resources.

Historically, Tanh and sigmoid non-linearities dominated, but ReLU's advantages have led to its widespread adoption. A key issue with saturated functions like sigmoid and Tanh is the "vanishing gradient" phenomenon, where gradients diminish as network depth increases, complicating backpropagation. In contrast, ReLU maintains a constant gradient for positive inputs and produces sparse representations by yielding zero for certain inputs.

Despite its benefits, ReLU faces challenges, particularly the "Dying ReLU" problem, where neurons become inactive due to zero gradients. To mitigate this, alternatives like Leaky ReLU are proposed, which retain some sensitivity to negative inputs.

The paper also covers fully connected layers in CNNs, where neurons are interconnected akin to traditional neural networks. These layers, while powerful, come with the drawback of requiring a significant number of parameters, leading to extensive training times. Techniques like dropout are suggested to reduce connections and nodes, exemplified by networks like LeNet and AlexNet, which efficiently manage computational complexity.
