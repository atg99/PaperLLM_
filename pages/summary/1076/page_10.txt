The paper discusses key components of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), focusing on pooling methods and activation functions. Pooling techniques, such as max pooling, average pooling, and global pooling, are employed to downsample feature maps by selecting the maximum or average values within subregions. While pooling helps reduce dimensionality, it poses a challenge as it does not indicate the presence of features, potentially leading to a loss of important information in CNN ratings.

Following the convolution layer, non-linearity is introduced through activation functions. These functions, which include Sigmoid, Tanh, and the widely used ReLU, transform the weighted sums of inputs to produce outputs. Non-linear activation layers are essential for enabling CNNs to learn complex patterns and for facilitating error backpropagation during training. The paper highlights that ReLU has gained popularity due to its efficiency in processing, contrasting with the earlier dominance of Sigmoid and Tanh functions. Overall, the integration of pooling and activation functions is crucial for enhancing the performance of CNN architectures.
