This paper discusses the critical role of hyper-parameter selection in the performance of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), emphasizing their sensitivity to even minor adjustments. It highlights the evolution of CNN architectures from simpler models like AlexNet to more complex ones such as ResNet and DenseNet, which contain billions of parameters and require substantial data and computational resources for training. 

The author advocates for the development of lightweight and compact networks to minimize redundancy while balancing speed, memory usage, and accuracy for specific applications. Even though smaller models may lead to reduced detection accuracy, techniques such as hint learning, knowledge distillation, and enhanced pre-training can help mitigate this issue. 

Moreover, the paper indicates that using blocks instead of layers can significantly enhance CNN performance. It provides a comprehensive overview of deep learning technologies, tracing their history through various innovations and applications, and includes a taxonomy of deep learning tasks. The research covers supervised, unsupervised, and hybrid learning approaches, emphasizing their real-world applicability based on specific problem contexts. 

Finally, the author summarizes key challenges associated with CNNs, particularly how the number of layers influences processing time and network performance. The paper concludes with a declaration of no external funding and no conflicts of interest.
