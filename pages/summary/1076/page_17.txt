The paper discusses various architectures of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), focusing on their contributions, strengths, and gaps. DenseNet-121 is highlighted for its unique architecture that allows for direct information transmission between layers, addressing the vanishing gradient problem and requiring fewer parameters. Its design promotes maximum data flow and prevents the redundancy of feature maps. However, it faces challenges with the significant increase in parameters as the number of feature maps grows.

EfficientNet, introduced in 2019, employs a compound scaling method that uniformly adjusts the width, depth, and resolution of models, resulting in seven models that outperform existing state-of-the-art CNNs while being more efficient. This method showcases the advantages of CNNs over traditional classification algorithms by effectively learning key features for object representation in images.

The paper also delineates the critical role of image classification in multimedia processing within the Internet of Things (IoT). It emphasizes the relevance of CNNs in applications such as medical image analysis and real-time target tracking. The distinctions between image classification, object localization, and object detection are clarified, with object detection being the most complex as it requires identifying and labeling multiple objects within an image.

Overall, the paper underscores the significance of various CNN architectures in enhancing classification and detection tasks in computer vision, while also addressing inherent challenges and advancements in the field.
