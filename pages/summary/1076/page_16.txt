This paper presents a comparative analysis of various popular Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) architectures, highlighting their main contributions, strengths, and gaps. 

1. **LeNet-5 (1998)**: This architecture is recognized as the first popular CNN, featuring 7 layers (5 convolutional and 2 fully connected). Its key contributions include the utilization of spatial correlation to reduce computation and parameter count, along with automated feature hierarchy discovery. However, it struggles with scaling to diverse image classes and exhibits weak feature extraction.

2. **AlexNet (2012)**: With 8 layers (5 convolutional and 3 fully connected), AlexNet builds upon LeNet-5 by employing more complexity, depth, and breadth. It introduces techniques such as ReLU activation, dropout, and overlapping pooling, supported by NVIDIA GTX 580 GPUs. While it enhances feature extraction at multiple levels, it faces challenges like dormant neurons and aliasing artifacts due to large filter sizes.

3. **ZfNet (2014)**: This architecture focuses on middle-level feature conceptualization and improves upon AlexNet by reducing filter size and stride in the first two layers. However, further processing is required for effective visualization of its outputs.

4. **VGG (2014)**: VGG consists of 16-19 layers (13-16 convolutional and 3 fully connected) and is characterized by a homogeneous structure and small kernel sizes. It enhances model accuracy through small convolutional filters but is computationally expensive due to fully connected layers.

5. **GoogLeNet (2015)**: Featuring 22 layers with 9 inception modules, GoogLeNet introduces a block concept that separates transformation and merging processes. It utilizes multiscale filters and reduces parameters through bottleneck layers and global average pooling. Nevertheless, its diverse topology complicates parameter modification and may lead to loss of useful information.

6. **Inception-V3 (2015)**: This architecture resolves representational bottlenecks by substituting large filters with smaller ones, enhancing feature representation and network efficiency. It employs batch normalization for expedited training but suffers from design complexity and lack of uniformity.

7. **ResNet (2016)**: Featuring extensive layers (50, 101, or 152), ResNet introduces skip connections that aid in retaining knowledge over long periods and reduce overfitting. Its innovative design mitigates the vanishing gradient problem but presents challenges such as intricate structures and excessive hyperparameter adaptation.

Overall, the paper underscores the evolution of CNN architectures, showcasing advancements in feature extraction, computational efficiency, and model complexity, while also noting inherent limitations across different designs.
