The paper discusses various architectures of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and their evolution in the field of computer vision. It highlights the foundational role of CNNs, beginning with LeNet, which was introduced by LeCun in 1998 for handwritten digit recognition. LeNet's limited capacity hindered advancements in computer vision.

In 2012, AlexNet marked a significant breakthrough with five convolutional layers and two fully connected layers, showcasing the potential of deep learning in computer vision. Following AlexNet, ZFNet made improvements in visualization techniques, allowing for better understanding of CNNs. VGG introduced in 2014 emphasized the impact of depth using smaller convolutional kernels, while GoogLeNet considered both depth and width, optimizing parameters to prevent overfitting.

The introduction of ResNet by He et al. in 2015 utilized residual connections, enabling networks to achieve depths of up to 152 layers, enhancing efficiency and accuracy in image recognition. DenseNet, proposed in 2017, built on ResNet's principles, promoting feature reuse and reducing parameters through layer concatenation.

The paper also mentions BoostCNN, a model from 2018 that integrates various network architectures within a boosting framework to optimize performance iteratively. Overall, the evolution of CNN architectures reflects a continuous effort to enhance learning capabilities and application in diverse domains.
