The paper discusses key concepts related to Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), focusing on the effects of stride and padding on feature maps. It highlights that increasing the stride value reduces the dimensionality of the resulting feature map while significantly affecting the border size of the input image due to padding. Padding enlarges the input image, consequently increasing the feature map size.

Filters in CNNs serve to detect specific features in images, akin to traditional image processing filters. The paper explains that these filters are initialized before training with task-specific filters for better performance. Weight sharing is emphasized as a crucial aspect of CNNs, where a single set of weights is applied across all pixels, reducing training time and resource costs.

The concept of stride is introduced, illustrating how it influences the overlap between filter applications. An example using a 6x6 image shows that with a stride of one, the maximum output size is 4x4, while modifying the stride can reduce both output size and overlap.

The paper also addresses the potential loss of detail at image edges during convolution, proposing zero padding as a practical solution. An example calculation demonstrates how zero padding can maintain the original output size, ensuring that edge details are captured effectively. The relationship between input size, filter size, and stride is formalized in a mathematical equation, providing clarity on output size calculations.
