The paper discusses the challenges and advancements in dynamic networks, particularly in the context of adversarial attacks and interpretability. It highlights that input-adaptive models can be misled into activating all intermediate layers or producing misleading predictions, even for straightforward samples. The paper notes that traditional adversarial training methods are insufficient in defending against these attacks, indicating a gap in the understanding of dynamic network robustness.

Furthermore, it emphasizes the black-box nature of dynamic networks, which complicates the interpretability of their mechanisms. However, the adaptive inference paradigm aligns with human visual processing, potentially paving the way for enhanced transparency. The ability to analyze model activation and reliance on specific input features may lead to improved interpretability in deep learning models.
