The paper discusses advancements in spatial-wise dynamic networks within the context of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and highlights the importance of adaptive computation based on spatial locations in images. It emphasizes that not all pixels contribute equally to predictions, suggesting that processing only a subset of pixels can enhance efficiency. The work categorizes dynamic networks into three levels: pixel level, region level, and resolution level.

At the pixel level, two main approaches are identified: pixel-wise dynamic architectures and pixel-wise dynamic parameters. Pixel-wise dynamic architectures focus on adjusting network structures for individual pixels, utilizing methods such as dynamic sparse convolution, which performs convolutions only on strategically sampled pixels, and dynamic additional refinement, which first processes the entire feature map before selectively applying additional computation to salient pixels. This allows for spatially adaptive computation, improving efficiency while maintaining performance.

On the other hand, pixel-wise dynamic parameters involve the application of data-dependent parameters at different pixels to enhance representation power. Techniques such as dynamic weights are implemented to adjust convolutional operations based on the specific needs of each pixel, allowing for improved adaptability and performance.

Overall, the paper underscores the potential of spatial-wise dynamic networks to reduce computational redundancy while maintaining or enhancing model performance, particularly in visual learning tasks.
