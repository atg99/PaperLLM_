The paper discusses various approaches to enhance video recognition efficiency through adaptive computation in recurrent neural networks (RNNs). It outlines methods for dynamically allocating computational resources based on frame importance, which can be achieved in two main ways: dynamically updating hidden states and performing adaptive pre-sampling of key frames.

In the section on dynamic RNNs, the paper highlights three strategies for video recognition: 1) treating unimportant frames with minimal computation, referred to as "glimpse"; 2) implementing early exiting to make predictions based on only a portion of the video; and 3) dynamic jumping, where the network learns to skip unimportant frames. These strategies aim to reduce redundancy and enhance processing efficiency.

The second approach discussed is dynamic key frame sampling, which prioritizes salient frames through techniques like temporal attention and sampling modules. This method focuses on efficiently selecting the most informative frames or clips for prediction, thereby improving overall inference efficiency.

The paper also emphasizes the challenges of training dynamic networks compared to static models, particularly in making data-dependent decisions during inference. It outlines decision-making schemes within dynamic networks, including confidence-based criteria, which allow the model to output predictions early if a certain confidence level is reached.

Overall, the paper presents a comprehensive overview of adaptive techniques in dynamic networks for video recognition, highlighting their potential to improve computational efficiency and effectiveness in processing sequential data.
