In summary, dynamic networks present several challenges and opportunities in their design and application. Current methods like early exiting and policy networks, while effective, often lack theoretical foundations and may introduce computational overheads. Generalization issues arise due to distribution mismatches between training and inference data, necessitating new theoretical frameworks. Additionally, architecture design specifically for dynamic networks is under-explored, with potential for significant improvements in efficiency and performance. The applicability of these networks to a wider range of tasks beyond classification is limited, particularly in complex scenarios like object detection. The gap between theoretical and practical efficiencies remains, especially with existing hardware optimized for static models. Lastly, the robustness of dynamic networks against adversarial attacks is an emerging area requiring further investigation to ensure both classification accuracy and efficiency are maintained under adversarial conditions.
