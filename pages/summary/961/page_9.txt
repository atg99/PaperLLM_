The paper discusses various methods for dynamic inference in neural networks, focusing on region-level and resolution-level adaptations. It begins with the region selection module, which generates transformation parameters to perform inference on specific regions of an image. The dynamic region-aware convolution (DRConv) is introduced, which utilizes a segmentation mask to create data-dependent kernels for improved object recognition.

The paper identifies two main approaches within region-level dynamic networks: dynamic transformations and hard attention mechanisms. Dynamic transformations, such as affine and projective transformations, enhance model generalization by adapting to variations in the input. Hard attention mechanisms, particularly those employing recurrent neural networks (RNNs) and class activation mapping, strategically select patches for processing, thus improving model efficiency.

Resolution-level dynamic networks are also examined, highlighting the need for adaptive resolutions in processing input images. This can be achieved through adaptive scaling ratios and selective activation of sub-networks with varying resolutions. By addressing spatial redundancy, these networks optimize computational resources while maintaining performance.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the significance of dynamic inference techniques in enhancing the efficiency and effectiveness of neural networks, particularly in tasks involving varying object shapes and sizes.
