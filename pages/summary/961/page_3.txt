The paper discusses dynamic networks that adapt their computation based on the sample characteristics, aiming to improve efficiency and representation power. It categorizes these networks into two main types: those that adjust their architecture dynamically and those that adapt parameters within a fixed computational graph. 

Dynamic architectures are explored through several strategies, including varying network depth, width, and employing dynamic routing within a SuperNet. These strategies help to optimize computation, especially for easier samples, while preserving performance for more challenging ones. 

Early exiting is a prominent technique highlighted in the paper, where the network can terminate processing at intermediate layers for simpler inputs. This is achieved through methods like cascading models, utilizing intermediate classifiers, and implementing multi-scale architectures. The multi-scale dense network exemplifies this approach by integrating various sub-networks to handle different resolutions, thus facilitating faster and more accurate predictions.

The paper concludes with a discussion on future research directions and open problems in the domain of dynamic networks, emphasizing the potential for further enhancements in model efficiency and adaptability.
