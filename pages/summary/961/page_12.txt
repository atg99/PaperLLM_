In summary, the paper discusses various strategies for decision-making in dynamic networks, focusing on confidence-based criteria, policy networks, and gating functions. Confidence is typically measured through SoftMax outputs, entropy, and other metrics, which guide the inference process based on predefined thresholds. Policy networks adaptively adjust network structures for different inputs but may face limitations in compatibility with various architectures. Gating functions offer a flexible approach for controlling network operations, although they often necessitate specialized training techniques due to their non-differentiable nature.

Training dynamic networks involves optimizing multiple objectives, such as minimizing cumulative losses in multi-exit networks and encouraging sparsity to reduce computational redundancy. Techniques like gradient estimation and reparameterization are employed to address the challenges posed by non-differentiable functions, facilitating end-to-end training. Overall, these strategies aim to enhance the efficiency and accuracy of dynamic networks while managing the trade-offs inherent in model complexity and performance.
