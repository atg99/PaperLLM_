In the realm of RNN-based dynamic text processing, traditional RNNs are critiqued for their static inference, leading to redundant computations as different tokens contribute variably to tasks. To address this, dynamic RNNs are developed, allowing for adaptive updates of hidden states based on the significance of each input token. This can involve skipping updates for unimportant tokens, implementing coarse updates that selectively compute hidden states, or utilizing hierarchical structures to manage updates at different levels. 

The paper discusses several dynamic update strategies, such as skipping the update entirely for less informative tokens, coarse updates that reduce computational operations, and selective updates in hierarchical RNNs that leverage the intrinsic structure of texts. 

Additionally, the concept of early exiting is introduced, enabling models to terminate processing once sufficient information is gathered. This approach is complemented by dynamic jumping, where models can skip reading certain tokens entirely, thereby optimizing processing time.

In video recognition, similar dynamic methodologies are applied, treating video frames as sequential inputs that can benefit from temporal-wise computation adjustments. This strategy aims to reduce computational load by focusing on critical frames while bypassing less informative ones, ultimately enhancing the efficiency of video analysis. 

Overall, the exploration of temporal-wise dynamic networks presents a significant advancement in optimizing computation for sequential data processing, whether in text or video contexts.
