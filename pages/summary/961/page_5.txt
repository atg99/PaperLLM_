The paper discusses various approaches to enhance the efficiency of Mixture of Experts (MoE) models and Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) through adaptive mechanisms. It introduces soft weighting and hard gating schemes for MoE, highlighting that the soft weights require all branches to execute, which does not reduce computation at test time. In contrast, hard gating allows only a subset of experts to be activated, improving inference efficiency. The paper cites examples like HydraNet and dynamic routing networks that implement hard MoE in various architectures.

Additionally, the paper explores techniques for dynamically activating convolutional channels in CNNs, addressing the redundancy of channels. It contrasts static pruning methods, which remove filters permanently, with adaptive width approaches that activate channels based on input data. This includes multi-stage architectures and dynamic pruning using gating functions, which decide the execution of channels at each layer.

The discussion extends to dynamic routing within SuperNets that allow for flexible computation paths based on the input. It describes how routing nodes allocate features to different paths, with probabilities guiding the activation of specific paths. The work emphasizes the importance of efficient inference mechanisms in modern neural networks, enabling them to adaptively manage their computational resources based on the characteristics of the input data.
