The paper provides a comprehensive survey of dynamic networks that perform adaptive computation in deep learning. It categorizes dynamic networks based on three granularities: sample-wise, spatial-wise, and temporal-wise. The survey highlights the evolution of adaptive inference techniques, which have roots in earlier methodologies like model ensembles and spiking neural networks, although the latter is excluded from this review due to its limited application in vision tasks.

The authors emphasize the growing interest in dynamic inference within modern deep architectures, which presents new research questions and challenges. The survey aims to provide an overview of recent advancements in this field, establish connections between various subareas to prevent redundant efforts, and outline key challenges and future research directions.

The structure of the survey is organized into sections, with the first section focusing on sample-wise dynamic networks that adapt their architectures based on input samples. Subsequent sections delve into spatially and temporally adaptive models, followed by a discussion on decision-making strategies and training techniques for dynamic models. The paper aims to serve as a resource for researchers interested in dynamic networks and to foster further exploration in this rapidly developing area.
