The paper "XLNet: Generalized Autoregressive Pretraining for Language Understanding" introduces XLNet, a novel pretraining method that combines the advantages of autoregressive language modeling and the bidirectional context modeling of denoising autoencoders like BERT. The authors argue that while BERT's approach of masking inputs allows for better context understanding, it fails to account for dependencies between masked tokens and leads to a pretrain-finetune discrepancy.

XLNet addresses these issues by maximizing expected likelihood over all permutations of the input sequence, enabling the model to learn bidirectional contexts effectively. Additionally, XLNet incorporates concepts from Transformer-XL, enhancing its autoregressive capabilities. The results demonstrate that XLNet significantly outperforms BERT across 20 different tasks, including question answering and sentiment analysis, highlighting its effectiveness in natural language understanding.

The paper emphasizes the importance of unsupervised representation learning in natural language processing and discusses the limitations of existing pretraining methods, ultimately presenting XLNet as a more robust alternative. The authors provide empirical evidence of XLNet's superior performance under comparable experimental conditions, asserting its potential for advancing language understanding tasks.
