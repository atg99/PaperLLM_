The paper discusses a novel approach to language modeling that integrates ideas from Transformer-XL with a permutation-based objective for autoregressive (AR) learning. The method utilizes two streams of attention: a query stream and a content stream. The query stream leverages past representations without accessing the current input, while the content stream incorporates both past representations and the current input, allowing for standard self-attention updates during fine-tuning.

To address the challenges posed by permutation language modeling, the authors propose a partial prediction strategy. This involves predicting only the last tokens based on a factorization order, which reduces optimization complexity and enhances convergence speed. By splitting the input into non-target and target subsequences, the model maximizes the log-likelihood of the target tokens conditioned on the non-target tokens.

The paper also highlights the incorporation of techniques from Transformer-XL, specifically relative positional encoding and segment recurrence. These techniques allow the model to cache and reuse hidden states from previous segments, thus improving efficiency without being dependent on the permutation order of the segments.

Furthermore, the model is designed to handle multiple input segments, a common requirement in tasks like question answering. This is achieved by concatenating two segments and applying permutation language modeling while reusing memory from the same context.

Overall, the proposed method aims to enhance the performance of language models by leveraging the strengths of both autoregressive modeling and Transformer-XL's mechanisms, resulting in a more efficient and effective pretraining framework.
