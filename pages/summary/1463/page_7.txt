The paper discusses the performance of XLNet, particularly in comparison to BERT, through various natural language understanding tasks. Despite some underfitting at the end of training, the authors conduct an ablation study using the XLNet-Base-wikibooks model and implement a bidirectional data input pipeline. They adopt a span-based prediction method for fine-tuning, sampling lengths for prediction targets within a specific context.

In a fair comparison with BERT, the results reveal that XLNet consistently outperforms BERT across multiple datasets, including SQuAD and MNLI, when both models are trained under similar conditions. The authors present detailed performance metrics showing XLNet's advantages in tasks such as reading comprehension and document ranking, as demonstrated in several tables.

Upon scaling up, XLNet shows improved accuracy compared to other models like RoBERTa and GPT, further emphasizing its effectiveness in natural language processing tasks. The results indicate that XLNet not only benefits from larger datasets but also exhibits inherent improvements over its BERT predecessor. The authors note that their comparisons exclude models like ALBERT due to significant differences in computational requirements, ensuring a fair evaluation of XLNet's capabilities. Overall, the findings suggest that XLNet represents a substantial advancement in the field of natural language understanding.
