This paper presents XLNet, a generalized autoregressive (AR) pretraining method that leverages a permutation language modeling objective. The study compares XLNet's performance against BERT and other models across various tasks, including RACE, SQuAD2.0, MNLI, and SST-2, using a consistent architecture based on a 12-layer configuration with hyperparameters similar to BERT-Base. The results demonstrate that XLNet outperforms BERT, with significant contributions from its integration of Transformer-XL and the permutation language modeling approach.

Key findings include the importance of the memory caching mechanism, which enhances performance on tasks with longer contexts, and the roles of span-based prediction and bidirectional input in improving model efficacy. Interestingly, the next-sentence prediction objective from BERT does not yield improvements in the context of XLNet, prompting its exclusion from the model's design.

The paper concludes that XLNet's architecture and training methodology yield substantial advancements over previous pretraining objectives, establishing it as a potent framework for natural language understanding tasks.
