The paper discusses the limitations of existing autoregressive (AR) language models, particularly BERT, which uses artificial symbols like [MASK] during pretraining that are not present during fine-tuning, leading to a pretrain-finetune discrepancy. BERT's assumption that predicted tokens are independent given unmasked tokens oversimplifies the modeling of high-order, long-range dependencies in natural language.

To address these issues, the authors propose XLNet, a generalized autoregressive method that combines the strengths of AR language modeling and autoencoding (AE) while mitigating their drawbacks. XLNet maximizes the expected log likelihood of a sequence with respect to all possible permutations of the factorization order, allowing each position in the sequence to utilize context from both left and right tokens, thereby capturing bidirectional context.

Additionally, XLNet avoids data corruption and the pretrain-finetune discrepancy inherent in BERT by not relying on masked tokens. It employs an autoregressive objective that facilitates the use of the product rule for joint probability factorization, overcoming BERT's independence assumption.

The paper also highlights enhancements in XLNet's architecture, incorporating segment recurrence mechanisms and relative encoding schemes from Transformer-XL to improve performance on longer text sequences. The authors propose a reparameterization of the Transformer(-XL) network to effectively handle the ambiguity arising from permutation-based language modeling.

Empirical results demonstrate that XLNet consistently outperforms BERT across various tasks, including GLUE language understanding, reading comprehension, and text classification.

In summary, XLNet introduces a novel pretraining objective and architectural improvements to effectively learn bidirectional contexts while addressing the limitations of previous models in autoregressive language modeling.
