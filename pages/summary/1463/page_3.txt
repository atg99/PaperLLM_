The paper discusses the differences between autoregressive (AR) language modeling and BERT's denoising auto-encoding approach, highlighting their respective strengths and weaknesses. BERT constructs a corrupted version of a text sequence by masking a portion of its tokens and aims to reconstruct the masked tokens based on the corrupted input. This method, while effective, relies on an independence assumption for reconstructing masked tokens, which can lead to discrepancies during fine-tuning since the [MASK] symbol does not appear in downstream tasks.

In contrast, AR language modeling does not involve input corruption and factors the joint probability without making independence assumptions, allowing it to leverage the context of preceding tokens. However, AR models only consider tokens to the left, limiting their ability to capture bidirectional context.

To address these limitations, the paper proposes a new pretraining objective called permutation language modeling. This approach combines the advantages of both AR models and BERT by allowing the model to learn from all positions in both directions while maintaining the autoregressive framework. It does so by sampling different factorization orders for the sequence during training, enabling the model to gather contextual information from all tokens.

The permutation objective retains the original sequence order while adjusting the factorization order, ensuring that the model encounters natural text sequences during fine-tuning. This method avoids the independence assumption and the discrepancies associated with BERT's masked tokens, ultimately enhancing the model's ability to understand and predict based on bidirectional context.
