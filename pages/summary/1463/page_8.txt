This paper presents a comprehensive evaluation of several state-of-the-art models, including BERT, RoBERTa, and XLNet, across various datasets and tasks, specifically focusing on reading comprehension and text classification. 

In the SQuAD2.0 and SQuAD1.1 benchmarks, XLNet outperforms BERT and RoBERTa, achieving an Exact Match (EM) score of 87.9 and a F1 score of 90.6 on the dev set for SQuAD2.0, and 89.7 and 95.1 respectively on SQuAD1.1. On the test set leaderboard, XLNet maintains its lead with scores of 87.926 and 90.689. 

For text classification tasks, various models are compared across datasets such as IMDB, Yelp, DBpedia, and Amazon. XLNet shows competitive results, although specific scores for certain datasets are not provided. 

In the GLUE benchmark, the paper highlights the performance of different models, with XLNet achieving strong results in both single-task and multi-task settings, particularly excelling in tasks that require longer context understanding. 

An ablation study is conducted to investigate the impact of specific design choices, including the permutation language modeling objective, the use of Transformer-XL as the architecture, and implementation details like span-based prediction and bidirectional input. The study aims to quantify the contributions of these elements to the overall performance of XLNet compared to BERT.

Overall, the findings underscore the advantages of XLNet in handling complex tasks and its robustness across diverse datasets, emphasizing its architecture and training objectives as key factors in its superior performance.
