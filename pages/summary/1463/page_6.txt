The paper discusses the architectural differences between XLNet and BERT, focusing on the implementation of relative segment encodings in XLNet. Unlike BERT, which uses absolute segment embeddings, XLNet employs a relative encoding approach inspired by Transformer-XL. This method encodes segments based on whether two positions belong to the same segment, enhancing generalization and enabling fine-tuning on tasks with more than two input segments.

The authors highlight that both BERT and XLNet perform partial prediction, which aids in reducing optimization difficulty by only predicting tokens with sufficient context. However, BERT's independence assumption limits its ability to model dependencies between targets, whereas XLNet can capture these dependencies effectively.

The paper also details the pretraining process for XLNet, which utilizes a large dataset including BooksCorpus and English Wikipedia, alongside additional resources like Giga5 and Common Crawl. The authors conducted extensive filtering to ensure data quality, resulting in a significant amount of text for training. The XLNet-Large model mirrors the architecture of BERT-Large while employing a full sequence length of 512 during training. The pretraining was executed on TPU v3 chips over 500K steps, aimed at providing a comprehensive comparison with BERT. 

Overall, the paper emphasizes the advantages of relative segment encoding in XLNet, particularly its capacity to learn dependencies and improve generalization, which are critical for advanced language modeling tasks.
