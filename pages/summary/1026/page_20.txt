The study involved a systematic review of 84 sources related to artificial intelligence, focusing on normative ethical stances. These sources were selected based on specific criteria, including language, institutional origin, and explicit references to AI or related concepts. The research employed qualitative data analysis using Nvivo software, with two researchers independently coding the content in multiple cycles. The first cycle involved inductive coding, resulting in 3457 codes, of which 1180 pertained to ethical principles. Following this, a code mapping process was conducted to minimize bias and categorize the codes into themes, relying on normative ethical literature for guidance. Thirteen ethical categories emerged, with some merged due to thematic similarities. The final analysis involved focused coding to assess significance and frequency of ethical principles, supported by consistency checks against relevant literature and a reflective equilibrium approach to ensure alignment between general principles and specific policy judgments.
