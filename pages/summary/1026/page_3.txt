The paper examines the interests of various stakeholders in shaping AI ethics to align with their priorities. It raises concerns about the private sector's role, suggesting that their involvement may sometimes obscure social issues or evade regulatory scrutiny. The central questions addressed include whether there is a convergence among different groups on the ethical principles for AI and the nature of any divergences that exist. To explore these questions, the authors conducted a scoping review of existing ethical guidelines for AI, aiming to map the global landscape and assess whether a consensus is forming around ethical principles and their implementation.

The results of the review identified 84 documents outlining ethical principles or guidelines for AI, with a notable increase in publications, particularly after 2016. The analysis reveals that most documents were produced by private companies and governmental agencies, followed by academic institutions and inter-governmental organizations. The findings are intended to inform a variety of stakeholders, including scientists and governmental bodies, about the trends in ethically responsible AI innovation.
