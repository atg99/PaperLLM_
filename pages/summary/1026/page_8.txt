The paper discusses the importance of transparency in AI systems, highlighting its justifications, applications, and methods of implementation. Transparency is linked to efforts to enhance explainability and interpretability, which are essential in areas such as data usage, human-AI interaction, automated decision-making, and the ethical purpose of AI applications. The primary aim of transparency is to minimize harm and improve AI systems, while also addressing legal concerns and fostering trust among users. 

To achieve transparency, the paper suggests that AI developers and deployers should disclose various types of information, including the use of AI, source code, data handling, limitations, and the responsibilities associated with AI systems. There is a call for explanations to be provided in non-technical terms to ensure comprehensibility. The paper notes that while audits are proposed mainly by data protection offices and non-profit organizations, the private sector tends to focus on technical solutions. Alternative approaches include oversight, stakeholder interaction, and mechanisms for whistleblowing.

The paper also addresses justice, fairness, and equity in the context of AI, stressing the need to prevent and mitigate bias and discrimination. It emphasizes respect for diversity, inclusion, and equality, while also advocating for the right to appeal decisions and seek redress. Fair access to AI technologies and their benefits is highlighted, along with the public sector's concern regarding AI's impact on the labor market and broader societal issues. The importance of utilizing accurate, complete, and diverse data for training AI systems is also underscored to minimize biases.
