The paper discusses the ethical landscape surrounding artificial intelligence (AI), highlighting a notable imbalance in the focus on non-maleficence over beneficence within the AI community. This trend aligns with the negativity bias, where negative outcomes receive more attention than positive possibilities. Existing guidelines predominantly emphasize protecting privacy, dignity, and autonomy, often overlooking how these principles might be advanced through responsible AI innovation.

Trust in AI is identified as a critical ethical issue, with less than one-third of sources addressing it. While many private sector sources advocate for building public trust through education, some caution that such trust could reduce scrutiny and accountability for AI producers, challenging the prevailing belief that public trust is essential for ethical governance.

The paper also notes a concerning underrepresentation of sustainability and solidarity in AI ethics discourse. The environmental impact of AI, particularly its high energy consumption, is a significant concern, yet discussions often omit the potential for AI to benefit ecosystems. Additionally, solidarity is rarely mentioned outside the context of job loss prevention, despite the potential for AI to contribute positively to humanitarian efforts, as exemplified by initiatives from organizations like the United Nations Office for Project Services. The paper calls for a broader integration of these themes into the ethical narrative surrounding AI.
