The paper discusses the complexities surrounding the ethical guidelines for artificial intelligence (AI), highlighting the need for clarity in several areas. It identifies key issues such as the prioritization of ethical principles, resolution of conflicts between these principles, enforcement of ethical oversight, and compliance by researchers and institutions. A notable gap exists between the formulation of ethical principles and their practical implementation, which cannot be addressed solely through technical solutions or top-down approaches.

While no single ethical principle is universally endorsed, several principles—transparency, justice and fairness, non-maleficence, responsibility, and privacy—are frequently mentioned across guidelines. This suggests a potential convergence towards these principles in global AI policy. The emphasis on transparency, justice, and fairness indicates a growing moral urgency to ensure transparent processes in AI development and use, as well as to mitigate risks of increased inequality if these considerations are neglected. 

The themes of transparency and justice are closely linked to the concept of responsibility, suggesting that promoting these values entails greater accountability for AI developers and users. The paper argues that transparency should be viewed as a proethical condition that supports other ethical practices rather than an ethical principle in itself. Despite the emphasis on responsibility and accountability in current guidelines, there is a lack of focus on the integrity of stakeholders involved in AI. This disconnect points to a broader issue where existing guidelines do not adequately translate principles into actionable requirements, leaving many principles vague or unconnected to necessary actions for their realization.
