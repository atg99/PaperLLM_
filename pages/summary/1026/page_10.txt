The paper discusses the multifaceted considerations surrounding the implementation of artificial intelligence (AI), emphasizing the need for monitoring through audits and assessments by various stakeholders, including internal units and independent entities, particularly in relation to established standards for AI outcomes. It highlights the concept of 'dual-use' risks, suggesting that potential damages from AI should be assessed, reduced, and mitigated, with clear attribution of liability emphasized.

The paper raises issues of responsibility and accountability in AI, noting that while 'responsible AI' is frequently referenced, definitions remain vague. Recommendations include acting with integrity and clarifying legal liability in contracts. It contrasts approaches that focus on underlying processes leading to harm with the importance of whistleblowing and promoting diversity in AI development. There is ongoing debate regarding who is responsible for AI's actions, whether it be developers, designers, or institutions, and whether AI itself should be held accountable.

Privacy is presented as both a fundamental value and a right, often linked to data protection and security. The paper categorizes suggested methods for achieving privacy into technical solutions, calls for further research and awareness, and regulatory measures. It advocates for legal compliance and the adaptation of regulations to address AI's unique challenges, including potential certification processes. Overall, the paper underscores the complexity of ethical considerations in AI development, particularly regarding responsibility, accountability, and privacy.
