The paper discusses the evolving landscape of ethical considerations in artificial intelligence (AI) through the lens of the "Ethically Aligned Design" initiative. It emphasizes the necessity of soft governance mechanisms, like Independent Review Boards (IRBs), to evaluate the ethical implications of AI applications in scientific research, particularly in academia. However, such oversight may not extend to AI applications by governments or private entities without significant changes to IRB authority.

The international consensus highlights the importance of principles such as transparency, non-maleficence, responsibility, and privacy in ethical AI development. The authors argue for the inclusion of underrepresented ethical principles, such as human dignity, solidarity, and sustainability, to enhance the ethical framework surrounding AI. They advocate for a shift from merely formulating principles to implementing them in practice, suggesting that a global agenda for ethical AI should harmonize cross-national and cross-domain efforts while respecting cultural diversity and moral pluralism.

The study acknowledges several limitations, including the reliance on gray literature, which is less accessible and replicable than peer-reviewed sources. To address this, the authors developed a discovery and eligibility protocol. Additionally, language bias may have affected the corpus, skewing results towards English-language documents. The qualitative nature of the content analysis presents its own challenges, though these were mitigated through an inductive coding strategy involving independent reviewers. Lastly, the paper notes the potential for rapid changes in AI guidance documents, which may impact ongoing research and policy development.
