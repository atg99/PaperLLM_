The paper presents an analysis of ethical principles identified in existing AI guidelines, highlighting a range of principles across 84 documents. The most frequently mentioned principle is transparency, appearing in 73 documents, followed by justice and fairness (68), non-maleficence and responsibility (60 each), and privacy (47). Other principles such as beneficence, freedom and autonomy, trust, sustainability, dignity, and solidarity are mentioned less frequently.

Despite the prominence of these principles, the analysis indicates that no single ethical principle is universally agreed upon across all documents. However, there is a noted convergence around transparency, justice and fairness, non-maleficence, responsibility, and privacy, with these principles referenced in over half of the sources. 

The thematic analysis further uncovers significant variations in how these principles are interpreted and the recommendations or concerns associated with them. The detailed thematic evaluation that follows aims to explore these divergences and provide a clearer understanding of the ethical landscape in AI guidelines.
