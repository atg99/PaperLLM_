The paper presents a geographic analysis of the issuers of ethical AI guidelines, revealing that the majority of documents are produced in the United States (20 documents) and the European Union (19 documents), followed by the United Kingdom (14 documents) and Japan (4 documents). Other countries such as Canada, Iceland, Norway, the United Arab Emirates, India, Singapore, South Korea, and Australia each have one document. The G7 member states are emphasized for their collective endorsement of a distinct statement.

The analysis of target audiences indicates that a significant number of guidelines (27 documents or 32.1%) are aimed at multiple stakeholder groups, while 24 documents (28.6%) are self-directed towards stakeholders within the issuing organization. Additionally, some guidelines focus on the public sector (10 documents, 11.9%), the private sector (5 documents, 6.0%), and specific stakeholders such as developers (3 documents, 3.6%), organizations (1 document, 1.2%), and researchers (1 document, 1.2%). Notably, 13 sources (15.5%) do not specify their intended audience.

The content analysis identifies eleven key ethical values and principles prevalent in these guidelines: transparency, justice and fairness, non-maleficence, responsibility, privacy, beneficence, freedom and autonomy, trust, dignity, sustainability, and solidarity. These principles reflect the ethical considerations that guide the development and implementation of AI technologies.
