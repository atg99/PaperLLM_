The paper discusses the complexities surrounding the development of ethical AI in the context of increasing humanitarian costs due to climate change. It highlights the intertwined principles of sustainability and solidarity, which are not adequately represented compared to other ethical principles. While there is a general agreement on the need for ethical AI, significant divergences exist in the interpretation and implementation of these principles. For instance, the push for larger datasets to reduce bias conflicts with the need for individual control over personal data to ensure privacy. Similarly, the necessity to avoid harm can clash with the need to balance risks and benefits, which may vary depending on whose well-being is prioritized.

These discrepancies pose challenges for creating a unified global agenda for ethical AI, emphasizing the need for cooperative efforts among government organizations to harmonize AI policies without disregarding cultural and moral pluralism. The paper advocates for the development of deliberative mechanisms to address disagreements among stakeholders from diverse global backgrounds. It also notes that harmonization in technology governance often involves standardization efforts, as demonstrated by initiatives from organizations like the Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers. The overarching challenge remains to balance the need for international alignment with the respect for cultural diversity in the governance of AI.
