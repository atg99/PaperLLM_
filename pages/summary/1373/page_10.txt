The paper discusses the diversity of agent-based programming approaches, ranging from traditional models like BDI to more innovative simulation and planning techniques. It notes the integration of agent concepts with other programming paradigms, especially object-oriented programming, as a means to attract novice programmers who may find pure agent programming intimidating. While hybrid languages can serve specific applications, the paper argues that pure agent programming languages are essential for fully addressing the complexities of agent-based abstractions.

A significant limitation identified is the lack of public implementation for some agent programming languages, which hinders their usability and comparability. The authors call for more in-depth studies to define fundamental features of agent-oriented programming and to assess how these features fit within various agency models. Furthermore, the paper highlights the challenges of conducting quantitative performance comparisons due to the rapid evolution of programming languages, particularly in academia.

To foster community engagement and facilitate evaluations, the authors advocate for the creation of agent-specific benchmarks. They also suggest leveraging existing complex scenarios from the annual Multi-Agent Programming Contest (MAPC) to develop realistic case studies that could better demonstrate the practical applicability of agent programming languages. 

In conclusion, the paper underscores the need for advancements in APLs and methodologies to increase the adoption of agent technology, while also addressing the perceived lack of incentives for developers to transition to agent-oriented programming.
