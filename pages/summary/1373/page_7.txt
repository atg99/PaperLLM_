The paper discusses various programming languages and frameworks designed for agent-oriented programming (AOP) and multi-agent systems (MAS), highlighting their unique features and applications. PLACE is introduced as an HTN planner that incorporates action durations and a plan repairing mechanism, enabling agents to adapt to unexpected environmental changes. The Programming Language for Synchronous Agents (PLASA) is noted for its platform independence and human-readable syntax, facilitating cooperative applications among robots in dynamic settings.

The Relational Model Multi-Agent System (RMAS) is presented as a database-centric approach for integrating reasoning and control in Cyber-Physical Systems, utilizing Matlab and SQLite. SARL aims to provide a minimalistic yet extensible language for AOP, focusing on key concepts such as concurrency and dynamic reconfiguration, while creating its own Domain-Specific Language.

The paper addresses the gap between Agent-Oriented Software Engineering (AOSE) methodologies and agent-based modeling and simulation (ABMS), proposing the PEABS process to enhance simulation efficiency. It also explores cognitive agent architectures based on the BDI model integrated into the GAMA modeling language, emphasizing user-friendliness for non-experts.

ALLEGRO and YAGI are mentioned as high-level control programming formalisms for robotic applications, with YAGI providing bindings to popular robotics frameworks. Lastly, the Cognitive Affective Agent Programming Framework (CAAF) enables agents to compute emotions based on belief-desire theory, enhancing their cognitive capabilities.
