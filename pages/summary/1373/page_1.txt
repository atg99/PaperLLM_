This paper provides a systematic review of agent-based programming for multi-agent systems (MASs), an area within artificial intelligence that focuses on intelligent and autonomous agents. These agents operate by making decisions based on available information about their environment and other agents. The review discusses both established and new agent programming languages, their extensions, and comparative studies of these languages. It highlights the applications of agent programming found in the literature.

The introduction outlines the significance of MASs in AI, noting their rapid development and the abundance of research, tools, and conferences in this field. Despite this growth, MASs are underutilized, primarily due to the lack of incentive for developers to transition to agent programming languages. The paper explains that many behaviors easily implemented in mainstream programming languages do not encourage the adoption of specialized agent programming languages.

The authors describe intelligent agents as entities capable of reasoning, autonomy, social collaboration, context perception, and proactive action. They draw a parallel between agent-oriented programming (AOP) and object-oriented programming (OOP), emphasizing that agents serve as the foundational elements in AOP, similar to objects in OOP. The paper concludes by pointing out that agents are particularly effective in applications requiring distributed computation and communication among components, making agent technology relevant for various reasoning tasks.
