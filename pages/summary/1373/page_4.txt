This paper presents a systematic review of agent-based programming languages, extensions, comparisons, and applications. The review process involved identifying 400 records through database searches, which were subsequently screened to yield 266 records after removing duplicates. Out of these, 69 full-text articles were assessed for eligibility, leading to the inclusion of 43 studies in qualitative synthesis and 15 in quantitative synthesis or meta-analysis. The review highlights the prevalence of Belief-Desire-Intention (BDI) as a dominant model in agent programming languages. The findings are organized into sections covering various aspects of agent-based programming, establishing a comprehensive overview of the current state of research in the field.
