The paper provides a comprehensive review of the advancements in agent programming, particularly focusing on BDI-based approaches. It highlights the integration of artificial intelligence techniques into agent programming languages as a critical challenge for future research, essential for broader adoption of agent-oriented programming (AOP).

The authors recommend that further research should address usability issues prevalent in current agent programming languages (APLs). Despite the introduction of various frameworks and models in the last five years, the usability of these languages remains a significant barrier to widespread usage. Many APLs exhibit expressiveness and power but suffer from inadequate documentation, maintenance, and comparative evaluations, which deter potential users.

The review categorizes contributions to the field into four areas: programming languages, their extensions, comparisons, and applications. It notes that while many extensions and new languages have been proposed, a substantial number lack practical implementation and effective evaluation. The authors stress the importance of qualitative and quantitative comparisons to identify gaps and drive improvements in existing languages.

Furthermore, the paper identifies a scarcity of real-world applications of APLs. The authors advocate for more application-based research to demonstrate the practical features of agent-based programming, thereby enhancing its usability and acceptance outside the agent community.

The research was funded by the UK Industrial Strategy Challenge Fund, and the authors declare no conflicts of interest. Abbreviations used in the manuscript include AI for artificial intelligence, AOP for agent-oriented programming, and AOSE for agent-oriented software engineering.
