The Situation Calculus model focuses on representing and reasoning about dynamic worlds, allowing agents to understand changes in their environment over time. This model supports the formalization of actions and their effects, which is crucial for agents operating in complex and ever-changing situations.

The paper systematically reviews the advancements in agent-based programming, emphasizing the latest languages, platforms, and frameworks for developing multi-agent systems (MASs). It encompasses both theoretical and practical perspectives, thereby bridging the gap between the two. The review aims to provide insights for both experts and non-experts, aiding their comprehension of current trends and future research directions in the field.

The structure of the paper includes a historical overview of agent-based programming, the methodology for the systematic review, findings from the literature, discussions on the results, and concluding remarks. By covering a broad spectrum of agent programming languages and avoiding a narrow focus on specific areas, the paper contributes significantly to the understanding of the state of agent-based programming technologies.
