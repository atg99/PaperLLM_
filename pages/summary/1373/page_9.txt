the paper presents a comprehensive overview of agent programming languages (APL) and their applications in multi-agent systems (MAS). It highlights the lack of thorough evaluations of various APLs, noting that some comparative studies have shown that agent languages like Jason can compete with actor-based languages. The paper discusses an evaluation framework for assessing domain-specific modeling languages for MAS, focusing on both qualitative and quantitative aspects.

Additionally, it reviews the comparison of pseudocode algorithms with implementations in specific languages like JADEL and evaluates parallel platforms for multi-agent simulations. The authors also delve into the diverse applications of agent-based programming, emphasizing its utility across various domains, such as the Multi-Agent Programming Contest, which fosters research through complex benchmarking scenarios.

Moreover, the paper cites recent applications of agent-based models in simulating COVID-19 transmission and in the energy sector, particularly for microgrid control and optimization. It references studies that explore the interaction between human activities and land-use changes, as well as the development of interactive modeling environments like FlowLogo for groundwater modeling. Finally, it discusses the potential of agents in creating self-managed Internet of Things systems, highlighting their distributed and adaptive capabilities.
