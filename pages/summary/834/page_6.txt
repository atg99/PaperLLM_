The paper discusses the trade-off between model size and performance metrics in the context of quantization techniques for deep learning models, particularly focusing on InceptionV3. It highlights the importance of determining appropriate bit precision settings for various layers of the model to optimize performance while maintaining a desired model size. 

Key findings include the identification of a block-diagonal structure in the Hessian matrix, allowing for efficient computation of Hessian traces for different inputs. This structure enables the averaging of Hessian traces across blocks, which can be computed quickly without needing to process the entire dataset. 

A significant limitation of traditional relative sensitivity analysis is its inability to specify exact bit precision settings for layers, despite indicating which layers are more sensitive. The paper proposes a method to reduce the search space for bit precision settings by applying sensitivity constraints based on average Hessian traces, thus making the selection process more manageable.

The authors introduce a Pareto frontier approach to efficiently navigate the reduced search space of bit precision settings. This method ranks the settings based on the total second-order perturbation they induce, as defined by a specific metric. The findings suggest that this approach can lead to better automated bit selection, enhancing the quantization process while maintaining model performance.
