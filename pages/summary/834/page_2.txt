The paper discusses advancements in mixed-precision quantization for neural networks, particularly focusing on the ResNet20 architecture. While quantization can enhance model efficiency without significantly impacting accuracy, ultra-low-bit quantization often leads to performance degradation. The paper reviews various methods such as non-uniform quantizers, progressive quantization-aware fine-tuning, and existing mixed-precision approaches, highlighting the challenges posed by the vast search space for optimal bit settings across network layers.

The authors note that current techniques, including reinforcement learning and differentiable neural architecture search (DNAS), either require excessive computational resources or are sensitive to parameter initialization, complicating real-time applications. To mitigate these issues, they propose HAWQ-V2, which builds upon the HAWQ framework. Key contributions of HAWQ-V2 include:

1. A theoretical analysis indicating that the average Hessian trace serves as a more effective sensitivity metric than the top eigenvalue heuristic used in HAWQ.
2. The introduction of a Pareto-frontier based method to automate the determination of bit precision settings across layers, eliminating the need for manual intervention.
3. The development of a fast algorithm utilizing Hutchinson’s method to compute Hessian trace information efficiently in PyTorch.

These enhancements aim to improve the accuracy and efficiency of mixed-precision quantization in neural networks, addressing the limitations of existing methods.
