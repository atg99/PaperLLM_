The paper presents an analysis of the average Hessian trace of different blocks in two convolutional neural networks, InceptionV3 and ResNet50, on the ImageNet dataset. It highlights the significant differences in the average Hessian trace across various blocks, indicating how the sensitivity of each layer to perturbations can vary.

The authors introduce a lemma that establishes a relationship between the loss landscape of two layers, denoted as B1 and B2, when quantized with the same perturbation magnitude. The lemma asserts that the loss function's behavior is consistent across these layers under certain conditions. The proof relies on Taylor's expansion, demonstrating that the loss function remains locally convex and that the trace of the Hessian can be used as a measure of sensitivity.

Additionally, the paper discusses the challenges associated with computing the Hessian trace due to the lack of direct access to the Hessian matrix elements. The authors emphasize that while calculating the Hessian may seem daunting, understanding its properties can provide valuable insights into the sensitivity of neural network layers to quantization and perturbations. Overall, the findings underscore the importance of analyzing Hessian traces in understanding neural network behavior.
