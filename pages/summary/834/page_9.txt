The paper presents quantization results for RetinaNet-ResNet50 on the Microsoft COCO 2017 dataset, highlighting the performance of various quantization methods including direct quantization, mixed-precision quantization, and the state-of-the-art HAWQ-V2. HAWQ-V2 demonstrates significant improvements in mean Average Precision (mAP) while maintaining a compact model size, outperforming previous methods by notable margins.

The study includes an ablation analysis to emphasize the importance of selecting bit-precision settings that minimize model perturbation. It shows that HAWQ-V2 achieves over 1% higher accuracy with reduced model size compared to settings that induce larger perturbations. Additionally, the paper compares the effectiveness of using the average Hessian trace as a sensitivity metric versus using simple parameter perturbation, finding that HAWQ-V2 with the Hessian trace yields better accuracy while maintaining a smaller model size.

The authors conclude that their proposed mixed-precision quantization method and efficient computation of the Hessian trace through matrix-free algorithms lead to state-of-the-art results in both image classification and object detection tasks. Future work is suggested to explore hardware-specific metrics, eliminate floating-point arithmetic, and enhance training efficiency by leveraging the Hessian for adjusting precision.
