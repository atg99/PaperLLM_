The paper discusses HAWQ-V2, an advanced method for determining bit precision settings in model quantization. The approach leverages second-order perturbation analysis, specifically focusing on the average Hessian trace and L2 norms of quantization perturbations across different model layers. By sorting these elements, HAWQ-V2 selects bit precision settings that minimize perturbation, which has been shown to enhance generalization post quantization-aware fine-tuning. This method simplifies the precision selection process, eliminating the manual effort required in previous methods like HAWQ, and achieves superior performance with minimal time cost.

Empirical results illustrate the effectiveness of HAWQ-V2 through the application of Hutchinson’s method for Hessian trace estimation. The algorithm converges rapidly with sufficient iterations, allowing for efficient computation of average Hessian traces across layers of models like ResNet50. HAWQ-V2 demonstrates significant speed improvements, being up to 120 times faster than prior searching algorithms.

In practical applications, HAWQ-V2 outperforms other quantization methods on ResNet50 and InceptionV3. It achieves higher accuracy without relying on traditional quantization heuristics or manual adjustments, indicating that the automatic selection of bit precision settings leads to better model performance. The results highlight the advantages of using second-order information for precision selection in quantization tasks.
