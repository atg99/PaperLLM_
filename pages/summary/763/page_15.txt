The paper discusses the Expando-Mono-Duo design pattern for text ranking utilizing pretrained sequence-to-sequence models, specifically focusing on the duoT5-3B model's performance on the MS MARCO passage development set. It evaluates the effectiveness of different candidate sizes (k1) in reranking passages, illustrating that lower values of k1 yield better gains in mean reciprocal rank (MRR) due to the impact of reshuffling results among top ranks. The results indicate that effectiveness tends to decline with increasing k1, except for the Sym-Sum method, and highlight that the team's leaderboard submissions utilized k1=50 with Sym-Sum.

The paper also presents findings from the TREC 2020 Deep Learning Track on passage ranking, detailing various configurations of the Expand-Mono-Duo design. The results demonstrate that query expansion through pseudo-relevance feedback significantly improves ranking effectiveness, as shown by comparisons between standard models like BM25 and those enhanced by RM3. Notably, the authors emphasize the importance of having dense relevance judgments in TREC for validating the effectiveness of query expansion, contrasting this with the sparse judgments from the MS MARCO dataset that do not adequately reward such methods.

Furthermore, the study reveals that applying RM3 on top of a doc2query-T5 expanded index yields beneficial effects in several metrics, although not in nDCG@10. It highlights the effectiveness of mono/duoT5 reranking, where pairs of configurations are analyzed to show improvements in ranking without increasing recall, emphasizing the robustness of the reranking approach in the context of the evaluation.
