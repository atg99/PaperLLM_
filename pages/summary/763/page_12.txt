The paper discusses the first-stage retrieval process used in the TREC-COVID initiative, which involves combining results from three indexes through reciprocal rank fusion. A key takeaway from the initial round of TREC-COVID was the significance of effective query formulation. The information needs were structured into three fields: "query," "question," and "narrative," with empirical results indicating that combining the "query" and "question" fields yielded the best performance. 

Researchers from the University of Delaware developed a query generation technique that enhanced queries by utilizing non-stopwords from the query field and named entities from the question field, which was shared with participants in subsequent rounds. This led to two main query generation approaches: fusion1, which used concatenated "query" and "question" fields, and fusion2, which employed the UDel query generator.

In rounds 2 through 5, these fusion runs served as community baselines, allowing participants to build upon them. The authors further refined their approach by applying document expansion using doc2query-T5, resulting in augmented indexes for retrieval. The reranking pipeline was designed to process outputs from both fusion runs and their expanded versions.

Additionally, the paper details the use of the monoT5 model, which was fine-tuned on the MS MARCO passage dataset and further on the Med-MARCO subset, aimed at improving retrieval effectiveness with medical queries. Overall, the study highlights the iterative improvements in query formulation and retrieval strategies throughout the TREC-COVID rounds.
