The paper discusses the Expando-Mono-Duo design pattern for text ranking using pretrained sequence-to-sequence models, specifically focusing on the application of monoT5 and duoT5. 

In stage H1, the monoT5 model serves as a pointwise reranker that evaluates the relevance of documents retrieved in stage H0 by estimating a score indicating how relevant a document is to a given query. This relevance classification approach aims to improve ranking metrics, such as Mean Average Precision (MAP) or Mean Reciprocal Rank (MRR), compared to the initial rankings. MonoT5, an adaptation of the monoBERT model, utilizes T5, a popular transformer model, where ranking tasks are framed as sequence-to-sequence tasks. The model is fine-tuned to output "true" or "false" based on document relevance during inference, with probabilities computed via softmax applied to these outputs.

Stage H2 introduces duoT5, a pairwise reranker that processes pairs of documents to determine which is more relevant to a query. It estimates the probability that one document is more relevant than another, using a sequence format that incorporates both documents alongside the query. DuoT5 builds upon the foundational work of duoBERT, adapting the methodology to T5. 

The paper highlights the challenges of handling long texts that exceed the input limits of transformer models and suggests that effective solutions have been developed, with further details provided in a subsequent section. Overall, the design pattern aims to enhance text ranking performance through advanced reranking techniques leveraging pretrained models.
