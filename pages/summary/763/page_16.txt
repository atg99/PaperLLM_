This paper discusses the effectiveness of various retrieval techniques in the TREC 2020 Deep Learning Track Passage Ranking Task and the MS MARCO Document Ranking Task. The authors present a series of experiments comparing different configurations that incorporate BM25, RM3, and document expansion techniques such as doc2query-T5 and monoT5/duoT5 models.

The results indicate that incorporating reranking significantly improves effectiveness across all first-stage configurations. Notably, conditions that include document expansion outperform those relying solely on pseudo-relevance feedback, suggesting that while pseudo-relevance feedback enhances recall, its contribution to overall effectiveness is less clear when document expansion is employed. The best performance, as indicated by metrics like nDCG@10, was achieved without query expansion, highlighting the potential superiority of document expansion methods.

Additionally, the paper points out the limitations of comparing document and query expansion due to the inherent differences in input lengths and contexts. The authors argue that document expansion, which can utilize longer texts, may provide more contextual information than query expansion, which typically deals with shorter queries.

In the MS MARCO document ranking task, the authors note the constraints imposed by the evaluation process and emphasize the focus on unique experimental conditions. The findings suggest that reranking and document expansion are crucial for enhancing retrieval effectiveness, warranting further investigation in future research.
