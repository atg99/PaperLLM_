The experimental results from applying the Expando-Mono-Duo design pattern demonstrate its effectiveness across five ad hoc retrieval tasks, consistently achieving near state-of-the-art performance.

The paper details the methodologies used for fine-tuning and reranking, highlighting the importance of using a sliding window approach and maximizing passage scores to derive overall article scores. The authors emphasize that while they did not utilize task-specific data for fine-tuning, the model's performance remained competitive, showcasing the robustness of the Expando-Mono-Duo framework.

In the MS MARCO passage ranking task, the authors present various configurations and their corresponding results, noting that their best configuration was at the top of a competitive leaderboard at the time of submission. They provide comparative scores from other leading models, emphasizing that their approach is effective, though not the absolute best, indicating that ongoing advancements in the field could further enhance their methodology.

Overall, the findings reinforce the potential of the Expando-Mono-Duo design pattern, particularly in leveraging larger sequence-to-sequence models like T5 for improved text ranking outcomes.
