The paper presents the Expando-Mono-Duo design pattern for text ranking using pretrained sequence-to-sequence models, evaluated through multiple rounds of submissions in the TREC-COVID competition. The results are summarized across five rounds, each containing varying numbers of topics and different model combinations.

In Round 1, the best performing submission, sabir.meta.docs, achieved an nDCG@10 of 0.6080, followed by T5R1 monoT5-3B at 0.5223. Round 2 saw an improvement with the GUIR_S2_run1 submission reaching an nDCG@10 of 0.6251, closely followed by covidex.t5 monoT5-3B at 0.6250. Round 3 continued this trend, with SFDC-fus12-enc23-tf3 achieving the highest nDCG@10 of 0.6867, while r3.duot5 combined monoT5-3B and duoT5-3B to reach 0.6626.

In Rounds 4 and 5, the submissions showcased the effectiveness of the Expando-Mono-Duo pattern, with covidex.r4.d2q.duot5 reaching the highest nDCG@20 of 0.7219 in Round 4, and covidex.r5.d2q.2s achieving 0.7539 in Round 5. The paper highlights that different metrics were used in the initial rounds compared to the final two, and notes the strategic limitations imposed by the competition rules on the number of submissions.

Overall, the findings underscore the utility of the Expando-Mono-Duo design pattern in enhancing text ranking performance, with incremental component rollouts revealing various effective configurations. The results provide a comparative analysis against other teams' submissions, emphasizing the pattern's potential in improving ranking systems.
