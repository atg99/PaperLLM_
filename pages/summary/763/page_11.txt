The paper discusses the TREC-COVID challenge organized by NIST in response to the COVID-19 pandemic, aimed at improving access to reliable scientific evidence. The evaluation process spanned five rounds from April to August 2020, using the CORD-19 dataset, and involved evolving information needs as new topics were introduced. Each round's relevance judgments were gathered using standard pooling methods, with previously judged documents excluded from consideration in subsequent rounds.

The authors detail their participation in TREC-COVID, focusing on the Expando-Mono-Duo design pattern. They utilized a model trained for the MS MARCO passage ranking task without fine-tuning it on the CORD-19 corpus. Due to computational constraints, expansions were generated only from article abstracts, employing a sliding window method to manage input length. The generated queries demonstrated surprising quality, indicating that the pretrained model effectively handled scientific text even without domain-specific fine-tuning.

Starting from round 2, the authors implemented a fusion approach combining results from three distinct indexes: one for titles and abstracts, another for full-text articles, and a paragraph-level index that segmented full-text articles into individual paragraphs. This strategy allowed for the retrieval of multiple paragraphs, with the highest-scoring paragraph representing the article's score. This method enhanced the retrieval effectiveness by leveraging diverse sources of evidence.
