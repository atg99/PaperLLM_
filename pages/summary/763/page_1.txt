The paper introduces the "Expando-Mono-Duo" design pattern for addressing text ranking challenges in ad hoc retrieval tasks. This design pattern leverages pretrained sequence-to-sequence models within a multi-stage ranking architecture, diverging from traditional encoder-only models like BERT. The approach consists of three core components: 

1. **Expando**: This involves document expansion techniques that enhance keyword representations of texts before they are indexed, referred to as "doc2query."
2. **Mono**: A pointwise reranking model that refines initial candidate retrieval generated through keyword search.
3. **Duo**: A pairwise reranking model that further optimizes the ranking of candidates.

The authors present experimental validation of this design pattern across various benchmark datasets, including MS MARCO, TREC 2020 Deep Learning Track, and TREC-COVID. The results demonstrate that the Expando-Mono-Duo pattern achieves effectiveness that is at or near state-of-the-art levels, often employing a zero-shot approach that does not require task-specific training data.

The paper emphasizes the generalizability of this design pattern for different text ranking tasks, supporting its claim by providing open-source implementations in the Pyserini IR toolkit and PyGaggle neural reranking library. The authors conclude that their findings justify considering this approach as a formal design pattern for text ranking in information retrieval contexts.
