In this paper, the authors Ronak Pradeep, Rodrigo Nogueira, and Jimmy Lin explore aggregation techniques for scoring documents based on pairwise relevance scores during the inference phase. Four methods are proposed: Sum, Sum-log, Sym-Sum, and Sym-Sum-Log. The Sum method calculates a total score for a document by summing the pairwise scores, while Sum-log incorporates logarithmic transformation to penalize lower scores. Sym-Sum and Sym-Sum-Log extend these methods by including the score for the probability of the false token, enhancing stability and accuracy in scoring.

The candidates are reranked based on these scores to produce a final list. The duoT5 model, while computationally intensive due to the extensive inference required, is designed to improve early precision in ranking. The paper emphasizes that the final output is intended for practical use, either by human evaluators or downstream applications.

The experimental validation of the proposed design pattern is conducted across five different tasks, including the MS MARCO passage and document ranking tasks, as well as evaluations in the TREC 2020 Deep Learning Track and the TREC-COVID challenge. Specifically, the MS MARCO dataset consists of 8.8 million passages, with detailed statistics on passage lengths and the distribution of query-relevant pairs provided. The authors aim to demonstrate the effectiveness of their Expando-Mono-Duo design in varying contexts.
