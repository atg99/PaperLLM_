The paper discusses the Expando-Mono-Duo design pattern for text ranking using pretrained sequence-to-sequence models, specifically in the context of passage ranking tasks. It evaluates models on the MS MARCO passage ranking dataset and the TREC 2020 Deep Learning Track, noting differences in the number of queries and relevance judgments. The MS MARCO dataset is characterized by sparse relevance judgments, while TREC employs denser judgments through traditional pooling techniques.

In the first stage of the proposed pipeline, documents in the MS MARCO corpus are expanded using queries generated by the doc2query-T5 model. This model was fine-tuned on (query, relevant passage) pairs from the dataset, employing a specific training setup with a learning rate of 10^-3 over 4,000 iterations. The paper highlights that top-k sampling, with k set to 10, produced superior queries compared to beam search, leading to better ranking effectiveness.

The authors report on the computational aspects of their experiments, noting that fine-tuning on a Google TPU v3-8 took under 1.5 hours, while generating queries for the entire corpus required approximately 40 hours. The inference process is designed to be parallelizable, ensuring efficiency in handling the large dataset. Overall, the study emphasizes the importance of query expansion and the effectiveness of using pretrained models in text ranking tasks.
