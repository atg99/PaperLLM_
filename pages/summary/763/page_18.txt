They present various configurations combining BM25, RM3, and document/query expansion techniques using monoT5-3B and duoT5-3B models, evaluating their performance through metrics such as MAP, nDCG@10, MRR, and R@1K.

The results indicate that while applying pseudo-relevance feedback through RM3 improves recall metrics, it does not enhance other performance indicators when used with doc2query-T5. The findings suggest that document expansion techniques are generally more effective than query expansion alone, as evidenced by the comparison of various conditions. Specifically, conditions that included document expansion consistently outperformed those relying solely on query expansion.

In the TREC-COVID evaluation, the authors highlight the automatic run submissions, which were conducted without manual intervention, contrasting them with feedback and manual categories. The paper emphasizes the importance of understanding the limitations of comparing scores across rounds due to different evaluation methodologies.

Overall, the study reinforces previous findings regarding the effectiveness of document expansion and provides insights into the interplay between different ranking strategies in information retrieval tasks.
