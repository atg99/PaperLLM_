Table 1 demonstrates the impact of titles on accuracy and Kappa scores for the ESIM and transformer models, indicating that adding titles significantly enhances performance, particularly in Kappa scores. Table 2 further explores the effects of concatenating evidence, revealing that transformer models outperform ESIM across various configurations, especially when titles are included.

Table 3 presents retrieval rates, showing a notable increase in evidence retrieval as additional features, like named entities and genre-specific elements, are incorporated. The highest retrieval rate is achieved with entire articles, indicating the importance of comprehensive data in enhancing retrieval effectiveness.

Table 4 summarizes the FEVER scores for several systems, with results indicating that the integration of named entities and film-related features leads to improved performance. The authors emphasize that the imbalance in the FEVER One dataset complicates model training, necessitating class reweighting for the ESIM model to avoid bias towards the majority class. The use of Cohen’s Kappa is justified as a more reliable evaluation metric compared to accuracy, particularly in unbalanced datasets. Overall, the paper advocates for the effectiveness of transformer networks over ESIM in handling the complexities of the FEVER dataset, particularly when enhanced with title and entity information.
