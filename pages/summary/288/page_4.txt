Initial evidence retrieval achieved a rate of 66.1% with a baseline system, which utilized TFIDF to select relevant articles and sentences. Subsequent enhancements, such as incorporating titles into the TFIDF calculations and using named entity recognition, significantly improved retrieval rates to 80.8% and then to 81.2% for specific cases like film titles.

Further optimization involved expanding the number of retrieved sentences from five to a maximum of fifty per article, resulting in a dramatic increase in evidence retrieval to 90.1%. This expansion, despite increasing computational demands, allowed for a broader base of evidence to support claims. The study found that evaluating evidence separately rather than in groups yielded better performance metrics. Final evaluations on the test set showed improvements in FEVER scores, with the system that used entire articles achieving notable rankings in both FEVER score and evidence F1.

Overall, the findings underscore the importance of effective evidence retrieval in enhancing the performance of entailment classifiers, with a focus on minimizing heuristics and leveraging the capabilities of Transformer Networks for classification tasks.
