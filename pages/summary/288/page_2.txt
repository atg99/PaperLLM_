The proposed architecture consists of twelve blocks, each incorporating a multi-head masked self-attention layer, layer normalization, a feed-forward network, and an additional layer normalization. After the twelfth block, the model splits into two branches: one for entailment classification using matrix multiplication and softmax, and another for next-token prediction based on a hidden state multiplied by token embeddings. The language modeling branch is pre-trained on the BookCorpus dataset, and both branches are fine-tuned on the FEVER dataset.

The study introduces a reframing of entailment by evaluating individual premise statements instead of concatenating them, as done in the baseline FEVER system. Training data is gathered based on the top five sentences with the highest TFIDF scores related to a claim from Wikipedia, with sentences labeled according to their truth values. This results in two configurations: "FEVER One," which evaluates sentences individually, and "FEVER Five," which uses concatenated sentences as in the baseline approach. The authors note a small subset of claims requiring multiple statements as evidence, which they chose not to address extensively.

To classify claims based on individual evidence sentences, three rules are applied: supporting evidence leads to a supported classification, refuting evidence with no supporting evidence results in a refuted classification, and lack of supporting or refuting evidence classifies the claim as insufficiently supported. The approach resolves conflicts in favor of supporting evidence, especially in cases of ambiguous entities.

To enhance the model's understanding of context, the title of the Wikipedia page is included in the data, which is termed "FEVER Title One." The baseline system achieves an accuracy of 0.505 using Decomposable Attention, while implementing the Enhanced Sequential Inference Model (ESIM) improves this to 0.561. The study acknowledges potential confusion with named entities due to the handling of out-of-vocabulary words and suggests a method to allocate multiple indices for such words to improve model performance.
