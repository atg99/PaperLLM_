This paper presents a tutorial on Bayesian Networks (BNs) for researchers studying psychopathology, emphasizing their utility in identifying causal relationships among symptoms in mental disorders. BNs represent conditional independence relationships through directed acyclic graphs (DAGs), allowing for a clearer interpretation of causal effects compared to traditional partial correlation networks, which lack directional edges. The authors aim to educate researchers on how to estimate BNs in R using three algorithm families, illustrated with an empirical dataset on depressive symptoms.

The paper situates BNs within the broader context of network theory in psychopathology, which views mental disorders as arising from interactions among symptoms. While existing network models primarily focus on pairwise interactions, they often fail to provide causal insights due to their undirected nature. BNs address this limitation by enabling researchers to discern causal directions, thus enhancing the understanding of symptom interactions.

The authors also discuss common challenges and questions regarding BNs, advocating for their adoption in psychological research to improve causal inference in the study of mental health. The tutorial includes practical guidance and links to supplementary materials, such as data and code, to assist researchers in applying these concepts effectively.
