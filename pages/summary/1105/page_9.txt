The paper discusses various algorithms for learning Bayesian networks (BNs), highlighting the use of the qgraph package to visualize averaged networks. The averaged network is plotted in a circular layout, as shown in Figure 3. 

It introduces two categories of algorithms: constraint-based and score-based algorithms. 

1. **Constraint-Based Algorithms**: 
   - The grow-shrink (GS) algorithm and the incremental association (IAMB) algorithms are noted for learning the Markov blanket for each node to minimize tests required by the Independence Check (IC) algorithm. The initial assumption is that the network does not have any edges.

2. **Score-Based Algorithms**: 
   - These algorithms evaluate candidate BNs by assigning scores based on how well the Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) reflects the data's dependence structure. Two scoring methods mentioned are the Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC) and posterior probabilities from flat priors. The paper emphasizes that score-based algorithms often utilize heuristic search methods, which may not guarantee the highest scoring DAG, unlike exact search algorithms that are impractically slow.

3. **Hill-Climbing Algorithm**: 
   - The Hill-Climbing (HC) algorithm is a greedy search method that iteratively modifies the network structure (by adding, removing, or reversing edges) to improve its score. It begins with an empty DAG and continues to adjust until no further modifications yield a higher score.

The paper concludes by illustrating the differences between a Bayesian Network learned through the PC algorithm and a regularized partial correlation network, emphasizing edge thickness and color saturation to denote edge weights.
