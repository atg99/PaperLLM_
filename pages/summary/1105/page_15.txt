This collection of references focuses on various aspects of Bayesian networks, particularly in the context of statistical analysis, causal inference, and psychological research. Key contributions include the development of algorithms for learning Bayesian network structures from data, methods for handling ordinal and discrete variables, and applications in understanding mental health conditions, such as post-traumatic stress disorder and bipolar disorder.

Notable works include Pearl's foundational texts on causality, which establish the theoretical underpinnings of Bayesian networks. Additionally, the papers by McNally and collaborators explore the utility of network analysis in psychopathology, addressing controversies and challenges in the field. The use of directed acyclic graphs in epidemiological research is also highlighted, showcasing the intersection of statistical methods and psychological phenomena.

Recent advancements, such as efficient algorithms for large-scale Markov blanket discovery and Bayesian hypothesis testing, demonstrate the ongoing evolution of the field. The references also cover practical implementations in R, providing tools for researchers to apply these methodologies in their studies.

Overall, the literature reflects a rich tapestry of theoretical and practical insights into Bayesian networks, emphasizing their relevance in both statistical research and psychological applications.
