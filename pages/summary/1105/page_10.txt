The paper discusses various algorithms for learning Bayesian Networks (BNs), focusing on score-based and hybrid algorithms. 

Score-based algorithms include the greedy equivalence search, which minimizes the search space by operating over equivalence classes, the tabu search, which explores beyond local optima to find better directed acyclic graphs (DAGs), and genetic algorithms that evolve structures through mutation and crossover while preserving high-scoring configurations. The tabu algorithm is implemented in the BNtabu function, while genetic algorithms are represented by the BNrs function.

Hybrid algorithms combine constraint-based and score-based approaches, demonstrating effectiveness in many applications. An example is the sparse candidate algorithm, which iteratively restricts candidate parents for each variable to a small subset and maximizes the score among these candidates. This algorithm is implemented in the rsmax2 function.

The paper also addresses common issues in learning BNs, particularly the choice of algorithm. A comprehensive simulation study indicates no systematic performance differences among the three classes of algorithms, and individual algorithms do not consistently outperform others. Researchers are encouraged to report their chosen algorithm and conduct robustness checks using other algorithms to disclose variations in edge directions.

Finally, the paper highlights the assumptions necessary for causal inference, noting that BNs within the same equivalence class are probabilistically indistinguishable. Causal relationships should be inferred cautiously, relying on edges with defined directions in the completed partial DAG for the equivalence class.
