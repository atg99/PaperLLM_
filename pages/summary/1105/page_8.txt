The paper discusses the concept of conditional independence among random variables and its significance in constraint-based algorithms, particularly in the context of Bayesian networks (BNs). Conditional independence between two variables X and Y given a third variable Z implies that knowledge of X does not affect the likelihood of Y occurring when Z is known, and vice versa. This notion is essential for testing conditional independence, which serves as a foundation for constraint-based algorithms, as these relationships establish the constraints themselves.

Three primary tests for conditional independence are highlighted: the asymptotic v2 mutual information test for categorical and mixed variables, Hotelling’s exact test for continuous variables, and the Jonckheere-Terpstra test for ordinal variables. A comprehensive review of these tests is available in existing literature.

The paper further elaborates on the Inductive Causation (IC) algorithm, the first structure learning constraint-based algorithm, which begins with a complete graph. The IC algorithm iteratively removes edges between pairs of variables that are conditionally independent given a set of other variables. It also establishes directional relationships based on specific criteria. Although modern algorithms have emerged, they are derived from the IC algorithm and share its fundamental steps.

The PC algorithm, an implementation of the IC algorithm, operates on a saturated network and performs tests to refine the network structure. The paper provides instructions for using the R package bnlearn to execute the PC algorithm and visualize the resulting Bayesian network using the qgraph package. The process includes loading necessary libraries, running the PC algorithm, plotting the network, and stabilizing the Bayesian network through bootstrapping. The final network is constructed by including edges that appear with sufficient frequency and directionality in the bootstrapped samples.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of conditional independence testing and the IC algorithm in the context of Bayesian networks, providing a foundational understanding for further applications in statistical modeling and causal inference.
