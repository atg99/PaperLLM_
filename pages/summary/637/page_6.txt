The study highlights the importance of model parameters, especially the number of hidden units, which significantly impacts prediction precision. The optimal number of hidden units was found to be 100 for the SZ-taxi dataset and 64 for the Los-loop dataset, indicating that overly complex models can reduce prediction accuracy due to increased computational difficulty.

Experimental results demonstrate that the T-GCN model outperforms traditional forecasting methods such as History Average, ARIMA, and SVR, as well as other neural network-based approaches. The T-GCN model showed reductions in RMSE of up to 52.3% compared to ARIMA and significant improvements in accuracy metrics. This underlines the effectiveness of the T-GCN model in handling complex, nonstationary time series data, particularly in spatio-temporal contexts. Overall, the findings affirm the T-GCN model's superiority in traffic forecasting, emphasizing its capability to integrate both spatial and temporal features for enhanced prediction performance.
