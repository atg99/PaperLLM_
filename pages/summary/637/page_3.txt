This paper discusses advancements in traffic forecasting models, particularly focusing on the limitations of traditional parametric models and the advantages of nonparametric approaches. Traditional models, while simple and convenient, fail to account for the nonlinearity and randomness inherent in traffic data, such as accidents. Nonparametric models, including k-nearest neighbor, support vector regression, fuzzy logic, Bayesian networks, and neural networks, address these issues by leveraging historical data to identify statistical patterns.

Recent developments in deep learning have led to the emergence of deep neural network models that effectively capture the dynamic characteristics of traffic data, significantly improving prediction accuracy. These models can be categorized based on their consideration of spatial and temporal dependencies. Some focus solely on temporal aspects, while others, like the deep belief network combined with regression models, have demonstrated improved performance by capturing random features.

However, many models still overlook spatial dependencies, which are crucial for accurate traffic state prediction. To enhance spatial feature characterization, researchers have proposed various architectures, such as those integrating convolutional neural networks (CNNs) with recurrent neural networks (RNNs) to address both spatial and temporal dynamics. Newer methods, including the fusion convolutional long short-term memory network, have also emerged, emphasizing the importance of considering exogenous factors.

Despite these advancements, CNNs have limitations in modeling complex traffic networks due to their inherent design for Euclidean spaces. The introduction of graph convolutional networks offers a promising solution by effectively capturing structural features of traffic networks. The paper proposes a novel neural network approach that combines these methodologies to better capture the complex temporal and spatial features of traffic data for forecasting purposes.

The methodology section defines the problem of traffic forecasting as predicting traffic information over time based on historical data. It introduces two key definitions: the road network represented as an unweighted graph and the feature matrix that captures traffic attributes, such as speed. The objective is to learn a mapping function considering both the road network topology and the feature matrix to enhance traffic forecasting accuracy.
