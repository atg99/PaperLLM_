The paper discusses the development and evaluation of a Temporal Graph Convolutional Network (T-GCN) model designed for spatio-temporal traffic prediction. The T-GCN model integrates graph convolutional networks and gated recurrent units to effectively capture both spatial dependencies from urban road networks and temporal dynamics of traffic data.

The architecture of the T-GCN is illustrated, highlighting the processes involved in spatio-temporal traffic prediction, with specific calculations provided for update and reset gates, as well as the overall output. The model's loss function is defined to minimize the error between actual and predicted traffic speeds, incorporating L2 regularization to prevent overfitting.

Two real-world datasets are employed for model evaluation: the SZ-taxi dataset, which contains taxi trajectory data from Shenzhen, and the Los-loop dataset, which consists of real-time traffic speed data from Los Angeles County. Both datasets include adjacency matrices to represent spatial relationships and feature matrices for temporal traffic speed changes.

For performance evaluation, five metrics are utilized: Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE), Mean Absolute Error (MAE), and accuracy, with the aim to assess the predictive capabilities of the T-GCN model over various time intervals. Overall, the T-GCN model demonstrates a robust approach to traffic prediction by addressing the complexities of spatial and temporal dependencies in traffic data.
