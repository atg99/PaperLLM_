The paper presents the Image-and-Spatial Transformer Networks (ISTNs) for image registration, specifically focusing on the integration of Regions of Interest (SoI) to enhance registration accuracy. The study evaluates the effectiveness of ISTNs against unsupervised (STN-u) and supervised (STN-s) models through quantitative metrics such as Dice scores and average surface distances (ASDs).

The experimental setup includes skull-stripped, intensity-normalized images with binarized segmentation label maps serving as SoI information. The authors conducted a series of tests, comparing both explicit (ISTN-e) and implicit (ISTN-i) variants of the model, demonstrating that ISTNs consistently outperform the baseline methods in terms of accuracy. The results indicate that while STN-u/s struggle to leverage SoI information effectively, ISTNs achieve high Dice scores and low ASDs, often nearing the best-case scenario.

The paper also highlights the impact of iterative refinement, which significantly boosts the accuracy of all approaches. Notably, ISTNs trained with a limited dataset of 100 pairs surpass the performance of STN-u and STN-s trained on 1,000 pairs, showcasing the data efficiency and advantages of test-specific refinement. The qualitative results further illustrate the effectiveness of the proposed methods in improving image registration outcomes. 

Overall, the findings suggest that ISTNs, through their unique architecture and use of SoI, represent a significant advancement in the field of image registration.
