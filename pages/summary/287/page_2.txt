The authors discuss the limitations of existing neural network-based image registration techniques, highlighting that most approaches either replicate traditional methods or do not leverage the full potential of deep learning. 

The paper illustrates the benefits of structure-guided image registration, particularly in focusing on structures of interest (SoI) rather than just overall image alignment. The authors point out that current supervised approaches either depend on synthetic transformations or traditional methods, which may introduce biases and limit generalization. In contrast, unsupervised methods optimize traditional cost functions but do not fundamentally improve upon them due to their reliance on the same principles.

The authors propose that while hybrid methods combining supervised and unsupervised techniques show promise, the field has yet to fully utilize the advantages of neural networks. They argue that for clinical applications, registration accuracy should be prioritized over speed, suggesting that future research should focus on developing methods that can learn new representations and adapt to diverse image types effectively.
