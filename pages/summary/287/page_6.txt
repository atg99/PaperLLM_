In this paper by M.C.H. Lee et al., the authors focus on the effectiveness of the mean squared error (MSE) loss function for various tasks, noting its flexibility in incorporating different types of Structure of Interest (SoI) information through real-valued images. They highlight the straightforward representation of binary segmentations and the encoding of anatomical landmarks using distance maps or smoothed centroid maps.

A key feature of their proposed Iterative Spatial Transformation Networks (ISTNs) is the ability to perform structure-guided, test-specific refinement based on learned representations. This enables the use of inferred images for registration techniques without requiring annotations for test images, as the necessary representations are generated by a trained Interpolation Transformation Network (ITN).

The authors discuss the generic nature of the ISTN architecture, which does not assume a specific spatial transformation model. They present two transformation models: a typical parameterization for affine transformations and a B-spline parameterization for non-linear registration. The affine transformation is decomposed into translation, rotation, scaling, and shearing matrices, allowing for intuitive bounding of transformation parameters.

The experimental evaluation focuses on pairwise registration of brain MRI images, utilizing 420 subjects from the UK Biobank Imaging Study to create training, validation, and testing datasets. This study demonstrates the practical application of the ISTN framework in medical imaging.
