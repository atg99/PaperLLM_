The paper discusses Image-and-Spatial Transformer Networks (ISTNs), focusing on their training methodologies using implicit and explicit loss functions. It highlights the effectiveness of these networks in capturing spatial information of images, particularly through the integration of auxiliary data such as segmentation masks and landmark maps.

The explicit ISTN employs a loss function that combines supervised learning with an ITN loss, which penalizes discrepancies between the SoI encoding and the ITN output. This approach facilitates the learning of representations that accurately reflect the structure of interest in the images.

In contrast, the implicit ISTN utilizes a more intertwined loss function that integrates outputs from the ITN and the SoI encodings, promoting end-to-end training optimized for image registration tasks. The results from training with both methodologies are illustrated through a toy example, demonstrating the evolution of ITN representations and their ability to guide accurate structure-based registration.

The paper emphasizes that while the explicit approach yields sharper representations, both methods effectively enhance the learning of useful image representations, underscoring the importance of incorporating auxiliary information during training.
