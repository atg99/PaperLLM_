The paper presents a novel framework called Image-and-Spatial Transformer Networks (ISTNs) aimed at improving image registration in medical imaging. Traditional methods often rely on intensity-based registration, which involves optimizing transformation parameters to minimize a dissimilarity measure between images. However, these direct, non-iterative approaches may not provide the best alignment for Structures-of-Interest (SoI), particularly when accurate registration is critical.

The authors argue that incorporating information about SoI, such as segmentations or landmarks, can enhance the registration process. ISTNs leverage this information to learn new image representations specifically optimized for registration tasks. This approach allows for an iterative refinement of transformation parameters during testing, resulting in highly accurate registrations even with limited training data.

The effectiveness of ISTNs is demonstrated through experiments on pairwise 3D brain registration and synthetic data, showcasing significant improvements over traditional methods. This work highlights the potential of deep learning techniques in addressing long-standing challenges in medical image registration.
