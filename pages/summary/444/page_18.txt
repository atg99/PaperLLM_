The paper discusses the theoretical foundations of residual neural networks (ResNets) and their optimality conditions in relation to U-Nets. It establishes that for any index j, the relationship between the loss functions Lijj(Ui) and Ljj(Uj) holds, particularly as i approaches infinity, leading to diminishing truncation errors. It posits that if a certain function QiU is Vi-measurable, then QiU must reside within the framework of Fijj. If this does not yield a minimal loss, it contradicts the assumption of optimality for U.

Proposition 1 asserts that if U is a residual U-Net, then Ui operates as a ResNet that is conditioned on a preconditioned function Upre. The proof illustrates that the ResNet can be expressed as the sum of a preconditioned component and a residual component.

Theorem 2 states that for time t greater than or equal to zero and indices j and i, the relationship QiXj(t) equals Xi(t) holds true. It further elaborates on the decomposition of Xi(t) into Haar wavelet frequencies, where each component exhibits specific variance characteristics. The transformation into Haar wavelet representation is facilitated by a defined mapping Ti, which incorporates a Haar matrix and a diagonal scaling matrix.

The paper describes how to express Xi in terms of its Haar frequency components and elaborates on the evolution of time-varying random variables within this framework, emphasizing the integration of various frequency components in the representation of Xi.
