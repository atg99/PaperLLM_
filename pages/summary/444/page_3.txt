The paper presents a formal definition of U-Nets, emphasizing their role as operators on specific spaces. It introduces projection operators that map input data to compressed representations, enabling U-Nets to handle a variety of architectures, including those used in diffusion models and operator learning for PDEs. The framework allows for the incorporation of multiple channels and problem-specific information, facilitating scalable neural network designs for complex geometries.

The U-Net architecture comprises encoder and decoder subspaces, with the former serving as a change of basis mapping on the input space and the latter learning transitions between different resolutions. The roles of the encoder and decoder are highlighted as fundamentally different, with the decoder relying on information from the encoder through skip connections. The projection operators are tailored to specific tasks, such as pooling operations, to extract compressed inputs.

A key principle of U-Nets is preconditioning, where the output from a lower resolution U-Net is used to inform the next level, aiding the transition between resolutions. The design leverages reﬁnable basis functions, particularly in cases involving square domains, to enhance the approximation process. Overall, the paper expands the understanding of U-Nets beyond traditional applications, opening avenues for handling diverse geometrical configurations.
