In this section, the authors analyze the role of the encoder in U-Nets, specifically comparing Residual U-Nets with Multi-ResNets. They implement the Multi-ResNet by substituting the traditional parameterized encoder with a multi-level Discrete Wavelet Transform (DWT). The skip connection for each resolution is derived from the lower-lower part of the DWT at that level, allowing for a coarse reconstruction of the original input image.

The authors further investigate the encoder's impact on generative modeling using diffusion models, particularly focusing on performance metrics such as the exponential moving average FID score. They compare Haar wavelet Residual U-Nets with Multi-ResNets, noting that diffusion models tend to perform better with a parameterized encoder. Unlike the other tasks analyzed, the Multi-ResNet does not surpass the Residual U-Net in performance, despite having a similar parameter count.

The authors present several hypotheses for this observation, including the potential inadequacy of Haar wavelets for the diffusion model's input space, the limitations of the FID score as a measure of image quality, and the possibility of suboptimal allocation of saved parameters in the decoder. They illustrate these findings with visual comparisons of samples generated by both models, highlighting the challenges in discerning quality differences despite variations in FID scores.
