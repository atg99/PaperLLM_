The paper discusses the implementation of staged training for Multi-ResNets in the context of diffusion models, specifically using a Residual U-Net architecture. The training process is conducted in four stages, with resolutions decreasing from 32x32 to 4x4 pixels, allowing for effective multi-resolution training and sampling on the MNIST dataset. The results show a significant improvement in performance metrics, indicated by a Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) score of 8.2 for the model trained without freezing parameters, compared to a score of 59.8 when freezing was applied.

Additionally, the paper presents quantitative results for the Multi-ResNet compared to a classical Residual U-Net on Navier-Stokes and Shallow Water datasets. Both architectures have similar parameter counts, but the Multi-ResNet exhibited higher relative mean square errors (r-MSE) than the Residual U-Net, indicating potential trade-offs in performance. Specifically, the r-MSE for Navier-Stokes was 0.0105 with the Multi-ResNet compared to 0.0083 for the Residual U-Net, while for the Shallow Water dataset, it was 0.7715 versus 0.4640, respectively. This suggests that while Multi-ResNets show promise in staged training, they may require further optimization to match or exceed the performance of traditional architectures in certain applications.
