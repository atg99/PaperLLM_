The paper discusses the representation and analysis of a random variable X in terms of its pixel basis and frequencies, particularly focusing on the variance of its Haar coefficients under a diffusion process. It begins with the expression of X as a function of a standard normally distributed random variable, highlighting the time-dependent coefficient vectors. The variance of the transformed random vector is computed, showing that it remains consistent across elements due to the symmetry in the matrix representation.

The analysis reveals that the variance for each element of the transformed vector is uniform, and specific variance values are derived for different components. The paper further explains how the diffusion process at resolution i can be embedded into a higher resolution (i + 1) while maintaining consistency in the means and variances of the respective random vectors.

The results indicate that the noise process at a given frequency has a natural extension, with higher frequency components experiencing increased diffusion rates. The mapping from a space of functions to a sequence space is also addressed, emphasizing that the transformation leads to a sequence with monotonically increasing variance in the Haar wavelet sequence space.

Overall, the findings illustrate the behavior of the diffusion process in a structured manner, linking variances across different resolutions and showcasing the implications of this embedding in the context of functional analysis and probability theory.
