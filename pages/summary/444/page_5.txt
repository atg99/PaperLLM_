The paper discusses the concept of preconditioning in residual networks, particularly in the context of U-Nets and their architecture. It highlights how preconditioning on the identity allows the residual encoder to learn a change of basis for the input space, which is further elaborated in section 3.1. The residual decoders also benefit from preconditioning, as they can leverage lower resolution data and skip connections to enhance learning.

Proposition 1 establishes that a U-Net at resolution i functions as a ResNet preconditioned on a lower resolution U-Net, indicating that the residual encoder learns from the increase in resolution. The authors suggest that this relationship allows for a new interpretation of ResNets as a scaling limit, distinct from existing models like Neural ODEs, warranting future research.

An example provided is the Haar Wavelet Residual U-Net, designed for image processing, which utilizes multi-resolution Haar wavelet spaces. This architecture is also applicable to other data types, such as PDEs, illustrating its versatility.

The paper then transitions into the generalization of U-Net designs, aiming to extend their application beyond image data. It emphasizes the importance of selecting appropriate encoder subspaces, noting that Haar wavelets are advantageous for image representation. The encoder learns to adjust the basis for optimal performance, minimizing the need for extensive data learning.

The concept of Multi-ResNets is introduced, where the encoder is set to the identity, allowing for efficient learning when the initial choice of subspaces is favorable. This reinforces the idea that the encoder's function can be minimized with an optimal prior, leading to more efficient models overall. The Haar Wavelet Multi-ResNet is presented as a specific instance of this framework.
