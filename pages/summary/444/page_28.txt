The analysis explores the effectiveness of staged training for multi-resolution training and inference in generative modeling with diffusion models. The experiments involved training on various datasets, including MNIST and CIFAR10, with specific iterations and epochs defined for each resolution. The results highlighted the performance of a diffusion model trained using Algorithm 1, demonstrating that freezing weights during training allows the final model to generate samples across all resolutions. However, preliminary tests indicated that freezing lower-resolution weights could negatively impact performance, particularly at higher resolutions. This was evidenced by comparisons of samples and FID scores for CIFAR10, where training without freezing yielded significantly better results, leading to the decision not to pursue the freezing option further.

In addition, the analysis addressed the performance of Residual U-Nets and Multi-ResNets in PDE modeling tasks, specifically for Navier-Stokes and Shallow water simulations. The findings revealed that staged training resulted in poorer performance, characterized by higher standard deviations and outlier runs. This contrasted with the generative modeling results, where staged training had minimal impact. The authors noted the potential for future investigations to enhance the stability and performance of staged training in PDE modeling contexts.
