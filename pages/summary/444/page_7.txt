The paper discusses the integration of U-Nets into the modeling of complicated geometries, particularly through the use of tessellations, which are essential for various engineering applications. The authors focus on U-Nets designed for triangular domains, leveraging the self-similarity of right triangles to construct a refiable Haar wavelet basis. This approach allows U-Nets to encode geometric structures directly into their architecture, bypassing the need for data-driven learning of geometry.

The paper highlights that traditional U-Nets typically operate on unit squares, whereas the proposed design adapts to triangular domains, allowing for the construction of subspaces from basis functions. The authors also delve into how their U-Net framework can be applied to more complex geometries, such as manifolds, without requiring modifications to the underlying diffusion process.

Furthermore, the paper explores the effectiveness of U-Nets in diffusion models, particularly in image processing. The authors analyze the role of average pooling in U-Nets, which aligns with the forward diffusion process and enhances their performance by addressing high-frequency noise. The theoretical results presented in the paper demonstrate that U-Nets exploit the exponential decay of noise in high-frequency components during the denoising process.

In summary, the paper establishes a framework for U-Nets that effectively encodes geometric structures, enabling their application in complex geometries and highlighting their utility in diffusion models through a theoretical lens.
