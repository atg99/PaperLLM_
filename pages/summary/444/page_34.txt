The paper emphasizes the critical role of preconditioning in residual learning through a synthetic experiment involving different functions. It contrasts the effectiveness of two preconditioners, Rpre(v) = v and Rpre(v) = |v|, when learning the ground-truth functions w(v) = v^2 and w(v) = v^3. The findings reveal that Rpre(v) = v serves as a strong initial guess for w(v) = v^3, while Rpre(v) = |v| performs poorly for this function and vice versa for w(v) = v^2. This demonstrates that the suitability of a preconditioner is dependent on the specific problem at hand and can significantly impact optimization outcomes.

The experimental setup involved training a residual network (ResNet) using a grid of values and functions, where the expressivity of the network was intentionally limited to ensure that the effects of preconditioning could be isolated and observed. The ResNet was structured with three linear layers and incorporated weight normalization to maintain a consistent step size during training. The results were found to be robust across various hyperparameter combinations, reinforcing the conclusion that the choice of preconditioner is fundamental in achieving effective learning in neural networks.
