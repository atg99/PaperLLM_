The paper presents ablation studies focusing on the significance of architectural components in Multi-ResNets, particularly in U-Nets. 

In the first ablation study, the importance of skip connections is highlighted. The authors hypothesize that since Multi-ResNets perform linear transformations in the encoder, skip connections could be removed. However, the findings reveal that skip connections are essential for U-Nets, especially Multi-ResNets. Experiments comparing Residual U-Nets and Multi-ResNets with and without skip connections demonstrate a significant performance drop when skip connections are omitted. This is particularly evident in Multi-ResNets, which struggle to compensate for the absence of higher-frequency information needed for accurate output predictions. The results underscore that the encoder compresses information crucial for the decoder's performance.

In the second ablation study, the focus shifts to the role of preconditioning in U-Nets. The analysis compares a Residual U-Net with multiple subspaces against a ResNet with a single subspace. By modifying the Residual U-Net to function similarly to a ResNet, the study examines the impact of preconditioning between input and output spaces. The results indicate that the self-similarity structure in U-Nets, which facilitates preconditioning, significantly contributes to their empirical success. The Residual U-Net consistently outperforms the single subspace ResNet, reinforcing the value of U-Net architecture in efficiently handling complex data representations.

Overall, the ablation studies substantiate the critical roles of skip connections and preconditioning in enhancing the performance of U-Nets and Multi-ResNets in modeling partial differential equations.
