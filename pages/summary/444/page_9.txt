The paper presents a comparative analysis of the performance of Multi-ResNets and classical Residual U-Nets in the context of PDE modeling and image segmentation tasks. The quantitative results, shown in Table 1, indicate that Multi-ResNets can achieve competitive and sometimes superior performance compared to traditional U-Nets, despite having a similar number of parameters. Specifically, Multi-ResNets outperformed classical U-Nets by 29.8%, 12.8%, and 3.4% on average across three random seeds in various tasks.

The authors argue that the encoder's role in U-Nets can be optimized by using a fixed hierarchy of linear transformations, such as a multi-level Discrete Wavelet Transform with Haar wavelets, instead of a parameterized encoder. This approach results in reduced computational costs while maintaining or enhancing performance. However, the paper also notes that in certain contexts, like generative modeling with diffusion models, Multi-ResNets may underperform compared to Residual U-Nets due to suboptimal initial basis choices.

The findings suggest that future research should focus on optimizing the selection of basis functions for encoders and effectively utilizing saved parameters in Multi-ResNets. Overall, the study emphasizes the potential of Multi-ResNets as a more efficient alternative to classical U-Nets in specific applications.
