This paper discusses the development of diffusion models on spherical geometries and the application of U-Nets in finite element contexts, particularly for partial differential equation (PDE) surrogate modeling. 

In the first section, the authors propose a method to handle L2(X)-valued data on the globe by leveraging a triangular basis U-Net that directly encodes the spherical geometry, avoiding the need for local coordinate transformations. They illustrate this process using a triangulated model of the globe, where each triangle pixel is associated with a diffusion process defined in terms of a specific encoding string. The authors introduce Haar wavelets on the triangular elements, allowing for natural projection maps in L2 over the spherical domain. The construction of the U-Net is based on the span of constant functions and wavelets across the triangles, facilitating the modeling of diffusion processes in any triangulated space.

In the second section, the focus shifts to U-Nets designed for finite elements, emphasizing their potential for PDE surrogate modeling. The authors compare the performance of their Multi-ResNet architecture to Residual U-Nets in a square domain, noting the latter's limitations. They propose a U-Net design over basis functions that incorporate boundary constraints and smoothness assumptions, reflecting the principles of finite element methods for solving PDEs. The paper outlines the weak formulation of a model problem and describes how solutions can be represented in terms of an orthogonal basis, leading to the resolution of linear systems with diagonal structures.

Overall, the work aims to enhance the capability of U-Nets in complex geometrical settings, particularly for applications in machine learning and PDE analysis.
