A Hilbert space is defined as a vector space equipped with an inner product that induces a complete norm. Two vectors within this space can be considered orthogonal if their inner product equals zero. This paper specifically focuses on two Hilbert spaces: L2([0,1]), which consists of square-integrable functions with an inner product defined by the integral of their product over the interval, and H1_0([0,1]), which includes once weakly differentiable functions that vanish at the endpoints, with an inner product based on their weak derivatives.

An orthogonal basis for a vector space is formed by a sequence of pairwise orthogonal elements that span the space, with examples provided for both L2([0,1]) and H1_0([0,1]).

The paper also introduces wavelets as refinable basis functions for L2([0,1]) that exhibit self-similarity. The Haar wavelets serve as an example, with the mother wavelet being a constant function and the children wavelets derived from it. This family of wavelets forms an orthogonal basis in L2([0,1]). Additionally, functions derived from the Haar wavelets are shown to be orthogonal in H1_0([0,1]).

The encoding of the Haar basis is expressed through a Kronecker product, and the transition between pixel representation and Haar basis is facilitated by a specific mapping that incorporates a Haar matrix and a diagonal scaling matrix. This mapping enables the representation of piecewise constant functions using Haar basis coefficients, exemplified with a four-pixel function. Further insights into wavelets are suggested to be found in specialized textbooks.
