This paper discusses the development and extension of several repositories for machine learning applications, primarily focusing on the implementation of various neural network architectures and training algorithms. Key contributions include the implementation of Residual U-Net architectures and Multi-ResNets, along with a staged training algorithm that includes a strict version that freezes parameters. The authors also integrated logging capabilities with Weights & Biases and made adjustments for ease of running code with different hyperparameters.

The experiments utilized five datasets: MNIST, a custom MNIST-Triangular, CIFAR10, Navier-Stokes, Shallow Water, and the MICCAI 2017 White Matter Hyperintensity segmentation challenge dataset. While MNIST-Triangular is a custom implementation, the other datasets generally present data over square or rectangular domains with varying resolutions. Notably, the Navier-Stokes and Shallow Water datasets require significant storage, approximately 120 GB and 150 GB unzipped, respectively.

The computational resources for the project included local machines with advanced CPU and GPU capabilities for development and a shared large compute cluster with A100 GPU nodes for running experiments. The authors provided approximate run times for each dataset, noting that runs on MNIST and MNIST-Triangular take about 30 minutes, while CIFAR10 runs take several days. The Navier-Stokes and Shallow Water datasets require approximately 1.5 days and 1 day, respectively.

The codebase incorporates various existing assets and libraries, including Weights & Biases, PyTorch, and others, each with specific licenses. The authors utilized tools like GitHub Copilot and occasionally ChatGPT to assist in code development and writing.
