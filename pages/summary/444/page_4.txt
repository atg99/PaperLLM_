The paper discusses the structural properties of U-Nets, particularly their self-similarity when applied to spaces with refinable geometry. This self-similarity facilitates efficient multi-resolution approximations and modular designs, where a U-Net at a lower resolution can serve as a coarse approximation for a higher resolution. The expressiveness of U-Nets is tied to their decoder operators, and the paper aims to investigate how increasing resolution affects the representation of functions through U-Nets in regression contexts.

The authors define the target space as a Hilbert space, which allows the examination of orthogonal subspaces. The choice of basis for these subspaces, such as Fourier frequencies or wavelets, is crucial as it can influence the learning efficiency of the U-Net. The relationship between finite and infinite resolution U-Nets is explored through Theorem 1, which establishes that solutions from finite resolution problems converge to those of infinite resolution as resolution increases. This convergence implies that the choice of input space must balance expressiveness with efficiency to optimize learning outcomes.

Additionally, the paper introduces the concept of ResNets in relation to U-Nets, formalizing the notion of Residual U-Nets. ResNets are defined as mappings that consist of a preconditioned component and a residual component. The relationship between U-Nets and ResNets suggests that the latter can enhance the training process of the former by providing a solid initialization from which the U-Net can learn residuals. The authors emphasize that the training difficulty of ResNets is contingent upon the deviation from the preconditioner, which has implications for the design of encoder and decoder operations within U-Nets.
