The paper discusses the integration of Partial Differential Equation (PDE) modeling and image segmentation using a Multi-ResNet architecture. It presents a detailed analysis of the noise effects introduced by a forward diffusion process in a Haar wavelet basis, emphasizing that higher-frequency wavelet coefficients are more susceptible to noise compared to lower-frequency ones. The optimal recovery of signals amidst this noise is facilitated by soft thresholding, which helps distinguish the signal from noise.

The analysis posits that U-Nets with average pooling effectively exploit this phenomenon by discarding high-frequency noise-dominated subspaces. This allows the networks to focus on lower-frequency signals that are less affected by noise, enhancing their efficiency in denoising diffusion models. The paper argues that this inductive bias, combined with the coupling of subspaces via preconditioning, enables U-Nets to learn signals effectively without interference from noise.

Three main experimental analyses are conducted: the performance of Multi-ResNets with a parameter-free encoder, multi-resolution training and sampling, and U-Nets that encode the topological structure of triangular data. The experiments are evaluated across three tasks: generative modeling of images, PDE modeling, and image segmentation, using metrics such as FID score, rollout mean-squared error, and Sørensen-Dice coefficient. The datasets employed include MNIST, a triangular variant of MNIST, CIFAR10, as well as datasets for PDE modeling and the WMH segmentation challenge.

The results underscore the significance of skip connections in U-Nets for effective information compression and the advantages of multi-resolution structures. The paper provides a PyTorch codebase for replication and further exploration of the proposed methodologies.
