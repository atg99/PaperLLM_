The paper discusses various methods for providing explanations in artificial intelligence, particularly in healthcare applications, emphasizing the importance of transparency in model decisions. Three primary types of explanations are identified: feature maps, textual explanations, and example-based explanations. Feature maps utilize gradients to indicate input importance, while textual explanations provide semantic descriptions of model decisions, exemplified in image captioning. Example-based explanations draw from similar instances to elucidate model behavior.

The discussion on Explainable AI (XAI) highlights the role of saliency maps, which measure input feature importance via gradient values. However, they face challenges in accurately representing feature importance, especially in saturated model regions. Guided backpropagation modifies the gradient backpropagation process but shares similar limitations. To enhance interpretability, methods like Layer-wise Relevance Propagation (LRP) and Deep Taylor Decomposition (DTD) are proposed, allowing for better quantification of neuron contributions to outputs.

The paper concludes with a focus on the potential of CAM as a visualization tool in neural networks, underscoring its ability to reveal the network's focus areas, albeit with constraints on its generalizability across different models.
