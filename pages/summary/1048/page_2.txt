The paper discusses the concepts of explainable artificial intelligence (XAI) within the context of black-box models, particularly in medical applications. It distinguishes between "explainable," which refers to post hoc methods that provide insights into predictions, and "interpretable," which offers human-understandable outputs directly from the model. The definition of explainability is noted to vary based on the prediction task and the target users.

The flowchart presented in the paper contrasts black-box AI, which only delivers results like class predictions without transparency, with XAI methods that enable users to understand the reasoning behind predictions. This is particularly important in high-risk fields such as healthcare, where AI decisions can have serious consequences. The paper highlights the increase in XAI research, particularly in medical fields, driven by the need for trust in AI models among both expert users and the general public.

The authors emphasize that users can be categorized into two groups: those with AI expertise, who focus on the model's mechanics, and those without, such as clinicians and the public, who require more detailed explanations. The paper advocates for collaborative efforts between academic researchers and clinicians to improve understanding and trust in AI-driven diagnostics.
