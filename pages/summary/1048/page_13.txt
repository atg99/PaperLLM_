The paper discusses the challenges and developments in Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI) within the healthcare sector, particularly in clinical decision-making. It emphasizes the importance of human-centered approaches to XAI, which aim to create interpretable technology that clinicians and patients can trust. Key points include the necessity for clear and comprehensible explanations, as patients are unlikely to accept diagnoses derived from opaque AI systems. The paper outlines the need for sophisticated interpretation methods to enhance trustworthiness and mentions three focal areas for improvement: causality, interactivity, and counterfactual explanations.

The deployment of AI systems in healthcare also faces practical challenges, including the complexity of collaboration among various stakeholders like designers, clinicians, and AI product managers. A new management framework is suggested to facilitate social decision-making, ensuring that the needs of both clinicians and patients are met. Training for clinicians on AI fundamentals and the generation of performance metrics to assess bias and accuracy are recommended to foster trust.

The paper categorizes the evaluation of XAI methods into human-centered and computer-centered approaches. Human-centered evaluations rely on feedback from domain experts, which can be costly, while computer-centered evaluations use algorithms to assess explanation quality. The effectiveness of various XAI methods, such as backpropagation, CAM, LIME, and SHAP, is discussed, highlighting their strengths and limitations in providing reliable explanations. Overall, the paper calls for significant advancements in the design and application of XAI technologies to improve their usability and reliability in healthcare settings.
