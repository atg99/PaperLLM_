The paper discusses the growth of publications on explainable artificial intelligence (XAI) in the medical field, highlighting its relevance from 1976 to 2021. It outlines seven key requirements proposed by the European Union to foster trust in AI models, which include human oversight, technical robustness, privacy, transparency, fairness, social well-being, and accountability. 

The section on confidentiality and privacy emphasizes the systemic risks associated with real-time data updates in AI systems. It identifies security threats related to the black-box nature of AI, including network attacks and system bias, which pose significant risks in medical contexts. Consequently, various studies have explored solutions to enhance data security in XAI models.

Ethics and responsibilities are also crucial, as the medical sector demands clarity on AI usage and its ethical implications. Irresponsible AI can adversely affect medical staff and patient safety, necessitating early-stage inspections and accountability measures. The concept of responsible AI is examined to establish clearer responsibilities in technological domains, with explainability serving as a vital factor in delineating these responsibilities.

Moreover, the paper addresses bias and fairness, noting that AI models trained on datasets can inherit biases that may skew results, such as favoring certain demographics in recognition tasks. This bias can stem from data selection, algorithms, and user interactions, underscoring the potential for increased bias and human error in big data applications. 

Overall, the paper underscores the importance of addressing these challenges to ensure the responsible deployment of AI technologies in healthcare.
