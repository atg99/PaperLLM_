This paper discusses the integration of explainable artificial intelligence (XAI) in medical imaging, particularly focusing on radiomics features. It references various studies that demonstrate the application of machine learning techniques in predicting mutations in non-small-cell lung cancer, improving survival predictions in breast cancer, and enhancing the interpretability of medical images through XAI methods. The authors highlight the importance of explainability in AI models for healthcare, citing research that shows XAI can outperform traditional statistical methods like Cox regression.

The paper also reviews the potential of deep learning and multimodal approaches, such as combining medical imaging with electronic health records, to improve diagnostic accuracy. It addresses challenges faced in the clinical implementation of radiomics, particularly in glioblastoma and prostate cancer, and emphasizes the need for reliable interpretability in AI systems to support clinical decision-making.

Key concepts include the effectiveness of various XAI techniques, the role of attention mechanisms in model explanations, and the significance of deep learning in generating medical reports. The authors advocate for continued exploration of XAI methodologies to enhance understanding and trust in AI applications within the medical field.
