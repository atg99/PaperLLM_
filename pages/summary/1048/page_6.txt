The paper discusses the two main types of explanations in explainable artificial intelligence (XAI): intrinsic and post hoc explanations. Intrinsic explanations involve models that are inherently understandable, such as linear regression models, which provide answers along with corresponding explanations. Conversely, post hoc explanations arise from external methods applied after a model, like neural networks, has been trained, utilizing techniques such as backpropagation and class activation mapping.

Additionally, XAI models can be categorized into model-specific and model-agnostic explanations. Model-specific explanations are tailored to specific applications, relying on the model's structure, while model-agnostic explanations can be applied across various models without special requirements, exemplified by methods like local interpretable model-agnostic explanations (LIME).

The scope of explanations can be classified into local and global explanations. Local explanations treat the model as a black box, focusing on individual inputs and their contributing variables, while global explanations derive insights from the model itself, understanding how it predicts outputs based on variable interactions.

Finally, the paper identifies three main forms of explanations generated by XAI methods: feature-based, textual, and example-based, noting that some methods can produce multiple types of explanations.
