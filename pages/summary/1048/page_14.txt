The paper discusses the challenges and future directions of explainable artificial intelligence (XAI) in medical imaging, focusing on the need for interpretable models in high-risk medical situations. While current XAI techniques are primarily post hoc, methods like LIME and Grad-CAM are popular among clinicians for their usability, with LIME achieving a satisfaction rate of 78%. However, many clinicians remain dissatisfied due to a lack of in-depth understanding of the explanations provided. The paper highlights several innovative approaches aimed at creating self-explaining models, including calculating loss for output feature maps and utilizing prototype learning for graph neural networks. The authors emphasize the importance of global explainability to foster trust in clinical settings and recommend integrating XAI with other techniques such as domain adaptation and federated learning. The paper concludes by summarizing the various XAI methods and their applications in medical imaging, discussing the necessity of explainable models for radiomic analysis, and outlining the prospects and challenges for future research in this field.
