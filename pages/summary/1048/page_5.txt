The paper discusses the application of interpretable methods in medical imaging and healthcare, highlighting various studies that utilize explainable artificial intelligence (XAI) techniques to enhance algorithm performance. A table summarizes key contributions from multiple research papers, categorized by organ, modality, and specific contributions. 

Several notable findings include the use of Class Activation Map (CAM) and Grad-CAM methods for interpreting X-ray and CT images to classify diseases such as pneumonia, COVID-19, and ulcerative colitis. Techniques like attention mechanisms and Shapley additive explanations (SHAP) are employed to improve model explainability and predictive accuracy in electronic health records (EHR) and imaging datasets. 

The paper distinguishes between intrinsic and post hoc XAI models: intrinsic models are designed to be interpretable by their structure, while post hoc models analyze existing trained models for insights. This classification underscores the diversity of approaches in the field of XAI and their relevance to medical applications. Overall, the research emphasizes the importance of interpretability in AI-driven healthcare solutions.
