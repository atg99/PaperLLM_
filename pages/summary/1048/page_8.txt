The paper discusses various algorithms and techniques for enhancing interpretability in neural networks, particularly in image classification tasks. One commonly used method is Class Activation Mapping (CAM), which has limitations in flexibility due to its fully connected layer structure. To address this, Gradient-CAM is introduced, which employs gradients to derive weight values by forwarding the network to capture feature layers and backpropagating to compute the Grad-CAM matrix.

Occlusion sensitivity is another approach explored, which assesses how the model's predictions change when parts of an image are occluded. This method helps identify the importance of different regions in the decision-making process, with greater output value reductions indicating more critical areas.

The paper also presents Testing with Concept Activation Vectors (TCAV), a method developed by Google AI to relate textual concepts to model predictions. TCAV aims to capture high-level concepts and quantify their significance in classification outcomes, although its application in medical contexts requires further research.

Lastly, the Triplet Network (TN) framework is discussed, which utilizes a sample-based training approach with three inputs: an Anchor, a Positive, and a Negative. This unsupervised learning method focuses on minimizing the distance between the Anchor and Positive while maximizing the distance from the Negative, thereby facilitating sample similarity explanations.
