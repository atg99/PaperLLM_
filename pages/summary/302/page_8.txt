The paper presents an analysis of various models for text classification, focusing on their performance through parameter settings and evaluation metrics. The dataset comprises 50,000 samples, split evenly into training and test sets, with an average word length of 237.71 and a vocabulary size of 10,000. The word vectors were generated using the skip-gram method from Word2vec, with an embedding size of 500. Key parameters include a dropout ratio of 0.2 and a batch size of 128, with the Adam optimizer and L2 regularization employed.

Performance evaluation utilized metrics such as accuracy, F1-score, precision, and recall, defined mathematically to assess the models' effectiveness in classification tasks. The results indicate varying performance levels among models including CNN, LSTM, MLP, hybrid models (CNN + Bi-LSTM), and a proposed model. 

In experiments with a fixed data size of 20,000 samples, the proposed model demonstrated superior accuracy stability after initial increases, particularly around epoch 12, while other models exhibited different patterns. When analyzing the impact of data size with a fixed epoch count, both the hybrid and proposed models showed improved accuracy as the data size increased, with CNN reaching a plateau at a size of 11,000 samples. Overall, the study highlights the importance of model choice and parameter tuning in achieving optimal classification results.
