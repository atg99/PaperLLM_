The paper discusses the limitations of traditional recurrent neural networks (RNNs) in preserving long-term information and addressing the vanishing gradient problem, which the long short-term memory (LSTM) architecture mitigates through its gating mechanism. LSTMs have proven effective in text classification, and there is ongoing research to enhance their accuracy.

To further improve sentiment classification accuracy for unstructured text, the study introduces a hybrid model combining bidirectional LSTM (Bi-LSTM) and convolutional neural network (CNN) techniques. This model utilizes the strengths of both architectures: CNN excels in feature extraction by treating text as a one-dimensional image and capturing relationships between adjacent words, while LSTM provides contextual information sequentially.

The proposed model enhances performance with an attention mechanism that aligns input biases and assigns weights to relevant components, reducing the number of parameters needed for training. Additionally, pretrained Word2vec weights from a large corpus contribute to improved accuracy.

Experimental results demonstrate the superiority of the proposed model, achieving an accuracy of 0.9141 and an F1 score of 0.9018, surpassing traditional CNN, LSTM, multi-layer perceptron (MLP), and hybrid models. The paper is structured to include a literature review, model architecture, experiments, results, analysis, future work, and conclusions. 

Key concepts such as Word2vec, which creates distributed vector representations of words and captures their contextual relationships, are also discussed, emphasizing its relevance in semantic and information retrieval tasks.
