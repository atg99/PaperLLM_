The paper discusses the application of transfer learning in neural networks, highlighting experiments that demonstrate its effectiveness in reducing training time. Specifically, it presents results from experiments involving datasets IBM29 and VT50, where prior knowledge from dataset VR14 significantly shortened training sequences compared to starting from scratch. For instance, training time for dataset VT50 was reduced to 199 iterations from 545, showcasing an increase in learning speed by approximately 2.8 times.

The main focus is a historical review of transfer learning research from 1972 to 1985, noting that this period had limited exploration of the topic in neural networks due to prevailing skepticism about their capabilities. The resurgence of interest in neural networks post-1986, spurred by the Parallel Distributed Processing book, also rekindled interest in transfer learning.

The paper outlines early terminologies used to describe transfer learning, such as "sequential learning" and "lifelong learning," before the term re-emerged in 1991 with a framework introduced by Pratt and colleagues. This framework categorizes transfer learning into four types: literal transfer learning, direct memory access (DMA) transfer, problem decomposition for subnetwork training, and indirect transfer involving rule-based knowledge representation.

These insights emphasize the advantages of leveraging previously acquired knowledge to enhance the training efficiency of neural networks, thus underlining the significance of transfer learning in advancing machine learning methodologies.
