The paper by S. Bozinovski presents a geometric interpretation of transfer learning, utilizing a coordinate space framework to illustrate the relationship between different learning tasks. The key concepts are represented through a convex cone in the (pi, pj) plane, where the position of the coordinate origin indicates the type of transfer learning occurring.

In this framework, the coordinate system T0 signifies no transfer learning, as the learner starts from a homogeneous initial condition (e.g., all memory values are equal). In this case, the learner must undergo a complete learning process for both patterns to achieve recognition.

When the coordinate origin is within the solution region (T4), positive transfer learning occurs, allowing the learner to correctly recognize patterns without additional learning. Conversely, if the origin is in the negative convex cone (T-4), negative transfer learning is evident, requiring a longer learning process due to prior incorrect classifications.

The paper further defines a numerical index for transfer learning based on the geometric interpretation. This index, derived from the signs of intercepts of boundary lines in the coordinate system, quantifies the degree of transfer learning. The index TL ranges from -4 to +4, with specific values indicating different scenarios: TL = 2 for positive transfer (T2), TL = -2 for negative transfer (T-2), and TL = 0 for no transfer (T0). A TL value of 4 indicates successful classification of both patterns without additional learning, demonstrating positive transfer from a previous task.

Overall, the paper provides a structured approach to understanding transfer learning through geometric representation and introduces a quantitative measure for assessing its effectiveness in neural networks.
