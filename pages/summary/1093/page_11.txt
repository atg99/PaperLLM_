The paper provides a comprehensive review of the early developments in transfer learning research, particularly focusing on the 1970s and early 1980s, a period that has been largely overlooked by contemporary researchers. It highlights the origins of transfer learning, which some sources attribute to 1993, and points out that significant contributions were made as early as 1976, when a foundational paper was published in Croatian at a symposium in Slovenia.

The review discusses how the research from 1990 to 2000 primarily explored the various forms and potential directions of transfer learning, while fundamental concepts, such as measuring transfer learning, were largely neglected. This gap was later addressed in the 2000s, linking the insights from the early research to current trends in transfer learning.

The paper also notes early applications of transfer learning with datasets involving character recognition, which laid the groundwork for contemporary machine learning research. Specifically, it contrasts the early use of datasets like the Computer Terminals and IBM29(40) with modern examples that utilize more complex character datasets.

In conclusion, the paper asserts that it enhances the understanding of transfer learning by connecting pioneering work from the 1970s and 1980s to current research, emphasizing the importance of the 1976 paper that introduced mathematical modeling and measures for transfer learning. Acknowledgments are given to several professors who supported the author's research throughout different periods.

Overall, this review enriches the historical context of transfer learning and underscores its evolution from its early theoretical foundations to its current applications in machine learning.
