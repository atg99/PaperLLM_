This paper discusses the implications of transfer learning within a mathematical framework, particularly focusing on the relationships between tasks and the outcomes of learning sequences. The authors introduce inequalities to analyze both positive and negative transfers of learning, questioning whether prior tasks can lead to shorter or longer sequences in subsequent tasks.

The core inequalities (12) are reformulated to express how the parameters of one task depend on another, leading to a system of inequalities (13) that illustrate these relationships. By manipulating these inequalities, the authors derive further expressions (14) that reveal the bounds on parameters related to the tasks.

The paper presents geometric interpretations of these inequalities, emphasizing the significance of patterns in a teaching sequence. It illustrates that the coefficients derived from the patterns imply the existence of a solution region for classification tasks, represented as a convex cone in a geometric space. This cone is characterized by the angle between patterns, which reflects their similarity based on feature overlap.

Theorem 2 asserts that there is always a way to choose a teaching sequence that allows a learner to classify different patterns correctly, regardless of previous learning experiences. The discussion culminates in a geometric representation that contextualizes positive and negative transfer learning, highlighting the importance of understanding the underlying relationships between different tasks and their associated learning sequences. The teaching space is defined as an integer space, indicating that only certain discrete solutions exist for successful classification.
