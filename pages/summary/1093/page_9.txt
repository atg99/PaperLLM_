The paper discusses the concept of transfer learning, particularly focusing on its positive and negative effects as evidenced by experimental results conducted between 1976 and 1981. A key measure is introduced, denoted as SL, which quantifies the similarity between two tasks based on their respective teaching sequences. SL values range from 0 to 1, indicating varying degrees of overlap in learning tasks.

The experiments illustrate three scenarios: tabula rasa (no prior knowledge), positive transfer, and negative transfer. In tabula rasa conditions, the teaching sequence for learning patterns E, T, and F was lengthy, highlighting the challenges of starting without any prior knowledge. In contrast, when prior exposure to Task 1 (learning A and B) occurred, the teaching sequence for Task 2 (learning E, T, and F) was significantly shorter, demonstrating positive transfer learning. Conversely, if the network first learned E and F and then attempted to learn A and B, the teaching sequence length increased, indicating negative transfer.

Further experiments on recognizing letters from computer terminals using a specific perceptron teaching strategy revealed that prior learning (Task 1) significantly reduced the iteration count needed to learn a new set of letters (Task 2) compared to starting from scratch. The results underscore the importance of transfer learning in enhancing training efficiency and effectiveness across tasks.
