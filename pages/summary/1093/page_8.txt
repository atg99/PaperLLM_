The paper discusses a transfer learning model applicable to multi-class and multi-template scenarios, formalized through a mathematical model. The model distinguishes between the teacher's side, which involves a similarity matrix and a distribution vector reflecting pattern occurrences during teaching, and the learner's side, which includes initial memory conditions and threshold differences for classifying patterns. The objective is to optimize training sequences for efficiency, either focusing solely on teaching trials or incorporating test trials as well.

An experimental investigation conducted from 1976 to 1981 utilized various datasets of letters and symbols, analyzed on different computer systems. The study specifically highlights an experiment under tabula rasa conditions that examines the influence of pattern similarity on learning. It reveals challenges in distinguishing similar patterns due to feature overlap, necessitating repeated exposure to certain images for effective learning. This underscores the importance of understanding pattern relationships and the complexities involved in identifying distinct patterns within a learning framework.
