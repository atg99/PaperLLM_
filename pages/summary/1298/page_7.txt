The paper presents a comparative analysis of various video prediction models, focusing on the CW-V AE model's performance over long prediction horizons. In experiments conducted on the Moving MNIST dataset, CW-V AE demonstrates superior retention of digit identities across a 1000-frame prediction horizon, while other models, such as RSSM and SVG, begin to lose this information much earlier—around 300 frames and 50 frames, respectively. The results indicate that CW-V AE effectively maintains global information despite variations in camera viewpoints.

The evaluation further categorizes the accuracy of video predictions based on different movement scenarios of an agent within a room or hallway. The findings reveal that CW-V AE consistently outperforms its closest competitor, VTA, indicating the advantages of its temporal hierarchy in capturing high-level details over time.

In terms of digit position accuracy, CW-V AE maintains reliable predictions for up to 100 frames, after which it still generates plausible sequences. The performance of RSSM is similar in the initial stages but declines more rapidly than CW-V AE. The paper also provides insights into the hierarchical structure of CW-V AE, showing that deeper levels of hierarchy tend to distribute information differently, with lower levels retaining more short-term dependencies.

Overall, the results underscore the effectiveness of CW-V AE in long-horizon video predictions, highlighting its capability to retain identity and positional accuracy compared to other models.
