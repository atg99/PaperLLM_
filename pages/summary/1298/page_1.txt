The paper "Clockwork Variational Autoencoders" by Vaibhav Saxena, Jimmy Ba, and Danijar Hafner addresses the challenge of predicting long video sequences, which requires understanding long-term dependencies. Existing models can generate sharp images but struggle to maintain accuracy over extended prediction horizons. The authors introduce the Clockwork Variational Autoencoder (CW-VAE), which utilizes a hierarchical structure of latent sequences that operate at varying temporal intervals. This design allows the model to effectively capture both slow and fast-changing elements in video data.

The CW-VAE outperforms leading video prediction models across four diverse datasets with sequences of up to 1000 frames. The authors also establish a new benchmark using Minecraft for long-term video prediction. Through experiments, they demonstrate that the model's slower latent levels represent slowly changing objects, while the faster levels capture rapid changes. The findings highlight the advantages of hierarchical latents and temporal abstraction in improving video prediction accuracy, making CW-VAE a significant advancement in the field. All code related to this research is publicly available.
