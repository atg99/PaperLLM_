The paper evaluates the performance of various video prediction models, focusing on CW-VAE, which employs a temporally abstract hierarchy. Results indicate that CW-VAE significantly outperforms other models, including VTA, RSSM, and SVG, across multiple datasets and metrics. In GQN Mazes, CW-VAE excels in predicting high-level details, while in Moving MNIST, it maintains superior accuracy in digit identity predictions over time. The aggregated performance ranks reveal that CW-VAE consistently ranks higher than both a non-temporally abstract model (NoTmpAbs) and other competitors, demonstrating the advantages of temporal abstraction in video prediction.

In the MineRL Navigate evaluation, CW-VAE successfully predicts diverse terrain variations while maintaining long-term dependencies, unlike the other models, which either lack diversity or fail to capture consistent movement. For the KTH Action dataset, CW-VAE accurately tracks a person's motion, while other models exhibit issues with task retention and prediction consistency. In GQN Mazes, CW-VAE effectively predicts wall and floor patterns over extended frames, contrasting with the limitations of other models, particularly in maintaining texture continuity. Overall, the findings underscore the effectiveness of CW-VAE in video prediction tasks, highlighting the importance of temporal abstraction in improving model performance.
