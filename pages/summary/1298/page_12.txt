The listed references encompass a range of influential works in the field of deep learning, particularly focusing on video prediction, generative models, and hierarchical representations. 

Mujika et al. introduce fast-slow recurrent neural networks, emphasizing their efficiency in processing temporal data. Oh et al. explore action-conditional video prediction within Atari games, highlighting the application of deep networks for dynamic environments. Pertsch et al. propose a goal-conditioned hierarchical predictor for long-horizon visual planning, showcasing advancements in planning under uncertainty.

Radford et al. present a method for unsupervised representation learning using deep convolutional generative adversarial networks, contributing to the understanding of generative models. Rezende et al. discuss stochastic backpropagation for deep generative models, enhancing approximate inference techniques. Schuldt et al. focus on recognizing human actions through local support vector machines, a key step in action recognition.

Serban et al. develop a hierarchical latent variable model for dialogue generation, while Shelhamer et al. introduce clockwork convolutional networks for video semantic segmentation, addressing challenges in video analysis. Sønderby et al. propose ladder variational autoencoders, which improve the learning of complex distributions.

Srivastava et al. investigate unsupervised learning of video representations using LSTMs, advancing video feature extraction strategies. Unterthiner et al. discuss generative models of video, proposing new metrics and identifying challenges in this domain. Vahdat and Kautz introduce NVAE, a deep hierarchical variational autoencoder, enhancing generative modeling capabilities.

Vaswani et al. present the transformer model, which revolutionizes attention mechanisms in deep learning. Vondrick et al. work on generating videos with scene dynamics, contributing to the synthesis of realistic video sequences. Weissenborn et al. focus on scaling autoregressive models for video, aiming to improve efficiency and effectiveness.

Wichers et al. explore hierarchical long-term video prediction without supervision, advancing unsupervised learning techniques. Zhang et al. present SOLAR, a structured representation for model-based reinforcement learning, which aids in the development of more effective reinforcement learning algorithms. Lastly, Zhao et al. discuss learning hierarchical features from generative models, further enriching the understanding of representation learning.

Overall, these works collectively advance the fields of video analysis, generative modeling, and representation learning, highlighting the interplay between deep learning techniques and practical applications.
