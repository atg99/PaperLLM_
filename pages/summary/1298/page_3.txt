This paper introduces the Clockwork Variational Autoencoder (CW-VAE), a model designed to address the challenges of long-term dependencies in video sequences. Traditional video prediction models often struggle to retain information over extended periods, which is essential for understanding both local and global content. The CW-VAE employs a hierarchical structure of recurrent latent variables that transition at varying speeds, allowing it to predict video frames at multiple time scales.

Key features of the CW-VAE include:

1. **Temporal Hierarchy**: The model consists of multiple levels where each level transitions at a different rate. Higher levels transition more slowly, capturing long-term dependencies, while lower levels capture faster-changing information.

2. **Latent States**: At each timestep, the model defines active timesteps for each level, where new latent states are generated. This allows for conditioning k consecutive latent states on a single latent state from the level above, enhancing the model's ability to learn temporal correlations.

3. **State Transitions**: The latent chains are organized such that each level updates its state every k timesteps, effectively allowing for the copying of previous states when not in an active transition.

4. **Joint Distribution**: The model factorizes the joint distribution of video frames and latent states into reconstruction terms based on the lowest level latents and state transitions conditioned on previous latents.

The paper emphasizes the effectiveness of CW-VAE in learning long-term correlations in video data, showcasing its ability to represent content that changes at different rates within the temporal hierarchy.
