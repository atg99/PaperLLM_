This paper investigates the performance of a Clockwork Variational Autoencoder (CW-V AE) in generating video predictions using different levels of information stored within its hierarchical structure. The study utilizes datasets such as GQN Mazes and Moving MNIST to analyze how varying access to context images affects the model's predictions.

Figure 7 illustrates the content stored at the top level of the CW-V AE. It contrasts ground truth video data with predictions generated under different conditions, specifically when the top-level stochastic variables are drawn from a prior distribution. It is observed that while the camera positions and nearby walls remain consistent, the textures differ from the context, indicating that global texture information is stored at the top level, while more transient details are captured in the lower and middle levels.

Additionally, the paper presents findings on the KL divergence at various levels when adapting to different speeds of moving digits in Moving MNIST. The results show that faster moving digits lead to higher KL values at the lower level, suggesting that lower levels encode more immediate information, while slower speeds result in greater information retention at higher levels. This reinforces the model's capability to store and utilize information hierarchically.

The analysis further includes a comparison of the quality of open-loop video predictions based on varying temporal abstraction factors. The CW-V AE demonstrates superior performance in maintaining long-term dependencies compared to baseline models such as RSSM and SVG, effectively predicting accurate frames for a significantly longer duration before losing temporal context.

Overall, the findings highlight the importance of hierarchical information storage in video prediction models, indicating that a two-level structure may suffice for certain datasets, and underscore the potential of CW-V AEs in capturing intricate temporal dependencies within video data.
