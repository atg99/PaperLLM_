The paper introduces the Clockwork Variational Autoencoder (CW-V AE), which employs a structured approach to video prediction through a hierarchical model that utilizes components such as encoders, decoders, and transitions. Each latent state at a level is informed by the observed frames and previous states, facilitating a posterior transition and a prior transition for predictions. The model embeds observed frames using a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN), computing a diagonal Gaussian belief based on input features and prior samples. 

Training is conducted using the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) as the objective, which balances reconstruction loss and Kullback-Leibler (KL) regularization across levels. The KL regularizers restrict information flow, promoting efficient utilization of latent states while managing the complexity of information storage as the model ascends the hierarchy. 

The CW-V AE also differentiates between stochastic and deterministic components within its latent states, employing a Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) for updating deterministic variables. The performance of the model is evaluated against state-of-the-art video prediction methods across four diverse datasets, including the MineRL Navigate dataset and KTH Action dataset. The paper also explores various aspects of the model's behavior, such as the effects of clock speeds and information dynamics across levels, with the source code and predictions available online.
