The paper presents the Fast Semantic Segmentation Network (Fast-SCNN), designed for real-time semantic segmentation on high-resolution images. With the growing need for real-time computation in autonomous systems, Fast-SCNN addresses this demand by combining an encoder-decoder framework with a two-branch architecture. The authors introduce a novel "learning to downsample" module that allows the network to compute low-level features across multiple resolutions simultaneously. This approach merges high-resolution spatial details with deep features from lower resolutions, achieving a mean intersection over union accuracy of 68.0% at a speed of 123.5 frames per second on the Cityscapes dataset.

The significance of fast semantic segmentation is highlighted, particularly for applications requiring quick input processing, such as augmented reality and robotics. The paper discusses the limitations of existing methods, which often rely on deeper networks for global context and shallower networks for spatial detail, while emphasizing the need for efficient designs that balance speed and accuracy.

Fast-SCNN distinguishes itself by sharing the computations of initial layers across branches, akin to skip connections, thus enhancing computational efficiency. The authors validate their model's performance through experiments with ImageNet pre-training and Cityscapes data, demonstrating that large-scale pre-training is not essential for effective results. Additionally, they note that even faster computation can be achieved with subsampled inputs without altering the network architecture.
