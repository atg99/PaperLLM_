The paper discusses advancements in efficient segmentation models, highlighting the evolution from early models like SegNet and ENet to more recent architectures such as ICNet, ContextNet, BiSeNet, and GUN. These models utilize two-branch and multi-branch systems to learn global context at reduced resolutions while capturing boundaries at full resolution. Despite these advancements, achieving state-of-the-art real-time semantic segmentation remains challenging, often requiring high-end GPUs. Fast-SCNN emerges from this landscape by integrating a shared shallow network path for detail encoding and efficient context learning at low resolution.

The paper categorizes efficiency techniques in Deep Convolutional Neural Networks (DCNNs) into four areas: 

1. **Depthwise Separable Convolutions**: Techniques like MobileNet reduce computational costs by decomposing standard convolutions.
2. **Efficient Redesign**: Networks such as Xception and MobileNet-V2 utilize inverted bottleneck residual blocks for enhanced efficiency.
3. **Network Quantization**: This technique reduces runtime by converting floating point operations to integer or binary operations.
4. **Network Compression**: Pruning reduces the size of pre-trained networks, improving runtime and reducing memory requirements.

Fast-SCNN particularly leverages depthwise separable convolutions and residual bottleneck blocks while introducing a two-branch model that allows for shared feature extraction across multiple resolutions. The paper also notes that while pre-training on auxiliary tasks has been shown to enhance accuracy in other models, Fast-SCNN, designed with low capacity, does not gain significant benefits from pre-training. Instead, aggressive data augmentation and increased training epochs yield comparable results.

In summary, Fast-SCNN represents a novel approach to efficient real-time semantic segmentation, building on the strengths of two-branch and encoder-decoder architectures while emphasizing computational efficiency and effective training strategies.
