The paper discusses the performance of Fast-SCNN in segmentation tasks, particularly focusing on the benefits of skip connections and the impact of pre-training on different datasets. The visualization in Figure 3 illustrates that Fast-SCNN's segmentation results improve with skip connections, especially at boundaries and smaller objects. However, the overlap between the Cityscapes dataset and ImageNet is limited, suggesting that pre-training on ImageNet may not significantly enhance performance due to the differing capacities needed for both domains.

To address this, the authors incorporated 20,000 coarsely labeled images from Cityscapes, which are more aligned with the task. Despite this, Fast-SCNN trained with coarse data showed similar performance to the original model without pre-training, indicating that the enhancements were minimal and largely due to random initialization variations.

The paper also highlights the challenges of using auxiliary tasks, including the need for architectural changes and resource constraints. Results illustrated in Figure 4 indicate that while the ImageNet pre-trained models perform better initially, training from scratch allows the model to achieve comparable accuracy with extended training. 

Lastly, the study evaluates Fast-SCNN's performance at lower input resolutions, as relevant for embedded devices with limited processing power. At quarter resolution, Fast-SCNN achieves an accuracy of 51.9%, demonstrating its adaptability to various operational contexts.
