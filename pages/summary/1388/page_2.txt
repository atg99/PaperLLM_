Fast-SCNN is presented as a real-time semantic segmentation algorithm that efficiently balances speed and accuracy. It employs a dual-branch encoder structure that retains runtime efficiency by limiting the use of skip connections and maintaining a shallow architecture for feature sharing. The algorithm achieves a mean intersection over union (mIoU) of 68.0% at 123.5 frames per second on high-resolution images (1024x2048 pixels), outperforming previous models like BiSeNet, which achieved a mIoU of 71.4% but at a slower rate.

The model is designed with low capacity, utilizing only 1.11 million parameters, which allows it to run on embedded devices and enhances generalization. The authors investigate the effects of pre-training on Fast-SCNN and find that it offers minimal improvement in performance, suggesting that training for more epochs yields comparable results without the need for additional resources.

Key contributions of the paper include the development of Fast-SCNN as a fast and competitive segmentation algorithm, the adaptation of shallow learning modules for efficient feature extraction, and the validation of low-capacity models against pre-training methods. The paper also discusses existing works in semantic segmentation, emphasizing the importance of real-time execution and low resource consumption. The foundational work of fully convolutional networks (FCNs) and subsequent architectures, including U-Net and PSPNet, is acknowledged, highlighting their contributions to the field while noting their limitations in real-time applications.
