The article discusses the complexities and evolving nature of blockchain systems, particularly in the context of autonomous systems (AS). Unlike traditional networks with clear physical boundaries, blockchain operates on a decentralized, peer-to-peer (P2P) model where node participation is dynamic and often anonymous. This anonymity can lead to potential risks, such as state-sponsored actors consolidating computational power for malicious purposes.

The future of blockchain AS is envisioned as a network of interconnected systems, each defined by various axes, including:

1. **Identifiable and Authenticable Participation**: Membership in a blockchain AS can vary from tightly controlled environments, where all nodes are registered and monitored, to more decentralized setups where participation is discretionary. Strategies such as anonymous-verifiable identity schemes may balance anonymity and accountability.

2. **Trust and Assurance**: The reliability of nodes is crucial, especially in consortium arrangements. Nodes must demonstrate a capacity for secure operations and truthful reporting, which can be reinforced through trusted computing technologies.

3. **Business Model**: The specific goals of a blockchain AS can dictate the level of required participation and trust. Different applications, such as defense supply chains versus consumer goods, will have varying constraints and trust requirements.

The article also presents a use case involving interdomain transactions between two permissioned blockchain systems. It illustrates the process of transferring asset ownership from one blockchain to another, emphasizing the need for authoritative record-keeping to facilitate future transactions. Overall, the paper highlights the intricate balance between anonymity, trust, and operational requirements in the development of blockchain systems.
