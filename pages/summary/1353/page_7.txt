The article discusses the challenges and considerations involved in achieving interoperability among various blockchain systems, particularly in the context of transaction confirmation and governance models. It highlights that applications can rely on temporary confirmations from a mediating blockchain system while awaiting final confirmations from the destination system, drawing parallels to TCP-based messaging systems.

A key aspect of the proposed interoperability framework is the establishment of a common standardized transaction format and a minimal operations set that all blockchain systems would recognize, regardless of their technological implementations. This concept mirrors the foundational principles of the Internet, where the minimal IP datagram serves as a common unit of data transmission.

The article identifies several factors that differentiate blockchain systems, including governance models, confirmation speed, and consensus strength. Governance encompasses human policies, operational rules encoded in the blockchain, and application-specific smart contracts. Confirmation speed is influenced by the size of the participating node population, while consensus strength relates to the node count and its accessibility, especially in anonymous systems.

Overall, the article emphasizes the need for a structured approach to unify diverse blockchain technologies, ensuring reliable communication and transaction processing across different platforms.
