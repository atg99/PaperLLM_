This article discusses the development of an interoperability architecture for blockchain autonomous systems, focusing on various key aspects that influence the interaction between different blockchain systems. The paper outlines several critical dimensions of blockchain systems: 

1. **Degrees of Permissionability**: The distinction between permissionless and permissioned blockchains impacts user participation and raises questions about how data can be referenced across different blockchain systems.

2. **Degrees of Anonymity**: It identifies two relevant degrees of anonymity—identity anonymity for end users and anonymity for nodes involved in transaction processing. Different combinations of these degrees can exist, such as permissioned systems requiring authenticated nodes while allowing unverified end users.

3. **Cybersecurity and Assurance Levels**: The integrity and reliability of a blockchain system are heavily dependent on the security of its nodes. Vulnerabilities in nodes can significantly undermine the overall utility of the blockchain.

The article also introduces the concept of **blockchain gateways**, which facilitate interoperability and manageability across diverse blockchain systems. It draws parallels to Internet architecture, suggesting that blockchain systems may evolve into interconnected networks with varying consensus protocols and security measures.

The paper differentiates between **intradomain nodes**, which operate within a single blockchain domain, and **interdomain nodes** (or gateways), which handle transactions between different blockchain systems. It advocates for equipping all nodes within a blockchain system with the capability to act as gateways, thus enabling dynamic interactions across various blockchain domains.

Finally, the paper emphasizes the importance of defining the perimeter for blockchain autonomous systems, akin to how network operators on the Internet manage their networks. This definition enables operators to establish administrative controls and understand their responsibilities, thereby supporting the design of effective routing topologies tailored to their business needs.
