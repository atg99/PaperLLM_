This type of service emphasizes coordination among different parties and blockchains, often requiring consensus mechanisms to validate the transaction across all involved systems. The latency for this type of confirmation can vary significantly based on the number of parties and the complexity of the consensus process.

The article outlines a framework for enhancing interoperability among blockchain autonomous systems (AS), drawing parallels with early Internet architecture challenges. Key questions are raised regarding application awareness of blockchain constructs, the distinguishability and addressability of blockchain systems, and the placement of reliability functions. It emphasizes the need for semantic interoperability to convey the functional goals of diverse blockchain applications and proposes benchmarks for performance evaluation.

The paper proposes three service types for blockchain applications: immediate direct confirmation for low-latency needs, delayed mediated confirmation for applications accepting temporary confirmations, and multiparty mediated confirmation for transactions involving multiple parties. This classification aims to tailor blockchain services to specific application requirements, enhancing overall system efficiency and user experience.
