This paper explores the design philosophy necessary for creating interoperable blockchain systems, drawing parallels with the architecture of the Internet. The authors, Thomas Hardjono, Alexander Lipton, and Alex Pentland, highlight the growing interest in blockchain technology for enhancing visibility in decentralized peer-to-peer networks. However, significant challenges remain, particularly regarding interoperability, survivability, and manageability of these systems.

The authors argue that understanding the principles that made the Internet scalable and resilient is crucial for addressing these challenges within blockchain technology. They emphasize that interoperability is essential for the long-term viability of blockchain systems, advocating for the development of standardized components to reduce costs and improve reusability.

The paper is structured to first outline the fundamental goals of Internet architecture, which were established during the Cold War to ensure robust communication networks. It then recasts these goals in the context of blockchain, proposing design principles that should be consistent across implementations. The discussion also introduces the concept of blockchain gateways, akin to Internet routing gateways, which could facilitate interoperability among different blockchain systems.

In conclusion, the paper aims to provide a foundational framework for building interoperable blockchain architectures, stressing the importance of aligning blockchain design with the successful principles of Internet architecture.
