This article discusses the role of gateways in enhancing blockchain interoperability and establishing trust between different blockchain Autonomous Systems (ASs). It draws parallels to existing systems like the Domain Name System (DNS) and the Online Certificate Status Protocol (OCSP) within public key infrastructure (PKI). 

The article highlights the significance of trusted hardware, such as Trusted Platform Module (TPM) and Intel's Software Guard Extensions (SGX), in facilitating the functions of these gateways. Trusted hardware can help ensure the integrity and security of the gateways, which are essential for the mutual verification of identities, attestation of device status, session key establishment, and transaction reporting.

Additionally, the text emphasizes the necessity of peering agreements for blockchain interoperability, akin to those used by Internet Service Providers (ISPs). These agreements should detail the identification of gateways, trust establishment mechanisms, and liabilities, thereby providing a structured approach to blockchain interactions.

In conclusion, the article advocates for a design philosophy similar to that which has underpinned Internet architecture to foster scalable and effective interoperability among blockchain systems. Recognizing blockchains as ASs is crucial for developing systems that can support seamless interaction and data referencing across different networks.
