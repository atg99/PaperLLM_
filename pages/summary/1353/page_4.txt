The article discusses the concept of Autonomous Systems (ASs) as a foundational element of internet architecture, emphasizing their role in facilitating scalable and manageable routing. An AS is defined as a network or group of networks under a single administrative control, enabling efficient routing and connectivity. The authors highlight several key characteristics of ASs: they promote scalability by addressing performance issues locally, they allow for domain-level control through a distributed topology, they ensure unique identifiability of devices within a domain, they facilitate inter-domain communication through gateways, and they are owned by legal entities that manage routing information.

The authors draw parallels between the AS paradigm and the current landscape of blockchain systems, noting the lack of interoperability among various blockchain designs, which resembles early local area networks that could not communicate with one another. They argue that, similar to the internet's evolution, the future of blockchain will likely consist of interconnected "islands" of different systems. The authors stress that interoperability is crucial for the viability of blockchain technology, advocating for its integration at both the network and application levels to enable comprehensive and effective interactions among diverse blockchain systems.
