The article by Hardjono et al. discusses the development of an interoperability architecture for blockchain autonomous systems. It highlights the importance of understanding blockchain performance through various metrics beyond just throughput, emphasizing the need for scalability and other dimensions. The authors argue that interoperability necessitates a reevaluation of how permissioned and permissionless blockchain systems can interact without relying on third parties. 

A crucial aspect of this interoperability is semantic compatibility at both the value level and the mechanical level. While mechanical interoperability is essential for facilitating interactions, it does not inherently ensure value-level compatibility. The paper underscores the role of legal contracts in providing meaningful definitions to blockchain constructs, such as coins and tokens, which are vital for establishing trust and quantifying risks within blockchain systems. 

This exploration lays the groundwork for future advancements in blockchain technology, aiming to enhance both its functionality and integration across different platforms.
