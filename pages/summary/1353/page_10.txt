This article discusses an interoperability architecture for blockchain autonomous systems, focusing on the transaction flows between two separate blockchain systems, BC1 and BC2. The process begins when User A initiates a transfer to User B by submitting a transaction to BC1, addressing it to User B's public key in BC2. Since BC1 cannot directly interact with BC2, gateway nodes are responsible for processing the transaction.

The process involves several key steps. First, gateway G1 in BC1 processes User A's transaction and locates gateway G2 in BC2 for trust negotiations. G1 must mask the private transaction identifier from BC1 with a public identifier for future reference. This requires maintaining a mapping of private and public identifiers.

Next, G1 and G2 establish technical trust through a key establishment protocol involving the exchange of credentials and trust parameters. Once trust is established, G2 submits a new local transaction to BC2 that references the asset from BC1, marking it as belonging to User B. G2 then creates a signed assertion confirming the transaction's status in BC2 and sends this information back to G1.

G1 subsequently marks the asset in BC1 as moved and references the new public identifier in BC2. Users A and B can then confirm the status of their respective transactions in their blockchain systems.

The article also emphasizes the role of gateways in managing the visibility and referenceability of transaction identifiers, particularly in private or permissioned blockchain systems. It discusses identifier masking, which allows for confidentiality of transaction information, akin to network address translation. Furthermore, it suggests that gateways can implement resolution functions to manage identifier translation, ensuring that queries regarding transaction identifiers can be effectively handled across different blockchains.
