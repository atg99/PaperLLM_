The paper addresses the critical issue of interoperability within blockchain systems, emphasizing its significance for achieving technical trust and ultimately facilitating global commerce. It posits that interoperability can be understood at two levels: mechanical and value. At the mechanical level, interoperability is essential for establishing a measurable degree of technical trust among different blockchain systems, which in turn enables the development of legal frameworks to assess risks associated with these technologies.

The authors define an "interoperable blockchain architecture" based on the NIST definition, highlighting that it involves distinct blockchain systems capable of executing atomic transactions across heterogeneous platforms. This definition underlines the need for data recorded in one blockchain to be accessible and verifiable by another.

Survivability is identified as a key component of interoperability, drawing parallels with Internet architecture where communication must persist despite network failures. In the context of blockchain, survivability refers to the ability of transactions to be completed even amidst attacks on the blockchain systems. The paper proposes a redefinition of survivability to mean the confirmation of application-level transactions regardless of the underlying blockchain systems involved.

Furthermore, the authors discuss the complexity of ensuring reliable transaction completion, suggesting that application-level transactions may consist of multiple subtransactions across different blockchains. This necessitates a focus on the reliability and timely completion of these transactions, independent of the specific blockchains used.

In summary, the paper highlights the challenges and necessary conditions for achieving interoperability in blockchain systems, emphasizing that a robust framework for technical and business trust is essential for seamless global transactions.
