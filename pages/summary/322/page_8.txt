The paper investigates the effectiveness of attention-based probing tasks in understanding dependency parsing using BERT's attention maps and GloVe embeddings. The results indicate that a simple model combining these elements performs well, suggesting that BERT captures certain syntactic aspects as a by-product of self-supervised training. This aligns with previous studies that highlight BERT's capability to understand hierarchical language structures without direct supervision from curated datasets.

The authors also explore the similarity among attention heads within the same layer by measuring the Jensen-Shannon Divergence between their attention distributions. Through multidimensional scaling, they visualize these attention heads, revealing distinct clusters that exhibit similar behaviors. The proximity of heads within a layer suggests that they share similar attention distributions, which is noteworthy given other research advocating for diverse attention behaviors to enhance Transformer performance. Overall, the findings contribute to the understanding of how BERT's architecture and training can lead to syntactic sensitivity.
