paper investigates the attention mechanisms of BERT, particularly how different attention heads focus on specific token types during processing. The analysis is based on attention maps extracted from BERT-base over 1000 segments of Wikipedia text, structured to follow BERT's pre-training setup.

Key findings include that early attention heads predominantly attend to special tokens such as [CLS] and [SEP], while deeper heads show a higher focus on punctuation marks like periods and commas. Notably, some heads specialize in attending to the previous or next token, with several heads in the first few layers allocating over 50% of their attention to these neighboring tokens.

The study also highlights that a significant portion of attention is directed towards [SEP], especially in layers 6-10, which raises questions about the systematic nature of this behavior rather than it being a mere artifact of training. Although [SEP] is essential for segment-level information aggregation, the authors express skepticism regarding this interpretation and suggest that the attention patterns warrant further exploration. The research contributes to understanding BERT's inner workings and its treatment of different token types in language processing tasks.
