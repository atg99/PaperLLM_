The referenced works explore various aspects of neural networks and their relationship with linguistic structures and tasks in natural language processing. 

Xing Shi et al. investigate whether string-based neural machine translation (MT) models learn source syntax, contributing to the understanding of syntactic representations in MT. Strubell et al. introduce a linguistically-informed self-attention mechanism for semantic role labeling, emphasizing the importance of linguistic features in improving model performance.

Sundararajan et al. propose axiomatic attribution methods for deep networks, focusing on interpretability and feature importance. Tenney et al. highlight how BERT re-establishes classical NLP pipelines, demonstrating its effectiveness across various tasks. They further probe contextualized word representations to uncover insights about sentence structure.

Tu et al. examine multi-head attention mechanisms with disagreement regularization, suggesting that diverse head outputs enhance model robustness. Vaswani et al. present the "Attention is All You Need" framework, revolutionizing sequence transduction tasks with a purely attention-based architecture.

Vig provides methods for visualizing attention in transformer models, enhancing our understanding of model behavior. V oita et al. analyze multi-head self-attention, revealing that specialized heads are critical for performance, while others may be pruned without loss of capability.

Wiseman et al. focus on coreference resolution, developing features for ranking anaphoricity and antecedents. Lastly, Zhang and Bowman argue that language modeling imparts more syntactic knowledge than translation tasks, underscoring the significance of auxiliary tasks in learning syntax. 

Overall, these studies collectively advance the field of NLP by examining the interplay between neural architectures and linguistic principles, providing insights into model interpretability and performance optimization.
