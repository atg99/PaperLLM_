The paper analyzes BERT's attention mechanisms to understand the linguistic features it captures through self-supervised training. While previous research has focused on model outputs or internal vector representations, this study investigates the attention heads of BERT, a large pre-trained Transformer model.

The authors identify common patterns in BERT's attention, noting that certain heads attend to delimiter tokens, specific positional offsets, or broadly across sentences. Notably, attention heads within the same layer often exhibit similar behaviors. The analysis reveals that some attention heads correlate strongly with linguistic concepts such as syntax and coreference. For instance, specific heads effectively identify direct objects of verbs, determiners of nouns, and coreferent mentions, achieving over 75% accuracy.

The paper proposes an attention-based probing classifier to further demonstrate that significant syntactic information is encoded in BERT's attention, reinforcing the notion that the model learns meaningful linguistic structures from unlabeled data. The findings underscore the interpretability of attention mechanisms and their role in understanding language representation in neural networks.
