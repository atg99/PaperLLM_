The paper presents a study on the effectiveness of BERT's attention mechanisms in understanding syntactic structures, specifically through the lens of coreference resolution and syntactic head prediction. It evaluates various probing classifiers designed to assess BERT's capabilities without back-propagating into its architecture, focusing on two main probing methods: Attention-Only Probe and Attention-and-Words Probe.

The Attention-Only Probe utilizes a linear combination of attention weights to predict the syntactic head of a word based on the attention distribution from BERT's heads. Meanwhile, the Attention-and-Words Probe enhances this by incorporating GloVe embeddings to create word-sensitive weights, thereby improving accuracy in determining syntactic relationships.

Results from experiments conducted on the Penn Treebank dev set show that the Attention-and-Words Probe significantly outperforms several baselines, achieving a UAS (Unlabeled Attachment Score) of 77. This suggests that BERT's attention maps effectively capture English syntax. Comparisons with a structural probe indicate that BERT's attention may contain as much syntactic information as its vector representations, although the two methodologies are not directly comparable due to differences in their training and output structures.

Overall, the findings underscore the potential of BERT's attention mechanisms in syntactic analysis and coreference resolution tasks, highlighting the importance of integrating word embeddings for improved performance in probing classifiers.
