The paper explores the attention mechanisms of BERT and proposes analysis methods to understand how these models capture linguistic knowledge. It highlights that attention dropout may lead to apparent redundancy in BERT’s attention heads, as some weights are zeroed during training. The authors review related work, noting that while previous studies have examined language model outputs and internal vector representations, their focus is on understanding the linguistic information encoded in attention maps.

The paper discusses several approaches to analyzing attention, referencing studies that have visualized BERT's attention, correlated attention with syntax, and identified important attention heads in machine translation models. Despite some research suggesting that attention weights do not correlate with model predictions, the authors argue that their analysis aims to uncover the syntactic relationships learned by attention heads, regardless of their direct impact on predictions.

In conclusion, the authors assert that probing attention maps can reveal significant linguistic insights and should be incorporated into the toolkit for analyzing neural networks in natural language processing. They acknowledge the contributions of reviewers and note support for one of the authors through a Google PhD Fellowship.
