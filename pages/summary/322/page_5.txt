The paper discusses the performance of various BERT attention heads in relation to specific linguistic phenomena, demonstrating their ability to capture syntactic relationships. The findings indicate that Head 9-6 shows 76.3% accuracy in prepositions attending to their objects, while Head 8-11 achieves 94.3% accuracy in noun modifiers attending to their nouns. Additionally, Head 8-10 records 86.8% accuracy for direct objects and their verbs. Other notable performances include Head 7-6 with 80.5% accuracy for possessive pronouns and apostrophes, and Head 4-10 with 82.5% accuracy for passive auxiliary verbs. Head 5-4, which focuses on coreferent mentions, shows a lower accuracy of 65.1%. The attention maps illustrate the strength of attention weights, with red highlighting significant behaviors. The results suggest that BERT's attention heads, despite lacking explicit training on these tasks, exhibit strong syntax-sensitive behavior, indicating the effectiveness of self-supervised training in capturing linguistic structures.
