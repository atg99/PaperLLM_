The paper investigates the performance of BERT's attention heads in relation to dependency parsing and coreference resolution tasks. It presents findings from an analysis of various dependency types, as shown in Table 1, where attention heads were evaluated based on their accuracy in predicting syntactic relationships. The results indicate that most dependency relations, except for the object of prepositions (pobj), show that dependent words tend to attend to their heads rather than vice versa. This observation suggests that BERT's learning process, which is self-supervised, allows it to capture syntactic behaviors that may not align with conventional human annotations.

The paper also examines coreference resolution using the CoNLL-2012 dataset, where it measures antecedent selection accuracy. BERT's attention heads demonstrated notable performance, surpassing simple baselines and approaching the accuracy of a rule-based coreference system. This improvement is particularly evident in handling nominal mentions, indicating BERT's ability to recognize synonyms and related terms.

Additionally, the authors propose a method to assess the collective syntactic knowledge of BERT by developing attention-based probing classifiers. This approach treats the outputs of attention heads as fixed, providing insights into the distribution of syntactic knowledge across multiple heads and their effectiveness in dependency parsing tasks. Overall, the paper highlights the potential of BERT's attention mechanisms in reflecting both syntactic and semantic aspects of language.
