The paper presents a novel approach called GTN for classifying multivariate time series data, demonstrating its effectiveness through experimental results compared to several benchmark models. The key concept behind GTN is the recognition of hidden correlations between channels at specific time steps, emphasizing the importance of capturing both temporal and spatial information. 

GTN utilizes a two-tower framework, where each tower's encoder is designed to explicitly capture step-wise and channel-wise correlations through self-attention mechanisms. The Step-wise Encoder focuses on temporal features by calculating pair-wise attention weights across all time steps, employing techniques similar to the original Transformer architecture. In contrast, the Channel-wise Encoder assesses attention among different channels without positional encoding, reflecting the independence of channel order in multivariate time series.

To merge the outputs from both encoders, GTN introduces a gating mechanism instead of simple concatenation. This mechanism learns the weights of each tower's output, enhancing the model's performance by dynamically adjusting the contribution of each encoder's features.

The experimental evaluation of GTN involved 13 multivariate time series datasets from the Baydogan archive, with no preprocessing applied. GTN was compared against various deep learning models, including Fully Convolutional Networks, Residual Networks, and others, to assess its classification accuracy. The results indicated that GTN performed competitively, showcasing its potential as an effective method for multivariate time series analysis.
