The paper presents the Gated Transformer Network (GTN) as an innovative approach to multidimensional time series analysis, utilizing gating mechanisms to merge two transformer network architectures that model channel-wise and step-wise correlations. The authors visualize the t-SNE results of the embedding layer on the AUSLAN dataset, highlighting various discovered shapelets that represent significant patterns in the data. These shapelets include a deep M-shape, a sharp trending-up subsequence, a plateau followed by a deep drop, a recovery trend, and a plateau. The visualization indicates that closely positioned points in the vector space can reveal interesting shapelets, suggesting that GTN has the potential to learn meaningful patterns from the data.

Further, the authors visualize the extracted features after gating using the Japanese Vowels dataset, demonstrating that GTN effectively projects data into a separable space, enhancing classification performance. The paper concludes that GTN achieves competitive performance against state-of-the-art deep learning models across thirteen datasets, with an ablation study providing insights into the contributions of various model components. The qualitative analysis of attention maps and other visualizations enhances the interpretability of the model. The findings lay a robust foundation for future research on Transformer Networks in time series classification.
