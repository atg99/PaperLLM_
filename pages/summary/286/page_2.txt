This paper introduces Gated Transformer Networks (GTN) for multivariate time series classification, leveraging self-attention mechanisms to capture dependencies within the data. The authors demonstrate that merging two towers of Transformer Networks, which model channel-wise and step-wise correlations, significantly enhances classification performance.

GTN was evaluated on thirteen benchmark datasets, showing competitive results against state-of-the-art deep learning models through comprehensive ablation studies. The authors also conducted qualitative analyses to visualize the features learned by GTN, highlighting the effectiveness of its feature extraction capabilities. Additionally, preliminary explorations into the interpretability of GTN's attention maps revealed how self-attention facilitates extraction of relevant features in both channel-wise and step-wise contexts.

The related work section reviews prior studies that utilized deep learning methods for time series classification, including convolutional networks and recurrent architectures. The authors note the emerging interest in applying Transformer Networks to time series tasks, while their work represents a comprehensive study on this topic, focusing specifically on classification tasks.

The GTN architecture adapts traditional Transformers with three key modifications: an embedding layer that uses a fully connected structure with non-linear activation, a two-tower design to handle multivariate inputs, and a gating mechanism to merge the outputs effectively. This approach allows the model to better capture temporal relationships inherent in time series data.
