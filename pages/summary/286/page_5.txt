The analysis presented in the paper focuses on the channel-wise attention mechanism and its implications for time series data classification. Figure 2 illustrates various assessments, including the channel-wise attention map and distance metrics like DTW and Euclidean distance. The channel-wise attention map reveals that channels c6 and c7 have high attention scores when co-firing with channel 11 (c11), indicating similar trends and shapelets. Conversely, channel c3 shows a low attention score despite a small DTW distance with c11, highlighting that DTW does not necessarily correlate with trend similarity.

The study further examines the step-wise attention, using Euclidean distance as a metric due to its fixed nature across channels. This analysis suggests that both distance and shapelet similarity influence attention score calculations, although the effects warrant further exploration.

The paper also investigates the gating mechanism in the model's architecture, which differentiates weights between step-wise and channel-wise towers. The ablation study indicates that the gating approach yields better performance than concatenation, with a tendency to favor the step-wise tower, as observed in various sample weights. The average gating weights reflect this trend, supporting the conclusion that the model effectively learns to prioritize step-wise information for improved classification outcomes.

Lastly, the embedding output analysis employs t-SNE to visualize the transformed time series data. The resulting clusters indicate distinct shapelets corresponding to different time steps, revealing how the embedding layer consolidates information into meaningful groupings. Overall, the findings underscore the importance of attention mechanisms and gating in enhancing time series classification through learned representations.
