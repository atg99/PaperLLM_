The paper investigates the analysis of speech signals in the time domain using MATLAB. It employs the Support Vector Machine (SVM) algorithm, introduced by Vladimir Vapnik in 1995, for classification and pattern recognition. SVM is designed to create hyperplanes that separate different categories of input data, primarily focusing on binary classification. It operates effectively in high-dimensional feature spaces and is noted for its ease of training and superior scalability compared to neural networks.

The SVM classifiers can be linear or non-linear, utilizing kernel functions to enhance performance. In this study, the radial basis kernel function is applied during the training phase to confine training datasets within specified boundaries. The LIBSVM tool facilitates the SVM classification process, where feature values extracted from speech signals are labeled according to emotional states—Happy, Angry, Sad, and Fear. After training the model, it is capable of predicting emotional states using testing datasets.

Two datasets are utilized: LDC and UGA. The UGA dataset comprises 100 speech samples from University of Georgia students, with 70 samples for training and 30 for testing. The LDC dataset includes 100 speech samples, each lasting one to two seconds, also split into 70 for training and 30 for testing, featuring approximately 25 utterances per emotional class.

The results section presents simulation outputs, including the waveform and speaking rate of utterances, along with features such as Energy, Pitch, and various coefficients (MFCC and LPCC). These outputs are visually represented in figures included in the paper.
