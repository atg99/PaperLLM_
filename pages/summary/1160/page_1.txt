In this project, the authors focus on classifying speech emotions into four categories: sadness, anger, fear, and happiness. They utilize samples from the Linguistic Data Consortium and UGA databases, extracting key features such as energy, pitch, MFCC coefficients, LPCC coefficients, and speaker rate. The classification is performed using Support Vector Machine (SVM) with two strategies: One against All (OAA) and Gender Dependent Classification. A comparative analysis is conducted between these classification strategies and the feature extraction methods (LPCC and MFCC).

The introduction highlights the significance of human-computer interaction and the ability of computers to learn from experiences to detect emotions through speech. Various classification methods exist, including K-Nearest Neighbor, Artificial Neural Networks, Hidden Markov Models, and SVM. The authors emphasize the importance of feature extraction, using MFCC and LPC to prepare data for SVM training.

The paper references several studies that have employed similar methodologies, showcasing the versatility of SVM and different feature extraction techniques in emotion classification. Overall, the research aims to enhance emotion detection through speech, contributing to the field of human-computer intelligence.
