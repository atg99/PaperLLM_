The paper categorizes various search strategies used in feature selection, detailing methods such as the Exponential search, Sequential search, Random search, and Optimal feature selection approaches. 

Table 1 illustrates different search strategies, highlighting their alternative names and examples. Exponential search is synonymous with complete search or exhaustive search. Sequential search includes greedy hill climbing techniques like Sequential Forward Selection (SFS) and Sequential Backward Selection (SBS), along with their floating variants and best-first search methods. Random search refers to heuristic methods, including simulated annealing, genetic algorithms, and particle swarm optimization.

Table 2 outlines the merits and demerits of these search algorithms. For instance, SFS and SBS are praised for their quick computation and simplicity but criticized for selecting redundant features and not achieving optimal results. Conversely, methods like SFFS and SFBS are effective in reducing redundancy and achieving optimal outcomes yet face challenges due to their NP-hard nature in searching all subsets. Exhaustive search and branch-and-bound methods are optimal but struggle with large datasets.

The paper also discusses stopping criteria for feature selection processes, emphasizing their role in reducing computational complexity and preventing overfitting. Common stopping criteria include predefined numbers of features or iterations, percentage advancements over iterations, and evaluation function-based criteria. The selection of these criteria is influenced by earlier choices in the feature selection process.
