The paper discusses various methods for feature selection, focusing on both traditional and wrapper approaches. Traditional mutual information (MI) has limitations, as it often discards only a few redundant variables. To address this, Joint Mutual Information (JMI) uses Conditional MI to eliminate all redundant features while identifying relevant ones. In 2005, Peng, Long, and Ding introduced the minimal-Redundancy-Maximal-Relevance (mRMR) heuristic algorithm, which effectively removes redundant data while maintaining classification accuracy. Meyer and Bonetmpi's 2006 filter selection method, Double Input Symmetrical Relevance (DISR), enhances supervised classification by utilizing information theoretic measures to identify intrinsic features with more information about the target class.

Song, Ni, and Wang in 2013 proposed the Fast clustering-based feature selection algorithm (FAST), which employs graph theory and Minimum Spanning Tree (MST) techniques to cluster features, effectively removing irrelevant and redundant features through symmetric uncertainty measures.

In contrast, wrapper methods treat feature subset selection as a black box, relying on inductive algorithms to evaluate the accuracy of training models based on selected feature subsets. Kohavi and John (1997) highlighted this complexity in wrapper methods. Korfiatis et al. (2013) introduced the LM-FM method, which selects the best subset of features through Local Maximization followed by Floating Maximization, integrating support vector machines (SVM) for improved classification.

However, wrapper methods often struggle with computational efficiency. To address this, Chen and Chen (2015) proposed the Cosine Similarity Measure Support Vector Machines (CSMSVM), which incorporates cosine distance into SVM during feature selection, enhancing computational efficiency and reducing classification error rates. This method optimizes the margin in SVM while improving overall performance.
