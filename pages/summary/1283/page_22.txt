The paper discusses various methodologies and approaches in feature selection, particularly focusing on unsupervised and supervised techniques. Key references include works on the Weighted Pairwise Fisher Criteria for multiclass linear dimension reduction, the Trace Ratio Criterion for feature selection, and the application of the Helmholtz principle in supervised and unsupervised contexts. It also highlights the significance of statistical dependence measurement through Hilbert-Schmidt norms and explores the use of spectral analysis for feature selection across different learning paradigms. The integration of dense subgraph finding with feature clustering and the application of low-rank approximation methods are examined as innovative approaches. Additionally, the paper references various studies that contribute to the understanding of feature selection in the context of neural networks, EEG classification, and action recognition, showcasing a diverse range of applications and methodologies within the field.
