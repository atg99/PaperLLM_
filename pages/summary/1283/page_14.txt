In summary, the paper discusses various methods for unsupervised and semi-supervised feature selection, emphasizing their reliance on graph-based approaches and matrix factorization techniques. Key contributions include Hu et al.'s Graph Self Representation Sparse Feature Selection (GSR-SFS), Du et al.'s Robust Unsupervised Feature Selection via Matrix Factorization (RUFSM), and Qi et al.'s Regularized Matrix Factorization Feature Selection (RMFFS). These methods aim to improve feature stability and selection accuracy by integrating different regularization techniques, such as l2,1-norm. The evolution from rank-based to spectral clustering and graph-based learning methods for feature selection is highlighted, with a focus on the effectiveness of l2,1-norm methods. The semi-supervised feature selection section outlines the process of using graph Laplacian methods to utilize both labeled and unlabeled data, detailing the stages of assessing affinity, constructing sparse weighted subgraphs, and selecting features accordingly.
