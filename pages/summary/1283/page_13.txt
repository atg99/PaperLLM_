The paper discusses various methods for unsupervised feature selection that address the limitations of traditional approaches which calculate feature measures independently, ignoring their interrelationships. 

Z. Li et al. in 2012 and Y. Yang et al. in 2011 introduced a spectral-based clustering approach that utilizes matrix factorization to explore the cluster structure of data. This method employs a sparsity regularization model based on a learned graph Laplacian for feature selection.

In 2014, Bandyopadhyay et al. proposed a dense sub-graph approach for feature clustering. Their method represents the feature set as a graph, with features as vertices and edge weights determined by inter-feature similarity computed using mutual information. Feature selection occurs in two stages: first, identifying the densest sub-graph with non-redundant features, and second, minimizing feature sets through clustering.

X. Wang et al. in 2016 developed the Unsupervised Spectral Feature Selection (USFS) method, which employs an l1-norm graph and spectral clustering to select discriminative features from unlabelled datasets. However, the effectiveness of this approach in addressing manifold structures remains unclear.

Wen et al. also in 2016 proposed the Unsupervised Optimal Feature Selection (UOFS), which utilizes an l2,1-norm regularization matrix. This approach aims to optimize classification by eliminating the two-phase process of graph construction and subspace learning found in l1-norm methods. UOFS employs sparse representation for feature selection and extraction.

In 2017, S. Wang and H. Wang introduced a method based on low-rank approximation and structure learning. This method reformulates the feature selection problem as matrix factorization with low-rank constraints, capturing sparsity through the l2,1-norm method. However, it raises questions about adaptively learning feature subsets.

The same year, Y. Liu et al. presented the Diversity-Induced Self-Representation (DISR) method, which leverages self-representation properties and the Augmented Lagrange Method for optimization. By emphasizing diversity, DISR captures more information about the data, effectively reducing redundancy among features.

Overall, these studies collectively aim to enhance unsupervised feature selection through various innovative approaches, addressing the challenges of redundancy and inter-feature relationships.
