In 2015, Panthong and Srivihok proposed a wrapper feature selection (FS) algorithm that integrates ensemble learning algorithms with various search strategies, including Sequential Forward Selection (SFS), Sequential Backward Selection (SBS), and Optimize selection. They evaluated combinations of these strategies with classifiers such as Decision Trees and Naïve Bayes, leading to five specific methods: FBDT, BBDT, OBNB, OADT, and OANB. Their findings indicated that incorporating evolutionary algorithms with heuristic search significantly enhanced prediction accuracy.

In 2017, Das et al. introduced a wrapper FS method utilizing harmony search, a meta-heuristic inspired by musical processes, to select suitable words in the Bangla language. This approach diverged from traditional heuristic searches by applying harmony search for subset selection.

To address the time complexity associated with wrapper methods, Wang et al. proposed a novel approach that combines wrapper and filter methods using the Markov blanket technique. This technique efficiently identifies and removes redundant features based on their relevance to the target class, reducing the number of computations required by the wrapper method.

Masood, Soh, and Jiang also presented a ranking-based incremental search strategy, WRANK-ELM, in 2017. This method improves time complexity by using an incremental approach rather than exhaustive search, thus optimizing computational costs while evaluating selected features with the Extreme Learning Machine classifier.

Lastly, Bermejo introduced a combined wrapper method for feature selection, tested on fish age prediction. This ensemble wrapper approach calculated the cross-validation (CV) error for each subset of selected features, identifying the subset with the lowest CV error as the best. The collective performance demonstrated significant improvements in accuracy.
