The paper emphasizes the urgency of developing such systems due to the limitations of current diagnostic methods, including the subjective inconsistencies among radiologists and the shortage of trained professionals in low-resource countries. 

It highlights that chest X-rays, while currently the best method for diagnosing pneumonia, often lead to misclassification, which can result in inappropriate treatment and patient deterioration. The use of artificial intelligence, particularly convolutional neural networks (CNNs), has shown promise in improving the classification of pneumonia types. Various studies have explored different deep learning techniques and model architectures, achieving notable accuracy rates in distinguishing between normal and pneumonia cases as well as between bacterial and viral pneumonia.

The paper also discusses the potential of transfer learning and data augmentation to enhance model performance. While some studies have reported high accuracy rates, it concludes that there is still significant potential for improvement in the accuracy of pneumonia classification using deep learning methods.
