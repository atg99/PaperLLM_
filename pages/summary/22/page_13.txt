This paper investigates the performance of various deep learning networks in the automatic detection of pneumonia using chest X-ray images. It compares four popular convolutional neural network (CNN) architectures: AlexNet, ResNet18, DenseNet201, and SqueezeNet, across multiple performance metrics including accuracy, sensitivity (recall), specificity, precision, area under the curve (AUC), and F1 scores. The results indicate that DenseNet201 achieves the highest accuracy of 0.98 for classifying normal and pneumonia cases, outperforming the other models.

Additionally, the paper compares its findings with recent studies, highlighting that DenseNet201 also surpasses other models in accuracy and precision metrics. The authors suggest that training with a larger dataset and employing an ensemble of pre-trained CNNs could further enhance detection accuracy. The study concludes that a deep-CNN-based transfer learning approach is effective for pneumonia classification, with DenseNet201 being the most reliable model among those tested. Future work is recommended to improve the model's performance through larger datasets and advanced techniques.
