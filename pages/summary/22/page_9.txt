The paper discusses various image augmentation techniques employed to enhance the performance of convolutional neural networks (CNNs). Specifically, it describes the rotation operation, where images are rotated counterclockwise by 45 degrees (315 degrees clockwise), and scaling, which involves a 10% magnification of the image size. Additionally, image translation is performed with a 10% shift both horizontally and vertically.

The study also explores the visualization of activation layers within CNNs. By analyzing the activations of the first convolution layer across different network models, the researchers observed that the strongest activation channels corresponded to edge detection, particularly responding to light-dark contrasts.

To evaluate the performance of the trained CNNs, the study utilized five-fold cross-validation and assessed six metrics: accuracy, sensitivity (recall), specificity, precision, area under the curve (AUC), and F1 score. The formulas for calculating these metrics are provided, emphasizing the comprehensive evaluation of the networks' classification capabilities. Overall, the paper highlights the importance of augmentation techniques and performance metrics in improving the effectiveness of image classification using CNNs.
