The paper discusses the use of transfer learning with deep Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN) for the automatic detection of pneumonia from chest X-ray images. Pneumonia is a serious lung infection caused by bacterial or viral agents, leading to significant mortality, particularly among children. Early diagnosis is crucial for effective treatment.

The authors employed four pre-trained CNN models—AlexNet, ResNet18, DenseNet201, and SqueezeNet—to classify a dataset of 5247 chest X-ray images into three categories: normal, bacterial pneumonia, and viral pneumonia. The study reports three classification schemes: normal vs. pneumonia, bacterial vs. viral pneumonia, and a comprehensive classification of all three categories. The achieved classification accuracies were 98% for normal vs. pneumonia, 95% for bacterial vs. viral pneumonia, and 93.3% for the three-category classification, marking the highest reported accuracies in the literature for these tasks.

The findings suggest that the proposed methodology can significantly aid radiologists in diagnosing pneumonia more rapidly and can facilitate efficient screening processes, such as at airports, for pneumonia patients.
