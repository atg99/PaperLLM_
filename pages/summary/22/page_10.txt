The results indicate that deeper convolutional networks, such as DenseNet201, are effective at learning complex features and improving classification performance across different pneumonia types.

The study employed a systematic approach to evaluate the performance of various deep learning algorithms through transfer learning. The experiments were structured in three phases, progressively refining the classification from normal versus pneumonia to distinguishing between bacterial and viral pneumonia. Performance metrics, including accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, precision, area under the curve (AUC), and F1 score, were utilized to assess model efficacy comprehensively.

The findings highlight the potential of convolutional neural networks in medical image classification, particularly in diagnosing pneumonia, suggesting that deeper architectures can significantly enhance diagnostic accuracy. The results advocate for the continued exploration of advanced neural network models in clinical settings to improve patient outcomes.
