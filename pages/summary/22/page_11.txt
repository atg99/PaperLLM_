The paper presents a comparative analysis of different convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for classifying pneumonia from chest X-ray images. The study highlights the performance of DenseNet201, which achieved the highest accuracy across three classification schemes: 98% for normal versus pneumonia, 93.3% for normal, bacterial, and viral pneumonia, and 95% for bacterial versus viral pneumonia. The evaluation metrics, including the area under the curve (AUC) and receiver-operating characteristics (ROC) curves, further confirm DenseNet201's superiority over other algorithms.

The classification process utilized true positive (TP), true negative (TN), false positive (FP), and false negative (FN) metrics to assess the model's performance accurately. The results indicate that DenseNet201 consistently outperforms other CNN models in terms of various performance indices, as summarized in the performance matrix.

Additionally, the authors compare their findings with recent studies, noting that other models like CXNet-m1 and VGG16 also reported high sensitivity, precision, and accuracy in pneumonia detection. However, DenseNet201 demonstrated superior results across the evaluated classification schemes. The paper concludes by providing a comprehensive overview of the performance of various models in pneumonia detection, underscoring the effectiveness of DenseNet201 in this domain.
