This section of the paper discusses various convolutional neural networks (CNNs) used for pneumonia detection, specifically highlighting DenseNet201 and SqueezeNet. 

DenseNet, or Dense Convolutional Network, is noted for its efficiency as it requires fewer parameters than traditional CNNs by avoiding redundant feature maps. It consists of narrow layers with 12 filters and has several variants, including DenseNet121, DenseNet169, DenseNet201, and DenseNet264. The study utilizes DenseNet201, which allows each layer direct access to the original input image and gradients from the loss function, significantly reducing computational costs and enhancing image classification performance.

SqueezeNet is another CNN trained on the ImageNet database, utilizing over one million images. It boasts 50 times fewer parameters than AlexNet, based on a fire module architecture that includes a squeeze layer with 1x1 filters and an expand layer with a mix of 1x1 and 3x3 convolution filters. The researchers employed a pre-trained SqueezeNet model to detect pneumonia and differentiate between bacterial and viral pneumonia.

Overall, the use of these advanced CNN architectures facilitates effective pneumonia detection while minimizing computational demands.
