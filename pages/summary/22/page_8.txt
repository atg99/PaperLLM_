The paper discusses the preprocessing steps and methodologies used in training convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for classifying chest X-ray images. Key points include the resizing of images to meet the input requirements of different pre-trained CNN models, such as AlexNet and ResNet18, which necessitated specific dimensions (227x227 pixels for AlexNet and SqueezeNet, and 224x224 pixels for ResNet18 and DenseNet201). The images were also normalized according to the standards of the pre-trained models.

Data augmentation techniques were employed to enhance the limited dataset size, which is crucial for improving the classification accuracy of deep learning algorithms. The authors implemented three augmentation strategies: rotation, scaling, and translation. For rotation, images were rotated up to 360 degrees, with a specific instance of 315 degrees counterclockwise used in the study. Scaling involved a 10% magnification of the image, while translation included a 10% shift both horizontally and vertically.

Additionally, the paper examines the activation layers of the CNNs to understand feature detection. The first convolutional layer was analyzed for activation patterns, revealing that it primarily detects edges and color variations. The study further involved performance evaluations across different classification scenarios: distinguishing between normal and pneumonia cases, and differentiating between bacterial and viral pneumonia.

Overall, the methodology emphasizes the importance of preprocessing and data augmentation in enhancing the performance of deep learning models in medical image classification tasks.
