The paper discusses the use of object keypoints in a Markov Decision Process (MDP) framework to enhance learning efficiency and exploration in reinforcement learning. The authors propose a two-part approach focusing on the abstraction of visual states through transporter keypoints.

In section 3.2.1, the authors hypothesize that learning object keypoints in a task-agnostic manner can simplify policy learning. By utilizing a neural fitted Q-learning variant and incorporating keypoint coordinates as input, they assert that the control policy can be more straightforward, avoiding the need to relearn visual features. The Transporter network, which is pre-trained using a random policy, provides a foundational representation, allowing for behavior learning based on environment rewards.

In section 3.2.2, the authors explore the concept of keypoint-based options for more efficient exploration. They argue that traditional exploration methods, which often involve random action sampling, are inefficient. By leveraging the learned keypoints to create a new action space grounded in keypoint coordinate control, they demonstrate improved exploration efficiency. They define four intrinsic reward functions corresponding to movements of keypoints in cardinal directions and train multiple Q functions to maximize these rewards. The exploration strategy involves sampling from this reduced action space, leading to a significant reduction in the search space compared to using raw actions.

Overall, the paper presents a compelling argument for the utility of object keypoints in enhancing both learning speed and exploration efficiency in reinforcement learning tasks, particularly in complex environments like Atari games. The results indicate that the proposed methods can lead to better performance and reduced computational complexity.
