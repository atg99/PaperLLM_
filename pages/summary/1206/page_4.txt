The paper discusses the method for unsupervised discovery of object keypoints from video sequences, particularly in the context of a challenging game called Montezuma's Revenge. It demonstrates that the learned keypoints maintain temporal consistency across numerous environment steps. The study highlights the identification of the most controllable keypoints, which align closely with the game's avatar, showing consistent tracking throughout the state space.

In the methodological section, the authors outline their approach to extracting two-dimensional keypoint locations from images without manual labeling. They utilize pairs of frames that differ only in object pose or appearance to reconstruct the second frame from the first. The process involves computing feature maps through a convolutional neural network (CNN) and marginalizing keypoint detector feature maps. A transported feature map is created by suppressing keypoint locations and compositing features from both frames, followed by refinement of the reconstruction.

The final goal is to minimize pixel-wise squared error between the target and reconstructed frames, enabling the keypoint network to effectively track moving entities. The method distinguishes itself by enforcing explicit spatial transport, enhancing correlation with image locations and thereby improving long-term tracking capabilities. The paper emphasizes the importance of this approach for applications in data-efficient reinforcement learning and exploration strategies.
