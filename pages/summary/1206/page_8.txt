The paper investigates the use of keypoint options for efficient exploration in reinforcement learning, specifically within Atari games. It compares the performance of random actions to a learned model that utilizes controllable keypoints. The authors present quantitative results showing that their approach significantly outperforms the random actions baseline, particularly in terms of discovering rare and rewarding states.

The methodology involves a distributed off-policy learner with a CNN architecture and LSTM for action selection, using a simultaneous learning approach for the Transporter model and control policies. The experiments demonstrate that the learned keypoints facilitate temporally extended exploration, allowing the agent to navigate various environments effectively, such as Montezuma’s Revenge, Seaquest, and Ms. Pacman, without relying on extrinsic rewards.

The findings suggest that visual abstractions, when paired with simple control algorithms, lead to data-efficient reinforcement learning strategies. The paper emphasizes that while pre-training keypoints with random policy data is insufficient for certain games, the right approach can yield substantial improvements in exploration efficiency and skill acquisition in complex environments.
