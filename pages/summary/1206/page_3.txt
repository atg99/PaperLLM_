This section presents visualizations of keypoints learned through the proposed method compared to state-of-the-art unsupervised object keypoint discovery methods by Jakab et al. and Zhang et al. The visualizations demonstrate that the proposed method achieves more spatially aligned keypoints in domains such as Atari ALE and Manipulator. The quantitative evaluations supporting these findings are provided in a subsequent figure, with additional visualizations available in supplementary materials.

The related work discusses advancements in unsupervised object keypoint discovery, highlighting the encoder-decoder architecture with a differentiable keypoint bottleneck introduced by Jakab and Gupta. The proposed method builds upon this architecture by incorporating a feature transport mechanism, which enhances spatial alignment of the learned keypoints. In contrast to Zhang et al., whose method relies on temporal transformations and multiple loss terms, the proposed approach utilizes a straightforward pixel-wise L2 loss without needing access to transformation data. Other works in the field either require known transformations or produce dense correspondences rather than discrete landmarks.

The paper notes that while deep generative models have advanced, they do not explicitly reason about geometry, and unsupervised keypoint learning has been less explored in control literature. The referenced model has limitations due to its use of fully connected layers, leading to non-spatial latent embeddings. Additionally, studies on object-centric representations face challenges in capturing detailed representations over extended time frames.
