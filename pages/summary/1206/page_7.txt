The paper presents a novel agent architecture designed for data-efficient reinforcement learning, specifically in Atari games, utilizing a recurrent variant of Neural Fitted Q-iteration. The architecture, termed Keypoint Q-Network (KeyQN), leverages keypoint coordinates and features extracted from observations to enhance learning efficiency.

The study compares the performance of KeyQN against other established methods such as Rainbow, SimPLe, and PPO across various Atari games. Results indicate that KeyQN outperforms these methods in four out of five games tested, showcasing its effectiveness in learning control policies from limited data. The paper includes detailed metrics on precision and recall for detected keypoint trajectories, emphasizing the importance of long-term consistency in object tracking for successful control.

Figures illustrate the agent's architecture and its performance metrics, revealing that the Transporter, trained offline with a random policy, successfully tracks salient object keypoints over extended timeframes, thus contributing to the overall performance improvements in the reinforcement learning context.
