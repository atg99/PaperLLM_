The paper evaluates a new object keypoint detector's long-term tracking capabilities against established methods by Jakab et al. and Zhang et al. The evaluation spans various Atari games and a manipulator environment, focusing on precision and recall across trajectory lengths from 1 to 200 frames. Results indicate that the proposed method significantly surpasses the baselines in most games, achieving 100% precision on Pong, while showing comparable performance on Ms. Pacman for longer trajectories.

The experiments are divided into two main sections: the first assesses long-term tracking ability, while the second explores the keypoint detector's application in data-efficient learning and exploration within Atari games. The datasets utilized include varied levels from Atari ALE and the Manipulator environment, chosen for their complexity and diversity in object interactions.

For long-term tracking evaluation, the authors utilize a range of games, collecting data through random exploration to train their model. Keypoints are derived from game-specific rules and simulator geoms, with the number of keypoints aligning with the maximum number of moving entities in each scenario.

In terms of baseline comparisons, the paper employs the same architecture for Jakab et al.'s method and uses Zhang et al.'s publicly available implementation for a fair assessment. All methods are trained for a consistent number of optimization steps, ensuring a level playing field for performance evaluation. The supplementary material contains additional details regarding implementation and data generation.
