The paper concludes that stable object keypoints can be learned across numerous environmental steps without relying on task-specific reward functions. This finding suggests that object keypoints can serve as a versatile representation for efficient control and reinforcement learning. The authors highlight the necessity for further research to scale keypoints for reliable performance in more complex datasets and environments. Additionally, tracking objects over extended time sequences could facilitate the understanding of object dynamics and affordances, which may enhance learning policies. A noted limitation is the model's inability to manage moving backgrounds, suggesting the potential integration of recent methods that account for camera motion to improve feature transport between frames. Overall, the experiments demonstrate the viability of learning visual abstractions and employing simple algorithms to achieve data-efficient control policies and exploration strategies.
