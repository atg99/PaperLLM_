The paper presents a novel architecture called Transporter, which is designed to discover and track keypoints in videos, addressing challenges in reinforcement learning (RL) environments. The model utilizes object motion to transform a source video frame into a target frame, focusing on spatially, temporally, and geometrically aligned keypoints. 

During training, the model predicts spatial feature maps and keypoint coordinates for both frames using a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) and the fully-differentiable KeyNet. Keypoints are transformed into Gaussian heatmaps, and two main operations are performed during the transport phase: zeroing out features at specific locations and replacing features in the source image with those from the target image. A refinement ConvNet then inpaints missing features and cleans the image.

The authors assert that existing methods struggle with keypoint tracking due to variability in object characteristics. Transporter, however, learns accurate and consistent keypoints that can track objects even as they deform. The paper highlights two applications of these keypoints in control and RL: improved data efficiency in reinforcement learning on Atari games and the ability to learn skills for exploration based on the manipulation of keypoint coordinates.

Key contributions of the paper include:
1. Transporter achieves state-of-the-art performance in learning object keypoints across various RL environments, demonstrating robustness to changes in object number, size, and motion.
2. Keypoints as inputs for policies outperform existing model-free and model-based RL methods using significantly fewer environment interactions.
3. The learned skills enable an efficient action space for exploration, leading to improved performance in challenging Atari environments without reliance on rewards or task-specific learning.
