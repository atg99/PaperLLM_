The paper discusses the implementation of an INT8 training framework for MobileNetV2 on the CIFAR-10 dataset, focusing on the challenges and implications of quantizing gradients to 8-bit integers. The authors highlight that symmetric uniform quantization is chosen for its efficiency and hardware compatibility. The quantization process involves clipping and scaling to convert floating-point data into fixed-point representations, followed by dequantization to retrieve approximate values.

A significant aspect of the study is the exploration of stochastic rounding to minimize quantization errors, although it acknowledges that perturbations from quantization can hinder convergence during training. The authors present empirical results demonstrating that direct gradient quantization can lead to unstable training, particularly in certain architectures like MobileNetV2, resulting in performance degradation.

The analysis reveals that gradients exhibit unique distribution characteristics, such as being sharp and wide, which can exacerbate issues related to quantization. The paper emphasizes the critical role of gradients in optimization and highlights the need for careful consideration when implementing gradient quantization to avoid detrimental effects on model performance.
