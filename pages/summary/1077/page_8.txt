The paper presents a novel INT8 training framework for deep convolutional neural networks (DCNNs), showcasing its effectiveness through extensive experiments on various datasets including CIFAR-10, ImageNet, PASCAL VOC, and COCO. 

Key findings include competitive accuracy levels compared to full precision (FP32) models across multiple architectures. On the CIFAR-10 dataset, the proposed method achieved an accuracy of 91.95% with ResNet-20 and 93.38% with MobileNetV2, while maintaining 95.00% with InceptionV3. In the ImageNet dataset, the method also demonstrated significant performance, achieving 69.67% with ResNet-18 and 73.29% with ResNet-34.

For object detection tasks on PASCAL VOC and COCO datasets, the INT8 training showed minimal degradation in mean Average Precision (mAP), indicating robustness in performance. Specifically, the mAP drop was less than 1.8% for both Faster R-CNN and RetinaNet architectures.

The paper also highlights the speed advantages of the proposed INT8 training method, reporting an average speedup of 1.63 times in the forward process and 1.94 times in the backward process on NVIDIA Pascal GPUs compared to FP32 training. 

The authors introduce two theoretical principles to stabilize training and propose techniques such as Direction Sensitive Gradient Clipping and Deviation Counteractive Learning Rate Scaling. They conclude that their approach not only reduces training time by 22% but also paves the way for future advancements in unified INT8 training in the deep learning community.
