In the paper "Towards Unified INT8 Training for Convolutional Neural Network," the authors explore a unified framework for low-bit training, specifically focusing on 8-bit (INT8) quantization for convolutional neural networks (CNNs). The motivation behind this research stems from the need to accelerate both inference and training processes in deep learning, as traditional methods can be computationally intensive and time-consuming.

The authors identify four key characteristics of gradients that are crucial for effective gradient quantization. They conduct a theoretical analysis to establish a convergence bound for INT8 training and propose two fundamental principles to ensure stability during the training process. These principles lead to the development of two innovative techniques: Direction Sensitive Gradient Clipping, which minimizes directional deviations of gradients, and Deviation Counteractive Learning Rate Scaling, which adjusts learning rates to prevent improper updates in the presence of quantization errors.

The empirical results demonstrate that the proposed framework achieves accurate and efficient INT8 training across various neural network architectures, including MobileNetV2 and InceptionV3, as well as in object detection tasks. The framework shows notable flexibility, allowing it to run effectively on standard hardware and reducing training time by 22% on Pascal GPUs with minimal optimization. The authors assert that their work paves the way for a comprehensive approach to INT8 training in CNNs, addressing challenges that previous studies have not successfully resolved.
