This paper discusses the challenges and solutions associated with INT8 training for deep neural networks, highlighting its potential advantages over FP16 training. While backward propagation is more time-consuming than forward propagation, INT8 operations can be significantly faster and more power-efficient, making them attractive for training on general GPUs. However, quantizing gradients to 8 bits often leads to instability in training, as gradient distortion can misdirect optimization efforts. 

The authors identify four unique characteristics of gradient distribution that complicate quantization: sharpness and width, evolutionary changes, depth-specific behavior, and structure-specific traits. To address these challenges, they theoretically establish a convergence bound relating to gradient quantization error and learning rate. They propose two techniques to stabilize INT8 training: Direction Sensitive Gradient Clipping, which adapts clipping based on training progression to minimize directional deviations, and Deviation Counteractive Learning Rate Scaling, which ensures stable parameter updates despite accumulated gradient deviations.

The paper makes several contributions, including the identification of the aforementioned characteristics, the theoretical groundwork for convergence in INT8 training, and the development of a flexible framework that achieves stable training across various networks and tasks. Notably, the authors demonstrate practical INT8 training acceleration on lower-end GPUs, achieving about 22% speedup without extensive optimization. The paper also reviews related works, emphasizing the scarcity of comprehensive studies on quantized training, particularly for backward propagation. Overall, the proposed INT8 training framework offers a promising avenue for efficient deep learning model training.
