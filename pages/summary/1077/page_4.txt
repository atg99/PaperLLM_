The paper discusses the challenges of gradient quantization in deep learning, highlighting four key characteristics: 

1. **Distribution Shape**: The gradient distribution is sharp and quantized, leading to significant quantization errors when uniform quantization is applied.
2. **Evolution During Training**: As training progresses, the gradient distribution becomes sharper and narrower, indicating that fixed quantization settings are inadequate.
3. **Layer Depth**: Shallow layers tend to have sharper distributions with larger extreme values compared to deeper layers, resulting in more severe quantization loss for earlier layers.
4. **Structural Differences**: Different layer structures, such as those found in MobileNetV2, exhibit distinct gradient patterns, complicating the quantization process further.

These observations suggest that gradients are fundamentally different from weights and activations, necessitating specialized techniques for gradient quantization to stabilize training and achieve effective INT8 training.

The paper also establishes a theoretical foundation for analyzing the connection between gradient quantization and training stability using the concept of regret. It highlights that the average regret is influenced by three terms, with the quantization error of gradients being particularly impactful. To enhance convergence stability during INT8 training, the authors propose two principles: reducing quantization error and scaling down the learning rate.

Finally, the paper indicates the need for novel techniques that accommodate the unique characteristics of gradients in the context of INT8 training.
