The paper presents datasets for training, validation, and testing in the context of remote sensing applications. Specifically, it outlines the categorization of samples for the Salinas Valley (SV) dataset and the Botswana (BS) dataset, detailing the total number of samples along with their distribution across training, validation, and testing phases.

For the SV dataset, a total of 54,129 samples are categorized into 16 classes, with the majority of samples allocated for testing. The class with the highest number of samples is "Grapes-untrained," while others include various forms of broccoli, lettuce, and fallow land. 

In the BS dataset, 3,248 samples are classified into 14 categories, including water, various grass types, and acacia woodlands, with a similar structure of distribution across training, validation, and testing.

The paper also discusses various machine learning architectures used for processing these datasets, including Support Vector Machine (SVM) with a radial basis function kernel, and several convolutional neural networks (CNNs) such as CDCNN, SSRN, FDSSC, and DBMA. Each architecture has specific input sizes based on the number of spectral bands, and the training parameters, including batch size and optimizer settings, are standardized across the models. 

This structured approach ensures a comprehensive analysis of the datasets while leveraging advanced deep learning architectures for enhanced classification performance.
