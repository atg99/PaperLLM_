The paper presents a comparative analysis of various algorithms for remote sensing data classification, focusing on their training and testing times across different datasets, namely Indian Pines, Pavia University, Salinas, and Botswana. The algorithms evaluated include Support Vector Machines (SVM), Contextual Deep Convolutional Neural Networks (CDCNN), Spectral-Spatial Residual Network (SSRN), Fast Dense Spectral-Spatial Convolution (FDSSC), Double-Branch Multi-Attention (DBMA), and the proposed method.

Key findings reveal that the proposed method demonstrates a balance between training efficiency and classification accuracy. For instance, in the Indian Pines dataset, the proposed method required 69.83 seconds for training and 5.60 seconds for testing. In contrast, FDSSC and DBMA required significantly more time for training, while SVM consistently showed lower training times but less accuracy. The proposed method outperformed others in accuracy despite longer training times compared to SVM, indicating its effectiveness in scenarios with limited training samples.

Further assessments included varying the proportions of training samples, which confirmed that the proposed method retains its effectiveness even with severely limited data. The results from ablation experiments were also discussed, emphasizing the robustness of the proposed approach in enhancing classification performance while managing computational resources effectively. Overall, the proposed method stands out for its superior accuracy and efficiency in processing remote sensing data.
