The paper discusses the architecture of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) with a focus on dense connections and attention mechanisms in the context of remote sensing. It highlights how each convolution layer generates feature maps with a specific number of channels, influenced by the number of layers and kernels. The output channels from a dense block can be mathematically expressed, showing a linear relationship between the number of channels and convolution layers.

A significant limitation of 3D-CNNs is the equal weighting of spatial pixels and spectral bands, which does not account for their varying contributions to feature extraction. To address this, the paper introduces the attention mechanism, inspired by human visual perception, which emphasizes informative areas while downplaying non-essential ones. This mechanism has been effectively applied across various domains, including image categorization and scene segmentation.

The paper details the spectral attention block, which computes a channel attention map from the initial input through matrix multiplication and softmax operations. This mechanism enhances the significance of certain channels, ultimately refining the feature extraction process in remote sensing applications. The proposed approach aims to improve the performance of CNNs by leveraging dense connections and attention mechanisms for more effective feature representation.
