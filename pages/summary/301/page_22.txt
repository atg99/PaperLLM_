This paper presents a novel end-to-end framework known as the Double-Branch Dual-Attention Mechanism Network (DBDA) for hyperspectral image (HSI) classification. The DBDA framework utilizes original 3D pixel data directly, avoiding the need for extensive preprocessing to reduce dimensionality. It consists of two branches designed to capture spectral and spatial features, utilizing densely connected 3D-CNN layers with batch normalization.

The paper highlights the effectiveness of the attention mechanism, demonstrating that both the spatial and spectral attention modules significantly enhance classification accuracy, with an average overall accuracy (OA) improvement of 4.69% across four datasets. The spatial attention mechanism shows a greater average improvement (2.18%) compared to the spectral mechanism (0.97%).

Additionally, the choice of activation function is examined, comparing the performance of the Mish function against the commonly used ReLU. The results indicate that the DBDA framework using Mish outperforms that using ReLU, achieving OA improvements of up to 4.00% on various datasets. This performance enhancement is attributed to Mish’s ability to accelerate counter-propagation.

Overall, the proposed DBDA framework outperforms existing state-of-the-art algorithms, particularly in scenarios with limited training samples, while also reducing computational time. The authors suggest future work could involve applying the framework to other hyperspectral images and exploring further reductions in training time.
