The paper discusses a methodology for hyperspectral image classification using a dual-branch deep learning architecture, referred to as DBDA. The architecture consists of two branches: a spectral branch and a spatial branch. The spectral branch processes the hyperspectral data to extract spectral feature maps, while the spatial branch captures spatial features through a series of convolutional layers, batch normalization, and dropout techniques. The spatial branch's implementation details include various convolutional layers designed to reduce dimensions and enhance feature representation, culminating in a global average pooling layer that outputs spatial feature maps.

The classification process involves concatenating the spectral and spatial feature maps, which is preferred over addition because it maintains the independence of the features from different domains. The final classification is performed using a fully connected layer with a softmax activation function.

Additionally, the paper introduces training strategies such as early stopping to prevent overfitting and a dynamic learning rate adjustment method using cosine annealing. This method adjusts the learning rate throughout the training process to help the model escape local minima.

Overall, the methodology aims to improve classification accuracy in hyperspectral imaging by effectively integrating spectral and spatial information while employing robust training techniques.
