The paper discusses the implementation of spectral and spatial attention blocks in a neural network framework, aimed at enhancing feature representation in remote sensing applications. 

In the spectral attention block, the influence of different channels on each other is quantified through a matrix multiplication process, resulting in a reshaped spectral attention map. This process involves combining the features of all channels to capture long-range dependencies and improve feature discriminability. The final spectral attention map is expressed mathematically, incorporating a learnable parameter that initializes at zero and is updated throughout training.

The spatial attention block operates on an input feature map, employing two convolutional layers to generate new feature maps. These maps are reshaped and multiplied to compute spatial attention feature maps, which evaluate the correlation between pixels. A softmax function is applied to derive a normalized representation of pixel interactions. The original feature map is then processed to produce a final output that integrates weighted contributions from all positions, effectively modeling long-range contextual information in the spatial dimension.

Overall, the proposed attention mechanisms aim to enhance the model's ability to capture and utilize both spectral and spatial relationships in the input data, leading to improved performance in remote sensing tasks.
