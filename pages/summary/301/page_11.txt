The paper discusses strategies to prevent overfitting in neural networks, particularly in the context of a specific model. It highlights the importance of using a suitable activation function, specifically the Mish function, which introduces nonlinearity and improves convergence speed compared to the conventional ReLU function. The Mish function is defined mathematically, demonstrating its upper and lower bounds and its differentiability.

In addition to the activation function, the authors implement several training techniques to mitigate overfitting. The early stopping strategy is employed to halt training if the loss function does not improve over a specified number of epochs, which in this case is 20. This approach helps save training time and reduces the risk of overfitting.

Dynamic learning rate adjustment is also utilized, specifically through the cosine annealing method. This technique allows the learning rate to vary dynamically during training, which helps the network navigate local minima effectively.

Moreover, a dropout layer is integrated into the model between the batch normalization and global average pooling layers in both spectral and spatial branches. This method randomly drops units during training, which helps prevent co-adaptation of units and reduces overfitting. The dropout rate used in this framework is set at 0.5.

Overall, the combination of Mish activation function, early stopping, dynamic learning rate adjustment, and dropout layers constitutes a robust strategy for enhancing model performance while mitigating overfitting.
