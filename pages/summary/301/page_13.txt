The paper discusses the setup and evaluation of a deep learning framework for remote sensing data classification, specifically focusing on two datasets: Indian Pines (IP) and Pavia University (UP). The training and validation samples for both datasets are detailed in tables, highlighting the distribution of classes and the total number of samples.

For the Indian Pines dataset, a total of 10,249 samples are categorized into 16 classes with specific allocations for training, validation, and testing. Similarly, the Pavia University dataset comprises 42,776 samples across 9 classes, also with defined training, validation, and testing splits.

The experimental setup involves comparing the proposed deep learning framework, referred to as DBDA, against existing classifiers, including CDCNN, SSRN, FDSSC, and the advanced DBMA, alongside a support vector machine (SVM) using a radial basis function (RBF) kernel. The experiments are conducted on a standardized platform with a powerful GPU, ensuring consistent conditions for evaluating the time consumption of training and testing across the different classifiers. The paper indicates that further details on each method will be provided in subsequent sections.
