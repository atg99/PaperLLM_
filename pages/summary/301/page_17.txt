The paper presents an evaluation of various classification methods applied to different datasets, including the UP, SV, and BS datasets. Key metrics such as overall accuracy (OA), average accuracy (AA), and kappa statistics are reported for each method. 

For the UP dataset, the proposed method outperforms others, achieving class-specific accuracies exceeding 89%, demonstrating its effectiveness in distinguishing between different classes. The method utilizes sufficient training samples, allowing it to avoid overfitting, which is a challenge in methods like DBMA.

In the SV dataset, the proposed method also shows superior performance, particularly with high accuracy rates across multiple classes. The results indicate that the proposed framework effectively captures both spatial and spectral features, which is crucial for accurate classification.

The BS dataset, characterized by a smaller sample size, still yielded impressive results with the proposed method achieving 96.24% OA, surpassing DBMA by 2.81%. This improved performance is attributed to the method's ability to effectively leverage spatial and spectral information even with limited data.

Overall, the proposed framework demonstrates significant improvements over existing methods, with enhancements in OA, AA, and kappa statistics, showcasing its robustness and reliability in handling various datasets and potential overfitting scenarios. The paper emphasizes the importance of architecture and sample size in achieving effective classification results in remote sensing applications.
