The paper discusses the architecture of the DBDA network, which is designed to effectively capture both spectral and spatial features from input data. The network consists of two main branches: an upper spectral branch and a lower spatial branch. The spectral branch includes a dense spectral block and a channel attention block, aimed at extracting spectral features. Conversely, the spatial branch is made up of a dense spatial block and a spatial attention block, focused on capturing spatial features.

The spatial branch processes input data shaped as 9x9x200, with an initial 3D-CNN layer sized at 1x1x200 to reduce the spectral dimensions. This results in feature maps of shape 9x9x1 with 24 channels. The dense spatial block, which consists of multiple 3D-CNNs with batch normalization, further processes these maps, using kernels of size 3x3x1. The resulting feature maps are then passed through the spatial attention block. This block enhances the discriminative power of the spatial features by weighting the coefficients of each pixel, thereby improving the overall feature extraction process. The use of dropout is also emphasized to prevent co-adaptation between units, ensuring more reliable feature representations.
