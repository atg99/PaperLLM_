The paper presents a detailed evaluation of various classification methods applied to remote sensing datasets, specifically focusing on the BS and SV datasets. It includes a comparison of performance metrics such as overall accuracy (OA), average accuracy (AA), and kappa statistics for different algorithms: Color SVM, CDCNN, SSRN, FDSSC, DBMA, and the proposed method.

In Table 9, results for the SV dataset with 0.5% training samples show that the proposed method achieves the highest OA of 97.51%, AA of 98.00%, and a kappa value of 0.9723, outperforming other methods across most classes. Similarly, Table 10 displays results for the BS dataset with 1.2% training samples, where the proposed method also leads with an OA of 96.24%, illustrating its effectiveness in accurately classifying data despite limited training samples.

The paper emphasizes that the proposed method's superior performance is attributed to its ability to effectively capture spatial and spectral features, which is crucial given the small size of the training dataset. Additionally, it discusses the methods' efficiency, noting that while SVM is faster due to its pixel-based approach, the proposed method balances accuracy and efficiency better than others, particularly among 3D-CNN-based models.

In terms of running time, the proposed method demonstrates less training time compared to FDSSC and DBMA while achieving better accuracy, indicating its higher convergence rate. Overall, the findings confirm the proposed method's robustness and adaptability in handling remote sensing classification tasks with limited data.
