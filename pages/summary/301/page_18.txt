The paper presents a comparative analysis of various classification methods applied to different datasets, specifically focusing on the SV and BS datasets. Results indicate that the proposed method outperforms others in terms of accuracy, achieving over 93% accuracy for each category in the SV dataset, which contains 16 classes. The performance of the proposed method is notably better than that of FDSSC due to the sufficient number of training samples, with only 0.5% of the dataset required for effective training.

In contrast, the UP dataset, which has only 9 classes, shows that CDCNN performs weaker than SVM, highlighting the influence of class complexity on algorithm performance. For the BS dataset, despite its smaller size with only 3,248 labeled samples, the proposed method achieves an overall accuracy of 96.24%, surpassing DBMA by 2.81%. This improvement is attributed to the method's enhanced ability to capture spatial and spectral features effectively. Classification maps and categorized results for all datasets are provided, illustrating the superior performance of the proposed approach.
