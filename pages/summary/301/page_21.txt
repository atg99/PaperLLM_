The paper investigates the effectiveness of the attention mechanism and the impact of training sample proportions on the performance of a proposed deep learning framework, DBDA, for remote sensing data analysis. The results indicate that the Mish activation function outperforms ReLU in the context of DBDA.

In examining the proportion of training samples, the study tests various percentages (0.5% to 10% for IP and BS datasets, and 0.1% to 10% for UP and SV datasets) to determine their effect on overall accuracy (OA). The findings show that as the amount of training data increases, the accuracy improves across all tested models, with DBDA consistently outperforming others, particularly with limited samples. This demonstrates the method's efficiency in reducing labeling costs while maintaining high accuracy.

Furthermore, the study assesses the contribution of the attention mechanism by removing the spatial and spectral attention modules. The results reveal that both attention mechanisms significantly enhance accuracy, with an average improvement of 4.69% OA across the datasets. Notably, the spatial attention mechanism yields a greater improvement (2.18%) compared to the spectral mechanism (0.97%). Overall, the incorporation of attention mechanisms is confirmed to be crucial for the model's performance.
