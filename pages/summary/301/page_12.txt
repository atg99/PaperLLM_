The paper explores the implementation of a dynamic learning rate using the cosine annealing method, which helps to optimize network training by avoiding local minima. The effectiveness of the proposed model, referred to as DBDA, is evaluated through experiments on four hyperspectral imaging (HSI) datasets: Indian Pines, Pavia University, Salinas Valley, and Botswana. 

The accuracy of the model is assessed using three metrics: overall accuracy (OA), average accuracy (AA), and Kappa coefficient (K). These metrics quantify the model's performance in classifying land cover classes, with higher values indicating better classification results. The experiments also measure the running time of each framework to gauge efficiency. 

Despite the inherent data demands of deep learning algorithms, the DBDA demonstrates robust performance even with minimal training samples, indicating its potential for applications where labeled data is scarce. The paper details the specifications of each dataset, including the number of spectral bands, wavelength ranges, land cover classes, and spatial resolutions, to provide context for the experimental results.
