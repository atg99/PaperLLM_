The paper discusses the architecture and functionality of 3D convolutional neural networks (CNNs) with a focus on batch normalization (BN), ResNet, and DenseNet. The 3D CNN structure involves input feature maps and includes nonlinear activation functions that enhance the network's capabilities. 

ResNet employs skip connections, allowing data to bypass layers, which mitigates issues related to vanishing and exploding gradients that often arise in deeper networks. The residual block, a fundamental component of ResNet, computes its output by adding the input to the output of a hidden block containing convolutional and activation layers. 

DenseNet builds on ResNet's principles by connecting all layers directly, facilitating optimal information flow. Instead of summing features as in ResNet, DenseNet concatenates them, resulting in a denser network structure. The dense block outputs a combination of all preceding feature maps, significantly increasing the number of connections compared to traditional networks with the same number of layers.

The paper emphasizes the advantages of these architectures in improving deep learning performance while addressing common gradient issues, underscoring the relevance of the Mish activation function in their framework.
