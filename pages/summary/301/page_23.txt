In this paper, a novel framework for hyperspectral image (HSI) classification is presented, which incorporates two branches to effectively capture spectral and spatial features. An adaptive self-attention mechanism is applied to both branches, enhancing their performance. The activation function Mish is utilized to improve convergence speed, while dynamic learning rates, early stopping, and dropout layers are implemented to mitigate overfitting. 

Experimental results indicate that this framework outperforms existing state-of-the-art algorithms, particularly in scenarios with limited training samples, and also demonstrates reduced processing time compared to FDSSC and DBMA. The authors conclude that the proposed structure is more advantageous for HSI classification. Future work will involve applying this framework to additional hyperspectral datasets and addressing the challenge of further reducing training time.
