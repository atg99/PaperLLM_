The paper presents various classification methods applied to remote sensing datasets, specifically focusing on the performance of the proposed framework, DBDA, against traditional methods like SVM, CDCNN, SSRN, FDSSC, and DBMA. The training process involves setting an epoch limit of 200, and training is terminated if validation loss does not improve over 20 epochs.

For the IP dataset, the results show that the proposed method outperforms others, achieving an overall accuracy (OA) of 97.51%, an average accuracy (AA) of 98.00%, and a kappa statistic of 0.9723. The method demonstrates significant improvements, especially for classes with limited training samples, such as class 7, where it achieved 92.59% accuracy compared to much lower scores from other methods.

In the BS dataset, with only 1.2% training samples, the proposed framework also outperformed DBMA by 2.81%, achieving an OA of 96.24%. The results indicate that DBDA effectively captures spatial and spectral features, leading to better classification outcomes.

The UP dataset results further highlight the robustness of the proposed method, achieving the highest OA of 95.38%, AA of 96.47%, and kappa of 0.9474. The paper concludes that the DBDA method provides stable and reliable performance, especially in scenarios with limited training data, by employing a flexible attention mechanism and other strategies to mitigate overfitting.
