The paper discusses advancements in Deep Transfer Learning (DTL) and its significance in overcoming challenges faced by traditional Deep Learning (DL) models, particularly the reliance on large labeled datasets and high training costs. Key findings from various studies, including a comprehensive analysis involving over 1200 experiments across diverse image domains and task types, highlight the critical role of fully connected layers near the output in models. It was observed that the importance of model components increases from input to output layers, aligning with the notion that earlier layers capture general features while later layers specialize in specific tasks.

The research also identifies three main factors influencing transfer learning: image domain differences between source and target tasks, variations in task types, and the size of datasets. Techniques such as data normalization and augmentation were employed to enhance model accuracy, utilizing the HRNetV2 backbone, which allows for easy adaptation across datasets.

The evaluation of transfer learning models revealed several insights: transfer learning consistently outperforms training from scratch; a suitable source task can yield significant performance gains; within-domain transfers are particularly beneficial; and larger datasets provide positive transfer effects to smaller datasets. The findings suggest that DTL not only facilitates improved model performance but also holds promise for continual learning, which could advance the pursuit of Artificial General Intelligence. Overall, the paper emphasizes the growing importance of DTL in addressing the limitations of traditional DL methodologies.
