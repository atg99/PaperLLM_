This paper provides a comprehensive review of deep transfer learning (DTL), exploring various methods and their limitations. It identifies two primary constraints in DTL approaches: catastrophic forgetting and overly biased pre-trained models. Catastrophic forgetting occurs during fine-tuning when significant weight changes may erase previously learned knowledge, hindering continual learning. To mitigate this, techniques such as freezing earlier layers of a pre-trained model are employed. However, this can lead to an overly biased model, as it restricts the learning of new features from the target data.

The paper discusses the technique of Progressive Learning, which introduces new layers to a frozen pre-trained model, allowing for some adaptation to new tasks while still facing challenges with distant source-target data. A proposed solution to both catastrophic forgetting and bias involves vertically expanding the model by adding new nodes to the frozen layers, enhancing the learning capacity while preserving existing knowledge.

The review also categorizes and analyzes over thirty recent studies in DTL, highlighting the efficacy of methods such as fine-tuning, freezing CNN layers, and progressive learning for various machine learning problems. The findings from two experimental studies provide insights into effective DTL approaches and underscore the importance of addressing the limitations associated with current DTL techniques. Overall, the paper emphasizes the potential for successful transfer learning and deep continual learning through innovative model expansion strategies.
