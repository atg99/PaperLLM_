The paper discusses the categorization of Deep Transfer Learning (DTL) and its relationship to traditional Transfer Learning (TL). DTL can be classified into homogeneous and heterogeneous categories based on the similarity of source and target data. The categorization is subjective and can vary depending on the context. For instance, X-ray images may be considered heterogeneous compared to tree species photos in a specific domain, but homogeneous in a broader context that includes diverse data types like audio and text.

Additionally, DTL is categorized based on label availability into transductive, inductive, and unsupervised learning. Transductive learning uses labeled source data only, inductive learning utilizes labeled source and target data, while unsupervised learning has no labeled data.

The paper also outlines four applied approaches to DTL: instance-based, feature-based, parameter-based, and relational-based/adversarial-based. Instance-based approaches rely on selected instances from source data with various weighting strategies for the target data. Feature-based methods map data from both domains into a more homogeneous space, further divided into asymmetric and symmetric subcategories. Parameter-based methods involve utilizing knowledge from pre-trained models, adjusting layers as necessary. Lastly, relational/adversarial-based approaches focus on extracting transferable features through learned relationships or generative adversarial networks.

The paper emphasizes that while model-based and adversarial-based techniques have been less explored, the other categories have been extensively studied and remain relevant to DTL. Figure 1 in the paper illustrates the taxonomy of these categories.
