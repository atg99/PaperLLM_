Deep Transfer Learning (DTL) primarily utilizes model-based approaches to address domain adaptation between source and target data. These techniques often involve combinations of pre-training, freezing, fine-tuning, and the addition of new layers. A pre-trained model, trained on source data, serves as the foundation for these processes. Freezing layers means that parameters remain unchanged, while fine-tuning initializes some parameters with pre-trained values, enhancing training on target data.

A notable technique in DTL is Progressive Learning or Progressive Neural Networks (PNNs), which was introduced by Google DeepMind in 2016. This technique emulates human skill acquisition by building new skills on previously learned ones, thus avoiding catastrophic forgetting. In DTL, the earlier layers of a deep learning model typically perform feature extraction, while the latter layers focus on classification or prediction.

The paper identifies the most effective DTL approach as freezing earlier and middle layers from a related pre-trained model while fine-tuning the lateral layers for new tasks. Additionally, recent advancements in DTL have been systematically reviewed, focusing on publications from the past five years. The review categorized the studies into three main model-based approaches: fine-tuning, freezing CNN layers, and progressive learning, along with a category for adversarial-based approaches.

The systematic literature review (SLR) method was employed to select thirty-eight relevant publications based on specific inclusion criteria: publication within the last five years, reproducibility, practical application in machine learning problems, and generalizability. The findings indicate a predominance of model-based strategies in DTL, with limited exploration of adversarial methods.
