The paper discusses the role of games as effective testbeds for AI algorithms, tracing their significance back to early pioneers like Alan Turing and John von Neumann. Games allow for the creation of perfect simulators and the generation of unlimited data, leading to notable advancements in AI capabilities. Several AI systems have demonstrated human-level or superhuman performance across various games, including Checkers (Chinook), Chess (Deep Blue, AlphaZero), Go (AlphaGo), and multiplayer games like Dota 2 (OpenAI Five) and StarCraft II.

The paper categorizes games into three sections: board games, card games, and video games, corresponding to different types of information and player interactions. Board games, characterized by perfect information and two-player scenarios, have long served as benchmarks for reinforcement learning and AI methodologies. Notable techniques employed include heuristic search, deep reinforcement learning, Monte Carlo tree search, and counterfactual regret minimization.

The discussion highlights the advancements in AI through various approaches, such as neural networks combined with TD learning in Backgammon, and the evolution of techniques used in more complex multi-player games. The paper acknowledges that while the classification covers significant areas, it does not encompass all game types, like single-agent puzzles such as the Rubik's Cube. It also references additional literature for further exploration of AI in games, including storytelling and specialized competitions within the gaming domain.
