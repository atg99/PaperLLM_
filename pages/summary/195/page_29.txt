The paper discusses two significant advancements in reinforcement learning: Universal Value Function Approximators (UVFAs) and Hindsight Experience Replay (HER). 

Schaul et al. (2015) introduce UVFAs, which generalize value function approximators by considering both states and goals. This approach leverages the structural similarities in goal representation and the resulting value function, enabling training through direct bootstrapping with a variant of Q-learning. The resulting greedy policy can effectively generalize to new action-goal pairs, presenting a scalable solution unlike previous methods.

Andrychowicz et al. (2017) build on the concepts of UVFAs to address the sparse reward problem through HER. This technique involves storing all transitions during episodes, not just for the original goal but also for alternative goals. By replaying trajectories with different goals using off-policy reinforcement learning algorithms, HER mitigates the impact of the original goal on environment dynamics, improving learning efficiency. The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of HER when combined with DQN and DDPG in various robotic tasks, such as pushing, sliding, and pick-and-place operations. 

Overall, both UVFAs and HER represent significant strides in reinforcement learning, enhancing the ability to learn from diverse experiences and improving performance in complex tasks.
