The section discusses two key concepts in the realm of visual control and interactive perception within the fields of computer vision and reinforcement learning. 

Visual control focuses on creating policies derived from visual inputs, as seen in various applications such as gaming, robotics, and self-driving vehicles. It emphasizes the necessity of integrating computer vision techniques rather than solely relying on deep learning frameworks like CNNs. Notable examples include DQN and AlphaGo, which illustrate the importance of tailored approaches in visual control tasks.

Interactive perception is highlighted as a critical component for enhancing visual understanding. The work by Jayaraman and Grauman introduces a deep reinforcement learning method that employs recurrent neural networks for active visual completion. Their approach seeks to optimize decision-making regarding what to observe in order to gain additional information without the reliance on labeled data. The method rewards decisions based on the accuracy of predictions for unobserved views and is illustrated through tasks involving panoramic natural scenes and 3D object shapes. The authors also address the potential for generalization and transferability of their approach to diverse tasks and environments.
