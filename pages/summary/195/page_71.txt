Barreto et al. (2017) introduce a transfer framework aimed at tasks with varying reward functions but identical environment dynamics. This framework is built on two foundational concepts: successor features and generalized policy improvement. Successor features serve as a value function representation that separates environment dynamics from rewards, adapting the successor representation for continuous tasks with function approximation. Generalized policy improvement allows for the operation on multiple policies simultaneously, as opposed to the singular policy focus in traditional dynamic programming. The authors present two theorems that guarantee performance for tasks before and after exposure to similar tasks, demonstrating their method's effectiveness in navigation tasks and robotic arm control.

Gupta et al. (2017a) address the multi-skill problem, enabling two agents to learn various skills through a common representation that maps states and skill execution. Their algorithm maximizes the transfer of informative features to acquire new skills, utilizing a similarity loss metric, autoencoder, and reinforcement learning, with validation conducted on simulated robotic manipulation tasks.

Kornblith et al. (2018) explore transfer learning in computer vision, assessing the transferability of ImageNet architectures and features across 13 classification models for 12 tasks. Their findings indicate that while ImageNet architectures generalize well across datasets, fixed features do not transfer effectively.

Recent research in transfer and multi-task learning includes works by various authors, highlighting ongoing developments in the field. Additionally, Li and Malik (2017) propose automating unconstrained continuous optimization algorithms using reinforcement learning, formulating the optimization problem in terms of state, action, and policy. Their approach aligns the learning of the optimization algorithm with the reinforcement learning process.

Xu et al. (2018) investigate the optimal formulation of return in reinforcement learning, proposing a gradient-based meta-learning algorithm that adapts meta-parameters online during interaction with the environment. Their experiments, particularly in Atari games, yield promising results, indicating applicability to other components of the return function and hyperparameter tuning.
