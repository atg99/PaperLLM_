He et al. (2016a) introduce a dual learning mechanism to address the data scarcity in machine translation. This method leverages the feedback loop between translations in both directions—A to B and B to A—enhancing translation models through a policy gradient approach that uses language model likelihood as a reward signal. Experiments demonstrate that with only 10% bilingual data for initialization and additional monolingual data, the dual learning method performs comparably to traditional neural machine translation techniques utilizing full bilingual data in English to French tasks. The mechanism's applicability extends to other dual-task scenarios such as speech recognition versus text-to-speech and question answering versus question generation.

In the context of dialogue systems, which include conversational agents and chatbots, Jurafsky and Martin (2017) classify these systems into task-oriented and chatbots. Task-oriented agents focus on completing specific tasks through brief interactions, while chatbots engage in extended conversations for entertainment. The evolution of dialogue systems has progressed from rule-based approaches to data-driven methods enhanced by deep learning, where reinforcement learning is crucial. Key components of a dialogue system typically include language understanding, dialogue management, and natural language generation.

Dhingra et al. (2017) present KB-InfoBot, an end-to-end goal-oriented dialogue system that utilizes reinforcement learning to improve multi-turn information access. This system innovatively integrates differentiable operations for knowledge base querying, overcoming limitations of prior symbolic methods that hindered end-to-end training. They adapt the episodic REINFORCE algorithm to optimize dialogue acts and knowledge retrieval.

Su et al. (2016) propose an online learning framework that concurrently trains dialogue policy and reward models via active learning, addressing challenges associated with unreliable user feedback. Their empirical findings indicate that this approach significantly reduces the need for manual data annotation and minimizes the impact of noisy feedback in dialogue policy learning.

Li et al. (2016) advocate for the use of deep reinforcement learning to generate dialogues that optimize future rewards, focusing on informativeness, coherence, and ease of response, thus tackling common issues in sequence generation.
