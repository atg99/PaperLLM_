The paper discusses experiments demonstrating that generalizable skills in tasks with random maps stem from a rich representation of spatial environments, facilitated by a temporal hierarchy and an explicit memory module. The results indicate that the agents achieve human-level performance and exhibit greater collaboration compared to human participants, all while being trained on fewer than 2000 commodity computers.

The authors acknowledge several limitations in their work, including challenges in maintaining diversity within agent populations, the greedy nature of the meta-optimization via Population Based Training (PBT), and the variance introduced by temporal credit assignment in the reinforcement learning (RL) updates.

Additionally, the paper references notable advancements in AI, such as Sun et al. and Pang et al. achieving success against built-in AI in StarCraft II, and OpenAI Five's development of a Dota 2 agent using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) and self-play, which surpassed human players despite requiring substantial computational resources of 256 GPUs and 128,000 CPU cores.
