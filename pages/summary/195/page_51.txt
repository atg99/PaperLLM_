The paper discusses the integration of relational reinforcement learning (RL) with statistical relational learning and classical AI for knowledge representation and reasoning. It highlights the historical development of relational RL, including foundational work by Dzeroski et al. in 2001, surveys by Tadepalli et al. in 2004, and advancements such as relational MDPs by Guestrin et al. in 2003 and object-oriented MDPs by Diuk et al. in 2008. Recent contributions include graph networks by Battaglia et al. in 2018 and various deep learning approaches addressing relational reasoning.

The paper also addresses the concept of causality as articulated by Pearl in 2018, who identifies three key challenges for machine learning systems: adaptability, explainability, and understanding causal relationships. Pearl's three-layer causal hierarchy includes association, intervention, and counterfactual reasoning, with the latter subsuming the former two. He proposes a structural causal model to facilitate automated reasoning tasks, including causal discovery and mediation analysis. Recent studies employing deep learning to explore causality are also noted.

In the reasoning domain, Battaglia et al.'s graph network is presented as an approach to achieve combinatorial generalization, alongside other models such as relation networks and relational memory cores. The paper underscores the importance of relational reasoning and its applications.

Finally, Lake et al. in 2016 advocate for the development of machines that emulate human-like learning and thinking. They emphasize the necessity of constructing causal world models, understanding entities, and leveraging intuitive physics and psychology. Key components for achieving this include developmental cognitive capabilities, rapid model building, and efficient inference methods. The paper suggests that these elements are crucial for advancing AI towards human-level intelligence.
