Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning (HRL) is a framework that enables learning, planning, and knowledge representation with temporal abstraction across multiple levels. It addresses challenges such as sparse rewards and long horizons by exploring high-level goals. Key concepts in HRL include sub-goals, options, skills, and macro-actions, all contributing to its modular structure which facilitates transfer and multi-task learning.

Options, a core component of HRL, are defined within a Markov Decision Process (MDP) as a set of states, a guiding policy, and a termination condition. This framework allows for the formation of semi-Markov decision processes (SMDPs), where learning and planning algorithms from MDPs can be adapted for options. Options are typically learned with predefined sub-goals and pseudo-rewards, showing effective performance in various applications like Atari games and Minecraft.

Recent advancements in automatic options discovery have gained attention, with approaches such as the Strategic Attentive Writer (STRAW) and Feudal Networks (FuN) leading the way. The overarching principle of HRL is divide and conquer, simplifying complex goals into manageable sub-goals, which can be tackled using traditional reinforcement learning methods.

The hierarchical DQN (h-DQN) model proposed by Kulkarni et al. organizes intrinsically motivated deep RL modules hierarchically, operating at different time scales. It consists of a meta-controller that selects goals and a controller that executes actions to achieve those goals. The system maximizes both extrinsic and intrinsic rewards, demonstrating its effectiveness in discrete environments.

Overall, HRL represents a significant advancement in reinforcement learning, allowing for more structured and efficient approaches to complex decision-making tasks.
