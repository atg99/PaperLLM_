The section explores various applications of reinforcement learning (RL) across different fields, including chemistry, mathematics, and music, highlighting innovative approaches and methodologies.

In chemistry, Segler et al. (2018) introduce an advanced retrosynthesis technique that combines Monte Carlo tree search with symbolic rules and utilizes three deep neural networks to enhance computer-aided synthesis planning. Their approach significantly outperforms previous systems and draws inspiration from the AlphaGo methodology. Additionally, Popova et al. (2018) utilize deep RL for de novo drug design, focusing on generating molecules with specific desired properties.

In mathematics, the discussion centers on the use of deep learning to tackle partial differential equations (PDEs). Pan et al. (2018) propose a data-driven RL controller for high-dimensional systems governed by PDEs, emphasizing the importance of spatial relationships in action dimensions. Their work demonstrates superior sample efficiency compared to traditional RL methods.

In the domain of music, Jaques et al. (2017) present Sequence Tutor, which integrates maximum likelihood estimation with RL to enhance the quality and structure of generated musical sequences. This system uses a Reward RNN to pre-train and subsequently employs off-policy RL to generate sequences that meet domain-specific rewards while maintaining diversity. The challenge of designing a reward function that captures the aesthetic qualities of music is noted, underscoring the limitations of purely data-driven approaches.

Overall, the section illustrates the versatility of RL in addressing complex problems across diverse scientific and artistic disciplines, paving the way for future research and applications.
