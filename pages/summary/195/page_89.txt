The paper discusses the limitations of traditional sequence models in dialogue systems, particularly highlighting issues of myopia and repetitive responses when maximizing the probability of generating a response based on the previous dialogue turn. To address these challenges, the authors propose a reward function that encapsulates desirable properties for dialogue generation and employ policy gradient methods to optimize long-term rewards. They suggest exploring alternative reward modeling approaches, including those from Su et al. (2016) and techniques such as inverse reinforcement learning and imitation learning, while acknowledging the potential costs and suboptimal human behavior in these methods.

Additionally, the paper reviews various advancements in dialogue system research. Tang et al. (2018a) introduce subtask discovery for hierarchical dialogue policy learning via dynamic programming, expanding on prior work by Peng et al. (2017a). Williams et al. (2017) enhance data efficiency in dialog training by integrating RNNs with domain knowledge. Lewis et al. (2017) focus on end-to-end learning for negotiation dialogues, while Zhang et al. (2016b) explore end-to-end speech recognition using CNNs. Xiong et al. (2017) present Microsoft's conversational speech recognition system from 2017, and Zhou et al. (2018) propose an emotional chatting machine. Li et al. (2017) develop an end-to-end task-completion neural dialogue system utilizing both supervised and reinforcement learning. The paper also references a survey by Serban et al. (2018) on corpora for building dialogue systems and lists several recent studies that contribute further to this field.
