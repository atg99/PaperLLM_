The paper reviews various advancements in object localization and tracking using deep reinforcement learning (RL) and attention mechanisms. Mnih et al. (2014) introduce the recurrent attention model, while Caicedo and Lazebnik (2015) present an active detection model utilizing DQN for precise object localization. Jie et al. (2016) enhance object search through a tree-structured RL approach that maximizes localization accuracy. Other notable contributions include Mathe et al. (2016) focusing on policy search for visual object detection, and Kong et al. (2017) employing collaborative multi-agent RL for joint object search.

In motion analysis, Supančić and Ramanan (2017) formulate tracking as a partially observable decision-making process and utilize deep RL for policy learning. Rhinehart and Kitani (2017) introduce DARKO for modeling long-term goals in first-person camera scenarios through inverse RL.

For scene understanding, Wu et al. (2017b) propose a semi-supervised method for encoding scene information using a deterministic rendering function. They replace traditional decoder architectures to handle scene information more effectively. Wu et al. (2017a) suggest a paradigm combining convolutional perception, physics, and graphics engines to understand physical scenes without human annotations.

Integration with natural language processing (NLP) is also explored, with Xu et al. (2015) integrating attention mechanisms into image captioning. Other works focus on video captioning and goal-driven dialogue systems, utilizing deep RL for optimization. The paper highlights the interdisciplinary approach in merging computer vision with NLP to enhance visual understanding and interaction capabilities.
