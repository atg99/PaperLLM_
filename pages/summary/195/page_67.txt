The paper discusses advancements in relational reinforcement learning (RL) as proposed by Zambaldi et al. in 2018. They aim to enhance sample efficiency, generalization, and interpretability in deep RL by integrating a network architecture with fully connected layers, convolutional neural networks, and a relational module that employs self-attention for reasoning about entity interactions. Their experimental results in the BOX-World navigation task and various mini-games in the StarCraft II Learning Environment demonstrate effective relational reasoning capabilities.

Wang et al. (2018b) introduce NerveNet, which models the neural nervous system as a graph to learn structured policies for continuous control, using graph neural networks for information propagation and action prediction. Their evaluations in the OpenAI Gym environment highlight its effectiveness in transfer and multi-task learning.

Keramati et al. (2018) present strategic object-oriented RL (SOORL), focusing on model learning and strategic exploration, achieving positive rewards in the challenging Atari game Pitfall!. However, Aytar et al. (2018) report superior results in the same game using a self-supervision and RL approach.

Yang et al. (2018) propose planning-execution-observation-RL (PEORL), which combines hierarchical RL with symbolic planning for dynamic environments. The authors emphasize that while Zambaldi et al. use pixel feature vectors as entities, Keramati et al. rely on bounding boxes for object detection, and Yang et al. utilize manually crafted symbolic knowledge. They suggest that further performance enhancements could be achieved through advanced reasoning techniques for entity and relation identification in images.
