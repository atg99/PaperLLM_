Al-Shedivat et al. (2018) introduce a meta-learning framework to address continuous adaptation in non-stationary and competitive environments by modeling these environments as sequences of stationary tasks. They develop a gradient-based meta-learning algorithm inspired by MAML, which anticipates environmental changes to update policies. This approach manages Markovian dynamics at two hierarchical levels: the upper level focuses on task dynamics while the lower level deals with MDPs for specific tasks. The authors test their method on a multi-leg robot locomotion task in a MuJoCo simulator, where non-stationarity is induced by selectively paralyzing robot legs, and on iterated adaptation games in a 3D RoboSumo environment featuring adversarial competition. They suggest that task trajectories can inform subsequent tasks, creating dependencies, but acknowledge potential limitations in utilizing additional data and challenges from significant distributional shifts.

The paper also reviews various one-shot and zero-shot learning models, including Lake et al. (2015) for handwritten character recognition, Koch et al. (2015) with siamese networks for image recognition, and Vinyals et al. (2016) with matching networks. Other notable contributions include Duan et al. (2017) for imitation learning in robotics and Johnson et al. (2017) for zero-shot translation in multilingual systems.

In the realm of transfer and multi-task learning, the authors summarize definitions and methodologies from Taylor and Stone (2009) and Pan and Yang (2010), highlighting that transfer learning encompasses inductive, transductive, and unsupervised forms. They emphasize the role of multi-task learning in improving generalization by leveraging shared representations across related tasks. Whye Teh et al. (2017) propose Distral, a method for joint training of multiple tasks through a distilled policy that captures the commonalities among task behaviors. This design addresses challenges in transfer learning, such as conflicting gradients and task dominance, and demonstrates empirical advantages over baseline algorithms like A3C in various environments.
