This paper explores the potential of using StarCraft and Texas Hold’em Poker as testbeds for multi-agent systems, highlighting the importance of extending DeepStack to accommodate multi-player dynamics in various gaming formats. StarCraft, with its intricate gameplay involving numerous actions and strategies, offers a rich environment for developing human-level AI agents. The authors suggest leveraging video commentary to extract strategies, which could enhance agent design through a combination of reinforcement learning, computer vision, and natural language processing.

Citing previous works, the paper notes the success of self-supervision techniques in learning from YouTube videos and emphasizes the relevance of hierarchical reinforcement learning approaches that have shown promise in achieving super-human performance in strategy games. The discussion shifts to end-to-end learning paradigms, referencing notable models such as AlexNet and AlphaGo Zero, which utilize raw inputs for various tasks. 

However, the authors raise concerns about sample efficiency and the substantial computational resources required for these approaches, exemplified by the performance disparity between Rainbow and distributional DQN. Additionally, the paper addresses the vulnerability of deep learning models to adversarial examples, which can lead to significant classification errors in critical applications.

The authors argue for the necessity of incorporating manual engineering and knowledge-based components into the end-to-end learning framework. They propose an “intelligence” component that integrates common knowledge, principles, and algorithms into the learning process. This hybrid approach is posited as a more effective strategy for achieving robust AI systems, particularly in practical applications where perfect simulations and unlimited data are not available. The paper concludes that a combination of symbolic reasoning and connectionist methods could pave the way for significant advancements in AI development.
