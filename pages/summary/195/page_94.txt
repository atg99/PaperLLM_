Heaton et al. (2016) present deep learning applications in finance, specifically focusing on deep portfolios. They argue that deep learning can outperform traditional financial methods, such as linear factor models and statistical arbitrage techniques. Their case study on smart indexing for the biotechnology IBB index employs a four-step algorithm: auto-encoding to create efficient input representations, calibrating to form portfolios that meet objectives, validating to balance errors, and verifying to ensure compliance with validation requirements. They note that univariate activation functions like ReLU can be viewed as combinations of financial call and put options.

Bao et al. (2017) explore stock price forecasting by integrating wavelet transforms, stacked auto-encoders, and long-short term memory networks. Wavelet transforms help in noise reduction of stock price time series, while stacked auto-encoders generate high-level features for predictions. LSTM is used for forecasting based on these features. Their evaluation across six market indices shows promising predictive accuracy and profitability through metrics such as Mean Absolute Percentage Error, correlation coefficient, and Theil's inequality coefficient.

In option pricing, American options pose challenges due to their early exercise feature, unlike European options, which can be priced using the Black-Scholes formula. The pricing of American options involves calculating the conditional expected value of continuation, a problem addressed by Longstaff and Schwartz (2001) using the least squares Monte Carlo method.

Portfolio optimization traditionally relies on Markowitz's mean-variance analysis, with recent interest in dynamic multi-period optimization influenced by empirical evidence of return predictability. Reinforcement learning methods have been proposed for portfolio management, exemplified by Moody and Saffell (2001) and extended by Deng et al. (2016) using deep neural networks. Risk management remains crucial in portfolio formation, with Value-at-Risk and conditional Value-at-Risk being prominent measures, the latter offering advantageous mathematical properties. Deep reinforcement learning methods may enhance solutions in risk management, particularly in dynamic portfolio optimization contexts.
