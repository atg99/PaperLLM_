The paper discusses the concept of models in reinforcement learning (RL), defining a model as an agent's representation of its environment, which includes a state transition model and a reward model, typically assumed to be known. It contrasts model-free RL approaches, which can handle unknown dynamical systems but require extensive sampling, with model-based RL approaches that learn value functions or policies in a more data-efficient manner but may struggle with model identification and accuracy.

The integration of model-free RL with online planning is highlighted as a way to enhance value function estimation, referencing Sutton's Dyna framework that combines real experiences with simulated trajectories. Key planning techniques such as Monte Carlo tree search (MCTS) and upper confidence bounds (UCB) are also discussed, with MCTS being exemplified through AlphaGo.

The paper outlines various advanced deep learning architectures designed for RL, including value iteration networks and imagination-augmented agents, emphasizing the importance of learning strategies. It also references works that ensure efficiency in tabular cases and presents frameworks and methodologies for probabilistic learning and model predictive control.

Sutton's views on planning with learned models emphasize the necessity of knowledge and flexibility in achieving goals, as well as the importance of predictive learning and causal models. Geffner's discussion further distinguishes between model-free learners and model-based solvers, positioning planners as specialized solvers for goal-directed models.

Overall, the paper provides an overview of the interplay between model-based and model-free approaches in RL, emphasizing the significance of planning, knowledge acquisition, and the development of sophisticated learning architectures.
