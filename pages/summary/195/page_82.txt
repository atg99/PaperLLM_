Robotics is a prominent application area for reinforcement learning (RL), with diverse uses in sectors such as manufacturing, supply chain, and healthcare. However, integrating RL into robotics presents several challenges, including high dimensionality, inadequate modeling of system dynamics, model uncertainty, and complexities in specifying rewards and goals. RL offers various strategies to address these challenges, such as state-action discretization, value function approximation, and structured policies. It also leverages prior knowledge through demonstrations and structured tasks, as well as through mental rehearsal to mitigate simulation biases and enhance optimization efficiency.

The paper references several key studies and surveys related to RL and robotics, including works on autonomous helicopter control, glider soaring, and navigation methods, highlighting the importance of collaboration between robots and humans in production environments. It also introduces the concept of sim-to-real, emphasizing the difficulties of transferring learning from simulated environments to real-world applications. This transfer learning process is critical, as simulations often fail to accurately reflect real-world scenarios.

To bridge the simulation-reality gap, various methods have been proposed, such as dynamics randomization for training recurrent policies and using progressive networks to support transfer learning and domain adaptation. These approaches aim to ensure that policies developed in simulation can be effectively implemented in real robots, thereby enhancing their performance in practical tasks. The paper also mentions specific successful applications, including dexterity learning for in-hand manipulation and the use of progressive networks for tasks involving real-world robots.
