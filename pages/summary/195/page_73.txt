Vinyals et al. (2015) introduce pointer networks, a novel approach for learning conditional probabilities of output sequences by utilizing attention mechanisms to select elements from input sequences. They evaluate the efficacy of pointer networks on three combinatorial optimization problems: planar convex hulls, Delaunay triangulations, and the planar Traveling Salesman Problem, highlighting their graph-agnostic nature compared to previous methods like those by Dai et al. (2017).

The paper further discusses AutoML, which focuses on automating machine learning processes. It mentions various initiatives and competitions in the field, such as the NIPS 2018 AutoML for Lifelong Machine Learning Competition. A significant aspect of AutoML is neural network architecture design, an area addressed by Zoph and Le (2017) through neural architecture search using reinforcement learning (RL). Their method generates neural architectures from scratch, optimizing for validation accuracy, and demonstrates competitive performance on the CIFAR-10 and Penn Treebank datasets.

Zoph et al. (2017) explore transferring learned architectures from small to large datasets, while Baker et al. (2017) employ a meta-learning approach utilizing Q-learning for CNN architecture generation. Other works, including those by Jin et al. (2018) and Cai et al. (2018), investigate Bayesian methods and resource-efficient approaches for architecture search.

Recent advancements in neural architectures, such as the attention-based model by Vaswani et al. (2017) and the MultiModel by Kaiser et al. (2017a), showcase manual design efforts aimed at enhancing performance across various tasks. These developments reflect the growing industrial impact of neural architecture design, with companies like Google actively pursuing AutoML initiatives to improve applications in vision, NLP, and more.
