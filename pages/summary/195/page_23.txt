The paper provides an overview of the core elements of reinforcement learning (RL), highlighting the roles of the value function, policy, and model. It categorizes RL problems into prediction, control, or planning, and distinguishes between model-free and model-based approaches, as well as value-based and policy-based methods. A central theme is the exploration vs. exploitation tradeoff, which is crucial in RL scenarios.

The paper details various RL methodologies, including value-based methods such as TD-learning, Q-learning, SARSA, and Deep Q-Networks (DQN). It also covers direct policy search methods, including policy gradient techniques like REINFORCE and trust region methods such as Trust Region Policy Optimization (TRPO) and Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). Additionally, it discusses actor-critic methods that integrate both value function and policy, and maximum entropy methods that connect value- and policy-based approaches, including soft Q-learning and Path Consistency Learning (PCL).

Dynamic programming techniques, such as policy iteration and value iteration, are also examined, with references to notable applications like AlphaGo and DeepStack. The subsequent chapters of the paper are structured to explore these core elements in detail, focusing on the value function, policy, reward system, model-based RL, exploration vs. exploitation, and representation.
