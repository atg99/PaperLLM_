The value function is a core concept in reinforcement learning, representing the expected cumulative discounted future rewards associated with states or state-action pairs. Classical algorithms like Temporal Difference (TD) learning and Q-learning are used to learn these value functions, which are essential for deriving optimal policies. A significant advancement in this field is the introduction of Deep Q-Networks (DQN), which addresses stability and convergence issues in Q-learning by incorporating experience replay and target networks, making the learning process more akin to supervised learning. 

DQN utilizes convolutional neural networks (CNNs) to approximate action value functions and has demonstrated exceptional performance across various tasks, particularly in playing Atari games. It effectively mitigates instability—often referred to as the "deadly triad"—which arises from the combination of off-policy learning, function approximation, and bootstrapping. The instability is primarily due to correlations in sequential observations and the dramatic impact small updates can have on the policy and data distribution.

To combat these issues, DQN employs experience replay, where sequences of observations are stored and sampled randomly to reduce correlations, and target networks, which maintain separate parameters updated periodically to minimize correlations between action values and target values. The loss function used for updating the network parameters is based on the difference between estimated and target action values.

Additionally, recent research is exploring policy-based methods and their integration with off-policy Q-learning approaches. This evolution in reinforcement learning emphasizes the importance of general value functions, which consider goals as parameters, highlighting the potential for further advancements in AI.
