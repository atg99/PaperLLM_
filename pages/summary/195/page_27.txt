Hessel et al. (2018) introduce Rainbow, a reinforcement learning framework that integrates several advancements, including DQN, double Q-learning, prioritized replay, dueling networks, multi-step learning, value distribution, and noisy nets. This combination enhances data efficiency and performance in Atari games. An ablation study indicates that omitting either prioritization or multi-step learning leads to decreased performance, though the impact of each component varies across different games.

Munos et al. (2016) present Retrace, an off-policy control algorithm designed for safe and efficient return-based learning. This method minimizes variance and allows for the effective use of samples from any behavior policy, analyzing convergence to the optimal action value without relying on the Greedy in the Limit with Infinite Exploration assumption. Retrace is further extended for actor-critic methods by Gruslys et al. (2017).

In the context of value-based reinforcement learning, Bellemare et al. (2017) challenge the reliance on expected values by proposing a value distribution approach. This method captures the distribution of returns, contrasting with traditional expected value methods. They establish the distributional Bellman equation, which incorporates the randomness of returns and demonstrates the contraction of the policy evaluation Bellman operator using the Wasserstein metric. The authors argue that approximating value distributions offers advantages over expected values, such as preserving multimodality and reducing the impact of nonstationary policies on learning. They empirically validate their approach with experiments on Atari games.

Bellemare et al. (2017) also introduce a categorical algorithm for approximate distributional reinforcement learning, utilizing discrete distributions characterized by a set of atoms. They address challenges related to disjoint supports in the Bellman update by projecting sample updates onto the support of the value distribution.
