The paper discusses the integration of reinforcement learning with function approximation, particularly in the context of nonstationarity, bootstrapping, and delayed targets as highlighted by Sutton and Barto in 2018. It emphasizes the significance of linear function approximation due to its theoretical advantages prior to advancements in deep Q-networks. The pseudo code for TD(0) with function approximation is presented, showcasing the update mechanism for the approximate value function.

A key challenge addressed is the "deadly triad" issue, which arises from the combination of off-policy learning, function approximation, and bootstrapping, leading to instability and divergence in learning algorithms. The paper notes that instability does not stem from learning or sampling alone, as dynamic programming can also experience divergence with function approximation. It points out that the root cause of this instability remains unclear, with many unresolved issues in off-policy learning.

The paper further details various algorithms designed to tackle these challenges, including approximate dynamic programming algorithms, least square temporal difference (LSTD), fitted-Q algorithms, residual gradient algorithms, and others. Each algorithm has its own strengths, such as data efficiency, convergence guarantees, and computational efficiency. Empirical comparisons and studies on sample efficiency are also referenced, contributing to a comprehensive understanding of the current state of research in reinforcement learning with function approximation.
