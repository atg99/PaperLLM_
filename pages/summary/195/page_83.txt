The paper discusses various approaches to robotic perception and control, emphasizing imitation learning and value-based learning. Sadeghi et al. (2018) introduce a convolutional recurrent neural network that enables a robot arm to servo towards objects, leveraging memory of past movements to inform actions. This approach utilizes reinforcement learning (RL) to consider the long-term effects of actions, while also adapting visual layers with a limited set of realistic images for improved performance.

Finn et al. (2016b) explore inverse reinforcement learning, proposing the use of nonlinear cost functions via neural networks to structure costs effectively. Duan et al. (2017) present one-shot imitation learning, where a neural network is trained on pairs of demonstrations to predict actions for sampled states, enhancing generalization to new tasks through soft attention mechanisms.

Further developments in one-shot meta-imitation learning are highlighted in the works of Finn et al. (2017c) and Yu et al. (2018), which utilize model-agnostic meta-learning (MAML) to adapt policies based on limited demonstrations from different sources. These methods show promise in bridging the gap between teleoperated and human demonstrations, allowing for rapid adaptation to new tasks.

The paper also addresses value-based learning techniques, focusing on the successor representation (SR) framework. Zhang et al. (2017) apply deep reinforcement learning with successor features to facilitate knowledge transfer in robot navigation tasks, demonstrating effectiveness in both simulated and real-world environments. The integration of these various methodologies highlights the ongoing advancements in robotic learning and control.
