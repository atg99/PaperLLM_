The manuscript discusses the evolution and significance of deep learning and reinforcement learning in the context of artificial intelligence. It highlights the shift from manual feature engineering to deep distributed representations that address the challenges of the curse of dimensionality. Despite the advantages of deep learning, such as its generality and flexibility, it faces limitations, including a lack of interpretability, difficulties in hyperparameter tuning, and challenges in reproducibility, particularly in deep reinforcement learning.

The text references David Silver's conjecture that artificial intelligence can be represented as a combination of reinforcement learning and deep learning, suggesting a path toward achieving artificial general intelligence. The manuscript outlines key questions related to deep reinforcement learning, including its current state, issues, and potential solutions, which will be explored in subsequent chapters.

The structure of the book is detailed, beginning with foundational concepts in artificial intelligence and progressing through core elements and mechanisms of reinforcement learning, followed by various applications across diverse fields such as robotics, natural language processing, and healthcare. The manuscript aims to serve as a resource for novices and experts alike, providing a high-level conceptual approach while acknowledging the complexity of deep reinforcement learning topics.

The authors intend to keep the content relevant and up-to-date, supported by supplementary blogs to track advancements in the field. The manuscript is designed to complement existing literature, particularly Sutton and Barto's classic work on reinforcement learning, and emphasizes the importance of original papers for deeper understanding.
