The paper discusses the limitations and applications of AlphaGo Zero, emphasizing its reliance on large datasets generated through self-play, which poses challenges in fields like healthcare and robotics where data collection is difficult. While AlphaGo's algorithms, including deep learning, reinforcement learning, and Monte Carlo tree search, have shown promise in various applications such as game-playing, scheduling, and constraint satisfaction, the authors note that these techniques cannot be directly transferred to scenarios lacking perfect models or sufficient data.

The paper also highlights advancements in poker AI, particularly DeepStack, which has successfully defeated professional players in Heads-up No-Limit Hold'em by utilizing counterfactual regret minimization to address information asymmetry. It suggests that further exploration is needed in multi-player settings, as current studies focus on single hands in poker.

Despite AlphaGo's significant achievements marking a milestone in AI, the pursuit of artificial general intelligence remains ongoing. The authors argue that the success of AlphaGo, especially AlphaGo Zero, represents a triumph for AI and its underlying techniques, which could influence both classical and emerging areas in artificial intelligence.
