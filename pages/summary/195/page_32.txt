The paper discusses the application of reinforcement learning algorithms, particularly focusing on the Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (DDPG) and the Asynchronous Advantage Actor-Critic (A3C) methods in solving simulated physics tasks within the MuJoCo environment. DDPG is highlighted for its efficiency, solving tasks with significantly fewer experience steps compared to Deep Q-Networks (DQN), while still requiring many training episodes typical of model-free reinforcement learning approaches. DDPG operates in an end-to-end manner, utilizing raw pixel inputs.

Hausknecht and Stone's extension of DDPG to parameterized action spaces is noted, particularly in the context of simulated RoboCup soccer. The paper then shifts to A3C, an actor-critic algorithm that learns both a policy and a state value function. A3C is presented as a superior asynchronous method that effectively handles both discrete and continuous action spaces.

The paper includes a pseudo code for the A3C algorithm, detailing its operation across actor-learner threads. A3C updates its policy and value function estimates after a set number of actions or upon reaching terminal states, employing n-step returns to enhance learning efficiency. The algorithm's structure allows for comprehensive updates, resulting in improved performance through reduced variance and accelerated learning via bootstrapping. Key equations illustrate the process of updating the value function and the policy gradient, emphasizing the algorithm's effectiveness in various reinforcement learning scenarios.
