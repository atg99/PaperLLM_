The paper discusses advancements in natural language processing (NLP) models, particularly focusing on the efficiency and capabilities of attention mechanisms and memory networks. It highlights that attention mechanisms, as introduced in the Transformer model by Dehghani et al. (2018), offer better time efficiency and parallelization compared to recurrent models. Tang et al. (2018b) further support this by examining how self-attention and CNNs can better capture long-range dependencies in text.

The paper also explores memory networks, starting with Weston et al. (2015), who developed an architecture that combines inference with long-term memory for question answering tasks. This framework allows dynamic reading from and writing to a memory base. Sukhbaatar et al. (2015) build on this by introducing a recurrent attention model over an external memory, enhancing the model's capabilities in question answering and language modeling.

Graves et al. (2016) introduce the Differentiable Neural Computer (DNC), which allows neural networks to read and write to external memory, solving complex structured problems that traditional networks struggle with. The DNC learns to manipulate data through end-to-end gradient descent, showing significant improvements in tasks such as question answering and reasoning.

Wayne et al. (2018) propose the MERLIN model, which addresses challenges of partial observability by utilizing extensive memory formatted for optimal information storage, evaluated in behavioral tasks within psychology and neurobiology. Overall, these advancements suggest a promising direction for enhancing NLP tasks through improved memory and attention mechanisms.
