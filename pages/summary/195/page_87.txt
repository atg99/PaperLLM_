The paper discusses advancements in dialogue systems and sequence generation, highlighting key tutorials and workshops in the field. Notable tutorials include Neubig (2017) on neural machine translation, Agarwal et al. (2018) on end-to-end goal-oriented question answering, and Monroe (2017) on translation. Workshops at NIPS cover conversational AI and machine learning applications in language processing.

Focusing on sequence generation, the paper emphasizes its applicability across domains such as text, music, and molecular structures, with a primary focus on text generation. Traditional models use n-grams or neural networks to predict the next word based on previous words, but they face challenges like exposure bias and discrepancies between training and evaluation metrics.

Ranzato et al. (2016) introduce MIXER, a sequence-level training algorithm that aligns training and testing objectives. Bahdanau et al. (2017) enhance this with an actor-critic algorithm, employing techniques to stabilize training and improve performance. Yu et al. (2017) present SeqGAN, integrating adversarial methods into sequence generation.

In machine translation, neural approaches have overtaken traditional methods. The sequence-to-sequence model encodes input sequences into fixed-length vectors for decoding. Bahdanau et al. (2015) further improve this with a soft-attention mechanism, addressing limitations of fixed-length encoding. This paper highlights the evolution and methodologies in dialogue systems and sequence generation, underscoring their significance in the NLP landscape.
