The paper discusses the dichotomy of human cognitive processes as described by Kahneman, distinguishing between System 1, which is intuitive and rapid, and System 2, which is analytical and deliberate. It explores the landscape of model-based reinforcement learning (RL), referencing various methodologies and integrating insights from recent literature.

Key methods in model-based RL are categorized into those with a reinforcement learning flavor, such as Dyna and VPN, those aligned with optimal control like GPS and NAF, and those that incorporate physics models as highlighted by Lake et al. The paper delves into policy gradient methods, distinguishing between likelihood ratio techniques and value gradient approaches, including the innovative stochastic value gradients (SVG) that blend model-free and model-based strategies. SVG uniquely learns models and policies simultaneously while mitigating the risks associated with inaccurate model predictions through direct interaction with the environment.

The paper highlights several novel contributions to the field. Oh et al.'s value prediction network (VPN) integrates both model-free and model-based approaches to predict future values and rewards, evaluated on tasks such as 2D navigation and Atari games. Temporal difference models (TDMs) proposed by Pong et al. enable efficient learning by combining off-policy learning with model-based control, and are tested on robotic tasks. Farquhar et al. introduce TreeQN, utilizing a recursive tree structure to enhance Q-function estimates, evaluated in box-pushing tasks and Atari games. Other notable methods include imagination-augmented agents (IA2) and normalized advantage functions (NAF), which extend Dyna to continuous tasks, as well as the TEXPLORE-VANAIR algorithm that incorporates intrinsic motivations.

Overall, the paper presents a comprehensive overview of contemporary advancements in model-based RL, emphasizing the integration of various methodologies to enhance learning efficiency and model accuracy in complex environments.
