AutoRL aims to automate various aspects of reinforcement learning, including state representation, function approximators, learning algorithms, and hyperparameters. This would parallel existing efforts in AutoML for machine learning tasks. The foundational significance of reinforcement learning is highlighted by its capacity to encapsulate the broader AI challenge, as noted by Russell and Norvig, and further justified by David Silver's conjecture linking artificial intelligence to reinforcement learning and deep learning.

The paper emphasizes the universal approximation capabilities of multilayer feedforward networks and the potential of deep learning combined with reinforcement learning to address computable tasks, which is central to AI objectives. Despite the successes of deep RL in domains like gaming, there remains a gap in practical applications that yield substantial commercial value. Hence, there is a call for developing impactful applications of deep RL, particularly leveraging techniques from AlphaGo.

The authors suggest investing in AlphaGo techniques, such as deep learning, reinforcement learning, Monte Carlo tree search, and self-play, for a variety of applications, including game playing, robotics, and online recommendation systems. While acknowledging the limitations of these techniques, they foresee an expanding role in practical applications.

In summary, the paper outlines six research directions that represent both challenges and opportunities for deep reinforcement learning, emphasizing the need for systematic studies, multi-agent problem-solving, learning from structured entities, optimal representation design, automation in reinforcement learning, and the development of commercially viable applications.
