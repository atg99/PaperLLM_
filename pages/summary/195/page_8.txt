This chapter provides an overview of key concepts in artificial intelligence (AI), machine learning, deep learning, and reinforcement learning. It notes that while a detailed introduction to AI and its subfields is beyond the scope of this chapter, it recommends several comprehensive resources for further reading, including survey papers and textbooks.

The relationship between these fields is illustrated, emphasizing that deep reinforcement learning sits at the intersection of deep learning and reinforcement learning. Machine learning is categorized into supervised, unsupervised, and reinforcement learning, with deep learning applicable across these categories. The evolution of these fields is highlighted, particularly their relevance to classical AI challenges such as logic and reasoning.

AI is defined through various perspectives, referencing Russell and Norvig's work. The first definition, "acting humanly," is associated with the Turing Test, which assesses a machine's ability to exhibit intelligent behavior indistinguishable from that of a human. Key capabilities required for passing the Turing Test include natural language processing, knowledge representation, automated reasoning, machine learning, computer vision, and robotics.

The second definition, "thinking humanly," relates to cognitive modeling and aims to create machines that can think like humans. The chapter indicates that while understanding the principles of intelligence is a primary focus for AI researchers, replicating human behavior is not always the goal of AI development. This section sets the groundwork for further exploration of AI's foundational concepts.
