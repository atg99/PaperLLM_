Unsupervised learning is essential for leveraging unlabelled data and is a key component in the pursuit of artificial general intelligence. It encompasses non-probabilistic models such as sparse coding, autoencoders, and k-means, as well as probabilistic models that focus on density functions. The latter can be further divided into tractable models like belief nets and neural autoregressive models, and non-tractable models such as Boltzmann machines and variational autoencoders. Implicit density functions are represented by generative adversarial networks (GANs) and moment matching networks.

Self-supervised learning, a subset of unsupervised learning, generates labels from the data itself. Techniques such as unsupervised auxiliary learning and GANs exemplify this approach. Notably, self-supervised learning strategies include curiosity-driven exploration and unsupervised video object segmentation, which aim to enhance learning efficiency and navigation capabilities without direct supervision.

The paper also highlights unsupervised auxiliary learning, specifically the UNREAL framework, which utilizes pseudo-reward functions to improve learning efficiency in environments where extrinsic rewards are sparse. UNREAL comprises a base agent, pixel control, reward prediction, and value function replay, all working together to maximize learning from available training signals. This approach illustrates how auxiliary tasks can facilitate the understanding of rewarding states, thereby enhancing the overall learning process in reinforcement learning contexts.
