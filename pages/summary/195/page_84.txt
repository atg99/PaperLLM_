Sherstan et al. (2018) introduce a method to speed up the construction of General Value Functions (GVFs) using successor representation within a continual learning framework, allowing new GVFs to be learned more rapidly by leveraging previously acquired knowledge.

In the realm of policy-based learning, Levine et al. (2016) propose guided policy search (GPS), which combines perception and control systems in an end-to-end manner. This approach maps raw image data directly to motor torques, addressing data efficiency by transforming policy search into a supervised learning task. GPS alternates between trajectory-centric reinforcement learning (RL) and supervised learning, leading to improved long-horizon performance and effective visuomotor policy training for complex manipulation tasks. Yahya et al. (2017) extend this concept to a multi-robot system for a vision-based door opening task. Zhu et al. (2017b) focus on target-driven visual navigation in indoor environments, utilizing an actor-critic model and a simulation platform called AI2-THOR to generate extensive training data.

In model-based learning, Gu et al. (2016) develop normalized advantage functions (NAF) for experience replay in continuous tasks, while their subsequent work (2017a) introduces an asynchronous NAF algorithm with safety constraints for robotic manipulation. Finn and Levine (2017) enhance model predictive control (MPC) by integrating it with action-conditioned video prediction, enabling manipulation of unseen objects without labeled data. Chebotar et al. (2017) combine model-based linear quadratic regulator (LQR) techniques with guided policy search to generalize their approach to deep neural networks. Lee et al. (2017) investigate visual servoing by merging features from object classification, predictive dynamic models, and Q-iteration algorithms.

Lastly, in the context of autonomous driving, O’Kelly et al. (2018) explore rare-event simulation for autonomous vehicles, while Fridman et al. (2018) introduce DeepTraffic, a micro-traffic simulator for deep reinforcement learning. Yu et al. (2018) release the BDD100K dataset, a comprehensive collection of diverse driving videos, contributing valuable resources for research in this domain.
