Computer vision focuses on how computers interpret and understand digital images or videos, with deep learning significantly advancing the field. Key milestones include the introduction of AlexNet by Krizhevsky et al. in 2012, which drastically reduced error rates in image classification tasks. He et al. developed ResNets in 2016 to facilitate training of deep neural networks through shortcut connections. Subsequent advancements in image segmentation were made with Fast R-CNN, Faster R-CNN, and Mask R-CNN.

Generative adversarial networks (GANs), introduced by Goodfellow et al. in 2014, have gained attention for their ability to generate new data. Research has focused on stabilizing GAN learning, with notable contributions like Wasserstein GANs and Least Squares GANs. Various GAN applications in computer vision include CycleGAN and DualGAN.

Disentangled factor learning is another area of interest, with methods like variational autoencoders and InfoGAN being proposed for unsupervised learning. Additionally, frameworks like Attend-Infer-Repeat enable efficient inference in structured image models, while some studies show that object detectors can learn scene recognition without labeled objects.

Reinforcement learning (RL) has emerged as a powerful tool for various computer vision tasks, including classification, detection, and captioning. It fosters interactive perception, where perception and environmental interaction enhance each other across multiple applications such as object segmentation and grasp planning.

The paper also highlights the importance of integrating computer vision with other fields like motor control and language reasoning. It discusses visual interpretability in deep learning and mentions tools like Lucid for feature visualization.
