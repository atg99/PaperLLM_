The paper discusses the challenges and advancements in computer Go, highlighting the immense search space and the complexities of position evaluation. AlphaGo, developed by Silver et al. in 2016, marked a significant milestone by defeating top human players, including the European champion and world champion Lee Sedol. AlphaGo Zero further advanced the technology by learning without human data, and Alpha Zero generalized this model to other domains.

The operational framework of AlphaGo comprises two main components: the neural network training pipeline and Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS). The training pipeline involves the development of a supervised learning policy network from expert moves, a fast rollout policy, and reinforcement learning (RL) components, including the RL policy and value networks. The supervised learning network utilizes deep convolutional neural networks with specific architectures to predict legal moves based on game state data.

In the MCTS phase, AlphaGo conducts a lookahead search to select moves, building a partial game tree through a systematic process of selection, expansion, evaluation, and backup of node values. This structured approach enables AlphaGo to make informed decisions and ultimately excel in the game of Go.
