The paper discusses various approaches to exploration in reinforcement learning (RL), particularly focusing on intrinsic motivation and count-based methods. It references foundational works and recent advancements in deep RL exploration strategies. 

Levine (2018) categorizes exploration methods into three classes: optimistic exploration methods, which utilize exploration bonuses to estimate state visitation; Thompson sampling methods, which learn distributions over Q-functions or policies; and information gain methods, which evaluate the value of visiting new states. All these approaches are based on the principle of optimism in uncertainty.

Count-based methods guide an RL agent's behavior using visit counts to minimize uncertainty. However, traditional count-based methods struggle in large domains. Bellemare et al. (2016) introduce pseudo-counts, a density model that unifies count-based exploration and intrinsic motivation by incorporating information gain. They demonstrate pseudo-count's theoretical benefits over prior methods, applying it in various settings, including Atari games.

Ostrovski et al. (2017) extend the pseudo-count approach by emphasizing the importance of density model selection and proposing the use of a neural density model (PixelCNN) to enhance exploration, particularly in sparse reward scenarios. Tang et al. (2017) present a method that employs hash codes for state mapping, allowing for efficient counting and reward bonuses to guide exploration.

In the realm of intrinsic motivation, Houthooft et al. (2016) propose variational information maximizing exploration (VIME), which balances external reward and intrinsic surprise by optimizing information gain through Bayesian neural networks. Pathak et al. (2017) further investigate curiosity-driven exploration by leveraging intrinsic rewards from self-supervised learning in tasks like VizDoom and Super Mario Bros.

Overall, the paper synthesizes key developments in exploration techniques within large-scale RL problems, highlighting the significance of both intrinsic motivation and count-based methods in enhancing agent performance in complex environments.
