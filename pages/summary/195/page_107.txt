The paper discusses the integration of intelligent components into machine learning systems, emphasizing the importance of leveraging human intelligence and existing knowledge rather than solely relying on deep learning techniques. It critiques the approach of rediscovering fundamental principles, such as Newton's laws, through extensive data analysis, and suggests that self-supervised learning could enhance the incorporation of intelligence in systems.

The authors propose a specific research direction focused on learning from entities rather than raw inputs. They argue that improving the efficiency of learning systems in terms of sample, time, and space, while achieving interpretability and reducing errors, is essential. For instance, in the context of a complex game like Montezuma’s Revenge, they suggest that a system capable of recognizing entities and their relationships in video frames would enable reinforcement learning (RL) agents to operate more effectively than if they processed raw pixel data.

The paper highlights recent advancements in computer vision and natural language processing (NLP) that support this direction. It references various studies that explore unsupervised learning, video object segmentation, and representation learning across modalities, suggesting a growing maturity in these areas. The authors advocate for a comprehensive investigation of vision, motor control, and language reasoning in conjunction with RL to enhance representation for RL problems.

Additionally, they emphasize the significance of neural network architecture in deep learning and RL, proposing that RL may require distinct optimal representations tailored to its unique structures, such as the Bellman equation. The authors call for a holistic approach that integrates perception and control in the design of neural networks, pointing to recent efforts in this area as a promising avenue for future research.
