This paper discusses advancements in neural network architectures tailored for reinforcement learning (RL), highlighting several notable models such as value iteration networks, predictron, and BBQ-Networks. It addresses challenges to traditional sequence modeling approaches, specifically questioning the effectiveness of recurrent neural networks (RNNs) compared to convolutional neural networks (CNNs). Recent studies demonstrate that CNNs can outperform RNNs across various tasks.

The paper also explores the role of memory in neural networks, noting the significance of long-term data storage and models like Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) and differentiable neural computers (DNC). These models aim to enhance reasoning capabilities and manage complex structured problems.

Additionally, the paper examines grid-like representations in navigation tasks, referencing studies that utilize vector-based navigation and grid cell representations to improve performance in deep RL scenarios. It emphasizes the importance of memory and attention mechanisms in facilitating reasoning, mentioning relational memory cores and compositional attention networks.

Lastly, the paper delves into knowledge representation and reasoning in AI, advocating for a comprehensive understanding of how knowledge can be represented and integrated into learning systems. It discusses various approaches, including symbolic methods and the influence of inductive biases, while highlighting the need for network architectures that enhance reasoning capabilities. Overall, the paper underscores the importance of these advancements in addressing fundamental issues in AI.
