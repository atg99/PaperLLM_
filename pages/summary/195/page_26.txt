The paper discusses advancements in Deep Q-Network (DQN) methodologies, focusing on various enhancements to improve learning efficiency and performance in reinforcement learning tasks. Zahavy et al. (2018) explore the implications of action elimination in DQN, while foundational concepts can be found in Sutton and Barto's work, specifically regarding the "deadly triad" in Chapter 11 and a comprehensive overview of DQN in Chapter 16.

A significant contribution is the introduction of Double DQN (D-DQN) by van Hasselt et al. (2016), which addresses the overestimation bias present in traditional Q-learning approaches. This is achieved by decoupling action selection from value estimation, allowing the algorithm to utilize the online network for selecting actions while evaluating their values with a target network.

Additionally, the paper highlights the implementation of prioritized experience replay, proposed by Schaul et al. (2016). This technique enhances learning efficiency by prioritizing transitions based on their temporal-difference (TD) errors, allowing more significant experiences to be replayed frequently, thus improving performance in environments like Atari games.

Wang et al. (2016b) introduce the dueling architecture, which separates the estimation of state value and advantage functions to compute the action value function more effectively. This architecture promotes faster convergence compared to standard Q-learning. The paper suggests replacing the max operator with an average for greater stability in combining these functions.

Overall, these enhancements—Double DQN, prioritized experience replay, and dueling architecture—collectively improve the performance of DQN and D-DQN, demonstrating their applicability and effectiveness in reinforcement learning scenarios.
