The paper discusses the fundamental tradeoff in reinforcement learning (RL) between exploration of uncertain policies and exploitation of the current best policy. This tradeoff presents a central challenge in online decision making, where the agent must choose between leveraging existing information for immediate gains or seeking additional information to enhance long-term decision-making.

Key principles for balancing exploration and exploitation include naive methods, optimism in the face of uncertainty (such as optimistic initialization and upper confidence bounds), probability matching, and information state search. These principles are rooted in the multi-armed bandit problem, which involves selecting actions from a set of options, receiving rewards, and aiming to maximize cumulative rewards over time.

The paper defines the multi-armed bandit problem using a tuple of actions and a probability distribution of rewards. It introduces concepts such as action-value functions, optimal values, and regret, which quantify the performance of the agent's decisions. The greedy algorithm selects actions based on the highest estimated value but may lead to suboptimal choices. The epsilon-greedy strategy offers a solution by allowing a balance of exploration and exploitation.

Optimistic initialization encourages exploration by starting action values at high estimates, although it can sometimes lead to suboptimal selections. The paper also highlights upper confidence bounds (UCB) as a significant method in bandit problems, which is extended to Monte Carlo tree search applications, including notable algorithms like AlphaGo. By estimating an upper confidence for each action, agents can make informed decisions that maximize potential rewards while accounting for uncertainty.
