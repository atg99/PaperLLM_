The paper discusses the integration of reinforcement learning (RL) and relational learning, highlighting its potential for enhancing problem-solving capabilities in AI. Relational RL, as proposed by Dzeroski et al. and further explored by Tadepalli et al., addresses challenges such as function approximation, generalization, task transferability, and the incorporation of prior knowledge. Guestrin et al. introduced relational Markov Decision Processes (MDPs), while Diuk et al. focused on object-oriented MDPs, both contributing to the relational RL framework.

The paper also covers relational learning, with Battaglia et al. examining how to embed relational inductive biases into deep learning architectures. They contrast fully connected networks, convolutional neural networks, and recurrent neural networks, each with varying degrees of relational inductive bias. Battaglia et al. propose graph networks to achieve combinatorial generalization through explicit entity and relationship representation.

Additionally, Santoro et al. introduce relation networks for enhanced relational reasoning in neural networks, while their later work presents a relational memory core to manage relational tasks using self-attention mechanisms. Other notable contributions include Chen et al.'s iterative visual reasoning framework, which combines local and global models, and Hudson and Manning's MAC cell, designed for interpretability and efficiency in reasoning tasks. The paper emphasizes the importance of these advancements in addressing complex AI challenges.
