Representation is a crucial aspect of reinforcement learning (RL), machine learning, and artificial intelligence (AI). It encompasses various elements of RL problems, including state/observation, action, value function, reward function, and transition probability. The concept of "representation" in representation learning parallels "feature" in feature engineering, focusing on the automatic discovery of effective features.

In RL, representation extends beyond function approximation to include problem representation frameworks such as Markov decision processes (MDPs), partially observable MDPs (POMDPs), and predictive state representations (PSRs). It also involves knowledge representation, reasoning, and causality, aiming to unify the contributions of deep learning across different facets of deep reinforcement learning.

For small problems, tabular representations suffice, while larger problems necessitate function approximation to mitigate the curse of dimensionality. Linear function approximation employs various basis functions, while recent advancements in deep neural networks have demonstrated significant success. Common architectures include multi-layer perceptrons (MLPs), convolutional neural networks (CNNs), recurrent neural networks (RNNs), and specialized networks tailored for RL tasks, such as value iteration networks (VINs) and value prediction networks (VPNs).

General value functions (GVFs) represent knowledge of the world, while hierarchical representations like options and feudal networks facilitate temporal abstraction. The integration of relational learning and reasoning into RL has led to renewed interest in networks designed for reasoning, including graph neural networks (GNNs) and relational networks (RNs).

Despite extensive efforts in representation, the unique nature of RL suggests that optimal representations may differ from traditional models like CNNs and RNNs. A holistic approach that integrates perception and control is advocated for discovering optimal representations in RL, potentially leveraging learning-to-learn techniques.
