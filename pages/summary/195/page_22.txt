The paper discusses the formulation of reinforcement learning (RL) problems as Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) defined by a 5-tuple consisting of states, actions, transition probabilities, rewards, and a discount factor. A key concept in RL is the value function, with Bellman equations serving as foundational elements for developing RL algorithms. Temporal difference learning techniques are essential for evaluating value functions, while control algorithms aim to discover optimal policies. Recently, policy-based methods have gained popularity.

Reinforcement learning algorithms can be categorized based on various factors, including their reliance on value functions or policies, whether they are model-free or model-based, and their approach to backups—such as one-step returns or multi-step returns. The combination of off-policy learning, function approximation, and bootstrapping poses challenges like instability and divergence, known as the "deadly triad."

Theoretical guarantees exist for linear function approximation methods, including Gradient-TD and Emphatic-TD. The integration of deep learning with RL, termed deep RL, has led to significant advancements, exemplified by algorithms like Deep Q-Network and AlphaGo, which have demonstrated remarkable performance.

The paper also provides resources for further exploration of deep RL, recommending various textbooks, online courses, and summer schools. It highlights common benchmarks for evaluating RL algorithms, including Atari games for discrete control and simulated robots in OpenAI Gym for continuous control. Notable frameworks and tools mentioned include the Arcade Learning Environment, OpenAI Gym, MuJoCo, DeepMind Lab, and the Dopamine framework from Google AI.
