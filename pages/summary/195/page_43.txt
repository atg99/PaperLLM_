This paper discusses the theoretical foundations of exploration strategies in reinforcement learning (RL) and multi-armed bandit problems, primarily utilizing Hoeffding's inequality. It establishes that for independent and identically distributed random variables bounded in [0,1], the probability of the expected value exceeding the sample mean can be bounded, leading to the development of the Upper Confidence Bound (UCB) algorithm. The UCB1 algorithm selects actions based on the estimated value and an exploration bonus derived from the logarithm of the time step and visit counts, which ensures optimal action selection as the number of trials increases.

The paper contrasts UCB with other exploration strategies such as epsilon-greedy and optimistic initialization, highlighting UCB's logarithmic regret compared to the linear regret of other methods. It also discusses different exploration bonuses used in various algorithms, like model-based interval estimation and Bayesian exploration bonuses.

Thompson sampling is introduced as a probabilistic approach to action selection that optimally balances exploration and exploitation by sampling from posterior distributions. Gittins indices are mentioned as a Bayesian method for addressing multi-armed bandit problems, although they can be computationally intensive for larger problems.

The paper extends the discussion to contextual bandits, where additional information is utilized to enhance exploration strategies. Techniques from multi-armed bandits are also applicable to full RL problems, with methods such as R-MAX and DQN incorporating exploration bonuses for improved performance.

Intrinsic motivation is presented as a concept for exploration that encourages agents to seek surprising states that can lead to significant updates in their understanding of the environment. Overall, the paper emphasizes the need for effective exploration strategies in RL to achieve better learning outcomes and reduce uncertainty in reward functions and transition probabilities.
