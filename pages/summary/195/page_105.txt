The paper discusses critical issues in deep reinforcement learning (RL) research, emphasizing the challenges related to reproducibility, evaluation techniques, and algorithm performance. Henderson et al. (2018) highlight that experimental results in deep RL are significantly affected by various hyperparameters, including network architecture, reward scale, random seeds, and environment settings, making reproducibility difficult. They advocate for comprehensive reporting of implementation details and hyperparameter settings to enhance reproducibility and recommend conducting multiple trials with varied random seeds and appropriate significance tests.

Khetarpal et al. (2018) address differences in evaluation methods between RL and supervised learning, proposing a structured evaluation pipeline. Levine (2018) identifies challenges in deep RL such as stability, efficiency, hyperparameter tuning, and reward specification. Despite the valuable insights from these studies and benchmark papers, there remains a gap in systematic comparative studies of deep RL algorithms.

The paper proposes two primary research directions. The first direction is a systematic comparative study of deep RL algorithms, encouraging the evaluation of various algorithms like DQN, A3C, and PPO, with the goal of providing thorough analyses and guidelines for practitioners. The second direction focuses on tackling multi-agent problems, which present unique challenges like non-stationarity. The paper suggests exploring approximation solutions and striving for super-human performance in multi-agent systems, emphasizing their potential applications in modeling complex interactions in society.

Overall, the paper underscores the need for comprehensive studies in deep RL to address existing challenges and improve the field's development.
