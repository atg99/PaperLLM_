The paper discusses the evolution and integration of reinforcement learning (RL) with deep learning, leading to the emergence of deep reinforcement learning (deep RL). It highlights the historical context of RL as a method for sequential decision-making across various fields, emphasizing its trial-and-error learning approach. Recent advancements in deep learning, facilitated by big data, computational power, and innovative algorithms, have revitalized interest in RL, particularly through breakthroughs such as deep Q-network (DQN), AlphaGo, and DeepStack. 

DQN is significant for single-player games and has influenced many RL algorithms. AlphaGo achieved remarkable success in complex two-player games, setting a benchmark in artificial intelligence and inspiring further research in game-playing and other applications like robotics and scheduling. DeepStack addressed challenges in imperfect information games, contributing to strategic decision-making in various fields.

The paper also outlines the development of novel algorithms and methods that enhance deep RL capabilities, including asynchronous methods, trust region methods, and generative adversarial imitation learning. These innovations expand the applicability of deep RL, suggesting its potential in areas like chemical synthesis planning and multi-agent systems.

The authors argue that deep learning aids RL by providing automatic feature extraction and enabling end-to-end learning, thus reducing reliance on domain expertise. Overall, the paper presents a comprehensive overview of the advancements and implications of deep RL in contemporary research and applications.
