Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) was introduced by Schulman et al. in 2017 as a method to enhance the stability and reliability of reinforcement learning while offering simpler implementation and improved empirical sample complexity. PPO allows for shared parameters in a neural network for policy and value functions, leveraging a truncated version of Generalized Advantage Estimator (GAE) for variance reduction in policy parameter estimation. It has demonstrated strong performance across various tasks, including continuous tasks in MuJoCo and discrete Atari games, and is noted for its ease of use at OpenAI.

The paper also discusses advances in combining the stability of policy gradients with the sample efficiency of off-policy learning. Levine (2018) connects reinforcement learning with control through probabilistic inference and highlights the benefits of maximum entropy reinforcement learning for exploration and compositionality. 

Soft Q-learning, developed by Haarnoja et al. in 2017, optimizes maximum entropy policies through energy-based methods and improves exploration. The soft actor-critic, proposed in 2018, aims to maximize both expected rewards and entropy. The relationship between entropy-regularized Q-learning and policy gradient is established by Schulman et al. 

Path Consistency Learning (PCL), introduced by Nachum et al. in 2017, generalizes traditional Bellman consistency and links softmax consistent action values with optimal policies. PCL bridges value-based and policy-based reinforcement learning by utilizing multi-step path-wise consistency. Trust-PCL, proposed in 2018, addresses sample inefficiency in trust region methods by maximizing rewards with relative entropy regularization, thus enhancing stability and off-policy sample efficiency. 

Dai et al. (2018) reformulate the Bellman equation into a primal-dual optimization problem, providing convergence guarantees for non-linear function approximation and introducing smoothed Bellman error embedding as a solution method.
