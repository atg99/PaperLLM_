Iterative policy evaluation is a method used to assess a given policy by applying Bellman expectation backup iteratively. The process updates the value function for all states, converging to the value function of the policy. Policy iteration alternates between evaluating the current policy and improving it, ultimately leading to an optimal policy and value function. A variant, generalized policy iteration (GPI), allows for any evaluation and improvement methods.

Value iteration is another approach that seeks an optimal policy by applying Bellman optimality backup iteratively, updating the value function until it converges to that of an optimal policy. Both synchronous and asynchronous dynamic programming (DP) methods can be employed, including approximate DP techniques.

The Bellman equations play a crucial role in defining the value function and its operators, which are shown to be contractions with fixed points. This property is essential for proving the convergence of various algorithms like policy iteration and value iteration.

Monte Carlo methods are distinct in that they learn from complete episodes without prior knowledge of transition or reward models, relying on sample means for estimation. They are suitable for episodic tasks and utilize empirical mean returns for policy evaluation. On-policy Monte Carlo control follows a generalized policy iteration framework, while off-policy learning evaluates a target policy based on a behavior policy, allowing for the reuse of experiences from previous policies or observations from other agents.
