The paper discusses recent advancements in reinforcement learning (RL) and planning, highlighting key contributions from various authors. 

Leonetti et al. (2016) introduce Domain Approximation for Reinforcement Learning (DARLING), which combines RL with planning to enable agents to adapt to their environments while constraining their behaviors to reasonable choices. Their evaluation on a service robot within an office setting demonstrates the effectiveness of this approach.

In the planning domain, Guez et al. (2018) present MCTSnets, a neural network architecture that leverages simulation-based search. This architecture optimizes a simulation policy, an evaluation network, and a backup network in an end-to-end manner, successfully applied to the Sokoban planning problem.

Tamar et al. (2016) propose Value Iteration Networks (VIN), a differentiable planning module that approximates the value iteration algorithm. Unlike traditional planning, VIN is model-free and integrates reward and transition probabilities into the neural network. VIN has shown versatility across various tasks, including gridworlds and navigation challenges, although its applicability is limited to specific state dynamics.

Silver et al. (2016b) introduce the Predictron, which merges learning and planning into a unified end-to-end training process using a Markov reward process. This model predicts values through multiple planning steps and can serve as a Q-network in control tasks.

Additionally, Silver et al. (2012) propose Temporal Difference Search (TD search), combining TD learning with simulation-based search to update value functions from simulated experiences. Other contributions include memory-augmented MCTS by Xiao et al. (2018) and Universal Planning Networks by Srinivas et al. (2018), which further advance the intersection of learning and planning methodologies.
