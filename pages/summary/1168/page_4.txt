This paper focuses on the K-value selection algorithm for the K-means clustering method, emphasizing the challenges associated with determining the optimal number of clusters, K. The authors summarize various representative methods for K-value selection and provide experimental validation using the Iris dataset from the UCI Machine Learning Repository.

The experimental setup utilized an Intel Core i5 dual-core CPU, with the Iris dataset comprising 150 samples across three classes, each with four attributes. The paper highlights two main methods for determining the optimal K-value: the Elbow Method and the Gap Statistic Algorithm.

1. **Elbow Method**: This method calculates the sum of squared errors (SSE) for different K values and identifies the point where the SSE decreases rapidly, indicating a suitable K-value. A notable inflection point was found at K=2, suggesting that this value yields the best clustering effect.

2. **Gap Statistic**: Proposed by Tibshirani, this algorithm utilizes Monte Carlo sampling to create reference distributions and compares their clustering results with the original dataset. The optimal K-value corresponds to the maximum Gap statistic, determined by comparing expectations derived from random samples.

Both methods were evaluated through experimental simulations, with results indicating that K=2 provided the most effective clustering of the Iris dataset. The paper provides pseudocode for both algorithms, illustrating the computational steps involved in determining the K-value.
