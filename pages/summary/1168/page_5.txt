The paper discusses two clustering evaluation methods: the Gap Statistic and the Silhouette Coefficient. 

The Gap Statistic algorithm aims to determine the optimal number of clusters (k) by comparing the within-cluster dispersion of the data to that of a reference distribution. It uses a pseudo code that involves generating sample sets, calculating the log of the within-cluster sum of squares (Wk), and iteratively computing the Gap values. The Silhouette Coefficient, introduced by Peter J. Rousseeuw, measures cohesion and separation in clusters. It quantifies how close a data point is to its own cluster compared to other clusters, with values ranging from -1 to 1. A higher Silhouette value indicates a better fit of the model. The calculation involves determining the average intra-cluster distance (a(i)) and the average inter-cluster distance (b(i)). The Silhouette value is computed based on these distances, allowing for the evaluation of cluster quality.

In summary, the paper presents effective methodologies for assessing clustering performance, highlighting the significance of both the Gap Statistic and the Silhouette Coefficient in determining optimal cluster numbers and evaluating cluster quality.
