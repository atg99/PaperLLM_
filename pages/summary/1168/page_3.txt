The paper discusses the importance of the K-value in the K-means clustering algorithm and its impact on clustering results, including local and global optimality. It highlights the challenge of defining an appropriate K-value, which is crucial for determining the number of clusters in a dataset. Initially, the "shooting the head" method was used for K-value estimation, but various optimization methods have since been proposed.

The research summarizes representative methods for K-value selection, providing analysis and experimental verification. The experiments were conducted on the Iris dataset from the UCI Machine Learning Repository, which includes three classes of iris species with 150 samples and four attributes each. To enhance clustering effects, only two dimensions of the dataset were utilized.

The paper introduces the Elbow Method, which employs the sum of squared errors (SSE) to assess clustering performance. By calculating SSE for different K-values, a rapid decline in SSE indicates an optimal number of clusters. The algorithm's pseudo code is provided, illustrating the process of determining K through the analysis of the K-SSE curve, where an inflection point indicates the optimal K-value. The results suggest that K=2 yields the best clustering effect for the Iris dataset.
