The Canopy algorithm demonstrated a K value of 2 for the Iris dataset, with a relatively low execution time of 2.120 seconds, making it a practical choice compared to the other methods analyzed.

The paper discusses four K-value selection algorithms: Elbow Method, Gap Statistic, Silhouette Coefficient, and Canopy, each with distinct characteristics and performance metrics. The Elbow Method relies on finding an inflection point in the sum of squared errors (SSE) but can struggle when the inflection point is not clear. The Gap Statistic compares observed and expected data set values but has high computational demands, particularly for large datasets. The Silhouette Coefficient balances cluster cohesion and separation but also faces scalability issues due to its quadratic complexity. In contrast, the Canopy algorithm efficiently clusters data with overlapping subsets and shows promise for initialization in larger clustering tasks.

The experimental results summarized in Table 1 highlight that while all algorithms identified K=2 for the Iris dataset, they differed significantly in execution times, with the Canopy algorithm being notably faster than the Gap Statistic and Silhouette Coefficient methods. Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of selecting a suitable algorithm based on the specific dataset and computational constraints.
