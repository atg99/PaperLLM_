The paper discusses the architecture and functioning of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) in the context of computer vision (CV). It highlights the role of pooling layers, such as max pooling, in reducing redundancy by extracting important features from input images. The CNN architecture typically consists of multiple convolutional and pooling layers followed by a fully connected layer that transforms the output into a feature vector. This vector is then processed by an output layer, often utilizing functions like Softmax to generate class probabilities.

While acknowledging the effectiveness of deep learning (DL) in CV, the paper emphasizes that DL is just one tool among many, with convolution being a fundamental image processing technique used in various applications, including edge detection. The paper points out that DL is not a universal solution and that traditional CV methods can be more appropriate for certain problems.

In the section on traditional CV techniques, the paper lists several feature-based approaches, including Scale Invariant Feature Transform (SIFT), Speeded Up Robust Features (SURF), Features from Accelerated Segment Test (FAST), Hough transforms, and geometric hashing. These techniques are often paired with conventional machine learning classifiers like Support Vector Machines and K-Nearest Neighbours to enhance performance in CV tasks.
