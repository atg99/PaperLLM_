This paper argues that traditional computer vision (CV) techniques can be more efficient and simpler to implement than deep learning (DL) for many applications.

Algorithms like SIFT and basic color thresholding are general and not class-specific, allowing them to perform consistently across various images. In contrast, features from deep neural networks are tailored to specific training datasets, which can lead to poor performance on unseen images if the dataset is not well-constructed. This limitation highlights the need for careful consideration when choosing between traditional CV and DL approaches.

For example, distinguishing between products based on color on an assembly line can be effectively achieved using simple color thresholding rather than a complex DL model, provided there is sufficient data for training. The risk of overfitting with DL models, especially when training datasets are limited, complicates the model tuning process due to the high number of parameters involved, making them less transparent compared to traditional CV methods.

Traditional techniques remain relevant, particularly for simpler problems that can be efficiently executed on low-cost microcontrollers or to refine data for DL applications. The paper mentions that traditional methods can complement DL by enhancing data quality through augmentation and aiding in dataset annotation.

The discussion extends to more complex challenges in computer vision, such as robotics, augmented reality, and scene understanding, where traditional techniques offer valuable solutions that may not be easily replicated with DL alone. The paper emphasizes the importance of understanding the strengths and limitations of both approaches to optimize performance in various CV applications.
