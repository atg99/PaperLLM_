The paper discusses the advancements in performance achieved through subspace methods and deep convolutional prediction, highlighting significant improvements in runtime compared to existing state-of-the-art techniques. However, it also addresses the limitations of deep learning (DL) in certain computer vision (CV) applications. 

DL is particularly effective for closed-end classification problems where sufficient data is available, but it struggles with dynamic problems like robotics, augmented reality, and video processing that require traditional techniques. Marcus et al. identify ten concerns regarding DL, emphasizing the necessity of integrating other methods to approach artificial general intelligence. 

One major limitation of DL is its poor capability in learning visual relations, such as distinguishing between multiple objects in an image. The authors suggest that incorporating feedback mechanisms like attention and perceptual grouping could enhance abstract visual reasoning. Additionally, machine learning models often face challenges with priors, indicating that not all knowledge can be derived from data alone, particularly in 3D computer vision where strong priors are essential for effective modeling.

The paper also highlights the emerging field of 3D vision, which has seen advancements in 3D Convolutional Neural Networks (3D CNNs). This area, referred to as Geometric Deep Learning, has applications in video classification, computer graphics, and robotics. The paper focuses on the design of 3D CNNs that maintain spatial information by ensuring the depth of the 3D convolutional kernel is less than that of the 3D input volume.
