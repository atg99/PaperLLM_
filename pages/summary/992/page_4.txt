The paper discusses the significant transformation in the workflow of computer vision (CV) engineers due to the advent of deep learning, particularly convolutional neural networks (CNNs). Traditional methods that relied on hand-crafted feature extraction have shifted towards a focus on deep learning architectures. The rise of CNNs has dramatically improved object recognition capabilities, a change fueled by increased computing power and the availability of large datasets for training.

The seminal paper on ImageNet classification using deep CNNs has garnered over 3000 citations, underscoring the impact of this technology. CNNs operate using kernels, or filters, which are trained to identify specific features in images, such as edges. The convolution process involves applying these kernels to input images and calculating the dot product to assess the presence of features, generating a confidence value.

To enhance the learning of kernel weights, the output from the convolution layers is combined with a bias term and processed through non-linear activation functions. Common activation functions include Sigmoid, TanH, and ReLU (Rectified Linear Unit). ReLU is preferred in image recognition tasks due to its biological relevance, efficiency in representation, and reduced susceptibility to the vanishing gradient problem. This transition to deep learning methodologies has revolutionized the field of computer vision.
