The paper discusses the iterative nature of training models in computer vision (CV), emphasizing the importance of transfer learning to reduce training time. It notes the resurgence of traditional techniques like the discrete Fourier transform, which can enhance convolution speed. For specialized tasks, less data is required, often in the hundreds or thousands, as opposed to millions needed for general image classification. Techniques such as data augmentation and pre-processing are crucial to enhance training data quality and quantity. Data augmentation involves manipulating images to create variations, while pre-processing prepares data for better pattern recognition.

The paper also highlights the advantages of edge computing in improving efficiency by running algorithms locally, which reduces latency, costs, and data transmission issues. This approach enhances privacy and security by minimizing the transfer of sensitive information. It discusses the benefits of hybrid models combining conventional CV and deep learning (DL), particularly utilizing heterogeneous computing architectures that optimize workload distribution among various processors. Implementations have shown significant latency reductions in object detection when using digital signal processors (DSPs) over CPUs.

Hybrid approaches, integrating hand-crafted features with deep learning, are noted for their effectiveness in edge applications, such as facial expression recognition. These methods aim to bridge the gap between traditional feature extraction and deep learning capabilities. Additionally, the incorporation of data from multiple sensors, like synthetic aperture radar imagery, enhances the depth and accuracy of models, particularly in complex tasks like 3D robot vision.
