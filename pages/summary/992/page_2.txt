This paper discusses the challenges posed by deep learning (DL) in computer vision (CV), including issues related to computing power, time, accuracy, and the characteristics and quantity of inputs. It contrasts DL with traditional handcrafted feature definition approaches that previously dominated the field. The significant advancements in DL make it difficult to cover all aspects and sub-domains addressing current CV problems.

The paper begins by reviewing traditional algorithmic methods in CV, highlighting their applications as alternatives or complements to DL, particularly in scenarios where DL may fall short. It then examines recent efforts to integrate DL with CV, focusing on state-of-the-art techniques for emerging technologies like 3D perception, including object registration, detection, and semantic segmentation of 3D point clouds.

The discussion extends to the challenges of achieving 3D DL performance comparable to 2D DL and considers the broader implications of increased 3D utilization in CV. 

The paper also differentiates between descriptive and predictive analysis, emphasizing that descriptive analysis involves creating mathematical models based on observed data, while predictive analysis seeks to discover underlying rules to minimize prediction errors. Machine learning, and by extension DL, moves away from traditional programming towards a training framework where systems learn from extensive datasets. DL, as a subset of machine learning, utilizes artificial neural networks inspired by the human brain to learn complex patterns efficiently and without supervision, marking a recent area of interest in the field.
