The conclusion of the paper emphasizes the enduring relevance of traditional computer vision (CV) techniques, despite the rise of deep learning (DL) in recent years. It argues that knowledge from past innovations remains valuable, particularly for addressing complex 3D CV challenges. The paper presents a case for the utility of traditional CV methods, highlighting scenarios where they may serve as more suitable alternatives to DL, especially when the latter is excessive for specific tasks.

Furthermore, the paper discusses how traditional CV techniques can enhance DL performance through hybrid approaches, contributing to advancements in areas such as the Internet of Things (IoT) and emerging fields like simultaneous localization and mapping (SLAM) and geometric deep learning. It underscores that traditional techniques can reduce training time and data requirements, proving beneficial in contexts where DL is not yet fully established.

In summary, the paper aims to demonstrate that traditional CV techniques still hold significant value and can complement modern data-driven methods, asserting that there is much to learn from the foundational knowledge established prior to the AI boom.
