Deep Learning (DL) offers enhanced accuracy and versatility for computer vision (CV) applications but demands significant computing resources. Hybrid approaches that integrate traditional CV methods with DL leverage the strengths of both, proving particularly effective in high-performance systems requiring rapid deployment. For instance, in security cameras, CV algorithms can efficiently detect features like faces or moving objects, which can then be processed by a Deep Neural Network (DNN) for tasks such as identity verification. This method conserves computational resources by applying the DNN only to specific image patches rather than entire frames.

The combination of machine learning metrics and deep networks has gained popularity due to its ability to generate superior models. Hybrid implementations can result in substantial performance improvements, achieving reductions in multiply-accumulate operations by 130 to 1,000 times and enhancing frame rates by about 10 times compared to purely DL methods. Additionally, these hybrid systems utilize approximately half the memory bandwidth and require significantly fewer CPU resources.

However, DL also presents challenges. Although it can achieve higher accuracy, this often entails billions of additional mathematical operations and greater processing power requirements. Dedicated hardware, such as high-powered GPUs and TPUs for training, and AI-accelerated platforms like VPUs for inference, are essential for developers. 

The effectiveness of DL in vision processing is closely tied to image resolution. High-resolution images are crucial for tasks like object classification, particularly in scenarios requiring distant object detection. Techniques like SIFT features and optical flow can help identify regions of interest, thereby optimizing image resolution and reducing the data and time needed for training.

Moreover, DL relies heavily on large datasets, often necessitating millions of records for effective training. For example, datasets like PASCAL VOC, ImageNet, and COCO contain hundreds of thousands to millions of images across various categories. In situations where large datasets or high computing resources are lacking, traditional methods become more viable. Additionally, the training duration for DNNs can be extensive, varying from hours to days based on hardware availability.
