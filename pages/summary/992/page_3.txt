The paper discusses the advantages of deep learning (DL) over traditional computer vision (CV) techniques, particularly in the context of object detection in mobile robotics. It highlights that advancements in processing hardware and self-organization of interactions among small units have led to better fault tolerance and adaptability in complex processes. 

DL has seen rapid progress due to improvements in computing power, memory capacity, and sensor technology, enhancing the performance and cost-effectiveness of vision-based applications. Unlike traditional CV methods, which rely on feature extraction and expert-driven algorithms, DL leverages large datasets to train neural networks that automatically identify and learn the most relevant features for classification tasks. This end-to-end learning approach reduces the need for manual feature selection and fine-tuning, allowing for greater accuracy in tasks such as image classification, semantic segmentation, and object detection.

The traditional approach involves using established CV techniques like SIFT and SURF for feature extraction, which can be cumbersome and reliant on the engineer's judgment, especially as the number of object classes increases. In contrast, DL models can be retrained with custom datasets, making them more flexible and efficient. The paper concludes that deep neural networks (DNNs) significantly outperform traditional algorithms, despite requiring more computational resources.
