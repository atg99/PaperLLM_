The paper discusses the ActFormer model for generating human motion, emphasizing its robustness through data augmentation techniques. It introduces the GTA Combat dataset, which is a synthetic multi-person motion capture dataset created using the game GTA-V. This dataset captures complex interactive actions among multiple characters, making it suitable for evaluating the model's performance in chaotic fighting scenarios.

The experiments section details the evaluation of ActFormer on single-person and multi-person motion generation tasks using various datasets, including NTU-13, NTU RGB+D 120, and BABEL. The NTU datasets provide motion clips with noisy skeleton joint coordinates, which are refined for better quality. The BABEL dataset offers higher-quality motion data with semantic labels.

Evaluation metrics include action recognition accuracy and FID scores, leveraging a pre-trained ST-GCN model for action recognition. The paper highlights the importance of measuring root translation in multi-person scenarios and proposes two FID calculations: the whole FID (considering all samples collectively) and the mean FID (averaging FID scores across action categories), arguing that the latter is more appropriate for action-conditional generation.

Overall, the paper presents a comprehensive framework for assessing the ActFormer model through robust datasets and evaluation metrics that account for the complexities of human motion generation.
