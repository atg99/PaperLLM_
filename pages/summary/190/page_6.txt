The paper presents a comparison of various methods for single-person and multi-person motion generation, focusing on the performance of the proposed ActFormer model against established baselines such as Action2Motion, CSGN, and ACTOR. The evaluation metrics include accuracy (Acc.) and Fréchet Inception Distances (FIDm, FIDw, and FIDa), with higher accuracy and lower FID values indicating better performance.

In single-person motion generation, ActFormer outperforms all competitors, achieving an accuracy of 99.9% and the lowest FID values across datasets including NTU-13, NTURecon-1P, and BABEL. Notably, it excels particularly on the challenging BABEL dataset, which is characterized by long-tailed data distributions.

For multi-person motion generation, the results show that ActFormer also leads the performance metrics on the NTU-2P dataset and demonstrates significant advantages on the GTA Combat dataset. The methodology involves using two approaches to calculate FID: treating multi-person motions as a whole and aggregating single-person features through a recognition model.

The implementation details highlight the use of specific data splits for training and the adaptation of existing models to conditional generation tasks. The paper emphasizes the adaptability of ActFormer to various motion representations and its effectiveness in capturing human interactions in multi-person scenarios, which has not been addressed by prior works.

Overall, the study establishes ActFormer as a state-of-the-art solution for both single and multi-person motion generation tasks, showcasing its strong performance across multiple datasets and its ability to handle complex motion dynamics.
