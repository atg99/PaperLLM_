The paper presents ActFormer, a GAN-based Transformer framework designed for general action-conditioned human motion generation. It evaluates the framework on challenging benchmarks, including NTU-2P and GTA Combat, demonstrating superior performance compared to previous methods in generating both single-person and multi-person motions.

An ablation study highlights the impact of interaction encoding configurations on various metrics such as accuracy and FID scores. The complete model outperforms simpler configurations, suggesting the significance of interaction mechanisms in generating realistic motions. Qualitative results illustrate the framework's ability to generate coherent multi-person actions, showcasing synchronization in poses and trajectories during specific scenarios like "Take a photo" and "Combat" actions. However, a noted limitation is the model's struggle with sparse interactions in scenarios with multiple individuals, indicating challenges in effectively grouping interactions.

In conclusion, while ActFormer significantly advances the field of human motion generation, it acknowledges the need for further development, particularly in synthesizing human-object interactions.
