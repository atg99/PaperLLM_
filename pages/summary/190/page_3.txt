This paper discusses the development of ActFormer, a framework for action-conditioned motion generation that builds on existing models such as Action2Motion and ACTOR. Unlike Action2Motion, which uses a GRU architecture for frame-by-frame motion generation, and ACTOR, which employs a Transformer architecture with a sequence-level latent distribution, ActFormer integrates a sequence-level latent prior sampled from a Gaussian Process. This approach enhances temporal correlations, facilitating more realistic motion generation.

The paper also highlights the importance of large-scale motion capture (MoCap) datasets for training motion generators. While existing datasets like AMASS provide extensive motion data, they lack semantic action labels. The newly released BA-BEL dataset addresses this gap by offering fine-grained action labels, which enhances the benchmarking capabilities for motion generation tasks. The NTU RGB+D dataset is noted for its action labels and diverse motion representations, and the authors contribute the GTA Combat dataset to complement existing datasets with more complex multi-person interactions.

In terms of methodology, the ActFormer framework is designed to generate human motion sequences based on a semantic action label and a latent vector sampled from a prior. The generation process begins with sampling a latent vector sequence, which is then processed by a Transformer-based generator. This generator transforms the latent sequence and the action label into a motion sequence for single or multiple persons, utilizing a structure that allows for the incorporation of temporal correlations and scalability.

The paper concludes with a focus on the transition from single-person to multi-person motion generation, noting that the existing framework can be adjusted to accommodate the additional complexity of multiple interacting characters.
