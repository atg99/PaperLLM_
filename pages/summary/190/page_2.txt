This paper presents a novel approach to action-conditioned human motion generation, addressing key challenges such as generating long, realistic, and diverse motion sequences and handling multi-person interactions. Traditional methods often rely on Markovian assumptions and auto-regressive models, which can lead to the "mean-pose" problem, where the model produces repetitive outputs. In contrast, the proposed Action-Conditioned Motion Transformer (ActFormer) utilizes a Gaussian Process latent prior to capture temporal correlations while employing a Transformer architecture for its ability to handle non-local dependencies. This integration allows for effective single-person motion generation.

Additionally, the paper addresses the challenge of multi-person motion generation, which has not been extensively explored in previous works. ActFormer synchronizes multiple individuals in a group by sharing the same latent vector sequence while distinguishing them through positional encodings. This enables the model to generate realistic multi-person interactions.

The study also highlights the scarcity of motion capture datasets with diverse action labels, especially for multi-person interactions. To overcome this, the authors utilize the NTU RGB+D 120 and BA-BEL datasets, which contain over 100 action categories, and introduce a new GTA Combat dataset featuring approximately 7,000 motion sequences of combat behaviors.

The paper summarizes its contributions as follows: (i) the introduction of ActFormer, a GAN-based Transformer framework that excels in single-person motion generation; (ii) a pioneering effort to address multi-person motion generation; and (iii) the creation of the GTA Combat dataset to further research in this area. The findings demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed methods through experiments conducted on these datasets.
