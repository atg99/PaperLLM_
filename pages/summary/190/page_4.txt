The paper presents the ActFormer framework, designed to generate human motion sequences either for single or multiple individuals using a conditional generative adversarial network (GAN) approach. The model utilizes a latent vector sequence sampled from a Gaussian Process prior, along with specified action labels, to synthesize motion sequences. 

In the single-person mode, a motion sequence consists of T frames encoded from T tokens. For multi-person scenarios, the model employs a shared latent vector sequence among P individuals, enforcing synchronization in their motions. To distinguish between individuals, the model incorporates learnable positional encodings, combining temporal and person-specific encodings into a unified format for input tokens.

The ActFormer architecture consists of two main components: the Interaction-transFormer (I-Former) and the Temporal-transFormer (T-Former). The I-Former captures interactions among individuals at each time step, while the T-Former captures temporal correlations across time for each person. This dual-layered approach enhances the model's ability to represent complex motion dynamics.

The training process leverages a conditional GAN framework, where the generator synthesizes motion sequences based on action labels, and a discriminator assesses the authenticity of these sequences. The conditional Wasserstein GAN loss functions guide the training, ensuring that the generated sequences approach the quality of real motion data.

For the discriminator, a Spatial-Temporal Graph Convolutional Network (ST-GCN) is employed to analyze the motion sequences, which are structured as spatial-temporal graphs. The model concatenates part-wise motions from multiple individuals to capture their interactions effectively. To address the non-invariance of the concatenation operator with respect to person permutations, a data augmentation strategy is implemented, randomly permuting individuals in the training samples.

Overall, the ActFormer framework demonstrates a robust method for generating synchronized human motion sequences, effectively modeling both individual and collective dynamics through advanced neural network architectures and training strategies.
