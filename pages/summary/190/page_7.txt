The paper presents an ablation study evaluating various design choices in a motion generation framework, specifically focusing on single and multi-person action synthesis. Experimental results on the NTU-1P and NTU-2P datasets indicate that the full model significantly outperforms other configurations, achieving the lowest Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) scores and highest accuracy. 

Key findings include the importance of a Gaussian Process latent prior over a standard Gaussian latent prior, as evidenced by a noticeable increase in FID scores when the latter is used. The study also reveals that explicitly modeling skeleton topology with Graph Convolution does not enhance performance compared to the proposed ActFormer architecture, which effectively encodes human motions into vector-like token embeddings.

Positional encoding (PE) is shown to be more effective when learned rather than fixed, particularly in multi-person scenarios where a 2D combination of temporal and person-wise PE outperforms independent PE. For the discriminator in the GAN training framework, simple concatenation with data augmentation strategies yields better results than more complex designs.

The paper highlights that the success of the ActFormer model in multi-person motion generation is attributed to its shared latent vector sequences and the interaction modeling through I-Former, which captures human interactions effectively. Qualitative results showcase the model's ability to generate diverse and synchronized motions in both single and multi-person settings, demonstrating its potential for realistic action synthesis. 

Overall, the findings underscore the significance of latent prior design, architecture choices, and interaction encoding in enhancing the quality of generated human motions.
