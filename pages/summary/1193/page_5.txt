The paper presents a comparison of various methods for object detection on the MS-COCO dataset, focusing on the effectiveness of different data augmentation strategies and semi-supervised learning techniques. Table 1 summarizes the mean average precision (mAP) results for models trained with varying proportions of labeled data, demonstrating that both supervised and semi-supervised methods yield improved performance as the amount of labeled data increases. Notably, the STAC method shows competitive results across different data percentages, utilizing a robust data augmentation strategy involving global color and geometric transformations, as well as box-level transformations.

The data augmentation strategy is identified as a crucial factor for the success of consistency-based semi-supervised learning (SSL) methods. The authors extend existing augmentation techniques to enhance object detection, employing transformations such as color adjustments, geometric alterations, and Cutout to prevent trivial solutions. The experiments are conducted on the MS-COCO dataset, which includes a substantial number of labeled and unlabeled images, allowing for a thorough evaluation of the proposed methods under two SSL settings: one with limited labeled data and another with a full labeled dataset complemented by unlabeled data.

In addition to the MS-COCO experiments, the paper also evaluates the methods on the PASCAL VOC dataset, reporting mAP scores at different intersection over union (IoU) thresholds. The results indicate that the STAC method, particularly when augmented with additional unlabeled sources, consistently outperforms other approaches, suggesting its effectiveness in improving object detection performance with limited labeled data. Overall, the findings emphasize the importance of both data augmentation and the strategic use of unlabeled data in enhancing the capabilities of object detection models.
