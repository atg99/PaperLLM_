This section elaborates on the unsupervised loss formulations applicable to a variety of semi-supervised learning (SSL) algorithms, represented in a general form as shown in Equation (5). The notation p(x) is introduced for clarity, replacing p(x; θ) to reflect model predictions with parameters θ during training.

The unsupervised loss formulation of the STAC algorithm is related to the Noisy Student approach, integrating self-training and strong data augmentation. The authors highlight that this simple formulation of STAC significantly enhances object detection performance. They also suggest that more complex formulations like Mean Teacher and MixMatch/ReMixMatch can be adapted in place of certain design choices made for STAC, indicating potential areas for future research.

The section continues with specific formulations for various methods:

1. **Bootstrapping (Self-Training)**: Defined by the loss function as the cross-entropy between q and p, where weights are assigned based on the maximum predicted probability from the existing model.

2. **Entropy Minimization**: This method also uses cross-entropy as the loss and applies uniform weights, with the unique feature of backpropagating gradients through q.

3. **Pseudo Labeling**: This approach employs cross-entropy loss, with weights based on the highest predicted probability, and converts predictions into one-hot encoded labels.

4. **Temporal Ensembling**: The loss is based on the L2 norm between q and p, with weights set to one, and q is updated over time by combining past and current predictions.

The authors note that further details on the ramp-up and ramp-down processes for weights are omitted, as they depend on the specific optimization framework used.
