This paper presents a semi-supervised learning (SSL) framework for object detection called STAC, which integrates self-training and augmentation-driven consistency regularization. The framework generates pseudo labels for unlabeled data using a teacher model trained on labeled data, applying test-time inference and non-maximum suppression (NMS) to ensure high precision in predictions. The process involves two stages: first, training an object detector, such as Faster R-CNN, on labeled data until convergence, and second, applying strong data augmentations to unlabeled images to enhance model training with both labeled and pseudo-labeled data.

The authors describe their augmentation strategy, which includes global color transformations, geometric transformations, and Cutout techniques, inspired by successful methods like RandAugment. They evaluate STAC's performance on public datasets, specifically MS-COCO and PASCAL VOC, using various proportions of labeled data to assess the effectiveness of SSL in low-label regimes. The results show significant improvements in mean Average Precision (mAP) across different protocols, indicating the framework's efficacy.

Key contributions of the paper include the development of the STAC framework, the introduction of only two new hyperparameters (confidence threshold and unsupervised loss weight), and the establishment of new experimental protocols for SSL in object detection. The paper positions STAC as a simple yet effective method that builds upon existing SSL techniques while addressing the challenges of utilizing unlabeled data in object detection tasks.
