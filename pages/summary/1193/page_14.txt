This section discusses various semi-supervised learning techniques including Mean Teacher, Virtual Adversarial Training, Unsupervised Data Augmentation (UDA), FixMatch, and Noisy Student. 

Mean Teacher employs a consistency loss between two models, q and p, where q is an exponential moving average of p. The weight function w(x) is set to 1, focusing on the distance between the outputs of the two models. 

Virtual Adversarial Training introduces adversarial perturbations to enhance model robustness, maintaining a similar consistency loss framework. 

Unsupervised Data Augmentation leverages weak and strong augmentation techniques to generate pseudo labels and improve model training. It utilizes a consistency loss based on augmented inputs.

FixMatch follows a similar strategy as UDA, using weak augmentations for pseudo labeling and strong augmentations for training, also relying on a consistency loss.

Noisy Student builds on these concepts by training a model on labeled data to convergence before incorporating unlabeled data, using a consistency loss while also addressing class imbalance.

Overall, these methods aim to improve model performance by leveraging unlabeled data through various augmentation and consistency strategies.
