The section discusses two semi-supervised learning techniques: MixMatch and ReMixMatch. Both methods utilize MixUp for unsupervised loss, employing weak augmentations like translation and horizontal flipping. 

In MixMatch, the process involves combining two unlabeled data points, x and z, using a parameter φ drawn from a Beta distribution. The mixed output q(x) is a weighted sum of the augmented versions of x and z. The probability distribution p(x) is also derived from this weighted combination. 

ReMixMatch builds on the principles of MixMatch by incorporating strong augmentations, denoted as A(·), alongside weak augmentations. Similar to MixMatch, it mixes the unlabeled data points and applies the same weighting strategy. The outputs ~q(x) and ~q(z) are derived from the augmented data, and the resulting probability distribution p(x) is computed accordingly.

Both methods highlight the flexibility of applying MixUp not only between unlabeled data but also between labeled and unlabeled data, enhancing the learning process.
