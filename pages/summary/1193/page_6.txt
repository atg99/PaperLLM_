The paper investigates the effectiveness of various data augmentation strategies and learning schedules on the performance of object detection models, particularly focusing on the semi-supervised learning approach known as STAC. The experiments utilize the MS-COCO dataset under different labeled data protocols (5%, 10%, and 100%) and report mean Average Precisions (mAPs) to evaluate model performance.

The results, summarized in Table 3, demonstrate that models employing RandAugment consistently outperformed those using default augmentation techniques, with notable improvements in mAP at varying data percentages. Specifically, STAC significantly enhances performance, especially in scenarios with limited labeled data, yielding improvements of 5.91 mAP at the 5% protocol and 4.78 mAP at the 10% protocol compared to traditional supervised models. Additionally, STAC exhibits greater data efficiency, outperforming baseline models trained with more labeled data.

Implementation details highlight the use of the Faster RCNN and FPN framework with a ResNet-50 backbone. The training predominantly follows a quick learning schedule, although longer training is advantageous for complex augmentations and larger labeled datasets. Hyperparameters related to confidence thresholds and unsupervised loss are also optimized for improved results.

The findings suggest that leveraging unlabeled data in conjunction with robust augmentation strategies can enhance the learning capabilities of object detectors, particularly when labeled data is scarce. Comparisons with supervised models indicate that while STAC achieves substantial gains, it still falls slightly short of the highest performance seen in fully supervised models with advanced augmentation techniques. Further exploration of the relationship between the amount of unlabeled data and model performance is proposed for future work.
