In this paper, the authors detail their training methodology using Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD) with specific hyperparameters for image classification tasks on two datasets: IN-1M and IG-1B. For IN-1M, they utilize a mini-batch size of 256 across 8 GPUs, with an initial learning rate of 0.03, training for 200 epochs. For IG-1B, the mini-batch size increases to 1024 on 64 GPUs, with a learning rate of 0.12, and training extends over 1.25 million iterations.

The authors employ a linear classification protocol to validate their method, leveraging frozen features from unsupervised pre-training on IN-1M. They train a supervised linear classifier for 100 epochs, achieving a top-1 classification accuracy on the ImageNet validation set. A grid search identifies optimal hyperparameters, revealing that feature distributions differ significantly from those in supervised training.

An ablation study compares three contrastive loss mechanisms, demonstrating that all benefit from an increased K, the number of negatives used. The end-to-end mechanism shows similar performance to MoCo with small K, but is limited by mini-batch size. The memory bank mechanism, while capable of supporting larger dictionaries, underperforms compared to MoCo due to inconsistencies in keys from different epochs.

The authors also examine the impact of momentum on accuracy, finding that higher momentum values yield better results, highlighting the importance of a consistent dictionary in training. They present a comparison of their results against previous unsupervised learning methods, noting that their approach achieves competitive accuracy with standard ResNet-50 architectures, outperforming many competitors of similar model sizes. Overall, the findings support the advantages of larger models and the effectiveness of their proposed methods in contrastive learning frameworks.
