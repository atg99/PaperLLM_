The paper discusses feature ranking performance across various classifiers, focusing on the relative F1 score compared to a baseline where all features are utilized in a logistic regression model. It presents findings on methods such as Mutual Information, Random Forests, and ReliefF in the context of different datasets. 

The authors express gratitude for financial support from the Slovenian Research Agency and the European Union's Horizon 2020 program. They introduce the concept of global attention in feature-level activation, defining it mathematically through softmax functions applied to weight matrices. This global attention mechanism is further elaborated by contrasting it with a row-wise softmax approach.

The references cited include foundational works in machine learning and specific algorithms relevant to the study, such as Random Forests and XGBoost, indicating a well-rounded understanding of the field's literature. Overall, the paper contributes to the discourse on feature importance estimation using self-attention networks, providing both theoretical and practical insights.
