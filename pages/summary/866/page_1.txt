The paper "Feature Importance Estimation with Self-Attention Networks" by Blaž Škrlj et al. addresses the challenge of interpreting black-box neural network models, particularly in the context of tabular data. The authors introduce a novel architecture called Self-Attention Networks (SANs) to estimate feature importance, leveraging the attention mechanism that has gained traction in natural language processing.

The study compares the feature importance estimates produced by SANs with established methods such as ReliefF, Mutual Information, and Random Forest-based approaches. The authors conduct extensive empirical evaluations across ten real and synthetic datasets, demonstrating that SANs align well with the high-ranked features identified by traditional methods. Moreover, SANs are shown to uncover feature interactions that can enhance predictive performance.

The paper's contributions include the introduction of the SAN architecture, a comprehensive evaluation against established feature ranking methods, direct comparisons of feature importance estimates, and a theoretical analysis of SAN properties regarding space and time complexity. The findings suggest that attention mechanisms can effectively extend beyond key feature interactions to identify larger relevant feature subsets, providing a promising avenue for model interpretability in deep learning applications.
