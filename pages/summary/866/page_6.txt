The paper discusses the performance of Self-Attention Networks (SANs) in feature importance estimation, comparing them to various algorithms such as Mutual Information, Random Forests, and ReliefF. The analysis reveals that attention-based importances, specifically SANs, exhibit high similarity to each other and to the ReliefF algorithm. The results are presented through graphs demonstrating the ranking similarities among these approaches, with higher values indicating greater similarity.

Key differences highlighted include the ability of SANs to maintain a consistent mapping between input features and their meanings, unlike traditional attention mechanisms used in text processing. The authors empirically validate that SANs can produce attention vectors that yield comparable feature rankings relevant for classifiers like Logistic Regression. They suggest that further improvements in SAN performance could be achieved by enhancing the underlying neural network architectures and exploring various attention mechanisms.

The paper also introduces the FUJI score, which facilitates a comparative analysis of the feature importance assessment methods. Initial findings suggest that SANs are akin to Genie3 and ReliefF, hinting at a non-myopic feature importance computation. The authors acknowledge that further validation of this claim is necessary and outline plans for future work, including comparisons with the recently developed TabNet.

Additionally, the code used for reproducing the results is made available for academic users, promoting transparency and collaboration within the research community.
