The paper introduces the Attention Branch Network (ABN), a model designed to enhance visual explanation and improve the performance of deep convolutional neural networks (CNNs) in image recognition tasks. While existing CNNs excel in various tasks, understanding their decision-making processes remains challenging. Visual explanations, particularly attention maps, help interpret these decisions by highlighting significant regions of an image that contribute to classification.

ABN builds on response-based visual explanation models by incorporating a branch structure that focuses on attention mechanisms. This allows for an end-to-end training approach applicable across multiple image recognition tasks, including image classification, fine-grained recognition, and facial attribute recognition. The authors present experimental results demonstrating that ABN outperforms baseline models while effectively generating attention maps for visual explanations.

The paper also discusses the limitations of existing visual explanation techniques, particularly gradient-based methods that require backpropagation and modifications to the CNN architecture. In contrast, response-based methods like Class Activation Mapping (CAM) provide insights during inference but may compromise CNN performance due to architectural changes. ABN addresses these issues by maintaining performance while offering clear visual explanations, thereby contributing both to interpretability and efficacy in CNN applications.
