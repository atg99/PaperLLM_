The paper introduces the Attention Branch Network (ABN), a model designed to enhance the performance of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) through the integration of a response-based visual explanation model with an attention mechanism. The key innovation lies in the branch structure of ABN, which consists of three components: a feature extractor, an attention branch, and a perception branch. The feature extractor utilizes convolutional layers to generate feature maps, while the attention branch employs an attention mechanism to produce an attention map that aids in visual explanations. The perception branch utilizes both feature and attention maps to output class probabilities.

ABN is significant as it marks the first effort to improve CNN performance by incorporating visual explanations. It can be adapted to various baseline models, including VGGNet and ResNet, allowing for the generalization of attention maps across different networks and image recognition tasks. The model's ability to visualize attention maps during the forward propagation process enhances interpretability while simultaneously boosting CNN performance.

The paper also reviews existing methods for interpreting CNNs, categorizing them into gradient-based and response-based visual explanations. Gradient-based methods, such as SmoothGrad and Grad-CAM, rely on backpropagation to generate attention maps, while response-based methods, like Class Activation Mapping (CAM), visualize attention directly from feedforward responses. The authors argue that ABN, by extending CAM, effectively combines visual explanation with attention mechanisms without requiring backpropagation, thus making it compatible with various applications in image recognition.

Overall, the contributions of the paper include the development of ABN, its applicability to multiple baseline models, and the simultaneous enhancement of CNN performance and interpretability through the use of attention maps.
