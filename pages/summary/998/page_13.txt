This paper discusses the performance of algorithms in estimating age and gender from images, specifically focusing on the InsightFace model and its comparison with the DEX model. The results indicate a strong correlation (Pearson r = 0.973) in the estimated counts of persons detected by both models, which suggests a reliable global person prevalence rate of approximately 7.6 x 10^-3%. However, there is a notable weaker correlation observed in gender estimates (Pearson r = 0.723) and mean age estimates (Pearson r = 0.567). The authors highlight that these discrepancies suggest that even advanced algorithms may struggle with demographic shifts in datasets like ImageNet, raising concerns about their reliability and validity. 

The paper also addresses the ethical implications of gender classification, arguing against its scientific merit and emphasizing the moral issues associated with such tasks. Additionally, it references previous findings that identified misogynistic content within the ImageNet dataset, particularly in categories related to voyeurism and pornography, underscoring the need for careful consideration in dataset curation and algorithmic applications.
