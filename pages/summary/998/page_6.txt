The paper discusses the implications of using convolutional neural networks (CNNs) in image classification, particularly concerning dataset leakage and the perpetuation of harmful stereotypes. It argues that CNNs, while powerful, can inadvertently extract subsets of facial images, leading to potential dataset leakage. Furthermore, the act of classifying and labeling images grants classifiers the authority to define norms, often reinforcing dominant social ideologies that marginalize certain groups. This classification practice embeds historical biases, amplifying stereotypes and causing harm to already marginalized individuals.

The authors highlight that challenges such as bias and discrimination in AI systems are deeply rooted in social and cultural contexts, making straightforward solutions elusive. They caution against attempts to eliminate biases entirely, suggesting that such efforts may merely conceal them. Instead, they propose a "remove, replace, and open" strategy to address specific harms in datasets. This includes removing offensive labels from datasets like Tiny Images and ImageNet and replacing non-consensual images with those that are consensually sourced.

Additionally, the paper advocates for making large-scale visual datasets (LSVDs) open and accessible to allow for audits, promoting accountability in the scientific process. The authors emphasize that while no quick fix exists for the broader social issues at play, targeted remedies can contribute to long-term improvements.
