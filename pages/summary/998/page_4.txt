The paper discusses the ethical implications and societal threats associated with dataset curation practices in machine learning and artificial intelligence, particularly highlighting issues arising from large image datasets like the 80 Million Tiny Images dataset. The authors emphasize the urgent need for the ML and AI community to adopt more responsible practices, including the establishment of Institutional Review Boards (IRBs) for dataset curation.

One significant concern raised is the rise of reverse image search engines, which can compromise the privacy of individuals, especially those from vulnerable and marginalized groups. The authors illustrate this threat by noting that such technologies can facilitate the identification of individuals in sensitive contexts, such as sex work, leading to potential harm and exploitation. They cite examples of non-consensual image gathering and the risks posed by harmful online discourse, including revenge porn.

Furthermore, the paper critiques the trend toward increasingly larger and less transparent datasets. It argues that the legacy of datasets like ImageNet has encouraged the creation of even larger datasets without proper oversight, perpetuating risks and ethical dilemmas. The authors call for awareness and action within the community to address these challenges and mitigate potential harms associated with dataset use.
