The paper discusses the ethical implications of large-scale datasets in machine learning, particularly focusing on ImageNet. It highlights the challenges posed by non-consensual images and the complexities involved in categorizing individuals based on such datasets. While acknowledging the significance of ImageNet in advancing computer vision, the authors express concern over the lack of proactive ethical considerations during its creation and the broader implications for society.

The research emphasizes the need for the machine learning community to engage in critical discussions about the societal impact of their work, particularly on vulnerable populations. It suggests that ethical issues have often been treated as secondary, leading to systemic problems such as ethics shopping and bluewashing. To address these challenges, the authors propose solutions like audit cards and the development of meta-datasets for quantitative auditing, advocating for a culture shift that prioritizes justice and welfare.

The conclusion stresses the importance of awareness regarding the historical and political contexts of dataset curation, urging the establishment of Institutional Review Boards (IRBs) for large-scale projects. The paper aims to raise awareness about the necessity of justice-centered practices in machine learning, fostering a responsible approach to dataset usage and curation.
