In this paper, the authors Vinay Uday Prabhu and Abeba Birhane critically examine the ethical implications of large-scale vision datasets, particularly focusing on the ImageNet-ILSVRC-2012 dataset. They highlight significant issues related to consent, justice, and the presence of harmful content, including pornographic images, within these datasets. By conducting a quantitative analysis that considers factors such as age, gender, and content scoring, they reveal extensive ethical transgressions in dataset curation practices.

The authors emphasize the historical context of informed consent, tracing its roots back to post-World War II ethical codes, and argue that current practices in the era of Big Data often neglect these fundamental principles. They point out that many images used in datasets are collected without consent or proper ethical oversight, leading to privacy violations and potential harm to individuals and marginalized groups.

Through their research, the authors curate a look-up table of problematic images in ImageNet and discuss the broader societal risks posed by uncritical dataset practices. They propose the establishment of mandatory Institutional Review Boards (IRBs) for dataset curation processes as a corrective measure. The paper calls for greater accountability and ethical considerations in the development of computer vision technologies, asserting that the perceived benefits of large datasets come at a significant ethical cost. The authors have made their findings and associated resources available to the computer vision community to encourage further exploration and improvement in dataset ethics.
