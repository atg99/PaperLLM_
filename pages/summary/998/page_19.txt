This paper addresses the challenges and ethical considerations surrounding the curation of large-scale image datasets, particularly in the context of computer vision. It emphasizes the need for a cultural shift within the community to prioritize ethical standards in dataset creation and usage. The authors acknowledge critiques received during their research, including the moral implications of using pre-trained gender classification models and the risks associated with deanonymizing individuals in datasets. They recognize the ongoing efforts of ImageNet curators to improve dataset transparency and accountability, while stressing the responsibility of all dataset curators due to the potential societal impacts of their work.

The authors advocate for a proactive approach to ethics in dataset curation, contrasting it with the reactive measures often taken after issues arise. They highlight the slow progress in making meaningful corrections to datasets over the years, suggesting that priorities in the community need realignment. Additionally, they raise concerns about the capability of Big Data practices to address the needs of marginalized communities, arguing that current data harvesting practices often perpetuate biases and fail to consider the historical context of vulnerable populations.

The paper concludes with a wish list for the future, hoping to inspire significant changes in how large datasets are curated and utilized, ultimately fostering a more ethical and inclusive approach in the field of artificial intelligence.
