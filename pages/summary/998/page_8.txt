The paper discusses several methods for mitigating ethical concerns associated with image datasets, particularly focusing on reverse image search engines, privacy techniques, and dataset curation practices. It highlights the potential for users to request the removal of identifiable images from reverse image search engines, thereby reducing immediate harm. The authors propose using differentially private obfuscation techniques, such as DP-Blur, to protect individual identities in images, suggesting that this method can be integrated into dataset curation to address predictive power changes.

The paper also explores the use of synthetic images in model training, including hand-drawn sketches and GAN-generated images, which may provide ethical advantages and enhance model performance. In terms of ethical curation, the authors argue for clearer guidelines during the dataset creation process to filter out ethically problematic images, advocating for the integration of ethics checks in future curation efforts.

Furthermore, the authors introduce the concept of dataset audit cards, akin to model cards, which would provide transparency regarding dataset goals, curation methods, and known issues. They detail a quantitative analysis of the ImageNet dataset, revealing significant ethical transgressions through metrics such as age, gender distribution, and NSFW content. This analysis led to the creation of an audit card that encapsulates the findings, emphasizing the importance of context in evaluating dataset ethics. The authors also caution against the ethical implications of using pre-trained models for gender classification, noting the risk of systemic abuse and scientific flaws in such practices.
