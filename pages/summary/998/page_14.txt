The paper discusses the methodology for generating a hand-labeled dataset of images, focusing on the intersection of digital criminology and intersectional feminism. A hybrid human-in-the-loop approach was employed, beginning with a subset of images filtered using a model-annotated NSFW-average score, derived from the NSFW-Mobilenet-v2 model. This model classifies images into categories such as drawings, hentai, neutral, porn, and sexy, allowing the calculation of mean NSFW scores across various image classes.

Scatter-plots were used to visualize correlations between mean NSFW scores and mean gender scores from the DEX model across 1000 ImageNet classes. The Affinity Propagation algorithm identified five natural clusters, indicating that classes with lower mean gender scores were predominantly female. Further analysis introduced age demographics, revealing that classes with the highest NSFW scores featured young women.

The resulting shortlist comprised approximately 7000 images, which were hand-labeled by volunteers, leading to a curated list of 61 images with unanimous class assignments. This list has been open-sourced to encourage scholarly engagement and provide resources for researchers in regions with stringent laws regarding explicit content. The paper emphasizes the importance of context in interpreting objectionable content, using examples to illustrate the nuanced boundaries between eroticism and pornography. Figures within the paper provide visual representations of class-wise counts and distributions of the curated images.
