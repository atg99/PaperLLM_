This paper discusses the implications of disseminating large-scale image datasets, particularly in relation to legal and ethical considerations regarding explicit content. It highlights that merely possessing certain digital images can lead to legal repercussions in various countries, emphasizing the need for clear caveats in dissemination documents.

The study also examines the relationship between the semanticity of class labels in the ILSVRC2012 dataset and their corresponding NSFW (Not Safe For Work) scores. By generating dense word-vector embeddings and applying dimensionality reduction techniques, the authors visualize the semantic dimensions and their association with NSFW scores. Notably, certain classes, such as "brassier," "bikini," and "maillot," exhibit higher NSFW scores, indicating a peak in the semantic space.

Furthermore, the paper explores co-occurrence-based gender biases present in image datasets, drawing parallels with existing biases in Natural Language Processing (NLP). It references previous works that have identified gender stereotypes in occupation-related words and investigates whether similar biases exist in the representation of individuals in images from the ImageNet dataset. The authors analyze the gender distribution in images featuring specific dog breeds and musical instruments, presenting their findings through conditional violin plots. This research underscores the influence of societal biases on datasets and the potential downstream effects on model training and representation learning.
