This paper discusses the intersection of gender biases in dog breed ownership and musical instrument selection, as well as the implications of using datasets containing images of infants in light of privacy concerns. The authors categorize dog breeds according to the American Kennel Club and analyze the gender distribution of individuals in relation to perceived masculinity of dog groups. Their findings indicate that breeds such as Non-Sporting, Working, and Herding exhibit a male co-occurrence bias, aligning with historical stereotypes of masculinity.

In the context of musical instruments, the paper highlights how certain instruments are stereotypically associated with femininity or masculinity, resulting in skewed gender representation within image datasets. Instruments like the cello and flute are shown to have a pro-women bias, while drums and brass instruments lean towards male representation.

The paper also raises concerns about the presence of images of infants in various image classes, noting the potential legal ramifications under the EU's GDPR, which emphasizes the need for specific protection of children's personal data. The authors advocate for collaboration with legal experts to address these issues.

Lastly, the paper introduces the concept of a "blood diamond effect" in the realm of generative art created using neural networks, cautioning against the ethical implications of training models on datasets that may include sensitive or problematic content. The authors call for a critical dialogue within the artistic community regarding these ethical dilemmas as the field of AI-generated art continues to expand.
