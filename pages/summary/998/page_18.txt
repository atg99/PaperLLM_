The paper presents an analysis of gender bias in image classification, specifically focusing on dog breeds and musical instruments, using data visualizations such as violin plots and gender skewness scores. The categorized violin plots illustrate mean gender scores across various dog-breed image groups, highlighting differences in representation between women-majority and men-majority categories.

Additionally, the paper discusses an error analysis that critiques the reliance on classification accuracy metrics within the computer vision community. The authors conducted an examination of class-wise top-5 accuracies using ResNet50 and NasNet models, particularly in classes with asymmetric human co-occurrence between training and validation sets. The findings indicate a statistically significant drop in accuracies for classes with the highest human-delta ratios, underscoring the necessity of addressing human representation in image data. This analysis serves as a call to action for researchers to consider gender bias and representation in their datasets and algorithms.
