The paper addresses ethical concerns surrounding the use of problematic seed images in training neural art models, particularly those that include pornographic, non-consensual, voyeuristic content, and underage nudity. It draws a parallel to the blood-diamond syndrome in jewelry, highlighting how the origins of training data can influence the final artistic output. 

An example discussed involves a neural artwork generated using the GanBreeder app, which was created from specific seed classes linked to inappropriate imagery, namely "Bikini" and "Brassiere." The authors conducted an online critique of the artwork and found that reviewers were unaware of the troubling origins of the seed images. The analysis revealed that many images in these classes contributed to a dataset that was ethically problematic due to their voyeuristic and non-consensual nature.

The paper emphasizes that the issue is not merely about the visual representation of female undergarments but rather about the ethical implications of the datasets used, which contain harmful content. The authors present data visualizations, including cross-categorical scatter plots and histograms, to illustrate the prevalence of these problematic image categories, reinforcing the argument for a more conscientious approach to dataset curation in machine learning.
