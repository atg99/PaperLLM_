The paper discusses the ethical implications and biases inherent in machine learning (ML) systems, particularly in the context of image classification and object detection. It highlights that these systems often display higher error rates for marginalized demographic groups, such as individuals with darker skin tones and transgender people, which can lead to tangible harm. The authors reference D’Ignazio and Klein's call for a shift in ethical considerations from a narrow focus on fairness to a broader understanding of justice, emphasizing the need for a critical examination of how data is sourced and labeled.

The paper critiques the curation process of datasets like ImageNet, which is built on the flawed WordNet taxonomy. It points out that many datasets inherit problematic structures and labels that perpetuate historical prejudices. The authors also highlight issues related to the labeling process, including the exploitation of gig workers involved in image annotation. Furthermore, they note the inadequacies of human annotation procedures, such as the limitation of assigning a single label per image, which does not reflect the complexity of real-world images.

Overall, the paper underscores the need for the ML community to address these ethical challenges and biases to create more just and equitable systems.
