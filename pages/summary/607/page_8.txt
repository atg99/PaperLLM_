The paper discusses the recognition of Arabic Sign Language (ArSL) using deep neural networks, specifically through a fine-tuning process applied to the VGG16 and ResNet152 models. The dataset utilized for training consisted of 25,600 images representing 32 classes of ArSL gestures. The dataset was divided into 80% for training and 20% for validation. 

During the training, 100 epochs were executed, with each epoch processing multiple batches of images while calculating the loss function via categorical cross-entropy. Stochastic Gradient Descent was employed for optimizing the model, with a learning rate of 0.0001 and a momentum of 0.9. The training process involved data augmentation and careful monitoring of training and validation loss and accuracy.

The VGG16 model achieved a validation accuracy of 99% by the 40th epoch and reached a peak accuracy of 99.45% at the 92nd epoch. The methodology involved under-sampling to balance class representation and systematically tracking performance metrics throughout the training process. Overall, the study demonstrates effective application of deep learning techniques for Arabic Sign Language recognition.
