The paper discusses the recognition of Arabic Sign Language using deep neural networks, specifically through fine-tuning pre-trained models such as VGG16 and ResNet152. The methodology begins with image augmentation techniques applied to the ArASL dataset to enhance the training data. These augmentations include rescaling, random horizontal flipping, random rotations, height and width shifts, and random zooming.

The dataset comprises a variety of gestures, each with a specified number of images. For instance, gestures like AIN and FA have 2014 and 1955 images, respectively, among others.

The fine-tuning process involves adapting the chosen models to work with the smaller dataset by loading pre-trained weights into the model layers. This approach not only optimizes performance but also reduces the number of epochs required for training. The final step of the process includes adding a softmax classification layer to facilitate the recognition of Arabic Sign Language gestures effectively. The paper illustrates the fine-tuning steps, emphasizing the efficiency and effectiveness of using established models for this specific application.
