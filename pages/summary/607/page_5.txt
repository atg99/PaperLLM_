The paper focuses on the recognition of Arabic Sign Language using deep neural networks, specifically through fine-tuning. It discusses the utilization of datasets comprising static images of Bengali sign language, which include 37 gestures and achieve an accuracy of 96.33%. 

In the proposed procedure, the authors outline the neural network models used, detailing data collection, under sampling, augmentation, and the fine-tuning processes. Two prominent convolutional neural network (CNN) architectures are highlighted: VGGNet and Residual Network (ResNet). 

VGGNet, which won the ImageNet Challenge in 2014, is noted for its fewer layers compared to state-of-the-art models while still attaining a 7.3% error rate in overall accuracy. Its architecture is widely available for various deep learning frameworks, making it a popular choice among researchers. 

ResNet, introduced a year later, features variations in depth ranging from 32 to 152 layers, demonstrating that increased depth correlates with improved accuracy. The architecture incorporates a residual function that enhances the model's capabilities even with larger and more complex networks, achieving a 3.57% error rate on the ImageNet test set and securing first place in the 2015 ImageNet competition. 

Overall, the paper emphasizes the effectiveness of these CNN models in advancing the recognition of Arabic Sign Language through innovative deep learning techniques.
