In the paper by Shi et al., the authors discuss the challenges of determining the optimal number of clusters in clustering algorithms, particularly highlighting the limitations of existing methods like hierarchical clustering and fuzzy clustering, which require a preset number of clusters. The paper emphasizes that estimating the potential optimal cluster number is crucial for effective clustering. 

The Elbow method is introduced as a traditional technique for this purpose, which involves performing K-means clustering over a range of cluster numbers and plotting the sum of squared errors (SSE) to visually identify an "elbow" point. However, the authors note that this method can be subjective and may not always provide a clear solution, especially when the SSE curve is smooth.

To address these shortcomings, the authors propose a quantitative discriminant method that enhances the Elbow method by providing a straightforward value for the estimated optimal cluster number. This new approach incorporates K-means++, MinMaxScaler for normalization, and the cosine of the interaction angle of the elbow as criteria for determining the cluster number.

The paper is structured as follows: Section 2 offers a review of related work, Section 3 details the simulated and benchmark datasets used to validate the proposed method, and Section 4 concludes the findings. Overall, the authors aim to improve the accuracy and reliability of determining the optimal cluster number in clustering analysis.
