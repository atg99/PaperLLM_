The paper presents a quantitative discriminant method for determining the optimal number of clusters in clustering algorithms, addressing a common challenge in machine learning. Clustering is essential for statistical data analysis but typically requires a predetermined number of clusters, which can be difficult to ascertain without prior knowledge of the dataset. The Elbow method, a popular technique for identifying the optimal cluster number, often relies on manual identification of elbow points from visual curves, which can be problematic when the curves are smooth.

To improve this process, the authors propose a new method that utilizes a statistical metric to estimate the optimal cluster number. The approach involves normalizing the average degree of distortion derived from the Elbow method to a range of 0 to 10. It then calculates the cosine of intersection angles between elbow points and uses the arccosine theorem to compute these angles. The minimal intersection angles are identified as the estimated optimal cluster number.

Experimental results on simulated datasets and the Iris Dataset indicate that this new method outperforms the widely used Silhouette method in estimating the optimal number of clusters. The findings underscore the effectiveness of the proposed approach in enhancing the accuracy of cluster number determination in data analysis.
