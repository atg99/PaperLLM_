The paper presents a new method for determining the optimal number of clusters in datasets, comparing its effectiveness to the traditional Silhouette method. The authors demonstrate that their proposed method accurately identifies the correct cluster number in various datasets, including one with mixed uniform and multivariate normal distributions, where the Silhouette method fails. 

The study uses the well-known Iris Dataset, splitting it into sepal and petal subsets to evaluate clustering performance. Results indicate that the proposed method consistently identifies the actual number of clusters, showing a clear minimum angle at the correct index, while the Silhouette method does not provide a definitive maximum silhouette coefficient. Execution times also reveal that the proposed method is faster than the Silhouette method for the experimental datasets.

In conclusion, the paper critiques the Elbow method for its subjective nature and introduces a new approach based on the interaction angle of adjacent elbow points. The experimental results validate that the proposed method not only aligns with the true cluster number but also outperforms the Silhouette method, suggesting its potential as a more reliable tool in clustering analysis.
