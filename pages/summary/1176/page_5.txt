In this section of the paper, Shi et al. outline a method for determining the optimal number of clusters, denoted as Kopt, in a given dataset using a combination of geometric and statistical techniques. They begin by normalizing the sum of squared errors (SSE) across a defined range [0-10] for the dataset, which is represented in a two-dimensional space defined by points i and j. The Euclidean distance between these points is calculated using a specified formula.

The authors introduce the concept of angle ∠αj formed by three adjacent data points and derive its value through another formula. The smallest angle in the set α is identified as the optimal elbow point, which signifies the estimated ideal number of clusters, Kopt, for the dataset.

The implementation process consists of two main steps: first, initializing the range for Kopt, bounded by Kmin and Kmax; second, calculating the SSE, mean distortion, and normalized values for each k within this range. The algorithm detailed in Algorithm 1 includes steps for fitting the dataset to the K-means model, training it, and computing the normalized mean distortion for each k value.

This methodology emphasizes the systematic approach to identifying the optimal clustering configuration by leveraging geometric relationships and error metrics, providing a structured framework for analysis in clustering tasks.
