Third, we extend our findings to the Minimax-DQN algorithm, which adapts DQN for two-player zero-sum Markov games. We analyze how this approach, which computes target values through Nash equilibrium rather than action maximization, still aligns with the Bellman operator's application to the Q-network. We quantify the suboptimality of the policies produced by Minimax-DQN, showcasing convergence rates that indicate the action-value function approaches the optimal solution with a bounded statistical error.

In summary, this paper offers a rigorous theoretical framework for understanding deep Q-learning algorithms, specifically DQN and its adaptations, contributing to both the theoretical foundations and practical applications in reinforcement learning.
