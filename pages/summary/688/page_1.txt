The paper presents a theoretical analysis of the Deep Q-Network (DQN) algorithm, highlighting its algorithmic and statistical properties. Despite DQN's empirical success, its theoretical foundations remain underexplored. The authors simplify DQN to capture its essential features and establish convergence rates for the action-value functions generated by the algorithm under mild assumptions. They differentiate between statistical error, which accounts for bias and variance from using deep neural networks, and algorithmic error, which converges geometrically to zero. 

The analysis validates the techniques of experience replay and target networks, pivotal for DQN's performance. Additionally, the authors introduce the Minimax-DQN algorithm for zero-sum Markov games, extending the analysis of DQN to quantify the differences between the policies produced and the Nash equilibrium regarding convergence rates. The study aims to provide a solid theoretical foundation for DQN, enhancing understanding of its mechanisms and implications in reinforcement learning.
