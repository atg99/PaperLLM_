This paper introduces Self-Attention Generative Adversarial Networks (SAGANs), which integrate a self-attention mechanism into the GAN framework to enhance image generation. The self-attention module effectively captures long-range dependencies, improving the model's ability to generate complex images. Visualization of attention maps demonstrates that SAGAN allocates attention based on color and texture similarity rather than merely spatial proximity. This results in better structural accuracy in generated images, as evidenced by the generation of dogs with distinctly separated legs.

SAGAN is compared with leading GAN models for class-conditional image generation on ImageNet, achieving superior performance metrics, including the highest Inception score and lower FID scores. The inclusion of self-attention allows SAGAN to excel in generating images with intricate geometric or structural patterns, such as goldfish and Saint Bernards, while showing less advantage in texture-dominated classes.

The paper concludes that SAGAN sets a new state-of-the-art in class-conditional image generation on ImageNet, attributing its success to the self-attention mechanism, spectral normalization for stable training, and TTUR for accelerated training of regularized discriminators.
