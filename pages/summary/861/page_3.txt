The paper introduces Self-Attention Generative Adversarial Networks (SAGAN), which enhance the traditional GAN framework by incorporating a self-attention mechanism. While conventional GAN models primarily rely on convolutional layers that process local information, SAGAN allows for the efficient modeling of long-range dependencies in images. This is achieved by adapting the non-local model to the GAN architecture, enabling both the generator and discriminator to consider relationships between distant spatial regions.

The self-attention module transforms image features from a previous hidden layer into two feature spaces to calculate attention scores. These scores determine how much the model focuses on different locations while synthesizing regions of the image. The output of the attention layer is then combined with the original input features, with a learnable scalar allowing the network to initially focus on local cues before gradually incorporating non-local information.

Training involves minimizing the hinge version of the adversarial loss for both the generator and discriminator. To stabilize the training process, the authors implement spectral normalization and endorse the two-timescale update rule, which helps manage learning rates in regularized discriminators.

Overall, SAGAN represents a significant advancement in GAN architecture by effectively addressing the challenges of long-range dependency modeling.
