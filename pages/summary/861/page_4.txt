In contrast, the models employing spectral normalization in both the generator and discriminator, especially when combined with the imbalanced learning rates (TTUR), exhibit significantly improved stability and performance. 

The experimental results demonstrate that the proposed methods effectively stabilize GAN training and allow for fewer discriminator updates per generator update, thus reducing computational costs. The evaluation metrics used include the Inception score (IS) and the Fréchet Inception distance (FID), which provide insights into the quality and realism of generated images. The findings indicate that while IS is useful for assessing class diversity, FID offers a more comprehensive measure of realism, correlating better with human evaluations.

Overall, the results from extensive experiments on the LSVRC2012 (ImageNet) dataset validate the effectiveness of spectral normalization for both the generator and discriminator, as well as the use of imbalanced learning rates, leading to more stable and efficient GAN training. These advancements contribute to the potential for improved performance in image generation tasks, as evidenced by comparisons with state-of-the-art methods.
