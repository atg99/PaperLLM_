The proposed stabilization techniques, including spectral normalization and two-timescale learning rates, significantly enhance the stability and quality of the generated images. The model can effectively utilize 1:1 balanced updates without the need for adjusting update ratios, leading to improved convergence speed.

Training results indicate that while the quality of generated samples may not always improve steadily, the application of these stabilization techniques results in consistent sample quality throughout extended training periods. Specifically, the introduction of self-attention at higher feature map levels yields superior performance, as evidenced by improved Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) scores. This suggests that self-attention mechanisms are particularly effective in capturing long-range dependencies in image features, enhancing the capabilities of both the generator and discriminator. Overall, the findings underscore the effectiveness of the proposed methods in advancing GAN training and improving generated image quality.
