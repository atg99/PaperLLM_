This approach allows for attention-driven modeling of long-range dependencies in image generation, addressing limitations of previous GANs that primarily relied on local spatial information.

The authors highlight that while convolutional GANs excel at generating textures, they struggle with capturing geometric or structural patterns in certain image classes. This is attributed to the local nature of convolutional operations, which can hinder the effective modeling of long-range dependencies. By integrating self-attention, SAGAN can consider all feature locations simultaneously, enabling the generator and discriminator to work more cohesively across the entire image.

Additionally, the paper discusses the impact of generator conditioning on GAN performance, noting that the use of spectral normalization improves training dynamics. The results demonstrate that SAGAN significantly outperforms previous models, achieving an Inception score of 52.52 and reducing the Fréchet Inception distance to 18.65 on the ImageNet dataset. Visualizations reveal that the attention layers effectively focus on object shapes rather than fixed local regions, further enhancing the model's generative capabilities. Overall, SAGAN represents a notable advancement in image synthesis, effectively balancing the need for detailed local features and long-range dependencies.
