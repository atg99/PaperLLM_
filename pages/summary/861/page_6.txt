Self-Attention Generative Adversarial Networks (SAGAN) introduce a self-attention mechanism to enhance the performance of GANs. The results show significant improvements in Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) and Inception Score (IS) when using self-attention compared to traditional residual blocks. For instance, with 1 million iterations, the model utilizing self-attention achieves an FID of 22.96, outperforming residual blocks across various configurations.

The study highlights that self-attention allows the network to focus on relevant features based on color and texture rather than merely spatial proximity. This capability enables the generation of coherent images, where attention is distributed in a way that maintains consistency across the image elements, as illustrated in the provided figures. The findings underscore that the advantages of SAGAN come not just from increased depth but from the effective learning of long-range dependencies that standard convolutional approaches cannot capture. 

Overall, the paper demonstrates that integrating self-attention into GANs leads to improved image generation quality, as evidenced by both quantitative metrics and qualitative visualizations of attention allocation during the image generation process.
