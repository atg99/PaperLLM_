The paper presents significant advancements in dense text detection by introducing a mask decoder based on a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) and an Instance-Aware Mask Learning (IAML) approach. The MLP mask decoder demonstrates a 3.5% improvement in performance, addressing learning confusion and enhancing robustness. In contrast, IAML shows a decrease in performance when decoupled from proposal boxes, as it relies heavily on bounding box accuracy. The proposed method, denoted as MAYOR, outperforms the Mask R-CNN baseline by 5.1% in F-measure on the DAST1500 dataset, with an additional 0.7% improvement when using IAML. 

Experiments conducted on the RotDAST dataset further validate the effectiveness of the IAML approach, particularly under varying rotation angles. The results indicate that MAYOR maintains robust performance, achieving notable improvements in F-measure compared to Mask R-CNN, especially at higher rotation angles.

The paper also explores the impact of hyper-parameter k in the adaptive label assignment (ALA) in Region Proposal Network (RPN), revealing that k values are robust across different settings. The use of ALA allows for a reduction in aspect ratios, enhancing the flexibility of anchor design. Additionally, the combination of localization and objectness losses yields optimal performance, with objectness loss alone still achieving a commendable F-measure.

Overall, the findings underscore the robustness and effectiveness of the proposed MLP mask decoder and IAML in improving dense text detection capabilities.
