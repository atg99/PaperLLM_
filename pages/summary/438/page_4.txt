The paper discusses the challenges faced by Mask R-CNN when dealing with dense text instances, particularly the learning confusion issue that arises during mask learning. This issue occurs when two close text instances, denoted as A and B, generate similar feature vectors in the Region of Interest (RoI) feature representation, leading to mislabeling during training. Specifically, when training with features from instance A, the closest feature to a reference point in A is treated as positive while the closest feature in B is treated as negative, resulting in conflicting labels that degrade performance.

To address this, the authors propose modifications to the mask decoder architecture. They introduce two new variants: the locally connected (LC) predictor and the fully connected (FC) predictor, both of which eliminate weight sharing to mitigate the learning confusion. The paper provides empirical evidence showing that the LC predictor significantly outperforms the traditional convolution-based decoder, and the FC predictor further improves performance by utilizing more contextual information.

The proposed MLP mask decoder enhances robustness against the learning confusion issue, but it still persists in standard pixel-aligned mask learning. To resolve this, the authors introduce instance-aware mask learning (IAML), which focuses on learning a global representation of the mask distribution rather than pixel-wise classification. This approach resembles bounding box regression, allowing for a more detailed mask representation. The paper concludes that IAML, combined with the MLP decoder, provides a more effective solution for mask learning in dense text scenarios.
