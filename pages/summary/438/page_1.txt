The paper "Mask is All You Need: Rethinking Mask R-CNN for Dense and Arbitrary-Shaped Scene Text Detection" by Xugong Qin et al. addresses challenges in using Mask R-CNN for scene text detection, particularly in dense text scenarios where multiple instances may overlap. The authors identify two key issues: the learning confusion in the mask head and the complexity of anchor settings in the region proposal network (RPN) due to variations in text size and shape.

To tackle the first issue, the authors propose replacing the existing "deconv-conv" decoder in the mask head with a multi-layer perceptron (MLP) decoder, enhancing the model's robustness. They introduce instance-aware mask learning, which allows the mask head to predict the shape of entire instances rather than classifying individual pixels, leading to more accurate and compact mask predictions.

For the second issue, the authors suggest an adaptive label assignment strategy that ensures all instances, especially those with extreme aspect ratios, are adequately associated with anchors, simplifying the anchor configuration process across different datasets.

The proposed method, named MAYOR, demonstrates state-of-the-art performance on five benchmarks, including DAST1500 and MSRA-TD500, showcasing its effectiveness in handling dense and arbitrarily shaped text detection in scenes.
