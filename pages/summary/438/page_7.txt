The paper presents a comparative analysis of various text detection methods on several datasets, including ICDAR2015, CTW1500, and Total-Text, with a focus on the performance of the proposed method called MAYOR. The results are organized in tables that detail precision, recall, F-measure, and frames per second (FPS) for each method.

MAYOR demonstrates significant effectiveness in detecting dense and arbitrary-shaped texts, achieving state-of-the-art results on the DAST1500 benchmark with recall, precision, and F-measure scores of 85.5%, 87.8%, and 86.6%, respectively, without utilizing external data. This performance surpasses that of ReLaText, despite the latter's use of advanced graph convolutional networks and additional pretraining data.

In evaluations on multi-oriented text, MAYOR outperforms existing state-of-the-art methods on the MSRA-TD500 and ICDAR2015 benchmarks, achieving F-measure scores of 85.2%, 91.7%, and 88.3% without external data. The method shows particular strength in refining proposals through global modeling, reducing localization errors.

When external datasets are incorporated, MAYOR achieves even higher performance, with F-measure scores of 89.2% and 89.3% on ICDAR2015, further confirming its capability in detecting multi-oriented texts effectively. Overall, the results indicate that MAYOR stands out among contemporary methods due to its top-down modeling approach and robust performance across various text detection challenges.
