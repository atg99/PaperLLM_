This paper introduces MAYOR, an advanced text detection framework addressing the challenges faced by Mask R-CNN in dense and arbitrary-shaped text detection. The study identifies two primary issues: the difficulty in managing dense text cases and the inadequacy of manually designed anchors for varying aspect ratios.

To tackle the first challenge, the authors highlight the learning confusion issue in the mask branch of Mask R-CNN. They propose two enhancements: the use of a multi-layer perceptron (MLP) decoder to replace the traditional "deconv-conv" architecture, which improves robustness, and the implementation of instance-aware mask learning (IAML) that focuses on predicting the overall shape of text instances instead of classifying pixels individually.

For the second challenge, the paper presents adaptive label assignment (ALA) in the region proposal network (RPN). This method involves a two-step process to increase the number of positive samples and ensures that high-quality samples are selected based on matching quality, thus improving performance without relying on complex anchor designs.

The contributions of the paper include an analysis of the learning confusion issue, the introduction of the MLP decoder, the instance-aware mask learning approach, and the adaptive label assignment method. The effectiveness of MAYOR is validated through experiments on multiple public datasets, demonstrating its capabilities in both scene text detection and general instance segmentation without the need for additional pretraining datasets, while also ensuring efficient inference times.
