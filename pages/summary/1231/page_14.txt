The study evaluates the performance of various algorithms in comparison to the default settings of the ranger algorithm. The findings indicate that while differences in performance are small, on average, all tested algorithms outperform the ranger default. The top-performing algorithm shows an average error rate improvement of 0.013 (MMCE) and 0.007 (AUC) over ranger. This aligns with previous research suggesting that random forest algorithms, including ranger, have limited tunability.

The tuneRanger methods consistently outperform ranger's default settings across all metrics. Specifically, tuning for specific measures yields the best results among the tuneRanger algorithms, although tuneRangerBrier achieves the highest average rank for error rate, not tuneRangerMMCE. Other methods like caret and tuneRF also perform better than ranger default, with the exception of logarithmic loss, but they are generally outperformed by tuneRanger methods.

mlrHyperopt shows competitive performance similar to tuneRanger but has a longer runtime, particularly for larger datasets, as it employs 10-fold cross-validation instead of the out-of-bag method used by tuneRanger. The analysis of average runtimes reveals that tuneRF is typically the fastest tuning algorithm, although there are exceptions.

The study further explores the impact of tuning only the mtry parameter compared to tuning mtry, node size, and sample size together. Results indicate that tuning all three parameters yields a notable average improvement in error rate (0.004), AUC (0.002), Brier score (0.010), and logarithmic loss (0.014). Thus, the findings suggest that tuneRanger is preferable for larger datasets due to its efficiency and effectiveness in performance improvement.
