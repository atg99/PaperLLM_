In this section, the paper discusses a benchmark study comparing various algorithms for random forest tuning. The package tuneRanger is utilized with default settings to optimize different performance measures, including Brier score, mean misclassification error (MMCE), AUC, and logarithmic loss. Additionally, the study examines the impact of tuning only the mtry parameter.

The study employs three other R packages, mlrHyperopt, caret, and tuneRF, with their default settings, although mlrHyperopt is excluded from performance measure variations due to expected long runtimes. The standard random forest algorithm implemented in the ranger package serves as a reference point, as it allows for faster execution through parallelization.

The benchmark utilizes datasets from OpenML, specifically the OpenML100 suite, focusing on binary classification datasets without missing values. A total of 39 datasets are classified into small and big categories based on estimated runtimes, resulting in 26 small and 13 big datasets. For small datasets, a 5-fold cross-validation is executed 10 times, while for big datasets, only a single 5-fold cross-validation is performed. Algorithms are compared based on average evaluation measures: MMCE, AUC, Brier score, and logarithmic loss.

The results section outlines the methodology for identifying outliers through boxplots and subsequently averages and ranks the performances of the algorithms compared: tuneRangerMMCE, tuneRangerAUC, tuneRangerBrier, tuneRangerLogloss, hyperopt, caret, tuneRF, and the ranger default.
