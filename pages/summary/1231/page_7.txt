The section discusses the tunability of the random forest (RF) algorithm, emphasizing that while it generally performs well with default settings, some performance improvement can be achieved through tuning. Probst et al. (2018) found that RF is less tunable than algorithms like support vector machines, but tuning can still yield modest gains, particularly in high-cost classification scenarios. The study identified that tuning the hyperparameter mtry resulted in the most significant average improvement in Area Under the Curve (AUC), followed by sample size, while node size had a minimal effect.

For evaluating the performance of RF, k-fold cross-validation is a common strategy, though using out-of-bag observations is recommended for its efficiency, especially with large datasets. This method generally provides reliable performance approximations, although it may have biases in specific scenarios, such as very small datasets.

The section also outlines various tuning search strategies. Basic methods like random search and grid search specify candidate hyperparameter values upfront, while more advanced techniques, including F-Race and sequential model-based optimization (SMBO), adaptively select hyperparameters based on previous evaluations. SMBO is highlighted as a key approach used in subsequent software implementations. Overall, the tuning of RF hyperparameters can enhance performance, and the choice of evaluation and search strategies plays a critical role in this process.
