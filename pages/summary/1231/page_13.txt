The paper presents an analysis of the performance differences of various models compared to the default ranger model, as illustrated through boxplots. Key metrics evaluated include error rate, AUC, Brier score, and logarithmic loss, with the performance being assessed both with and without outliers.

For the error rate, lower values indicate better performance, while higher values are preferable for AUC. The boxplots reveal that the tuned models exhibit varying degrees of performance improvements or declines across these metrics. Specifically, the error rates demonstrate a slight improvement in some models when outliers are excluded.

The Brier score and logarithmic loss also favor lower values for optimal performance, and the results show that the models generally perform better after excluding outliers. The AUC, which benefits from higher values, reflects a similar trend in performance enhancement.

Overall, the findings suggest that tuning models can lead to improved performance metrics, particularly when outliers are removed from the analysis. The paper emphasizes the importance of considering outliers in model evaluation to obtain a clearer picture of performance.
