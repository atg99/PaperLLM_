Subspace-based adaptation focuses on finding a common intermediate representation that can bridge the gap between different domains. This approach typically involves creating low-dimensional representations for both source and target data, often utilizing dimensionality reduction techniques like principal component analysis (PCA). These representations are then analyzed on a low-dimensional Grassmann manifold, where the distance between subspaces indicates the domain shift. Techniques such as Sampling Geodesic Flow (SGF) involve determining a geodesic path between the source and target points on this manifold, sampling various subspaces along this path, and projecting data from both domains onto these sampled subspaces to facilitate classification.

Geodesic Flow Kernel (GFK) enhances SGF by integrating an infinite number of subspaces to characterize the transition between domains more effectively. Subspace alignment (SA) directly reduces discrepancies by learning a linear mapping function that aligns subspace bases, while Subspace Distribution Alignment (SDA) extends SA by aligning both the bases and the distributions of the subspaces.

Transformation-based methods, on the other hand, focus on transforming original features into a new representation that minimizes discrepancies in both marginal and conditional distributions while maintaining the underlying data structure. Techniques like Transfer Component Adaptation (TCA) and Joint Domain Adaptation (JDA) utilize metrics such as Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) and Kullback-Leibler Divergence to measure dissimilarities across domains. JDA further refines TCA by aligning both marginal and conditional distributions, utilizing dimensionality reduction techniques and pseudo labels when labeled data is unavailable in the target domain.

Reconstruction-based methods aim to address domain discrepancies through sample reconstruction in an intermediate representation. The Robust Visual Domain Adaptation with Low-rank Reconstruction (RDALR) method, for example, learns a linear projection matrix to transform source samples into a form that aligns with target domain samples, thereby reducing domain shifts effectively.
