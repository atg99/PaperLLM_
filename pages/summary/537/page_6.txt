The paper presents a study on the challenges posed by imbalanced datasets in healthcare, particularly in the context of classifying electrocardiogram (ECG) signals and human activity recognition (HAR). It highlights the issues of rare classes, such as atrial flutter and AV junctional rhythm, which constitute less than 0.1% of measurements, motivating the use of unsupervised representation learning techniques.

The dataset utilized for ECG classification features approximately 5 million data points from a limited number of individuals, showcasing the effectiveness of the proposed framework even in data-scarce scenarios. A novel encoder architecture, a 2-channel 1-dimensional strided convolutional neural network, is employed to handle high-frequency ECG measurements, consisting of six convolutional layers with a down-sampling factor of 16 and a window size of 2500 samples, resulting in a 64-dimensional representation.

For HAR, the study employs a dataset from the UCI Machine Learning Repository, containing accelerometer and gyroscope data from 30 individuals performing six activities. The data is pre-processed to extract 561 features, and activity samples are concatenated to create a full-time series for each subject. A single-layer RNN encoder processes the data with a window size of 4, capturing approximately 15 seconds of activity and encoding it in a 10-dimensional vector space.

Results focus on the clusterability of latent representations and downstream classification performance across all datasets. The evaluation of clusterability demonstrates how well the framework learns and encodes representations, with the expectation that signals from the same underlying state will cluster together. Visualizations using t-SNE reveal that representations learned via the proposed method outperform alternative approaches, particularly in separating time series generated by complex models. Cluster validity indices, including the Silhouette score and Davies-Bouldin index, further support the effectiveness of the representation learning framework in achieving coherent clusters in the encoding space.
