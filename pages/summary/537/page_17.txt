The paper presents a study on human activity recognition (HAR) signal representations using T-SNE visualizations for various baseline methods, including Triplet Loss and CPC. The visualizations illustrate how different algorithms encode time series data, with each point representing a 10-dimensional encoding of a signal window. The study includes two sets of experiments: one with a larger encoding size and another with a smaller size to analyze the impact of dimensionality on representation.

Figures A.4 and A.5 demonstrate the encoding trajectories of the HAR signals, highlighting the relationship between the original time series and their encodings across different latent states. Additionally, the paper explores a simulation experiment with a reduced encoding size of 3 dimensions, illustrating how the underlying states of the signal are captured despite the limited dimensionality. The findings emphasize the effectiveness of dimensionality reduction techniques in representing complex time series data in HAR tasks.
