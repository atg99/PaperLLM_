The paper discusses the importance of accurately estimating the neighborhood range within the Temporal Neighborhood Contrastive (TNC) framework, emphasizing the balance required to avoid overlap of information and ensure distinct underlying states are recognized. It introduces the use of the Augmented Dickey-Fuller (ADF) test for automatic adjustment of neighborhood size based on signal behavior. 

The authors argue that while windows within a neighborhood share similar properties, signals outside this region can act as negative samples in contrastive learning. However, this can lead to sampling bias, where negative examples may inadvertently resemble the reference, thus affecting learning performance. To mitigate this bias, the paper proposes treating samples outside the neighborhood as unlabeled rather than strictly negative, employing concepts from Positive-Unlabeled (PU) learning to refine the loss function.

In PU learning, a classifier is developed using labeled positive data and a mixture of unlabeled data, which may contain both positive and negative samples. The paper outlines two approaches to handling unlabeled data: one that identifies negative samples and another that assigns lower weights to unlabeled samples. The latter approach, as detailed by Elkan and Noto, involves assigning weights to the unlabeled samples based on their likelihood of being positive, facilitating an unbiased classifier.

The TNC framework incorporates these weight adjustments into its loss function, accounting for potential positive samples in the non-neighboring distribution. The encoder aims to distinguish between representations of neighborhood samples and those from outside. The TNC consists of an encoder that maps the windows to a lower-dimensional representation and a discriminator that evaluates the probability of two samples originating from the same temporal neighborhood. This dual-component structure is designed to ensure that the encoder effectively preserves neighborhood properties in the encoding space, enhancing the overall performance of the learning framework.
