The paper discusses the challenges and advancements in unsupervised representation learning for time series data, particularly in the healthcare domain. It highlights that, while significant progress has been made in other fields like vision and natural language processing, time series learning remains underexplored. Early methods included auto-encoders and seq-to-seq models, which focused on reconstructing input signals. However, these methods struggle with complex time series, prompting the development of alternative approaches such as Contrastive Predictive Coding and Time Contrastive Learning, which predict future data points without full reconstruction.

The paper introduces a novel framework called Temporal Neighborhood Coding (TNC), aimed at learning the dynamics of non-stationary signals and modeling temporal progression. TNC is particularly relevant in healthcare, where understanding patient transitions between clinical states is critical but often lacks labeled data. The authors demonstrate that TNC produces generalizable representations applicable to various tasks, including classification and clustering, and can integrate with different encoder architectures. Additionally, TNC's representations can support other applications like anomaly detection, which is challenging in supervised settings with sparse labels.
