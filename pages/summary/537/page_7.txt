This paper presents a comparative analysis of different representation learning methods for time series data, specifically focusing on TNC, CPC, and Triplet Loss. The study utilizes T-SNE visualizations to illustrate the clustering of signal representations across various datasets, highlighting the effectiveness of TNC in distinguishing latent dynamics. 

The clustering quality is assessed through Silhouette and DBI scores, with results showing that TNC consistently outperforms other methods across multiple datasets, including simulated ECG waveforms and Human Activity Recognition (HAR). CPC shows competitive performance in certain contexts, notably in HAR, while Triplet Loss struggles in differentiating similar states in non-stationary signals. K-means clustering applied to the original time series generally yields poor results, although it performs better on structured waveforms like ECG.

Additionally, the paper evaluates the classification capabilities of the learned representations by training a linear classifier. The results indicate that TNC representations yield classification performance comparable to a fully supervised model, outperforming CPC and Triplet Loss in terms of prediction accuracy and area under the precision-recall curve (AUPRC). This suggests that TNC effectively captures relevant features from time series data, making it suitable for downstream classification tasks. Overall, the findings underscore the advantages of TNC in representation learning for time series analysis.
