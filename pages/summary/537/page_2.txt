The proposed Temporal Neighborhood Coding (TNC) framework leverages the local smoothness of time series signals to facilitate the learning of meaningful representations. By defining a temporal neighborhood for each time window, TNC captures the relationships between adjacent time points while ensuring that the learned representations reflect the underlying dynamics of the data. The method distinguishes between nearby and distant signals in the representation space, thus encoding temporal information effectively.

TNC's contributions include the introduction of a novel unsupervised learning approach tailored for non-stationary multivariate time series, the establishment of a temporal neighborhood concept with stationary properties, and the adaptation of Positive Unlabeled Learning techniques to mitigate biases in sampling negative examples. The framework has been evaluated across various datasets, demonstrating its effectiveness in generating transferable representations for downstream tasks such as classification and clustering. Notably, TNC outperforms existing unsupervised methods and approaches supervised techniques in classification performance, underscoring its potential applicability in clinical settings where labeled data is scarce.
