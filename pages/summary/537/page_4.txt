The paper presents the TNC framework, a general approach for learning representations in time series data using an unsupervised learning method. The framework involves an encoder that learns the distribution of sample windows from both neighboring and non-neighboring time points. The discriminator is trained to classify whether two samples are from the same neighborhood, outputting a probability close to 1 for neighboring samples and close to 0 for distant ones.

The objective function for this training is defined in Equation 1, which focuses on maximizing the likelihood estimation of the discriminator's predictions. The framework allows for flexibility in encoder selection, as it can utilize any parametric model suited for the time series characteristics. However, a more complex discriminator may complicate the interpretation of the latent space.

To define the neighborhood parameter, the paper employs the Augmented Dickey-Fuller (ADF) test to identify stationary regions in non-stationary time series data. The ADF test helps determine the neighborhood size around each sample window, ensuring that the selected range represents a region where the signal remains relatively stationary. This approach allows for adjusting the neighborhood size while keeping the window size constant throughout the experiment.
