The supplementary figures presented in this paper focus on the TNC framework's ability to encode high-dimensional ECG signals and its application to Human Activity Recognition (HAR) data. Figure A.3 illustrates the encoding trajectory of a single ECG sample, highlighting a notable change in representation around the 40-second mark due to an artifact. The analysis, supported by clinical expertise, indicates that increased activation of features 0-10 between 80 and 130 seconds correlates with a rise in heart rate, a potential indicator of arrhythmia detected by the TNC framework. 

Figure A.2 provides a T-SNE visualization of waveform signal representations across different unsupervised learning baselines, with color coding for arrhythmia classes, while Figures A.4 and A.5 extend this analysis to the HAR dataset, demonstrating the TNC framework's effectiveness in capturing distinct underlying states within the signal's latent representations. Overall, these visualizations underscore the framework's capability to discern meaningful patterns within complex clinical and activity data.
