The text discusses the mechanics of a feed-forward neural network and introduces recurrent neural networks (RNNs). It starts by defining the error signal for a hidden unit in a feed-forward network and outlines the process for adjusting weights until outputs fall within an acceptable range. 

The focus then shifts to RNNs, which are characterized by their dynamic nature and internal state at each time step, resulting from circular connections among neurons and self-feedback mechanisms. This structure allows RNNs to retain information from past events, effectively creating a memory for time series data.

The basic architecture of RNNs is presented, highlighting the Elman network, which resembles a three-layer neural network but incorporates context cells to store outputs from the hidden layer. These outputs are fed back into the hidden neurons, allowing for enhanced learning through standard error backpropagation. The comparison between feed-forward networks and Elman networks is illustrated in accompanying figures.
