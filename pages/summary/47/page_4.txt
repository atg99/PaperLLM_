The paper discusses the functioning of a perceptron, a basic unit of artificial neural networks. It defines the weighted input as z=Pn i=1Wix i and the state of the perceptron as s=z+bas. For the perceptron to activate, its state must surpass a specified threshold. Single perceptrons can represent several useful Boolean functions, including AND, OR, NAND, and NOR. However, they are restricted to learning only linearly separable functions, which are problems where classes can be separated by a hyperplane in an n-dimensional space.

To illustrate linear separability, the paper compares the OR and XOR functions using a two-dimensional graph. The OR function is linearly separable, as its outputs can be distinctly separated by a single line, whereas the XOR function cannot be separated in this manner. The weights of a trained perceptron will correspond to the line that separates the inputs. Overall, the paper emphasizes the limitations of single perceptrons in learning complex functions, necessitating the use of networks for non-linear problems.
