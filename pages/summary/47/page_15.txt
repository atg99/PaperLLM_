The paper discusses methods for adjusting weights in recurrent neural networks, focusing on two algorithms: Backpropagation Through Time (BPTT) and Real-Time Recurrent Learning (RTRL). 

In BPTT, the error signal for a non-input unit is defined as the gradient of the total error with respect to the unit's activation. The weights are updated by summing the gradients over all time steps from a specified time t0 to t. The paper emphasizes that this method involves unrolling the error signal over time, which allows for effective weight updates based on past errors.

Conversely, RTRL operates without the need for error propagation across time, collecting all necessary gradient information as input is streamed to the network. While this approach eliminates the need for dedicated training intervals, it incurs a higher computational cost per update cycle. RTRL also requires storing non-local information, relying on the concept of output sensitivity. The training objective in RTRL is to minimize the overall network error at each time step, with the gradient of total error being a cumulative sum from previous steps.

Overall, both algorithms aim to optimize network performance but differ significantly in their computational strategies and requirements for error handling.
