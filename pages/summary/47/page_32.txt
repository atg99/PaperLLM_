The paper discusses the architecture of Gated Recurrent Units (GRUs), which include gating mechanisms known as the reset gate and the update gate. The reset gate's activation at time t+1 is defined by a squashing function applied to its state, which is influenced by biases and weighted inputs from previous inputs and hidden states. The update gate follows a similar structure, with its activation also determined by a squashing function based on its state and weighted inputs.

Both gates function as standard units in recurrent networks, but their unique characteristic lies in how the activations of GRU units are computed. Specifically, each GRU unit has an associated candidate activation that combines external inputs, the activation of the reset gate, gated recurrent connections from previous hidden states, and a bias term. This design allows GRUs to effectively manage information flow and maintain memory over time.
