The paper discusses the challenges of error propagation in recurrent neural networks, specifically focusing on the problems of vanishing and exploding gradients. These issues arise during the backpropagation process, where weight updates are calculated after training over a time interval. The update formula involves the total error gradient with respect to the weights, which is influenced by the backpropagated error signals from various neurons.

The analysis reveals that the error signal's propagation can lead to two distinct outcomes. If the scaling factor of the error exceeds one, the error can grow exponentially, resulting in oscillating weights and unstable learning. Conversely, if the scaling factor is less than one, the error diminishes exponentially, which can hinder the network's ability to learn effectively.

To illustrate this, the paper presents a mathematical formulation that describes the relationship between error signals across time steps in the network. It emphasizes that both scenarios—blowing up and vanishing errors—are detrimental to the learning process, making it critical to address these issues in the design of recurrent neural networks.
