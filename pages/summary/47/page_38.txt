The referenced papers primarily focus on the development and application of recurrent neural networks (RNNs) in various tasks related to speech and handwriting recognition. Key contributions include:

1. **Comparison of Network Types**: The work by Graves et al. (2003) compares spiking and differentiable RNNs for spoken digit recognition, highlighting the strengths and weaknesses of each approach.

2. **LSTM Networks**: Several papers (Graves et al., 2005; 2014) emphasize the use of Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks for rapid retraining on speech data and advancing towards end-to-end speech recognition, showcasing LSTM's effectiveness in handling sequential data.

3. **Connectionist Temporal Classification**: The introduction of connectionist temporal classification (Graves et al., 2006) allows for the labeling of unsegmented sequence data using RNNs, which is crucial for tasks where the alignment of input and output is not predefined.

4. **Multi-Dimensional RNNs**: Research on multi-dimensional RNNs (Graves et al., 2007) expands the capabilities of traditional RNNs by processing data in multiple dimensions, enhancing performance in complex recognition tasks.

5. **Hybrid Speech Recognition**: The hybrid systems combining deep bidirectional LSTMs (Graves et al., 2013) demonstrate improved performance in speech recognition by leveraging the strengths of both deep learning and traditional models.

6. **Handwriting Recognition**: Several studies (Graves et al., 2009; 2013) present novel systems for unconstrained handwriting recognition, with a focus on the application of RNNs to achieve high accuracy in real-world scenarios.

7. **Neural Turing Machines**: The concept of Neural Turing Machines (Graves et al., 2014) introduces a new architecture that combines neural networks with external memory, providing a framework for more complex computational tasks.

Overall, this body of work illustrates significant advancements in the application of RNNs and LSTMs in both speech and handwriting recognition, contributing to the broader field of artificial intelligence and machine learning.
