This section discusses training methods for recurrent neural networks (RNNs), focusing on two primary algorithms: Backpropagation Through Time (BPTT) and Real-Time Recurrent Learning (RTRL). BPTT is the more commonly used method, and it operates by unfolding the RNN over a finite time period into a feed-forward neural network (FFNN) structure. This unfolding allows for the application of standard backpropagation techniques to calculate weight updates.

In BPTT, the RNN is expanded temporally, resulting in multiple layers that correspond to each time step, all sharing the same weights. After running through a sequence, the network computes the error at the output layer and propagates this error backward through all time steps to update the weights based on the accumulated error deltas.

RTRL, in contrast, is an online learning algorithm that updates weights as data is collected, differing in how weight changes are computed compared to BPTT. The paper briefly outlines both methods due to their combined use in training Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) RNNs. The iterative nature of the backpropagation algorithm is emphasized, where errors are calculated for each unit across all time steps within a defined epoch, allowing for effective training of recurrent architectures.
