The paper discusses the relationship between output gates and memory blocks in a recurrent neural network, specifically focusing on the propagation of errors and weight contributions associated with these gates. It establishes that if the output gate of a memory block is equal to a certain value, the equality holds under the condition that the error is truncated to prevent excessive propagation through feedback connections. 

The individual error of a memory cell within a block is defined, taking into account the recurrent connections and the influence of the forget gate. The equations presented illustrate how the weight contributions for the output gates, memory cells, input gates, and forget gates are derived from the errors associated with these cells. The analysis emphasizes the importance of the recurrent connections in shaping the propagation of errors and the adjustments to the weights in response to these errors. 

The derivation of weight contributions relies on calculating partial derivatives with respect to the memory cell states and their associated weights. This approach highlights the interconnectedness of the gates and memory cells in the context of training recurrent networks, ensuring that the contributions from individual components are accurately reflected in the learning process.
