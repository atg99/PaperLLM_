The paper discusses the activation function of the GRU unit at time step t+1, defined by the equation yu(t+1) = yupdu(t+1)yu(t) + (1 - yupdu(t+1))eyu(t+1). This equation highlights similarities to LSTM's forget and input gates, suggesting a structural resemblance between GRU and LSTM architectures.

The paper also outlines significant applications of LSTM-RNNs across various domains. Early learning tasks demonstrated LSTM's capability to tackle complex problems such as recalling precise real numbers from noisy sequences, learning context-free languages, and tasks requiring timing and counting. Notably, LSTM was effectively utilized in meta-learning and reinforcement learning to address non-Markovian tasks with long-term dependencies.

In cognitive learning tasks, LSTM-RNNs excelled in applications like speech and handwriting recognition, machine translation, and emotion recognition from speech. The paper details the evolution of speech recognition capabilities, starting from early neural language modeling to the successful integration of LSTM and its bidirectional variant, BLSTM. The BLSTM-CTC variant surpassed traditional Hidden-Markov-Model systems, with further advancements leading to outstanding performance in large vocabulary speech recognition tasks when paired with hybrid architectures.
