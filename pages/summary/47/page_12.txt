The paper discusses various types of recurrent neural networks (RNNs), specifically focusing on Elman and Jordan networks. Elman networks feature context cells that receive input from the hidden layer, while Jordan networks receive input from the output layer. A partially recurrent neural network with self-feedback in the hidden layer is also described. 

Training RNNs differs from training feed-forward neural networks (FFNNs) due to the need to propagate information through recurrent connections across time steps. The primary learning algorithms for training RNNs in temporal, supervised tasks are backpropagation through time (BPTT) and real-time recurrent learning (RTRL). BPTT involves unfolding the network in time to form an FFNN, followed by applying the generalized delta rule for weight updates, functioning as an offline learning algorithm. In contrast, RTRL forwards propagates gradient information, allowing for online data collection during training.
