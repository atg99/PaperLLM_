The paper discusses the importance of evaluating communication quality between robots and humans, particularly for social robots, to enhance their effectiveness in tasks requiring interaction. It highlights ongoing benchmarking efforts aimed at certifying robots' abilities for market readiness.

In the context of auditory communication, robots can express emotions through sound production, utilizing both artificial sounds and natural motor noises. Speech can be generated via text-to-speech systems, which can be enhanced through dialogue management systems that adapt in real-time, although these systems often require significant computational resources and network connections, potentially leading to communication lags. To mitigate these delays, strategies such as simulated thinking gestures or interjections can be employed. The paper notes advancements in speech interpretation technologies, influenced by developments in home assistants, while acknowledging ongoing challenges in achieving fluent conversational dialogue.

Visual communication is also explored, where robots can utilize light, images, and motion to convey messages. Various methods, including LED displays and touch screens, are mentioned, though the reliance on screens can reduce the perceived richness of interaction. Projection techniques, such as those used in the Furhat robot, allow for expressive facial movements with lower mechanical complexity. The paper concludes by emphasizing the potential of integrating animated and telepresence features into robots to enhance user interaction.
