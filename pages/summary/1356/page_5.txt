This paper discusses the various stimuli involved in human-robot interaction (HRI), categorizing them into explicit, implicit, and involuntary interactions. It emphasizes the importance of viewing these interactions as an integrated whole, while intentionally leaving out physical design aspects of robots, such as shape and materials, which also play a critical role in communication. The authors acknowledge the growing scientific community dedicated to overcoming the challenges of effective communication with robots, aiming to create robots that can function as true companions in human activities.

The paper also includes a section on funding, ethical standards, and compliance, stating that there are no conflicts of interest and no studies involving human or animal subjects. It is published under a Creative Commons license, allowing for distribution and adaptation with appropriate credit.

Additionally, the paper provides references to recent literature highlighting significant trends and applications within the field of social robotics, including their use in healthcare, education, and service industries. The references underscore the importance of robot design and behavior in fostering effective interaction and the broader implications of social robots in various societal contexts.
