The paper discusses the various aspects of communication in human-robot interaction (HRI), emphasizing that communication extends beyond language to include all relevant sensory channels. It highlights the growing significance of robots capable of communication in diverse settings such as public spaces, education, and personal care.

The author notes that effective interaction relies on both intentional and unintentional communicative acts, utilizing channels such as hearing, sight, and touch. Successful communication necessitates coherent signal production and interpretation, which can be either programmed or learned through machine learning techniques when appropriate data sets are available.

Key findings indicate that physical and psychological factors significantly influence HRI, distinguishing it from human-computer interaction. The paper reviews recent literature on various interaction channels and underscores the importance of aligning robot capabilities with their physical and computational limitations to foster effective communication and relational dynamics.

Ultimately, the paper calls for the benchmarking of communication functionalities in interactive robots to facilitate their integration into the market, suggesting that communication, while vital, is just one of many functionalities that need to be developed and assessed.
