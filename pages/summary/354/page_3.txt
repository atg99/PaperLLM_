This paper presents a comparative analysis of the output characteristics of two AI models, GPT-4 and GPT-3. The focus is on the frequency of unique verb-noun pairs generated by each model, as well as the length of their output sequences. The findings indicate that GPT-4 produces a higher frequency of unique verb-noun pairs (5229) compared to GPT-3 (6133), suggesting a more diverse vocabulary usage. 

Additionally, the analysis reveals that GPT-4 tends to generate longer output sequences than GPT-3.5, with the latter showing a longer tail in output length distribution due to its iterative data collection process, which aims to eliminate similar instruction instances. Despite this, GPT-4's generated data exhibits better alignment performance in instruction-following tasks, indicating its enhanced capability in understanding and responding to user inputs effectively. 

Overall, the study highlights the advancements in AI language models, particularly in their ability to generate varied and contextually appropriate responses.
