The paper presents a performance comparison of various chatbots, including LLaMA, Alpaca, Vicuna, and GPT-4, focusing on their instruction-following capabilities in both English and Chinese. The evaluation is conducted using a reward model based on feedback data, highlighting the effectiveness of this approach.

In the analysis, LLaMA tuned with GPT-4 outperforms its counterparts, including Alpaca and the base LLaMA model, despite not matching the performance of larger commercial models like GPT-4. The study particularly emphasizes the performance of chatbots in Chinese, comparing responses generated from English translations and those directly produced in Chinese.

Key findings include the consistency of performance metrics across different models and languages, with translated responses generally performing better than generated ones. This discrepancy is attributed to the richer training data available in English. The paper also notes that as the length of the ground truth responses increases, the performance of LLaMA-GPT4 and GPT-4 improves, indicating a better ability to follow instructions in more complex scenarios.

Overall, the results underscore the importance of instruction tuning and the differences in performance based on language and response generation methods.
