The paper "OPT: Open pre-trained transformer language models" by Susan Zhang et al. introduces the OPT model, a series of large-scale pre-trained transformer language models aimed at advancing the research community's access to state-of-the-art natural language processing capabilities. The authors emphasize the importance of transparency in AI development and provide comprehensive details about the model architecture, training methodology, and evaluation benchmarks. They also discuss the potential implications of releasing such models for both research and practical applications.

In contrast, the work by Ruiqi Zhong et al. titled "Adapting language models for zero-shot learning by meta-tuning on dataset and prompt collections" explores a novel approach to enhance language models' performance in zero-shot learning scenarios. The authors propose a meta-tuning framework that fine-tunes models on a variety of datasets and prompts, enabling them to generalize better to unseen tasks. This method demonstrates significant improvements in performance, highlighting the potential for adapting existing models to diverse applications without extensive retraining.

Both papers contribute valuable insights to the field of language modeling, focusing on accessibility and adaptability in AI technologies.
