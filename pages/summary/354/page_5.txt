The paper evaluates the alignment quality of instruction-tuned large language models (LLMs) based on the criteria of helpfulness, honesty, and harmlessness (HHH) as defined by Anthropic. The study compares the performance of two models: Alpaca, which is aligned to GPT-3, and LLaMA-GPT4, aligned to GPT-4. Human evaluations were conducted using Amazon Mechanical Turk with 252 user-oriented instructions.

Results indicate that for helpfulness, LLaMA-GPT4 significantly outperformed Alpaca, receiving 54.12% of positive votes compared to Alpaca's 19.74%. In the honesty and harmlessness categories, both models saw a substantial number of votes for ties, but Alpaca slightly edged out in these criteria. 

Further comparisons of LLaMA models trained with GPT-4 against the original GPT-4 showed that the instruction-tuned LLaMA models performed similarly to GPT-4 across all three alignment criteria. The findings suggest that instruction tuning with GPT-4 data effectively maintains the alignment with human values in LLaMA models. Overall, the study underscores the importance of alignment in AI systems to ensure they meet human expectations and ethical standards.
