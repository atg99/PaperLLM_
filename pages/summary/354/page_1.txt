This paper presents an innovative approach to instruction tuning of large language models (LLMs) by utilizing GPT-4 to generate instruction-following data. The authors highlight that previous research has demonstrated the efficacy of machine-generated data in enhancing LLMs' zero-shot capabilities, eliminating the need for human-written instructions. Their experiments with instruction-tuned LLaMA models indicate that the 52,000 instruction-following samples generated by GPT-4 outperform those produced by prior state-of-the-art models in terms of zero-shot performance on new tasks.

The introduction outlines the evolution of instruction tuning, emphasizing methods such as Self-Instruct tuning that align LLMs with human intent using data from advanced instruction-tuned teacher models. The paper notes the success of models like ChatGPT and GPT-4 in providing opportunities to improve open-source LLMs. The authors specifically mention LLaMA, an open-sourced model that competes with proprietary models like GPT-3, and the adoption of Self-Instruct tuning for better instruction adherence.

Key contributions of the paper include the release of 52,000 instruction-following samples in English and Chinese generated by GPT-4, along with feedback data that assesses the outputs of three instruction-tuned models. Additionally, the authors developed instruction-tuned LLaMA models and reward models based on this data. They propose a comprehensive evaluation framework that incorporates human assessments based on alignment criteria, automatic evaluations using GPT-4 feedback, and ROUGE-L metrics on unseen instructions.

The paper concludes by acknowledging that this is a preliminary release, with plans for dataset expansion and further fine-tuning of larger models.
