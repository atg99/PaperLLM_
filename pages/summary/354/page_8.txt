This paper explores the effectiveness of instruction tuning in large language models (LLMs) using GPT-4, highlighting how LLaMA-GPT4 closely mimics GPT-4's performance on unnatural instructions across different response lengths. The analysis reveals that while shorter responses often provide simpler ground truth answers, both LLaMA-GPT4 and GPT-4 tend to embellish these with additional wording, which negatively impacts their ROUGE-L scores.

The paper reviews the current landscape of instruction tuning in NLP, referencing various models and benchmarks such as FLAN, T0, and OPT-IML Bench, which aim to enhance instruction-following capabilities. It also discusses the rise of open-source LLMs, noting projects like BLOOM, GPT-J, and Alpaca, which focus on creating text-based assistants aligned with human values. These models are increasingly being adapted for multi-modal applications, as seen with OpenFlamingo and LLaMA-Adapter.

In conclusion, the authors present 52K instruction-following instances generated by GPT-4, along with fine-tuned model checkpoints from LLaMA, to support advancements in open-source LLMs. They suggest future research directions, including expanding data scales and implementing reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) to further enhance LLM training and performance.
