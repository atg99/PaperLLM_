The paper discusses the instruction-tuning of language models, focusing on the development of two models using supervised fine-tuning based on the LLaMA 7B checkpoint. The first model, LLaMA-GPT4, is trained on 52,000 English instruction-following examples generated by GPT-4, while the second model, LLaMA-GPT4-CN, is trained on a similar number of Chinese examples. The training follows a schedule outlined in previous research to ensure fairness in comparison.

A significant aspect of the paper is the exploration of Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF), which seeks to align the behavior of language models with human preferences. The authors highlight the importance of reward modeling in RLHF, where the task is to predict a scalar reward for a given prompt and response. This process typically requires extensive comparison data, which has been a challenge in existing open-source models due to the high costs involved in labeling. However, the authors note that GPT-4 has demonstrated capabilities in identifying errors and evaluating response quality. To advance RLHF research, the authors created comparison data using GPT-4.

The paper also describes the training of a reward model based on the OPT 1.3B architecture, which rates various responses using scores assigned by GPT-4. The objective of this model is to minimize the difference in scores between pairs of responses.

In terms of experimental results, the paper emphasizes the challenges of evaluating large language models (LLMs). The authors aim to assess the instruction-following capabilities of their self-instruct tuned models using three established datasets: User-Oriented-Instructions-2522, Vicuna-Instructions-803, and Unnatural Instructions. These datasets consist of a variety of instructions and questions designed to evaluate the models' performance on tasks that are not strictly aligned with traditional NLP benchmarks.
