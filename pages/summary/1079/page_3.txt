The equations presented in the paper illustrate the computation of gradients with respect to the weights in a Recurrent Neural Network (RNN). Specifically, the gradients with respect to Wxh and Whh are derived and expressed in terms of the output and hidden states. The introduction of Truncated Backpropagation Through Time (BPTT) is discussed as a method to address the numerical instability arising from the accumulation of gradient values, which can lead to either vanishing or exploding gradients during training.

Vanishing gradients occur when small eigenvalues in matrix multiplications lead to a diminishing gradient across layers, effectively nullifying the contributions of earlier states. Conversely, exploding gradients arise from large eigenvalues that excessively amplify the gradient, resulting in drastic weight updates. 

To combat these gradient-related issues, LSTMs have been proposed, featuring gated mechanisms that enhance the model's ability to learn over extended sequences. The design of LSTMs allows for the effective retention and discarding of information, thus enabling better performance on tasks that require understanding over long time horizons. The paper sets the stage for a deeper exploration of LSTM architecture and its advantages over traditional RNNs.
