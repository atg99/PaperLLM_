The paper discusses the implementation of the attention mechanism in sequence-to-sequence (seq2seq) models, particularly in the context of handling long sequences. It explains that traditional methods would construct an encoder vector from the last hidden state of the encoder network, but the attention mechanism introduces shortcuts between the context vector and the entire source input. This allows the model to focus on specific parts of the input sequence when generating outputs.

Figure 5 illustrates an alignment matrix where white indicates high correlation and black indicates low correlation, showcasing how the attention mechanism can be applied not only to pairs of sentences in different languages but also to the same sentence, referred to as self-attention.

The mechanism involves calculating a context vector, which is a weighted sum of the hidden states of the input sequence, with weights determined by alignment scores. These scores indicate how well input-output pairs match, enabling the model to prioritize certain hidden states based on their relevance to the current output.

The paper also highlights various implementations of score functions used in alignment models, noting the presence of two trainable weight matrices in the alignment process. This section is further supported by a table that summarizes the different score functions and their applications in published works. Additionally, an appendix is referenced for a more visual explanation of the attention mechanism.
