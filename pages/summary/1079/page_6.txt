The paper discusses the Encoder-Decoder architecture, which is designed for mapping a fixed-length input sequence to a fixed-length output sequence. The architecture includes an encoder, typically implemented with Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) such as LSTMs or GRUs, which processes input elements sequentially to produce hidden states. The final hidden state, known as the Encoder Vector or context, encapsulates the information from the entire input sequence and serves as the initial hidden state for the decoder network.

The decoder, also composed of RNNs, generates an output sequence based on the hidden state received from the encoder. However, the fixed-length nature of the Encoder Vector presents challenges, particularly with longer sequences, as it must contain all necessary information from the source sentence. This limitation has spurred the introduction of Attention mechanisms, which enhance the model's ability to focus on relevant parts of the input sequence dynamically, akin to human visual perception.

The Attention mechanism transforms input sentences into a matrix format, allowing for the identification of correlations between sequence elements. This approach improves the model's performance by addressing the bottleneck created by the fixed-length context representation. The paper sets the stage for a deeper exploration of these solutions in subsequent sections.
