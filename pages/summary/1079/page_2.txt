The paper discusses the differences between Feedforward Neural Networks (FNNs) and Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs), highlighting their structural distinctions and the implications for training. In RNNs, the hidden state at time t, denoted as Ht, recursively incorporates information from its previous state Ht-1, allowing it to maintain a memory of past inputs. This is contrasted with FNNs, where the computation is static and does not retain historical information.

The mathematical formulations for the hidden and output states in both networks are presented, with RNNs demonstrating a recursive relationship. The paper introduces Backpropagation Through Time (BPTT), a specialized adaptation of the backpropagation algorithm for RNNs. BPTT unfolds the RNN to resemble a FNN, enabling the application of backpropagation across time steps.

The authors define a loss function L that quantifies the difference between the outputs of the RNN and the target values. Various loss definitions, such as Mean Squared Error and Cross Entropy Loss, can be utilized depending on the specific application. The process of calculating gradients with respect to the weight matrices (Wxh, Whh, and Who) is outlined, using the chain rule to derive the necessary partial derivatives for updating the weights during training. This allows for effective learning in RNNs, despite their complex temporal structure.
