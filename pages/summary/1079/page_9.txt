The paper discusses the architecture of the Transformer model, specifically highlighting its use of multi-headed attention, skip connections, and layer normalization in both the encoder and decoder. It emphasizes the embedding of inputs and outputs, along with the application of positional encoding to indicate the proximity of sequence elements. The output from the decoder is transformed into a logits vector through a linear layer, which corresponds to the learned vocabulary, and a softmax function is applied to convert scores into probabilities for word selection.

Additionally, the paper introduces Pointer Networks (Ptr-Nets), which enhance the traditional seq2seq model by allowing the output to consist of pointers to elements within the input sequence rather than pre-defined categories. This adaptation enables Ptr-Nets to effectively tackle combinatorial optimization problems, including planar convex hulls, Delaunay triangulations, and the symmetric planar Traveling Salesman Problem.
