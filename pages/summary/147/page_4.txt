The paper presents a comparison of language modeling approaches using two datasets: PTB and E2E. The statistics of these datasets are summarized in Table 1, which provides information on the number of sentences and vocabulary size.

In terms of model performance, Table 2 details the negative log likelihood (NLL) and perplexity (PPL) results for various models, including the proposed HR-V AE and several baselines. The results indicate that the HR-V AE model outperforms all baselines in both datasets, showcasing significant reductions in NLL and PPL. For example, in the PTB dataset, the NLL improved from 96 to 79 and PPL from 98 to 43 when compared to the vMF-V AE baseline. Similar improvements are observed in the E2E dataset.

The analysis of loss functions reveals insights into model behavior. The KL loss for the V AE-LSTM-base collapses to zero, leading to poor generative performance, while V AE-CNN and vMF-V AE show non-zero KL losses but exhibit overfitting on the test set. The authors propose holistic KL regularization as a solution to mitigate KL collapse, achieving better reconstruction errors during both training and testing.

Finally, the paper includes examples of sentence reconstructions, highlighting that while the vMF-V AE model often generates repeated or unrelated words, the sentences reconstructed by the HR-V AE model are more grammatical and closely resemble the ground truth. This demonstrates the effectiveness of the proposed approach in generating high-quality sentences.
