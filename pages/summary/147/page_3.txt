The paper discusses the implementation of a hierarchical variational autoencoder (HR-V AE) model utilizing a two-layer LSTM architecture for both the encoder and decoder, with the flexibility to apply other RNN types like GRU. A significant aspect of the model is the imposition of a standard normal prior on the hidden states of the RNN-based encoder, which enhances regularization during the learning process. The model estimates parameters for a normal distribution based on the concatenation of the hidden and cell states, aiming to minimize the KL divergence between the learned distribution and the standard Gaussian prior.

The experimental setup includes evaluations using two public datasets: the Penn Treebank (PTB) and the end-to-end (E2E) text generation corpus. The model's performance is compared against three strong baselines that utilize variational autoencoders (VAE) for text modeling, each with different configurations including LSTM and CNN architectures. The experiments are conducted under two settings: one where the decoder receives both the latent variable and the ground truth word, and another where the decoder operates without the ground truth word, allowing for a more challenging evaluation of the model's capabilities. 

The findings suggest that the HR-V AE model effectively addresses the KL collapse issue without the need for complex engineering to balance the reconstruction and KL loss terms, maintaining a simple 1:1 ratio between them.
