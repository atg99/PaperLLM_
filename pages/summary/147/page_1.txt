In the paper "A Stable Variational Autoencoder for Text Modelling," the authors Ruizhe Li, Xiao Li, Chenghua Lin, Matthew Collinson, and Rui Mao address the challenge of latent variable collapse in Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) when applied to text modeling. This issue, also referred to as KL loss vanishing, occurs when the posterior distribution collapses to the prior, causing the model to disregard latent codes during generative tasks. This problem is notably prevalent in VAE-RNN architectures used for text generation.

The authors propose a novel architecture called Holistic Regularisation VAE (HR-VAE), which effectively circumvents latent variable collapse. Unlike existing VAE-RNN models that typically impose a standard normal distribution prior only on the last hidden state of the RNN encoder, the HR-VAE model applies regularization to all hidden states of the encoder. This approach leads to a more stable training process and significantly enhances the quality of generated text compared to previous models.

The paper highlights various strategies previously employed to mitigate latent variable collapse, such as KL annealing, varying decoder architectures, and alternative prior distributions. However, these methods often require intricate balancing or sophisticated model designs. In contrast, the HR-VAE offers a simpler solution without the need for extensive engineering, thereby improving the robustness of text modeling with VAEs.
