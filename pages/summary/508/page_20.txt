The paper discusses challenges and advancements in self-supervised representation learning, particularly focusing on its applications in natural language processing, computer vision, and graph learning. It highlights the issue of transferring knowledge across datasets, emphasizing that traditional learning paradigms often assume data distribution consistency, which is frequently violated in practice. The success of self-supervised representation learning in NLP is attributed to extensive language corpora, but challenges remain in tasks like machine translation where embedding spaces vary across datasets.

The authors explore the role of sampling strategies in enhancing the performance of contrastive learning methods such as MoCo and SimCLR, suggesting that leveraging large amounts of negative and augmented positive samples could further improve results. However, they note that these methods tend to excel primarily in classification tasks and face limitations in areas such as language generation and entity extraction. The early degeneration problem, where models overfit the pretext tasks too quickly, poses a significant challenge for contrastive learning, and the authors call for new techniques to tackle this issue.

In conclusion, the survey categorizes self-supervised learning methods into generative, contrastive, and generative contrastive types, discussing their advantages, disadvantages, and specific applications. It emphasizes the potential of self-supervised learning to efficiently utilize vast amounts of unlabeled data, positioning it as a pivotal development in deep learning. The paper also outlines fundamental problems and future research directions in the field.
