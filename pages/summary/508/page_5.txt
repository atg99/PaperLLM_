The optimization objective is to minimize the reconstruction loss, which measures the difference between the original and reconstructed data.

3.3.2 Variants of AE Models
Several variants of autoencoders have been developed to enhance their capabilities. These include denoising autoencoders, which are trained to reconstruct the original input from a corrupted version, and variational autoencoders (VAEs), which introduce a probabilistic twist by modeling the latent space as a distribution rather than a fixed vector. VAEs allow for more robust generation of new data samples by sampling from the learned latent distributions.

3.4 Hybrid Generative Models
Hybrid generative models combine elements from different generative approaches, such as merging the strengths of AR, flow-based, and AE models to achieve better performance across various tasks. These models leverage the context modeling capabilities of AR models, the density estimation of flow-based models, and the reconstruction abilities of AE models, leading to improved generative capabilities and flexibility.

In summary, this section outlines key generative self-supervised learning methods, focusing on auto-regressive models, flow-based models, and auto-encoding models, each with distinct advantages and applications across domains. The development of hybrid models further emphasizes the trend towards integrating various generative techniques to enhance learning outcomes.
