The paper titled "Self-supervised Learning: Generative or Contrastive" by Xiao Liu et al. explores the emerging paradigm of self-supervised learning (SSL) as a solution to the limitations of deep supervised learning, particularly its reliance on manual labels and susceptibility to adversarial attacks. The authors highlight the rapid advancement of SSL in representation learning across various domains, including computer vision, natural language processing, and graph learning.

The survey categorizes existing SSL methods into three main types: generative, contrastive, and generative-contrastive (adversarial). Each category is thoroughly examined, with discussions on various models, such as auto-regressive, flow-based, and auto-encoding models, alongside their advantages and disadvantages. The paper also delves into contrastive learning techniques, including context-instance and instance-instance contrast, and their application in semi-supervised learning.

Additionally, the authors provide theoretical insights into why SSL is effective, discussing concepts like divergence matching, evidence lower bounds, and the relationship between contrastive learning and supervised learning. The paper concludes with a discussion on open challenges and potential future directions for self-supervised learning research.

Overall, this comprehensive survey serves as a valuable resource for understanding the current landscape of self-supervised learning methodologies and their implications for future developments in artificial intelligence.
