The paper discusses various methods in self-supervised learning, particularly focusing on tasks that involve understanding spatial relationships and maximizing mutual information. It highlights that models may predict relative positions of image patches or solve jigsaw puzzles to augment positive samples, as demonstrated in PIRL. The concept of Next Sentence Prediction (NSP) in language models, introduced by BERT, is critiqued for its limited effectiveness, leading to ALBERT's alternative approach of Sentence Order Prediction (SOP) which emphasizes semantic coherence.

The section on maximizing mutual information (MI) outlines its statistical foundations and applications in contrastive learning. Deep InfoMax is introduced as a pioneering model that maximizes MI between local image patches and their global contexts, using a summary function to derive context vectors. Contrastive Predictive Coding (CPC) follows, extending MI concepts to audio data by associating segments with their contexts, while also improving data efficiency through multiple negative samples.

Additionally, AMDIM builds on Deep InfoMax by enhancing positive associations through different image views. CMC differentiates itself by focusing on instance-instance similarity rather than context-instance similarity. In language pre-training, InfoWord aims to maximize MI between a global sentence representation and its n-grams, while Deep Graph InfoMax applies similar principles in graph learning, treating node representations as local features and averaging neighboring nodes for context. The paper provides insights into the evolution and methodologies in self-supervised learning, underscoring the significance of spatial relations and mutual information in model training.
