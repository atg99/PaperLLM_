The paper discusses the evolution of contrastive learning methods in graph neural networks and highlights the importance of mutual information (MI) maximization in traditional approaches like Deep Graph InfoMax. However, recent studies question the reliance on MI, suggesting that improvements may be more closely related to the architecture of encoders and the efficiency of negative sampling strategies in metric learning.

The paper introduces instance-instance contrastive learning, which shifts focus from MI to directly comparing local representations of different samples. This method emphasizes the significance of instance-level representations, asserting that they are more critical for tasks like image and sentence classification, where specific instances hold more relevance than contextual elements.

Cluster-based discrimination methods, notably DeepCluster, have shown competitive performance compared to supervised models by generating pseudo labels through clustering techniques. The training process involves iteratively clustering representations and training a discriminator to differentiate between samples from the same cluster. Local Aggregation further refines this approach, advocating for a more effective soft-clustering strategy to enhance performance.

Overall, the paper underscores the transition from MI-based methods to instance-instance contrastive learning, highlighting the effectiveness of cluster-based techniques in improving representation learning within self-supervised frameworks.
