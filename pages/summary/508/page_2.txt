The introduction highlights the evolution of self-supervised learning as a response to the limitations of supervised learning, such as the need for extensive labeled datasets and vulnerability to errors and attacks. Self-supervised learning offers a solution by allowing models to generate their own labels from the data, enabling them to learn from fewer labeled examples. The paper distinguishes self-supervised learning from unsupervised learning, emphasizing its goal of recovering and predicting parts of input data rather than merely identifying patterns.

The survey aims to provide a comprehensive overview of self-supervised learning in various domains, including natural language processing, computer vision, and graph learning, outlining the theoretical foundations, categorization of methods, and identifying future research opportunities. The paper's contributions include a detailed review of existing models, a classification framework, and an analysis of the field's current challenges and future prospects.
