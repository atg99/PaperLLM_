4.1.2 Maximize Mutual Information
In contrast, the Maximize Mutual Information (MI) approach emphasizes the direct relationships between local features and their global context, disregarding the relative positions of the components. This technique is beneficial in situations where the local parts are more critical for understanding the overall context than their spatial or sequential relationships.

The paper highlights the evolution of self-supervised learning, particularly noting the advantages of contrastive learning over traditional generative models in representation learning tasks. While generative self-supervised learning has been foundational, it faces limitations in both classification performance and the nature of its objectives. The contrastive learning framework has emerged as a powerful alternative, effectively addressing high-level abstraction challenges and improving performance across various tasks, particularly in classification scenarios. The discussion underscores the shift towards generative-contrastive self-supervised learning, which combines strengths from both approaches while overcoming inherent shortcomings in generative models.
