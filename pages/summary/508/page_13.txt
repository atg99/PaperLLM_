The paper discusses advancements in graph learning, highlighting that GCC outperforms earlier models like struc2vec and GraphWave in learning transferable structural knowledge. It also emphasizes the importance of semi-supervised learning in bridging the gap between self-supervised pre-training and downstream tasks, particularly through self-training methods that leverage both labeled and unlabeled data.

The paper outlines the self-training process, where a model is initially trained on labeled data to generate pseudo labels for unlabeled data. This iterative process enhances model performance. Notably, self-training has shown to be effective when combined with contrastive pre-training methods, such as the MoCo framework, which has been successful in image classification tasks but has limitations in object detection.

The authors present a three-step framework from Chen et al. that merges self-supervised pre-training with semi-supervised self-training, illustrating its effectiveness in achieving better performance with fewer labeled examples. The discussion also reflects on the pros and cons of contrastive learning, noting its efficiency in discriminative tasks while identifying challenges related to scaling it to natural language processing and improving sampling efficiency in negative sampling processes.

In conclusion, the paper advocates for further exploration into the integration of self-supervised and semi-supervised learning techniques, suggesting that this combination could lead to more data-efficient deep learning paradigms.
