The Variational Quantized Variational Autoencoder (VQ-VAE) architecture replaces traditional hidden distributions with a quantized vector dictionary and employs a pre-trained PixelCNN to model hierarchical image features. VQ-VAE-2 further enhances image generation capabilities, competing with state-of-the-art models like BigGAN by leveraging a multi-scale hierarchical structure.

Variational auto-encoders (VAEs) and their variants have proven effective in various domains, including image and video generation, and have been adapted for graph representation learning. The Variational Graph Auto-Encoder (VGAE) utilizes graph convolutional networks to reconstruct adjacency matrices, while models like DVNE and vGraph focus on uncertainty modeling and community detection within graph structures, respectively.

Hybrid generative models, which combine auto-regressive (AR) and auto-encoding (AE) approaches, offer additional flexibility. The Masked Autoencoder for Distribution Estimation (MADE) modifies traditional autoencoders to adhere to AR constraints, allowing for efficient computation of high-dimensional joint probabilities. In natural language processing, the Permutation Language Model (PLM) exemplifies this combination, enabling bidirectional context learning through permutation-based training strategies, as seen in XLNet.

Additionally, flow-based models like GraphAF integrate AR principles with auto-encoding for molecular graph generation, facilitating iterative processes and exact likelihood calculations.

Overall, generative self-supervised learning demonstrates significant potential across various tasks by effectively modeling data distributions, contributing to its success in numerous applications.
