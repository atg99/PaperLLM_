The paper discusses the capabilities of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) in learning disentangled representations, highlighting their advantages over supervised learning methods. Supervised models often fall prey to spurious correlations, as exemplified by their reliance on background elements, such as grass or snow, to distinguish between dogs and wolves. In contrast, GANs, particularly through the InfoGAN framework, can effectively learn independent features by sampling latent codes that represent various image characteristics.

InfoGAN introduces a method to maximize the mutual information between the latent code and generated images using variational inference, leading to improved disentangled representations, as evidenced by experiments on datasets like MNIST. Further research, including GAN dissection, has explored the causal relationships between different features in the generated images, revealing the potential for GANs to separate objects from backgrounds.

The paper also delves into the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) concept in Variational Autoencoders (VAEs), which aims to approximate posterior distributions through optimization. The mutual information maximization in contrastive learning, as seen in methods like Deep Infomax, parallels the objectives of GANs, suggesting a shared probabilistic foundation despite differing learning targets. Overall, the findings underscore the significant capabilities of GANs and similar self-supervised methods in learning disentangled features, paving the way for advancements in representation learning.
