The paper discusses the limitations of DeepCluster and introduces improvements made by the Local Assignment (LA) approach. Key drawbacks of DeepCluster include its exclusive sample clustering and reliance on cross-entropy loss, while LA enhances performance by utilizing a local soft-clustering metric and identifying neighbors independently for each example. The paper also draws parallels between LA and VQ-VAE, noting that both utilize quantization strategies to improve representation learning.

The paper emphasizes that clustering-based discrimination can enhance the generalization of pre-trained models, particularly in transitioning from pretext tasks to downstream applications. ClusterFit is mentioned as a method that introduces a cluster prediction fine-tuning stage to improve representation performance.

However, the two-stage training paradigm of cluster discrimination is criticized for being time-consuming and less effective than instance discrimination-based methods like CMC, MoCo, and SimCLR, which eliminate the clustering stage and incorporate efficient data augmentation techniques. SwAV is highlighted as a solution that integrates online clustering and multi-view data augmentation, achieving better performance with smaller models.

In graph learning, M3S applies DeepCluster-style self-supervised pre-training to enhance semi-supervised predictions by generating pseudo labels from unlabeled data.

The paper further explores instance discrimination, starting with InstDisc and its evolution into CMC and MoCo. CMC enhances instance discrimination by utilizing multiple views of images, while MoCo employs momentum contrast to increase the number of negative samples, improving training efficiency through a two-encoder system and a large queue for negative samples.

Despite these advancements, the paper critiques MoCo's simplistic positive sample strategy and highlights the need for more complex augmentation techniques, as exemplified by PIRL and SimCLR, which utilize multiple data augmentations to create challenging positive pairs. Overall, the paper outlines the evolution of clustering and instance discrimination methods in representation learning, noting their respective strengths and weaknesses.
