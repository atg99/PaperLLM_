This paper introduces a novel convolutional neural network (CNN) designed for Chinese named entity recognition (NER) that incorporates a rethinking mechanism to address common ambiguities in word identification. The authors argue that traditional models often struggle with conflicts among words due to the limitations of current Chinese word segmentation methods. To tackle this issue, the proposed model processes both the entire sentence and potential words in parallel using a CNN with a convolution window size of two. This allows for better integration of characters and potential words.

The rethinking mechanism enhances the model's ability to refine conflicting lexicon information by incorporating a feedback layer. This layer utilizes high-level semantic features to adjust the weights of the embedded words after analyzing the complete sentence, thereby improving the accuracy of word identification.

The paper outlines three main contributions: the introduction of a CNN structure that efficiently integrates lexicons into Chinese NER, the application of a rethinking mechanism to resolve conflicts between potential words, and the demonstration of improved performance on four NER datasets, achieving up to 3.21 times faster results compared to state-of-the-art methods.

The authors build upon previous research that emphasizes the benefits of character-based methods over word-based methods for Chinese NER and highlight the inefficiencies of existing models that do not adequately address word conflicts. By presenting a lexicon rethinking CNN (LR-CNN), the authors claim their approach is both more efficient and effective in handling the complexities of Chinese NER.
