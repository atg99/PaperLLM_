This paper presents a novel convolutional neural network (CNN) model designed for Chinese Named Entity Recognition (NER), which effectively integrates lexicons and employs a rethinking mechanism to enhance performance. The model is evaluated against four datasets—OntoNotes, MSRA, Weibo, and Resume—demonstrating significant improvements in F1 scores and processing speed compared to baseline models, particularly the lattice LSTM.

Key findings include:
1. The LR-CNN consistently outperforms the lattice LSTM across all datasets and sentence lengths, especially excelling with shorter sentences where it achieves an F1 score nearly 12% higher.
2. The processing speed of the LR-CNN remains stable as sentence length increases, while the lattice LSTM's speed declines significantly, with the LR-CNN being up to five times faster for sentences longer than 100 characters.
3. An ablation study reveals that lexicons are crucial for model performance, with a 9% drop in F1 score on the Weibo dataset when lexicons are excluded. The rethinking mechanism further enhances performance by resolving conflicts between potential matching words.
4. The LR-CNN with lexicons and the rethinking mechanism yields superior results, with a notable improvement of 5% on the OntoNotes dataset compared to its version without the rethinking feature.

In conclusion, this work highlights the effectiveness of the proposed model in improving Chinese NER tasks, achieving faster processing times and better accuracy than existing methods.
