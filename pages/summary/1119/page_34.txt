This paper presents a comprehensive survey of algorithms aimed at reducing computation time in multiobjective optimization problems that are computationally expensive. It identifies 45 algorithms published between 2008 and 2016, categorizing them based on the type of approximation—problem, function, or fitness approximation. The most prevalent method found was function approximation or surrogate modeling, which is crucial for saving computation time.

The authors highlight key challenges associated with metamodel management, including the metamodel's creation, updating, training time, selection of metamodel type, timing for updates, and constraint handling. These challenges are essential for enhancing algorithm efficiency in solving black-box problems, where prior knowledge about problem characteristics is limited.

The paper evaluates algorithm efficiency based on the number of function evaluations, problem dimensions, and specific problem characteristics like multimodality or disconnected Pareto fronts. It compares algorithms according to various criteria, revealing that Kriging and neural networks are the most commonly employed metamodels. Additionally, most algorithms utilize dominance-based evolutionary strategies and typically address problems with three or fewer objectives, with a notable lack of constraint handling.

The authors also discuss the potential of problem and fitness approximation algorithms in reducing computational complexity and function evaluations. Promising strategies include the use of ensemble metamodels and hybrid approaches combining different types of approximations. The paper concludes with intentions to tackle identified issues in future research.
