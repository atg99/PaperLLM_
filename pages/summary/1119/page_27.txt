The paper discusses a series of algorithms designed to optimize multi-objective problems using evolutionary strategies and surrogate modeling techniques. The algorithms focus on maintaining a fixed archive size while generating offspring through variation operators and selecting individuals based on their proximity to current nondominated solutions.

The first algorithm, Pareto-SVM, was tested against eight benchmark problems with varying decision variables. It demonstrated comparable performance to existing algorithms like NSGA-II and MO-CMA-ES, achieving similar results with fewer function evaluations. An extension of Pareto-SVM introduced a rank-based aggregate surrogate model, allowing for the classification of individuals into multiple classes based on dominance relationships, which improved performance in fewer evaluations compared to the original Pareto-SVM and showed similar results to NSGA-II and MO-CMA-ES.

Another advancement, ASM-MOMA, employed metamodels to predict distances to nondominated individuals rather than objective functions. This approach utilized linear regression, support vector regression, and multilayer perceptron models, leading to similar performance as NSGA-II and IBEA but requiring fewer evaluations, particularly when using linear regression.

The LAMM-MMA algorithm further enhanced ASM-MOMA by implementing multiple local metamodels, partitioning training data based on individual distances to nondominated individuals, and allocating weights accordingly. This algorithm outperformed IBEA and matched ASM-MOMA's performance in terms of function evaluations.

Lastly, the NN-SS-IBEA algorithm utilized a neural network metamodel to predict contributions to hypervolume rather than objective functions. This allowed for efficient evaluation and inclusion of individuals with significant hypervolume contributions into a fixed-size archive, optimizing the overall process.

Overall, the paper presents a progression of algorithms that leverage surrogate modeling to enhance performance in multi-objective optimization, emphasizing efficiency and the effective management of solution archives.
