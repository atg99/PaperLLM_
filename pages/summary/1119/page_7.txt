In summary, the paper discusses a multi-stage evolutionary algorithm (EA) framework for multiobjective optimization, emphasizing the transition from stage 1 to stage 2 through the use of metamodels. It outlines the generation of an offspring population and the creation of metamodels for computationally expensive objective and constraint functions. Key steps include evaluating individuals with either metamodels or original functions and selecting nondominated individuals for the final population.

The paper also highlights challenges associated with using metamodels in multiobjective optimization compared to single-objective scenarios. These challenges include the complexity of selecting and updating metamodels, as multiple objectives can complicate the approximation process. The discussion reveals that while many algorithms default to a single metamodel for all objectives, there is a lack of clear guidelines on employing different metamodels for distinct objectives or constraints. Additionally, the need for effective strategies to update metamodels is emphasized, focusing on the dual goals of convergence and diversity in the optimization process. Overall, the paper provides insights into the intricacies of integrating metamodels within evolutionary algorithms for multiobjective optimization while identifying areas for further research and improvement.
