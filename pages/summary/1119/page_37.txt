The referenced papers collectively contribute to the field of multi-objective optimization using evolutionary algorithms and surrogate models. Datta and Regis (2016) introduce a surrogate-assisted evolution strategy tailored for constrained optimization, enhancing efficiency. Deb (2001) provides foundational insights into multi-objective optimization through evolutionary algorithms, while Deb et al. (2010) explore hybrid approaches for estimating nadir objective vectors.

Deb and Nain (2007) present a meta-modelling procedure employing artificial neural networks, emphasizing adaptability in multi-objective scenarios. The NSGA-II algorithm, developed by Deb et al. (2002), is recognized for its speed and elitism, serving as a benchmark in the domain. The work by Deb et al. (2005) identifies scalable test problems for evolutionary optimization, facilitating robust algorithm evaluation.

Dennis and Torczon (1995) discuss the management of approximation models in optimization, highlighting their significance. Ducheyne et al. (2003) question the utility of fitness inheritance in practical applications, contributing to the discourse on evolutionary strategies. The jMetal framework for multi-objective optimization is detailed by Durillo et al. (2010), showcasing its design and architecture.

Emmerich et al. (2005) utilize hypervolume measures as selection criteria in evolutionary multi-objective algorithms, while their subsequent work (2011) delves into hypervolume-based expected improvement and its computational properties. Additionally, Emmerich et al. (2006) investigate the use of Gaussian random field metamodels in optimization, demonstrating their effectiveness in both single and multi-objective contexts. The concept of metamodel-assisted evolution strategies is further explored by Emmerich et al. (2002).

Fonseca and Fleming (1995) offer a comprehensive overview of evolutionary algorithms in multi-objective optimization, establishing a foundation for subsequent research. Finally, Forrester and Keane (2009) review recent advancements in surrogate-based optimization, underscoring its growing relevance in the field. Collectively, these works illustrate the ongoing evolution and sophistication of methodologies in multi-objective optimization.
