The collection of references explores various methodologies and advancements in multi-objective evolutionary optimization. Wang et al. discuss a data-driven surrogate-assisted approach aimed at optimizing trauma systems, highlighting the integration of data in evolutionary algorithms. Wilson et al. focus on efficient exploration of the Pareto frontier using surrogate approximations, essential for balancing multiple objectives effectively. Yang et al. address the management of approximation models in multi-objective optimization, emphasizing the importance of model accuracy.

Yuan and Guangchen compare neural networks and kriging methods for developing simulation-optimization metamodels, indicating the significance of model selection in optimization tasks. Zhang and Li introduce MOEA/D, a multi-objective evolutionary algorithm based on decomposition, which has been influential in the field. Subsequent works by Zhang et al. evaluate the performance of MOEA/D on various test instances and explore its application to expensive multi-objective optimization using Gaussian process models.

Zheng et al. utilize genetic algorithms for designing vector quantization codebooks, demonstrating the practical applications of evolutionary methods. Zhu et al. propose a multi-objective variable-fidelity optimization method for genetic algorithms, further expanding the toolkit for optimization practitioners. Zitzler’s work encompasses a comprehensive analysis of evolutionary algorithms for multi-objective optimization, providing foundational insights and empirical comparisons that inform future research.

In summary, these references collectively contribute to the understanding and development of multi-objective evolutionary algorithms, showcasing a range of techniques and applications that enhance optimization processes across various domains.
