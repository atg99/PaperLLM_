In stage 2, the focus shifts to building or updating a metamodel for the objective and constraint functions based on the archive of evaluated individuals. This metamodel allows for faster evaluations of the new population created through evolutionary algorithm operators.

The framework consists of two main stages. Stage 1 involves initializing a population, evaluating individuals with original functions, and iteratively creating new populations using evolutionary operators until a specified number of generations is reached. In Stage 2, the metamodel is utilized to evaluate new individuals, with the option to revert to original function evaluations if necessary. If a stopping criterion is met, the process concludes by selecting non-dominated individuals as the final population. If not, the cycle continues, maintaining a dynamic approach to both function approximation and evolutionary optimization.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the efficiency gained through function approximation techniques, particularly in evolutionary algorithms, highlighting the importance of metamodels and their role in managing computational costs associated with evaluating complex objective functions.
