The paper emphasizes the importance of algorithms aimed at minimizing computational costs and the number of function evaluations in multiobjective optimization problems. It discusses various shortcomings of existing algorithms, such as their ability to handle constraints, high-dimensional decision and objective spaces, and training times.

Promising strategies for overcoming these limitations are identified, including effective management of function approximation techniques to deal with problems involving multiple objectives. Additionally, the paper provides guidelines for selecting appropriate algorithms based on the characteristics of the problem, including the dimensions of the objective and decision spaces and available computational resources.

The structure of the paper includes several sections: Section 2 introduces key concepts and terminology related to reducing computational burdens in multiobjective optimization. Section 3 reviews different algorithms within a general approximation framework, comparing them based on various criteria such as approximation types and problem characteristics. Section 4 discusses issues surrounding the use of different algorithms and highlights promising elements that can enhance their effectiveness. Finally, Section 5 concludes with a summary of findings and suggestions for future research directions.

The paper defines multiobjective optimization problems, clarifying that they involve minimizing multiple conflicting objective functions subject to certain constraints. The notion of Pareto optimality is central, where a decision vector is considered Pareto optimal if no other vector can improve one objective without worsening another. The paper also discusses the ideal and nadir objective vectors, which provide bounds for the objective function values in the Pareto front, and introduces the concept of metamodels as approximations to reduce computational expense in optimization tasks.
