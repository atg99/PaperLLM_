The paper discusses advancements in evolutionary algorithms (EAs) for handling computationally expensive multi-objective problems (MOPs). It highlights the effectiveness of Kriging as a metamodel in approximation methods, with TCH and WS scalarizing functions showing superior performance in different approximation types. The discussion emphasizes the importance of selecting and updating metamodels and suggests that utilizing a combination of different approximations can significantly reduce computation time. 

The authors propose the use of hybrid algorithms that integrate function approximation to enhance convergence rates near Pareto optimal solutions. They note that the choice of metamodel is often arbitrary, advocating for an ensemble of metamodels to avoid limitations of single metamodel reliance. Examples from the literature illustrate strategies for assigning weights to fitness values from multiple metamodels and updating them based on solution accuracy, which can lead to faster convergence and improved exploration of the search space for viable Pareto optimal solutions. Future work includes developing a comprehensive algorithm that incorporates these insights.
