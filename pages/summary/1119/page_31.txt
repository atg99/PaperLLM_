The paper discusses various approaches in the literature for enhancing the efficiency of algorithms, particularly in the context of multiobjective optimization problems (MOPs). One promising trend is the combination of different approximations, such as fitness inheritance and function approximation, which serves to reduce computation time and complexity. Multilevel approaches, which involve solving different problems at various levels or using different solvers for governing equations, are also highlighted as needing further exploration.

Metamodels are increasingly utilized to predict solution quality instead of relying solely on objective functions. Various studies have employed metamodels to assess hypervolume contributions, distances from nondominated individuals, and to classify individuals based on dominance relations or predict their ranks. This alternative use of metamodels presents a promising avenue for improving optimization outcomes.

Hybrid evolutionary algorithms (EAs) that merge global and local search strategies are noted for their ability to enhance convergence rates towards the Pareto front. However, the integration of these algorithms with function approximation in computationally intensive MOPs remains underexplored. Some existing studies have utilized local searches alongside function approximation to capitalize on the benefits of both methodologies.

The paper emphasizes the importance of performance metrics for evaluating algorithm efficiency, citing commonly used metrics such as generational distance, inverted generational distance, and hypervolume. A summary table illustrates the variety of performance metrics and benchmark problems used across different studies, demonstrating the diversity in testing conditions within the field.

Finally, the paper outlines guidelines for practitioners and engineers in selecting suitable algorithms for real-world problems. It underscores the complexity involved in generalizing algorithm efficiency due to the varying evolutionary algorithms, surrogate techniques, and relevant parameters employed in the literature. Key considerations for algorithm selection include the dimensions of both objective and decision spaces.
