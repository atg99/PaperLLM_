This paper presents a comprehensive survey of advancements in evolutionary algorithms (EAs) tailored for multiobjective optimization problems (MOPs) involving computationally expensive objective functions. 

The advantages of EAs, such as their ability to produce a set of nondominated solutions and handle various types of variables without assumptions on convexity, are countered by their drawbacks, particularly the lack of guaranteed convergence and high function evaluation costs. To address these issues, the paper reviews various strategies, particularly focusing on the implementation of function approximation and metamodelling techniques, which have gained traction in both evolutionary computation and machine learning.

The survey categorizes 45 algorithms published between 2008 and 2016, emphasizing the predominance of Kriging as a function approximation method. It differentiates algorithms into Kriging and non-Kriging based categories, illustrating the broad applicability of Kriging techniques. Additionally, it discusses the classification of these algorithms within a general function approximation framework, contributing to a better understanding of their application in EAs.

Lastly, it evaluates the efficiency of different algorithms and highlights real-world applications where these techniques have successfully reduced computational time, thereby enhancing the practicality of EAs in solving complex optimization problems.
