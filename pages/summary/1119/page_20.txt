The paper discusses the application of algorithms that utilize metamodels for solving multi-objective optimization problems, particularly in scenarios where feasible regions may be empty. The effectiveness of these algorithms was evaluated through extensive testing on benchmark problems and real-world applications in manufacturing and robotics.

Key methodologies include the use of Radial Basis Functions (RBF) as metamodels for approximating objective and constraint functions, where individuals are generated through random sampling and Gaussian perturbations. The selection of individuals for re-evaluation is guided by their distance from existing solutions in both decision and objective spaces, ensuring a focus on improving solutions while minimizing constraint violations.

Additionally, the paper explores the use of multiple metamodels, highlighting the advantages of employing different approaches such as polynomial approximation, Kriging, and radial basis function within an NSGA-II framework. The results indicate that the performance of the algorithms is significantly enhanced when using Gaussian-generated individuals and that the choice of metamodel can lead to varying optimization outcomes.

Overall, the findings underscore the importance of metamodeling in multi-objective optimization and provide insights into the development of more effective algorithms that can handle complex problem spaces.
