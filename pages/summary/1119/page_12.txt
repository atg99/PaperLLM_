The paper discusses various multi-objective optimization algorithms that utilize metamodeling techniques, particularly focusing on the use of Kriging models. It introduces the K-MOGA algorithm, which partitions the decision space into nondominated and dominated sets. The minimum mean distance (MMD) is calculated between these sets using a predicted objective vector from the metamodel, and individuals that do not meet a specified threshold based on the standard deviation from the Kriging model are re-evaluated. K-MOGA was tested against traditional MOGA on multiple benchmark and real-world problems, demonstrating similar solutions with fewer evaluations.

The KD-MOGA extends K-MOGA by incorporating a fixed number of individuals generated through constrained maximum entropy design, which enhances population diversity. It was also tested on the same problems as K-MOGA, exhibiting comparable performance with fewer evaluations.

Additionally, the paper presents the combined AASO-AAMO algorithm, which builds a metamodel for each objective and constraint function, selecting individuals for re-evaluation based on max-min distance criteria. This algorithm was applied to a fatigue design problem, noting that the efficiency was not compared to algorithms without metamodels due to practical challenges.

Lastly, the MOEA/D-EGO algorithm employs a Kriging metamodel in conjunction with a decomposition-based evolutionary algorithm. It uses a Chebyshev scalarizing function to transform multi-objective problems into single-objective ones, maximizing expected improvement through local searches and clustering techniques. This approach aims to enhance training efficiency for the metamodel. Overall, the paper emphasizes the effectiveness of integrating metamodels into evolutionary algorithms for multi-objective optimization.
