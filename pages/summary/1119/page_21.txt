The paper discusses the evaluation and comparison of various metamodels in the context of optimization problems. It highlights the effectiveness of re-evaluating individuals obtained from one metamodel using others, specifically noting that individuals derived from Kriging exhibited the least error when re-evaluated with different metamodels. A graphical comparison of nondominated individuals from the proposed algorithm against results from earlier studies showed improved outcomes when using Kriging and radial basis functions, while polynomial approximations yielded similar results. However, the algorithm's performance without metamodels was not assessed, nor were computational efficiency metrics provided.

The authors further explore the use of five distinct radial basis functions within the EA MODE-LD+SS framework. Each objective function was assigned a separate metamodel, and individuals selected for metamodel updates were chosen based on a Chebyshev scalarizing function, which aimed to optimize accuracy and individual diversity. The algorithm was validated against five aerodynamic shape optimization problems, demonstrating superior hypervolume results with fewer function evaluations when metamodels were employed.

Additionally, the paper examines the SS-MOMA algorithm, which utilized different metamodels tailored to specific optimization problems. This approach raises concerns regarding its applicability to black-box problems due to the assumption of known function landscapes. The study also tested modifications to the achievement scalarizing function and incorporated a local search algorithm, demonstrating effectiveness across various benchmark problems and an airfoil optimization scenario. The results indicated that using the Tchebycheff function with specific reference points achieved better performance with fewer function evaluations, as measured by the Inverted Generational Distance (IGD).
