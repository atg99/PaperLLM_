The paper discusses various strategies to enhance the efficiency of optimization algorithms, particularly in aerodynamic shape optimization. It highlights the use of simplified equations, such as replacing the 3-dimensional Navier-Stokes equations with 2-dimensional Euler equations, to reduce computational complexity. This method has been applied in multiple studies for solving aerodynamic shape optimization problems.

Additionally, the paper explores data-driven optimization algorithms, which are useful when analytical forms or simulation models for objective functions are unavailable. These algorithms rely on data collected from physical experiments to achieve Pareto optimal solutions. A specific example includes the work by Wang et al., which formulates a multi-objective problem (MOP) based on trauma system data, addressing the trade-off between data accuracy and size.

The concept of fitness approximation is also examined, where metamodels are used to estimate elements instead of direct objective functions. The paper reviews fitness inheritance methods introduced to improve genetic algorithms, distinguishing between averaged and proportional inheritance approaches. Empirical results indicate that fitness inheritance can enhance performance in specific contexts, particularly for problems with convex and continuous Pareto fronts.

Moreover, the application of metamodels in classifying individuals into nondominated and dominated sets is discussed, exemplified by the Pareto-SVM algorithm, which employs support vector machines for classification and regression tasks. The paper concludes with a description of an updating criterion for metamodels inspired by evolutionary algorithms, ensuring effective representation and selection of individuals within the optimization process.
