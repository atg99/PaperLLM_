The paper discusses the significance of various parameters affecting evolutionary algorithms, particularly focusing on population size, archive size, dataset size for training metamodels, and maximum function evaluations. It highlights that the population size is critical, as evidenced by previous studies, and that the archive size influences training time. The dataset sizes for training and updating surrogates should be adjusted according to resource availability, which can impact algorithm performance. 

The classification of algorithms based on metamodel usage is introduced, specifically differentiating between Kriging and non-Kriging based algorithms. The section on Kriging based algorithms delves into specific implementations, such as SMS-EGO, which uses a DACE model for multi-objective optimization. This algorithm's performance is compared against ParEGO, demonstrating superior results when using hypervolume as a performance metric, although it did not perform as well on other criteria in certain cases.

Another Kriging-based approach, K-MOGA, modifies NSGA-II by incorporating a metamodel to approximate objectives and assess domination status for individuals, leading to effective re-evaluation and updating of solutions. Overall, the paper emphasizes the critical role of metamodels in enhancing the efficiency of evolutionary algorithms for multi-objective optimization.
