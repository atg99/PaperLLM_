The paper discusses advanced algorithms designed to address optimization problems with a large number of decision variables by employing metamodels, specifically focusing on Kriging and non-Kriging methods. The HDMR model utilizes Kriging to approximate component functions, generating multiple models for decision variables and objectives. NSGA-II is employed for optimization, with updates to the HDMR models based on re-evaluated individuals in the decision space. The algorithm was validated against 17 benchmark problems, showing superior performance in terms of Inverted Generational Distance (IGD) compared to traditional NSGA-II methods.

In contrast, non-Kriging based algorithms, such as MOPSA-EA, leverage alternative metamodels like neural networks. This approach involves generating offspring individuals through genetic operations, with fitness values adjusted based on parent evaluations. The algorithm uses a fixed archive size, maintaining a balance of nondominated individuals through a selection process that emphasizes diversity. MOPSA-EA was tested on various benchmarks and demonstrated competitive results against established algorithms, outperforming them in several metrics while maintaining comparable performance in others.

Overall, both types of algorithms aim to enhance the efficiency of multiobjective optimization by integrating metamodeling techniques, showcasing advancements in evolutionary computation for complex decision-making scenarios.
