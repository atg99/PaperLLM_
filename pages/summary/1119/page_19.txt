The proposed method was evaluated on a variety of test problems, demonstrating its capability to effectively approximate the objectives and constraints using radial basis functions. The algorithm's approach involved generating new individuals through a mutation operator, followed by evaluating these individuals with the constructed metamodels. Feasible solutions were identified through nondominated sorting, and the best individuals were re-evaluated to refine the metamodels.

The algorithms discussed in the paper highlight various strategies for enhancing multiobjective optimization through metamodeling techniques. ELMOEA/D-DE showed superior performance on ZDT problems and other benchmarks compared to existing algorithms. GOMORS utilized radial basis functions to optimize objectives efficiently, outperforming NSGA-II and ParEGO on benchmark and groundwater remediation problems. SS-MOMA demonstrated the effectiveness of Tchebycheff scalarization over weighted sum in generating diverse solutions. Lastly, the approach by Datta and Regis emphasized the importance of updating metamodels based on feasible solutions while initially disregarding feasibility constraints.

Overall, the paper illustrates the effectiveness of integrating metamodeling techniques in multiobjective optimization, achieving improved performance across various test cases and demonstrating the potential for further advancements in the field.
