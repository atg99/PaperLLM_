The references listed cover various approaches and methodologies in the field of multi-objective optimization, particularly focusing on surrogate-assisted techniques and evolutionary algorithms. Liu and Collette (2014) emphasize the enhancement of optimization processes through clustering algorithms. Loshchilov et al. (2009, 2010) present different surrogate models aimed at improving multi-objective optimization, highlighting the significance of dominance-based Pareto-surrogates.

Luo et al. (2014) explore the use of Kriging models for many-objective optimization, specifically addressing the efficient calculation of expected hypervolume improvement. Martinez and Coello (2013) discuss the integration of radial basis function networks with MOEA/D for tackling expensive optimization problems.

In the context of industrial applications, Mengistu and Ghaly (2008) demonstrate the use of evolutionary methods and artificial neural network-based surrogate models for aerodynamic optimization of turbomachinery blades. Mitra and Majumder (2011) focus on successive approximate models in multi-objective optimization for iron ore induration processes.

Mlakar et al. (2015) introduce GP-DEMO, which combines differential evolution with Gaussian process models for multi-objective optimization. Osyczka and Kundu (1995) propose a genetic algorithm-based method for solving generalized multicriteria optimization problems.

Overall, these works contribute to the advancement of optimization techniques by integrating surrogate models, clustering algorithms, and evolutionary strategies, thereby enhancing the efficiency and effectiveness of multi-objective optimization across various applications.
