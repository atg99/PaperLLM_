The paper discusses the development and evaluation of a multiobjective surrogate assisted (MOSA) algorithm that integrates a modular neural network (MNN) as a metamodel with NSGA-II for solving optimization problems. The proposed algorithm was benchmarked against a traditional µ-MOGA without a metamodel and another algorithm referenced in the literature. Results indicated that the MOSA algorithm achieved superior spread and convergence metrics in fewer function evaluations for benchmark problems, although it was not tested against µ-MOGA in the context of a structural engineering problem.

The algorithm operates by constructing a metamodel for each objective function, using a fixed number of better-performing individuals to form the population for NSGA-II. Nondominated individuals are identified and re-evaluated based on dominance criteria. Individuals are categorized into two sets: A1, which includes those that dominate at least one individual from a nondominated set A, and A2, which includes individuals that are neither dominated nor dominating. The selection for re-evaluation is based on Euclidean distances for A1 and the perimeter of rectangles formed by A for A2. 

If both sets A1 and A2 are empty, the algorithm regenerates offspring. The selected individual is then added to an archive to update the metamodel. The algorithm's flexibility in handling multiple objectives was not explicitly addressed in the paper. A practical application to coastal aquifer management demonstrated the algorithm's efficiency, taking 26 hours for original function evaluations compared to only 63 minutes for metamodel training, yielding similar results to NSGA-II with fewer evaluations.
