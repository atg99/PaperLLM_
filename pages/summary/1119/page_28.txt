It was evaluated against existing algorithms such as MOEA/D-RBF and IBEA across various benchmark problems and a real-world scenario. The results indicated that the new algorithm, NN-SS-IBEA, outperformed its competitors in eight out of twelve benchmark problems and also excelled in the real-world problem setting, showcasing its effectiveness in handling a specified number of function evaluations.

Additionally, the paper discusses a related algorithm that utilized nondominated individuals for classification, employing a metamodel to establish dominance relationships among solutions. This approach involved multiple classification algorithms, with a focus on SVM and classification trees, which showed strong performance in locating the knee region of the objective space.

Another algorithm presented in the study utilized a metamodel to predict individual rankings within the objective space, establishing boundaries for classification. This method was tested on nineteen benchmark problems, where it outperformed NSGA-II, SPEA2, and MOEA/D in several performance metrics, including generational distance and hypervolume.

A comparative study on metamodelling techniques and scalarizing functions was also conducted, leveraging various approximation methods on a dataset from the DTLZ suite. The study highlighted the effectiveness of different metamodels, including Kernel ridge regression and Kriging, alongside scalarizing functions based on Tchebychef and weighted sums.

Overall, the findings underscore the advancements made through the proposed algorithms and methodologies in improving multi-objective optimization processes.
