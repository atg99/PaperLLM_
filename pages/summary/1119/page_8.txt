The paper discusses the re-evaluation of individuals in the objective space and representative individuals using original functions to enhance approximation accuracy in multiobjective optimization. Representative individuals may include those closest to cluster centers or those with the highest fitness values, while individuals with low approximation accuracy can also be selected for re-evaluation. The literature reflects adaptations of single-objective metamodel updating criteria for multiobjective contexts, such as expected improvement and expected hypervolume improvement, which aid in selecting individuals for re-evaluation.

The paper emphasizes the significance of training time for metamodels, which becomes more critical in multiobjective optimization due to varying training data requirements for different objective functions. It highlights that many algorithms neglect to mention training time, often underestimating its impact on computation time reduction.

Two additional challenges affect both single and multiobjective optimization: the choice of metamodel and the timing of updates. The selection of metamodels is often arbitrary or based on their popularity, with limited guidance available in the literature. Popular techniques include radial basis function networks and Kriging, chosen based on problem characteristics. The use of an ensemble of metamodels is suggested to address this challenge, though criteria for their selection and simultaneous usage remain unresolved.

Finally, determining when to update the metamodel is crucial, as it can influence the accuracy of approximations and the overall optimization process.
