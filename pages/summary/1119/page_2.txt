This paper provides a comprehensive survey of 45 recent algorithms aimed at addressing computationally expensive multiobjective optimization problems using evolutionary algorithms, focusing on literature published between 2008 and 2016. It highlights the challenges posed by the extensive number of function evaluations required in these algorithms and discusses the role of approximations, particularly function approximations (or surrogates), in mitigating computation time.

The authors categorize the surveyed algorithms based on the type of approximation employed—problem, function, or fitness approximations—with a strong emphasis on function approximation methods. They evaluate these algorithms against various criteria, including the metamodelling techniques used, the specific evolutionary algorithms applied, the nature and dimensions of the problems tackled, constraint handling, training times, and evolutionary control mechanisms.

In addition to presenting the algorithms, the paper identifies promising elements and significant challenges within the literature regarding approximation methods and numerical settings. The authors also provide guidance on selecting suitable algorithms for specific computationally expensive multiobjective optimization problems, taking into account the dimensions of both the objective and decision spaces as well as the available computation budget.

Overall, the survey serves as a valuable resource for understanding current methodologies in the field and offers insights into future research directions.
