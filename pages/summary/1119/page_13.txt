A multiobjective variable-fidelity optimization (VFO) algorithm was introduced, utilizing Kriging as a metamodel in conjunction with NSGA-II. The algorithm begins by solving an approximated problem with low-fidelity functions for a set number of generations. The resulting nondominated individuals are archived and re-evaluated with original functions. A Kriging model is constructed for each objective, and an adaptive strategy is employed to evaluate individuals using either the metamodel or original functions based on the metamodel's accuracy. The VFO algorithm was tested on ZDT benchmark problems and a structural engineering problem, although efficiency metrics for the ZDT problems were not provided. In the structural problem, a comprehensive search evaluated approximately 65 million combinations, leading to a comparison of results from multiple studies that varied key parameters.

The VFO was further extended by employing multiple local metamodels rather than a single global one, particularly for high-dimensional objectives. K-means clustering was used to partition data in the decision space, allowing for the construction of these local metamodels. This extended algorithm was validated on six benchmark problems and one real-world problem, showing improved performance over both the original VFO and NSGA-II. The results indicated that the proposed algorithm effectively handled the challenges posed by both the benchmark and real-world scenarios.
