The paper discusses the use of metamodels in multiobjective optimization, focusing on their role in predicting objective function values and handling constraints. It highlights the importance of determining when to update the metamodel, noting that some algorithms may not require updates if the existing metamodel is sufficient for approximation. The paper identifies three main challenges in using metamodels: the need for a suitable set of solutions to train them, the difficulty of obtaining feasible solutions for constrained problems, and the selection and update of metamodels.

The analysis covers 30 algorithms from the literature, categorizing them based on the type of metamodel used—single, multiple, or ensemble—and emphasizing the prevalence of Kriging as the preferred single metamodel due to its ability to provide uncertainty information alongside predictions. The paper further divides algorithms into those utilizing Kriging and non-Kriging metamodels, while acknowledging that most algorithms are based on dominance-based evolutionary algorithms and can employ any metamodel.

Additionally, the paper includes a comparison of the algorithms based on metamodel type, evolutionary control strategies, and characteristics of the optimization problems addressed. A table summarizes the parameter values used in the algorithms, indicating where parameters are not applicable or not provided. Overall, the paper contributes to the understanding of metamodel application in multiobjective optimization by addressing key challenges and classifying existing algorithms.
