The paper evaluates various image augmentation techniques applied to Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) to determine their effectiveness in improving image generation quality. It addresses key questions regarding the necessity and impact of augmenting both real and generated images, as well as the role of consistency loss in enhancing GAN performance.

The authors conducted extensive experiments comparing the performance of different augmentation techniques when applied solely to real images versus both real and generated images. They found that augmenting only real images is ineffective, while augmenting both significantly improves GAN generation performance. Additionally, they demonstrated that incorporating consistency regularization further enhances results, outperforming previous benchmarks.

Key contributions include:
- A comprehensive evaluation of various augmentations to guide future research.
- Empirical evidence supporting the need for augmentations on both real and generated images, achieving a FID score of 11.03, an improvement over previous models.
- Demonstration that consistency loss is more effective than contrastive loss as a regularization strategy.
- Establishment of a new state-of-the-art FID score for conditional image generation on CIFAR-10, reducing it from 9.21 to 8.30 through optimal augmentation and regularization strategies.

The paper outlines the augmentation techniques studied, the experimental setup, including datasets and GAN architectures, and the evaluation metrics utilized. Overall, it provides valuable insights into the application of image augmentations in GANs, emphasizing the importance of a combined approach for optimal performance.
