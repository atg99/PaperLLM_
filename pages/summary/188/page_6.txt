The paper explores the impact of image augmentations on the performance of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), specifically BigGAN and SNDCGAN, using consistency regularization techniques. It presents experimental results indicating that applying augmentations to both real and fake images enhances generation performance, as measured by the Fréchet Inception Distance (FID). The authors analyze various augmentation techniques, categorizing them into spatial and visual transforms. Spatial transforms, such as zoom-out and translation, are found to be more effective than visual transforms like brightness adjustments. 

The study highlights that with optimal selection of augmentation types and strengths, significant improvements over state-of-the-art results can be achieved. For instance, the best FID for SNDCGAN was 14.72 using a zoom-out strength of 0.4, surpassing previously reported results. The paper also introduces the use of contrastive loss in GAN training, suggesting that it could further enhance performance by regularizing the training process. This research emphasizes the importance of a detailed analysis of augmentation techniques to improve GAN applications.
