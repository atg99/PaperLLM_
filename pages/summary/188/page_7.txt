The paper introduces Cntr-GAN, a generative adversarial network that incorporates contrastive loss to enhance the discriminator's performance by utilizing augmented copies of both real and fake images. CntrLoss encourages the model to differentiate between various image representations while bringing augmentations of the same image closer together. The study specifically employs augmentation techniques from the SimCLR framework and focuses on the performance of Cntr-GAN using the BigGAN architecture.

Experimental results show that Cntr-GAN outperforms the baseline BigGAN without augmentation, achieving a Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) of 12.27 compared to 14.73 for vanilla BigGAN. However, it falls short of BCR-GAN's performance, which achieves an FID of 9.21. When combining CntrLoss with BCR, the model achieves a new state-of-the-art FID of 8.30, highlighting the complementary nature of these regularization techniques.

The paper further investigates the impact of different augmentation strategies on Cntr-GAN's performance, revealing a preference for stronger spatial augmentations over visual ones. The optimal performance is noted with specific augmentation strengths, particularly achieving the best FID of 11.87 with adjusted SimCLR augmentations.

Overall, the findings suggest that Cntr-GAN benefits significantly from effective augmentation strategies, leading to improved generation performance compared to traditional GAN approaches. The paper includes additional analyses and visual results in the appendix for further insights.
