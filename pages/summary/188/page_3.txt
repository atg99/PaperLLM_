The paper investigates the impact of various image augmentation techniques on Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), specifically using the CIFAR-10 dataset. It introduces three advanced augmentation methods, denoted as T, and normalizes the original images. The strength of the augmentations is selected uniformly from weak to strong.

The evaluation employs the Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) metric, which is recognized for its consistency with human evaluations, to assess the quality of generated images. The study uses two GAN architectures: SNDCGAN for unconditional generation and BigGAN for conditional generation, with specified training hyperparameters.

Initial findings reveal that augmenting only real images does not enhance GAN training performance. This aligns with previous research, suggesting that a different approach—augmenting both real and generated images—yields better results. The paper sets the stage for further exploration of augmentation strategies combined with regularization techniques in subsequent sections.
