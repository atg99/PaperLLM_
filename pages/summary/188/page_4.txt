The paper discusses the impact of image augmentations on the performance of the SNDCGAN model when trained on the CIFAR-10 dataset. It highlights the following key findings:

1. The baseline FID score of SNDCGAN without data augmentation is 24.73. The performance can be significantly improved by augmenting both real and generated images during training, as shown in the FID comparisons.

2. Simply augmenting both real and fake images allows the vanilla SNDCGAN to match the performance of recently proposed models like CR-GAN. The Balanced Consistency Regularization (BCR) further enhances generation fidelity when applied to augmented images.

3. Spatial augmentations yield better results than visual augmentations, while certain augmentations that produce images outside the natural data manifold, such as InstanceNoise, do not enhance GAN performance.

4. The paper also notes that augmenting only real images can degrade performance, leading to higher FID scores due to the introduction of augmentation artifacts that confuse the discriminator.

5. By augmenting both real and fake images before feeding them into the discriminator, the model can avoid the issues caused by artifacts, resulting in improved generation performance.

Overall, the findings suggest that a dual augmentation strategy is beneficial for enhancing the performance of GANs, challenging the conventional practice of augmenting only real images.
