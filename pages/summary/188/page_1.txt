This paper investigates the role of image augmentations in enhancing the training of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) for image synthesis, an area that has not been extensively explored in prior research. The authors systematically evaluate various augmentation techniques and provide guidelines for their application in both vanilla GANs and regularized GANs. The findings reveal that augmenting both real and generated images significantly improves the quality of generated outputs, allowing vanilla GANs to achieve results comparable to state-of-the-art models.

Furthermore, the integration of augmentation with other regularization methods, such as contrastive loss and consistency regularization, leads to even better image generation quality. The authors report new state-of-the-art results for conditional image generation on the CIFAR-10 dataset when utilizing these combined techniques. The paper highlights the challenges of selecting appropriate augmentation strategies for GANs, noting that traditional methods often lead to undesired artifacts in generated images. This study emphasizes the importance of systematic approaches to augmentation in the context of GAN training, offering valuable insights for future research and applications.
