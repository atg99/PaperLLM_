The paper investigates the impact of data augmentation on the performance of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), highlighting how imbalanced augmentations can lead to generation artifacts. It emphasizes that augmenting both real and fake images significantly reduces these artifacts compared to only augmenting real images. The authors experimented with annealing augmentation strength during training, finding that it lessens the effectiveness of augmentations but does not alter their relative efficacy.

Additionally, the study explores the composition of multiple augmentation transforms, demonstrating that a combination of translation and brightness adjustments yields better results than single transformations. The research situates itself within the broader context of data augmentation, noting its critical role in enhancing the robustness and generalization of deep learning models, particularly in visual recognition systems.

The authors review related works, indicating that while data augmentation has evolved in various domains, its application in GANs remains limited. They reference studies that have attempted to stabilize GAN training through different augmentation techniques, including Gaussian noise and regularization methods. 

In conclusion, the paper asserts that augmenting both real and generated samples is essential for realistic image generation in GANs. It further argues that consistency regularization enhances performance beyond contrastive loss alone, achieving state-of-the-art results. The findings aim to facilitate future research on augmentations in GAN applications.
