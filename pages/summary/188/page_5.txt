The paper discusses the impact of image augmentations on the performance of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs). It presents findings from experiments that show how augmentations can reduce the Frechet Inception Distance (FID) between real and generated images, indicating improved overlap of their distributions. The results demonstrate that augmentations, particularly those causing spatial changes like translation, zoom in, and zoom out, are more effective than visual changes such as brightness adjustments.

The authors argue that augmenting both real and fake images enhances GAN training by increasing the support overlap between their distributions. They note that while augmentations can improve performance, not all augmentations or strengths are beneficial, as excessive alterations may lead to generated images that deviate from natural distributions.

The paper also explores the effect of augmentations on advanced GAN models like Consistency Regularized GAN (CR-GAN) and Balanced Consistency Regularization (BCR-GAN), which leverage augmentations to enhance training stability and output quality. Overall, the findings suggest that incorporating spatially impactful augmentations into GAN training is a significant yet underexplored area in the field.
