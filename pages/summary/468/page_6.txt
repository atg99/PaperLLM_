The paper discusses the training and parameter optimization of the SegNet model for classifying karst wetland vegetation. Key parameters included the learning rate, set to 0.001, gamma at 0.1, momentum at 0.8, and weight decay at 0.0001. The batch size was limited to 8 due to computational constraints with a dataset of 100,000 images. The study varied the EPOCH values from 5 to 15 for Area A and 5 to 10 for Area B to observe their influence on model performance.

To enhance classification accuracy, an optimized image algorithm was developed to address stitching artifacts that arose from the segmentation process. This involved cropping UAV images into smaller sub-images, expanding their size, classifying them, and then re-trimming and stitching them back together.

Additionally, the study evaluated the effectiveness of multi-class classification by fusing multiple single-class SegNet models. This was achieved using a maximum probability method to combine predictions from four single-class models, determining the most probable vegetation type at each pixel.

The paper outlines various model configurations, including single-class models for different vegetation types and a multi-class model utilizing texture features. The results indicate the potential of the optimized SegNet model and the fusion approach for improved classification of karst wetland vegetation.
