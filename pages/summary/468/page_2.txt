This paper discusses the challenges and advancements in mapping karst wetland vegetation, particularly in Huixian National Wetland Park, China. It highlights the limitations of traditional field surveys in karst environments, which are often expensive and hazardous. In contrast, remote sensing techniques, including UAVs, offer cost-effective alternatives for vegetation classification.

The study emphasizes the unique characteristics of karst wetlands, which differ significantly from other wetland types due to their hydrogeological conditions, resulting in fragmented and heterogeneous vegetation patches. Traditional shallow machine learning algorithms have been commonly applied for vegetation classification but require extensive feature selection and are time-consuming. In response, this research proposes a novel approach using deep learning, specifically a single-class SegNet model, to address issues such as class imbalance and dataset size.

Key contributions of the study include the development of a new classification technique that fuses multiple single-class SegNet models, the creation of a semantic labeling dataset through object-based random forest classification combined with visual interpretation, and an optimized post-classification algorithm to refine model predictions. The study area, Huixian National Wetland Park, boasts significant ecological value but has faced degradation due to human activities and invasive species.

UAV imagery was collected under optimal conditions, utilizing advanced georeferencing techniques to ensure high accuracy in mapping. Overall, this research provides valuable insights into effective methodologies for karst wetland vegetation classification, with implications for conservation and management efforts.
