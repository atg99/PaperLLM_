This study investigates the effectiveness of a single-class SegNet model for classifying karst wetland vegetation, focusing on the impact of incorporating textural features into the model. The results indicate that the fusion of multiple single-class SegNet models enhances classification accuracy. Specifically, the model trained with 15 epochs (EPOCH 15) outperformed those trained with 5 or 10 epochs in vegetation classification tasks.

The research highlights the role of textural features in improving the model's performance. The inclusion of these features in the dataset led to better visual representations of karst water and aquatic flora compared to karst grass and broad-leaved forest classifications. The model with EPOCH 15 and textural features achieved a high F1-score of 0.97 for karst aquatic flora, demonstrating its efficacy in monitoring the spatial distribution of karst wetland vegetation using UAV images.

Comparisons between models revealed that those with textural features consistently outperformed those without, achieving a 4% increase in F1-score and a 6% increase in kappa coefficient. The study concludes that combining single-class SegNet with textural features significantly enhances the model's ability to discriminate between different types of karst vegetation, with notable accuracy improvements across various classifications.
