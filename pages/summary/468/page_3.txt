This paper presents a methodology for classifying karst wetland vegetation using UAV imagery and machine learning techniques. The study utilized Pix4D Mapper software to process original UAV images, generating Digital Orthophoto images through several pre-processing steps, including image quality checks and the creation of a dense point cloud. The authors employed the ENVI 5.4 software for image mosaicking and georeferencing.

Field measurements were conducted between July 10 and 25, 2018, at Huixian National Wetland Park, where vegetation types and geographic locations of 612 sample plots (1 × 1 m) were recorded using Trimble R6 GPS-RTK. The samples were categorized into eight types, including karst water and karst grass, and were divided into training (2/3) and testing (1/3) datasets for machine learning purposes.

The study implemented a multi-class SegNet model alongside a fusion of single-class SegNet models, each with varying EPOCH values. The methodology involved UAV image collection, preprocessing, and the combination of object-based random forest classifications with visual interpretation to create a semantic labeling dataset. The classification accuracy of the models was subsequently verified using the testing datasets.

Overall, the research emphasizes the integration of UAV technology and advanced machine learning techniques for precise vegetation classification in karst wetland ecosystems.
