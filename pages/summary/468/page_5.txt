The paper discusses the methodology for classifying karst wetland vegetation using two distinct approaches: Random Forest (RF) classification and SegNet-based pixel-wise semantic segmentation. For the RF model, optimal tuning parameters were set to 1200 trees and 8 split variables, utilizing the randomForest package for training and classification.

The SegNet architecture is highlighted for its effectiveness in semantic segmentation, characterized by an encoder-decoder structure and pixel-wise classification. The model balances accuracy with computational efficiency, benefiting from its symmetrical design and pooling/upsampling features, which enhance the localization of remote sensing images. The study incorporates textural features calculated from the grey-level co-occurrence matrix (GLCM) to improve the classification of wetland vegetation.

A training dataset for the SegNet model was created, incorporating texture features derived from UAV-derived digital orthophoto maps (DOM). The dataset was divided into 100,000 images, with 75% used for training and 25% for testing. Data augmentation techniques were applied to enhance the training dataset. The study developed four single-class SegNet models targeting specific vegetation types and two multi-class models for comprehensive mapping.

Overall, the research demonstrates a robust framework for the classification of karst wetland vegetation, leveraging both RF and SegNet methodologies to achieve high accuracy and precision in remote sensing applications.
