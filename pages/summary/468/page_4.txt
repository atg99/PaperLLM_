The paper discusses the methodology for classifying karst wetland vegetation utilizing a combination of object-based random forest (RF) classifications and visual interpretation, enhanced by a SegNet-based model. The study outlines five key components: the selection of values for classification, the incorporation of texture features, the development of a post-classification algorithm and conditional random field (CRF) for optimizing results, and the evaluation of the multi-class SegNet model alongside multiple single-class models.

The semantic labeling dataset was created by integrating object-based RF classifications with visual interpretations, correcting misclassifications through field measurements. The process for object-based RF classification includes three main steps: 

1. **Image Segmentation**: Multi-scale segmentation algorithms were employed to analyze UAV images, with optimal parameters determined through an automated tool based on iterative segmentations.
2. **Feature Selection**: A comprehensive approach considered various image features, including spectral, texture, and shape features, calculated using eCognition software. Textural features were derived from the grey-level co-occurrence matrix, and the Recursive Feature Elimination algorithm was applied to select the most relevant features for classification.
3. **Model Training and Prediction**: The final classification process used the selected features to train the RF model for accurate vegetation classification.

The study emphasizes the importance of combining machine learning techniques with visual validation to enhance classification accuracy in complex wetland environments.
