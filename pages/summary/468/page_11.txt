This paper investigates the classification accuracy of karst wetland vegetation using SegNet models, focusing on the impact of EPOCH values and textural features. Key findings include:

1. The single-class SegNet models demonstrated improved performance when using EPOCH 15 compared to EPOCH 10, with Model-g achieving an F1-score of 0.92 and a kappa coefficient of 0.83 for karst grass. The combination of EPOCH 15 and texture features further enhanced these metrics, resulting in an F1-score of 0.95 and a kappa coefficient of 0.90 for Model-w classifying karst water.

2. Multi-class SegNet classifications utilizing textural features provided more accurate representations of karst vegetation compared to models without texture features. The models overestimated classifications for karst broad-leaved forest while underestimating karst grass.

3. Visual assessments indicated that the multi-class SegNet models with texture features outperformed the fused single-class models, particularly in identifying karst grass and karst broad-leaved forest.

4. Overall, the models achieved over 0.7 F1-score for all vegetation types, with karst water receiving the highest accuracy at 0.90 F1-score using EPOCH 15.

The results highlight the effectiveness of incorporating texture features and optimal EPOCH values in enhancing the classification accuracy of karst wetland vegetation.
