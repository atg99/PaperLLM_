The study evaluates the effectiveness of SegNet models in classifying karst wetland vegetation, comparing multi-class and single-class approaches. The results indicate that the fusion of single-class SegNet models generally yielded higher F1-scores than the multi-class models, particularly in classifying karst vegetation, except for karst grass. The classification accuracy improved with increased EPOCH values, with notable enhancements in kappa coefficients and F1-scores for various vegetation types. Specifically, karst water achieved an F1-score exceeding 0.86 using multi-class models, while the highest F1-score for karst grass was observed with the single-class SegNet model at EPOCH 10. These findings suggest that EPOCH values significantly influence the classification accuracy of karst wetland vegetation, and the visual analysis revealed challenges in distinguishing between different land use types due to spectral similarities. Overall, the study underscores the importance of model selection and parameter tuning in remote sensing applications for ecological monitoring.
