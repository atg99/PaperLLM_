The study evaluates karst vegetation classifications using a fusion of single-class SegNet models, focusing on the performance of different EPOCH values and texture features. The models with EPOCH 10 and 15 achieved F1-scores above 0.71 and kappa coefficients over 0.76 for all vegetation types, with no significant differences in accuracy between these EPOCH values. SegNet classifications for karst water excelled, reaching a F1-score of 0.95 and a kappa coefficient of 0.87 at EPOCH 15, showing a 6% improvement over EPOCH 10.

When combining EPOCH values with texture features, the model maintained high classification accuracy across vegetation types, except for karst aquatic flora. The best performance was observed with EPOCH 10 and texture features, achieving a F1-score of 0.97 for karst water. The study also compared multi-class SegNet models and fused models, finding that the multi-class model performed better for most vegetation types, with the fused model achieving a 14% increase in kappa coefficient.

Additionally, object-based random forest (RF) classifications were compared to fused SegNet classifications. Both methods achieved over 0.87 overall accuracy with object-based RF performing exceptionally well, particularly for karst water and broad-leaved forests, achieving over 90% producer’s accuracy across categories. The study highlighted some limitations of the SegNet classifications, such as difficulties in identifying small patches of aquatic flora and mixed pixel classification errors.

The authors suggest that incorporating additional data sources, such as spectral bands and DSM data, could improve classification accuracy, especially given the high spectral similarity of wetland vegetation. Overall, the findings demonstrate the potential of deep-learning algorithms in accurately classifying karst wetland vegetation using high-resolution UAV imagery.
