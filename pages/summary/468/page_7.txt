The paper outlines a study on karst wetland vegetation classification using single-class SegNet models, evaluating their performance through various metrics such as error matrix, Kappa coefficient, overall accuracy, Recall, F1-score, and class-specific accuracies. The research adhered to ethical standards, obtaining necessary permissions for conducting experiments in Huixian National Wetland Park.

Four single-class SegNet models were developed, each utilizing different EPOCH values (5, 10, 15) to assess their impact on classification accuracy. Results indicated that increasing EPOCH values generally improved classification outcomes. Specifically, the model with EPOCH 15 provided the most accurate representations of karst wetland vegetation, achieving high F1-scores across all land cover types, though challenges persisted in distinguishing karst grass and karst broad-leaved forest.

The study found that the model-a, which focused on karst aquatic flora, achieved the highest classification accuracy (F1-score of 0.96), followed by model-w (0.87) and model-g (0.83). While the karst broad-leaved forest was effectively classified in model-w with a Kappa coefficient of 0.91, karst grass remained difficult to classify, with model-g improving its F1-score to 0.83 at EPOCH 15. Overall, the findings underscore the effectiveness of the chosen models for karst vegetation classification, particularly with optimized EPOCH settings.
