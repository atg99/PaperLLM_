This paper presents a novel method for classifying karst wetland vegetation using deep learning techniques and UAV images, addressing challenges such as vegetation fragmentation and heterogeneity. The study focuses on Huixian National Wetland Park, the largest karst wetland in China, and employs a fusion of single-class SegNet models alongside an optimized post-classification algorithm to enhance accuracy. Key findings include: 

1. The fusion of four single-class SegNet models outperformed the multi-class SegNet model, achieving an overall classification accuracy of 87.34%.
2. The optimized post-classification algorithm effectively reduced stitching artifacts, further improving classification accuracy.
3. Single-class SegNet models demonstrated superior performance compared to multi-class models, with F1-Scores improving by 10 to 25%.
4. Both the fusion of single-class SegNet models and the object-based Random Forest classifier yielded classification accuracies exceeding 87%.

The study highlights the ecological significance of karst wetlands and the necessity for effective vegetation mapping to aid in their conservation and management, given the detrimental impacts of human activities on these ecosystems.
