This paper presents a novel approach to classify karst wetland vegetation in Huixian National Wetland Park by using a fusion of multiple SegNet deep-learning networks and UAV RGB images. The study demonstrates that utilizing a single-class SegNet model for each land cover type and then fusing these classifications via a maximum probability approach yields superior results compared to traditional multi-class SegNet models. Specifically, the fused SegNet classifications achieved a kappa coefficient of 0.81, representing an 11% improvement over multi-class classifications.

The research highlights the importance of EPOCH values in model performance, with an EPOCH value of 15 resulting in a 6 to 14% increase in classification accuracy compared to EPOCH values of 5 or 10. Additionally, incorporating textural features into the UAV image dataset significantly enhanced classification accuracy, yielding an improvement of 7 to 14% in kappa coefficients.

The findings confirm the effectiveness of the proposed post-classification algorithm in reducing stitching artifacts from SegNet classifications, thus improving overall accuracy to over 87%. However, the study notes limitations in not accounting for phenological differences and the insufficiency of UAV RGB images with textural features to fully distinguish vegetation communities. Future research will focus on integrating multi-temporal UAV multispectral and hyperspectral images, alongside LiDAR data, and exploring other deep-learning frameworks for enhanced vegetation classification.
