The paper discusses the multifaceted impacts of ethics documents in organizations and their wider environments. It categorizes these impacts into internal, external, public discourse, policy, and stakeholder ecosystem influences.

1. **Internal Impacts**: Organizations can implement changes motivated by ethics documents, such as introducing new ethical review processes and revising hiring practices to enhance diversity. Companies like Microsoft and Google are highlighted as examples of organizations that have made significant changes in their business practices based on ethical considerations.

2. **External Impacts**: Ethics documents can affect the practices of other entities, shape public perception, and enhance corporate reputations. For instance, Denmark's strategy for an ethics label aims to guide consumers toward responsible businesses. Additionally, the values guiding AI development are increasingly recognized as vital to brand value.

3. **Public Discourse**: These documents can frame ethical issues, influencing which problems are prioritized or overlooked. For example, some organizations focus on process transparency rather than technical explainability, potentially downplaying significant issues like privacy loss and labor displacement while emphasizing economic growth.

4. **Policy**: Ethics documents can inform governance and policy-making, impacting funding priorities, national policies, and research agendas. The paper notes instances, such as the U.S. National AI R&D Strategic Plan, where ethical discussions have influenced policy decisions.

5. **Stakeholder Ecosystem**: The documents can reshape educational priorities and research funding, influencing both technical and social science fields. They may also foster collaboration among various organizations focused on AI ethics.

The paper concludes by discussing factors that may predict the success of ethics documents, emphasizing the importance of engagement with law and governance, as well as the specificity of recommendations. Documents that are detailed and contextually relevant are more likely to achieve their intended impact.
