This paper investigates the ongoing development and implications of AI ethics documents, focusing on three primary questions: who is creating these documents, why they are being produced, and their potential impacts on global AI governance. The analysis, still in progress, highlights the significant role of both public and private sectors in shaping AI ethics and policy.

In the public sector, government agencies have been the predominant creators of AI ethics documents, producing over half of the analyzed materials. Key contributors include countries from the Global North, such as those in the European Union, the United States, and emerging economies like China and India. However, the dominance of wealthier nations raises concerns about the representation of low- and middle-income countries in global AI discussions. The focus of affluent countries on local policy issues may neglect critical areas for the Global South, potentially leading to imbalances in AI solution development.

The private sector, responsible for about a quarter of the documents, consists mainly of large multinational technology corporations like Microsoft and Google. These organizations advocate for their own ethical standards and governance strategies, often promoting self-regulation while opposing government oversight. This creates a power imbalance, with smaller businesses and consumers having limited influence over AI policy.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the need for more inclusive discourse in AI governance to avoid exacerbating global inequalities and to ensure that the interests of vulnerable communities are adequately represented.
