The paper discusses two primary motivations behind the signaling of social responsibility and leadership in the context of ethics documents related to artificial intelligence. 

The motivation of signaling social responsibility involves organizations or governments presenting themselves as socially responsible, regardless of their actual commitment to genuine social responsibility. This signaling can serve various purposes, including protecting reputation, minimizing risks of protests, or differentiating products as ethical. However, it can also be a facade, masking a lack of genuine action or even harmful practices, a phenomenon referred to as "ethics washing."

The motivation of signaling leadership pertains to entities attempting to position themselves as leaders in AI policy and strategy, even if they do not hold an established leadership role. This signaling can enhance access to markets, improve reputation, and create a perception of being a key player in global discussions. Countries like India and Malta showcase their strategies as efforts to join an elite group of nations in AI leadership, indicating the competitive nature of this signaling.

The paper acknowledges that these motivations are complex and often overlap, as organizations may simultaneously signal leadership and responsibility while genuinely engaging in ethical practices. Additionally, the paper emphasizes the need for further research to disentangle stated motivations from actual ones, as well as to assess the impact of ethics documents on global AI governance. 

The influence of these documents extends beyond their individual contents; they can shape public discourse, inspire other organizations, and serve as tools for differentiation in a competitive landscape. The ongoing dialogue among various ethics documents suggests a collaborative yet competitive environment where organizations learn from one another while also striving to establish their unique positions. 

Overall, the paper highlights the multifaceted motivations behind ethics documents and their potential impacts on the global AI landscape, underscoring the importance of understanding the underlying intentions of organizations in this evolving domain.
