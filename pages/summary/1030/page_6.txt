This paper examines ethics and policy themes in artificial intelligence (AI) through an analysis of over 80 AI documents from various creators, including governments and NGOs. It highlights the importance of actionable recommendations, such as the suggestion for the Centre for Data Ethics and Innovation to explore the Open Banking model for secure personal data sharing, contrasting it with Intel's call for comprehensive privacy regulations, which lacks immediate actionability.

The paper discusses several key factors that influence the effectiveness of these documents, including reach and exposure, enforceability and monitoring, and plans for iteration and follow-up. Highly cited documents or those with numerous signatories, like the Montréal Declaration for Responsible AI, are perceived as more valuable. The willingness of organizations to submit to rigorous scrutiny serves as a sign of commitment to their proposals, while plans for ongoing development indicate a serious approach to implementation.

In conclusion, the study offers a typology of motivations behind AI ethics documents and assesses their potential impact on global governance. It emphasizes the need for ongoing research and practice as AI's ethical, social, and policy implications evolve, moving from principles to concrete policies.
