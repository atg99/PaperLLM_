The NGO sector, comprising 18 diverse entities, includes established organizations and newly formed groups focused on AI. It encompasses professional associations, think tanks, and documents from international collaborations, such as the Asilomar AI Principles and the Montréal Declaration for Responsible AI. These documents are notable for their participatory development process, often involving numerous participants and events globally. While they express ethical insights, they frequently adopt an outsider stance, urging changes in the private and public sectors without clear operational plans or target audiences. This raises questions about the influence these organizations can exert on AI governance, despite their citations in national AI strategies.

Organizations produce AI ethics documents for various motivations, which may not always align with stated intents. A typology of six motivations has been developed, categorized into three pairs: end goals, target audiences, and signaling. The first motivation, social responsibility, emphasizes promoting societal benefits and mitigating harm, exemplified by documents such as IEEE's Ethically Aligned Design and the OECD Principles on Artificial Intelligence. The second motivation, competitive advantage, highlights the pursuit of economic and political benefits, as seen in China's AI Development Plan and strategies from Malta and Mexico, although this motivation can coexist with genuine social responsibility.

The next motivations focus on strategic planning and intervention. Internal strategic planning involves creating ethics documents to drive organizational change, guiding R&D and public engagement. In contrast, external strategic intervention aims to influence the broader environment, such as preempting regulations through voluntary standards. Documents may also frame AI ethics narratives, highlighting economic potential while downplaying risks to vulnerable populations. Ultimately, the motivations for producing AI ethics documents can be complex and multifaceted, serving both specific ends and broader strategic purposes.
