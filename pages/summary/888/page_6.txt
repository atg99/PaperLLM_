The paper presents a comparison of various state-of-the-art artistic text style transfer methods, including Image Analogy, Neural Style Transfer (NST), Doodle, T-Effect, and UT-Effect. These methods are evaluated based on their ability to transfer styles using a single style image and its corresponding structure map. The authors highlight that while some methods, like Image Analogy and T-Effect, yield rigid and unnatural contours by directly copying texture patches, others, such as NST and Doodle, struggle with color deviations and may produce unrecognizable text due to implicit representation of shape characteristics.

In contrast, the proposed method utilizes a bidirectional shape matching strategy coupled with adversarial learning to effectively capture and transfer accurate shape characteristics and vivid textures. This results in visually appealing outputs that surpass the quality of the compared methods.

The paper also discusses the network training process, employing a controllable ResBlock architecture, Gaussian noise addition for diversity, and a structured training regimen using the Adam optimizer. The authors detail the parameters and the gradual adjustment of sampling ranges to stabilize training. The experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of their approach in achieving superior artistic text style transfer results. Code and pretrained models are made available for further research.
