This paper presents a novel method for artistic text style transfer that allows for continuous control over various parameters, enhancing the flexibility and quality of stylization compared to existing methods. The qualitative comparison demonstrated in Figure 8 highlights the limitations of two other methods: stroke-controllable neural style transfer (SC-NST) and UT-Effect. SC-NST struggles with glyph deformation despite allowing texture size adjustments, while UT-Effect faces challenges with global stylization consistency and detail preservation due to its greedy patch matching approach. In contrast, the proposed method achieves a smooth continuous transformation with fine details, exemplified by the growing process of leaves in the stylized output.

The efficiency of the proposed method is also notable, requiring only 0.43 seconds per image on a specific CPU and 16 milliseconds with a GPU, which suggests potential for near real-time user interaction. 

An ablation study was conducted to analyze the contributions of different components within the model. The baseline model, which includes only a texture network, showed poor shape consistency. The model without the Controllable ResBlock (CR) demonstrated limited adaptability, while the full model, incorporating both the glyph network and texture network, produced high-quality artistic text with consistent shape and texture.

Additionally, the sketch module was evaluated for its effectiveness in transferring shape characteristics, further strengthening the mapping between source and target domains. Overall, the proposed method outperforms existing techniques in both quality and efficiency, making it a significant advancement in the field of artistic style transfer.
