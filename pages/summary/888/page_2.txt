This paper addresses the challenge of fast controllable artistic text style transfer, specifically focusing on the real-time adjustment of glyph deformations using a single style image. While previous studies have explored fast style transfer methods, there has been little focus on real-time control of glyph deformations, which is essential for effective text style transfer. The authors highlight two main challenges: the subjective nature of glyph deformation, which lacks a clear parameterization, and the absence of large-scale paired datasets for training models on multi-scale glyph stylization.

To overcome these challenges, the paper introduces the Shape-Matching GAN, which employs a bidirectional shape matching strategy to establish mappings between source styles and target glyphs through both backward and forward transfers. The authors propose a sketch module that simplifies the style image into various coarse levels, creating robust multi-scale shape mappings for data-driven learning. Additionally, a scale-controllable module called Controllable ResBlock is developed, enabling the network to infer style features continuously based on user input.

The contributions of this work are threefold: it investigates the problem of fast controllable artistic text style transfer with a novel framework, develops a sketch module for creating paired training data at various scales, and presents the Shape-Matching GAN for generating diversified artistic text in real-time. The paper also reviews related works in image style transfer, artistic text style transfer, and multi-scale style control, emphasizing the advancements made in comparison to existing methods.
