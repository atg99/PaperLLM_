The paper presents a method for text image reconstruction and stylization using Generative Networks. The process involves two main components: structure transfer and texture transfer.

In the structure transfer phase, the generator model GB is trained to reconstruct a sampled text image from a dataset, minimizing the reconstruction loss Lrec_B and incorporating a conditional adversarial loss Ladv_B to ensure the generated images have text-like contours. The total loss function balances these two components along with their respective weights. The output, denoted as ˜Xℓ, represents the simplified shape of the text, which is critical for the glyph network GS.

For the glyph network GS, the authors introduce a Controllable ResBlock that allows for flexible mapping of many-to-one transformations. This design prevents model collapse by utilizing data augmentation techniques and ensuring that GS can adapt to varying levels of deformation without merely memorizing the input. The network is optimized using reconstruction and adversarial losses, alongside an optional glyph legibility loss to maintain key structural features of the original text.

In the texture transfer phase, the resultant structure from GS is used to render textures, framed as an image analogy problem. A feed-forward network GT is trained for this purpose, utilizing reconstruction and adversarial losses, alongside a style loss to enhance rendering quality.

The experimental results section indicates that the proposed method effectively combines structure and texture transfer to achieve robust text stylization, with implementation details provided regarding network architecture based on the Encoder-Decoder framework of StyleNet.
