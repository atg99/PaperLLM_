This paper introduces a novel approach to artistic text style transfer that emphasizes the importance of glyph deformation, texture strength, and stroke size. The authors argue that traditional methods have overlooked the significance of glyph deformation, which is crucial for maintaining the artistic integrity of stylized text. They propose a controllable artistic text style transfer system that allows users to adjust the degree of glyph deformation while ensuring that the stylized text retains the shape characteristics of the reference style.

The paper outlines two primary requirements for their approach: artistry, which ensures that the stylized text mimics the shape characteristics of the style reference, and controllability, which allows for quick and continuous adjustments to glyph deformation. The authors differentiate their method from previous multi-scale style transfer methods that either do not allow for shape adjustments or do so inefficiently.

To achieve their objectives, the authors propose a bidirectional shape matching strategy. This involves simplifying the target structure map to various coarse levels and then mapping these levels back to the stylish shape features to facilitate multi-scale transfer. The method allows for the realization of artistry and controllability through a feed-forward network that learns to map style images from different coarse levels back to themselves.

The paper further discusses the technical challenges involved in simplifying shapes for text images and learning the many-to-one mapping without model collapse. The authors present their Shape-Matching GAN, which decomposes the style transfer process into structure and texture transfer stages, allowing for a focused approach to shape deformation.

In summary, this work presents a systematic and innovative framework for scale-controllable artistic text style transfer, highlighting the significance of glyph deformation and providing a solution that enhances both the artistry and usability of stylized text generation.
