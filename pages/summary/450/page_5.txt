The paper discusses a proposed approach for training a U-Net based discriminator in a Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) setup, specifically enhancing the discriminator's ability to provide consistent per-pixel predictions through the use of a CutMix operation. The core idea is to introduce a consistency regularization loss term, which ensures that the predictions of the discriminator on mixed images align closely with the mixed predictions derived from the individual real and generated images. This is formalized in Equation 7, where the L2 norm is used to measure the differences, promoting consistent outputs from the discriminator.

The overall loss for the discriminator, as outlined in Equation 8, combines the encoder and decoder losses with the consistency loss, weighted by a hyper-parameter λ. The generator's loss remains unchanged, maintaining the original structure of the GAN.

The implementation details describe how the U-Net discriminator is built upon the BigGAN architecture, emphasizing the integration of encoder and decoder features to facilitate effective information flow. Notably, the paper highlights modifications such as the removal of self-attention layers and the use of self-modulation in an unconditional model variant. The discriminator operates with a two-headed structure, computing GAN losses at both heads with equal weight.

In terms of training, the paper outlines the creation of CutMix images during each iteration, gradually increasing the probability of using these images to allow the generator to improve its sample quality. The consistency regularization is shown to have minimal overhead in training, primarily increasing the workload on the discriminator without significantly impacting performance.

Overall, the paper provides a comprehensive framework for enhancing GAN training through consistency regularization and detailed implementation strategies.
