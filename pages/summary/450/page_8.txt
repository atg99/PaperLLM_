This paper presents a novel U-Net based architecture for the discriminator in Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), aimed at enhancing image generation quality by providing both global and local feedback to the generator. The authors conduct an ablation study, demonstrating that integrating the U-Net architecture significantly reduces the median Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) scores compared to the baseline BigGAN model on the FFHQ and COCO-Animals datasets. Specifically, the FID improves from 12.42 to 10.86 for FFHQ and from 16.55 to 15.86 for COCO-Animals. Further enhancements through CutMix augmentation and consistency regularization yield even lower FID scores, achieving 7.63 for FFHQ and 13.87 for COCO-Animals.

The paper also compares the U-Net GAN with state-of-the-art models on the CelebA dataset, where U-Net GAN achieves an impressive FID of 2.95, surpassing BigGAN's score of 4.54 and COCO-GAN’s 5.74. The authors note that the U-Net discriminator's ability to provide nuanced feedback allows the generator to produce images with improved local and global coherence.

Additionally, the paper discusses the complementary nature of the encoder and decoder within the U-Net architecture, highlighting that their predictions often diverge, which enhances the feedback mechanism for the generator. The authors conclude that the proposed U-Net discriminator architecture, combined with consistency regularization, leads to a more effective image synthesis process, as evidenced by the improved performance across multiple datasets.
