This paper introduces a novel approach to enhance the performance of a discriminator in generative models by incorporating both an encoder and a decoder module. The discriminator, referred to as DU, learns to distinguish real from fake images by leveraging global and local information. The loss function for the discriminator is defined as the sum of the losses from both encoder (LDU_enc) and decoder (LDU_dec), allowing for a comprehensive evaluation of the image quality. The encoder loss is based on the scalar outputs, while the decoder loss evaluates per-pixel decisions, capturing both high-level features and local details through skip connections.

To further improve the discriminator's effectiveness, the paper proposes a consistency regularization technique. This method ensures that the discriminator's predictions remain stable under class-domain-altering transformations, such as CutMix. By applying CutMix, which combines patches from real and fake images, the discriminator is trained to focus on semantic and structural differences rather than arbitrary perturbations. A new synthetic training sample is created by mixing real and generated images according to a binary mask, ensuring that the overall mixed image is classified as fake by the discriminator. This approach encourages the generator to produce images that can effectively deceive the refined discriminator, ultimately enhancing the quality of generated images.
