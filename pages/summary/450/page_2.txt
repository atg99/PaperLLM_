This paper addresses the limitations of traditional GANs, particularly the tendency of discriminators to forget previous tasks during training, leading to poor quality in generated images. The authors propose a novel discriminator architecture based on U-Net, which simultaneously outputs global and local decisions regarding image authenticity. This design enhances the discriminator's ability to maintain a robust data representation, thus improving the generator's challenge in fooling the discriminator.

The U-Net discriminator functions as both a classifier and a segmenter, providing spatially coherent feedback to the generator. This architectural change fosters a stronger generator, as it must focus on both local and global image realism. The authors introduce the CutMix augmentation technique to enhance training, promoting consistency in per-pixel predictions and improving the discriminator's semantic and structural understanding.

The U-Net GAN model is evaluated against the BigGAN baseline across various datasets, demonstrating significant improvements in generated sample quality, as indicated by lower FID and IS scores. For instance, the U-Net GAN achieves a 4-point FID improvement on the FFHQ dataset and sets a new record for low FID on the CelebA dataset.

The paper positions itself within the broader context of advancements in GAN architecture, emphasizing the need for improved discriminators that can leverage both global and local features. By focusing on enhancing the discriminator rather than the generator, the proposed U-Net GAN contributes to ongoing research in generative models and their applications in computer vision.
