The paper presents a study on image synthesis using U-Net GAN, focusing on the quality of generated images and smooth interpolations in latent space. The experiments utilize three datasets: FFHQ, CelebA, and a subset of COCO and OpenImages referred to as COCO-Animals. FFHQ and CelebA are used for unconditional image synthesis, while COCO-Animals is employed for class-conditional image synthesis.

FFHQ consists of 70,000 high-quality human face images with diverse attributes, whereas CelebA contains 200,000 images of various celebrities showcasing different facial expressions and poses. The COCO-Animals dataset includes approximately 38,000 images across 10 animal classes, presenting a challenging scenario due to its limited size and variability in poses and backgrounds.

For evaluation, the study relies on the Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) and the Inception Score (IS), with FID being the primary metric due to its consistency with human judgment regarding image realism and variation. The experiments involve generating 50,000 synthetic images, with results based on the best or median FID from multiple runs. The training process is detailed, noting 400,000 iterations for FFHQ and COCO-Animals and 800,000 for CelebA, utilizing moving averages for generator weights to enhance stability in evaluations.
