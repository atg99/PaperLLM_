The paper presents a novel U-Net based discriminator architecture for generative adversarial networks (GANs) aimed at improving the synthesis of coherent images with realistic object shapes and textures. Traditional GANs face challenges in generating images that maintain both global and local coherence. The proposed U-Net discriminator provides detailed per-pixel feedback to the generator while also offering global image feedback, enhancing the overall quality of generated samples.

A key innovation in this work is the introduction of a per-pixel consistency regularization technique inspired by CutMix data augmentation. This encourages the U-Net discriminator to focus on semantic and structural differences between real and generated images, which further improves its training and the quality of the outputs. The results demonstrate that the U-Net discriminator outperforms state-of-the-art methods, achieving an average improvement of 2.7 FID points compared to the BigGAN baseline across various datasets, including FFHQ, CelebA, and COCO-Animals.

The introduction emphasizes that while GANs have seen significant advancements, challenges in achieving global semantic coherence and detail precision remain. The architecture's capacity to provide spatially coherent feedback allows the generator to iteratively refine its outputs, leading to higher fidelity images. The findings underline the importance of discriminator design in the GAN framework, suggesting that a U-Net based approach can significantly enhance image generation quality.
