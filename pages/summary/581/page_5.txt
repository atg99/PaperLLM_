The paper investigates the similarity between different instances of two types of neural network models: P-T and RI-T, focusing on feature similarity and model parameter distance. Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA) is employed to measure feature similarity across various layers of the networks, revealing that instances of P-T exhibit high similarity, particularly in the penultimate layer. In contrast, RI-T instances show significantly lower similarity to each other and to P-T instances, highlighting the impact of initialization on feature reuse.

Additionally, the paper examines the distance in parameter space, finding that RI-T models are generally farther apart than P-T models. This trend persists across individual modules and increases in higher layers of the network.

The authors also explore the loss landscape, identifying performance barriers based on the flatness of the loss basin. A flat basin allows for local perturbations without degrading performance, while narrow basins lead to quick increases in loss when deviating from optimal solutions. Through linear interpolation of weights from two distinct checkpoints, the study finds that models within the same flat basin maintain performance, whereas those from different basins face barriers. The results emphasize the importance of feature reuse and initialization in model performance and generalization.
