The paper investigates the effects of block-shuffling input images on the performance and optimization speed of models in transfer learning tasks. It presents findings based on test accuracy and average training accuracy over 100 fine-tuning epochs, with results illustrated in Figure 3. Key observations include:

1. **Performance Decline**: Both the RI-T (Random Initialization Transfer) and P-T (Pre-trained Transfer) models experience a drop in final performance as block sizes decrease, indicating increased task difficulty.
2. **Relative Accuracy Differences**: The relative accuracy difference between models trained from pre-trained and random weights diminishes with smaller block sizes, supporting the idea that reduced feature reuse leads to lesser benefits.
3. **Quickdraw Anomaly**: Unlike other tasks, quickdraw does not follow the trend of decreasing relative accuracy difference with smaller block sizes, suggesting that the pre-trained weights provide additional, non-visual benefits.
4. **Optimization Speed**: P-T shows stable optimization speed, while RI-T's optimization speed declines significantly with smaller block sizes, indicating that the advantages of transferred weights are independent of feature reuse.

The study concludes that feature reuse is crucial in transfer learning, especially when the downstream task shares visual characteristics with the pre-training domain. However, it also highlights the importance of low-level statistics, which, despite lacking semantic structure, positively impact transfer learning and optimization speed.

Additionally, the paper explores model mistakes beyond accuracy metrics, analyzing common and uncommon errors between models. It finds that P-T models tend to have fewer uncommon mistakes compared to RI-T models, with P-T making errors on ambiguous examples while RI-T errors occur on easier samples. This observation suggests that P-T models, having stronger priors, struggle more to adapt to the target domain. Overall, the results emphasize the nuanced interplay between feature reuse and model adaptation in transfer learning.
