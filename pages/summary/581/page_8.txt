The paper presents an analysis of transfer learning performance using different pre-training checkpoints, focusing on test accuracy and optimization speed after fine-tuning. The study highlights the significance of the optimal checkpoint, identified by the best validation accuracy during training, as a more effective starting point for evaluating network criticality compared to the final weight values. 

Criticality, defined as the sum of module criticalities, is explored through various layers of the network, revealing that lower layers capture more general features while higher layers specialize in target-specific features. The analysis indicates that as the path from input to output progresses, modules become increasingly critical, corroborating previous findings in the literature.

The investigation into which pre-trained checkpoint is most beneficial for transfer learning shows that performance improvements are more pronounced when starting from checkpoints where pre-training performance has plateaued, rather than solely relying on the highest pre-training performance. Additionally, while optimization speed benefits increase with checkpoint index, the final performance improvements continue to rise significantly beyond early checkpoints for certain tasks.

In summary, the findings emphasize the importance of checkpoint selection in transfer learning, demonstrating that pre-training performance and optimization speed are not always directly correlated with the effectiveness of transfer learning outcomes.
