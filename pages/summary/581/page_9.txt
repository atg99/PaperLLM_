This paper examines the relationship between optimization speed and final performance in transfer learning, concluding that improvements in speed do not necessarily correlate with performance gains. It highlights that earlier checkpoints in pre-training are often outside the convergence basin of the final model, which aligns with observations regarding the loss landscape. The authors argue that starting from earlier checkpoints can be beneficial, as performance plateaus after certain training stages.

The paper reviews related literature on transfer learning, noting that previous studies have questioned the universal success of transfer learning across tasks. It discusses findings from various authors regarding the generality of pretrained features, the significance of model size, and the impact of feature reuse in different domains, particularly in visual and natural language processing.

The authors investigate the loss landscape's flatness as an indicator of generalization performance and explore linear path connectivity in the parameter space between solutions. They provide insights into what is transferred during transfer learning and identify the roles of different network modules.

The conclusion emphasizes the importance of feature reuse and the characteristics of layers in neural networks, particularly that lower layers capture more general features. Future work involves exploring improved network initialization methods, such as using singular values for pre-trained models, and examining the effects of randomization on performance while maintaining convergence within the same basin. The study also suggests that averaging models within the same basin can yield stable performance outcomes.
