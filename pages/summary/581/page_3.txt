The paper investigates the effectiveness of transfer learning by comparing different model training strategies: pre-trained networks, networks initialized randomly, and models fine-tuned on target domains. It introduces notations for these strategies, including RI for random initialization, P for pre-trained models, RI-T for models trained from random initialization on the target domain, and P-T for models fine-tuned from pre-trained weights.

The study utilizes IMAGENET as the source domain and examines its transfer to various target domains, including CHEXPERT and three sets from DOMAINNET. The authors emphasize the hierarchical nature of human visual processing and draw parallels to feature hierarchies in convolutional neural networks. They assert that feature reuse is crucial for transfer learning, especially when target domains are small or lack diversity.

Results indicate that models fine-tuned from pre-trained weights (P-T) demonstrate significant performance boosts over those trained from random initialization (RI-T), particularly in domains visually similar to the source. However, even in visually distant domains, such as CHEXPERT and quickdraw, transfer learning still yields performance improvements. The authors also highlight the faster convergence of P-T compared to RI-T, suggesting that pre-trained weights provide benefits beyond mere feature reuse.

To further explore the role of feature reuse, the authors conducted experiments with modified downstream tasks involving block-shuffling of images, demonstrating that even when high-level features are disrupted, transfer learning can still be effective. This indicates that the advantages of transfer learning may extend beyond visual similarity between source and target domains.
