The paper presents an analysis of performance barriers across different solutions by evaluating test accuracy on DOMAINNETrea and quickdraw, as well as test AUC on CHEXPERT. The terminology of "basin" is critically examined, defining it as areas in parameter space with low loss values. The authors emphasize the importance of convex hulls and linear interpolation to avoid trivial connectivity results, proposing a formal definition for a basin in terms of a loss function and specific properties that must be met. 

Three key requirements for a convex set to be considered a basin are identified: the loss values for most points should be close to the expected loss, the loss for points near the basin should exceed the expected loss, and any extrapolation from points within the basin should result in increased loss. 

Figure 4 illustrates interpolation results, revealing no performance barriers between pre-trained solutions, indicating that pre-trained weights lead optimization towards a flat loss basin. Conversely, significant performance barriers are noted between solutions from random initializations, suggesting that different initializations can lead to disparate optimization landscapes. This finding underscores the role of initialization in navigating loss landscapes effectively.
