In the paper "What is being transferred in transfer learning," the authors, Behnam Neyshabur, Hanie Sedghi, and Chiyuan Zhang, explore the mechanisms behind successful transfer learning, particularly in scenarios where data is limited in the target domain. They highlight the importance of understanding which aspects of the neural network contribute to effective knowledge transfer from a source domain to a target domain.

The authors utilize various datasets, including CHEXPERT, a medical imaging dataset, and DOMAIN NET, which encompasses a range of visual styles from real images to sketches, to analyze transfer learning's effectiveness across diverse domains. Through their experiments, they investigate the concept of feature reuse by employing block-shuffling techniques on images, which disrupts visual features while preserving low-level statistics.

The findings reveal that while feature reuse is significant for successful transfer, it is not the sole factor; low-level statistical properties of the data also contribute to the transfer's efficacy. The study concludes that when training on pre-trained weights, models remain within the same basin of the loss landscape, suggesting that different instances of the model exhibit similarity in both feature and parameter spaces. This research provides valuable insights into the underlying processes of transfer learning and emphasizes the need for further exploration to fully comprehend its dynamics.
