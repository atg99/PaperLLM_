The paper discusses a model for session-based recommendations that integrates Graph Neural Networks (GNN) and Self-Attention mechanisms, referred to as GC-SAN. The output of the GNN layer is computed using update and reset gates, which determine the preservation or discarding of information. The self-attention layer enhances the model's ability to capture global dependencies and transitions within session data. The process involves feeding latent vectors from the GNN into the self-attention layer, followed by a point-wise feed-forward network to introduce nonlinearity and a residual connection to mitigate transmission loss.

The model employs multi-layer self-attention to extract sequential information, allowing for a more nuanced understanding of user behavior. The final session representation combines long-term preferences and current interests, leading to predictions for the next user clicks. The objective function for training minimizes the prediction error and includes regularization to prevent overfitting.

Experimental results are set to evaluate the performance of GC-SAN against state-of-the-art methods, analyzing the effects of hyper-parameters like weight factors and embedding sizes. The effectiveness of GC-SAN is tested on real-world datasets, Diginetica and Retailrocket, ensuring data quality through filtering and preprocessing steps. Overall, the proposed model aims to improve session-based recommendations by leveraging the strengths of GNNs and self-attention mechanisms.
