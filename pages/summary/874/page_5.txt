The paper presents a comparative study of various recommendation methods using two datasets: Diginetica and Retailrocket. The evaluation metrics employed include Hit Rate (HR@N), Mean Reciprocal Rank (MRR@N), and Normalized Discounted Cumulative Gain (NDCG@N), focused on recommending the top 5 and 10 items.

Table 1 summarizes the performance of several algorithms, including a simple popularity-based method (Pop), BPR-MF, IKNN, FPMC, GRU4Rec, STAMP, SR-GNN, and the proposed GC-SAN model. The results indicate that traditional methods like Pop perform poorly, while BPR-MF improves through personalization. IKNN and FPMC show variable performance across datasets, with neural network methods consistently outperforming traditional baselines. Among neural models, STAMP demonstrates superior performance, leveraging short-term memory, while SR-GNN excels by utilizing graph neural networks.

GC-SAN, employing a self-attention mechanism to capture long-range dependencies and short-term interests, achieves the highest performance across all metrics in both datasets. The results underline the effectiveness of deep learning techniques and the potential of GC-SAN for session-based recommendation tasks, marking a significant advancement in the field. 

Dataset statistics reveal 858,107 clicks for Diginetica and 710,856 for Retailrocket, with average session lengths of approximately 5.97 and 5.43, respectively. Overall, GC-SAN's performance highlights its validity and efficacy in session-based recommendations.
