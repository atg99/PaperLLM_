This paper presents a novel approach to session-based recommendation systems by introducing the Graph Contextual Self-Attention Network (GC-SAN), which combines the strengths of self-attention networks and graph neural networks (GNN). The primary objective is to enhance the representation of session sequences by leveraging the ability of self-attention to capture long-range dependencies and the GNN's capability to encode rich local contextual information.

GC-SAN constructs a directed graph from historical session sequences, enabling it to capture transitions among neighboring items. The model generates latent vectors for all nodes in the graph, which are then utilized in a self-attention mechanism to model long-range dependencies across the session. The session embedding vectors are formed by aggregating the latent vectors of all nodes, allowing the model to predict the probability of a user clicking on the next item by combining their global interests with local session-specific interests.

The contributions of this work include the development of a new graph contextual self-attention model that effectively enhances recommendation performance by integrating GNN and self-attention. The model addresses the limitations of traditional item-to-item recommendation methods and prior neural network approaches by providing both local and global contextualized representations.

Extensive experiments conducted on benchmark datasets demonstrate the effectiveness and superiority of GC-SAN compared to state-of-the-art methods. The paper also discusses the related work in session-based recommendation, highlighting the transition from classical methods to neural network and attention-based models, ultimately positioning GC-SAN as a significant advancement in this domain.
