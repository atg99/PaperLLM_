This paper presents the Graph Contextualized Self-Attention Network (GC-SAN) for session-based recommendation, integrating graph neural networks (GNN) to enhance performance. The authors analyze the model's effectiveness through metrics such as Hit Rate at 10 (HR@10) and Normalized Discounted Cumulative Gain at 10 (NDCG@10) across two datasets: Diginetica and Retailrocket.

Key findings include:
1. GC-SAN significantly outperforms traditional methods, with HR@10 scores of 0.5351 for Diginetica and 0.4380 for Retailrocket when employing the GNN module, compared to lower performance without it.
2. The weight factor influences performance, with optimal values between 0.4 and 0.8, indicating the importance of balancing long-range dependencies and short-term interests.
3. The number of stacked self-attention blocks (k) enhances performance, though too many can lead to loss of low-layer information, suggesting an optimal range for k.
4. The embedding size also impacts results, with GC-SAN performing better than baselines across various dimensions, peaking at an embedding size of around 100.

The paper concludes that GC-SAN effectively combines local short-term dynamics with global dependencies, consistently outperforming state-of-the-art methods in session-based recommendation tasks.
