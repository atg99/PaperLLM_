The paper presents a detailed analysis of a first-order discrete-time multi-agent system involving two types of agents, characterized by their states and interactions governed by specific protocols. The authors derive key parameters (k2, k3, k4) based on the agents' dynamics and network structure, establishing conditions under which consensus is achieved.

The central result is that the system reaches consensus if the underlying directed communication graph has a directed spanning tree. This is shown through mathematical inequalities and limits which demonstrate that as time progresses, the distance between agents' states converges to zero. The authors also discuss the implications of the feedback gain k1 and the network's connectivity, highlighting that proper tuning of k1 ensures the system's stability.

Furthermore, the paper extends the consensus protocol to accommodate both absolute and relative velocity information, indicating its versatility in application. The analysis is framed within a unified framework that connects discrete-time and sampled-data multi-agent systems, emphasizing the challenges posed by the hybrid nature of the proposed consensus protocol.

In summary, the findings underscore the critical role of graph structure in achieving consensus among hybrid multi-agent systems, providing a foundation for future research and applications in multi-agent coordination.
