The paper discusses the coordination control of heterogeneous multi-agent systems, particularly focusing on hybrid systems that include both continuous-time and discrete-time dynamic agents. The authors extend previous work on consensus protocols to address second-order consensus in these hybrid systems using graph theory and system transformation methods.

Key contributions of the paper include the design of two types of consensus protocols tailored for hybrid multi-agent systems, the establishment of necessary and sufficient conditions for achieving second-order consensus, and the development of a unified framework that integrates second-order consensus for both discrete-time and sampled-data systems.

The paper is structured as follows: Section 2 introduces foundational concepts from graph theory and describes the hybrid multi-agent system. Section 3 presents the main results, while Section 4 includes numerical simulations that validate the theoretical findings. The conclusion is drawn in Section 5.

The authors also provide a thorough explanation of algebraic graph theory, detailing the interactions among agents through weighted directed graphs, and define the characteristics of second-order consensus, which requires that both positions and velocities of agents converge over time.

Overall, the paper contributes significantly to the understanding of coordination in hybrid multi-agent systems, emphasizing the importance of consensus protocols in achieving desired collective behaviors.
