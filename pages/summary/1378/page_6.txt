The paper presents a detailed analysis of a first-order discrete-time multi-agent system, focusing on the consensus behavior of agents within a directed graph framework. It begins by formulating the system's dynamics, represented by equations that relate the states of agents and their interactions. The authors derive conditions under which the agents reach consensus, specifically highlighting the importance of the directed spanning tree property of the graph.

Key equations are provided to illustrate the evolution of the agents' states over time, with variables representing the agents' positions and velocities. The analysis includes inequalities that demonstrate the convergence of agent states and velocities to each other as time progresses, ultimately leading to the conclusion that the system achieves consensus.

The paper emphasizes that under certain conditions, including the non-zero diagonal elements of the interaction matrix and specific parameter settings, the distances between agents' states and velocities diminish to zero as time approaches infinity. This indicates that the agents not only synchronize but also stabilize their velocities, confirming the robustness of the consensus under the specified system dynamics.

Overall, the findings contribute to the understanding of how multi-agent systems can achieve agreement and stability through appropriate interaction designs and system configurations.
