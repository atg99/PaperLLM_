The paper addresses the second-order consensus problem in hybrid multi-agent systems, which consist of both continuous-time and discrete-time dynamic agents. Acknowledging the inherent heterogeneity of multi-agent systems, the authors propose two effective consensus protocols tailored for these hybrid systems. The analysis utilizes algebraic graph theory and system transformation methods, establishing necessary and sufficient conditions for achieving second-order consensus.

The introduction highlights the significance of multi-agent systems in various fields, including social and biological networks, and emphasizes the importance of consensus in coordinating the behaviors of autonomous agents. Previous research primarily focused on first-order dynamic agents, while this study expands the scope to second-order dynamics, relevant to systems governed by Newton's laws, such as robotics.

The paper offers theoretical insights supported by two examples that demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed protocols. Overall, it contributes to the understanding of consensus mechanisms in heterogeneous multi-agent environments.
