The paper discusses consensus protocols for hybrid multi-agent systems, focusing on second-order consensus. The authors establish that certain functions are increasing over a specified domain and present two types of consensus protocols for these systems.

In the first case, it is assumed that agents communicate with neighbors and update control inputs based on absolute velocity information at specific sampling times. The control input is defined through a consensus protocol involving an adjacency matrix and a feedback gain. A key theorem is presented, stating that the hybrid multi-agent system will achieve second-order consensus if a directed spanning tree exists in the communication graph and if the feedback gain is appropriately bounded.

The proof provided demonstrates the sufficiency of the conditions for achieving consensus by deriving relationships between the states and control inputs of the agents over time. The authors derive equations that describe the evolution of the agents' positions and velocities, ultimately linking them to the consensus criteria.

Overall, the paper contributes to the understanding of consensus mechanisms in hybrid multi-agent systems, providing theoretical foundations and conditions necessary for achieving desired consensus outcomes.
