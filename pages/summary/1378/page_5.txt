The paper presents a mathematical proof focusing on the dynamics of a second-order ordinary differential equation governing a system's behavior over discrete time intervals. It begins by establishing the relationship between position and velocity using equations derived from the system's dynamics. The sufficiency of conditions for stability is explored through the solution of the differential equation, leading to expressions for position and velocity at subsequent time steps.

Key findings include the derivation of specific constants and their relationships, such as r1 and r2, which characterize the system's response. The proof also addresses scenarios where certain parameters are zero, resulting in alternative expressions for position and velocity. The conditions under which these parameters lead to system stability are emphasized, particularly through the positivity of certain coefficients derived from the system's dynamics.

The paper concludes by demonstrating the implications of these mathematical formulations for understanding the stability and behavior of the system over time, reinforcing the significance of selected parameters in ensuring desired dynamical properties.
