This paper addresses the challenges of compressing Graph Neural Networks (GNNs), contrasting them with methods used for Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs). While CNN compression techniques such as designing shallow networks, pruning, compact layers, and quantization—including binarization—have been effective in vision tasks, GNNs present unique hurdles. These include the larger size of input graph data compared to the models, the shallow nature of many GNNs like the standard Graph Convolutional Network (GCN), and the distinct characteristics of node similarity in high-level semantic spaces versus low-level feature spaces.

To mitigate memory and complexity issues, the paper introduces the Binary Graph Convolutional Network (Bi-GCN), which binarizes both parameters and node attributes. This method reduces quantization errors by utilizing multiple feature selectors and attention weights, leading to significant reductions in computational complexity and memory consumption. A new back propagation method is also proposed that considers the relationships among binary weights, enhancing training efficiency.

The results indicate that Bi-GCN can achieve about 30 times reduction in memory consumption and approximately 47 times acceleration in inference speed while maintaining comparable accuracy to standard GCNs across benchmark datasets. The paper further discusses related works in sampling-based GNNs and CNN binarization, highlighting the significance of the proposed method in addressing memory and computational challenges in GNNs. 

Key contributions of the paper include the introduction of Bi-GCN, a new back propagation method, and evidence of performance improvements in memory efficiency and speed.
