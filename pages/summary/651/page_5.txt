This paper presents a back propagation process for training a binarized graph convolutional layer, referred to as Bi-GCN. The method begins with the input of gradients from the layer above and outputs the gradients for the current layer. The process involves calculating gradients of the binarized weights and the layer's output, using binary operations such as XNOR and bit count to replace traditional inner product calculations. This approach significantly enhances processing speed by minimizing floating-point operations.

The training process employs a loss function consistent with vanilla GCN, while introducing a novel back-propagation method tailored for the binarized layer. The gradients are approximated using a binary gradient to retain essential gradient information while controlling the influence of excessively large gradients through an indicator function. This function acts similarly to a hard tanh function, preserving performance and stability.

The paper also discusses the advantages of model size compression and processing acceleration achieved through binarization. Specifically, it highlights a significant reduction in parameter size, with a compression ratio that can reach approximately 31 times for a two-layered Bi-GCN compared to a full-precision GCN. This reduction is particularly beneficial for memory consumption, as the binarized parameters require less storage than their floating-point counterparts, thereby enhancing the efficiency of graph convolutional networks. Overall, the proposed Bi-GCN demonstrates promising results in both computational speed and resource utilization.
