In this paper, we introduce Bi-GCN, a novel approach that binarizes both the network parameters and the input node features. By converting the original matrix multiplications into binary operations, Bi-GCN significantly reduces memory consumption and accelerates inference speed. The theoretical analysis shows an average reduction of approximately 30 times in memory usage and a 47 times increase in inference speed on citation networks.

To effectively train Bi-GCN, we propose a new gradient approximation method for back-propagation. Extensive experiments validate that Bi-GCN achieves performance comparable to full-precision baselines while offering substantial improvements in efficiency. Furthermore, our binarization technique is versatile and can be applied to other GNN architectures, as demonstrated through various experimental results.

In conclusion, Bi-GCN addresses the limitations of current GNNs regarding memory and computational resource constraints, providing a promising solution for large-scale graph representation learning.
