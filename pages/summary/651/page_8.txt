This paper introduces Bi-GCN, a binarized version of Graph Convolutional Networks (GCN), aimed at enhancing inference speed and reducing memory consumption. The authors demonstrate that by binarizing both network parameters and node attributes, Bi-GCN can replace floating-point operations with binary operations, achieving an average reduction of approximately 30 times in memory usage and a 47 times acceleration in inference speed on citation networks.

The experimental results, presented in Tables 2 and 3, showcase the performance of various networks in transductive and inductive learning tasks across different datasets, including Cora, PubMed, Reddit, and Flickr. Bi-GCN maintains competitive accuracy compared to traditional GCN, indicating its effectiveness in learning from reduced representations.

The study also explores the effects of model depth, revealing that Bi-GCN performs better than GCN in deeper architectures, with a slower decline in accuracy and a better mitigation of overfitting issues. Additionally, it highlights that Bi-GCN can save more memory as the number of layers increases, while still achieving significant computational cost reductions.

Overall, the findings suggest that the binarization approach can be generalized to other graph neural networks, maintaining performance while offering substantial efficiency gains. The research is supported by various grants from Chinese scientific institutions.
