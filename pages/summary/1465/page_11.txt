The paper discusses the effectiveness of Contrastive Self-supervised Learning (CSSL) for language understanding, specifically through the CERT framework. It presents experimental results on two language tasks: CoLA and STS-B, highlighting the performance of different data augmentation methods, specifically back-translation and Easy Data Augmentation (EDA). 

The findings indicate that back-translation outperforms EDA in both tasks, likely due to its sentence-level augmentation that captures global semantics, while EDA focuses on local word or phrase modifications. This distinction makes augmented sentences from back-translation more challenging to predict as originating from the same original sentence, which is beneficial for developing better representations in CSSL.

Additionally, the paper examines the impact of different pretraining corpora on CSSL performance. Using training data from the target task (CoLA) yields better results for that task, while BERT's pretraining corpus is more effective for STS-B. The authors conclude that no definitive preference emerges between the two approaches, suggesting practitioners experiment with both to determine the most effective strategy for their specific tasks.

The paper also references the success of large-scale pretraining models, such as GPT, in language representation learning, which sets a context for the importance of effective pretraining in enhancing model performance.
