The paper presents CERT, a novel pretraining approach for language representation learning that builds upon existing models like BERT by incorporating contrastive self-supervised learning. CERT enhances pretrained models by training them on target task input texts, utilizing back-translation to create augmented sentences. The model learns to determine if two augmented sentences originate from the same original sentence. Evaluations on 11 tasks from the GLUE benchmark show that CERT outperforms BERT in most cases, highlighting the effectiveness of contrastive self-supervised learning in improving language representation.

Looking ahead, the authors intend to explore more complex loss functions for self-supervised learning, particularly a ranking-based loss. This method would involve augmenting sentences with a ranked list of related sentences and training the model to predict their order, which may lead to improved representation learning compared to the binary classification used in current contrastive methods.
