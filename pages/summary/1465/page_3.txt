The paper "CERT: Contrastive Self-supervised Learning for Language Understanding" explores the effectiveness of the CERT model through ablation studies that assess the impact of sentence augmentation methods and the source of pretraining corpora on its performance. The structure of the paper is organized into several sections: the methods and experiments are presented in Sections 2 and 3, related works are reviewed in Section 4, and the paper concludes in Section 5.

In Section 2, the authors discuss the pretraining of Transformers for language understanding, highlighting the prevalence of the Transformer architecture in recent language representation models. They describe the Transformer as an efficient encode-decoder architecture that utilizes self-attention instead of recurrent neural networks, enabling better parallel computation and capturing dependencies among tokens effectively. Each Transformer block consists of a self-attention layer and a position-wise feed-forward layer, with residual connections and layer normalization applied.

The subsection on BERT explains its architecture as a multi-layer bidirectional Transformer encoder that employs masked language modeling for training. BERT randomly masks input tokens and predicts them using the final hidden vectors produced by the encoder, allowing it to learn contextual representations. For downstream tasks, a new layer can be added on top of the pretrained BERT model, which can then be fine-tuned with task-specific labeled data. 

Overall, the paper emphasizes the significance of Transformer-based models in language processing and sets the stage for discussing the CERT model's contributions to this field.
