The paper introduces CERT, a novel approach for language representation pretraining that leverages contrastive self-supervised learning (CSSL). Traditional methods primarily focus on word-level predictions, which may overlook the global semantics at the sentence level. CERT addresses this limitation by defining predictive tasks at the sentence level, thereby enhancing the capacity to capture sentence-level semantics.

The methodology employs back-translation for sentence augmentation, creating multiple augmented versions of a source sentence. A Momentum Contrast (MoCo) framework is utilized to compare these augmented sentences, establishing whether they originate from the same original sentence or not. This comparison generates positive and negative pairs, which are used to compute contrastive losses that guide the pretraining of a language model like BERT.

The effectiveness of CERT is evaluated across 11 natural language understanding tasks within the GLUE benchmark, where it outperforms BERT on seven tasks, matches BERT on two, and underperforms on two others. Overall, CERT achieves a higher average score than BERT, demonstrating its efficacy in capturing sentence-level semantics through contrastive self-supervised learning.

Key contributions of the paper include the introduction of CERT as a new pretraining method focused on sentence-level tasks and its successful application in improving performance on a range of natural language understanding tasks.
