In section 5.3 of the paper, the authors present the results of their proposed model, CERT, in comparison to BERT on various GLUE tasks. The performance metrics are based on five random restarts of fine-tuning, with the median and best performances reported. 

The findings reveal that CERT outperforms BERT in median performance across five tasks (CoLA, RTE, QNLI, SST-2, QQP) and matches BERT on the other five tasks. In terms of best performance, CERT surpasses BERT on seven tasks, including CoLA, RTE, QNLI, STS-B, MNLI-m, MNLI-mm, and QQP, while performing similarly on MRPC and WNLI, and slightly worse on SST-2. 

Notably, CERT exhibits more significant improvements over BERT on tasks with smaller training datasets, such as CoLA and RTE, suggesting that the contrastive self-supervised learning (CSSL) approach is particularly beneficial for low-resource NLP tasks. The results indicate that CERT is effective in enhancing language representations, especially when training data is limited. 

Additionally, the authors provide a comparison with other state-of-the-art pretraining methods like XLNet, RoBERTa, ERNIE 2.0, and ALBERT, highlighting that CERT achieves competitive performance with a lower computational cost and on a smaller text corpus.
