The paper discusses advancements in language understanding models, particularly focusing on the evolution of architectures like GPT, BERT, BART, ALBERT, RoBERTa, XLNet, T5, and ERNIE 2.0. GPT defines a marginal probability for sequences using a Transformer decoder, with GPT-2 enhancing this by adjusting layer normalization. BERT-GPT combines BERT's encoding with GPT's decoding but suffers from performance issues due to separate pretraining. BART improves upon this by jointly training the encoder and decoder through a corruption and reconstruction method. ALBERT optimizes BERT's efficiency while RoBERTa enhances BERT's performance through careful tuning of training processes. XLNet addresses BERT's limitations by learning bidirectional contexts through permutation-based training. T5 proposes a unified text-to-text framework for various tasks, and ERNIE 2.0 introduces continual pretraining for improved language comprehension.

In the realm of contrastive self-supervised learning, the paper highlights its growing significance. Key contributions include research on data-efficient image recognition through contrastive predictive coding, development of contrastive representations for reinforcement learning, and supervised contrastive learning methods that enhance class clustering. Additionally, approaches for commonsense reasoning and contrastive learning atop pretrained networks are discussed, showcasing the versatility and effectiveness of contrastive methods in various contexts.
