The paper presents CERT, a novel approach for enhancing language understanding through contrastive self-supervised learning at the sentence level. Traditional pretrained language models like BERT and GPT primarily focus on token-level predictive tasks, which may not effectively capture the semantics of entire sentences. To overcome this limitation, CERT employs sentence augmentations generated via back-translation and fine-tunes a pretrained language encoder, such as BERT, by assessing whether pairs of augmented sentences derive from the same original sentence. 

The authors demonstrate the effectiveness of CERT by evaluating it on the GLUE benchmark, where it outperforms BERT in seven out of eleven natural language understanding tasks, matches BERT's performance in two tasks, and performs slightly worse in two others. Overall, CERT achieves a higher average score across all tasks compared to BERT. The methodology is straightforward and can be integrated into existing NLP pipelines. The associated data and code are publicly accessible for further research.
