This paper presents a comparative analysis of various Visual Question Answering (VQA) models on the VQA 2.0, TDIUC, and VQA-CP v2 datasets. The results indicate that the MuRel model achieves state-of-the-art performance across different metrics, particularly excelling in Overall Accuracy and arithmetic mean of per-type accuracies (A-MPT) on the TDIUC dataset, where it outperforms the second-best model significantly. MuRel's architecture, which incorporates a pairwise structure, enhances its ability to process spatial and semantic relationships, leading to notable improvements in areas such as Positional reasoning and Counting questions.

While MuRel shows strong performance in several tasks, its results on the Utility and Affordances task are notably lower, which affects its harmonic mean of per-type accuracies (H-MPT). This is attributed to the nature of the questions that focus on the potential uses of objects rather than direct visual recognition.

On the VQA-CP v2 dataset, MuRel demonstrates a substantial improvement over previous baselines, particularly in addressing question-oriented bias. The paper highlights that the iterative structure of MuRel is less susceptible to overfitting compared to traditional attention mechanisms, which often rely heavily on linguistic patterns.

Qualitative results reveal that MuRel effectively identifies and retains the most relevant image regions during processing, showcasing its capability to focus on pertinent information while discarding less relevant data.

Overall, the findings underscore MuRel's advancements in VQA tasks, particularly its robustness against question bias and its effective modeling of visual relationships.
