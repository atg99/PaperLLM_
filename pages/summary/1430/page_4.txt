The paper presents the MuRel network, a novel architecture designed for reasoning over visual information in relation to specific questions. Unlike traditional models that rely on scalar pairwise attention and Gaussian graph convolutions, the MuRel cell constructs relationship vectors by merging spatial and semantic representations for each image region. It computes a context vector by aggregating pairwise links, utilizing a max operator to minimize noise from interactions among regions.

The relationship vector is formulated through a bilinear fusion operator, which allows the model to learn spatial concepts and semantic correlations. The MuRel cell updates visual features iteratively, refining region representations with contextual and question information while addressing the vanishing gradient problem through a residual function.

The MuRel network processes state vectors through multiple iterations of the MuRel cell, generating a scene representation that captures object relations in relation to the question. This representation is then merged with the question embedding to compute scores for potential answers, with the highest score determining the final answer.

Additionally, the network supports advanced visualization techniques, allowing for the identification of important relationships between image regions relevant to a specific question. Contribution maps can be generated after each iteration, providing insights into the relevance of each region's features. The model also visualizes pairwise relationships by identifying the region most impacted by pairwise modeling and assessing the contributions of other regions to that focal region. Overall, the MuRel network demonstrates effective integration of visual context and question-specific information for improved reasoning and interpretability in visual tasks.
