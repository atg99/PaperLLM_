This paper presents an ablation study of the MuRel model, which is evaluated on the VQA 2.0, VQA-CP v2, and TDIUC datasets to investigate the contributions of its pairwise module and iterative processing. The results demonstrate that MuRel consistently outperforms baseline models, achieving notable accuracy gains across all datasets. Specifically, it shows improvements of +1.70 on VQA 2.0 and +1.50 on VQA-CP v2, indicating its superior ability to model interactions between questions and images, particularly under varying answer distributions.

The ablation study reveals that the inclusion of the pairwise module and iterative processing significantly enhances performance. The pairwise module alone provides accuracy gains of +0.44 on VQA 2.0, while the iterative process contributes +0.59. The combination of both methods yields the highest accuracy, with a notable gain of +1.01 on VQA 2.0, highlighting their complementary nature.

Further analysis of the iterative process shows that varying the number of reasoning steps impacts model performance. Networks with two and three iterations improve accuracy over single-step networks, though performance declines with four iterations, particularly for counting tasks, suggesting that more complex reasoning may benefit from deeper relational modeling.

In a comparison with state-of-the-art models on the VQA 2.0 dataset, MuRel outperforms recent contributions, including MU-TAN and MLB, indicating the effectiveness of retaining local information in multimodal vectors. Despite not extensively tuning hyperparameters, MuRel's performance remains competitive with top models, suggesting its robust design and ability to leverage spatial-semantic pairwise modeling effectively.
