The MuRel network is designed to enhance visual question answering (VQA) by merging question embeddings with spatially-grounded visual representations. It operates through a single MuRel cell, which processes localized vectors and employs a multimodal fusion component to update their representations. This network is notable for modeling pairwise relations between image regions by integrating spatial and semantic information. The importance map generated during processing reflects how often each region contributes maximally to the output.

In comparison to the FiLM network, which uses multiple residual cells and feature-wise affine modulation for multimodal interaction, MuRel simplifies the architecture by utilizing a single cell and a bilinear fusion strategy. This approach is deemed more effective for real-world data. While FiLM relies on a fixed spatial grid for feature representation, MuRel treats regions as a complete graph, allowing for complex relational modeling even among distant regions.

The effectiveness of the MuRel network is validated through experiments on three datasets: VQA 2.0, VQA Changing Priors v2, and TDIUC. Results indicate that MuRel outperforms a strong attention-based model across these datasets, demonstrating its capacity for generalization and detailed performance analysis on varied question types. The experimental setup includes the use of standard feature extraction methods and optimizations, aligning with contemporary practices in VQA research.
