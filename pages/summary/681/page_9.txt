The paper presents a framework for detecting anomalous sensor readings in automated vehicles using a Multi-Stage Attention mechanism combined with a Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM)-based Convolutional Neural Network (CNN). The study evaluates the performance of this method, referred to as MSALSTM-CNN, against a baseline approach and a fine-tuned ensemble method, WA VED. 

The results demonstrate that MSALSTM-CNN significantly enhances the anomaly detection rate, particularly for low-magnitude anomalies. The performance comparison shows that MSALSTM-CNN consistently outperforms other methods, achieving high accuracy and F1-scores across different types of anomalies, including instant, constant, gradual drift, and bias anomalies. For instance, it reached a maximum F1-score of 97.41% for constant anomalies and 97.62% for gradual drift anomalies.

The paper emphasizes the importance of accurate anomaly detection in the context of connected and automated vehicles, where sensor-generated data may contain faults or cyberattacks. The authors propose future work to develop probabilistic confidence methods to further validate the framework's outcomes. The findings highlight the potential of the MSALSTM-CNN method in improving the reliability of automated vehicle systems.
