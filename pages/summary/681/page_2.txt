The MSALSTM-CNN method proposed in this paper significantly improves anomaly detection rates across various types of anomalies, including instant, constant, gradual-drift, and bias anomalies. Specifically, it achieves increases in F-score of 2.54%, 1.85%, 1.36%, and 0.37% for single anomaly types, and 3.24%, 1.79%, 1.63%, and 1.15% for mixed anomaly types compared to baseline methods. This enhanced detection capability is crucial for reducing the risk of accidents caused by erroneous data.

The paper is structured into several sections: Section II reviews existing literature on anomaly detection methods in connected and automated vehicles (CAVs). Section III details the network model, dataset, and preliminary information. Section IV formulates the anomaly detection problem and describes the proposed methods. Section V discusses evaluation criteria and presents results, while Section VI concludes with future research directions.

The related work highlights various approaches to anomaly detection, including the use of CNNs and Kalman filters. However, the unique contribution of this study lies in the application of a multistage attention-based CNN for detecting anomalies in CAVs, a method not previously explored in the literature. The dataset used encompasses different anomaly types, and the study emphasizes the importance of data normalization and model tuning for improved performance. The research identifies a gap in the use of machine learning-based ensemble classifiers to enhance CNN-based anomaly detection, which the proposed WA VED method aims to address.

In conclusion, the MSALSTM-CNN method demonstrates robust performance in detecting low-magnitude anomalies, ensuring reliable data fusion in vehicular sensor networks and contributing to safer intelligent transportation systems.
