The paper evaluates the performance of the MSALSTM-CNN method in detecting various types of anomalies, comparing it to the WA VED method. The findings indicate that the MSALSTM-CNN consistently outperforms the WA VED method in accuracy, sensitivity, and F1-score across different experimental setups. The maximum observed F1-score for the MSALSTM-CNN method is 97.41%, while the WA VED method shows more variability and lower scores, particularly under less favorable conditions.

In terms of gradual drift anomaly detection, the MSALSTM-CNN method achieves an F1-score of 97.62%, showcasing its ability to handle complex patterns effectively due to the integration of advanced attention mechanisms. The bias anomaly detection results also favor the MSALSTM-CNN, with a maximum F1-score of 97.37%, attributed to its robustness with large datasets and complex sequences.

The paper also explores mixed anomaly types and demonstrates that the MSALSTM-CNN method performs well across various configurations and sensors. The highest F1-scores for different anomaly types were: 78.11% for instant anomalies, 90.43% for constant anomalies, 84.30% for gradual drift anomalies, and 90.45% for bias anomalies. Overall, the MSALSTM-CNN method shows a significant performance boost compared to baseline approaches, achieving a maximum F1-score of 99.02% for instant anomalies.

The results indicate that the MSALSTM-CNN method not only excels in single anomaly detection but also maintains competitive performance across mixed anomalies, establishing it as a superior choice for anomaly detection in intelligent transportation systems.
