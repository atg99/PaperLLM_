The paper presents a multi-stage attention mechanism integrated with a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) for anomaly detection in intelligent transportation systems. The proposed algorithm processes data from connected and automated vehicles (CAV) through a series of steps, including one-hot encoding, data scaling, and matrix convergence. The model employs a CNN for feature extraction followed by a Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) network enhanced with an attention mechanism to refine the predictions.

The ensemble method utilizes AdaBoost with weak learners, specifically Support Vector Machines (SVM) and Random Forests (RF), to improve classification accuracy in the presence of noisy data and outliers. Key parameters for AdaBoost are detailed, including the number of estimators and learning rates. SVM is described as a robust supervised learning method that utilizes kernel functions to handle non-linear separability, with specified parameters for optimal performance.

The WAVED algorithm is introduced for classifying instances as normal or anomalous, based on predicted and targeted confidences. A threshold of 80% is established for reliable classification, and methods for handling ties in votes among classes are outlined.

The results section evaluates the MSALSTM-CNN and WAVED methods against various anomaly types, including instant, constant, gradual drift, and bias. Performance metrics such as accuracy, sensitivity, precision, and F1 score are employed to assess the models' effectiveness in distinguishing between normal and anomalous instances, comparing them to state-of-the-art methods.
