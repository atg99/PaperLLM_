The paper presents an evaluation of the PGA-MAP-Elites algorithm across four robotic locomotion tasks, specifically QDWalker, QDHalfCheetah, QDAnt, and QDHopper, which are derived from the standard PyBullet environments. The primary goal of these tasks is for simulated robots to explore various walking strategies while balancing speed and energy consumption. Fitness is measured by the forward progress made during a simulation, factoring in energy penalties and survival rewards.

The evaluation involves comparisons with four other algorithms: standard MAP-Elites with directional variation, MAP-Elites-ES, QD-RL with a MAP-Elites archive, and a TD3 variant that also utilizes a MAP-Elites archive. Each task is tested across 20 random seeds, totaling one million controller evaluations.

Hyper-parameters for PGA-MAP-Elites are meticulously detailed, with a focus on neural network architecture and training configurations. The controllers consist of three layers, optimizing for performance while minimizing parameters. The behavior space is discretized into various niches for each task, facilitating a structured exploration of behaviors.

The evaluation metrics include the QD-score, which aggregates fitness across all solutions in the archive, adjusted to account for negative fitness values. This approach ensures fair comparisons among the algorithms by offsetting scores based on the lowest fitness found across all tasks. The paper ultimately aims to demonstrate the efficacy of PGA-MAP-Elites in discovering diverse and high-performing robotic behaviors.
