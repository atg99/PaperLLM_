This paper presents the PGA-MAP-Elites algorithm, a novel approach to building high-quality behavior repertoires for simulated robots that utilize large deep neural network (DNN) controllers. The algorithm combines policy gradient (PG) techniques with the exploration capabilities of genetic algorithms (GA) to enhance search power and data efficiency. Evaluations across four repertoire-building tasks demonstrate that PGA-MAP-Elites significantly outperforms existing methods, effectively scaling to controllers with over 20,000 parameters.

The study compares various algorithms, including MAP-Elites-ES and QD-RL, highlighting that while MAP-Elites-ES identifies some high-performing behaviors, it yields fewer overall behaviors and lacks in the QD-Score metric. The results indicate that MAP-Elites-ES may struggle in tasks with simpler behavior spaces, where optimizing for diversity does not correlate with performance improvement. QD-RL also underperforms, likely due to its method of directly optimizing for diversity, which does not align well with performance outcomes.

The authors conclude that PGA-MAP-Elites is particularly effective for locomotion tasks, but future research will explore its application in manipulation tasks and environments where behavior diversity aligns closely with performance. Additionally, the paper notes the limitations of using PG methods, such as the necessity for differentiable function approximators and the assumption of full observability, which do not restrict standard MAP-Elites variants.
