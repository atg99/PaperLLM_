The approach is structured around two parallel variation operators: one based on Policy Gradient methods and the other on Genetic Algorithms. The Policy Gradient operator utilizes two critic networks trained to approximate action-value functions, which provide gradient information to optimize the performance of the controllers. Meanwhile, the Genetic Algorithm operator facilitates diverse exploration of the solution space.

The algorithm begins with the initialization of an empty archive and critic networks. As the main loop iterates, it collects experiences from evaluated controllers to train the critics. In the early stages, random solutions are generated, while later iterations focus on selecting and varying existing solutions. The results of evaluations are stored in a replay buffer and used to update the archive, which tracks the best-performing controllers based on their fitness.

The training of the critics involves minimizing the loss between predicted and target action-values, with the target derived through the Bellman equation. This dual-critic approach enhances the robustness of the action-value predictions, allowing for more informed updates to the controllers.

Overall, PGA-MAP-Elites represents a significant advancement in behavior repertoire generation for DNN-controlled systems by effectively combining the strengths of Policy Gradient techniques with the exploration capabilities of Genetic Algorithms. This methodology addresses the computational challenges faced by MAP-Elites-ES and opens new avenues for efficient behavior optimization in complex environments.
