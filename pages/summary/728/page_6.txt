The paper presents a framework called PGA-MAP-Elites, which integrates policy gradient methods with the MAP-Elites algorithm to enhance performance in stochastic environments. The core of the approach lies in the training of critics and the variation operator, which collectively facilitate the optimization of controller policies.

The critic training process, detailed in Algorithm 4, involves iterating through a set number of training cycles. During each cycle, transitions are sampled from a replay buffer to compute action-value estimates using target networks, which help maintain stability in learning. The greedy controller is updated periodically to maximize these action-value predictions through the deterministic policy gradient method.

A key feature of the approach is the incorporation of noise into the target calculation, which promotes robustness and prevents convergence to suboptimal behaviors that may arise in narrow fitness landscapes. The update mechanism for the greedy controller and the critics utilizes the Adam optimization method, ensuring efficient convergence.

The variation operator, described in Algorithm 5, generates new controllers by applying both policy gradient and genetic algorithm techniques. This dual approach allows for a diverse exploration of the solution space, maintaining the foundational principles of MAP-Elites while leveraging the advantages of policy gradients. The offspring controllers are produced by adding Gaussian noise and displacing parameters along the direction from one parent to another, effectively combining exploration and exploitation strategies.

The implementation of the PGA-MAP-Elites framework is made publicly available, allowing for replication of the experiments conducted in the study. This work builds upon previous algorithms, integrating robust training and variation methodologies to improve the efficacy of evolutionary strategies in complex environments.
