The paper presents the results of an experimental study comparing the performance of the Policy Gradient Assisted MAP-Elites (PGA-MAP-Elites) algorithm against other algorithms in various evaluation tasks. The study utilizes three key metrics: Coverage, Max Fitness, and Fitness Difference. Coverage measures the total number of solutions found in the archive, while Max Fitness identifies the highest fitness solution. Fitness Difference assesses the robustness of solutions by comparing a single evaluation to the average of multiple evaluations for the current best solution.

The experimental results indicate that PGA-MAP-Elites significantly outperforms other algorithms across all tasks, as evidenced by higher Quality Diversity (QD) scores (p<10−5). The algorithm maintains the divergent search capability of MAP-Elites and demonstrates superior coverage (p<10−5) while also achieving high-performing solutions. Although standard MAP-Elites shows good coverage, it often yields low-fitness solutions, whereas PGA-MAP-Elites strikes a balance between coverage and high performance.

In terms of Max Fitness, PGA-MAP-Elites excels in the QDWalker and QDHopper tasks (p<0.01), although in the QDHalfCheetah task, it is statistically comparable to TD3, which performs better in QDAnt (p<10−6). The findings suggest that while TD3 can discover a few high-performing behaviors, PGA-MAP-Elites finds a greater number of medium to high fitness solutions, leading to superior overall archives.

The Fitness Difference metric reveals that PGA-MAP-Elites effectively learns robust behaviors across most tasks, with the exception of QDHopper, where the fitness difference indicates sensitivity to noise. This sensitivity may be linked to the genetic algorithm (GA) variation operator, suggesting that applying a Policy Gradient (PG) variation step after GA variation could enhance performance in such scenarios.
