This document references various significant works in the field of evolutionary computation and reinforcement learning. Notable contributions include the combination of evolutionary and gradient-based methods for policy search by Pourchot and Sigaud, which explores a novel approach to optimizing policies. Pugh et al. introduce the concept of quality diversity, emphasizing its importance as a new frontier in evolutionary computation.

The work by Salimans et al. presents evolution strategies as a scalable alternative to traditional reinforcement learning methods, highlighting their effectiveness in complex environments. Silver et al. focus on deterministic policy gradient algorithms, an essential aspect of reinforcement learning.

The MAP-Elites algorithm's performance, influenced by different encodings, is examined by Tarapore et al., while Vassiliades et al. discuss the scaling of the Multi-dimensional Archive of Phenotypic Elites Algorithm using Centroidal Voronoi Tessellations. The discovery of elite hypervolumes through interspecies correlation is further explored by Vassiliades and Mouret.

Additionally, Wang et al. investigate the concept of learning to reinforce learning, which addresses the meta-learning aspect of reinforcement strategies. Finally, the foundational work on Q-learning by Watkins and Dayan remains a crucial reference in the machine learning community, establishing a basis for many subsequent advancements in the field.
