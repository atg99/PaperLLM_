The paper presents an algorithm for constructing gradient trees as part of a generalized random forest framework. The algorithm, detailed in Algorithm 2, outlines the procedure for growing a gradient tree using a set of examples and a given domain. Key steps include creating a root node, initializing a queue, and iteratively processing nodes to compute estimating equations, derive pseudo outcomes, and make CART splits. The algorithm continues until no nodes are left to process, ultimately outputting the constructed tree.

The discussion also addresses the complexities that arise in certain cases, such as quantile regression, where the score functions may be discontinuous. Despite this, the authors adopt the standard practice of assuming smoothness in the expected score function. Two critical assumptions are introduced: the Lipschitz continuity of the expected score function in relation to the input variable, and the smooth identification of the M-function concerning the parameters. These assumptions ensure that the underlying functions exhibit desirable regularity properties necessary for the analysis.

Furthermore, the paper discusses additional assumptions regarding the properties of the score function, which are essential for establishing the validity of the empirical processes involved. Overall, the algorithm and assumptions laid out in the paper provide a robust framework for implementing gradient trees within generalized random forests.
