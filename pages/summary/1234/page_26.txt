The paper discusses a method for instrumental variable regression using generalized random forests, building on the transformation technique introduced by Robinson in 1988. The authors propose a procedure that involves centering outcomes by regressing out the marginal effects of covariates, allowing for the estimation of conditionally centered outcomes through leave-one-out estimation. They advocate for the use of centered outcomes in their simulations and offer an automated transformation option within their package, grf.

The approach is positioned within a broader context of nonparametric instrumental variables regression, referencing various studies that have explored similar methodologies, including kernel and series estimation techniques. The authors highlight the performance of their method in comparison to existing alternatives, such as nearest-neighbor and series regression, and emphasize the utility of centering in their analysis.

The paper revisits a motivating example from Angrist and Evans, examining the impact of child-rearing on labor-force participation among married mothers with at least two children. Utilizing a dataset derived from the 1980 census, the authors analyze the effect of having a third child on mothers' labor force participation, finding a local average treatment effect that suggests an average reduction in participation. They aim to extend this analysis by incorporating heterogeneity across various covariates, such as the mother's age, education, race, and the father's income, to identify conditional local average treatment effects.
