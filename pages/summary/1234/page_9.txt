The paper discusses the gradient tree algorithm, which focuses on optimizing the process of selecting splits in decision trees. The authors propose an approximate criterion for optimizing these splits, avoiding the computational expense of directly optimizing over all possible axis-aligned splits. Instead, they utilize gradient-based approximations for estimating parameters in child nodes.

The algorithm operates in two primary steps. In the labeling step, the authors compute the parent parameters and a derivative matrix from the parent data to generate pseudo-outcomes. In the regression step, they perform a standard CART regression on these pseudo-outcomes to identify the optimal split that maximizes a specific criterion.

The paper illustrates this process using a least-squares regression example, where the labeling step retains the original outcomes while the regression step aligns with traditional splitting methods. The proposed method streamlines the recursive partitioning procedure, enhancing computational efficiency while maintaining effective decision tree construction.
