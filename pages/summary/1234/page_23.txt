This paper discusses the limitations and advantages of two procedures for estimating treatment effects using generalized random forests, as proposed by Wager and Athey in 2018. Procedure 1 is sensitive to changes in the treatment effect function, while Procedure 2 is more robust to confounding. However, the authors note that the fixed nature of these procedures makes it challenging to utilize their respective strengths effectively.

The paper introduces an orthogonalization approach to improve estimation accuracy by regressing out the main effect of covariates. A simulation study is revisited, where different scenarios are analyzed: one with no confounding and treatment heterogeneity, another with confounding but no treatment effect, and a third combining both. Results indicate that generalized random forests with centering outperform both procedures in scenarios with both confounding and treatment heterogeneity.

The authors highlight the drawbacks of the original procedures—Procedure 1 struggles with pure confounding, while Procedure 2 performs poorly with strong heterogeneity. The paper also touches on the use of instrumental variables regression as a method to measure causal effects in situations where traditional assumptions about exogeneity may not hold, emphasizing the importance of identifying appropriate sources of randomness to ascertain causal relationships.
