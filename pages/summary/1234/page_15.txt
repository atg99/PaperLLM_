The paper discusses a method for approximating local estimators using a linearized approach, specifically focusing on the generalized random forest estimate. The authors introduce the influence function heuristic to derive a natural approximation, denoted as ˜θ∗(x), which relates to the true parameter value θ(x). The influence function ρ∗i(x) captures the effect of individual observations on the estimator, though it depends on unknown true parameters.

The approximation ˜θ∗(x) is formulated as a sum of weighted influence functions, resulting in a pseudo-forest estimate that aligns with the properties of U-statistics. This formulation allows the authors to analyze the averaged estimator ˜θ∗(x) using existing statistical results on U-statistics. A key challenge addressed is demonstrating that this influence-based statistic serves as a reliable approximation for the original estimator ˆθ(x).

To establish the validity of the approximation, the authors first prove the consistency of ˆθ(x) under specific assumptions. They present Theorem 3, which asserts that under these assumptions, the estimates converge in probability to the true parameters. The main technical contribution, detailed in Lemma 4, involves coupling the approximation with the desired outcomes. The paper emphasizes a structured approach to separating the analysis of moment estimators into consistency arguments and local approximation, referencing established statistical literature for support.
