The paper critiques the proliferation of abstract AI ethics principles, which often result in a disconnect between high ideals and practical implementations. Despite efforts to operationalize these principles, the translation of contested social concepts into technical guidelines remains problematic. The author argues that the focus on AI principles diverts essential resources from more effective approaches to addressing AI's social implications.

The paper identifies a commonality among AI ethics frameworks, condensing them into five core principles: beneficence, non-maleficence, autonomy, justice, and explicability. While these principles sound appealing, their definitions are ambiguous and lead to conflicting interpretations. For example, the concept of "beneficence" can clash with environmental sustainability goals, as differing ideologies can interpret sustainability in vastly different ways. This ambiguity extends to other key concepts in AI ethics, such as safety, well-being, and privacy, which are often laden with competing definitions.

The author warns that this lack of clarity allows for ethical compliance without genuine engagement, enabling companies to superficially adhere to principles while avoiding meaningful reflection. Additionally, the paper emphasizes that AI development is inherently social and cultural, shaped by a network of actors, rather than occurring in isolation. Consequently, the paper advocates for a broader consideration of AI justice that accounts for socio-political dynamics and concrete issues within AI governance.
