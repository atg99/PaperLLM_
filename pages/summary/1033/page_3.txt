The paper discusses the systemic issues surrounding ethics in AI development, emphasizing that the problems extend beyond individual biases in AI models to the broader organizational and cultural contexts within the tech industry. It argues that unethical AI is a consequence of an unethical industry, where toxic cultures perpetuate sexism and racism, as evidenced by documented cases of harassment and discrimination in tech companies. 

It highlights the lack of ethical training in the education of future technologists, pointing out that curricula often prioritize technical skills over ethical considerations, leading to an "a-ethical" environment where ethical dilemmas are overlooked. This absence of ethical engagement in both education and industry contributes to the development of technologies without regard for their social, moral, and cultural impacts.

The paper also critiques existing AI ethical frameworks, asserting that they lack enforcement mechanisms and often serve as mere marketing tools rather than effective regulatory measures. It notes that principles of AI ethics are not self-enforcing and that initiatives like advisory councils lack the authority to implement meaningful change. Overall, the paper calls for a reevaluation of how ethics is integrated into both AI education and industry practices to address the underlying social issues that influence technological development.
