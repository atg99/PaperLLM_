In the paper "The Uselessness of AI Ethics," Luke Munn critiques the burgeoning reliance on ethical principles in the field of artificial intelligence. As awareness of AI's potential dangers has increased, a multitude of ethical guidelines has emerged from both public and private sectors. However, Munn argues that these principles are largely ineffective. He identifies three main issues: the principles are often meaningless and contested, they exist in isolation from an industry that largely neglects ethics, and they lack enforceability, serving corporate interests instead.

Munn highlights that these ethical guidelines fail to address the significant racial, social, and environmental harms associated with AI technologies. He emphasizes a critical gap between lofty ethical principles and their practical application, suggesting that attempts to operationalize these principles often result in oversimplified translations of complex social issues into technical rules. This gap can divert resources away from more effective strategies for addressing AI's impact.

To conclude, Munn advocates for alternative approaches to AI justice that focus on broader systems of oppression and the importance of accuracy and auditing, rather than relying on ineffective ethical principles. The paper calls for a shift in perspective to tackle the real challenges posed by AI technologies.
