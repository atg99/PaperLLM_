The paper critiques the current state of AI ethics, arguing that it serves more as a shield for technology companies against regulation rather than a genuine guide for ethical behavior. Companies like Uber and Airbnb have effectively used ethical rhetoric to expand their operations while sidestepping legal responsibilities, as they often face little immediate consequence for their actions. This phenomenon, termed "ethics washing," allows corporations to maintain their reputation without altering their business practices or facing regulatory scrutiny.

The authors highlight a significant disconnect between ethical principles and their practical application in AI development. Despite the existence of ethical codes and guidelines, studies reveal that developers often do not incorporate these principles into their work. This gap between ethics and practice perpetuates the status quo, allowing companies to continue operating without substantial ethical oversight.

To address this issue, the authors call for a shift from ethical statements to enforceable legislation, emphasizing that laws have the democratic legitimacy and capacity for enforcement that ethical guidelines lack. They argue that integrating ethical considerations into the actual development processes of AI technologies is essential to bridge the gap between ideals and practice. Overall, the paper advocates for a more rigorous approach to AI ethics that prioritizes actionable standards over vague principles that can be easily co-opted by corporate interests.
