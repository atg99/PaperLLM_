The paper discusses the complexities and challenges of operationalizing AI ethics, highlighting that the current focus on ethical principles may overlook deeper social and political issues related to AI technology. It critiques the notion that technical solutions can resolve ethical concerns like fairness and privacy, arguing that these issues are contested and context-dependent. The author emphasizes that operationalization involves navigating tensions between competing ethical demands and translating these into technological forms, which is a nuanced and demanding process.

The paper suggests that the significant resources dedicated to developing AI ethics frameworks could be misallocated, diverting attention from more impactful initiatives. It advocates for a shift towards considering AI justice, which broadens the ethical inquiry beyond procedural issues to address power dynamics and systemic inequalities inherent in AI systems. This perspective calls for an intersectional approach that acknowledges how AI technologies can reflect and amplify existing social disparities.

The notion of "humanity" in AI design is also critiqued, as historical biases have influenced definitions of who is considered deserving of ethical consideration. Thus, the paper argues for a more comprehensive understanding of ethics in AI that encompasses a wider range of social contexts and power structures, rather than relying solely on established ethical principles.
