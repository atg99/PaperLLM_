The paper discusses three main contributions to the field of agent-based systems. The first contribution focuses on a simulation tool designed for modeling agent production processes in office environments. The second contribution employs an intelligent hybrid system to manage user interaction levels in museum exhibitions, aiming to address overcrowding by creating adaptive, multi-user interactive experiences. A case study was conducted with software agents representing an interactive exhibition module in a museum in Mexico. The third contribution introduces CogHelper, an orientation system for individuals with cognitive disabilities, aiming to enhance their independence by guiding users based on their preferences.

The paper also explores the significance of agent-based simulation as a method for modeling complex systems characterized by distributed interactions among autonomous agents. This approach encompasses various fields, including game theory and computational sociology, and it effectively captures the emergent behaviors of systems operating at different levels.

Furthermore, the work highlights the development of an online Double Action market simulation that addresses the fluctuating nature of perishable goods markets, demonstrating the effectiveness of multi-agent simulation in promoting truthful trading behavior and fair distribution of resources. Lastly, the concept of smart cities is introduced, emphasizing the necessity for innovative technologies to tackle issues arising from rapid urbanization, with MAS being integral to enhancing urban management and residents' quality of life.
