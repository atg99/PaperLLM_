This Special Issue aims to enhance the paradigm of MAS through contributions in various domains such as agent-oriented software engineering, multi-agent learning, agent-based simulation, and applications in contemporary fields like smart cities and ambient intelligence.

The complexity of MAS arises from the integration of multiple technologies, including software engineering for process structuring, artificial intelligence for decision-making in unpredictable scenarios, and concurrent programming for task coordination across different machines. Despite the emergence of various development platforms and tools, significant challenges remain in modeling and designing agent-based systems.

The Special Issue highlights three notable contributions in agent-oriented software engineering. The first work emphasizes the importance of accountability in MAS, proposing an interaction protocol named ADOPT, which ensures accountability by design. The second study introduces a development methodology for MAS in semantic web contexts, utilizing a domain-specific modeling language called SEA_ML to facilitate service or goods exchange among agents without currency. The third contribution presents a framework for developing intelligent agents on mobile devices, emphasizing linked data support, which allows agents to utilize beliefs from linked data environments during planning. This framework is anticipated to be crucial for future applications in cyber-physical systems and the Internet of Things.

Learning within MAS is essential as it enables systems to adapt and modify their actions dynamically, presenting significant potential for tackling problems involving unknown behaviors of other agents in the environment.
