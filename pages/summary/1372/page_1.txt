The editorial discusses the rising prominence of multi-agent systems (MAS) in various sectors such as e-commerce, logistics, healthcare, and smart cities, driven by technological advancements. These systems are recognized as critical tools for analyzing and developing new models in large-scale distributed and human-centered environments. The authors emphasize the importance of democratizing technology to allow non-technical users to engage with complex systems.

The introduction outlines the definition of intelligent agents and highlights their ability to perceive, reason, act, and interact within their environments. MAS are described as distributed systems comprised of autonomous agents that pursue individual objectives, often operating without centralized control. This autonomy enables MAS to handle complex applications requiring distributed data processing and dynamic decision-making.

Research in MAS focuses on how agents coordinate and collaborate to address complex challenges, with an emphasis on developing effective coordination mechanisms for agent teams. The authors note the complexities involved in programming intelligent agents, which must embody properties such as reactivity, proactivity, and sociability. Various proposals for agent architectures, communication languages, and decision-making mechanisms have emerged to address these challenges, reflecting the ongoing evolution and significance of multi-agent systems in contemporary technology.
