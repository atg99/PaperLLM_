This paper addresses the challenge of abnormal event detection (AED) in videos by introducing a novel approach utilizing a simplified version of Gated Recurrent Units (GRUs). The authors highlight the growing popularity of recurrent neural networks (RNNs) and their variants, particularly Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks, in handling sequential data, including tasks like speech recognition. Despite the effectiveness of GRUs in various applications, including speech and medical image processing, their potential in AED remains underexplored.

The main contributions of this research include:

1. Development of a new GRU architecture that eliminates the reset gate, allowing for better retention of significant past information while processing current input.
2. Introduction of the Single-Tunnelled GRU (SiTGRU), an end-to-end deep neural network designed for improved computational efficiency and performance in detecting abnormalities compared to standard RNNs.
3. Empirical results indicating that SiTGRU outperforms traditional GRU and LSTM methods on AED benchmarks, demonstrating superior generalization capabilities across multiple datasets and effectiveness in detecting motion abnormalities.

The paper is structured as follows: Section 2 reviews related works, Section 3 details the proposed methodology and model architecture, Section 4 presents experimental results, and Section 5 concludes the findings. The related work section discusses various approaches to AED, including handcrafted feature methods, deep learning techniques such as generative adversarial networks (GANs), and spatio-temporal neural networks that leverage LSTMs and 3D convolutional layers for enhanced learning of temporal and spatial features.
