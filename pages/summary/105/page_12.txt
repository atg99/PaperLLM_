The paper presents a comparative analysis of different recurrent neural network architectures—GRU, LSTM, and SiTGRU—across various datasets, specifically focusing on their performance in abnormality detection. It outlines the number of training and testing videos utilized from the Avenue, Ped1, and Ped2 datasets, with a total of 66 training videos and 69 testing videos.

In terms of generalization performance, SiTGRU outperforms both GRU and LSTM on the merged dataset, achieving an AUC of 0.707 and an EER of 0.343, indicating its superior capability in detecting anomalies. The performance on the Avenue16 test set shows similar trends, with SiTGRU achieving an AUC of 0.744 and a significantly lower EER of 0.305 compared to the standard RNNs.

The paper also highlights the training efficiency of the models, where SiTGRU demonstrates reduced training and testing times compared to GRU and LSTM. This efficiency is attributed to the absence of the reset gate in SiTGRU, which simplifies the training process. The training times for each architecture are provided, showing that SiTGRU consistently requires less time per epoch across all datasets.

Additionally, the paper emphasizes the practical implications of using SiTGRU, as its computationally efficient design makes it suitable for deployment on portable devices, enhancing the accessibility of abnormality detection technologies. Overall, the findings suggest that SiTGRU is a promising alternative to standard RNNs for real-time anomaly detection tasks.
