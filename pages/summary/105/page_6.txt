The paper presents a modified Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) cell intended to enhance detection performance in anomaly detection tasks. The proposed single-tunnelled GRU eliminates the reset gate and replaces the standard hyperbolic tangent activation function with a sigmoid activation function. This modification leads to a reduced number of parameters, which in turn lowers computational costs while maintaining the ability to capture diverse features from various frame sequences.

The authors argue that this new structure allows for better adaptation and generalization across different contexts, as evidenced by improved performance on abnormal event detection benchmarks compared to conventional recurrent networks. The generalization capabilities of the proposed model are discussed in Section 4.5.

In terms of performance evaluation, the paper introduces a regularity score metric to assess how effectively the model can detect abnormal events in unseen test data. This score is calculated based on the reconstruction error, defined as the Euclidean distance between the input video frames and their reconstructed versions. The regularity score is derived from this reconstruction error to gauge the model's ability to identify deviations from learned patterns.

The anomaly detection process is further detailed, focusing on the evaluation metrics such as True Positive Rate (TPR) and False Positive Rate (FPR), which are used to compute the Area Under the Receiver Operating Characteristic Curve (AUC) and Equal Error Rate (EER). These metrics are essential for assessing the accuracy of the anomaly detection capabilities of the proposed model.

Overall, the modifications to the standard GRU cell lead to a new model that demonstrates enhanced performance in anomaly detection tasks, supported by a robust evaluation framework.
