The paper addresses the challenge of generalizing abnormality detection models across different datasets in the context of anomaly event detection (AED). It focuses on the development of models that can learn from both single and multiple datasets, evaluating their robustness by testing them on unfamiliar datasets. The authors trained models using data from CUHK Avenue and UCSD datasets, emphasizing the need for a consistent evaluation measure, specifically the Area Under the Curve (AUC) by Equal Error Rate (EER), despite the differing contexts of training and testing.

The experiments utilized three well-known video anomaly detection datasets: CUHK Avenue, UCSD Ped1, and UCSD Ped2. Each dataset consists of videos taken in outdoor environments, where training sets include only normal events, while test sets contain both normal and abnormal events. The abnormal events vary across datasets, complicating the generalization process.

The CUHK Avenue dataset includes 16 training and 21 testing videos with a total of 15,328 frames in the training set. The UCSD dataset is divided into Ped1 and Ped2, with different numbers of training and testing videos and varying resolutions. The preprocessing steps involved converting raw video data into frames, normalizing pixel values, and creating video cuboids for model input. Data augmentation was applied to enhance the training set.

In terms of implementation, the authors used 85% of the training set for training and the remaining 15% for validation. The models were trained for 60 epochs using the Adam optimizer, which outperformed other optimizers in terms of performance. The authors noted that using sparse categorical cross-entropy loss led to the best results for UCSD datasets, while mean squared error loss was more effective for the Avenue dataset.
