The paper presents an analysis of the performance of different loss functions and optimizers in the context of anomaly detection using models based on Gated Recurrent Units (GRUs). The study evaluates the impact of these hyperparameters on model effectiveness across various datasets, specifically the Avenue, Ped1, and Ped2 test sets.

The performance comparison is summarized in tables that show the results of different combinations of loss functions—mean squared error and sparse categorical cross-entropy—and optimizers, including AdaGrad, Adam, and RMSprop. Notably, the combination of mean squared error with the Adam optimizer yielded the best results for the Avenue dataset, while the sparse categorical cross-entropy with Adam was most effective for the Ped1 and Ped2 datasets.

The paper also explores the role of update and reset gates in GRU architectures. It argues that removing the reset gate can enhance performance in abnormality detection. The authors provide a detailed analysis of the learning behavior when the reset gate is discarded, demonstrating that models with an inactive update gate fail to learn effectively, resulting in poor anomaly detection capabilities.

Overall, the findings suggest that careful selection of loss functions and optimizers is critical for optimizing model performance in detecting anomalies, and that architectural choices regarding gates in GRUs can significantly influence learning outcomes.
