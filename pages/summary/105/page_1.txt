The paper introduces the Single-Tunnelled Gated Recurrent Unit (SiTGRU), an innovative approach for abnormality detection in video data. Traditional methods have struggled with the complexities of long-term sequential data and the variability of abnormal events. SiTGRU addresses these challenges by optimizing the Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) architecture. It simplifies the GRU by removing the reset gate, which often neglects the significance of past information, and replaces the hyperbolic tangent activation function with a sigmoid activation to improve performance in deeper networks.

The authors highlight that abnormal event detection (AED) is critical in computer vision, allowing for automated surveillance and reducing the need for human monitoring. However, the task is complicated by the lack of large-scale annotated datasets and the subjective nature of defining "abnormal" events, which can vary across different contexts.

SiTGRU aims to enhance the capture of both appearance and motion features in long sequences, thereby improving detection and generalization across different datasets. The empirical results indicate that SiTGRU outperforms standard GRU and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks in various metrics while being computationally efficient, reducing training and testing times. The paper emphasizes the importance of deep learning in overcoming the limitations of handcrafted features in previous abnormality detection methods, showcasing the potential of SiTGRU in advancing this field.
