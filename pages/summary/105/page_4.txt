The paper discusses the implementation of a stacked GRU (Gated Recurrent Unit) network for modeling temporal sequences, particularly for abnormal event detection in video data. The authors highlight the importance of batch normalization in reducing internal covariate shift during training, which accelerates the process and improves generalization across different contexts.

The architecture consists of an input layer that constructs temporal cuboids from consecutive video frames, allowing for better representation of motion and appearance patterns. The encoding module, structured similarly to an Autoencoder, uses three GRU layers with batch normalization to compress the input data and extract spatio-temporal features. Conversely, the decoding module aims to reconstruct the original input from the encoded data, utilizing two GRU layers along with batch normalization.

The training process focuses on creating a model that can effectively identify anomalies by reconstructing input frames and comparing reconstruction costs. A higher reconstruction error indicates the presence of anomalies. The paper emphasizes the advantages of GRUs over traditional RNNs, particularly in overcoming the vanishing gradient problem, making them suitable for processing long-term sequences.

GRUs utilize two gates—update and reset gates—to manage information flow and maintain relevant temporal features, which is crucial for tasks involving sequential data. The paper concludes by asserting that the GRU architecture, with its simplified gating mechanisms, is effective for modeling tasks that demand both short-term and long-term dependencies in video sequences.
