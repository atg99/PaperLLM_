The paper discusses the limitations of standard Gated Recurrent Units (GRUs) in handling long-term sequential data, particularly in retaining crucial information during training. While traditional GRUs effectively mitigate the vanishing and exploding gradient problems, their gating mechanisms can lead to the omission of important content from previous time steps.

To address this issue, the authors propose a novel GRU model that modifies the conventional structure by eliminating the reset gate and replacing the hyperbolic tangent activation function with the sigmoid activation function for candidate memory states. The reset gate traditionally determines the relevance of the previous memory state in calculating the candidate memory state. However, the authors argue that in tasks such as abnormal event detection, retaining continuity of previous memory states is essential, making the reset gate dispensable.

Additionally, the paper highlights the redundancy between the update and reset gates, suggesting that similar activations can lead to ineffective candidate memory computations. By discarding the reset gate, the proposed model simplifies the GRU structure, resulting in a computationally lightweight cell. The switch to sigmoid activation is based on its effectiveness in training deeper networks and avoiding numerical instabilities associated with hyperbolic tangent and ReLU functions.

Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed modifications enhance the performance of the GRU model for detecting anomalies in sequential data, indicating its potential applicability in various time-dependent tasks.
