The paper presents Equivariant Transformer Networks (ETs), which utilize a sequence of ET layers to achieve self-consistency with respect to subgroups of a transformation group. Each layer aims to eliminate the influence of its corresponding subgroup. The authors describe a decomposition of a k-parameter transformation into one-parameter transformations, exemplified by the projective general linear group PGL(3;R). The stacking of ET layers is implemented in reverse order to maintain effectiveness, despite the lack of guaranteed self-consistency.

The implementation details highlight the use of coordinate transformations through resampling with a rectangular grid in a new coordinate system, employing bilinear interpolation for non-pixel-aligned points. To address the degradation of image quality from repeated resampling, the authors propose a lazy resampling method, which represents the transformed image implicitly until necessary.

The experimental evaluation focuses on two datasets: a modified version of MNIST distorted by projective transformations and the Street View House Numbers (SVHN) dataset. The Projective MNIST dataset includes 10,000 base images transformed using random projective parameters, with additional larger datasets created for controlled evaluation of data augmentation. The results demonstrate the sample efficiency advantages of ETs compared to standard Spatial Transformer (ST) layers and baseline CNN architectures, particularly in handling transformations not seen during training.
