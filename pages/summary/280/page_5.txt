Equivariant Transformer Networks (ETNs) introduce a novel approach to achieving local invariance in image processing through their ET layers. Unlike standard layers, ET layers exhibit a form of local transformation invariance, meaning that the output remains consistent when the input image undergoes transformations defined by a group G. This property emerges from the self-consistency of the pose predictor relative to the group, providing a robust inductive bias towards invariance.

To implement this self-consistency, the authors propose a method where a 2D CNN feature map generates a spatial distribution. This distribution is processed through a softmax function to predict the centroid coordinates, which align with the input through rescaling. The construction of a canonical coordinate system for target transformations is essential, particularly for one-parameter Lie groups, which facilitate transformations on R2, relevant for 2D image deformations.

The process involves solving first-order partial differential equations (PDEs) to derive invariant coordinate functions that minimize distortion and resampling artifacts. An example provided is the hyperbolic rotation transformation, where specific PDEs yield solutions that represent the transformation's effects.

Moreover, the paper discusses the limitations of single transformation groups, emphasizing that higher-dimensional transformations, such as those in the 8-parameter projective linear group, require more complex canonical coordinate systems. The authors note that while log-polar coordinates can accommodate certain transformations, achieving simultaneous self-consistency across multiple parameters demands stacked ET layers, indicating a pathway for enhancing the model's capability to handle diverse transformations in natural images.
