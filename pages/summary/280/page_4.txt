The paper discusses Equivariant Transformer Networks, specifically focusing on the development of Equivariant Transformers (ET) that address the limitations of standard CNN pose predictors. The authors propose a method that ensures self-consistency in predictions by leveraging the translation equivariance inherent in CNN architectures alongside specialized canonical coordinate systems tailored for specific transformation groups.

The section begins with an overview of canonical coordinate systems, foundational to the proposed method. These systems, initially described by Rubinstein et al. and expanded by Segman et al., allow for the transformation of input data into a new coordinate system where transformations appear as translations. This is crucial for achieving self-consistent predictions across various continuous transformation groups.

The paper presents a key proposition stating that if a function is self-consistent with respect to translation, it can also be made self-consistent with respect to a broader transformation group by utilizing canonical coordinates. This reduction to translation simplifies the problem of self-consistency.

The proposed Equivariant Transformer layer integrates these canonical coordinates to embed domain-specific invariances into the network architecture. The ET layer is defined as an image-to-image mapping that applies an inverse transformation based on predicted pose parameters, which are derived from a CNN designed to maintain self-consistency with respect to the chosen transformation group. The ET layer is differentiable, allowing for seamless incorporation into existing CNN frameworks, and serves as an advanced alternative to traditional Spatial Transformer layers.

Overall, the paper outlines a novel approach to enhancing pose prediction accuracy through the use of canonical coordinate systems and equivariant transformations, aiming to improve the robustness of CNNs in handling various transformations.
