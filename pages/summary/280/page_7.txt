Equivariant Transformer Networks (ETNs) are evaluated for their effectiveness in image classification tasks, particularly on the Projective MNIST and Street View House Numbers (SVHN) datasets. The Projective MNIST dataset features various transformations, including projective transformations not previously explored. The study compares classification error rates across different methods, including a baseline Cartesian CNN and a log-polar CNN, with varying numbers of sampled transformations.

Results indicate that ETNs consistently outperform both log-polar and standard transformer (ST) baselines, achieving a 43% relative improvement in error rates when trained with a single pose per prototype. The performance gains are attributed to the ET layers' ability to handle additional transformations effectively. However, the ST baseline with a complete transformation sequence was omitted due to training instability, despite extensive hyperparameter tuning.

The analysis of hyperparameter sensitivity shows that ETNs are more robust to variations in the initial learning rate compared to STs, indicating improved training stability due to the self-consistency constraint imposed on ETs. This was demonstrated through validation error rates across various learning rate settings.

For the SVHN dataset, which consists of RGB images of house numbers, the study assesses the impact of transformer layers on single-digit classification. The dataset's natural geometric variation, resulting from camera position differences, allows for a direct evaluation of the proposed methods without artificial transformations. The results from both datasets highlight the advantages of incorporating transformer layers into CNN architectures for enhancing classification accuracy and stability during training.
