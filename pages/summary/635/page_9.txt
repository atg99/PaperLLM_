The paper explores the fusion mechanism of network topology and node features in Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs), revealing that current methods are not optimal. It highlights the challenges associated with dense graphs, where feature smoothing becomes easier but may also introduce noise from additional edges. The authors review recent advancements in GCNs, including various approaches like spectral methods, attention mechanisms, and sampling techniques, which primarily focus on integrating network structure and node features for classification tasks. Notably, they reference works that demonstrate GCNs perform Laplacian smoothing and act as low-pass filters on node features. 

To address the identified shortcomings, the authors propose a multi-channel model named AM-GCN, designed to learn suitable importance weights for effectively fusing topology and node feature information. Extensive experimental results indicate that AM-GCN outperforms state-of-the-art models on real-world datasets, suggesting its effectiveness in adaptive information extraction for classification tasks.
