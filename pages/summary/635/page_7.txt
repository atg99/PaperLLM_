AM-GCN model and its variants were evaluated across six datasets, revealing significant insights into the effectiveness of the proposed framework. The results indicate that AM-GCN consistently outperforms its variants—AM-GCN without constraints, AM-GCN with a consistency constraint, and AM-GCN with a disparity constraint—across all datasets and label rates. This demonstrates the benefits of employing both constraints together, with the consistency constraint showing a more pronounced impact on performance than the disparity constraint.

Visualizations of node embeddings, particularly for the BlogCatalog dataset, further highlight the advantages of AM-GCN. Unlike other methods such as DeepWalk, GCN, and GAT, which resulted in mixed classes, AM-GCN produced a clear and compact structure with distinct class boundaries, indicating higher intra-class similarity.

Additionally, an analysis of the attention mechanism within AM-GCN revealed that attention values were generally higher for specific embeddings in the topology space compared to those in the feature space. This suggests that topological information is more critical for the model's performance in certain datasets. In some cases, kNN-GCN outperformed GCN, indicating variability in the importance of topology versus feature information depending on the dataset.

Overall, the findings underscore the robustness and effectiveness of the AM-GCN framework in learning node representations, particularly through the strategic use of constraints and attention mechanisms.
