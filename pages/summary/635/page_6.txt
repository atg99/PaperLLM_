The node classification results presented in Table 2 indicate that the proposed Adaptive Multi-Graph Convolutional Network (AM-GCN) consistently outperforms all baseline methods across various datasets and label rates. Notably, AM-GCN achieves the highest performance improvements of 8.59% on BlogCatalog and 8.63% on Flickr, showcasing its effectiveness. 

AM-GCN also demonstrates superior performance over GCN and kNN-GCN in all datasets, highlighting the benefits of its adaptive fusion mechanism, which allows for better information extraction compared to relying solely on GCN or kNN-GCN. The results reveal a structural difference between topology graphs and feature graphs, as evidenced by cases where the feature graph outperforms the topology graph in datasets like BlogCatalog, Flickr, and UAI2010. This underscores the importance of incorporating feature graphs into GCN frameworks.

Additionally, the improvements of AM-GCN are particularly pronounced in datasets with robust feature graphs (kNN), such as UAI2010, BlogCatalog, and Flickr. This suggests that AM-GCN effectively utilizes a more suitable kNN graph to enhance feature propagation and node representation learning. Overall, the findings affirm the advantages of AM-GCN in node classification tasks.
