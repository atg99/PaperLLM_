The paper discusses a method for learning node embeddings that effectively capture specific information from both feature and topology spaces. The original input graph is represented as Gt=(At, Xt), where At is the adjacency matrix and Xt is the feature matrix. The learned output embedding ZT from the topology graph can be calculated similarly to the feature space embeddings.

A key component of the approach is the Common-GCN, which is designed to extract node embeddings from both spaces while sharing weight matrices across layers. This facilitates the identification of common characteristics between the two spaces. The output embeddings from the topology and feature graphs, ZCT and ZCF, are averaged to derive a common embedding ZC.

An attention mechanism is introduced to weigh the importance of the specific embeddings ZT, ZC, and ZF. The attention values are computed for each embedding, allowing the model to prioritize the most relevant information for node classification tasks. The final embedding Z is constructed using these weights.

The paper also introduces two constraints to enhance the learning process. The consistency constraint ensures that the embeddings ZCT and ZCF, derived from the Common-GCN, maintain similarity. This is measured using L2-normalization and the Frobenius norm. The disparity constraint employs the Hilbert-Schmidt Independence Criterion (HSIC) to promote independence between the embeddings ZT and ZCT, as well as between ZF and ZCF, thereby enhancing the model's ability to capture diverse information.

Overall, the proposed method combines shared information extraction, attention weighting, and consistency and disparity constraints to improve the effectiveness of node classification tasks in graph-based learning.
