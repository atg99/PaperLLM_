The experiments confirm that the proposed Adaptive Multi-Graph Convolutional Network (AM-GCN) effectively assigns greater attention to more significant information, particularly in feature space compared to topology space. 

The analysis of attention distribution indicates that during training, the attention values for topology, feature, and common components initially converge but diverge as epochs increase. For instance, in BlogCatalog, topology's attention value diminishes while feature's value ascends, aligning with previous findings that highlight the superior performance of feature graphs over traditional GCN.

Furthermore, the parameter study reveals sensitivity to the consistency coefficient (γ), disparity constraint coefficient (β), and the number of nearest neighbors (k). Optimal performance is achieved within specific ranges for these parameters, suggesting a balance is necessary for effective model training. The AM-GCN demonstrates a stepwise learning process, emphasizing the importance of different embeddings throughout training, ultimately validating its adaptive capabilities in graph-based learning tasks.
