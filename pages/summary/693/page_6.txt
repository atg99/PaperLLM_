The paper discusses a Deep Q-Network (DQN)-based packet scheduling approach aimed at maximizing network lifetime while adhering to latency constraints in a multi-slave environment. 

**States:** The environment's state at time t is represented by a vector indicating the remaining lifetime of packets for N slaves. Each slave's state vector consists of the remaining lifetimes of the first k packets in its queue, with k set to 5 for practical purposes. The remaining lifetime is discretized into 20 levels, each representing a 0.2-second interval, to align with the range of connection intervals (CIs) specified in Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) standards.

**Actions:** An action at time t includes two components: the length of the CI and the number of packets sent by each slave. The action is expressed as a tuple, where the CI length is calculated based on an index that ranges from 0 to 31 to limit the action space. The maximum number of packets per slave is capped at five.

**Rewards:** The objective of the DQN-based scheduler is to maximize rewards through a function that incorporates both the CI length and a quality of service (QoS) metric. The reward function is designed to incentivize longer CIs and minimize packet losses. The QoS value depends on whether packets are sent or dropped, contributing to the overall reward.

The paper outlines the framework for a reinforcement learning approach to optimize packet scheduling, emphasizing the balance between efficiency and adherence to latency requirements.
