The paper discusses the application of a deep Q-network (DQN) for addressing real-world problems in wireless sensor networks (WSNs). The DQN utilizes a neural network to approximate the Q-function and incorporates two critical components—experience replay and a duplicated Q-network—to enhance learning stability. Experience replay allows for mini-batching during Q-network updates, while the target network, a replica of the main Q-network, stabilizes learning by providing consistent Q-values for training.

The proposed DQN-based scheduling algorithm formulates the scheduling of transmission parameters as a sequential decision-making problem within a reinforcement learning (RL) framework. In this setup, the master node acts as the agent, with the surrounding environment providing system state information and rewards. The master node observes the state at each time step and selects actions concerning the communication parameters based on the received state information from slave nodes.

The architecture of the DQN-based scheduler is illustrated, showing how the master node receives state information regarding packet lifetimes, chooses scheduling actions such as the communication interval (CI) length and the number of packets to transmit, and subsequently receives feedback in the form of rewards. This information, represented as a quadruple of current state, action, reward, and next state, is stored in a replay buffer for training the DQN. The parameters of the main Q-network are updated at each training step, ensuring continual improvement of the scheduling algorithm.
