The paper discusses a wireless sensor network (WSN) model comprising a master node and multiple slave nodes, focusing on packet scheduling and energy consumption. The master node employs a packet scheduler to dynamically determine the length of connection intervals (CIs) and the number of packets each slave should transmit. Slaves enter sleep mode after data transmission until the next CI begins. The system incorporates quality of service (QoS) parameters, specifically a latency limit for packets; if a packet exceeds this limit, it is discarded.

Energy consumption is a key aspect, with slave nodes powered by finite-capacity batteries while the master node has sufficient energy from high-capacity batteries or wired sources. The network's operational lifetime is primarily dictated by the slave nodes, which cease to function when their batteries are depleted. The model assumes one unit of energy is consumed for the transmission or reception of a 37-byte packet, with energy use scaling with packet size for different lengths.

The problem formulation includes the lifetime of each slave node, defined in terms of cumulative power consumption over CIs compared to the initial battery levels. The overall network lifetime is determined by the first slave node to deplete its battery, highlighting the importance of efficient energy management in extending network functionality.
