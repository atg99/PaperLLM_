In this paper, the authors focus on maximizing the lifetime of a wireless sensor network (WSN) by addressing the scheduling of slave nodes. The network's overall lifetime is dictated by the slave node with the shortest lifetime. The objective is to maximize the network lifetime while ensuring that the quality of service (QoS) requirements for each slave node are met. This is formulated mathematically to maximize the minimum lifetime of the slave nodes subject to their QoS metrics being greater than their respective target requirements.

The authors introduce a Deep Q-Network (DQN)-based scheduling algorithm as a solution to the scheduling problems within WSNs. They provide an overview of reinforcement learning (RL), a machine learning approach where an agent learns to make decisions by exploring actions and receiving rewards from the environment. The interaction between the agent and the environment allows the agent to learn an optimal policy to maximize cumulative rewards without prior knowledge of the environment.

The paper also discusses Q-learning, a widely used RL algorithm that helps in finding optimal action policies through a Q-function. The Q-function quantifies the value of taking a specific action in a given state and is updated based on the received rewards. However, the authors note that traditional tabular methods for updating the Q-function face challenges such as rapid growth in state-action space and slow convergence, particularly in complex environments. 

Overall, the paper presents a structured approach to enhance WSN performance through effective scheduling while integrating advanced machine learning techniques.
