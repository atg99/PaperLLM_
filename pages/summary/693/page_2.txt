The study addresses the challenges of traditional scheduling algorithms that struggle to manage the complex interactions between transmission parameters, QoS constraints, and network lifetime. By modeling the master as an agent in a reinforcement learning framework, the algorithm learns to make scheduling decisions that maximize network lifetime while ensuring that packets meet their QoS requirements. The DQN scheduler receives feedback from the network environment, which allows it to adapt its policy based on factors such as node population, mobility, and data arrival rates.

Extensive experiments were conducted to evaluate the performance of the DQN scheduler against other existing methods. The results highlighted the DQN's ability to adapt to varying network conditions and improve both network lifetime and QoS guarantees. Additionally, the analysis of the DQN scheduler's policy provides insights into reinforcement learning-based scheduling methods, potentially informing the design of non-AI scheduling solutions through reverse engineering.

The paper is organized into several sections: the system model and problem formulation, the proposed DQN-based scheduling algorithm, experimental methods and results, a review of related work, and a conclusion summarizing the findings and implications of the research.
