The paper discusses a scheduling scheme called DET, which operates by sending as many packets as possible from a queue based on a maximum connection interval (CI). The study employs a Deep Q-Network (DQN) based scheduler and evaluates its performance against the DET scheme through various metrics including cumulative reward, network lifetime, and transmission ratio.

Key parameters used in the numerical experiments include a connection interval length of 0.0075 to 3.8825 seconds, a packet arrival rate of 0.5 packets per second, and transmission speeds of 1 Mbps. The results indicate that the DQN scheduler converged after 350 episodes, with cumulative rewards, network lifetime, and transmission ratios stabilizing at 15, 800, 1450, and 0.99 respectively. An interesting observation was made around the 15th episode, where a sharp increase in network lifetime occurred due to a dominant strategy of not transmitting packets, which while increasing lifetime, was not optimal for cumulative rewards.

Further analysis compared the DQN scheduler's performance to DET, particularly focusing on two variations of DET based on packet lifetimes of 2 and 4 seconds (DET2 and DET4). The findings revealed that while the DQN was comparable to DET4, it outperformed DET2. This was attributed to the longer packet lifetime in DET4, which reduced packet loss. The study concluded that the DQN-based approach, through dynamic adjustment of transmission parameters, outperforms deterministic scheduling methods like DET, especially in varying communication environments.
