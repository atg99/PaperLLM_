This paper discusses a DQN-based packet scheduling algorithm designed for wireless sensor networks (WSNs), focusing on optimizing energy efficiency and quality of service (QoS). It highlights the impact of advertisement intervals on packet collisions and energy consumption, referencing previous studies that evaluated Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) modules and proposed Q-learning for resource scheduling to enhance energy efficiency while ensuring QoS.

The authors note that while many IoT studies prioritize energy efficiency and throughput, there is limited research addressing QoS. They compare their work to studies by Rioual et al. and Collotta et al., which also explored energy management through Q-learning and fuzzy-based scheduling, respectively. However, existing methods have limitations, such as operating in simplified environments.

The proposed DQN scheduler dynamically adjusts connection intervals (CI) and the number of packets transmitted by each slave node, adapting to changing network conditions. Experimental results demonstrate that this scheduler can extend network lifetime and maintain QoS by favoring immediate transmission of packets over batch processing.

The authors suggest future research directions, including the reuse of learned policies in mobile networks with fluctuating node populations and analyzing the effects of scheduling overhead on optimal policies.
