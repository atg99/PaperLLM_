This paper discusses the significance of multi-step methods in deep reinforcement learning, particularly focusing on architectures like Rainbow, Reactor, IMPALA, and Ape-X. It highlights the limited direct comparisons of these methods, often due to the omission of standard deviations in reported results, which complicates the ability to draw statistically significant conclusions. The authors address this gap by evaluating various algorithms in the smaller mountain car environment, allowing for more meaningful performance comparisons.

The study explores n-step versions of algorithms such as Retrace, Q-Learning, Tree Backup, Sarsa, and Q(λ), implemented in an architecture similar to DQN. The research investigates the impact of off-policy corrections, the parameter n, and target network update frequency on algorithm performance. 

The paper also provides a foundational explanation of reinforcement learning through the Markov Decision Process (MDP), detailing the interactions between an agent and its environment, the selection of actions based on a policy, and the goal of maximizing expected discounted rewards. The authors define the action-value function and present update rules based on stochastic approximation algorithms, emphasizing the importance of the target in the update function, particularly in the context of one-step and multi-step methods. 

Overall, this work aims to enhance understanding of multi-step reinforcement learning algorithms and contribute to the exploration of less-studied methods within this domain.
