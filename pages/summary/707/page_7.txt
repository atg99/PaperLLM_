This paper investigates the performance of various reinforcement learning algorithms, specifically focusing on the impact of the backup length (n) in n-step methods such as Sarsa and Q-learning. The study tests different values of n (1, 3, 5, 10, and 20) to evaluate their effect on average return over intervals of 50 episodes, based on results from previous work by Sutton and Barto (2018).

The findings indicate that the 20-step version of each algorithm generally outperformed those with smaller n values, particularly in terms of initial performance. However, the overall performance gains were primarily attributed to improved initial results, with algorithms using n values of 3 showing similar final performance to those using n=20. The results suggest that increasing n can enhance algorithm performance, but no detrimental impact was observed even at higher n values, aligning with previous research indicating n as a sensitive hyper-parameter.

Additionally, the study highlights that algorithms like Tree Backup, which depend heavily on bootstrapping, showed increased sensitivity to the frequency of target network updates. This sensitivity is theorized to arise from a learning mismatch between the target and update networks. The paper emphasizes the importance of the n parameter and the target network update frequency in optimizing reinforcement learning algorithms. Further details on performance metrics are provided in the appendix.
