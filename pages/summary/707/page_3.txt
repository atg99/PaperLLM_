The paper discusses various n-step algorithms in reinforcement learning, particularly focusing on the Sarsa algorithm and its extensions. It begins by addressing the bias that arises when the target Q-value does not match the actual Q-value. To mitigate this bias, the authors propose using longer trajectories that incorporate more future reward observations, leading to the formulation of the n-step target for Sarsa.

The n-step target is defined recursively, allowing for a simplified expression that can be applied to other algorithms, such as the n-step Tree Backup algorithm. The key distinction between Sarsa and Tree Backup is that Sarsa samples an action at every step, while Tree Backup averages over possible actions. The paper also introduces the Q(ε) algorithm, which integrates both n-step Sarsa and Tree Backup, with a parameter ε that adjusts the balance between sampling and expectation during training.

The discussion transitions to off-policy algorithms, explaining that these methods estimate action values based on a behavior policy different from the target policy. Off-policy learning necessitates corrections for policy mismatches, which can be implemented using importance sampling in the case of n-step Sarsa. The Tree Backup algorithm remains unaffected as it computes estimates directly under the target policy.

The paper further examines the Q(ε) algorithm's adaptation for off-policy learning through the combination of per-decision importance sampling and Tree Backup. Additionally, it introduces the n-step Retrace algorithm, which can be adapted from eligibility trace methods to the n-step framework, providing a target that incorporates corrections based on action-value functions.

Overall, the paper presents a comprehensive overview of the development and adaptation of n-step algorithms in both on-policy and off-policy contexts, highlighting their recursive nature and the importance of policy alignment in reinforcement learning.
