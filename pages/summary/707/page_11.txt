The paper presents the final performance of various n-step algorithms, specifically measuring the average return over the last 50 episodes across 100 independent runs. The results include average returns, sample standard deviations, and 95% confidence interval bounds for multiple algorithms including Sarsa, Q-learning, Tree Backup, Decaying, and Retrace at different step sizes (n=1, 3, 5, 10, 20).

Key findings indicate that the n-step Retrace algorithm achieved its best performance with n=5, although the 20-step version yielded the highest overall performance. This suggests that improved initial performance significantly contributes to overall results. The analysis aligns with previous work by Hessel et al. (2017), highlighting n as a sensitive hyper-parameter, particularly noting that performance nearly doubles when comparing n=1 to n=5 in Q-learning. However, the current study contrasts with Hessel et al. by demonstrating better performance with higher values of n than those used in the Rainbow architecture.
