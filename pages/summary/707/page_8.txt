The paper presents a systematic evaluation of the performance of various multi-step algorithms in deep reinforcement learning, particularly in relation to target network update frequencies. The study includes algorithms such as Sarsa, Q-learning, Retrace, Tree Backup, and Q(ε=0.5), analyzing their performance across update frequencies of 500, 1000, and 2000 time steps. 

Results summarized in Table 2 indicate that increasing the target network update frequency negatively impacts the performance of Retrace, Tree Backup, and Q(ε=0.5) more significantly than Sarsa and Q-learning. This supports the hypothesis that these algorithms are more sensitive to target network updates due to their reliance on retrieving estimates from the action value function.

The paper concludes with three main findings: First, off-policy correction can be disregarded without significant detriment to Sarsa and Q(ε), although it may enhance early training performance. Second, the backup length parameter n is crucial for improving algorithm performance, confirming its status as a sensitive hyper-parameter. Lastly, algorithms that depend more on the target network exhibit greater sensitivity to its update frequency, suggesting that they might benefit from not using a target network to maintain stability.

Overall, the findings provide insights into the dynamics of multi-step algorithms in deep reinforcement learning, highlighting the interplay between off-policy correction, backup length, and target network updates.
