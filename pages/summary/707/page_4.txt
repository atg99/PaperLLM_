The paper discusses the n-step Q-learning algorithm, which, while not strictly off-policy, has shown effective performance in practice. The target for n-step Q-learning is defined as a combination of immediate rewards and future action-value estimates. The algorithm diverges from previous methods in its base case when estimating action-values, particularly when n is greater than 1.

The paper then transitions to the Deep Q-Network (DQN) architecture, a popular approach for approximating action-value functions in high-dimensional state spaces. DQN utilizes a neural network to estimate action-values, trained by minimizing a specific loss function. This architecture involves two sets of parameters: one that is learned during training and another, updated less frequently, used for target computations.

DQN incorporates an experience replay buffer, which stores observations for training. This allows for off-policy learning, where the policy during training may differ from that during sample collection. The paper highlights the introduction of additional hyper-parameters related to the buffer, including replay memory size, replay start size, and mini-batch size.

Lastly, the authors describe how they adapted the DQN architecture for various algorithms discussed previously, modifying the loss function to accommodate different targets and incorporating importance sampling ratios for certain algorithms. This adaptation aims to enhance the performance and applicability of DQN across various reinforcement learning methods.
