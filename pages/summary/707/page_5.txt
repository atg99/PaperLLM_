The paper discusses the design and evaluation of algorithms in deep reinforcement learning, focusing on the implementation of Q-learning methods with off-policy corrections. It details the structure of the buffer where entries are stored, including state, action, reward, and parameters pertinent to each episode. 

Empirical evaluations are conducted using the mountain car environment to analyze the impact of off-policy corrections, the parameter n, and the frequency of target network updates on algorithm performance. A timeout of 5,000 time steps is enforced for episodes to ensure manageable runtimes, with a distinction made between timeouts and terminations to avoid detrimental effects on learning.

The network architecture consists of an input layer for the car’s position and velocity, a hidden layer with 1,000 rectified linear units, and an output layer for action-values. The RMSprop optimizer is employed with specific hyperparameters, and the replay memory is set to a size of 20,000. Agents utilize an ε-greedy policy with ε set to 0.1 without annealing.

The study includes a comparison between off-policy and on-policy methods, particularly examining the performance of algorithms such as Sarsa and Q(ε) under conditions of off-policy correction. This investigation is motivated by previous findings from the Ape-X architecture, leading to a hypothesis that certain algorithms may not suffer performance degradation without off-policy corrections. The paper outlines the methodology for testing these algorithms, including variations with and without off-policy adjustments, to better understand their efficacy in different scenarios.
