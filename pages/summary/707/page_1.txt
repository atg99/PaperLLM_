This paper investigates multi-step deep reinforcement learning methods, specifically focusing on the DQN target. The authors, J. Fernando Hernandez-Garcia and Richard S. Sutton, highlight the importance of multi-step methods like Retrace and n-step Q-learning in modern deep reinforcement learning agents. They emphasize that these methods are often evaluated within larger frameworks, leading to insufficient sample sizes for statistically significant performance conclusions, which complicates understanding the impact of specific algorithmic details.

The study combines several n-step action-value algorithms with a DQN-like architecture and evaluates their performance in the mountain car environment, chosen for its faster training times and larger sample sizes. The authors conduct statistical analyses on various factors, including off-policy correction, backup length, and target network update frequency.

Key findings include: 
1. Off-policy correction negatively affects the performance of Sarsa and Q(λ).
2. Increasing the backup length consistently enhances performance across all algorithms.
3. Sarsa and Q-learning demonstrate robustness to target network update frequency effects, unlike Tree Backup, Q(λ), and Retrace.

The paper discusses the critical role of defining the update target in reinforcement learning, as it influences the algorithm's bias, variance, convergence guarantees, and speed. It underscores the necessity for systematic studies of these algorithms to facilitate informed decisions in designing reinforcement learning agents. The introduction of the TD(λ) algorithm is noted as a significant advancement, unifying Monte Carlo estimation with one-step temporal difference methods, thus paving the way for the resurgence of multi-step methods in deep reinforcement learning.
