The paper investigates the performance of various reinforcement learning algorithms, specifically comparing on-policy and off-policy methods. The average return was used as a performance metric, analyzed at three time scales: initial, final, and overall performance over 100 independent runs. The findings indicate that on-policy versions of Sarsa and Q(σ = 0.5) outperformed their off-policy counterparts in overall performance. Off-policy algorithms demonstrated better initial performance but did not perform as well in later stages, with statistically significant differences noted via non-overlapping confidence intervals.

Table 1 summarizes the overall performance of the algorithms, showing that off-policy methods had higher average returns but greater variability compared to on-policy methods. The p-values from Welch's test confirm significant differences between the two approaches, indicating that while off-policy correction can enhance initial performance, it may negatively impact final and overall performance if applied naively.

The results suggest a potential strategy of using off-policy correction only during the early training phase to optimize overall performance. The paper also outlines plans to further explore n-step algorithms and their impact on performance in deep reinforcement learning, highlighting the trade-off between bias and variance in return estimates.
