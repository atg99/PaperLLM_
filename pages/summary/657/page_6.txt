The paper presents experimental results on various graph-based node classification methods applied to citation datasets (Citeseer, Cora, Pubmed) and a biological dataset (PPI). In Table 1, it summarizes the datasets, detailing the number of nodes, edges, classes, features, and labeled nodes. The citation datasets feature articles as nodes and citations as edges, with a Bag-of-Words representation derived from article abstracts. The task involves predicting article subjects based on these features and citation relationships.

Table 2 displays the accuracy of different classification methods for the citation datasets and the F1 micro-averaged score for the PPI dataset. The results highlight the performance of both existing algorithms and the authors' implementations, including their proposed models, N-GCN and N-SAGE, which show competitive results across the datasets.

The experiments follow established splits for training, validation, and testing as defined by previous studies. The authors emphasize their ability to replicate original algorithms like GCN and SAGE, demonstrating the flexibility of their approach. Overall, the findings contribute to the understanding of graph-based node classification methods, showcasing the effectiveness of their proposed models in both transductive and inductive settings.
