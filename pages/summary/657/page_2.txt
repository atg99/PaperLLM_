The paper presents a novel approach for semi-supervised node classification using a network of Graph Convolutional Networks (N-GCN). The architecture integrates multiple GCN modules and a classification sub-network, which are jointly trained on an upstream objective. This setup allows for the examination of the classification sub-network weights, revealing that under input perturbations, the model favors GCN modules that utilize higher powers of the adjacency matrix. This adaptation effectively enhances the receptive field of the convolutional filters, demonstrating that explicit random walks improve the representational capacity of traditional GCNs.

The paper is structured into several sections. Section 2 outlines the foundational concepts of semi-supervised node classification, detailing traditional label propagation algorithms and their regularization techniques. Section 2.2 discusses the principles of Graph Convolutional Networks, explaining how convolution is adapted to graph-structured data through transformations in the Fourier domain and the use of Chebyshev polynomials for low-rank approximations. The GCN model is described in detail, focusing on its layer-wise transformations and the training process aimed at minimizing classification error.

In Section 2.3, the paper explores node embedding methods, which represent nodes in a continuous vector space. These methods leverage random walks to generate sequences of nodes that mimic textual data, facilitating the application of word2vec-style algorithms for embedding learning.

Through experimental evaluations in Section 4 and comparisons with related methods in Section 5, the paper demonstrates the effectiveness of the proposed N-GCN model, achieving state-of-the-art results in various semi-supervised graph learning tasks. The conclusion in Section 6 summarizes the contributions and outlines directions for future research.
