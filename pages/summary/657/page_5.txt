This paper presents a series of algorithms for implementing a network of graph models, specifically focusing on Generalized Convolutional Networks (GCNs) and GraphSAGE. The main function outlined in Algorithm 1 defines a general implementation that normalizes the adjacency matrix and iterates through multiple graph models to create a classifier. Subsequent algorithms detail specific models such as GCN and SAGE, outlining their respective processes for feature extraction and representation learning.

Training of the models is conducted by minimizing the cross-entropy loss between the model outputs and known training labels. This includes a diagonal matrix to focus on specific entries, and parameters are optimized for both fully-connected and attention-based networks. The paper also introduces a replication factor for GCNs, indicating that each normalized adjacency matrix can be fed into multiple GCN modules for enhanced learning.

The authors contextualize their work within the broader landscape of graph models, noting the adaptability of their method to various architectures beyond vanilla GCNs, including GraphSAGE. The algorithms provided serve as a foundation for constructing flexible and efficient graph neural networks, highlighting their practical application in graph-based tasks.
