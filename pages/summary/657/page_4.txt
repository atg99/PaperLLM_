In section 3.2, the paper discusses the limitations of vanilla Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) in capturing information across distant nodes due to their reliance on indirect information flow through feature reduction and non-linear activation functions. The authors propose that directly utilizing random walk statistics can enhance the GCN's ability to leverage distant node information, similar to how node embedding methods like DeepWalk outperform traditional methods based on adjacency matrices.

To address this, the authors suggest feeding a K-degree polynomial of the normalized adjacency matrix to K instantiations of the GCN, allowing the network to capture higher powers of the adjacency matrix. This approach maintains the symmetric properties of the matrices, ensuring that the eigen-decomposition remains valid.

In section 3.3, the authors introduce a network of GCNs, where each GCN outputs a latent representation matrix for each node. They propose two classification networks: a fully-connected classification network and an attention-based classification network. The fully-connected network concatenates the outputs of all GCNs and applies a trainable layer, enabling joint training of the GCNs and classification parameters. 

The attention network, on the other hand, employs a softmax attention mechanism to create a convex combination of GCN outputs, allowing the model to weigh the importance of each GCN's output dynamically. It can be adjusted using parameters that can be manually set or learned during training. This setup encourages all GCNs to be independently useful, enhancing the overall model's performance by focusing on relevant features from various graph scales and the input features.
