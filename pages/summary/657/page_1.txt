The paper introduces N-GCN, a novel model that integrates multiple Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) to enhance semi-supervised node classification on graph-structured data. This approach leverages the strengths of GCNs and the insights gained from random walks, allowing N-GCN to train various GCN instances on node pairs identified at different distances through random walks. The model effectively learns to combine the outputs from these instances to optimize classification performance.

N-GCN demonstrates significant improvements over state-of-the-art methods on challenging datasets such as Cora, Citeseer, Pubmed, and PPI. Additionally, it can generalize to existing semi-supervised learning techniques, such as GraphSAGE, leading to the development of N-SAGE. The model is also noted for its resilience against adversarial perturbations, making it a robust choice for real-world applications where labeled data is scarce and edges are more readily available.

The paper emphasizes the importance of semi-supervised learning in practical scenarios, such as predicting user interests in social networks and screening patients in healthcare. The proposed model builds on foundational GCN concepts and incorporates advancements in random walk-based graph embeddings, ultimately aiming to recover labels for all nodes in a graph using both feature data and a limited set of labeled nodes.
