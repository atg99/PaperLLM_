The paper discusses the lack of global convergence results for independent policy gradient methods in finite state/action zero-sum stochastic games. The main contribution is the demonstration that independent policy gradient algorithms converge to a Nash equilibrium when learning rates adhere to a two-timescale rule. This rule involves the min-player using a smaller step size than the max-player, allowing for a slower timescale for one of the players. This approach helps prevent limit cycles in minimax optimization and extends its advantages to multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL).

The authors introduce technical conditions that quantify convergence rates, particularly addressing the challenge of under-exploration of hard-to-reach states. They define a minimax mismatch coefficient to assess the difficulty of reaching certain states, which generalizes existing results in single-agent settings. The minimax mismatch coefficient is shown to be a weaker measure than other mismatch concepts, like concentrability, while still being significant.

To ensure the bounded variance of the REINFORCE estimator, the authors require both players to employ ε-greedy exploration alongside the policy gradient updates. The main theorem states that if certain conditions regarding learning rates and exploration parameters are met, then the expected difference between the value functions of the players converges to a specified ε after a polynomial number of episodes. This result represents the first finite-sample global convergence guarantee for independent policy gradient updates in stochastic games, highlighting that agents can operate independently using their own trajectories while maintaining a single parameter vector in memory.
