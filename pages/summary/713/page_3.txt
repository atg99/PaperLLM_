The paper discusses a framework for analyzing stochastic games involving two players with distinct objectives, where each player selects actions based on observed states and receives rewards. The game progresses through a series of time steps, with probabilities governing state transitions and potential termination of the game. The authors introduce the value function, which captures the expected reward based on the strategies (policies) employed by both players. They highlight the existence of a minimax theorem, indicating that there are Nash equilibria in which the expected rewards align under certain conditions.

The focus of the study is on developing algorithms that can approximate these Nash equilibria. The authors define visitation distributions to track the frequency of state visits influenced by the players' policies, which is essential for understanding the game's dynamics. They adopt non-asymptotic notation for clarity in their analysis.

Additionally, the paper outlines an independent learning protocol where both players independently propose and execute their policies over multiple episodes while observing only their respective trajectories. This setup emphasizes the players' limited knowledge of each other's actions, which is crucial for studying independent reinforcement learning in stochastic games. The independent distributed protocol is characterized by the players' restricted access to the game and limited memory, preventing coordinated strategies that could lead to easier solutions for equilibria. This approach aims to ensure that the learning process remains decentralized and reflective of real-world scenarios where players may not have complete information about their opponents.
