The referenced works cover significant contributions to economic theory, reinforcement learning, and game theory, particularly in stochastic and multi-agent contexts. 

John von Neumann’s model of general economic equilibrium provides foundational insights into economic systems and their equilibria. Wei et al. explore online reinforcement learning within stochastic games, enhancing our understanding of adaptive decision-making in uncertain environments. Williams presents gradient-following algorithms that simplify connectionist reinforcement learning, contributing to the efficiency of learning algorithms.

Xie et al. focus on learning strategies in zero-sum simultaneous-move Markov games, utilizing function approximation and correlated equilibria, while Yang et al. address global convergence and variance reduction in nonconvex-nonconcave minimax problems, highlighting advancements in optimization strategies.

Zhang et al. present a selective overview of multi-agent reinforcement learning theories and algorithms, emphasizing the complexity and strategies involved. They also demonstrate that policy optimization can reliably converge to Nash equilibria in zero-sum linear quadratic games, showcasing the robustness of reinforcement learning methods. Additionally, their work on model-based multi-agent reinforcement learning tackles sample complexity challenges in zero-sum Markov games, aiming for near-optimal efficiency.

Lastly, Zinkevich et al. contribute to the understanding of regret minimization in games with incomplete information, a critical aspect of strategic decision-making in competitive environments. Collectively, these studies advance the theoretical and practical frameworks of reinforcement learning and game theory.
