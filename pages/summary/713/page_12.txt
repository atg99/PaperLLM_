The referenced works focus on various aspects of reinforcement learning, stochastic games, and optimization methods. Key contributions include:

1. **Nash Q-learning**: Hu and Wellman propose a framework for general-sum stochastic games, extending traditional Q-learning to handle complex multi-agent scenarios.

2. **Q-learning Efficiency**: Jin et al. analyze the efficiency of Q-learning, questioning its provable performance and establishing theoretical foundations.

3. **Robotics and Reinforcement Learning**: Kober et al. provide a comprehensive survey on the application of reinforcement learning in robotics, highlighting challenges and methodologies.

4. **Actor-Critic Algorithms**: Konda and Tsitsiklis present actor-critic methods, a foundational approach in reinforcement learning that combines policy and value function learning.

5. **Min-Max Problems**: Kong and Monteiro introduce an accelerated proximal point method for nonconvex-concave min-max problems, advancing optimization techniques.

6. **Saddle Point Methods**: Korpelevich details the extragradient method for saddle point problems, a significant concept in optimization theory.

7. **Convergence in Games**: Lee et al. establish linear last-iterate convergence for matrix and stochastic games, contributing to the understanding of equilibrium finding in games.

8. **Deep Reinforcement Learning**: Levine et al. demonstrate end-to-end training of deep visuomotor policies, bridging deep learning with robotic control.

9. **Generative Adversarial Networks**: Liang and Stokes discuss the importance of interaction in the convergence of GANs, emphasizing local convergence properties.

10. **Continuous Control**: Lillicrap et al. present methods for continuous control using deep reinforcement learning, a pivotal advancement in handling complex action spaces.

11. **Saddle-Point Problems**: Lin et al. explore gradient descent ascent methods for nonconvex-concave minimax problems, contributing to the optimization landscape.

12. **Markov Games**: Littman presents a framework for multi-agent reinforcement learning using Markov games, laying groundwork for cooperative and competitive scenarios.

13. **Exploitability Descent**: Lockhart et al. propose a method for computing approximate equilibria in sequential adversarial games, focusing on exploitability metrics.

14. **Hybrid Algorithms**: Lu et al. introduce hybrid block successive approximation for one-sided non-convex min-max problems, with practical applications.

15. **Coordination in Cooperative Games**: Matignon et al. survey coordination challenges in independent reinforcement learners within cooperative Markov games.

16. **Policy-Gradient Limitations**: Mazumdar et al. highlight the lack of convergence guarantees for policy-gradient methods in specific game settings.

17. **Stochastic Dynamics**: Mertikopoulos and Staudigl study stochastic mirror descent dynamics, analyzing their convergence in monotone variational inequalities.

18. **Adversarial Learning Cycles**: Mertikopoulos et al. examine cycles in adversarial regularized learning, providing insights into the dynamics of learning algorithms.

These works collectively advance the field of reinforcement learning and optimization, addressing theoretical foundations, practical applications, and algorithmic development in multi-agent environments.
