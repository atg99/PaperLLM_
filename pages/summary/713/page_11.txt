This compilation of references highlights significant contributions to the fields of computational complexity, reinforcement learning, game theory, and optimization. 

Key works include Anne Condon's exploration of algorithms for simple stochastic games, which lays foundational insights into game-theoretic strategies. Christoph Dann and Emma Brunskill's research on sample complexity in episodic fixed-horizon reinforcement learning advances our understanding of learning efficiency in decision-making processes.

Constantinos Daskalakis and Ioannis Panageas have multiple entries that delve into the convergence properties of optimization algorithms in zero-sum games and constrained min-max optimization, emphasizing the dynamics of last-iterate convergence. Their work on no-regret algorithms provides near-optimal strategies for zero-sum game scenarios.

Research by Damek Davis and Dmitriy Drusvyatskiy discusses stochastic methods for minimizing weakly convex functions, presenting convergence rates that enhance optimization techniques. 

The survey by Pablo Hernandez-Leal et al. addresses learning in multi-agent environments, particularly focusing on the challenges posed by non-stationarity, which is crucial for developing robust multi-agent systems.

Martin Heusel et al. contribute to the understanding of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) and their convergence properties, providing insights into achieving equilibrium in adversarial settings.

Overall, these references encapsulate critical advancements in algorithms, learning theory, and optimization, reflecting ongoing research efforts to enhance computational efficiency and decision-making in complex environments.
