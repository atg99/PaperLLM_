The paper discusses the dynamics of two-player zero-sum stochastic games using a two-timescale update rule, which combines policy gradient methods for the min-player and best response strategies for the max-player. The authors emphasize the importance of agreeing on different learning rates before the protocol can succeed. The results indicate that the min-player's iterates can achieve approximate Nash equilibria, albeit with an asymmetric guarantee favoring the min-player.

The paper contrasts the findings with traditional gradient descent algorithms, noting that while those can fall into limit cycles with high error in individual iterates, the proposed method ensures low average error across iterates. However, the authors acknowledge that their current guarantees focus on average performance rather than on individual iterates, identifying this as an area for future research.

The techniques employed build on recent advancements in single-agent reinforcement learning, leveraging a favorable gradient domination condition even in non-convex settings. This condition allows the application of standard optimization results to establish convergence guarantees in the two-player context.

The authors present Lemma 1, which formalizes the gradient dominance condition, indicating that achieving low regret for either player requires finding a near-stationary point. They highlight that existing black-box minimax optimization results are lacking in this area, and their new proof establishes that two-timescale updates can effectively find Nash equilibria under the specified conditions.

In conclusion, the paper lays the groundwork for further exploration of optimization strategies in stochastic games and identifies potential improvements in the dependence on key parameters. The complete proof and additional details are provided in the appendix.
