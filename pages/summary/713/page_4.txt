This paper investigates the convergence of independent policy gradient methods in zero-sum stochastic games, emphasizing the need for minimal coordination among players. It notes that while the gradient methods discussed satisfy certain constraints, they do not achieve strong independence, as players must coordinate on the faster timescale for two-timescale updates. The authors highlight that developing provable guarantees for strongly independent algorithms remains an open question.

The focus is on policy gradient methods, which are popular due to their simplicity. Players use continuous policy parameterizations and treat the value function as a continuous optimization objective, updating their policies through stochastic gradient descent based on their own trajectories. The central inquiry is when these independent agents can converge to a Nash equilibrium.

The paper details the challenges posed by independent learning in stochastic games, referencing Condon's results that indicate naive independent learning strategies may cycle and fail to converge to equilibria. However, it also acknowledges that certain mixed strategies, such as one player performing policy iteration while the other computes best responses, can lead to convergence.

Furthermore, the paper discusses the limitations of online gradient descent dynamics in nonconvex settings, noting that independent updates with the same learning rate can result in cycling behavior. To achieve stronger convergence guarantees, more advanced techniques like two-timescale updates or negative momentum are necessary. The authors conclude that existing methods for convex optimization cannot be directly applied to the nonconvex nature of stochastic games, highlighting the complexity of the independent learning problem.
