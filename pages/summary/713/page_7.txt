The paper discusses the application of two-timescale stochastic gradient descent-ascent methods for solving nonconvex-nonconcave minimax problems. The central focus is on ensuring convergence to an ε-suboptimal solution using specific learning rates for the x and y players, allowing the analysis of the gradient dominance condition. The authors draw upon previous work on gradient dominance to establish bounds on the y-player's immediate suboptimality and demonstrate that the y-player can approximate a best response effectively.

The paper highlights the challenges posed by the nonconvex-nonconcave setup compared to the previously analyzed framework, emphasizing the necessity of minimizing the squared gradient of the Moreau envelope of the x-player's optimization problem. The authors prove that the expected difference between the maximum and minimum values of the objective function can be controlled within polynomial episodes of the learning process.

In the discussion section, the authors raise the issue of achieving last-iterate convergence for independent policy gradient updates in stochastic games. They reference existing methods such as Korpelevich's extragradient method and Optimistic Mirror Descent, acknowledging that while these methods show promise in practice, establishing their formal convergence properties remains an open problem. The paper concludes by using von Neumann's ratio game as an example to illustrate the complexities involved in achieving last-iterate convergence.
