The paper discusses the convergence properties of independent reinforcement learning, particularly focusing on policy gradient methods within the framework of two-player zero-sum stochastic games. It highlights the disparity between centralized algorithms, which have seen recent theoretical advancements, and independent algorithms, which lack robust theoretical guarantees and may fail to converge due to distribution shifts caused by independent policy updates. 

The authors analyze a specific setting where two players, a max-player and a min-player, engage in a competitive scenario with discrete state and action spaces, operating under the assumption that they cannot observe each other's actions. They demonstrate that while independent policy gradient methods generally do not guarantee convergence, implementing a two-timescale update rule can lead to convergence towards a Nash equilibrium.

Additionally, the paper indicates that extending beyond two-timescale rules by integrating optimization techniques from matrix games may necessitate new analytical approaches. Their findings contribute to a broader understanding of nonconvex minimax problems, expanding the class of these problems amenable to provable algorithms. The results are significant for the field of multi-agent reinforcement learning, particularly in enhancing the stability and reliability of independent learning methods.
