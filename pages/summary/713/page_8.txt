The paper discusses the application of the extragradient method in minimax problems, particularly focusing on the Minty Variational Inequality (MVI) property and its implications for last-iterate convergence. Figures 1(a) and 1(b) illustrate the behavior of the extragradient method in a specific ratio game, while Figures 1(c) and 1(d) depict results from a randomly generated game. The MVI property is essential for establishing convergence, but the authors demonstrate that it fails in certain stochastic games, including the simple ratio game under consideration, even though the extragradient method converges in these cases.

The authors present a unique Nash equilibrium for the ratio game and outline the conditions under which the MVI property holds or fails. They pose an open problem regarding the last-iterate convergence of the extragradient method with a constant learning rate in the context of the ratio game. Furthermore, they suggest that additional experiments indicate potential last-iterate convergence for general stochastic games with a positive stopping probability, highlighting the importance of proving such results to enhance the algorithm's robustness without requiring complex updates.
