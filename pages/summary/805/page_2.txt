The paper discusses the application of deep learning (DL) techniques for the classification of viral genomes, emphasizing a novel approach to improve the efficiency of deep neural networks (DNNs) in this area. It outlines a framework called CHEER for hierarchical taxonomic classification of viral metagenomic data, which categorizes viruses into groups such as genus, family, and order.

Several previous studies are referenced, including ViraMiner and DeepVirFinder, which focus on identifying viruses and differentiating between viruses and prokaryotes. Other works explore subtype identification for specific viruses like HIV, Dengue, and coronaviruses, utilizing convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for classification.

The methodology section details the dataset used for training the models, which consists of 1081 virus sequences from families such as Alphasatellitidae and Anelloviridae. The study ensures balanced representation across classes by selecting and duplicating sequences, resulting in a total of 1600 sequences for analysis.

The DNN architecture employed in this research is a CNN with specific parameters outlined in the paper. The architecture integrates one-hot encoding for the mapping stage and CNN processing for effective virus classification, aiming to enhance computational efficiency through model compression strategies, which have not been previously applied in related works. 

Overall, the research contributes to the field of viral classification by proposing innovative methods to leverage deep learning, thus providing a comprehensive framework for future studies and applications in virology.
