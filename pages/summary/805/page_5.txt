The paper discusses weight compression techniques in convolutional neural networks (CNNs) through pruning and quantization. It presents two main architectures: P!Q iteration and Q!P iteration. 

In the P!Q iteration, weights are first pruned and then quantized. Figures illustrate this process, showing weights of the second layer before and after the iteration. The pruning is executed based on a cut-off threshold, and a new gradient is calculated after the quantization step.

Conversely, the Q!P iteration reverses the order, first quantizing the weights before pruning them. This method also relies on an error metric to compute a new gradient, which allows for further weight updates within the same mini-batch. The paper includes figures demonstrating the weight compression achieved through this iteration, emphasizing the effectiveness of the proposed approach.

The results section highlights the advantages of the Q!P iteration in viral classification tasks, showcasing experiments with different quantization levels and pruning thresholds. The findings suggest that more aggressive pruning can lead to better performance, emphasizing the importance of the chosen parameters in optimizing CNN weight compression strategies.
