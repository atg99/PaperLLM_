The paper introduces a novel training scheme named Q!Piteration, which involves quantization followed by pruning during each training iteration to enhance deep learning inference for viral classification. The study evaluates this method against traditional approaches using a confusion matrix, demonstrating that Q!Piteration achieves superior performance compared to the conventional P!Qepochs strategy, which consists of separate pruning and quantization epochs. The research applied this strategy to 1600 viral sequences across three families and one realm, revealing that Q!Piteration results in a more efficient compressed model. The findings indicate that the Q!Piteration approach is particularly effective for 8-bit quantized deep neural networks compared to 32-bit single-precision models. Future work will aim to extend these results to additional classification tasks. The study acknowledges financial support from CAPES and the Air Force Research Laboratory, among others.
