In this paper, the authors address the computational challenges associated with deep learning in viral classification by introducing a novel training strategy for model compression. This strategy emphasizes the importance of reducing the computational complexity of deep neural networks (DNNs) while maintaining high classification accuracy. The proposed approach involves weight quantization followed by pruning in each training iteration, contrasting with traditional methods that apply pruning before quantization.

The study demonstrates the effectiveness of this new training scheme by applying it to a dataset of 1600 viral sequences from four virus types across three families. The results reveal a significant reduction in the number of DNN weights by 77% and the number of operations by 58%, all while preserving the model's classification accuracy.

The paper highlights the urgency of efficient viral classification methods, especially in light of the COVID-19 pandemic, where rapid processing of genomic sequences is crucial for understanding and controlling outbreaks. By enabling efficient deep learning processing on local hardware, the proposed method enhances data privacy and reduces reliance on cloud computing, which can be costly and less secure.

Overall, the authors advocate for the adoption of their novel training strategy in the field of viral classification, arguing that it represents a significant advancement over conventional techniques by effectively balancing model compression and accuracy.
