This paper discusses the application of two strategies, Q!Piteration and P!Qiteration, for classifying a virus using convolutional neural networks (CNNs). The dataset was split into 80% for training and 20% for validation, maintaining this ratio across classes. Training involved mini-batches of 64 over 35 epochs. Figures 13 and 14 illustrate the relationship between CNN weight density and validation accuracy, showing that Q!Piteration outperforms P!Qiteration in maintaining accuracy above 99% at lower weight densities.

The cut-off threshold significantly affects density reduction, with Q!Piteration achieving higher compression rates than P!Qiteration, as evidenced by the data presented in Table V. The highest compression rates for P!Qiteration are approximately 62.6% for weight density and size, while Q!Piteration achieves about 77.4% for the same metrics. The paper also presents confusion matrix results for the Q!Piteration strategy, indicating a substantial reduction in DNN parameters exceeding 77%. Overall, the findings suggest that Q!Piteration is more effective in maintaining accuracy during severe compression compared to P!Qiteration.
