The section discusses variance heterogeneity and model building in regression analysis. Variance heterogeneity, characterized by residual errors that exhibit a trend, can be addressed by modeling variance as a function of independent variables or fitted values. Various diagnostic methods for heteroscedasticity in normal nonlinear regression models include likelihood ratio tests, score tests, and nonparametric tests. For non-normal models, tests for over-dispersion and under-dispersion are also applicable. Ignoring variance heterogeneity may lead to misleading confidence and prediction intervals, though parameter estimates might remain relatively unaffected.

The generalized least squares (GLS) method is recommended for handling correlated residuals with heterogeneous variances. This approach replaces the standard noise distribution with a more general covariance structure. Gaussian processes with Bayesian nonparametric inference are also highlighted as effective for heteroscedastic nonlinear regression.

In model building, the aim is to create realistic representations of stochastic behaviors in data. The paper outlines several widely used models, including polynomial models, which are linear in parameters but may overfit, and concave/convex curve models. It details specific types, such as exponential models that describe growth trends and asymptotic models that represent limited growth approaching a horizontal asymptote. These models can be fitted using functions in R, facilitating application across various real-world data analyses.
