This paper discusses the mathematical foundations of the Fisher scoring algorithm and its equivalence to the Newton-Raphson method in the context of generalized linear models, particularly focusing on logistic regression. It begins by defining the weight matrix W based on the first derivatives of the link function h and the variance function g. The gradient of the log-likelihood function is derived, leading to a formulation involving W, the design matrix X, and the response vector Y.

The Hessian matrix is also explored, with its components expressed in terms of the second derivatives of the link function and the response predictions. The paper highlights that the Hessian, given by -XTWX, is strictly negative definite, ensuring convergence of the algorithm under typical conditions. However, it notes potential computational issues when response values are near the extremes of 0 or 1, leading to singularities in the Hessian.

The iterative process of the Newton-Raphson algorithm is outlined, showing how it can be reformulated as the iteratively reweighted least squares (IRLS) algorithm. Each iteration solves a weighted least squares problem, where the weights are derived from the variance function. Importantly, the paper emphasizes that certain link functions can lead to degenerate cases, which may affect the stability of the algorithm.

In the special case where the identity function is used as the link and the error distribution is Gaussian, the algorithm reduces to ordinary least squares estimation. The paper concludes by reinforcing the significance of the choice of link function and its implications for the algorithm's performance and convergence.
