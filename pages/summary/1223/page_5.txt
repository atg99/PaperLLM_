This paper discusses the impact of parameterization on the performance of the Gauss-Newton estimator in nonlinear regression, emphasizing the significance of two types of nonlinearity: intrinsic nonlinearity and parameter-effects nonlinearity. Intrinsic nonlinearity remains constant regardless of parameterization, while parameter-effects nonlinearity can be mitigated through appropriate parameterization. If either type of nonlinearity is substantial, convergence of least squares estimates may fail, and the estimated covariance can vary significantly during iterations, undermining the reliability of statistical inferences based on asymptotic normality.

The paper introduces two curvature measures—intrinsic curvature and parameter-effects curvature—that assess the quality of linear approximations of nonlinear mean functions. Intrinsic curvature pertains to the dataset and mean function but not to parameterization, while parameter-effects curvature is influenced by all model aspects, including parameterization. High values of these curvatures indicate poor linear approximations. The R package MASS provides a function to calculate these curvature measures for nonlinear least squares models.

The study also compares the Wald, likelihood ratio (LR), and score tests, noting their asymptotic equivalence in large samples. However, the Wald test is sensitive to curvature, unlike the score test, which relies on the slope and curvature at a specific point. The paper suggests that models with lower parameter-effects curvature are preferable for accurate inferences.

The effects of curvature on statistical inference quality are illustrated through simulations using the Michaelis-Menten model, demonstrating how confidence regions for parameters can be unreliable when the mapped parameter curves are not uniformly distributed. The analysis includes confidence regions for parameters derived from simulated data, emphasizing the importance of understanding the curvature in model fitting.
