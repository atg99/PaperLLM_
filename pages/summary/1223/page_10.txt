The paper discusses the Weibull-type 2 model, which analogously relates to the Gompertz curve as the log-logistic curve does to the logistic curve. The model is defined by the equation f(x,θ)=θ1+(θ4−θ1) exp{−exp[θ2(log( x)−log(θ3))]}, with parameters interpreted similarly to those in sigmoidal curves.

In the simulation data analysis section, the authors compare nonlinear least squares (NLS) estimation with Bayesian approaches using a dataset where the error term is not normally distributed. The simulated data, based on the Michaelis-Menten model, consists of 100 points generated under specific conditions, including a Gamma error distribution. The NLS method is implemented in R, testing two sets of initial parameter values to assess convergence.

Results indicate that both methods yield good estimates and confidence intervals when provided with appropriate initial values. However, poor starting values lead to significant deviations from true estimates in NLS, while Bayesian methods maintain wider confidence intervals that include the true values, albeit with local optimum convergence issues.

Further reading sections discuss relevant topics such as the Levenberg-Marquardt algorithm for improving convergence in least squares, multicollinearity issues in regression models, and alternatives to nonlinear regression like nonparametric and semiparametric methods. The paper also introduces kernel estimators for smoothing in regression analysis, emphasizing their application in local weighted averages.
