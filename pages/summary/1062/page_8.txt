The paper investigates the generation of synthetic time-series data using various generative models, focusing on four datasets: stock prices, energy consumption, and event sequences. Stock data is characterized by continuous values and correlated features, derived from Google's historical stock data from 2004 to 2019. The energy dataset exhibits noisy periodicity and higher dimensionality, sourced from the UCI Appliances energy prediction dataset. The event dataset consists of discrete values and irregular timestamps, specifically a lung cancer pathways dataset with event sequences.

The models evaluated include TimeGAN, RCGAN, CRNNGAN, T-Forcing, P-Forcing, WaveNet, and WaveGAN. Visualizations using t-SNE demonstrate that TimeGAN-generated datasets align closely with the original data, outperforming other models in both discriminative and predictive scores. For instance, TimeGAN achieves a discriminative score of 0.102 for stock data, significantly better than RCGAN's 0.196. The predictive scores of TimeGAN are comparable to the original datasets, indicating its effectiveness in generating high-quality synthetic data.

Overall, the results suggest that TimeGAN is superior in generating synthetic time-series data across various metrics, achieving high fidelity to original datasets. The findings are supported by detailed quantitative comparisons, emphasizing TimeGAN's potential for applications that rely on synthetic time-series generation.
