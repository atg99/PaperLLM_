In this paper, we introduce Time-series Generative Adversarial Networks (TimeGAN), a novel framework that effectively integrates the strengths of both supervised and unsupervised learning to generate realistic time-series data across various domains. The model is designed to preserve the temporal dynamics of the training data by implementing a dual loss structure. The first component is an unsupervised adversarial loss applied to both real and synthetic sequences, while the second is a stepwise supervised loss that leverages the original data to guide the model in capturing the underlying conditional distributions.

Additionally, we propose an embedding network that creates a reversible mapping between high-dimensional features and lower-dimensional latent representations. This approach acknowledges that the complex temporal dynamics often stem from a smaller set of factors, thus enhancing parameter efficiency and aiding the generator in learning temporal relationships more effectively.

Our framework is also adaptable to mixed-data scenarios, allowing for the simultaneous generation of static and time-series data. TimeGAN represents a significant advancement by merging the flexibility of unsupervised GANs with the structured control of supervised autoregressive models. We validate our method through extensive experiments on both synthetic and real-world datasets, employing qualitative analyses like t-SNE and PCA to assess the similarity of generated distributions to the original ones, and quantitative evaluations using post-hoc classifiers to differentiate between real and generated sequences.

The results demonstrate that TimeGAN outperforms existing state-of-the-art benchmarks in generating realistic time-series data, highlighting its effectiveness in preserving the predictive characteristics of the original datasets. Overall, TimeGAN stands out as a pioneering approach that addresses the limitations of prior models by effectively capturing the stepwise dependencies inherent in time-series data.
