The paper discusses TimeGAN, which integrates adversarial components to learn temporal data generation. It operates through an embedding network for feature representation and a recovery function for mapping back to the original feature space. The embedding function creates latent codes from static and temporal data while the recovery function reconstructs the original data from these codes. 

The embedding and recovery functions are autoregressive, meaning that outputs depend only on previous inputs, and can utilize various architectures, such as recurrent networks or attention-based models. The generator in TimeGAN first outputs synthetic data into the embedding space, utilizing random vectors drawn from known distributions, which are then processed to create synthetic latent codes. 

The paper emphasizes the importance of a supervised loss to synchronize the latent dynamics of real and synthetic data, allowing for effective learning of the underlying temporal structures. The training scheme includes both forward propagation of data and backpropagation of gradients, ensuring that the adversarial network effectively learns the temporal dynamics through lower-dimensional representations.
