The paper discusses the architecture and training process of a generative model called TimeGAN, which integrates a generator and discriminator operating in an embedding space to create synthetic time series data. The discriminator function, denoted as d, takes both static and temporal codes, producing classifications for real and synthetic embeddings. The architecture employs a bidirectional recurrent network for this purpose.

The model's learning process involves two main objectives: the reconstruction loss (LR) and the adversarial loss (LU). The reconstruction loss ensures accurate reconstructions of original data from their latent representations, while the adversarial loss helps the generator learn to produce realistic synthetic data. The generator operates in both open-loop and closed-loop modes during training. In open-loop mode, it generates embeddings based on its previous outputs, while in closed-loop mode, it uses actual data embeddings to produce new latent vectors. 

To enhance the learning efficiency, the paper introduces an additional supervised loss (LS) that captures discrepancies between actual and generated distributions. This dual approach encourages the generator to better model the stepwise conditional distributions of the data. The paper includes visual representations of TimeGAN alongside other models, highlighting the function application and loss computation within the framework.
