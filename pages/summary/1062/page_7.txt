The paper discusses the implementation and evaluation of TimeGAN, a generative model designed to synthesize time-series data. The evaluation is based on the mean absolute error (MAE) of estimated probabilities for event-based data. Supplementary materials provide benchmarks, hyperparameters, and additional visualizations related to the model's performance.

The authors introduce two key innovations: a supervised loss function that enhances the capture of temporal dynamics and an embedding network that facilitates lower-dimensional adversarial learning. These advancements are illustrated through experiments with autoregressive multivariate Gaussian models. The results, summarized in Table 1, indicate that TimeGAN outperforms various benchmarks in generating high-quality synthetic data, particularly in scenarios with higher temporal and feature correlations. This supports the effectiveness of both the supervised loss mechanism and the embedding network.

Furthermore, the paper explores TimeGAN's performance across diverse time-series datasets characterized by varying properties such as periodicity and noise levels. For example, the authors simulate multivariate sinusoidal sequences to assess the model's capability in handling independent features.

Overall, the findings highlight TimeGAN's superior performance in generating synthetic time-series data compared to existing methods, emphasizing its potential applications in various fields where time-dependent data is crucial.
