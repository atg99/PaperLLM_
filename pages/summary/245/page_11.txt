The paper discusses advancements in deep learning architectures for melanoma detection, specifically highlighting DenseNet as an enhancement over traditional VGG models. Dutta et al. (2016) note that VGG-16/19 is slow and tends to assign large weights to parameters. In contrast, DenseNet, as proposed by Sun et al. (2020), improves upon ResNet's residual connections by linking all previous layers to the current one, minimizing redundancy. The paper also addresses data imbalance in lesion classes, particularly a bias towards the akiec class, as illustrated in barplots. Additionally, it analyzes key parameters for classifying lesions into benign and malignant categories, including border irregularities, asymmetry, color variations, and diameter measurements, which are essential for accurate diagnosis.
