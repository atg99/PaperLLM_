This paper discusses various convolutional neural network architectures, focusing on their application in skin cancer detection. The authors highlight the importance of enhancing learning rates through concatenation of feature maps rather than just adding residuals. They describe the ResNet architecture, which addresses the vanishing gradient problem by breaking down the network into micro units that aggregate into a macro unit, facilitating effective learning through identity mapping.

Inception V3 is also examined, recognized for its ability to perform multiple convolution types as a multi-level feature extractor while benefiting from its smaller size compared to ResNet and VGG architectures. The paper presents experimental results demonstrating that deep learning convolutional neural networks significantly outperform traditional machine learning algorithms in skin cancer classification.

The authors implemented and tested several pre-trained models, including ResNet, DenseNet, Inception V3, and VGG-16, on the HAM10000 dataset. They reported that the DenseNet-II model achieved the highest accuracy at 96.272%, followed by a combination of ResNet and DenseNet at 92%. Other models, including ResNet, DenseNet, and VGG-16, yielded accuracies of 86.90%, 87.30%, and 75.27%, respectively.

The analysis included performance metrics such as accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score, with DenseNet-II consistently showing superior results across all metrics. The results underscore the effectiveness of deep learning models in skin cancer detection, particularly the DenseNet-II architecture.
