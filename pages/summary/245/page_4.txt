The paper discusses the advancements in deep learning (DL) techniques and their applications in melanoma detection and other medical fields. Researchers have extensively explored various deep learning methods, demonstrating their superiority over traditional machine learning (ML) approaches in terms of performance and accuracy.

Cruz and Wishart (2006) utilized artificial neural networks to predict cancer recurrence based on clinical data, confirming the effectiveness of deep learning models. Guo et al. (2020) enhanced a 3D convolutional neural network (CNN) to automate tumor boundary detection, showcasing the model's ability to learn and adapt to optimal prediction paths. Poma et al. (2020) optimized CNN for image recognition, while Neethu et al. (2020) compared their CNN model for hand gesture recognition with other baseline models, achieving high accuracy.

Kämmer et al. (2017) explored the potential of deep convolutional neural networks (DCNN) in pre-treatment prognostication by correlating complex patterns with immune therapy. They developed DCNN classifiers to analyze whole slide images of metastatic melanoma, integrating clinical demographic data for improved accuracy.

The motivation to enhance DenseNet through DenseNet-II aligns with the insights from Albu et al. (2019), emphasizing the benefits of artificial neural networks in medical diagnosis and decision-making. The authors highlight the capabilities of ANNs in improving medical processes, providing accurate alerts, and reducing burdens on healthcare professionals.

The paper also underscores the ability of DCNNs to classify and predict cancer types from diverse skin lesion images, supported by Esteva et al. (2017) who tackled data insufficiency using a comprehensive dermatological image dataset. The work of Borlea et al. (2021) addresses challenges in processing large datasets, further validating the potential of deep learning in the medical domain.
