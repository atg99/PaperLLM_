The paper discusses the results of a Friedman test applied to the HAM10000 dataset, indicating that the performance differences among various algorithms are statistically significant at a p-value less than 0.05, allowing for the rejection of the null hypothesis. The proposed advanced model, DenseNet-II, enhances the existing DenseNet architecture by incorporating features from Inception V3. It introduces a structure that divides each network layer into two 2-dimensional convolutional layers, integrating both symmetric and asymmetric components, along with label smoothing and batch normalization, which contribute to its robustness in handling complex data.

The model's performance is benchmarked against established deep learning architectures such as InceptionV3, ResNet, DenseNet, and VGG-16, which have been effectively trained for 10,000 classes. Performance metrics including accuracy, precision, recall, and F1 score are utilized to evaluate the model. Additionally, the training and validation losses are visualized over different epoch values to provide insights into the model's performance. A heatmap of the confusion matrix is also presented to substantiate the model's effectiveness during the testing phase, highlighting its comparative analysis based on recall across various epochs.
