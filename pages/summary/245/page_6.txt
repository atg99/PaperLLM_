Data pre-processing is crucial for preparing images for deep learning convolutional neural networks. The initial challenge was that the images of moles did not have a direct correlation with lesion types, necessitating the addition of three columns to the dataset: cell type, cell type index, and image path. This modification addressed redundancy and ensured each image corresponded to a unique lesion type. 

Subsequently, duplicates in the dataset were filtered out through data frame manipulation. Normalization followed, where pixel values were adjusted from a range of 0-255 to 0-1 by calculating the mean and standard deviation of the RGB matrices, facilitating smoother model training. An imbalance in the dataset was noted, particularly with the 'Actinic Keratosis' class having significantly more samples than others. This imbalance could skew the F1 score, favoring the majority class.

To address this, three steps were taken: 
1. The dataset was augmented using the Keras function ImageDataGenerator() to increase the number of training images and balance class representation.
2. Equalization sampling was implemented to distribute the generated data across the required classes.
3. Focal loss, an advanced version of cross-entropy loss, was utilized to assign lower weights to the majority class, thereby mitigating the imbalance.

After these adjustments, the dataset was deemed suitable for application in machine learning or deep learning models.
