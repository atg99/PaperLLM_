This paper presents a systematic review of the application of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) in the classification of skin cancer, particularly melanoma. It highlights the advancements in deep learning techniques that have demonstrated superior performance compared to human dermatologists in various studies. Key findings indicate that CNNs can classify melanoma images with accuracy that meets or exceeds that of medical professionals, as evidenced by multiple comparative studies where deep learning models outperformed dermatologists in image classification tasks.

The review references a range of studies, including those that utilized different CNN architectures, such as VGG and DenseNet, to analyze dermoscopic images and histopathological data. Notably, research has shown that CNNs achieved high diagnostic accuracy, often surpassing the performance of a significant number of dermatologists in head-to-head evaluations. The potential for CNNs to serve as diagnostic tools in clinical settings is emphasized, suggesting their role in enhancing skin cancer detection and prevention.

The paper also discusses the importance of large datasets for training CNNs, as well as the need for transparency and interpretability in AI models used in healthcare. The review concludes with a call for further research to optimize these algorithms and integrate them into routine clinical practice, ultimately aiming to improve patient outcomes in skin cancer diagnosis.
