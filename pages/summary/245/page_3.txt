This paper presents a comprehensive evaluation of a proposed deep learning model for melanoma detection, benchmarked against state-of-the-art algorithms such as ResNet, DenseNet, and VGG-16. The model is validated using a comprehensive dataset, which allows for further testing on other datasets to assess its predictive efficacy, addressing limitations identified in earlier works by Cruz and Wishart and Esteva et al. This research aims to streamline treatment pathways for melanoma patients, aligning with contributions made by Upadhyay and Nagpal.

The article is structured as follows: Section 2 discusses related work in cancer detection using artificial intelligence, highlighting the disparity in literature concerning melanoma detection. Section 3 outlines the proposed model, while Section 4 details the experimental setup and results analysis. Finally, Section 5 concludes the study and suggests future research directions.

The related work section describes the advancements in cancer detection methodologies, distinguishing between machine learning and deep learning approaches. Early machine learning efforts, such as those by Cruz and Wishart, focused on cancer susceptibility, survivability, and recurrence prediction. Additionally, Ontiveros-Robles and Vijayalakshmi's works illustrate various methodologies that enhance classification accuracy and address dataset challenges.

Deep learning models are recognized for their high accuracy and efficiency in handling large datasets, making them particularly relevant for melanoma detection. The paper emphasizes the need for well-structured data and mentions a cloud-based solution proposed by Richter and Khoshgoftaar to facilitate data collection for developing melanoma risk prediction models. Overall, the paper underscores the transformative potential of AI in improving melanoma detection and treatment strategies.
