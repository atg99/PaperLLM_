The paper discusses the development of frameworks and methods for Federated Learning in Connected Autonomous Vehicles (FLCA V), focusing on resource allocation and sensor placement challenges. It introduces two key methods: Multi-Stage Resource Allocation (MLGRA) and Vehicle Resource Consumption and Sensor Placement (VRCSP) to optimize network management and sensor positioning. The performance of these methods is validated using a software platform called CarlaFLCA V, showcasing their effectiveness in both single-task and multi-task perception scenarios.

The results indicate that the proposed MLGRA method outperforms baseline approaches in terms of accuracy and resource utilization across different tasks and stages. The study emphasizes the importance of addressing simulation-to-reality transfer challenges, as real-world datasets include complex physical interactions not captured in simulations. Additionally, it highlights the need for strategies to manage perception uncertainties that can affect autonomous driving systems, suggesting that end-to-end approaches may help mitigate these issues.

The work is supported by various funding sources, including the Science and Technology Development Fund of Macao, the National Natural Science Foundation of China, and the Australian Research Council, among others. Future research directions include enhancing the transferability of models from simulations to real-world applications and improving the robustness of autonomous driving under uncertainty.
