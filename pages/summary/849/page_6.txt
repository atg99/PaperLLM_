This paper discusses the integration of Deep Neural Networks (DNNs) and various modalities in the context of Connected and Autonomous Vehicles (CAVs). It introduces three key graphs: the Task-DNN Graph, the DNN-Modality Graph, and the Modality-Vehicle Graph, each serving distinct purposes in the framework of task generation and perception.

The Task-DNN Graph clusters tasks such as box regression, object classification, and orientation classification, allowing similar tasks to share a common DNN with a unified feature extractor. This approach optimizes training by utilizing a weighted loss function that encompasses multiple metrics.

The DNN-Modality Graph highlights the correlation between different DNNs and their respective data modalities, with historical performance data used to predict resource requirements for each DNN-modality pair. The paper presents inverse-power models that effectively forecast perception accuracies for various DNNs, demonstrating a strong alignment with experimental data.

The Modality-Vehicle Graph emphasizes the relationship between data modalities and CAVs, noting that data samples can originate from multiple vehicles. It assumes that when data is independent and identically distributed, it can enhance the robustness of the model's predictions.

Additionally, the paper illustrates the MLGRA method’s performance predictor's accuracy against experimental data and discusses the road-assisted feature detection and the Vehicle-Road Contrastive Principle, addressing challenges like inaccurate CAV detection. Overall, the proposed framework aims at maximizing throughput and improving sensor pose optimization through effective data aggregation across vehicles and road sensors.
