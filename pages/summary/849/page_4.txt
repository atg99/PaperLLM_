The proposed architecture includes various elements such as cameras, radar, and communication units that facilitate vehicle-to-everything (V2X) interactions. The system employs a multi-stage training pipeline consisting of cloud pretraining, edge federated learning, and cloud federated learning, which allows for iterative updates and personalization of deep neural networks (DNNs) across vehicles and edge servers.

Three significant opportunities for improvement in the FLCAV domain are identified: 

1. **Dataset Analysis**: The need for analyzing CAV-specific datasets and understanding interdependencies across different learning stages has been overlooked in existing literature. 

2. **Sensor Placement**: Efficient placement of road sensors is crucial for detecting occluded objects while adhering to budget constraints. Traditional methods, such as integer programming and heuristics, often neglect the learning requirements specific to FLCAV.

3. **Software Engineering**: Developing a high-fidelity FLCAV simulator that closely mimics real-world scenarios is necessary, as current network-layer designs are typically validated through simplistic classification tasks, which do not align with the complexities of autonomous driving simulations.

In conclusion, the paper underscores the importance of a well-designed network architecture and the need for advanced methodologies in sensor placement and simulation to enhance the functionality and reliability of connected autonomous vehicles.
