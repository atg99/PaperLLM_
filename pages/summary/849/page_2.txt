The paper discusses the perception systems of Connected and Autonomous Vehicles (CAVs), emphasizing the integration of various sensory modalities and deep learning techniques to enhance vehicle understanding of their environment. Key categories of perception include sign recognition, weather classification, motion perception, and trajectory prediction. Each of these categories relies on multi-modal sensors such as cameras, LiDAR, and radar to gather data which undergoes processing through signal processors and deep neural networks (DNNs) to extract meaningful features.

The perception pipeline is designed to output critical information for collision avoidance, object tracking, and future state predictions of other agents on the road. For instance, trajectory prediction utilizes images from multiple cameras processed through vision transformers and LSTM networks to anticipate the movement of traffic agents based on their historical coordinates.

The paper also highlights significant challenges in CAV perception, particularly in handling corner cases within diverse and unpredictable environments. The increasing complexity of scenario spaces necessitates advanced methodologies to ensure reliable vehicle operation. Overall, the study underscores the need for sophisticated multi-task learning frameworks that leverage heterogeneous data sources to improve CAV performance in real-time decision-making.
