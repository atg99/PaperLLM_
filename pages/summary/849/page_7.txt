a framework for federated learning connected autonomous vehicles (CA V) that emphasizes the importance of optimizing sensor placement to enhance data quality and vehicle learning. The study critiques conventional sensor placement strategies that prioritize visibility over the diversity of data collected. Instead, the proposed method, termed Vehicle Road Sensor Placement (VRCSP), focuses on positioning sensors in critical scenarios where they can capture unique data that the onboard deep neural networks (DNNs) may miss due to occlusions or limited fields of view.

The VRCSP algorithm minimizes the similarity of detected objects between the CA V and the roadside sensors, thereby maximizing the calibration of false detections. The methodology involves registering missed detections and using this data to identify optimal sensor locations, which can be constrained by infrastructure costs and physical placement limitations. The implementation of this framework is supported by the CarlaFLCA V simulation platform, which allows for multi-modal data generation and supports various federated learning frameworks.

The results of this approach demonstrate significant improvements in the perception capabilities of roadside infrastructure through multi-modal multi-view sensor fusion. The paper outlines the software architecture for CarlaFLCA V, including the calibration and synchronization of sensory data, and discusses the challenges of real-world implementation, such as sensor errors and communication limitations. Ultimately, the framework aims to enhance the learning and operational efficiency of CA Vs in urban environments.
