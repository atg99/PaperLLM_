The paper presents SimMatch, a novel semi-supervised learning framework addressing the challenge of learning from limited labeled data in computer vision. SimMatch innovatively combines semantic similarity and instance similarity by applying consistency regularization at both levels. This approach encourages different augmented views of the same instance to maintain identical class predictions while respecting similarity relationships with other instances.

To enhance the learning process, the authors implement a labeled memory buffer that utilizes ground truth labels to bridge the gap between semantic and instance similarities. Furthermore, SimMatch introduces an unfolding and aggregation operation, enabling an isomorphic transformation between the two types of similarities. This mutual propagation of semantic and instance pseudo-labels results in the generation of more reliable matching targets.

Experimental results indicate that SimMatch significantly enhances performance in semi-supervised learning tasks across various benchmark datasets. Notably, with 400 training epochs, SimMatch achieves 67.2% and 74.4% Top-1 Accuracy with only 1% and 10% labeled examples on ImageNet, surpassing baseline methods and previous semi-supervised learning frameworks. The work highlights the effectiveness of leveraging both semantic and instance-level information in improving learning outcomes in scenarios with scarce labeled data.
