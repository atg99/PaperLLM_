The paper presents experimental results on self-supervised learning methods applied to ImageNet, comparing performance based on varying fractions of labeled data (1% and 10%). The table outlines the performance metrics, namely Top-1 and Top-5 accuracy, for various methods including Pseudo-label, FixMatch, CoMatch, and the proposed SimMatch. SimMatch demonstrates competitive performance, achieving Top-1 accuracies of 67.2% and 74.4% for 1% and 10% labeled data respectively, which is comparable to or exceeds other methods.

In transfer learning evaluations, SimMatch is shown to perform well across multiple datasets such as CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100, with accuracy metrics close to or better than BYOL, and significantly outperforming SimCLR and MoCo v2. The training efficiency analysis indicates that SimMatch is approximately 17% faster than FixMatch and CoMatch, attributed to its streamlined computational requirements since it only necessitates one forward pass through the network.

The ablation studies focus on various parameters affecting the performance of SimMatch. The pseudo-label accuracy is consistently high, and the optimal temperature for instance distribution sharpness is found to be 0.1. The smooth parameter α is also analyzed, revealing that α=0.9 yields the best results, while α=1.0 leads to a performance drop. Finally, the study investigates label propagation effectiveness, highlighting its role in enhancing the overall accuracy of the method.

Overall, the findings validate SimMatch's capability in producing high-quality representations suitable for classification tasks while maintaining efficient training processes.
