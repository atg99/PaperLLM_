The paper discusses a method for embedding similarity and consistency regularization in the context of weakly and strongly augmented views of data samples. It begins by defining weakly augmented embeddings and calculating their similarities using a dot product similarity function. The softmax layer processes these similarities to produce a distribution of probabilities, controlled by a temperature parameter. A similar process is applied to strongly augmented views, resulting in two distributions: qw for weakly augmented and qs for strongly augmented views.

To enhance the model's performance, the authors propose minimizing the difference between qw and qs using a cross-entropy loss, specifically for unlabeled examples. The overall training objective incorporates this instance consistency regularization alongside other loss functions.

Furthermore, the paper introduces a label propagation technique called SimMatch, which leverages labeled information to improve the quality of pseudo-labels generated for instances. A labeled memory buffer is maintained to store annotated examples, allowing for the assignment of class labels to embeddings. The process involves computing semantic and instance similarities, calibrating the instance pseudo-labels based on semantic similarities, and adjusting semantic pseudo-labels using instance similarities.

The authors propose a scaling and smoothing strategy for the regenerated pseudo-labels, ensuring that both semantic and instance-level information are integrated. This results in sharper pseudo-labels when similarities align and flatter distributions when they diverge. The paper concludes with discussions on the effectiveness of these strategies and references further analysis in the ablation study section.
