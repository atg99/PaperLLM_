The paper presents SimMatch, a method for generating semantic and instance pseudo-labels in the context of semi-supervised image classification. The process begins with weakly augmented samples from labeled data, where semantic and instance similarities are computed using class centers and labeled embeddings. These similarities are then fused through unfolding and aggregation operations to derive the pseudo-labels.

Previous approaches like CoMatch have utilized consistency regularization and contrastive learning but are sensitive to hyper-parameters, which can vary across datasets. In contrast, SimMatch is designed to be faster, more robust, and to achieve higher performance.

The semi-supervised learning framework is defined with labeled and unlabeled samples. Labeled samples are processed with weak augmentation to extract features, which are then mapped to semantic similarities using a prediction head. The unsupervised classification loss is calculated between predictions from weakly and strongly augmented samples, focusing on those that exceed a confidence threshold.

SimMatch also emphasizes instance-level similarity, encouraging the strongly augmented view to mirror the similarity distribution of the weakly augmented view. This holistic approach aims to enhance the classification performance in semi-supervised settings.
