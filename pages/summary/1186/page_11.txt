The references presented encompass a range of advancements in the field of machine learning, particularly focusing on semi-supervised learning, hashing techniques, and neural network architectures. 

Key contributions include:

1. **Semi-supervised Learning**: The work by Yi Xu et al. introduces DASH, a method utilizing dynamic thresholding to enhance semi-supervised learning capabilities, demonstrating significant improvements in performance metrics.

2. **Deep Hashing Techniques**: Erkun Yang and colleagues present two notable approaches: Pairwise relationship guided deep hashing for effective cross-modal retrieval and Distillhash, which focuses on unsupervised deep hashing through the distillation of data pairs.

3. **Neural Architecture Search (NAS)**: The Greedynas framework proposed by Shan You et al. aims at optimizing one-shot neural architecture search through a greedy supernet approach, facilitating faster model selection.

4. **Generalization in Deep Learning**: Chiyuan Zhang et al. emphasize the necessity of rethinking generalization in deep learning, contributing to the ongoing dialogue about the theoretical underpinnings of model performance.

5. **Mixup Strategy**: The Mixup technique by Hongyi Zhang et al. extends beyond traditional empirical risk minimization, offering a novel approach to data augmentation that enhances model robustness.

6. **Weakly Supervised Learning**: Mingkai Zheng and his team explore weakly supervised contrastive learning and relational self-supervised learning, highlighting the efficacy of these methods in leveraging limited labeled data for improved model training.

Overall, these contributions reflect ongoing innovations that address the challenges in machine learning, particularly in enhancing efficiency, performance, and theoretical understanding in various applications.
