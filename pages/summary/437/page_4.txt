The paper discusses the integration of an Edge Agreement Head into existing Mask R-CNN systems with minimal computational and memory overhead, facilitating its adoption without new hardware. It details implementation specifics, including the use of a mask size of 28x28 pixels and resizing images to 1024x800 pixels while zero-padding to maintain aspect ratios. The training process involves three stages over 160,000 steps on the MS COCO 2017 dataset, utilizing a batch size of 2 on a single GPU, and follows a structured learning rate adjustment and optimization strategy.

Experiments are conducted using the Matterport implementation of Mask R-CNN, focusing on various aspects of the Edge Agreement Head, including the influence of edge detection filters, loss function steepness, and the effect of weighting the Edge Agreement Loss. The training is standardized with random horizontal flips for data augmentation, and results are averaged across three runs to ensure consistency.

Key findings indicate that the Sobel filter significantly enhances training speed and mask quality compared to other filters, yielding a 7% relative improvement in average precision (AP) scores and larger gains in specific metrics. In contrast, the Laplacian filter showed minimal benefits, and combining multiple filters did not enhance performance. The paper acknowledges the limitations of COCO Mask metrics in assessing detailed mask quality, advocating for the mask loss as a more accurate measure of instance mask quality. Overall, the proposed modifications show a notable improvement in performance metrics, demonstrating the effectiveness of the Edge Agreement Head in enhancing Mask R-CNN.
