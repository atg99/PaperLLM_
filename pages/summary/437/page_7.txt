The paper discusses the impact of various weighting factors on the performance of the Edge Agreement Loss within the context of instance segmentation using the Mask R-CNN framework on the MS COCO dataset. It provides visual representations of loss trajectories for different alpha values across L2 and L4 loss functions, highlighting their effects on both total and mask-specific losses.

Table 3 presents a comparison of instance segmentation metrics, specifically the Average Precision (AP) scores, between the baseline and the proposed method for different mask sizes. The results indicate that the proposed method generally outperforms the baseline across various AP metrics, particularly for smaller mask shapes.

The paper also addresses the challenges posed by the binary nature of ground-truth masks versus the continuous output of the mask branch. It introduces a Gaussian smoothing technique for the ground-truth masks to mitigate distractive errors, particularly around boundaries. However, the findings reveal that smoothing does not necessarily enhance training performance as expected; in fact, using the Sobel filter without smoothing yielded better results in terms of training loss.

This suggests that the network benefits from learning from all errors, not just significant ones, contradicting initial assumptions regarding the advantages of smoothing. The paper concludes that while smoothing can focus on major mistakes, the presence of all errors is crucial for effective training. Further investigations into the effects of smoothing both ground truth and predicted masks are suggested for future work.
