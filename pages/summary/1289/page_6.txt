The paper presents Topological Autoencoders (TopoAE), a novel approach to dimensionality reduction that incorporates topological constraints into the standard autoencoder framework. The authors compare TopoAE to traditional methods such as PCA, Isomap, and t-SNE, using a consistent two-dimensional latent space for all techniques. The evaluation process involves splitting datasets into training and testing sets, with a portion reserved for hyperparameter tuning. 

The quality of the latent representations is assessed through low-dimensional visualizations, dimensionality reduction metrics, and reconstruction errors. Key metrics include the Kullback–Leibler divergence to evaluate density preservation, as well as various non-linear dimensionality reduction quality metrics such as root mean square error, mean relative rank error, continuity, and trustworthiness. 

Results indicate that TopoAE effectively maintains data density across multiple scales and achieves competitive values for continuity and reconstruction errors. Unlike traditional methods, TopoAE successfully captures complex relationships within data, outperforming others like t-SNE that may distort the original data structure. Overall, the findings suggest that the integration of topological constraints enhances the performance of autoencoders in capturing intricate data patterns while ensuring reliable reconstruction.
