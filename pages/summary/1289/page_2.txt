This paper presents a framework for topological autoencoders that aims to preserve the topological structures of data during the encoding process. The approach is based on the persistent homology of Vietoris–Rips complexes, which are constructed from point clouds by analyzing pairwise distances. As the distance threshold increases, topological features are created and destroyed, which can be recorded in persistence diagrams.

The authors detail the process of calculating persistent homology for a given point cloud, resulting in a tuple of persistence diagrams and pairings that represent the birth and death of topological features. The stability of these diagrams against small perturbations in the dataset is highlighted, and a method for comparing diagrams through bottleneck distance is introduced.

The core of the proposed method involves training an autoencoder to reconstruct input data while incorporating a topological loss function. This loss function measures the differences in topological features between the input data and its latent representation, ensuring that essential topological structures are maintained in the encoding process.

In summary, the paper outlines a novel approach to autoencoders that integrates topological considerations, leveraging persistent homology to enhance the representation of data in latent spaces while preserving critical topological features.
