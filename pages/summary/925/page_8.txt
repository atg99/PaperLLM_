The paper presents a robust evaluation of various defense methods against white-box attacks in deep metric learning (DML) datasets, specifically CUB, CARS, and SOP. The authors compare their proposed method, which utilizes a combination of techniques including Hard Mining (HM), Loss Gradient Adjustment (LGA), and Intermediate Class Similarity (ICS), against established state-of-the-art defenses such as EST and ACT.

Key findings reveal that while EST achieves a relatively high recall at rank 1 (R@1) at a low training cost, its effectiveness drops significantly when training costs increase. ACT demonstrates better efficiency but also shows a notable decline in R@1 across datasets. In contrast, the proposed method consistently outperforms these existing methods, achieving the highest efficiency and robustness with minimal degradation in R@1.

The authors emphasize the importance of selective training on both benign and adversarial samples to prevent model collapse, suggesting that their method, particularly through the Softhard sampling strategy, enhances robustness against various attacks. They acknowledge that while their approach excels in overall performance, the robustness observed may also be influenced by the sampling strategy employed.

In conclusion, the paper asserts that the proposed framework not only surpasses previous defenses in performance but also addresses the critical balance of robustness and performance on benign examples, highlighting the effectiveness of selective adversarial training.
