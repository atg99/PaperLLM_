The paper discusses the vulnerabilities of Deep Metric Learning (DML) to adversarial attacks, which can compromise safety, security, and fairness. Existing attacks can either completely disrupt image retrieval or subtly alter top-ranking results. In contrast, defenses against these attacks are less developed. Two notable methods are the Embedding Shifted Triplet (EST) and Anti-Collapse Triplet (ACT), which aim to enhance robustness but suffer from efficiency issues.

The authors propose a new approach involving a function that maps data points into an embedding space, using triplet loss to learn this embedding. They define "hardness" related to the triplet loss, which helps in manipulating the triplet to achieve a desired hardness level through adversarial perturbations. This process, termed Hardness Manipulation (HM), seeks to enhance the source triplet's hardness by optimizing adversarial examples. The optimization problem is solved using Projected Gradient Descent (PGD), and the resulting adversarial examples are utilized for adversarial training of the DML model.

The HM method is designed to efficiently train the model against adversarial attacks by maximizing the triplet loss while reusing gradients during the training process. This approach aims to achieve better adversarial robustness, training efficiency, and performance on benign examples, ultimately contributing to more resilient DML applications.
