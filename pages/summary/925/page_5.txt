The paper presents a study on the effectiveness of various triplet sampling strategies in deep metric learning (DML) and their impact on adversarial robustness. The authors focus on the mean and variance of hardness calculated across different sampling methods, such as Random, Semihard, Softhard, Distance, and Hardest, as outlined in Table 1. They conduct experiments on three datasets: CUB-200-2011, Cars-196, and Stanford-Online-Product, using an ImageNet-initialized ResNet-18 model with specific training parameters.

The experiments measure model performance on benign examples through metrics like Recall@1, Recall@2, mean Average Precision (mAP), and Normalized Mutual Information (NMI). Adversarial robustness is evaluated using the Empirical Robustness Score (ERS), which assesses the model's resilience against a collection of attacks.

Results are presented in Table 2, showcasing various combinations of source and destination hardness and their effects on Recall@1 and ERS. The authors find that not all combinations of sampling strategies yield effective results. Specifically, when the expected hardness of source triplets is greater than or equal to that of destination triplets, the model tends to experience collapse. However, certain combinations like Distance and Hardest show promise in maintaining robustness without triggering model collapse.

The paper concludes that the hardness range of the Semihard strategy is suitable for enhancing adversarial robustness and that effective combinations of sampling strategies can create balanced mini-batches of unperturbed and adversarial triplets. This study highlights the importance of selecting appropriate triplet sampling strategies to improve adversarial robustness in DML.
