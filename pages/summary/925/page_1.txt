The paper "Enhancing Adversarial Robustness for Deep Metric Learning" by Mo Zhou and Vishal M. Patel addresses the critical issue of adversarial vulnerability in deep metric learning (DML) models. The authors highlight the inadequacies of existing defenses, which often avoid min-max adversarial training to prevent model collapse from excessively hard examples. Instead, these methods typically rely on weak adversaries, leading to inefficiency and lower robustness.

To overcome these challenges, the authors introduce a novel approach called Hardness Manipulation, which efficiently perturbs training triplets to achieve a desired level of hardness, drawing from either a harder benign triplet or a pseudo-hardness function. This method is versatile, encompassing both regular training and min-max adversarial training as its extremes.

Additionally, the paper proposes Gradual Adversary, a series of pseudo-hardness functions that incrementally raise the hardness level during training, optimizing the balance between model performance and robustness. An Intra-Class Structure loss term is also introduced to enhance robustness and efficiency among both benign and adversarial examples.

Experimental results demonstrate that this approach significantly outperforms state-of-the-art defenses in terms of robustness, training efficiency, and performance on benign examples. The findings underscore the importance of effective adversarial training strategies in enhancing the security and reliability of DML applications.
