This paper introduces the concept of "hardness" for sample triplets in deep metric learning (DML), defined as the difference between the anchor-positive and anchor-negative distances. The authors propose Hardness Manipulation (HM) to adversarially perturb sample triplets, aiming to adjust their hardness to a specified level for improved adversarial training. The HM objective minimizes the L-2 norm of the difference between the triplet's current hardness and the target hardness. This approach is flexible, encompassing both regular training and min-max adversarial training as special cases.

The paper discusses the challenge of determining the appropriate "destination hardness." It suggests that the destination hardness should be moderately harder than the original triplet to avoid model collapse while maintaining effective adversarial learning. To address this, the authors introduce Gradual Adversary, a set of pseudo-hardness functions that adapt the hardness throughout training phases. They specifically design a Linear Gradual Adversary (LGA) function to maintain a Semihard hardness level based on empirical findings.

Additionally, the authors propose an intra-class structure loss term to enhance robustness, addressing the intra-class relationships that prior methods have overlooked. They validate their approach through comprehensive experiments on three DML datasets, demonstrating that their method significantly outperforms existing defenses in terms of robustness, training efficiency, and performance on benign examples.

The paper's contributions are threefold: the introduction of Hardness Manipulation for generating adversarial triplets, the development of the Linear Gradual Adversary function for balancing training objectives, and the proposal of an intra-class structure loss term to improve robustness and efficiency in adversarial training. The related works section reviews adversarial attacks and defenses, highlighting the limitations of current methods and the effectiveness of adversarial training despite its drawbacks.
