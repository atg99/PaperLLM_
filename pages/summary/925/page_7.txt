The paper investigates the effectiveness of various white-box attack strategies for evaluating the robustness of models in the context of defense mechanisms. It presents a series of experiments comparing different defense methods and their performance metrics, including retrieval accuracy (R@1, R@2), mean average precision (mAP), normalized mutual information (NMI), and class accuracy (CA+ and CA-). 

Key findings indicate that gradually increasing hardness in adversarial training, specifically using the method HM[S, gLGA], offers a balanced trade-off between embedding learning and adversarial robustness. This approach achieves competitive embedding robustness scores (ERS) while maintaining a lower penalty on R@1 performance compared to other methods. The study identifies that setting hardness too high or too low can lead to model collapse or limited improvements in robustness.

Additionally, the introduction of intra-class structure loss (LICS) in conjunction with hardness manipulation (HM) demonstrates improved training efficiency and robustness without significantly compromising retrieval performance. The combination of HM[S, gLGA] and LICS ultimately surpasses existing state-of-the-art defenses in terms of robustness, efficiency, and retrieval accuracy.

In summary, the findings suggest that the proposed methods effectively enhance model robustness while maintaining performance, contributing valuable insights into adversarial training strategies.
