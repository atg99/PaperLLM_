The paper discusses the concept of destination hardness (HD) in the context of deep metric learning (DML) and adversarial training. It introduces a flexible framework for defining HD, which can be a constant, derived from another benign triplet, or generated by a pseudo-hardness function. The relationship between the source triplet hardness (HS) and the upper bound of hardness (2) is critical, as pushing HS too close to 2 can lead to model collapse. Thus, HD should be chosen within the interval [HS, 2].

The authors propose a "Gradual Adversary" approach to address the diminishing effectiveness of adversarial examples towards the end of training. This involves using a pseudo-hardness function to slightly increase HD during late training phases, ensuring that adversarial examples retain their effectiveness. The proposed function, denoted as gB, allows for a controlled adjustment of HD based on the loss from previous iterations. 

The paper also highlights the importance of balancing the performance on benign examples with robustness against adversarial attacks. A "Linear Gradual Adversary" (LGA) function is introduced, which adjusts the hardness based on empirical observations to ensure that adversarial examples are appropriately weak in the early training phases and stronger in later phases.

Additionally, the authors address the intra-class structure, which has been overlooked in existing DML defenses. A new loss term, LICS, is proposed to enforce this structure by ensuring that the anchor and its adversarial counterpart are effectively separated from the positive sample, which can enhance robustness against attacks that attempt to alter rankings within the same class.

Overall, the paper presents a comprehensive method to improve adversarial training and DML by carefully managing hardness and incorporating intra-class structure into the training process.
