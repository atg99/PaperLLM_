The paper evaluates the proposed MTL and HT meta-batch methods through experiments focused on few-shot recognition accuracy and model convergence speed. The experiments utilize two datasets: miniImageNet and Fewshot-CIFAR100 (FC100), with detailed settings outlined for each.

miniImageNet, a well-established benchmark, consists of 100 classes with 600 samples each, featuring 84x84 color images. The dataset is divided into 64 classes for meta-training, 16 for meta-validation, and 20 for meta-testing. In contrast, FC100 presents a more challenging scenario with lower image resolution and stricter splits based on object super-classes, containing 100 classes with 600 samples of 32x32 color images.

The experiments adopt a 5-class classification approach, sampling episodes with varying shot settings. The authors employ deep neural networks (DNN) for training, stopping after 10,000 iterations, and use task sampling methods consistent with prior works.

The paper also details the network architecture, featuring two options: ResNet-12 and a simpler 4CONV model. ResNet-12, preferred for its superior performance, consists of multiple residual blocks and is trained on large-scale data. The feature extractor, meta-learner, and classifier architectures are defined, with an emphasis on the efficiency of a single fully connected layer for classification.

An ablation study is conducted to assess the effectiveness of the proposed methods, comparing them against baselines without meta-learning and various fine-tuning strategies. Results from the experiments are summarized in tables, highlighting the highest accuracies achieved. The findings demonstrate the proposed methods' effectiveness in improving few-shot learning performance across the tested datasets.
