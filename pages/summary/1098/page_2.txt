This paper introduces a novel meta-learning approach called meta-transfer learning (MTL) designed to enhance the performance of deep neural networks (DNNs) when trained on limited data. Unlike traditional methods that require numerous similar tasks for effective meta-training, MTL allows for faster convergence and reduces the risk of overfitting by leveraging weights from large-scale pre-trained models. The method incorporates two lightweight neuron operations, Scaling and Shifting, which modify the DNN weights without altering them, thus avoiding catastrophic forgetting.

The authors also present a hard task (HT) meta-batch strategy that optimizes the arrangement of training data to facilitate a challenging yet effective learning environment. This strategy focuses on re-sampling tasks based on their past performance, thereby pushing the model to improve through more difficult examples.

The contributions of this research are threefold: the introduction of the MTL method that capitalizes on pre-trained DNN weights for few-shot learning, the development of the HT meta-batch learning strategy, and extensive experimentation on benchmarks such as miniImageNet and Fewshot-CIFAR100, where MTL achieved state-of-the-art results. The work builds upon existing literature in few-shot learning, particularly in the supervised meta-learning paradigm, while also addressing the challenges of transfer learning.
