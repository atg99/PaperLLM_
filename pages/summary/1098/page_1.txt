This paper presents a novel approach to few-shot learning called meta-transfer learning (MTL), which enhances the adaptability of deep neural networks (DNNs) for tasks with limited labeled samples. The authors argue that traditional meta-learning methods often rely on shallow neural networks (SNNs) due to the overfitting tendencies of DNNs when trained on few examples. MTL aims to overcome this limitation by training DNNs to adapt to multiple tasks through learned scaling and shifting functions of the network weights.

The paper introduces a hard task (HT) meta-batch scheme, which serves as an effective learning curriculum, allowing the model to focus on more challenging tasks during training. The authors conduct experiments on two benchmarks, miniImageNet and Fewshot-CIFAR100, using (5-class, 1-shot) and (5-class, 5-shot) recognition tasks. The results demonstrate that MTL, combined with the HT meta-batch strategy, achieves superior performance compared to existing methods. An ablation study further confirms that both the MTL framework and the HT meta-batch contribute to improved convergence and accuracy.

Overall, the proposed method provides a promising direction for enhancing few-shot learning capabilities in deep learning systems, addressing the challenges posed by limited data availability.
