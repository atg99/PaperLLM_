In this section, the paper discusses the training of Deep Neural Networks (DNNs) on large-scale data, specifically focusing on few-shot learning. The initial phase involves pre-training on datasets like miniImageNet, where all-class data is merged to create a 64-class classifier. The feature extractor, denoted as Θ, is randomly initialized and optimized using gradient descent to minimize empirical loss, typically cross-entropy. The learned feature extractor is then frozen for subsequent meta-training and meta-testing phases, while the classifier is discarded due to varying classification tasks in few-shot learning scenarios.

The paper introduces a novel method called meta-transfer learning (MTL), which involves scaling and shifting (SS) operations during the meta-batch training process. Unlike traditional fine-tuning (FT) methods that update all parameters of the model, SS operations maintain the weights of the frozen feature extractor Θ and only optimize the classifier parameters. This approach mitigates overfitting and prevents catastrophic forgetting by keeping large-scale trained parameters intact.

For a specific task, the loss is computed and used to update the classifier through gradient descent, while the scaling and shifting parameters are optimized separately based on the test loss. The SS operations apply learned scalar parameters to modify the weights and biases of the frozen neurons, enhancing the model's adaptability to new tasks without modifying the underlying feature extractor.

The paper emphasizes the efficiency of the SS operations in reducing the number of learnable parameters compared to FT, thus addressing potential overfitting issues that can arise in few-shot learning contexts. Overall, the proposed MTL approach aims to improve the performance of few-shot learning by leveraging pre-trained models while minimizing the risks associated with parameter updates.
