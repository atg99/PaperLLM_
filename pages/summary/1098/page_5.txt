The paper discusses the benefits of using a method referred to as SS in the context of multi-task learning (MTL). SS provides three main advantages: it leverages a strong initialization from a pre-trained deep neural network (DNN) for faster convergence, it preserves DNN weights to mitigate catastrophic forgetting during task learning, and it is lightweight, reducing the risk of overfitting in few-shot learning scenarios.

The paper also introduces a novel approach for scheduling hard tasks in meta-training batches. Unlike traditional methods that randomly sample tasks, this method focuses on intentionally selecting failure cases from each task to create harder tasks aimed at enhancing the meta-learner's capabilities. The process involves splitting tasks into training and test sets, optimizing the base-learner on the training data, and identifying the hardest classes based on recognition accuracy.

The algorithms outlined detail the training process, which consists of two main stages: large-scale DNN training and meta-transfer learning. The hard task meta-batch re-sampling and continuous training phases are integrated into the training process, ensuring that the meta-learner is consistently challenged with difficult tasks.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the effectiveness of the proposed methods in improving the performance of meta-learning systems through strategic task selection and robust training techniques.
