The paper presents a novel few-shot learning method structured in three phases: large-scale deep neural network (DNN) training, meta-transfer learning (MTL), and meta-testing. 

1. **DNN Training Phase**: The initial phase involves training a DNN on a large dataset, such as miniImageNet, where the low-level layers are fixed to serve as a feature extractor.

2. **Meta-Transfer Learning Phase**: In this phase, the method focuses on learning Scaling and Shifting (SS) parameters for the feature extractor neurons. This is achieved through a meta-learning framework that allows the model to adapt quickly to few-shot tasks. The proposed HT meta-batch strategy enhances learning efficiency.

3. **Meta-Test Phase**: This phase evaluates the performance of the trained meta-learner on unseen tasks. The meta-learner assists the base-learner in adapting to new objectives, with final evaluation based on test results from unseen data points.

The paper emphasizes the distinction of their approach from existing methods by utilizing a meta-transfer learner that updates through all tasks rather than fine-tuning for each individual task. Additionally, it discusses concepts such as curriculum learning and hard sample mining, which further refine the learning process. The methodology incorporates two stages of optimization during meta-training: base-learning and meta-learning, ensuring effective adaptation and performance evaluation during meta-testing.
