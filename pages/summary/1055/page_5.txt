The paper by Gohel et al. discusses the current status and future directions of Explainable AI (XAI), focusing on the classification of XAI methods based on the type of data. The authors categorize XAI approaches into two main types: posthoc methods and transparent methods.

Posthoc methods are employed to interpret complex models that exhibit nonlinear relationships or high data complexity. These methods analyze the model's output after it has been trained, providing insights into the model's decision-making process. They generate understandable representations, such as feature importance scores and visualizations, which help users identify key features, quantify their importance, and detect potential biases. Notably, Local Interpretable Model-agnostic Explanations (LIME) is highlighted as a posthoc method that perturbs input samples to assess changes in output, thereby approximating the model's behavior in a localized context. Posthoc methods can be further divided into model-agnostic techniques, applicable to any AI/ML model, and model-specific techniques, which are tailored to particular learning algorithms.

In contrast, transparent methods include simpler models like logistic regression and support vector machines, which provide a clear justification of their predictions via local feature weights. These models exhibit three key properties: algorithmic transparency, decomposability, and simulatability. Algorithmic transparency ensures that users can understand the decision-making process, while decomposability allows for the explanation of each model component. Simulatability emphasizes the ability of humans to simulate the model's behavior, with simpler models being easier to interpret than more complex ones.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of interpretability in AI, outlining various XAI techniques that facilitate understanding and trust in machine learning models.
