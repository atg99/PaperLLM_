The paper by Gohel et al. discusses the current status and future directions of Explainable AI (XAI). It highlights various techniques for XAI, including LIME, SHAP, and taxonomy-based inductions, which enhance interpretability in AI models. The authors emphasize the growing use of voice assistants like Alexa and Siri, noting that the integration of visual agents in XAI systems fosters greater user trust compared to text or voice-only interactions. A user study demonstrated that users interacting with visual agents showed higher trust levels when visualizing neural network-based speech recognition models.

The paper also delves into the application of XAI with multimodal data, such as audio, video, and sensor data in industrial settings. It discusses the significance of XAI in predictive maintenance, particularly in aviation, where it can improve reliability and reduce costs by providing transparent failure diagnoses and remaining useful life predictions.

The conclusion stresses the necessity of XAI within AI/ML applications, outlining its objectives of transparency, fairness, and bias mitigation. The authors categorize XAI methodologies into post hoc and transparent approaches while advocating for further research into interpretable AI/ML methods. The paper positions XAI as a foundational aspect of responsible AI, promoting trust, confidence, and transparency in AI systems.
