The paper "Explainable AI: current status and future directions" by Gohel et al. discusses the growing importance of interpretability in artificial intelligence, particularly within natural language processing (NLP). The authors highlight the need for AI systems to not only make decisions but also to explain those decisions in a comprehensible manner to users. They review existing explanation methods, emphasizing the role of human-centered evaluations in determining the effectiveness of these methods.

The paper identifies key challenges in creating explainable AI, including the complexity of neural networks and the varying needs of users for explanations. It suggests that future work should focus on developing standardized evaluation metrics for explanations and integrating user feedback into the design of explanation methods. The authors also point out the necessity of interdisciplinary collaboration to enhance the interpretability of AI systems across different domains.

Through a comprehensive literature review, the paper situates the discussion of explainable AI within the broader context of machine learning and its applications, underscoring the ethical implications of deploying AI systems without sufficient transparency. The authors conclude by calling for a concerted effort to advance research in explainable AI to ensure that these systems can be trusted and effectively used in real-world applications.
