Gohel et al. explore the current status and future directions of Explainable AI (XAI), particularly in human-critical domains such as crime and healthcare. They introduce the Search for Evidence Counterfactual for Image Classification (SEDC), highlighting how minimal critical portions of an image, like the body of a warplane, can influence classification decisions. The concept of pertinent positives (PP) and pertinent negatives (PN) is explained, where PP is essential for justifying model decisions, and PN denotes factors whose absence is necessary for conclusions.

The paper also discusses advanced methods like SEDC-T, which focuses on reaching a predefined target class by removing image segments. This method provides deeper insights into why an image may not be classified correctly.

In the healthcare sector, the integration of XAI with AI-based diagnosis systems is emphasized, as trust in AI conclusions is crucial. The authors describe if-else diagnosis models as inherently explainable and suitable for symptomatic disease diagnosis, providing clear reasoning based on feature values.

Model-agnostic XAI methods like LIME (Local Interpretable Model-Agnostic Explanation) and SHAPLEY are introduced, which quantify the contributions of various factors to predictions. The paper differentiates between attribution-based and perturbation-based explanation methods for medical imaging. Attribution methods, such as LIME and DeepTaylor, focus on determining the weights of features, while perturbation methods analyze how changes in input features affect predictions.

Overall, the authors underscore the importance of explainability in AI applications, especially in sensitive areas like healthcare, where understanding model decisions can significantly impact patient outcomes.
