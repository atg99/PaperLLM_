The paper by Gohel et al. explores the current status and future directions of Explainable AI (XAI) by analyzing various approaches to generating explanations for machine learning models. The authors provide a comparative analysis of several techniques, highlighting their advantages and drawbacks.

1. **LIME (Local Interpretable Model-Agnostic Explanations)**: This approach is plug-and-play but suffers from instability in explanations and does not account for feature dependence. Future directions suggest exploring non-redundant instance-based justifications to improve local fidelity.

2. **SHAP (Shapley Additive Explanations)**: Optimized for speed, SHAP utilizes coalitional game theory to assign importance values to features. However, small perturbations can lead to varying explanations. The method can define the contribution of each feature, but it shares limitations with LIME regarding explanation size and feature dependence.

3. **LRP (Layer-wise Relevance Propagation)**: This method is suitable for neural networks but provides low-level abstract explanations. Improving class discriminativity through layer-wise attribution is recommended.

4. **Heatmaps**: While useful for feature importance visualization, they can be less interpretable for humans due to the individual pixel's lack of meaningfulness. Future work is needed for text-based explanations.

5. **SEDC and SEDC-T**: These approaches focus on human-centric explanations and counterfactual analysis for text classification, providing more than one irreducible explanation.

6. **Feature Importance**: This method offers explanations based on feature weights but can mislead users by focusing only on local features, neglecting global dependencies.

7. **Induction Techniques**: These approaches are convenient for programming but assume users can understand complex representations, necessitating generalization for diverse data.

8. **Provenance**: This technique provides natural language explanations that are accessible to lay users but may lack validation reliability.

The paper emphasizes the importance of counterfactual visual explanations, which offer contrastive reasoning for model predictions. By presenting explanations based on alternative outcomes, this method aligns with regulatory needs and enhances interpretability. The authors conclude that while various XAI methods exist, there is a need for further development to address current limitations and improve user understanding.
