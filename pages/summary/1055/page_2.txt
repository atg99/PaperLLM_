The paper by Gohel et al. discusses the current status and future directions of Explainable AI (XAI), emphasizing its significance in addressing the "wh" questions that traditional AI often overlooks. XAI is particularly crucial in critical fields such as healthcare, defense, and law enforcement, where understanding the rationale behind AI-generated answers is as vital as the answers themselves. This growing interest in XAI has made it a priority in both academia and industry, with the authors noting that while some progress has been made, further research is necessary to fully harness its potential.

The paper surveys existing XAI research from a multimedia perspective, recognizing that different media types—text, images, audio, and video—require distinct XAI methods. The authors categorize various XAI approaches for each media type, highlighting their advantages and disadvantages, and offering insights for future research, particularly in multi-modal applications.

The structure of the paper is organized as follows: it begins with a discussion of traditional blackbox AI models and their lack of transparency, which hinders public trust. The authors argue for the necessity of explainability in AI decision-making, especially as models become more complex with advancements in neural networks and deep learning.

Two case studies are presented to illustrate the importance of XAI. The first case involves predictive maintenance in an oil refinery, where explainable models can help identify causes of equipment failure, enhancing operational efficiency and safety. The second case focuses on video threat detection, where explainability can mitigate biases in AI models that may incorrectly flag innocent individuals as threats.

Overall, the paper underscores the need for transparent AI solutions that can provide clear explanations for their decisions, ultimately fostering trust and improving outcomes in various applications.
