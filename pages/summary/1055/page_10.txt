The paper by Gohel et al. discusses the current status of Explainable AI (XAI) and its future directions, particularly in healthcare applications. It highlights the use of occlusion techniques for feature extraction in image classification, specifically noting the computational expense involved and the trade-offs between tile size and accuracy.

In the context of healthcare, the paper emphasizes the effectiveness of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN) in classifying brain imaging, particularly for Alzheimer’s disease, while acknowledging the potential for overfitting in feature-based classifications. It suggests that post hoc analysis can help refine hyperparameters for improved accuracy, with methods like Guided Backpropagation, Layer-wise Relevance Propagation, and DeepShap proving beneficial for brain image classification.

During the COVID-19 pandemic, the integration of AI with explainability became crucial for diagnosing the disease using chest CT scans. The paper outlines key processes, including extracting lung information, classifying scans as COVID-positive or negative, localizing symptoms, and providing justifications for diagnoses. A detailed architecture for a COVID-19 detection model is presented, which utilizes convolutional LSTM layers to analyze sequences of lung CT scans, leading to the generation of segmented outputs for lobes and non-lung areas.

The discussion extends to Natural Language Processing (NLP), noting its inherent explainability and applications such as sentiment analysis and hate speech detection, suggesting a broader scope for XAI across various domains.
