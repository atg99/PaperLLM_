The paper by F. Isensee and Klaus H. Maier-Hein discusses the implementation of a pre-activation residual 3D U-Net architecture for image segmentation tasks. The architecture is built upon the framework of nnU-Net and employs pre-activation residual blocks consisting of instances of normalization, ReLU activation, and convolutional layers.

For network training, the authors utilized stochastic gradient descent with a batch size of two, opting for a patch size of 80 x 160 x 160 to balance contextual and fine-grained image information. Each training epoch consists of 250 batches, with the overall training spanning 1000 epochs. The loss function combines cross-entropy and dice loss, and multiple resolutions are employed for supervision to enhance gradient flow within the network. Training demands significant resources, requiring 12 GB of VRAM and approximately five days on Nvidia Titan Xp GPUs, with the implementation built using PyTorch.

The authors implemented extensive data augmentation techniques, including scaling, rotation, brightness adjustments, contrast modifications, gamma adjustments, and Gaussian noise to improve the robustness of the model. 

The dataset underwent modifications due to potential mislabeling in certain cases. Specifically, cases with IDs 23, 68, 125, and 133 were excluded based on disagreements between the network outputs and reference annotations. Additionally, cases 15 and 37 were confirmed faulty by challenge organizers, leading to the replacement of their annotations with segmentations generated by prior network iterations. These modifications were in accordance with the challenge organizers' guidelines.
