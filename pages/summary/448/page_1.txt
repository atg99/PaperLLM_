The paper discusses an attempt to enhance the performance of the 3D U-Net architecture, a widely used model for medical image segmentation, specifically in the context of the 2019 Kidney and Kidney Tumor Segmentation Challenge (KiTS). The authors, Fabian Isensee and Klaus H. Maier-Hein, augment the standard 3D U-Net with residual and pre-activation residual blocks to improve segmentation results. Despite these modifications, cross-validation indicates only minor improvements in performance.

For the final evaluation, the modified residual 3D U-Net was selected as it achieved a Composite Dice score of 91.23 on the test set, which allowed it to outperform all 105 competing teams and secure first place in the KiTS2019 challenge. The paper emphasizes the significance of semantic segmentation in improving treatment outcomes for kidney tumors, which are a prevalent health issue. The availability of annotated CT scans through the KiTS challenge addresses a gap in kidney tumor segmentation research, as previous datasets lacked specific tumor labels.

The authors note that while many effective segmentation algorithms utilize variations of the U-Net architecture, some recent studies have demonstrated that a standard U-Net can also yield commendable results, suggesting that further architectural innovations may not be strictly necessary in the medical imaging field.
