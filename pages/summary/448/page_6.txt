In the paper, Isensee and Maier-Hein present the results of their segmentation method evaluated on the KiTS 2019 challenge, where they achieved a Composite Dice score of 91.23, surpassing 105 competing teams and securing first place by a narrow margin. The study compared the performance of a simple U-Net architecture with residual U-Net architectures, finding no clear winner. Despite experimenting with model ensembling, the best single model, a residual 3D U-Net, was chosen for test predictions due to slightly higher scores.

The authors highlight that architectural modifications did not significantly enhance segmentation results, contrary to common expectations. They suggest that this lack of improvement may stem from potential issues such as suboptimal implementation of residual networks or poor hyperparameter choices. They advocate for more comprehensive studies with rigorous hyperparameter optimization to draw definitive conclusions.

Additionally, the paper notes an implementation error in the 3D U-Net that used leaky ReLU nonlinearities instead of ReLUs, although they do not expect this to affect the overall results. The authors emphasize their approach of comparing architectures based on practical constraints, specifically the limitations of GPU memory rather than merely the number of parameters or layers, which they consider a more realistic consideration for challenges.
