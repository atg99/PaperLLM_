The three architectures include a standard 3D U-Net, a residual 3D U-Net which incorporates residual connections to facilitate better gradient flow, and a pre-activation residual 3D U-Net that enhances learning by applying batch normalization before the activation function.

The authors conducted their experiments using the KiTS 2019 challenge dataset, which necessitated careful preprocessing of the CT images. They standardized the voxel spacing to 3.22 × 1.62 × 1.62 mm, ensuring a consistent image size of 128 × 248 × 248 voxels for training. This resampling was crucial for balancing the amount of contextual information captured by the CNNs against the detail required for accurately distinguishing between different tissue types, such as cysts and tumors.

Furthermore, the preprocessing included intensity normalization by clipping each case to the range [-79, 304], followed by a transformation to optimize the input for CNNs. This approach aimed to ensure that the intensity values of the same organ across different scans remained consistent, thus enhancing the network's ability to learn effectively.

The authors emphasize that their modifications and architectural variations are a response to the limitations of the original U-Net, aiming to improve performance on the KiTS dataset while addressing the challenges posed by inhomogeneous voxel spacings and the need for detailed image analysis. Overall, this paper contributes to the ongoing development of U-Net architectures by exploring innovative approaches to enhance their efficacy in medical image segmentation tasks.
