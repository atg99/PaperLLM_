In this paper, the authors present their approach to improve upon the 3D U-Net architecture for kidney and tumor segmentation in medical imaging, specifically for the KiTS 2019 challenge. The evaluation metrics used are Dice scores for both kidney and tumor labels, which are calculated using a five-fold cross-validation on the modified KiTS training dataset. 

The results, summarized in Table 1, show that various 3D U-Net architectures, including the standard 3D U-Net, Residual 3D U-Net, and Preactivated Residual 3D U-Net, yield similar performance metrics. The highest mean Dice score is achieved by the Residual 3D U-Net, with a kidney Dice score of 97.36 and a tumor Dice score of 85.73. Despite the expectation that ensembling models would enhance performance, the results indicate no significant improvement in composite Dice scores.

The authors highlight the challenge of selecting a model for submission due to the closely matched performance across architectures. Ultimately, they opt to use an ensemble of five Residual 3D U-Net models to predict outcomes for the 90 test cases, consolidating predictions by averaging softmax outputs. The results presented in Table 2 demonstrate that their method outperformed all competitors, securing victory in the KiTS 2019 challenge.
