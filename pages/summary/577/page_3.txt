The paper discusses the distinctions and connections between transfer learning and multi-task learning, emphasizing their roles in enhancing the generalization of machine learning tasks. Transfer learning focuses on leveraging knowledge from related domains to improve performance on a target task, while multi-task learning involves simultaneously learning related tasks, giving equal attention to each. Both approaches aim to improve learner performance through knowledge transfer and share modeling strategies such as feature transformation and parameter sharing.

The paper also introduces key notations and definitions relevant to transfer learning. A domain is defined as a combination of a feature space and a marginal distribution, while a task consists of a label space and a decision function. The concept of transfer learning is defined as utilizing knowledge from source domains to enhance decision functions for target domains, with distinctions made between single-source and multi-source transfer learning scenarios. The definitions are intended to provide clarity for subsequent discussions in the survey.
