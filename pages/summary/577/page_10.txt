Feature clustering is a technique aimed at obtaining a more abstract representation of original features, differing from traditional mapping-based feature extraction. It leverages co-clustering methods that simultaneously cluster both features and instances, as illustrated in the Co-Clustering Based Classification (CoCC) algorithm proposed by Dai et al. This algorithm is designed for document classification, where it utilizes co-clustering to transfer knowledge from labeled source documents to target-domain documents. The process involves iteratively minimizing mutual information loss through document and word clustering steps, ultimately leading to classification results.

Dai et al. also introduced Self-Taught Clustering (STC), which operates similarly to CoCC but does not require labeled data. STC assumes that source and target domains share common feature clusters, and it performs independent co-clustering tasks for each domain to identify these shared clusters.

In contrast to co-clustering methods, some approaches, such as Latent Semantic Analysis (LSA), Probabilistic LSA (PLSA), and Dual-PLSA, focus on extracting high-level concepts or topics from the original features. LSA employs Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) to reduce dimensionality and uncover the underlying meanings of words. PLSA builds on this by introducing latent class variables to model the relationship between documents and words, using the Expectation-Maximization (EM) algorithm for parameter estimation. Dual-PLSA extends PLSA by incorporating two latent variables to represent concepts for both documents and words.

Furthermore, Xue et al. proposed Topic-Bridged Probabilistic Latent Semantic Analysis (TPLSA), which integrates source and target domain PLSA models by assuming shared concepts. TPLSA incorporates concept constraints to utilize label information effectively, optimizing the objective function iteratively. Zhuang et al. further expanded this work with Collaborative Dual-PLSA (CD-PLSA) for multi-domain text classification, enhancing the adaptability of concept-based transfer learning approaches.
