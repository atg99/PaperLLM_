The paper discusses various methodologies for estimating instance weights in transfer learning, particularly focusing on adapting source-domain instances to improve target-domain learning. One prominent method is the Kullback-Leibler Importance Estimation Procedure (KLIEP), which minimizes KL divergence while selecting models. Building on such weight estimation techniques, the 2-Stage Weighting Framework for Multi-Source Domain Adaptation (2SW-MDA) is introduced, comprising instance weighting to reduce marginal distribution differences and domain weighting to address conditional distribution differences.

Another significant framework is TrAdaBoost, an adaptation of the AdaBoost algorithm for transfer learning. It updates weights of instances based on classification error rates, using a combination of labeled source and target-domain instances in each iteration to form a weak classifier. The final classifier emerges from the ensemble of these weak classifiers, enhancing performance by focusing on misclassified instances.

The paper also highlights extensions like Multi-Source TrAdaBoost (MsTrAdaBoost) which further refines the candidate classifier construction and instance weighting processes. Additionally, a general weighting framework proposed by Jiang and Zhai emphasizes minimizing cross-entropy loss across labeled and unlabeled instances, employing a heuristic method for weighting based on an auxiliary classifier.

The feature transformation strategy is noted as a common approach in feature-based methods, particularly relevant for cross-domain text classification tasks, although specific details on its implementation are not provided in the excerpt. Overall, the paper presents a comprehensive overview of instance weighting strategies and their applications in enhancing transfer learning effectiveness.
