The document outlines a course on Machine Learning, primarily based on Ethem Alpaydin's "Introduction to Machine Learning" and supplemented by Christopher M. Bishop's "Pattern Recognition and Machine Learning." The course covers foundational topics such as statistics, linear algebra, and optimization before delving into various machine learning techniques.

Key sections of the course include:

1. **Supervised Techniques**: 
   - Basic classification methods like decision trees and nearest neighbor, with applications in tumor type prediction.
   - Advanced classification using neural networks, SVMs, and ensemble methods, applied in cognitive state detection and joint movement detection with Microsoft Kinect.
   - Basic and probabilistic regression techniques, including logistic regression and Bayesian networks, with applications in vital sign monitoring.

2. **Feature Engineering**: 
   - Feature selection methods such as filter-based, embedded, and wrapper approaches, applied in bioinformatics and fMRI data analysis.

3. **Unsupervised Techniques**: 
   - Dimensionality reduction and cluster analysis methods, including k-Means and DBSCAN, with applications in drug discovery and gene expression analysis.
   - Frequent itemset mining and graph mining, particularly in medical imaging.

4. **Time Series Processing**: 
   - Techniques for preprocessing and motif discovery in time series data, with applications in ECG, EEG, and accelerometer data.

Attendance and grading policies are aligned with university regulations, with homework contributing 20% and quizzes 5% to the final grade. The course structure is designed to provide a comprehensive understanding of machine learning principles and applications in various domains.
