The paper presents a comparative analysis of distance calculation, modeling, and prediction times for two methods: RFSI (Random Forest Spatial Interpolation) and RFsp (Random Forest spatial prediction) in a synthetic case study. The results, averaged from 100 simulations, were obtained on a personal computer with specified hardware. The study reveals that RFSI significantly outperforms RFsp in both distance calculation and modeling times across various sample sizes, with RFSI showing consistent times regardless of the number of points. 

For prediction times, RFSI also demonstrates superior efficiency compared to RFsp and OK (Ordinary Kriging), particularly as the number of sample locations increases. The analysis highlights that the most important covariates for RFSI are the nearest observations, indicating a lesser importance of distances in this method. The feature importance was assessed using impurity, illustrating how the overall variance is reduced when utilizing specific features.

Furthermore, the paper discusses the sensitivity of RFSI to the selection of the number of nearest locations. Increasing the number of nearest locations results in smoother prediction maps, with an optimal number of locations identified for enhanced accuracy. However, it notes that with small sample sizes and limited data, the accuracy may not improve significantly. The findings suggest that while RFSI is more computationally efficient, the choice of parameters like the number of nearest locations and sample size plays a crucial role in the accuracy of predictions.
