The paper presents a comparative analysis of various models for predicting precipitation and temperature, focusing on their performance metrics such as hits, misses, RMSE, and overall accuracy. In the precipitation analysis, the models evaluated include STRK, IDW, RF, RFsp, and RFSI, with IDW and RFSI demonstrating the best overall accuracy at 91.6%. The results indicate that while STRK and RFSI maintained similar performance metrics, RF models exhibited greater spatial variation in prediction uncertainty, particularly in areas of high precipitation.

For the temperature case study, the authors optimized hyperparameters for IDW, RF, RFsp, and RFSI, identifying key covariates that influenced model performance. Seasonal fluctuations, MODIS LST images, and insolation were noted as significant factors for RF and RFsp, while RFSI relied heavily on the nearest observations alongside environmental variables. The study highlights the importance of model choice and configuration in accurately capturing precipitation and temperature patterns.
