The study presents an analysis of various models for predicting precipitation, specifically focusing on the performance of Random Forest (RF), Random Forest with spatial considerations (RFsp), and Random Forest with spatial interpolation (RFSI). A covariate importance plot indicates that the environmental covariates IMERG, TMAX, and TMIN play a role, but their overall impact is minimal.

In the accuracy assessment, RF underperformed due to its limited use of covariates and inability to leverage residual spatial autocorrelation. RFsp showed improved accuracy over RF by incorporating buffer distances but still lagged behind other methods like STRK, IDW, and RFSI, which captured spatial autocorrelation more effectively. Notably, RFSI outperformed STRK, suggesting its flexibility in modeling the relationship between environmental covariates and daily precipitation. Both IDW and RFSI without environmental covariates (RFSI 0) also demonstrated strong performance.

The results indicate that while environmental covariates have some predictive value, as evidenced by RF's R2 1:1 of 49.4%, their influence is minimal compared to spatial autocorrelation. The slight difference in performance between RFSI with and without environmental covariates (only 1%) reinforces the dominance of spatial factors in this case study. Overall, the findings highlight the importance of spatial relationships in precipitation modeling over environmental variables.
