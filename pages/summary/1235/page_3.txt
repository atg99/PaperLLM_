This paper introduces a novel approach called Random Forest Spatial Interpolation (RFSI) designed to enhance spatial interpolation techniques by incorporating geographical context through the inclusion of nearby observations and their distances as covariates. The authors note that existing methodologies, such as Geographical Random Forest and AINA, do not explicitly integrate actual observations from neighboring locations, despite this being a fundamental aspect of kriging and deterministic interpolation methods.

The objectives of the study are twofold: to present the RFSI method and to evaluate its performance against traditional deterministic interpolation techniques (Nearest Neighbour, Triangulated Surfaces, Inverse Distance Weighting), kriging, standard Random Forest, and RFsp. The performance evaluation employs both synthetic data generated from known random fields, where kriging is optimal, and real-world datasets, specifically a daily precipitation dataset for Catalonia and a mean daily temperature dataset for Croatia.

The methodology section outlines various interpolation methods utilized in the experiments. Deterministic methods aim to create a continuous surface from discrete measured points through mathematical functions. For instance, Nearest Neighbour interpolation assigns values based on the closest measured points, while Inverse Distance Weighting computes predictions as a weighted average of surrounding points.

The paper ultimately seeks to demonstrate the effectiveness of RFSI in improving interpolation accuracy, and a complete R script along with datasets for further analysis is made available via a GitHub repository.
