The paper presents a study on the Remote Forecasting of Spatial Interpolation (RFSI) with a focus on its application to precipitation data, highlighting the challenges posed by the spatial and temporal variability of precipitation. The performance of RFSI is compared against several interpolation methods including Space-Time Regression Kriging (STRK), Inverse Distance Weighting (IDW), standard Random Forest (RF), and RFsp. Other deterministic methods were excluded from consideration as they had been previously outperformed.

STRK was implemented using a multiple linear regression model to establish a trend function, followed by the interpolation of regression residuals utilizing space-time ordinary kriging. The trend model achieved an explanation of 40.9% of the daily precipitation variation, with a residual standard deviation of 5.3 mm. Notably, the study observed extreme residuals, highlighting the difficulties in modeling daily precipitation geostatistically. The paper also includes a histogram of the residuals and details a sum-metric semivariogram fitted to the residuals, which accounts for spatial, temporal, and spatio-temporal correlations.

Overall, the findings underscore the complexities involved in accurately modeling precipitation data and the effectiveness of RFSI in addressing these challenges.
