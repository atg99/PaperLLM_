The paper discusses the performance of various models for predicting precipitation, specifically focusing on the STRK model, IDW, and Random Forest (RF) models. The STRK model exhibited a negligible correlation over a few days, while the residual spatial correlation was significant, with a sill observed at approximately 100 km. Instances of negative precipitation predictions were adjusted to zero.

The semivariogram analysis for the STRK model revealed parameters such as a nugget of 0.00 mm², a sill of 0.89 mm², and a range of 218.8 km for spatial components. Temporal components showed a nugget of 1.63 mm², a sill of 4.15 mm², and a range of 2.6 days. The spatio-temporal component had a nugget of 9.51 mm², a sill of 11.30 mm², and a range of 91.7 km.

The paper also outlines the optimized hyperparameters for the IDW and RF models, indicating that the nearest observations play a crucial role in the RFSI model, with IMERG being the most significant covariate for both RF and RFsp models, followed by TMAX and TMIN. Distance from stations was deemed less important in the context of RFsp. Overall, the findings highlight the relative importance of specific covariates in precipitation modeling, emphasizing the need for careful selection of parameters in predictive models.
