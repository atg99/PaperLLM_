The study evaluates the performance of different interpolation methods—Inverse Distance Weighting (IDW), Random Forest spatial prediction (RFsp), and Random Forest spatial interpolation (RFSI)—across varying semivariogram nugget-to-sill ratios, ranges, and sample sizes. IDW outperformed both RFsp and RFSI under low nugget conditions, primarily due to its effectiveness in scenarios with strong spatial autocorrelation. However, as the nugget-to-sill ratio increases, IDW's performance declines since it disproportionately weights nearby observations, especially in cases with large semivariogram ranges where distant observations become more informative.

RFsp and RFSI better accommodate high nugget-to-sill ratios but require larger sample sizes for effective model training. Nearest Neighbor (NN) interpolation showed the weakest performance under large nugget-to-sill ratios and ranges, as it relies heavily on the nearest observation. Traditional Smoothing (TS) also performed poorly with large sample sizes but still outperformed NN in high nugget scenarios due to its ability to handle short-distance spatial variation.

RFSI demonstrated computational advantages over RFsp, particularly with large datasets, and allows for the inclusion of environmental covariates, enhancing its predictive capability. The optimal number of nearest locations (n) for RFSI was found to be dependent on spatial correlation and sample size, with recommendations suggesting tuning between 5 and 35, although a fixed value of 25 may suffice in many cases.

In real-world applications, RFSI combined with environmental covariates significantly improved prediction performance compared to methods like STRK and RFsp, which did not fully capture spatial correlations. In precipitation case studies, IDW performed comparably to RFSI and outperformed all other methods, reinforcing findings from previous research that IDW can match or exceed the performance of kriging methods in practical applications. Overall, the study highlights the contextual effectiveness of interpolation methods based on specific spatial characteristics and dataset attributes.
