The study evaluates various models for predicting precipitation, focusing on the performance of Inverse Distance Weighting (IDW), Random Forest (RF), and other statistical methods. The findings indicate that environmental covariates contribute little additional value after accounting for spatial variation, evidenced by the high accuracy of IDW interpolation. The results are contingent on sampling density, suggesting variability in different contexts. 

Scatter density plots reveal that RF and RFsp exhibit greater dispersion compared to STRK and RFSI, correlating with higher mean absolute error (MAE) and root mean square error (RMSE). IDW performed comparably to STRK and RFSI, particularly in modeling zero precipitation, defined as less than 1 mm. Overall, IDW and RFSI achieved the highest accuracy, effectively modeling both zero and non-zero precipitation values.

Predictions for specific days highlighted that RF models over-predicted extreme precipitation, while RFSI, STRK, and IDW provided more accurate estimates. Notably, STRK predicted negative precipitation in a significant percentage of cases, which were subsequently adjusted to zero. The study also points out that IMERG's low spatial resolution affects prediction maps, particularly in RF models, emphasizing the reliance on IMERG as a key feature.
