The paper presents a comprehensive overview of various methodologies and statistical techniques used in spatial interpolation and analysis of environmental data. It includes a list of abbreviations relevant to the study, such as Out-Of-Bag (OOB), Global Historical Climatological Network daily (GHCN-daily), and Quantile Regression Forest (QRF), among others.

The references cited span foundational works in geostatistics and environmental science, discussing topics such as precipitation averages, geostatistical principles, and the application of machine learning in spatial data modeling. Key contributions from authors like Thiessen, Willmott, and Matheron establish the theoretical underpinnings of spatial analysis, while later studies explore practical applications and advancements in the field.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of robust statistical frameworks in understanding and predicting spatial-temporal variables, highlighting the role of new methodologies like random forests in enhancing predictive modeling capabilities in environmental sciences.
