The paper discusses the implementation of the RFSI algorithm for simulating precipitation data across a 500x500 pixel grid, totaling 250,000 pixels. The simulations utilized a spherical semivariogram approach with varying parameters, including a mean of 20, a sill of 10 units, and different nugget-to-sill ratios. To ensure robust results, 100 simulations were conducted for each semivariogram combination, which helped mitigate the influence of incidental characteristics from individual realizations.

The study focuses on Catalonia, Spain, selected for its comprehensive meteorological station network and the availability of quality-checked precipitation data from the Global Historical Climatological Network (GHCN-daily). The dataset encompasses observations from 87 stations over three years (2016-2018), with a small percentage of data points removed due to quality assurance failures. A significant portion of the observations was zero, with the highest daily precipitation recorded at 220.9 mm.

For the kriging and random forest models used in the precipitation analysis, three environmental covariates were incorporated. Additionally, the study utilized IMERG data, which combines satellite and gauge information to estimate daily precipitation. The late run version of IMERG was specifically applied for this analysis, highlighting the integration of remote sensing data in precipitation modeling.
