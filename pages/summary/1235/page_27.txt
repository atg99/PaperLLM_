The paper discusses various machine learning approaches for spatial data interpolation and modeling, particularly focusing on environmental variables like air temperature and soil properties. It highlights the effectiveness of algorithms such as Random Forests and Support Vector Machines in improving predictions compared to traditional geostatistical methods. Key studies referenced include the evaluation of machine learning techniques for air temperature interpolation at Mt. Kilimanjaro, the mapping of soil properties in Africa, and the high-resolution mapping of daily climate variables in the United States. The authors emphasize the advancements in machine learning that allow for better handling of spatial heterogeneity and uncertainty in environmental data. The references provided cover a range of methodologies, applications, and theoretical frameworks relevant to spatial statistics and machine learning in environmental science.
