This paper discusses the development and evaluation of a novel spatial interpolation technique called Random Forest Spatial Interpolation (RFSI). Traditionally, methods like kriging and deterministic techniques such as inverse distance weighting have dominated spatial interpolation. While kriging and regression kriging utilize spatial autocorrelation and covariate information effectively, recent advancements in machine learning have introduced techniques like random forest and gradient boosting into this domain.

The authors highlight that previous machine learning approaches have not adequately incorporated spatial information, particularly the nearest observations and their distances to prediction locations. To address this gap, RFSI was proposed, integrating these spatial components into the random forest framework.

The performance of RFSI was compared against several interpolation methods, including ordinary kriging, regression kriging, and other random forest techniques, through three case studies. The first involved synthetic data based on normally distributed stationary random fields where ordinary kriging was expected to excel. The second and third studies focused on daily precipitation data from Catalonia, Spain, and daily temperature data from Croatia.

Results indicated that RFSI outperformed many deterministic interpolation methods and showed comparable performance to inverse distance weighting and RFsp in synthetic data. In real-world applications, RFSI generally outperformed regression kriging, inverse distance weighting, and standard random forest methods, while also demonstrating significantly faster computation, especially with large training datasets and high-resolution predictions.

The study emphasizes the importance of spatial interpolation in various scientific fields and positions RFSI as a robust alternative to traditional methods, combining the strengths of machine learning with spatial data considerations.
