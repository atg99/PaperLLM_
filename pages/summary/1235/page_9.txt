The paper discusses the application of cross-validation strategies in the assessment of accuracy for precipitation and temperature case studies. A target-oriented cross-validation approach, specifically nested leave-location-out cross-validation (LLOCV), was employed to ensure that entire stations were kept in the same fold, enhancing the robustness of model evaluation. The study utilized a nested 5-fold LLOCV for precipitation and a nested 10-fold LLOCV for temperature, allowing for the tuning of hyperparameters without involving the test data in the calibration process.

For Random Forest (RF) models, hyperparameters such as the number of variables to split at each node, minimal node size, and the ratio of observations to sample were optimized. Additionally, the nearest stations' number was tuned for RF Spatial Interpolation (RFSI) models. The results indicated that Ordinary Kriging (OK) outperformed other methods in all scenarios, with Inverse Distance Weighting (IDW), RFsp, and RFSI following closely behind. IDW performed best in low nugget scenarios, while RFsp and RFSI were superior in cases with higher nugget-to-sill ratios.

The paper also highlights that as the number of sample locations increased, the performance gap between RF models and OK diminished. Conversely, Nearest Neighbor (NN) and Trend Surface (TS) methods demonstrated poorer performance due to their reliance on limited data, particularly NN's focus on only the nearest observation, which proved inadequate in high-nugget situations. Overall, the findings emphasize the importance of method selection based on spatial data characteristics and sample size.
