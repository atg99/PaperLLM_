This paper presents Random Forest Spatial Interpolation (RFSI) as an effective method for predicting values at unknown locations using a combination of nearest observations. RFSI distinguishes itself from traditional methods like kriging by not being constrained to linear combinations and lacking stringent stationarity assumptions, allowing it to model non-linear relationships and complex variables with zero-inflated or skewed distributions. The method also allows for the assessment of the importance of nearest observations, which is a limitation in existing Random Forest approaches for spatial interpolation.

While RFSI shows promise, the authors acknowledge areas for improvement, particularly in integrating distance metrics more directly and incorporating temporal elements into the model. The paper includes supplementary materials such as figures illustrating temperature and precipitation estimates, accuracy metrics for interpolation methods, and a histogram of residuals.
