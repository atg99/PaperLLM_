In the study of spatial interpolation methods, the RFSI model demonstrated superior performance compared to IDW and STRK in temperature predictions, primarily due to the greater relevance of environmental covariates in this context. In the precipitation case, however, IDW outperformed STRK, and RFSI showed only marginal improvement over RFsp, indicating that in some scenarios, the nearest observations alone can sufficiently explain variability. The analysis highlighted that while environmental covariates contribute valuable information, their impact diminishes when neighboring observations are available. The R2 value for RF was 49.4%, indicating that these covariates do hold significance, but their importance was less pronounced in the precipitation case study.

RFSI effectively balances the use of neighboring observations and environmental covariates, adapting to which source provides more valuable information based on training data. The study also noted that spatial interpolation methods often smooth data, with RFSI exhibiting less smoothing compared to other methods in temperature predictions. RFSI's advantages over STRK and RFsp include its closer alignment with spatial interpolation principles, ease of fitting without requiring semivariogram modeling, and greater interpretative power regarding the importance of observations and covariates. However, measuring feature importance remains challenging in cases of cross-correlation among covariates.
