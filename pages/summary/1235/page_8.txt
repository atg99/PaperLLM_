The paper discusses the accuracy assessment methods employed in three case studies, focusing on various metrics including the coefficient of determination (R2 1:1), Lin’s concordance correlation coefficient (CCC), mean absolute error (MAE), and root mean square error (RMSE). R2 1:1 is specifically defined to avoid confusion with the Pearson correlation, calculated as the proportion of the total sum of squares (TSS) that is not explained by the error sum of squares (ESS).

In the synthetic case study, 600 simulated datasets were analyzed, with each dataset split into a sample and test dataset. This approach allowed for true accuracy metrics to be calculated since the actual values were known. Predictions were generated using kriging and deterministic interpolation methods, utilizing model parameters from the simulations to mitigate estimation errors. Accuracy metrics were averaged across all 100 realizations to ensure robustness against individual dataset characteristics.

For Random Forest (RF) models, sample datasets served as training data, with varying sizes (100 to 5000 locations) used to assess the sensitivity of accuracy metrics to sample size. The RF models were implemented using the R package ranger, with spatial covariates derived from the nearest locations using the knn function. Hyperparameters were kept at default values due to computational constraints, with 250 trees used for modeling. The paper also details how predictions were made using inverse distance weighting (IDW) and nearest neighbor (NN) methods, alongside kriging and time series (TS) predictions, utilizing functions from various R packages.
