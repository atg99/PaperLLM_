The paper discusses the challenges and advancements in deep residual learning for tasks such as face detection, segmentation, and object classification. The authors highlight that while deep residual learning offers advantages over traditional methods, particularly in computational efficiency and accuracy, it struggles with larger datasets due to scalability issues. They suggest clustering input data into smaller subsets as a potential solution to improve processing efficiency.

Feng et al. emphasize the requirement of large amounts of unlabeled data for effective training, noting their experiments with small labeled datasets yielded unsatisfactory results. They propose incorporating a few labels, possibly requiring human intervention, or adding a fully supervised component, alongside the creation of datasets with predefined metadata for enhanced supervision.

Another study cited concludes that deep residual learning is promising in image recognition, being computationally efficient and suitable for sparse data representations, without reliance on complex handcrafted features. The authors advocate for future research directions, including the integration of deep residual learning with neural architecture search, spatial domain convolutions, constrained adversarial loss functions, and Gaussian-based generative models. They also address the need to mitigate the negative effects of data noise in deep residual learning.

Mindy Yang et al. further propose the creation of datasets with predefined metadata to assist in supervision, highlighting the computational expense of deep residual learning, particularly its sensitivity to high dimensional data. They suggest combining deep residual learning with artificial intelligence techniques like reinforcement learning to advance the field of machine learning.
