Deep Residual Learning for Image Recognition explores two key applications: image steganalysis and image compression. 

In image steganalysis, a technique vital for protecting copyrighted material and identifying hidden inappropriate content, deep residual learning allows for the detection of data concealed within images. This method relies on deep residual networks to learn local patch features, organized into three stages: pre-processing, feature extraction, and classification. Challenges in steganalysis include managing false positives, extracting robust features to differentiate noise from data, and developing effective models with limited training data. Active learning approaches are suggested to improve feature extraction, though they may require significant human resources. The high false positive rate has discouraged some search engines from scanning for steganographic content, underscoring the need for more effective detection methods.

In image compression, research conducted by Christian Rathgeb et al. highlights that deep learning models can maintain accuracy while reducing training dataset sizes by up to 90% through effective feature learning. This reduction not only accelerates training times but also minimizes memory requirements for storing training data. The study examines various image compression techniques suitable for convolutional neural networks, emphasizing the efficiency gains achieved without compromising performance. 

Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of deep residual learning in enhancing image analysis capabilities, addressing both security concerns and resource efficiency.
