The paper discusses the performance evaluation of the Deep Residual Network (DRN) on various image recognition tasks, highlighting its superiority over previous state-of-the-art methods. DRN employs a new evaluation metric called Top-1 error, which the authors argue is a more reliable measure of performance compared to traditional accuracy metrics. They report that DRN achieved a top-1 error of 3.56% on the ImageNet dataset, significantly outperforming the previous method's error of 4.82%. Moreover, DRN also excelled in Top-5 error and Mean Average Precision, with results of 9.8% and 0.927, respectively, compared to 17.2% and 0.648 for the prior method.

The paper emphasizes the importance of using metrics like top-1 and top-5 errors, particularly in scenarios where false negatives are more critical than false positives. This allows for a clearer comparison between models with similar accuracy rates. The authors illustrate this with an example involving two models with identical accuracy but differing error profiles. They conclude that DRN's design, which incorporates deep residual structures and local information preservation, enhances its learning capability from extensive datasets, thereby reducing overfitting and improving accuracy across various tasks.

The findings indicate that while DRN benefits from large data training, it can still achieve competitive performance with smaller datasets, depending on the specific task requirements. The paper also notes the trade-offs involved in adjusting error rates, highlighting that different applications may prioritize different metrics based on their operational needs.
