Deep residual networks (ResNets) were introduced in 2015 as a solution to the challenges faced by traditional convolutional neural networks (CNNs) in image recognition tasks. The key innovation of ResNets is the incorporation of skip connections, which allow the input from earlier layers to be added to the output of later layers. This approach facilitates easier learning and enhances overall performance across various applications, including image classification, object detection, and semantic segmentation.

Despite their effectiveness, ResNets are computationally intensive, requiring significant resources for training. Issues such as training stability, computational efficiency, and the impact of network depth and complexity remain open areas for research. The traditional CNNs struggle with large feature maps and a high number of parameters, making them expensive and slow. In contrast, ResNets leverage skip connections to reduce the network size while retaining comparable performance.

Recent advancements, such as the work by Aryo Michael and Melki Garonga in 2021, further explore the integration of multi-scale features and element-wise pooling within residual networks. Their approach utilizes depthwise separable convolutions and various pooling techniques to create more efficient layers, ultimately aiming to address computational costs while preserving performance.

In conclusion, deep residual learning represents a promising avenue for image recognition and related tasks, with ongoing research needed to tackle challenges associated with computational efficiency and application to diverse computer vision problems.
