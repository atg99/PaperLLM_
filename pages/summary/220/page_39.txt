The paper provides a comprehensive survey of deep residual learning (DRL) and its applications in image recognition. It begins by reviewing the principles of DRL and highlights its successful use cases, including image classification, object detection, and semantic segmentation. Despite the advancements, the paper identifies ongoing challenges that need to be addressed for DRL to tackle more complex problems effectively. 

To enhance the performance of DRL, the authors recommend two primary strategies: first, increasing the number of layers in the neural network to improve depth and complexity; second, augmenting the number of filters in each layer to refine the model's capacity to learn from data. This survey underscores the potential of DRL while acknowledging the necessity for further development to fully exploit its capabilities in various applications.
