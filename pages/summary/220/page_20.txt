Deep residual networks address the difficulty of training deep models with many hidden layers by using techniques like adversarial training, where two models compete to optimize error. While deep learning excels at modeling complex nonlinear relationships and discovering intricate structures in data, it faces issues such as overfitting due to the large number of parameters and performance limitations in high-dimensional spaces like images.

The paper emphasizes that despite these challenges, deep residual learning has shown superior performance compared to traditional methods in image recognition. It also notes the importance of additional techniques, such as variational auto-encoders and approximate inference methods, to improve performance in high-dimensional scenarios. Furthermore, the robustness of deep residual networks to missing labels is acknowledged, although the difficulty in calculating loss functions when labels are absent is recognized as a significant hurdle.

The authors aim to provide a comprehensive overview of the current state of deep residual learning, including best practices for training deep models on GPUs, recent theoretical advancements, and practical implementations. The paper situates deep residual learning within the broader context of related fields, such as supervised and unsupervised classification, denoising autoencoders, and generative adversarial networks.

In summary, deep residual learning is positioned as a promising avenue for image recognition research, with ongoing developments expected to enhance its efficacy and applicability in various domains.
