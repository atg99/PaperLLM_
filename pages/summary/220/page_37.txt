The paper discusses the challenges associated with training deep residual networks, which are widely used in image recognition, speech recognition, and natural language processing. Key issues highlighted include the need for better methods to train networks for better generalization, the handling of large-scale feature maps, and the importance of batch normalization for stability and convergence.

One major challenge is the requirement for large datasets to train individual layers effectively, which limits the application of these models to smaller datasets, particularly in tasks like object detection and semantic segmentation. The computational expense of training networks with numerous parameters complicates the use of backpropagation, and while some methods attempt to reduce parameters, this often results in decreased accuracy.

The paper also notes that deep residual networks struggle with noise due to their sensitivity to features and initializations, making them prone to overfitting without regularization. Additionally, the networks face difficulties in capturing higher-order spatial dependencies due to the disparity between image and temporal dimensions.

Other significant challenges include poor generalization ability on small-scale networks, difficulties in parameter selection, and inadequate performance on multi-class and low-level vision problems. The paper emphasizes the difficulty of incorporating prior knowledge into these models and adapting them to new tasks, all of which hinder the effectiveness of deep residual networks in various applications.
