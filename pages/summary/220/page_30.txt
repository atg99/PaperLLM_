The paper discusses Residual Networks, or ResNets, which are a type of deep learning neural network based on the concept of residual learning. The author references a study that examined the impact of incorporating short-cut connections into convolutional neural networks. This approach enables the training of much deeper networks while effectively addressing the vanishing gradient problem. 

The fundamental structure of a ResNet consists of two 3x3 convolutional layers, each followed by a batch normalization layer and a ReLU activation function, with a short-cut connection (identity mapping) placed between them. This architecture facilitates the training of very deep networks. However, a potential drawback is the increased computation time due to the additional operations required. Despite this, the paper notes that the computational cost is generally outweighed by the accuracy improvements achieved through this method.
