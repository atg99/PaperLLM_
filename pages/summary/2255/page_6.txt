The paper presents an SVD-based task re-weighting scheme aimed at improving multi-task training, particularly in the presence of label noise. The algorithm takes multiple tasks, represented as datasets (X; yi), and computes a weight vector for each task through Singular Value Decomposition (SVD). The central steps include calculating the covariance matrix for each task, performing SVD to obtain a rank-r approximation, and determining task weights based on the projections into principal components.

Additionally, the authors introduce a covariance similarity score to quantify the similarity between tasks, which is invariant to column rotations. The optimization scheme discussed emphasizes the benefits of re-weighting tasks when some contain label noise. It illustrates that by up-weighting the more reliable tasks, one can mitigate the impact of noise from less reliable tasks, leading to better overall performance.

The paper also provides a theoretical framework, establishing that the optimal solution for the re-weighted loss aligns with the column span of a specific matrix derived from the SVD. This result is shown to hold in linear settings, although the authors note that it does not extend to non-linear scenarios, which they plan to explore in future work. The proposed re-weighting method is expected to enhance robustness in multi-task learning by effectively managing label noise.
