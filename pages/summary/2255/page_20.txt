The paper presents an analysis of a multi-task learning (MTL) framework, specifically focusing on the estimation error in the context of ReLU models. The authors derive bounds for the estimation error of the optimal solution in a given objective function, which is represented in equation 10. They assume that the input for one of the tasks follows a Gaussian distribution, highlighting the need for distributional assumptions due to the NP-hard nature of optimizing ReLU functions under worst-case scenarios.

The main result, Theorem 9, establishes that under certain conditions related to the input distributions and task parameters, the estimation error can be bounded with high probability. Specifically, the error is expressed in terms of the angles between optimal and estimated parameters, as well as the variance of the noise in the model. The authors emphasize that the proof follows a similar structure to previous theorems, ensuring that the assumptions regarding the unit vectors are maintained.

Overall, the paper extends previous results in the field of MTL by providing a rigorous analysis of the ReLU model, contributing to the understanding of estimation errors in this context.
