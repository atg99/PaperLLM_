The paper discusses the evaluation of multi-task learning (MTL) and single-task learning (STL) models for sentiment analysis, focusing on model capacity and transfer learning. Key findings include the impact of data size and covariance alignment on transfer rates, with results indicating that MTL can lead to negative transfer when source and target tasks have differing covariance matrices. The authors propose Algorithm 1 to mitigate this issue, demonstrating its effectiveness in correcting negative transfer, particularly when the source task has limited data. 

Cross-validation experiments are conducted to determine optimal model capacities for various architectures, including LSTM, CNN, and MLP. Results show that the best-performing MTL model capacities are consistently lower than the optimal capacities for STL models across all tested architectures. The study emphasizes the importance of model capacity selection and the potential of MTL to enhance performance in sentiment analysis tasks.
