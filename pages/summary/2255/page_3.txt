The paper discusses a multi-task learning (MTL) model that addresses the challenges of transferring knowledge between tasks. It presents a setup where k tasks share a common input dimension, and each task has its own output module. The objective is to minimize the overall loss across all tasks while allowing for the re-weighting of tasks based on their data sizes.

Two specific models are analyzed: a single-task linear model and a single-task ReLU model. The linear model assumes that task labels follow a linear relationship, while the ReLU model incorporates a non-linear activation function. The paper distinguishes between positive and negative transfer, defining positive transfer as the scenario where training on multiple tasks improves performance on a target task compared to training on that task alone.

The authors focus on three key components influencing transfer: model capacity, task covariances, and per-task weights. They emphasize that the output dimension of the shared module should be less than the total capacity of individual task modules to facilitate effective transfer. An example is provided, illustrating that if the shared module's capacity is equal to or greater than the number of tasks, no beneficial transfer occurs.

The analysis is primarily focused on regression tasks using squared loss, with additional synthetic experiments conducted on classification tasks to support the theoretical findings.
