The paper presents findings on the effects of task similarity in multi-task learning (MTL) compared to single-task learning (STL), particularly in regression and classification tasks. It demonstrates that higher cosine similarity between the STL models of two tasks leads to improved performance in the target task, with the gap between MTL and STL performance widening as task similarity increases. The study also identifies a negative transfer phenomenon when task similarity is too low.

In experiments with ReLU-activated models, similar trends were observed, confirming the robustness of these findings across different model settings. The synthetic tasks were designed with specific model parameters and data generation methods to assess how varying levels of task similarity impact performance. The results underscore the importance of task similarity in optimizing MTL outcomes and suggest that careful consideration of task relationships can mitigate negative transfer effects. Overall, the paper emphasizes the potential of MTL to enhance learning when tasks are sufficiently aligned.
