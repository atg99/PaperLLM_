The paper investigates the performance of multi-task learning (MTL) compared to single-task learning (STL) across various tasks and their similarities. The analysis includes both linear regression and logistic classification tasks, measuring MTL performance using Spearman correlation for regression and accuracy for classification. Results indicate that task similarity, quantified by cosine distance, impacts MTL effectiveness, with varying performance observed across different configurations.

For GLUE experiments, the authors utilize BERT LARGE while fixing its parameters to assess the impact of added alignment modules. A grid search is performed to optimize training parameters, ultimately selecting a learning rate of 2e-5 and 5 epochs.

In the ChestX-ray14 experiments, the configuration from previous research is applied, reporting AUC scores after fine-tuning for 20 epochs. Additional synthetic experiments explore the relationship between cosine similarity and model performance for both linear and ReLU settings, using generated datasets to analyze the effects of task similarity on MTL outcomes.
