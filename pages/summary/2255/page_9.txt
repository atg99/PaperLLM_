This paper discusses the advantages of multi-task learning (MTL) over single-task learning (STL) through empirical comparisons and theoretical insights. Key findings include the model capacity and accuracy metrics for various sentiment analysis tasks, demonstrating that MTL generally achieves better performance than STL on most tasks. Specifically, it highlights that constraining the shared module’s capacity is essential for optimizing performance. 

The authors introduce a covariance alignment method, revealing that aligning task covariances significantly improves accuracy across multiple task pairs. Experimental results confirm that after alignment, accuracy increases for 13 out of 15 task pairs, with a maximum improvement of 4.1%. Additionally, an optimization scheme is tested on the ChestX-ray14 dataset, showing that their method enhances performance compared to existing techniques.

The paper also reviews related work in multi-task learning, discussing theoretical frameworks that emphasize task relatedness and subspace selection. The authors reference foundational studies that have shaped the understanding of MTL and its implications for deep learning, providing a comprehensive overview of the field's evolution. Overall, the study presents a strong case for MTL by demonstrating its benefits in task performance and theoretical grounding.
