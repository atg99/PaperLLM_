This paper presents a theoretical analysis of the behavior of ReLU (Rectified Linear Unit) functions applied to truncated Gaussian random variables. It utilizes the Hoeffding bound to establish concentration results for the norms of these ReLU functions under certain conditions. The authors employ an epsilon-net argument to demonstrate that the sum of independent random variables, bounded by a logarithmic term, converges with high probability.

Key concepts include the expectation of the inner product of ReLU functions, which is linked to the cosine of the angle between the corresponding vectors. Bernstein’s inequality and the union bound are applied to derive bounds on the differences between these ReLU functions.

The paper further explores the geometric properties of unit vectors and establishes relationships between angles, leading to conclusions about the sine of the angles involved. The analysis culminates in estimating a parameter a2 related to the ReLU functions, showing that certain terms are bounded and confirming the overall results.

The findings contribute to understanding the concentration phenomena in high-dimensional spaces involving ReLU activations, which is significant for applications in machine learning and neural networks.
