The paper presents a method for covariance alignment in multi-task learning (MTL) to enhance performance on a target task by leveraging data from source tasks. The proposed algorithm involves aligning the covariance structures of multiple tasks through specific matrices and shared modules. The loss function is modified to facilitate this alignment, and the optimization is performed using gradient descent on the alignment matrices and output modules.

Data generation for the experiments involved three tasks, where two source tasks shared the same covariance structure with the target task, while the third task had a different covariance. The performance of the target task was analyzed based on the number of samples from the source tasks, revealing that effective transfer is contingent on the amount of data available from the source tasks and their covariance characteristics.

The authors provide a theoretical framework to quantify the necessary data points for positive transfer, indicating that the condition numbers of the covariance matrices play a crucial role. Specifically, they derive a theorem that outlines the relationship between the number of samples needed from the source task and the similarity of the tasks, as measured by their covariance.

Additionally, the paper extends the analysis to ReLU-activated regression tasks, addressing the complexities introduced by the ReLU function. Overall, the findings suggest that while MTL can facilitate positive transfer, the effectiveness is highly dependent on the data characteristics and alignment of task covariances.
