This paper discusses the classification performance of deep convolutional neural networks (CNNs) in screening COVID-19 from chest X-ray (CXR) images. The VGG16-TF2 model demonstrated the highest classification accuracy among various models. The Grad-CAM technique was utilized to visualize the significant regions in CXR images that differentiate normal cases, pneumonia, and COVID-19. The study highlights the challenges posed by the COVID-19 pandemic, including symptom overlap with other pneumonia types and limited medical resources, which underscore the necessity of effective screening methods.

The paper critiques prior research that achieved over 95% accuracy in classifying COVID-19 using CXR images but lacked detailed information on fine-tuning and qualitative evaluations. The authors emphasize the importance of understanding the effects of fine-tuning and layer depth on the performance of deep CNNs. They found that the VGG-16 model outperformed VGG-19, suggesting that deeper architectures do not always yield better results in medical image classification, particularly when fewer classes are involved.

The authors elaborate on the fine-tuning process, indicating that earlier convolutional layers capture generic features applicable to various classification tasks, while later layers are more specialized. By preserving early layer parameters and updating later ones during training on new datasets, the model can be effectively adapted for specific classification tasks. The findings suggest that careful consideration of model architecture and fine-tuning strategies is crucial for optimizing deep learning applications in medical imaging.
