The study evaluates the performance of various deep convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for COVID-19 screening, focusing on the impact of fine-tuning different convolutional blocks. The results indicate that the models fine-tuned with VGG16 as the backbone architecture outperformed those using VGG19, achieving an accuracy of 95.9%, specificity of 97.5%, sensitivity of 92.5%, and an AUC of 0.950. 

Fine-tuning the last two convolutional blocks yielded the best classification performance, while models with all convolutional blocks untrainable had the lowest accuracy. Notably, increasing the number of fine-tuned blocks beyond three resulted in decreased classification accuracy across all tested deep CNNs. The VGG19 models consistently showed lower classification accuracy compared to VGG16 models, regardless of the number of fine-tuned blocks used. 

The paper includes figures illustrating the relationship between the number of fine-tuned blocks and classification performance, as well as a confusion matrix and ROC curve for the best-performing model, VGG16-FT2. These findings emphasize the importance of selecting the appropriate architecture and fine-tuning strategy for effective COVID-19 classification.
