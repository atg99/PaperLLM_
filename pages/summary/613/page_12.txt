The paper discusses the effectiveness of transfer learning using deep convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for COVID-19 screening in chest X-ray (CXR) images. It highlights that increasing the degree of fine-tuning improves classification accuracy up to a certain point, beyond which performance declines. This indicates the necessity of identifying an optimal fine-tuning level as a crucial hyper-parameter affecting deep learning accuracy.

The study employs Grad-CAM, a visualization technique, to qualitatively assess classification adequacy. It reveals that while the best-performing CNN model identifies relevant image features within the lung cavity, it can misclassify images containing foreign objects, leading to false positives or negatives. The results emphasize the importance of not only achieving high classification scores but also ensuring the decisions made by the model are based on correct reasoning.

In conclusion, the authors assert that achieving high performance in COVID-19 screening with deep CNNs is not solely dependent on model complexity but also requires careful fine-tuning and validation of classification decisions through visual interpretation methods like Grad-CAM.
