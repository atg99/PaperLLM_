The paper discusses the methodology for classifying COVID-19 chest X-ray images using various datasets. The datasets, detailed in Table 1, comprise images categorized into three classes: normal, pneumonia, and COVID-19. The normal and pneumonia samples were sourced from the NIH chest X-ray dataset utilized in the RSNA pneumonia detection challenge, while COVID-19 images were obtained from multiple public datasets. To ensure balanced data for classification, images from other classes were randomly extracted based on the number of available COVID-19 images, resulting in a total of 1821 images.

The collected dataset was split into training and testing sets with an 80:20 ratio, and further divided for validation using 5-fold cross-validation. Due to variations in image contrast and dimensions from different sources, preprocessing was necessary. The study employed contrast limited adaptive histogram equalization (CLAHE) for contrast correction and resized all images to 800x800 pixels for uniformity.

For classification, the study utilized two deep convolutional neural networks (CNNs), VGG-16 and VGG-19, both pre-trained models from the Visual Geometry Group at the University of Oxford. These models were chosen for their established effectiveness in image classification, allowing for a comparative evaluation based on their differing layer depths while maintaining the same architectural framework.
