In this study, a 5-fold cross-validation approach was employed to train deep convolutional neural network (CNN) models using a dataset of 1458 images. The dataset was divided into five folds to mitigate overfitting and bias. Each fold was split into training and validation sets, with an 80 to 20% ratio. The independent validation sets were rotated among the folds to assess the training status. To further prevent overfitting, dropout was applied to the last fully connected layers, and early stopping was monitored based on validation loss.

The training was conducted on an NVIDIA DGX Station with an Ubuntu 18 operating system, utilizing the Keras library and TensorFlow backend. The initial learning rate was set to 0.00001, with the RMSprop algorithm as the solver. The ReduceLROn-Plateau method adjusted the learning rate when training performance plateaued. After training, the best model was evaluated using a test dataset.

Performance metrics including accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve, and precision recall (PR) curve were calculated. The formulas for accuracy, sensitivity, and specificity were provided, emphasizing the importance of true positive (TP), true negative (TN), false positive (FP), and false negative (FN) counts. Additionally, the area under the ROC curve (AUC) was calculated.

To interpret the model predictions, the gradient-weighted class activation mapping (Grad-CAM) technique was utilized, allowing the identification of significant regions in chest X-ray (CXR) images that contributed to the model's decision-making process regarding COVID-19 screening. The classification performance across three categories—normal, pneumonia, and COVID-19—was summarized in the results section.
