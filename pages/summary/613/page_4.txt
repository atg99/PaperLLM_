The study addresses the preprocessing and training of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for analyzing chest X-ray (CXR) images collected from multiple centers, which presented challenges due to varying contrast and dimensions. To standardize the images, contrast limited adaptive histogram equalization (CLAHE) was employed, and all images were resized to 800 × 800 pixels. The research utilized two CNN architectures, VGG-16 and VGG-19, both recognized for their efficacy in image classification tasks, including medical imaging. 

Fine-tuning was conducted to adapt pre-trained networks on a large annotated dataset to the specific task of disease classification from CXR images. The fine-tuning process involved sequentially unfreezing layers of the CNN to update their weights during training. A total of six fine-tuning steps were implemented, allowing for a comparative evaluation of the models based on layer depth. 

For model training, the dataset of 1458 images was divided into five folds for 5-fold cross-validation, ensuring independent training and validation sets to minimize overfitting and bias. An 80 to 20% split was used for each fold, and dropout techniques along with early stopping were applied to enhance model robustness. This structured approach aimed to optimize the performance of the CNNs in classifying diseases from CXR images while maintaining accuracy and efficiency.
