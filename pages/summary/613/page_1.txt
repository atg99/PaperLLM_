This study evaluates the scalability and fine-tuning of deep convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for screening COVID-19 using chest X-ray (CXR) images. The research is motivated by differences in CXR features between COVID-19 patients and those with other lung diseases. The authors utilized publicly available CXR images categorized into three groups: COVID-19, pneumonia, and normal. They compared the performance of two CNN architectures, VGG-16 and VGG-19, focusing on varying layer depths and degrees of fine-tuning.

The results indicated that a model fine-tuned with only 2 out of 5 blocks of the VGG-16 architecture achieved the highest area under the curve (AUC) value of 0.950 for COVID-19 classification. This finding suggests that deeper networks do not necessarily yield better performance when data is limited, and that optimal fine-tuning strategies are essential for developing efficient deep learning models in medical image classification.

The paper underscores the importance of early COVID-19 screening to manage the pandemic, highlighting the limitations of the gold standard RT-PCR test, which has a positive detection rate of only 60-70%. The study's conclusions advocate for tailored deep learning approaches to enhance diagnostic accuracy in the context of limited data resources.
