The paper presents a comparative analysis of various visual question answering (VQA) datasets, highlighting the unique characteristics of the OK-VQA dataset. The dataset comprises 14,055 questions and 14,031 images, focusing specifically on visual reasoning that requires external knowledge. Unlike other datasets, OK-VQA aims to reduce answer bias by filtering out questions with a high frequency of particular answers and those lacking inter-annotator agreement, resulting in a more challenging dataset.

The authors categorize the types of knowledge necessary to answer the questions, such as common-sense, geographical, scientific, and historical knowledge. They employed human annotators to classify the questions into ten distinct knowledge categories, ensuring that the labels are mutually exclusive.

The paper includes a statistical comparison of OK-VQA with other VQA datasets, demonstrating that it is one of the largest in its domain and highlights its diversity and difficulty. It emphasizes that the questions in OK-VQA often necessitate background knowledge that cannot be inferred solely from the images or questions, thus advancing the field of knowledge-based visual question answering.
