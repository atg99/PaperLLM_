The paper discusses the creation of the OK-VQA dataset, which aims to improve the evaluation of visual question answering (VQA) systems that require external knowledge. Traditional VQA datasets often consist of simple questions that do not necessitate much background knowledge, limiting their ability to assess an algorithm's reasoning skills. The authors highlight that over 78% of questions in existing datasets can be answered by individuals as young as 10 years old, indicating a lack of complexity.

To address this gap, the authors collected a new dataset using images from the COCO dataset, focusing on crafting questions that go beyond basic visual recognition. They employed MTurk workers to generate questions related to the images, with specific instructions to avoid simple inquiries about image content or counting. The initial round of labeling produced a mix of high and low-quality questions, necessitating a manual filtering process that reduced the pool from 86,700 to 34,921 questions.

The paper emphasizes the significance of creating a dataset that challenges VQA systems to utilize external knowledge, thereby enhancing their performance evaluation and contributing to advancements in the field.
