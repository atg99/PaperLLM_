The paper "EfficientNet: Rethinking Model Scaling for Convolutional Neural Networks" presents a new approach to scaling convolutional neural networks (CNNs) that achieves state-of-the-art accuracy with significantly fewer parameters. The authors demonstrate that their scaled EfficientNet models outperform existing models on five out of eight transfer learning datasets while using an average of 9.6 times fewer parameters.

The results show a detailed comparison of EfficientNet models against other prominent architectures like NASNet and Inception-v4 across various datasets, highlighting the accuracy and parameter efficiency of EfficientNet-B0 to EfficientNet-B7. For instance, EfficientNet-B0 achieves 98.1% accuracy on CIFAR-10 with only 4 million parameters, compared to NASNet-A with 85 million parameters. Furthermore, EfficientNet-B7 reaches 98.9% accuracy on CIFAR-10 while maintaining a significantly lower parameter count of 64 million.

The paper also discusses the computational efficiency of EfficientNet, noting that it requires fewer floating-point operations per second (FLOPS) than other networks with comparable accuracy. EfficientNet-B3, for example, is reported to achieve higher accuracy than ResNeXt-101 using 18 times fewer FLOPS. In terms of real-world performance, EfficientNet models demonstrate faster inference speeds, with EfficientNet-B1 running 5.7 times faster than ResNet-152.

Overall, the EfficientNet models not only advance the state of the art in accuracy for image classification tasks but also improve efficiency, making them suitable for deployment in resource-constrained environments. The results underscore the effectiveness of the proposed model scaling method, which balances depth, width, and resolution to optimize performance.
