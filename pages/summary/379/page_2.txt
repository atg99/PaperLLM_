2019). In this paper, the authors introduce EfficientNet, a novel approach to model scaling for convolutional neural networks (CNNs). They propose a compound scaling method that simultaneously adjusts the network's width, depth, and resolution with fixed ratios, contrasting with conventional methods that modify only one dimension at a time. This approach is grounded in the understanding that larger input images necessitate deeper networks and more channels to effectively capture detailed patterns.

The authors empirically demonstrate the effectiveness of their scaling method on existing architectures like MobileNets and ResNet, showing that the success of model scaling is contingent on the baseline network used. They leverage neural architecture search to create a new baseline network, resulting in a family of models termed EfficientNets. The results indicate that EfficientNets outperform other convolutional networks, achieving superior accuracy with significantly fewer parameters and faster inference times. For instance, EfficientNet-B7 surpasses the best existing GPipe accuracy while using 8.4 times fewer parameters.

The paper also reviews related work in the field of convolutional networks, discussing the trend towards larger models for improved accuracy, and the challenges posed by hardware limitations. The authors highlight the importance of efficiency alongside accuracy, especially in the context of mobile and resource-constrained environments. Overall, EfficientNet represents a significant advancement in CNN design, balancing performance and efficiency through a systematic scaling approach.
