The paper introduces EfficientNet, a novel approach to model scaling for Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs). Prior methods attempted to balance network width and depth but required extensive manual tuning. The authors propose a compound scaling method that uniformly adjusts network width, depth, and resolution using a compound coefficient. This method allows for efficient resource allocation and ensures that the increase in computational cost, measured in FLOPS, is manageable and proportional to the scaling factors.

The architecture of EfficientNet is built upon a baseline network termed EfficientNet-B0, developed through a multi-objective neural architecture search aimed at optimizing both accuracy and FLOPS. The authors employ a trade-off hyperparameter to balance these two objectives, resulting in a network that is efficient and effective. The main building block of EfficientNet is the mobile inverted bottleneck layer, enhanced with squeeze-and-excitation optimization.

The scaling process occurs in two steps: first, optimal scaling constants are determined through grid search, and second, these constants are applied to scale up the baseline network to create a series of EfficientNet variants (B1 to B7). The authors validate their scaling method by applying it to existing networks like MobileNets and ResNets, demonstrating improved accuracy across various models compared to traditional scaling methods.

Overall, the EfficientNet framework not only simplifies the scaling process but also achieves superior performance, highlighting its effectiveness in enhancing CNNs.
