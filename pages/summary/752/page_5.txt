The paper discusses a sequence-to-sequence (Seq2Seq) model for handwritten text recognition, focusing on the integration of teacher forcing and noise to enhance generalization. The model uses artificial tokens to denote the start and end of sequences, and during training, a teacher forcing mechanism is employed to ensure that the decoder receives the correct previous character. However, to avoid overfitting, the model incorporates a 10% probability of introducing noise by sampling from the decoder's output distribution.

The preprocessing phase involves normalizing contrast, correcting skew, and maintaining the aspect ratio of text line images by scaling them to a height of 64 pixels. Data augmentation techniques, like dilation and erosion, are applied randomly to mimic natural variations in handwritten text.

The experimental evaluation is structured into four subsections, starting with the datasets used, including the IAM Handwriting Database and others like Bozen and StAZH, which contain historical handwritten documents. The examination of a fixed encoder setup reveals limited success, leading to the exploration of a hybrid loss approach inspired by deeply-supervised networks. The performance metrics focus on Character Error Rate (CER), calculated as the normalized edit distance compared to the target character sequence, without utilizing a language model for enhancement.

Overall, the paper aims to assess the model's performance across various setups, comparing the results to current state-of-the-art methods while emphasizing the importance of attention mechanisms and positional encoding in improving recognition accuracy.
