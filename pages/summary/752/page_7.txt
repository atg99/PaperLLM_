This paper presents a novel approach to handwritten text recognition (HTR) using an attention-based sequence-to-sequence (Seq2Seq) architecture. The final model combines a convolutional feature extractor with a recurrent neural network (RNN) for decoding character sequences, effectively encoding both visual input and temporal context.

In the experiments, the model's performance is evaluated on several datasets, including IAM and Bozen, with results summarized in tables. The character error rates (CER) for the encoder and decoder are reported, revealing competitive performance. For instance, on the Bozen test set, the model achieves a CER of 4.66%, surpassing the best competitor from a related competition, while it obtains a CER of 4.87% on the IAM test set. These results showcase the model's effectiveness even without post-processing techniques, which are often employed by other state-of-the-art methods.

The paper emphasizes the flexibility of the Seq2Seq model, allowing it to operate without strict adherence to the Connectionist Temporal Classification (CTC) framework, thus facilitating better alignment between input and output sequences. The authors highlight a significant improvement of approximately 29% over previous works that utilized similar architectures.

In conclusion, the proposed model demonstrates strong performance on HTR tasks, rivaling state-of-the-art results, and lays the groundwork for future enhancements, particularly regarding the integration of language models for improved decoding accuracy.
