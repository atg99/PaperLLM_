The paper presents results on various encoder-decoder configurations for handwritten text recognition, focusing on validation Character Error Rate (CER) across different datasets. 

Table II shows the performance of a stand-alone encoder, achieving validation CERs of 3.53%, 6.29%, and 3.10% for the IAM, Bozen, and StAZH datasets, respectively. Table III explores the effects of different attention mechanisms and positional encodings on a fixed encoder setup using the IAM dataset. The content-based attention with positional encodings significantly improves the decoder's performance, while purely location-based attention and penalized features perform poorly.

To address the limitations of the fixed encoder, the authors introduce a hybrid loss allowing for encoder parameter adaptation, inspired by deeply-supervised networks. This approach retains text-predictive power while incorporating positional information for the decoder. Results in Table IV demonstrate that this setup enhances decoder performance and marginally improves encoder results compared to the stand-alone model, although positional encodings do not provide additional benefits.

Table V evaluates hybrid attention configurations, revealing that hybrid monotonic attention slightly outperforms hybrid chunkwise attention when utilizing gradient normalization and clipping. Learned positional encodings show better performance than fixed ones but do not enhance overall results. The findings suggest that the hybrid loss is not essential for handwritten text recognition if the interpretability of the encoder output is not a priority.
