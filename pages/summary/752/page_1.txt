The paper evaluates the effectiveness of sequence-to-sequence (Seq2Seq) models for handwritten text recognition (HTR), highlighting the limitations of traditional convolutional and recurrent neural networks that use Connectionist Temporal Classification (CTC). The authors propose an attention-based Seq2Seq model that integrates a convolutional neural network (CNN) as a feature extractor with a recurrent neural network (RNN) to encode visual information and temporal character context. A separate RNN is employed to decode the character sequence, allowing the model to focus on relevant features through attention mechanisms.

The study conducts comparative experiments on various attention mechanisms and positional encodings to achieve optimal alignment between input and output sequences. The proposed model is trained end-to-end and includes an optional hybrid loss that maintains an interpretable encoder output, beneficial for applications like keyword spotting. The results demonstrate competitive performance on the IAM and ICFHR2016 READ datasets, surpassing other recent Seq2Seq approaches without utilizing a language model.

The paper is organized into sections that review related works, describe the proposed methodology, detail implementation, present experimental setups and results, and conclude with reflections on future research directions. The findings suggest that the proposed Seq2Seq model provides a robust foundation for advancing HTR technologies and integrating external memory modules and language models in future studies.
