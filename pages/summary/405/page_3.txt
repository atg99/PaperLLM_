The paper presents a novel hybrid network architecture named ViT-YOLO, which integrates convolutional and self-attention mechanisms for improved object detection. This architecture builds upon the YOLOv4-P7 framework and is structured into three main components as illustrated in Figure 2. 

The first component, MHSA-Darknet, serves as the backbone of the model, incorporating multi-head self-attention into the original CSP-Darknet architecture. This integration enhances the extraction of differentiated features, particularly beneficial for processing drone-captured images that often present large scales and complex scenes. The use of transformers allows for capturing global context information, addressing the limitations of traditional convolutional networks.

The second component, BiFPN, replaces the PANet and is designed to aggregate features from various backbone levels, facilitating effective detection across different scales. This contributes to the model's robustness and accuracy.

The third component consists of the standard YOLO detection heads, which predict bounding boxes at five distinct scales. To further enhance performance, the model employs additional techniques such as Test-Time Augmentation (TTA) and Weighted Boxes Fusion (WBF). TTA applies data augmentation to the test dataset, while WBF utilizes confidence scores from proposed bounding boxes to create averaged boxes, improving model fusion outcomes.

Overall, the ViT-YOLO architecture aims to address challenges such as viewpoint variation and category confusion in drone imagery, enhancing the model's domain adaptation capabilities and ensuring robust object detection.
