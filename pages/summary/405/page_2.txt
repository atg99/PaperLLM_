In this paper, we address the challenges in UAV vision and the processing of multi-scale features. Traditional methods often combine low-level and high-level features through summation or concatenation, which can lead to feature mismatch and reduced performance. Our approach introduces learnable weights to assess the importance of different input features while employing a multi-scale feature fusion technique that integrates both top-down and bottom-up processes.

We propose a hybrid detector, ViT-YOLO, which builds on the one-stage detector design. The framework merges CSP-Darknet for feature extraction with multi-head self-attention to enhance context information and feature representation. Additionally, it utilizes a weighted bi-directional feature pyramid network (BiFPN) for effective cross-scale feature fusion. The YOLOv3 coupled head is employed for bounding box classification and regression tasks. To further improve detection performance, we implement Test-Time Augmentation (TTA) and Weighted Boxes Fusion (WBF).

Our experiments reveal that the ViT-YOLO architecture significantly surpasses existing state-of-the-art detectors on the VisDrone2019 test-challenge dataset, achieving a mean Average Precision (mAP) of 39.41. 

The key contributions of this paper are:
1. The integration of a multi-head self-attention block within the CSP-Darknet backbone for enhanced feature representation.
2. The introduction of a highly effective weighted BiFPN for improved feature fusion across scales.
3. The application of TTA and WBF to achieve competitive results on the VisDrone2019 benchmark.

In the context of related work, we highlight the evolution of object detection algorithms, focusing on one-stage detectors like the YOLO series, and the application of the Transformer model in various tasks, including object detection through approaches like DETR. We also discuss the significance of multi-scale feature fusion in object detection, referencing foundational works such as Feature Pyramid Network (FPN) and the recent BiFPN.
