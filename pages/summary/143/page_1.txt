The paper introduces the Guided Variational Autoencoder (Guided-VAE), an algorithm designed to enhance controllable generative modeling through latent representation disentanglement learning. The authors propose two strategies—unsupervised and supervised—to improve the capabilities of the standard Variational Autoencoder (VAE) while retaining its core architecture.

In the unsupervised strategy, a lightweight decoder is incorporated to learn latent geometric transformations and principal components, guiding the VAE's learning process. Conversely, the supervised strategy employs an adversarial mechanism to promote the disentanglement of latent variables, allowing one variable to be discriminative while others are adversarially discriminative.

Guided-VAE aims to create semantically interpretable latent representations without compromising the integrity of the VAE. The model is trained through multi-task learning, balancing the objectives of effective representation learning and controllable latent variables. Experimental results demonstrate that Guided-VAE offers improved synthesis, enhanced disentanglement for classification tasks, and reduced classification errors in meta-learning scenarios.

Overall, the paper highlights the advantages of Guided-VAE in achieving transparent and interpretable representation learning, showcasing its effectiveness in various applications including data modeling and few-shot learning.
