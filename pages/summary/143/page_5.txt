This paper presents the Guided-VAE model, which aims to improve the disentanglement of latent representations in generative models. The authors compare their method with existing models such as β-VAE and FactorVAE through qualitative and quantitative evaluations, particularly focusing on how well different models can disentangle latent factors related to geometric shapes and facial features.

In the qualitative results, the authors demonstrate that Guided-VAE effectively captures distinct geometry factors, including x-position, y-position, scale, and rotation, with clearer traversals in latent space compared to other methods. Each latent variable corresponds to a specific geometric feature, allowing for more controlled variations in the generated shapes.

Quantitative evaluations are conducted using metrics like Z-Diff, SAP, and Factor scores to assess disentanglement performance. The results show that Guided-VAE outperforms existing models, achieving the highest Z-Diff score of 99.2, indicating lower variance in inferred latent variables for fixed generative factors. Additionally, the model exhibits a significant improvement in representation transferability, as shown by lower classification errors across various dimensional settings on the MNIST dataset.

Overall, the Guided-VAE model demonstrates a superior ability to disentangle latent representations by employing a side objective, which complements existing methods and leads to enhanced performance in both disentanglement and representation learning tasks.
