This paper presents a novel generative model disentanglement learning method called Guided-VAE, which enhances the capabilities of variational autoencoders (VAE) through latent variable guidance. The work outlines two versions of Guided-VAE: unsupervised and supervised.

In the unsupervised version, deformable PCA is introduced as a subtask to improve the interpretability and controllability of latent variables during the VAE learning process. The supervised version employs an adversarial excitation and inhibition mechanism, which aims to enhance the disentanglement, informativeness, and controllability of latent variables.

Guided-VAE maintains the beneficial properties of traditional VAE while significantly improving its controllability, making it applicable to various generative modeling and representation learning tasks.

The related work section discusses the landscape of generative models, particularly focusing on GANs and VAEs, and emphasizes the recent interest in disentanglement learning. It highlights existing methods that impose constraints on latent variables but notes their limitations in altering the VAE architecture. The paper connects its approach to prior works, such as β-VAE and PCA, while introducing deformable PCA as a guiding mechanism for VAE training.

In the model section, the paper defines the components and formulations of Guided-VAE, beginning with the standard VAE setup and the introduction of latent variables. The discussions set the stage for the technical details that follow, ultimately leading to a comprehensive understanding of how Guided-VAE operates and its contributions to the field of generative modeling.
