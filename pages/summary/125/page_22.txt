The introduction to autoencoders emphasizes the importance of regularization in preventing these models from merely learning the identity function, which would hinder their ability to detect anomalies. Anomaly detection is illustrated through examples such as identifying fraudulent credit card transactions, which typically constitute about 0.1% of the dataset, and fault detection in industrial settings. It is crucial to note that for effective training, it is assumed that outliers have a negligible impact on the autoencoder's learning process.

However, the text highlights challenges in anomaly detection using autoencoders, particularly the risk of overfitting and result instability. Different architectures can yield varying reconstruction errors (REs), leading to inconsistencies in identified outliers. To mitigate these issues, a common strategy involves training multiple models and averaging their REs or selecting the maximum REs from various models.

The section acknowledges that while it provides useful insights, it does not aim to be a comprehensive guide to resolving these challenges. Advanced techniques, such as using ensembles of autoencoders, can also help address instability issues, particularly in scenarios with limited data.
