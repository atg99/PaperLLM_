This paper introduces autoencoders, focusing on their architecture and application in classification tasks. The encoder and decoder both utilize linear functions, and the mean squared error (MSE) is employed as the loss function for training. Input data is normalized to enhance performance.

In the classification segment, the paper explores the use of latent features derived from the MNIST dataset. By employing the k-nearest neighbors (kNN) algorithm with all 784 pixel values, the model achieves a 96.4% accuracy but requires approximately 16.6 minutes for training. In contrast, when utilizing latent features from a feedforward autoencoder with 8 neurons in the hidden layer, the accuracy drops to 89% but the computation time significantly reduces to just 1.1 seconds, demonstrating a substantial speed-up of around 1000 times with a minor decrease in accuracy. The analysis extends to the Fashion MNIST dataset, illustrating similar efficiencies in classification using latent features. The findings underscore the balance between computation efficiency and accuracy when employing autoencoders for classification tasks.
