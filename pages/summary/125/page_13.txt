The introduction to autoencoders discusses their role in dimensionality reduction using a bottleneck architecture. By utilizing a middle layer with fewer neurons than the input layer, autoencoders can efficiently extract essential features from datasets, offering a learned representation of the inputs. The effectiveness of this approach is demonstrated with examples from the MNIST dataset, where original digits are compared to their reconstructed versions. 

Figures illustrate the performance of an autoencoder with a middle layer of 16 neurons, which successfully reconstructs the digits, while a model with only 8 neurons fails to accurately recreate some characters, leading to significant misinterpretations. This highlights the importance of choosing an appropriate size for the bottleneck layer to balance feature extraction and reconstruction fidelity.
