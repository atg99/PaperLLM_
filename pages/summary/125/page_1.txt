This article provides a comprehensive overview of autoencoders, including their mathematical foundations, applications, and limitations. Autoencoders are a type of neural network that operates in an unsupervised learning context, where the model learns from unlabeled data. The article begins with a general introduction, detailing the structure and function of autoencoders, emphasizing the importance of the activation function in the output layer and the loss function. 

A key concept discussed is the reconstruction error, which measures how well the autoencoder is able to reconstruct the input data. The article highlights several typical applications for autoencoders, such as dimensionality reduction, classification, denoising, and anomaly detection. The origins of autoencoders are traced back to work by Rumelhart, Hinton, and Williams in 1986, underscoring their significance in the field of neural networks. Overall, the paper serves as an informative primer on autoencoders, elucidating their functionality and practical uses in machine learning.
