The paper discusses the applications of autoencoders, particularly focusing on their role in dimensionality reduction. Autoencoders utilize a bottleneck architecture where the latent features have a reduced dimension compared to the input data. This inherent design allows for efficient dimension reduction, enabling the use of these latent features in various tasks such as classification and clustering. 

One significant advantage of autoencoders over traditional Principal Component Analysis (PCA) lies in their computational efficiency. Unlike PCA, which requires the entire dataset for calculations and is limited to linear transformations, autoencoders can process large datasets more effectively by training on mini-batches and accommodating non-linear transformations. This flexibility makes autoencoders more suitable for practical applications with large amounts of data and numerous features, where PCA may become infeasible.

Additionally, the paper notes that under specific conditions, a feedforward autoencoder can achieve results equivalent to PCA. This highlights the potential of autoencoders as a powerful alternative for dimensionality reduction tasks in various applications.
