The paper introduces autoencoders and discusses their objective of minimizing the difference between input data and reconstructed output data using specific loss functions. Two commonly used loss functions for autoencoders are Mean Squared Error (MSE) and Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE). 

MSE is highlighted as the primary choice for regression problems, defined mathematically as the average of the squared differences between actual and predicted values. The formula for MSE is presented, emphasizing its applicability across various scenarios, regardless of the output layer activation function or data normalization methods. 

To demonstrate that the minimum MSE is achieved when the predicted output matches the input, the paper outlines the process of taking the derivative of the MSE with respect to a specific observation. It establishes that the condition for minimizing MSE occurs when the predicted value equals the actual value. Furthermore, it discusses the necessity of confirming that the second derivative is positive to ensure a minimum is reached. 

Overall, the section provides a foundational understanding of how autoencoders function in relation to loss minimization, particularly through the lens of the MSE loss function.
