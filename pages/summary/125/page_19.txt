Autoencoders are a powerful tool for dimensionality reduction, which can significantly reduce computation time with minimal impact on accuracy, particularly important in high-dimensional datasets due to the curse of dimensionality. They are also effective for anomaly detection across various datasets.

To illustrate anomaly detection with autoencoders, consider a simple model comprising three layers: an input layer with 784 neurons, a latent feature generation layer with 64 neurons, and an output layer with 784 neurons. Training this model on the MNIST dataset, which consists of 60,000 images, allows it to learn the normal patterns within this dataset.

When we introduce an image of a shoe from the Fashion MNIST dataset into the testing set of MNIST, we create a new dataset of 10,001 images. The shoe serves as an anomaly due to its distinct characteristics compared to the MNIST images. By employing the trained autoencoder, it can effectively identify the shoe image as an outlier among the other MNIST images, demonstrating the utility of autoencoders in detecting anomalies in data.
