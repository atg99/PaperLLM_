The paper introduces autoencoders, focusing on the mathematical foundations of their loss functions. It begins by confirming that a certain condition leads to a minimum, which is essential for understanding the optimization process in autoencoders. 

A key concept discussed is the use of binary cross-entropy (LCE) as a loss function when the output layer's activation function is a sigmoid, confining outputs between 0 and 1. This approach is particularly effective for autoencoders, even though binary cross-entropy is commonly used in classification tasks. The formula for LCE is presented, indicating that it sums over all observations and components of the input vectors.

The paper then explores the minimization of the binary cross-entropy function, demonstrating that minimizing LCE aligns with the goal of accurately reconstructing the input. To achieve this, the first derivative of LCE is calculated, leading to the conclusion that LCE is minimized when the reconstructed output matches the input for each observation, provided the input values are not strictly 0 or 1. This analysis emphasizes the relationship between minimizing the loss function and achieving effective reconstruction in autoencoders.
