The section introduces denoising autoencoders, which are designed to correct errors or noise in input data. An example is presented using handwritten digits from the MNIST dataset, where Gaussian noise is added to pixel values. The autoencoder learns to reconstruct the original images from the noisy inputs, effectively removing the random noise. Results demonstrate the model's efficacy in denoising, as illustrated by a comparison of noisy images, original images, and denoised outputs.

The discussion then transitions to convolutional autoencoders, highlighting their advantages over feed-forward architectures, particularly in image processing tasks. A comparison between a feed-forward autoencoder (FAA) and a convolutional autoencoder (CA) shows that the CA yields superior results due to its ability to efficiently handle image data. Both models were trained under identical conditions, emphasizing the performance benefits of convolutional layers in autoencoder designs.
