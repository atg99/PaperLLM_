The introduction to autoencoders outlines their primary function, which is to reconstruct input observations with minimal error. This concept is illustrated through the example of image datasets, where an autoencoder generates an output image closely resembling the input image. The paper defines an autoencoder as an algorithm designed to learn an informative representation of data that can be applied across various applications by effectively reconstructing input observations.

The architecture of autoencoders consists of three main components: an encoder, a latent feature representation, and a decoder. The encoder and decoder serve as functions, while the latent feature representation is typically a tensor of real numbers. The goal is for the autoencoder to achieve accurate reconstruction while also creating a meaningful latent representation. For instance, in the context of handwritten digits, latent features might include characteristics such as the number of strokes or angles of each line, rather than focusing on pixel gray values.

This approach highlights the human-like ability to extract essential information necessary for solving problems, such as writing digits, thereby facilitating tasks like feature extraction for classification or clustering, or enhancing the understanding of a dataset's fundamental features.
