The introduction to autoencoders discusses the challenges posed by high-dimensional datasets, particularly the curse of dimensionality. When analyzing a unit cube in d dimensions with m randomly distributed points, the length l of the smallest hyper-cube needed to contain at least one point can be calculated. Results show that as the dimensionality increases, the length l required grows, indicating that data becomes increasingly sparse. For instance, with 1000 points, the lengths required for dimensions 2, 10, 100, and 1000 are 0.003, 0.50, 0.93, and 0.99 respectively. This sparsity means that to train an algorithm effectively, a significantly larger number of observations is necessary than what is typically available in datasets. Additionally, if considering a smaller hypercube with a side length of 1/10, the average number of observations in this region decreases drastically with higher dimensions, highlighting the impracticality of finding sufficient data in high-dimensional spaces. For example, in 100 dimensions, it would require more observations than there are atoms in the universe to find even one observation in a tiny hypercube.
