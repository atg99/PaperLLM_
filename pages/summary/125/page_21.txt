The introduction to autoencoders highlights their application in anomaly detection within large datasets. The process involves training an autoencoder on the complete dataset or a known clean subset, followed by calculating the reconstruction error (RE) for each observation. Observations are then sorted by their RE, with those having the highest values classified as outliers. The effectiveness of this method relies on the assumption that outliers constitute a negligible portion of the dataset, which ensures they do not significantly affect the autoencoder's learning process. Understanding the results and the nature of the data is crucial for determining the number of outliers.
