This section evaluates Type III and IV coverage rates for prediction intervals generated by OOB, SC, and QRF methods, using a simulation setup without test cases. For each training dataset, a 10-dimensional predictor vector is utilized to generate prediction intervals. The exact Type IV coverage probability is computed based on the known conditional distribution of Y given X=x, while the Type III coverage rate is estimated by averaging the Type IV estimates across 200 training datasets.

Figures illustrate the Type IV coverage estimates for the different interval methods, showing that OOB and SC intervals exhibit similar performance in terms of Type III and IV coverage across various scenarios. In contrast, QRF intervals demonstrate greater variability and often yield different coverage values when compared to OOB and SC intervals. The performance of QRF intervals varies significantly across scenarios, performing better in some (e.g., Linear x Heteroscedastic scenarios) and worse in others.

Key factors influencing Type III and IV coverage include the size of the training dataset, the shape of the mean function, and the variance of the errors, particularly in heteroscedastic conditions. An example of a nonlinear mean function is provided, highlighting its effect on coverage rates.
