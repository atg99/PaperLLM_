The paper presents an analysis of prediction intervals using different methods: out-of-bag prediction intervals, split conformal prediction intervals, and quantile regression forests. The focus is on Type IV coverage rates under various conditions, including linear and nonlinear models with different characteristics such as homoscedasticity and heteroscedasticity, as well as heavy-tailed distributions.

The results are illustrated through boxplots that show the coverage rate estimates for sample sizes ranging from 200 to 5000. Each data point in the boxplots represents the average of 200 Type IV coverage estimates, which assess the probability that a given outcome falls within a specified interval. The findings indicate performance variations among the methods, highlighting the importance of selecting appropriate techniques based on data characteristics and sample size.

Overall, the study emphasizes the significance of understanding different methodologies for interval estimation in predictive modeling, particularly in the context of uncertain environments.
