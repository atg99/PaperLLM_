Quantile Regression Forest (QRF) is a method for estimating the conditional distribution of a response variable Y given predictor variables X. It utilizes the weights assigned to each data point based on random forest principles to create a discrete distribution from which quantiles can be derived. The QRF estimates the conditional cumulative distribution function (cdf) of Y given X, allowing the calculation of quantiles, which form the basis for prediction intervals.

The QRF approach involves defining weights that are dependent on the training dataset and the value of X, denoted as wi(x). By employing these weights, the QRF can provide an estimate of the conditional cdf, Hn(y|x), and subsequently determine the α-quantile, denoted bQα(x). The prediction interval for Y is then constructed using the α-quantiles, resulting in a 100(1-α)% prediction interval.

Meinshausen (2006) established that, under certain conditions, the approximation of the QRF conditional cdf converges uniformly in probability to zero as the sample size increases. In practical evaluations using five datasets, the average coverage rates for 95% QRF intervals ranged from 90.2% to 98.6%, demonstrating the method's effectiveness.
