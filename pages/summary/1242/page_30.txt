The paper presents a comparative analysis of prediction intervals generated by Quantile Regression Forests (QRF), Split Conformal (SC) methods, and Out-of-Bag (OOB) intervals across 60 datasets. The findings indicate that QRF intervals often exhibit Type I and Type II coverage rates that deviate from nominal levels, leading to instances of both over-coverage and under-coverage. When QRF intervals do align with nominal rates, they tend to be wider than OOB intervals. In simulations involving heteroscedastic errors, QRF intervals generally outperformed both OOB and SC intervals regarding Type III and Type IV coverage, which aligns with their design for Type III coverage. In contrast, OOB and SC intervals performed better in certain scenarios, particularly those involving homoscedasticity. The paper emphasizes the importance of evaluating the homoscedasticity assumption for datasets by analyzing residual plots of OOB prediction errors against estimated mean values.
