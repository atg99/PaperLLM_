The paper examines the performance of various prediction interval methods in the context of statistical modeling with correlated predictors. It specifically compares out-of-bag (OOB) prediction intervals, split conformal (SC) prediction intervals, and quantile regression forest (QRF) intervals across different sample sizes (n=200, 500, 1000, 2000, 5000). The results are presented through boxplots that illustrate the Type II coverage rate estimates, indicating the probability that the true response falls within the predicted intervals. 

Key findings show that the performance varies with sample size and method, highlighting the importance of choosing appropriate techniques based on data characteristics such as homoscedasticity and the presence of heavy tails. The analysis underscores the need for careful consideration of prediction interval methods to ensure reliable statistical inference in models with correlated predictors.
