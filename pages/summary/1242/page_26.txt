The paper discusses the evaluation of coverage probabilities in random forests across various datasets, specifically focusing on Type I and Type II coverage rates. It begins by noting that datasets with missing values are excluded and that a consistent number of trees (2000) is used for all random forests, with a nominal coverage rate of 0.9. Due to the nature of the datasets, Type III and IV coverage probabilities are not assessed. 

To empirically evaluate Type I and II coverage rates, the authors employ five-fold cross-validation, partitioning each dataset into five non-overlapping segments. Four segments form a training set for predicting response values in the fifth segment, allowing for the calculation of Type II coverage rates. This process is repeated across multiple partitions, yielding empirical Type I coverage rates through averaging.

Results are illustrated in figures that compare Type I and Type II coverage rates across 60 datasets. The findings indicate that both out-of-bag (OOB) and split-coverage (SC) prediction intervals maintain good Type I coverage rates around 0.9, while quantile regression forest (QRF) intervals often deviate significantly from this target. Additionally, OOB intervals are generally narrower than SC intervals across most datasets, and their widths are comparable to or narrower than those of QRF intervals, particularly in cases where QRF coverage rates are low. These results align with prior conclusions from simulation studies, reinforcing the effectiveness of OOB and SC prediction intervals in providing reliable coverage.
