The paper discusses the construction of split conformal prediction intervals for regression models using random forests. Specifically, it defines the absolute residuals \( R_i \) and determines the \( k \)th smallest value \( d \) from these residuals, where \( k \) is calculated based on a given formula involving the sample size \( n \) and a significance level \( \alpha \). The resulting prediction interval for a new observation \( Y \) is expressed as \( \left(b_{mn/2}(X) - d, b_{mn/2}(X) + d\right) \).

The authors reference work by Lei et al. (2018), which establishes that the prediction interval has a finite-sample coverage probability of at least \( 1 - \alpha \), providing a useful guarantee for the performance of these intervals. However, a noted limitation is that the intervals are calibrated based on a random forest constructed from half of the observations, which may lead to conservative estimates of interval width.

Despite this limitation, the authors find that split conformal intervals perform well in their simulations and analyses. They highlight the computational efficiency of the split conformal approach compared to traditional conformal methods, as it requires building only one random forest for point predictions and interval estimation, as opposed to constructing two forests in the split conformal method.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the practical benefits of the split conformal approach while acknowledging the trade-offs in interval width and computational requirements.
