This paper presents a novel approach to constructing prediction intervals using a random forest framework, differentiating itself from the random forest interval method described by Lei et al. in 2018. Instead of relying on a single random partition of the training set to calculate cross-validated prediction errors, the authors utilize out-of-bag (OOB) prediction errors derived from a single random forest model built on the entire training dataset. This OOB-based method also has the potential for application with various conditional mean estimation techniques beyond random forests, although the focus of this study remains on random forests.

The paper is structured as follows: Section 2 provides foundational knowledge about random forests, their mechanisms, and the proposed method for prediction interval construction. Section 3 introduces four types of coverage probabilities and discusses the asymptotic properties of the OOB prediction intervals. Section 4 reviews alternative methods for constructing prediction intervals within the random forest context. Section 5 presents a simulation study comparing the finite-sample performance of the proposed intervals against other methods, focusing on coverage rates and interval widths. Section 6 assesses the performance of these methods across 60 real datasets, with the relevant R code and datasets available on GitHub. The paper concludes with a discussion in Section 7, and supplementary materials include proofs and additional figures. 

Overall, the proposed OOB prediction interval method aims to enhance the reliability and applicability of random forest predictions.
