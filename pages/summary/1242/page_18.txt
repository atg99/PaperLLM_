The paper evaluates the Type I and Type II coverage rates of Out-of-Bag (OOB), Standard Calibration (SC), and Quantile Random Forest (QRF) prediction intervals across various simulation scenarios. Type I coverage is assessed by averaging 200 Type II coverage estimates from simulated datasets, revealing that OOB and SC intervals closely align with the nominal level of 0.9, while QRF intervals tend to over-cover or under-cover.

As the sample size increases, the OOB and SC Type II coverage estimates show reduced variation, stabilizing around 0.9. The study finds that both OOB and SC intervals maintain consistent widths across test cases, in contrast to QRF intervals, which display variability. The analysis includes comparison of interval widths, demonstrating that OOB intervals are generally narrower than those produced by other methods.

Supplemental materials provide additional figures summarizing the coverage rates and interval widths, indicating that as sample size increases, interval widths shrink for all methods. Overall, the findings suggest OOB and SC methods offer more reliable coverage and narrower intervals compared to QRF, especially as sample sizes grow.
