The paper discusses the evaluation of Type III and IV coverage for prediction intervals in random forests, particularly at a specified point x3. The study finds that the multivariate normal distribution of predictor variables assigns low probabilities to neighborhoods around x3, resulting in training datasets that often lack observations close to this point. Consequently, the random forest model may struggle with extrapolation, leading to variable Type IV and below-nominal Type III coverage levels, especially in scenarios with nonlinear mean functions and interactions.

Figures S.7 and S.8 illustrate these coverage issues, with OOB and SC intervals showing near-zero coverage in nonlinear scenarios. The findings suggest that regardless of the training observations weighted most heavily, the random forest predictions may significantly exceed the true mean function at x3, resulting in large prediction errors. The paper concludes that none of the prediction interval methods studied—OOB, SC, or QRF—are reliable for extrapolation in regions devoid of training data, indicating a gap in effective approaches for such predictions.

The data analysis section compares the performance of these prediction intervals across 60 actual datasets, with a significant portion sourced from previous research and spanning diverse fields. The datasets vary in sample size and the number of predictors, and the response variable is standardized prior to analysis to ensure consistent interval widths.
