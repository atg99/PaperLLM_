The paper presents a comparative analysis of interval estimation methods, specifically focusing on split conformal (SC) and quantile regression forest (QRF) techniques. The study examines the performance of these methods in relation to out-of-bag (OOB) interval widths under various conditions, including different data distributions characterized by homoscedasticity, heteroscedasticity, and heavy-tailed behavior. 

Figure 2 illustrates boxplots that display the log2 ratios of SC interval widths to OOB interval widths, as well as the log2 ratios of QRF interval width averages to OOB interval widths. These ratios are assessed for different sample sizes (n=200, 500, 1000, 2000, 5000) with correlated predictors following a normal distribution. 

The results indicate that the performance of the interval estimation methods varies depending on the underlying data characteristics and sample size, highlighting the implications for selecting appropriate methods in practice. Overall, the findings contribute to the understanding of how different interval estimation techniques behave under specific statistical conditions.
