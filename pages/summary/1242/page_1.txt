This paper addresses the challenge of determining prediction intervals in random forests for quantitative responses, an area often overlooked in machine learning. The authors propose a novel method for constructing prediction intervals based on the empirical distribution of out-of-bag prediction errors. These intervals can be derived from a single random forest model and are shown to have asymptotically correct coverage rates under certain regularity conditions. Through simulation studies and analysis of 60 real datasets, the authors compare the performance of their proposed intervals with quantile regression forests and recently developed split conformal intervals. The findings indicate that the proposed intervals are generally narrower than those from competing methods while still achieving coverage rates that align closely with nominal levels. This work contributes to the field by enhancing the reliability and efficiency of prediction intervals in random forest applications.
