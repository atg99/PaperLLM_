The paper discusses the properties of the empirical distribution function bFn(t) as an estimator for the true cumulative distribution function F(t). It establishes two main lemmas that highlight the convergence behavior of bFn(t) as the sample size n approaches infinity.

Lemma 1 demonstrates that under certain conditions, the supremum of the absolute difference between bFn(t) and F(t) converges in probability to zero as n increases. Additionally, it shows that the difference in the function values at two distinct points in the sample converges in probability to a non-zero limit.

Lemma 2 extends this result by considering the conditional probability given a set of observations. It establishes that the supremum of the absolute difference between the conditional probabilities and the true cumulative distribution function also converges to zero as n increases.

The paper further defines a target conditional coverage probability and shows that the difference between the empirical and true probabilities converges to a specific value as n increases, while accounting for a remainder term that diminishes as the sample size grows.

The proof for Lemma 1 relies on the continuity of the function F and the behavior of the empirical distribution function around specific intervals. The results collectively suggest that under the stated conditions, the empirical distribution function provides a reliable estimation of the true distribution as the sample size becomes large.
