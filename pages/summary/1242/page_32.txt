The paper discusses variations in residual plots, including out-of-bag (OOB) prediction errors versus important predictors and absolute OOB prediction errors versus estimated mean values, as tools for identifying discrepancies between assumptions and data. It highlights the utility of transforming the response variable for variance stabilization, although such transformations may not always be feasible or desirable. In those cases, modifications based on Lei et al. (2018) can be applied to accommodate nonconstant error variance. Specifically, Lei et al. propose Locally Weighted Conformal Inference, which enhances prediction intervals' empirical coverage when error variance is related to the predictor vector. 

The comparison between OOB and SC inference indicates that both methods yield similar coverage probabilities; however, OOB intervals are generally narrower and more informative. While SC intervals offer finite-sample coverage guarantees under broad conditions, the numerical results from simulations and analyses of 60 real datasets favor OOB intervals. The authors recommend using OOB intervals in conjunction with random forest point predictions to provide a credible range of response values for data interpretation.
