The referenced works encompass significant contributions to statistical modeling and machine learning, particularly focusing on regression and classification methods. 

Chipman et al. (2010) introduced Bayesian additive regression trees (BART), a flexible approach that combines Bayesian methods with tree-based modeling. Efron (1992) discussed jackknife-after-bootstrap standard errors, enhancing the understanding of influence functions in statistical inference. In a later work, Efron (2014) addressed estimation accuracy following model selection, highlighting the implications for statistical reliability.

Friedberg et al. (2018) presented local linear forests, a method that extends traditional forests by incorporating local linear regression techniques. Lei et al. (2018) contributed to distribution-free predictive inference for regression, providing a framework for valid predictions without relying on specific distributional assumptions.

Liaw and Wiener (2002) detailed the random forest algorithm, emphasizing its utility in classification and regression tasks. Lin and Jeon (2006) explored the combination of random forests with adaptive nearest neighbors, enhancing model performance through a hybrid approach. Meinshausen (2006) introduced quantile regression forests, which allow for the estimation of conditional quantiles, expanding the capabilities of traditional forest methods.

Mentch and Hooker (2016) focused on quantifying uncertainty in random forests, proposing methods for constructing confidence intervals and conducting hypothesis tests. Oshiro et al. (2012) investigated the optimal number of trees in a random forest, contributing to the understanding of model complexity. Finally, Scornet (2016) examined the relationship between random forests and kernel methods, highlighting theoretical connections and practical implications.

Overall, these works collectively advance the field of statistical modeling through innovative methodologies and theoretical insights, emphasizing the importance of model flexibility, uncertainty quantification, and practical applications in various contexts.
