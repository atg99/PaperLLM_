The paper presents an analysis of the performance of different statistical methods, specifically focusing on the interval widths produced by split conformal (SC) methods and quantile regression forests (QRF) compared to out-of-bag (OOB) interval widths. The study examines various conditions including nonlinear interactions, homoscedasticity, and heavy-tailed distributions across different sample sizes (n=200, 500, 1000, 2000, and 5000).

Key findings include boxplots illustrating the log2 ratios of SC and QRF interval widths relative to OOB interval widths under the condition where predictors are uncorrelated and normally distributed. The results indicate variations in interval width performance depending on the method and sample size, highlighting the robustness of the SC and QRF methods in estimating prediction intervals under different statistical properties.

The analysis emphasizes the importance of understanding how these methods behave in diverse scenarios, providing insights into their applicability and reliability in statistical modeling.
