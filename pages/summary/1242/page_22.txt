The paper discusses the bias in random forest predictions, particularly focusing on the mean response being strictly less than m(0) with a probability of one across all observations. This bias, especially at x=0, results in larger prediction errors and under-coverage for out-of-bag (OOB), split confidence (SC), and quantile random forest (QRF) intervals. The under-coverage is exacerbated in nonlinear and heteroscedastic cases, where prediction errors are combined from the training dataset rather than being specific to any x value. Consequently, OOB and SC intervals tend to over-cover values with low error variance and under-cover those with high error variance.

At x=0, the variance of errors is more than double the mean error variance across the predictor space, leading to significant under-coverage. In contrast, the prediction at x=1 shows improved performance across all intervals due to minimal random forest bias, as the average value at x near 1 aligns closely with m(1). This results in Type III and IV coverages nearing the nominal level in homoscedastic and heavy-tailed scenarios. The paper concludes that while QRF intervals experience similar bias issues as OOB and SC intervals, their adaptive width leads to better coverage in heteroscedastic situations. Overall, the findings emphasize the importance of understanding error variance and mean function shapes to improve prediction accuracy and coverage in random forest models.
