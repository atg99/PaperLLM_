This section discusses the tuning of the mtry and nodesize parameters for random forests using five-fold cross-validation to minimize the mean squared prediction error. The selected parameters are then fixed for subsequent evaluations of out-of-bag (OOB), split-conformal (SC), and quantile regression forest (QRF) intervals, with the latter involving an additional 20 repetitions for coverage probability estimation. The performance of these prediction intervals is assessed on the Concrete Strength dataset, with results indicating that OOB and SC intervals maintain an average coverage close to 90% and that OOB intervals are consistently narrower than SC and QRF intervals. The QRF intervals exhibit variability in Type I coverage rates based on tuning parameters. Overall, OOB intervals demonstrate superior performance across the examined parameter values.

In the concluding remarks, the authors advocate for OOB prediction intervals as a simple yet effective method for constructing prediction intervals from a single random forest. They provide theoretical backing for the asymptotic coverage of OOB intervals under certain conditions. Simulation results and analyses of multiple datasets affirm the reliability and efficiency of OOB intervals across diverse scenarios, suggesting their practical applicability in a wide range of problems.
