The paper discusses a novel model compression method, LeGR, specifically designed for pruning convolutional neural networks (ConvNets) across varying FLOP counts. It highlights the efficiency of LeGR in terms of both pruning time and accuracy when compared to existing methods such as AMC and MorphNet. The results show that LeGR significantly reduces pruning time by reusing the learned α-κ pair across different FLOP counts, unlike its counterparts which require separate searches for each count.

In the context of CIFAR-10 and ImageNet datasets, LeGR demonstrates superior performance, particularly with ResNet-56 and VGG-13, achieving higher accuracy and lower FLOP counts compared to several prior methods. The paper includes detailed comparisons, indicating that LeGR not only matches but often surpasses the accuracy of previous approaches while maintaining a focus on efficient exploration of the trade-off curve between speed and accuracy.

The findings underscore the practicality of LeGR in generating ConvNets for a range of FLOP counts without the need for repeated algorithm runs, thereby streamlining the model compression process. Overall, the results suggest that LeGR could be a valuable tool for researchers and practitioners aiming to optimize neural network architectures.
