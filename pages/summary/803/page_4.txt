The paper discusses LeGR-Pruning, an approach for pruning convolutional neural networks (ConvNets) to optimize performance while reducing computational load. The method involves learning layer-wise affine transformations represented by the α-κ pair, which facilitates the creation of filter masks to identify which filters can be pruned. The pruning process continues until the network meets a specified FLOP count, with a focus on maintaining validation accuracy.

To achieve this, the authors frame the learning of α and κ as an optimization problem aimed at maximizing validation accuracy. They utilize a regularized evolutionary algorithm for hyper-parameter optimization, demonstrating its efficacy in the neural architecture search space. The algorithm iteratively samples candidate α-κ pairs, evaluates their fitness based on validation accuracy, and mutates the best candidates to explore new configurations.

The authors emphasize the importance of the Pareto curve in understanding the trade-off between accuracy and FLOP count. They validate their approach through empirical testing, finding that a specific number of gradient updates works effectively across various datasets and network architectures.

Overall, the study presents a systematic method for optimizing ConvNets through pruning, balancing the need for computational efficiency with the goal of maintaining high performance.
