The paper presents LeGR, a novel method for filter pruning in convolutional neural networks (ConvNets) aimed at optimizing the accuracy-speed trade-off. The authors investigate the pruning of ResNet-56 for CIFAR-100 using LeGR, focusing on learning parameters α and κ under varying FLOP count constraints. They find that the performance of pruned networks remains consistent whether Assumption 1 is applied or not.

The study highlights the relationship between the approximation of the target ranking (ˆ⌧) and the effectiveness of the learned α-κ pair, suggesting that a closer fit to the actual target (⌧) enhances performance. Through experiments with different FLOP counts, they observe diminishing returns in accuracy improvements as ˆ⌧ increases, particularly in low-FLOP regimes where initial fine-tuning steps may not yield representative accuracy due to significant filter removal.

Additionally, the paper demonstrates the practical benefits of filter pruning using ResNet-50 and MobileNetV2 on different CPU architectures, showing that filter pruning can achieve near-linear speedups in runtime without the need for specialized hardware.

In conclusion, LeGR offers an efficient approach for practitioners to achieve ConvNet architectures with varying FLOP counts while maintaining competitive performance relative to existing pruning methods.
