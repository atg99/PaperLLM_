The paper "Towards Efficient Model Compression via Learned Global Ranking" addresses the challenges associated with pruning convolutional filters in ConvNets for model compression, particularly in the context of embodied AI applications like autonomous robots and drones. Traditional filter pruning methods require users to specify a target model complexity, such as model size or FLOP count, which can be difficult to determine given the nuanced trade-offs between accuracy and inference speed that these applications demand. The authors argue that the trial-and-error approach to finding an optimal balance is time-consuming and inefficient.

To improve this process, the authors propose a novel algorithm called LeGR, which learns a global ranking of filters across different layers of the ConvNet. This allows for the generation of multiple ConvNet architectures with varying accuracy and latency trade-offs by selectively pruning the least important filters. The results indicate that LeGR is significantly faster—by 2 to 3 times—than previous methods while achieving comparable or improved performance when applied to pruned ResNet-56 models on the CIFAR-100 dataset. The effectiveness of LeGR is further validated on ImageNet and Bird-200 datasets using ResNet-50 and MobileNetV2 architectures.

Overall, the paper contributes to the field of model compression by providing a more efficient framework for filter pruning that accommodates the complexities of real-world applications where speed and accuracy are critical.
