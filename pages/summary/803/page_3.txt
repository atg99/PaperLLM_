The paper discusses a method for generating a set of pruned convolutional networks (ConvNets) with varying complexities, as opposed to focusing on a single pruned model. It critiques existing approaches that iteratively prune and fine-tune filters, noting that these methods often yield pruned ConvNets with lower accuracy compared to their proposed approach. 

The authors introduce a global ranking system for filters across different layers, enabling the generation of ConvNets of different complexities by pruning the least important filters based on this ranking. They emphasize the importance of balancing accuracy and latency, using FLOP count as a measure of model complexity, which they find predictive of latency.

Two key assumptions underpin their global ranking method: 
1. The subset assumption posits that top-performing smaller ConvNets are a subset of larger ones, which allows for efficient generation of pruned models.
2. The norm assumption suggests that the `2 norm can rank filters locally within layers, but additional layer-wise affine transformations are needed to rank them globally.

The importance of each filter is defined using learned scale and shift parameters, which allows for effective pruning while meeting specific FLOP count requirements. The paper concludes with a method for learning these parameters, highlighting the need for careful evaluation across different model complexities to achieve optimal performance.
