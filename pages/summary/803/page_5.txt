In this paper, the authors present a method for optimizing the pruning of convolutional neural networks (ConvNets) through a layer-wise affine transformation approach, which allows for a more efficient search for optimal configurations. The optimization is based on the assumption of Lipschitz continuity in the loss function, providing a theoretical foundation for the pruning process. 

The evaluation of the proposed method, LeGR, is conducted on several image classification benchmarks including CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, ImageNet, and Birds-200. The training protocols are detailed, with specific hyperparameters for different datasets and models, including ResNet-56 and MobileNetV2. The authors compare LeGR against four other filter pruning methods: MorphNet, AMC, Fisher-Pruning, and a uniform pruning baseline. 

Results indicate that while some methods like Fisher-Pruning struggle due to a low learning rate, LeGR consistently outperforms other approaches, especially in scenarios requiring low FLOP counts. The paper emphasizes the importance of exploring the trade-off between model accuracy and inference latency, demonstrating that LeGR provides significant improvements in efficiency and performance compared to existing methods. The authors also highlight the computational costs associated with pruning, breaking down the time required for network searches across different FLOP counts. Overall, LeGR shows promise in enhancing the pruning process for ConvNets, making it a valuable contribution to the field.
