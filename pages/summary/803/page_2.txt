This paper addresses the inefficiencies in filter pruning for convolutional networks (ConvNets) used in embodied AI applications. Instead of generating a single ConvNet for each pruning procedure, the proposed method creates a set of ConvNets, allowing practitioners to explore various accuracy vs. speed trade-offs effectively. This approach aims to reduce the model compression overhead while maintaining comparable accuracy to state-of-the-art methods.

The authors introduce the learned global ranking (LeGR) algorithm, which ranks convolutional filters across layers. By removing the lowest-ranked filters, different ConvNet architectures can be easily obtained and fine-tuned. This method is positioned as a significant improvement over previous techniques that primarily relied on local filter importance metrics.

Key contributions of the paper include the development of LeGR, which is faster than existing pruning methods and provides similar accuracy across multiple datasets, including CIFAR-10/100, Bird-200, and ImageNet. The work also emphasizes a global ranking strategy for filter importance, addressing limitations of prior magnitude-based pruning methods.

The paper reviews related work in ConvNet compression, including weight quantization, efficient convolution operators, and neural architecture search. It categorizes previous filter pruning methods into two classes: those with pre-defined architectures, which often struggle with overall target architecture decisions, and those that learn architectures automatically but may not adequately address varying layer importance. The proposed approach aims to generate a diverse set of pruned ConvNets, offering flexibility in model complexity without requiring precise parameter specification.
