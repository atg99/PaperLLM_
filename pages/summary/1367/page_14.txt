The paper analyzes the behavior of a robot in two different conditions—speech and beep—focusing on how participants respond to the robot's actions. In the speech condition, the robot greets participants with polite utterances and engages in conversation, while in the beep condition, it uses a series of beeps to attract attention, omitting any verbal greeting. The results show that in the beep condition, the robot looks back and forth between the desired object and the participant more frequently than in the speech condition.

The analysis categorizes participants' responses based on their attention to the base, proximal, or distal scenes. Attention to the base scene reflects a lack of response to the robot, such as pointing out its limitations or engaging with its physical properties. Proximal scene attention indicates that participants are observing the robot's actions and interpreting them as meaningful, without attributing emotions or intentions to the robot. The study highlights that cultural differences may influence how individuals interpret and respond to robot behavior, emphasizing the need for context-sensitive analysis based on socio-cultural norms. The methodology is grounded in established practices from conversation analytical literature.
