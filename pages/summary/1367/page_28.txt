The paper discusses various studies and findings related to human-robot interaction, emphasizing the importance of humanoid robots as interaction partners. It highlights the concept of co-representation of robotic actions in social contexts, drawing on research such as the work by Fischer et al. (2012) which examines how humanoid robots can engage similarly to humans. The paper also references Stivers et al. (2009) on turn-taking in conversation, suggesting that social communication dynamics apply to interactions with robots.

Adaptive emotional expression in robots, as explored by Tielman et al. (2014), is noted for enhancing the interaction experience, particularly in child-robot scenarios. The role of human-in-the-loop control in robotic systems is discussed through the lens of Tsui et al. (2011), showcasing the need for human oversight in robotic actions. 

Turkle et al. (2006) contribute to the understanding of how children perceive relational artifacts, which informs the design of robots that facilitate emotional and social connections. Theoretical frameworks, such as Walton's (1993) analysis of metaphor in human-robot interactions and Wensveen et al. (2004) on feedback mechanisms, are also presented to underline the complexity of designing effective interaction systems.

The paper further examines the framing of robots either as social agents or machines, influencing children's social behaviors as identified by Westlund et al. (2016). Wykowska et al. (2016) discuss how embodied artificial agents can provide insights into human social cognition, while Złotowski et al. (2018) propose a model of dual anthropomorphism that explores the nuances of human perception of robots.

The overall findings underscore the evolving nature of human-robot interaction and the significance of emotional and social dimensions in designing robots that can effectively engage with humans. The paper was received in November 2019, revised in June 2020, and accepted in September 2020, with publication in October 2021.
