The paper discusses the use of brain imaging techniques to explore how individuals respond to robots in a manner similar to their interactions with humans. Research by Krach et al. (2008) indicated that participants rated their interactions with robots as more enjoyable and human-like based on the robots' human-like features, which was supported by corresponding brain activation patterns. In contrast, Kuz et al. (2015) found that different brain patterns emerged when participants recognized movements from anthropomorphic versus mechanical robots. Wykowska et al. (2016) suggested that responses to embodied artificial agents closely resemble those towards humans, making them suitable for studying human social cognition.

Despite these insights, existing methods do not effectively track anthropomorphism throughout an interaction. The paper proposes a novel method to describe the dynamics of anthropomorphizing behavior over time, emphasizing the need for a model that observes and classifies a wide range of human behaviors towards robots. This approach aims to account for various expressions of anthropomorphism, including gestures and verbal interactions, rather than focusing on isolated behaviors. The ultimate goal is to better understand the complexities of human-robot interactions and the factors influencing anthropomorphizing behavior.
