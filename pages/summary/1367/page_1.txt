In the paper "Tracking Anthropomorphizing Behavior in Human-Robot Interaction," Kerstin Fischer presents a novel methodology for dynamically tracking anthropomorphism in human-robot interactions. Traditionally, studies on this subject have relied on static responses or post-hoc measures, like questionnaires. Fischer's approach enables researchers to observe and analyze the moment-to-moment anthropomorphizing and non-anthropomorphizing responses of individuals during interactions with robots.

The research reveals significant variation not only among participants but also within individual responses, highlighting that people may perceive a robot as human-like in one moment and recognize its mechanical attributes in the next. This variability suggests that existing explanatory models of anthropomorphism may need reconsideration. The findings stem from a case study illustrating the proposed methodology, which could enhance understanding of how humans interact with robots and the underlying cognitive processes involved.

The paper emphasizes the importance of capturing the dynamic nature of these interactions, contributing to the broader discourse on human-robot interaction and the anthropomorphism phenomenon.
