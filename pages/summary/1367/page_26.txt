The collection of references presents a broad exploration of human-robot interaction (HRI) and the factors influencing it. Key studies highlight various dimensions of embodiment, social framing, and interaction strategies that impact how humans perceive and engage with robots. 

Fischer et al. (2012, 2014, 2015) investigate the linguistic and social aspects of HRI, emphasizing the importance of embodiment in initiating interactions and negotiating requests. Fogg and Nass (1997) discuss the psychological effects of robots that flatter, while Forlizzi (2007) examines the transition of robotic products into social entities through ethnographic methods. Fussell et al. (2008) focus on how people anthropomorphize robots, providing insights into social behaviors towards robotic entities.

The studies by Kahn et al. (2015) and Klapper et al. (2014) delve into psychological intimacy and the cognitive mechanisms behind automatic imitation in response to robots. The influence of design aesthetics on social capability attribution is explored by Hegel (2012), and Huang and Mutlu (2013) model humanlike gestures for robots to enhance interaction. 

Research on emotional intelligence and trust (Law et al., 2020) and the effects of physical embodiment (Lee et al., 2006) further enriches the understanding of HRI dynamics. Additionally, the works of Lemaignan et al. (2014) and Levinson (1983, 2006) contribute to the theoretical underpinnings of communication and interaction in social robotics.

Overall, these studies collectively underscore the complex interplay of cognitive, emotional, and social factors that shape human interactions with robotic systems, revealing the nuanced nature of HRI and its implications for design and application in various contexts.
