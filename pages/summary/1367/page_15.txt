The paper examines human-robot interactions, focusing on how participants anthropomorphize robots during these exchanges. It highlights instances where individuals treat robots as social agents, such as expecting them to have their own desires or responding politely. Analyzing a specific interaction with a participant (H101), the paper illustrates how attention is directed either towards the robot (proximal scene) or the social context of the interaction (distal scene).

Initially, the robot attempts to engage by moving its upper part and greeting the participant. However, the participant fails to notice the robot's movement, treating it more like furniture. When the robot greets the participant, the latter briefly acknowledges it but does not maintain eye contact, indicating a lack of adherence to typical human communication norms.

As the interaction progresses, the participant withholds feedback, suggesting a disconnect in joint interaction management. The robot's subsequent beeps are recognized but treated as non-social signals, indicating that the participant perceives the robot's behavior as attention-seeking rather than an invitation for social interaction.

The participant's response to the robot's gestures, such as instructing it to "pick a book," shows a lack of attribution of intention to the robot. However, when offering a "glass of water," the participant acknowledges a human need, indicating a moment of anthropomorphism. The interaction concludes with the participant exaggerating their actions and ultimately walking away, reflecting a blend of responses to proximal, distal, and base scenes throughout the exchange. This analysis underscores the complexities of human-robot interaction and the varying degrees of social engagement participants exhibit towards robots.
