The paper presents a study on anthropomorphizing behavior in human-robot interaction, specifically focusing on how participants respond to a robot's actions over time. Figure 1 illustrates the progression of participant H101’s responses, showcasing a dynamic interaction where the individual shifts attention between three scenes: base, proximal, and distal. This analysis highlights the unique nature of each interaction, revealing that participants exhibit varying behaviors such as attention to the robot, following its actions, and providing assistance.

The study involved analyzing 19 interactions using a coding scheme, with a high interrater reliability of 93.74% agreement and a Cohen’s kappa of 0.874, indicating strong consistency in the coding process. Different participants exhibited distinct behaviors; for instance, participant H111 initially ignored the robot before trying to interpret its actions, while participant H112 began with social engagement before largely disregarding the robot. This research underscores the complexity of human-robot interactions and the importance of context in shaping anthropomorphizing responses.
