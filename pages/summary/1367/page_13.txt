The paper explores the dynamics of anthropomorphizing behavior in human-robot interactions, focusing on the observable behaviors of individuals without making assumptions about the psychological mechanisms behind such behaviors. The proposed methodology aims to track and describe how participants respond to robotic actions over time, which could influence future explanatory models of anthropomorphism.

A case study is presented using data from spontaneous interactions in the lobby of Willow Garage, where participants engaged with a PR2 robot that communicated through speech and gestures. The study involved 19 participants, varying in age and background, who were unaware that their interactions were part of an experiment. The robot's behavior was pre-recorded and controlled remotely by an experimenter to ensure safety.

The analysis of the robot's behavior follows a depiction model, highlighting how specific actions (e.g., gestures and speech) are designed to evoke certain social interactions and intentions. The findings aim to bridge the gap between cognitive processes and observable behaviors, providing insights into human-robot interaction dynamics.
