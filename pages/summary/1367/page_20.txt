The paper presents a study on how participants react to a robot's actions based on different interaction conditions—specifically, verbal greetings versus beep signals. The analysis reveals that when the robot uses verbal communication, responses at the distal level occur more frequently and earlier in the interaction compared to when the robot uses beeps. In the beep condition, some participants do not respond at all to the robot's distal actions, while others do so at varying times, indicating unique behavioral patterns.

Quantitative comparisons using a Chi-Square test demonstrate a significant difference in participant behavior based on the interaction condition, particularly in how they perceive the robot as either a machine or an anthropomorphized entity. The study employs a qualitative methodology grounded in conversation analysis to examine observable behaviors in response to the robot's actions, with a focus on how each behavior is crafted across three levels: base, proximal, and distal scenes.

The findings suggest that the framing of the robot's communication significantly influences how participants engage with and interpret its actions, highlighting the importance of verbal cues in human-robot interaction.
