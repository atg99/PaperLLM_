The paper discusses the concept of permutation-invariance in graph neural networks, specifically focusing on the bi-level aggregation method for processing graphs. The proof establishes that if a graph G is transformed into an isomorphic graph G0 through a permutation, the bi-level aggregation function remains unchanged, demonstrating its permutation-invariance.

Theorem 1 asserts that the bi-level aggregation mapping is permutation-invariant based on its structure, where the low-level aggregation feeds into the high-level aggregation. The proof highlights that the low-level aggregation is also permutation-invariant due to its dependence on the constant structural neighborhood of the nodes, combined with a permutation-invariant aggregation function.

The authors compare their geometric aggregation scheme to existing methods, addressing two key weaknesses of Message Passing Neural Networks (MPNNs): the loss of structural information and the inability to capture long-range dependencies. They argue that their approach explicitly models structural relationships in latent space, contrasting with existing methods that learn implicit structures. Their method can be integrated with others to enhance performance, as it focuses on graph topology while others prioritize feature representation.

Additionally, the paper describes how the proposed scheme captures long-range dependencies by mapping distant yet similar nodes into a latent space neighborhood and leveraging structural information to weigh features effectively during aggregation. This allows for distinguishing informative nodes based on their geometric relationships to the target node.

Finally, the authors present a case study demonstrating the limitations of existing aggregators in distinguishing non-isomorphic graphs and illustrate how their structural neighborhood application enables this distinction, overcoming the challenges faced by traditional methods.
