The paper presents a novel method for distinguishing non-isomorphic graphs through a structural neighborhood approach, enhancing graph convolutional networks (GCNs) by implementing a geometric aggregation scheme called Geom-GCN. The key innovation lies in how nodes within the graphs are aggregated based on their geometric relationships to a central node, allowing different representations to emerge for nodes in distinct graphs.

The authors detail the implementation of Geom-GCN, which includes three essential modules: node embedding, structural neighborhood, and bi-level aggregation function. Node embedding is crucial for capturing connection and distance patterns within the graph. The paper describes three embedding methods—Isomap, Poincare embedding, and struc2vec—resulting in three variants of Geom-GCN. Isomap preserves distance patterns, while Poincare embedding and struc2vec maintain hierarchical and local structural features, respectively.

The structural neighborhood is defined as encompassing both the graph's immediate neighbors and those in the latent space. The paper outlines a method for aligning the average sizes of these neighborhoods, ensuring that they are comparable. A geometric operator is implemented to assess the relative positions of nodes in a two-dimensional space, categorized into four relationships based on their spatial orientation.

Overall, the proposed Geom-GCN framework shows promise for transductive learning in graph-based tasks, with the potential for more sophisticated implementations that could capture richer structural information.
