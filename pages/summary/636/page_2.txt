We identify two main limitations in traditional message-passing neural networks (MPNNs): the inability to distinguish contributions from different nodes during aggregation and the challenge of capturing long-range dependencies in graph structures. Standard MPNNs, such as Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs), aggregate messages from one-hop neighbors, leading to a loss of structural information and difficulties in discriminating non-isomorphic graphs. Furthermore, MPNNs tend to produce similar representations for proximal nodes, making them less effective for disassortative graphs where important information may come from distant nodes.

To address these issues, we leverage concepts from classical neural networks and network geometry. By utilizing a continuous latent space, we propose a geometric aggregation scheme, which allows for the construction of structural neighborhoods and the effective capture of long-range dependencies. Our approach involves mapping the graph into a latent space through node embeddings and employing a bi-level aggregator to update node feature representations while ensuring permutation invariance.

Through our experiments, we demonstrate that the geometric aggregation scheme outperforms existing methods in learning discriminative representations for graph-structured data, particularly in the context of disassortative graphs. This work highlights the potential benefits of integrating geometric principles into graph neural network architectures.
