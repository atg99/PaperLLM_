The paper presents a novel framework for graph neural networks (GNNs) that emphasizes the importance of both structural neighborhoods and a bi-level aggregation scheme. The authors define a structural neighborhood for a node v, denoted as N(v), which includes both a graph-based neighborhood Ng(v) and a latent space neighborhood Ns(v). Ng(v) consists of adjacent nodes in the graph, while Ns(v) includes nodes that are similar to v in the latent space, allowing for the capture of long-range dependencies in disassortative graphs.

A relational operator, represented as , is introduced to characterize the geometric relationships between nodes in the latent space. This operator ensures that each pair of nodes has a unique geometric relationship, facilitating the aggregation process.

The bi-level aggregation scheme incorporates two distinct aggregation functions to update the hidden features of nodes in a neural network layer. The low-level aggregation combines features of nodes with the same geometric relationship into virtual nodes, ensuring permutation invariance through the use of functions like Lp-norm. The high-level aggregation further processes these virtual nodes, extracting structural information and maintaining the identity of each virtual node.

The authors establish that their bi-level aggregation method guarantees permutation invariance, a critical property for GNNs, by providing definitions and lemmas that support this claim. Overall, the proposed approach effectively captures structural information from neighborhoods while ensuring the robustness of the GNN against permutations of nodes.
