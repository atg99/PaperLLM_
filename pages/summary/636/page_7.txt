The paper presents Geom-GCN, a novel graph neural network that implements bi-level aggregation for node classification tasks. The low-level aggregation function is based on the Graph Convolutional Networks (GCN) approach, utilizing a summation of normalized hidden features with a Kronecker delta function to restrict the inclusion of nodes based on their relationship to the target node. The degree of nodes is also factored into the aggregation process. 

In the high-level aggregation, features from all virtual nodes are concatenated across layers, except for the final layer, which employs a mean aggregation function. The overall bi-level aggregation is expressed through a mathematical formulation that incorporates a weight matrix and a ReLU activation function.

The performance of Geom-GCN is validated against state-of-the-art models, GCN and Graph Attention Networks (GAT), across various transductive node-label classification tasks on nine open graph datasets. These datasets include citation networks, webpage datasets, actor co-occurrence networks, and Wikipedia networks, each characterized by different node and edge counts, features, and classes.

The experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of Geom-GCN in handling diverse graph structures, leveraging its unique aggregation mechanism to improve classification performance. The comprehensive dataset statistics provide a foundational understanding of the experimental setup and the significance of the results.
