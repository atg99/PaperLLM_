The paper presents an experimental setup for evaluating three variants of Geom-GCN, utilizing different embedding methods: Isomap (Geom-GCN-I), Poincare (Geom-GCN-P), and struc2vec (Geom-GCN-S). The embedding space is set to two dimensions, and a relationship operator is applied with mean and concatenation as aggregation functions. A hyper-parameter search is conducted across all models, maintaining a consistent search space for fairness. Key hyper-parameters include dropout rates, initial learning rates, weight decay, and the number of hidden units, with Adam optimizer used for training. 

Results indicate that Geom-GCN achieves state-of-the-art classification accuracy across various datasets, with the Isomap embedding showing significant benefits even when preserving only connection and distance patterns. Notably, Geom-GCN-P demonstrates improvement in specific applications like disassortative or hierarchical graphs.

An ablation study assesses the contributions from two neighborhoods defined in graph and latent space. Variants using only one neighborhood are compared against GCN to measure performance improvements. The analysis also introduces an index to quantify homophily in graphs, revealing that citation networks exhibit higher homophily than disassortative networks like WebKB. Overall, the findings underscore the effectiveness of Geom-GCN and the importance of embedding methods in enhancing graph-based learning tasks.
