This paper discusses the performance of Geom-GCN, a graph neural network model, through various analyses of neighborhood aggregation in graph and latent spaces. The findings from Table 4 reveal three key patterns: first, both graph and latent space neighborhoods contribute positively to aggregation; second, neighborhoods in latent space are particularly beneficial in disassortative graphs, indicating effective information capture from disconnected nodes; and third, some variants using a single neighborhood outperform those using two, likely due to the introduction of irrelevant messages that degrade performance. The authors suggest that an attention mechanism could mitigate this issue and plan to explore it in future work.

Additionally, the paper examines the flexibility of embedding space combinations in Geom-GCN, presenting new variants that utilize different combinations of embedding spaces. Results in Table 5 show that certain combinations yield improved performance over those using a single embedding space, while others perform poorly. The authors emphasize the need for an automated framework to select optimal embedding spaces for Geom-GCN.

Finally, the paper addresses the time complexity of Geom-GCN, highlighting its theoretical time complexity as O(n * m * 2^jRj), where n is the input representation size and m is the number of hidden units in the non-linear transformation. The authors compare the real running times of Geom-GCN with other models such as GCN and GAT, underscoring the importance of time complexity in the context of large real-world graphs.
