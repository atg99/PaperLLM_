The paper presents a novel geometric aggregation scheme for graph neural networks, specifically implemented in a model called Geom-GCN, aimed at enhancing transductive learning and node classification on graphs. This approach addresses two key weaknesses of existing methods by extracting more structural information from graphs and enabling aggregation of feature representations from distant nodes through a latent space mapping.

Geom-GCN operates by defining geometric relationships to construct structural neighborhoods in both Euclidean and hyperbolic embedding spaces. Different embedding techniques are employed to ensure that the topology of the graph is preserved for various applications. The paper validates the effectiveness of Geom-GCN through extensive experiments on multiple open datasets, demonstrating that it achieves state-of-the-art results.

The contribution of the paper is threefold: first, it introduces the geometric aggregation scheme that functions in both graph and latent spaces; second, it details the implementation of Geom-GCN for transductive learning; and third, it provides thorough validation and comparative analysis against leading methods on challenging benchmarks.

The geometric aggregation scheme comprises three main components: node embedding, structural neighborhood construction, and bi-level aggregation. Node embedding maps graph nodes into a latent continuous space while preserving the graph's structure. The structural neighborhood identifies adjacent nodes, both in the graph and the latent space, facilitating the aggregation of information. The bi-level aggregation further refines this process by distinguishing between low-level and high-level aggregation methods.

Overall, the proposed Geom-GCN model and its geometric aggregation scheme represent a significant advancement in the field of graph neural networks, enhancing their ability to learn from complex graph structures.
