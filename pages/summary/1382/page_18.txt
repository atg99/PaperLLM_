The paper discusses various loss functions used in image segmentation, particularly focusing on the optimization of boundary precision in segmentations. The loss function is defined as Loss(q, p), which measures the discrepancy between predicted segmentation (p) and ground-truth segmentation (q). The parameter α in the loss function modulates the penalty for larger errors. The distance maps dp and dq represent the unsigned distances to the boundaries of the respective segmentations.

Different formulations of the loss function are presented, including a version that incorporates successive erosions and a circular convolutional kernel for soft thresholding. The authors reference previous work by Caliva et al., who emphasized penalizing errors near object boundaries, and Kim and Ye, who introduced a level-set energy minimization approach for semi-supervised brain MRI segmentation. This approach combines a standard multi-class cross-entropy loss with a regularization term.

Taghanaki et al. also critique the limitations of overlap-based loss functions, advocating for their use as regularizers alongside a weighted cross-entropy loss to address input-output imbalance. Their proposed "Combo Loss" integrates these concepts, balancing contributions from both the cross-entropy and overlap-based terms to improve segmentation performance.
