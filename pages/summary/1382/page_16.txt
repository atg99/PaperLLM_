The paper discusses challenges in image segmentation, particularly in medical imaging, focusing on class imbalance and the effectiveness of various loss functions. It highlights the performance of overlap measure-based methods in mitigating this issue. In Figure 12, the authors compare the behavior of seven loss functions for segmenting large and small objects, using specific parameters from prior research. The findings reveal that while loss values should ideally increase with more false positives and negatives, this is not consistently observed, especially for small objects where only combo loss and focal loss demonstrate a monotonic increase. This inconsistency leads to unstable optimization, with overlap-based functions showing significant fluctuations.

Section 5 reviews optimization function improvements in medical image segmentation, noting that the standard cross-entropy (CE) loss and its weighted variants have been widely utilized. Notably, Milletari et al. (2016) suggested that optimizing convolutional neural networks (CNNs) can outperform traditional CE loss when dealing with small foreground objects in a large background. Additionally, Li et al. (2019c) introduced a regularization term to the CE loss to promote smoother segmentation outputs. Overall, the paper emphasizes the need for effective loss functions to enhance image segmentation accuracy in medical applications.
