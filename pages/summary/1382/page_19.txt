The paper reviews various methods for deep semantic segmentation in both natural and medical images, focusing on loss function adjustments and data augmentation techniques. It highlights the significance of the Dice loss function, where parameters α and β control the contribution of the Dice term and the penalization of false positives and negatives, respectively. To avoid division by zero in calculations, add-one smoothing is employed.

The review emphasizes addressing class imbalance in segmentation tasks, with many methods incorporating weights or penalty terms in the loss function. Some approaches involve cropping around the object of interest to achieve better class balance before performing segmentation, as demonstrated in studies on multiple sclerosis lesions.

The paper also discusses data augmentation as a critical strategy to mitigate overfitting and class imbalance, particularly through image synthesis methods rather than mere modifications of existing images. It mentions the application of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) for generating novel images, which have proven effective across various segmentation tasks.

Notable works in this area include the use of Wasserstein GANs for generating labeled image data, which outperformed traditional geometric transformations in segmentation tasks. Additionally, incorporating semantic consistency into GAN architectures has led to significant performance improvements in semantic segmentation models. The review points out that while GAN-based augmentation techniques have been widely applied in medical imaging, there is an increasing interest in their use for natural image segmentation as well.
