The paper discusses various architectures for semantic segmentation, specifically focusing on SegNet and U-Net. SegNet is characterized by its convolutional structure without fully connected layers, utilizing max-pooling indices for upsampling, which allows it to reconstruct feature maps without additional learning. In contrast, Fully Convolutional Networks (FCN) employ a learning-based upsampling method, integrating corresponding encoder feature maps during the decoding process. The paper also highlights modifications to the original FCN architecture, including variations in depth and the incorporation of attention blocks, which enhance the performance of encoder-decoder networks in semantic segmentation tasks. Overall, the discussion emphasizes the evolution of these architectures and their effectiveness in processing image segmentation challenges.
