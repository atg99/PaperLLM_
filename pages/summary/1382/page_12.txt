The paper discusses advancements in medical image segmentation using deep learning techniques, particularly focusing on the application of convolutional networks and recurrent neural networks. Taghanaki et al. (2018) employed a fully convolutional network within a generative adversarial framework to effectively segment brain tumors from MRI images. This approach is supported by a range of studies that demonstrate the success of adversarial learning in medical image segmentation.

The paper also highlights the role of sequenced models, specifically recurrent neural networks (RNNs) and long short-term memory (LSTM) networks, which are designed to handle sequential data. RNNs have been utilized in medical image analysis to capture temporal dependencies in image sequences. For instance, Bai et al. (2018) combined fully convolutional networks with RNNs to enhance segmentation by integrating spatial and temporal information. Similarly, Gao et al. (2018) improved segmentation performance of brain MRI slices using LSTM and CNN, while Li et al. (2019a) employed U-Net for initial segmentation followed by LSTM refinement for pancreas segmentation in 3D CT scans.

The paper further explores optimization function improvements, particularly the design of new loss functions aimed at enhancing segmentation accuracy. Cross entropy is identified as the most commonly used loss function in image segmentation tasks. It evaluates pixel-wise predictions against ground truth vectors, with specific formulations provided for binary and multi-class segmentation scenarios. The formulation highlights the importance of effective loss functions in achieving better inference-time segmentation outcomes.
