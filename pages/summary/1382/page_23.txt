The paper discusses deep semantic segmentation methods applied to both natural and medical images, focusing on key metrics for evaluating segmentation performance. It outlines the Dice coefficient and the Jaccard index, which are used to measure the overlap between predicted and ground truth binary masks. The relationship between these two metrics is established, with Jaccard being a function of Dice.

An illustrative example is provided in Figure 14, highlighting a 5x5 overlap scenario with ground truth and predicted masks. The black pixels represent the object to be segmented, and the confusion matrix is utilized to derive several performance metrics, including precision, recall, specificity, pixel-wise accuracy, Dice coefficient, and Jaccard index.

The paper also reviews popular datasets for semantic segmentation of natural images, which encompass a variety of scenes such as indoor and outdoor environments, common objects, and urban landscapes. For a more detailed examination of these datasets, the authors reference the work of Lateef and Ruichek from 2019.
