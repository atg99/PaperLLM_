The paper provides a comprehensive review of deep semantic segmentation techniques applied to both natural and medical images. It discusses various loss functions used in segmentation tasks, specifically comparing cross entropy and Dice losses. The findings indicate that Dice loss is more effective for large objects, achieving a score of 0.96 with minimal false negatives, while struggling with smaller objects, yielding a score of 0.66. In contrast, the cross entropy loss remains consistent, scoring 0.83 for both scenarios.

The authors reference Chen et al. (2019), who integrated traditional active contour energy minimization into convolutional neural networks (CNNs) using a combined loss function that accounts for length and region. This approach aims to refine segmentation accuracy by optimizing contour predictions.

Additionally, the paper highlights the work of Karimi and Salcudean (2019), who developed a loss function based on Hausdorff distance to enhance the performance of segmentation models. This function incorporates both traditional distance metrics and the Dice loss, allowing for a nuanced assessment of segmentation quality.

Overall, the review underscores the importance of selecting appropriate loss functions for achieving optimal segmentation outcomes across varying object sizes and types in image analysis.
