The paper discusses advancements in multi-task models applied to medical images, highlighting various architectures and their applications. Chaichulee et al. extended the VGG16 model for patient detection and skin segmentation, demonstrating robustness across diverse conditions in neonatal intensive care unit images. He et al. developed a U-Net-like architecture for concurrent segmentation of thoracic organs and global slice classification in CT scans. Ke et al. introduced a multi-task U-Net to address object separation, instance detection, and pixel-wise labeling in food microscopy images. Additional multi-task models have been employed for detecting manipulated faces and diagnosing breast biopsy images.

The Mask R-CNN framework has been utilized for various segmentation tasks in medical imaging, including tracking cell migration, detecting nuclei, diagnosing oral diseases, segmenting neuropathic ulcers, and labeling ribs in chest X-rays. It has also been adapted for 3D volumes to enhance lung nodule detection and breast lesion categorization.

The paper further explores segmentation evaluation metrics, emphasizing pixel-wise and overlap-based measures. For binary segmentation, a confusion matrix is constructed to derive metrics such as precision, recall (sensitivity), specificity, and overall accuracy. The precision, recall, specificity, and accuracy formulas are provided, along with the introduction of the Sørensen–Dice coefficient and Jaccard index as popular overlap-based measures for assessing segmentation performance.
