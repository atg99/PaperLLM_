The paper discusses various potential research directions for deep semantic segmentation in both natural and medical images. It highlights the importance of integrating prior knowledge into models, particularly in medical image analysis, to improve interpretability. The challenges posed by diverse imaging modalities, noise, and limited data in medical imaging are emphasized, suggesting a need for research on the risks of using non-medical models for medical tasks.

Additionally, the creation of larger publicly available medical imaging datasets is advocated to facilitate accurate comparisons and improvements in segmentation techniques. The utilization of physics-based imaging simulators is proposed as a solution to the lack of annotated medical data, allowing for the training and augmentation of datasets.

The paper also notes the difficulties posed by the larger file sizes of medical images, which require processing in patches. This limitation hinders the ability of segmentation models to capture spatial relationships effectively. Thus, developing architectures that can handle these challenges is identified as a key area for future research.

Finally, the exploration of reinforcement learning techniques for semantic segmentation is suggested, as they may help models better mimic human object delineation by preserving local spatial information. Overall, the review provides a comprehensive overview of the current state and future directions for deep semantic segmentation in natural and medical images.
