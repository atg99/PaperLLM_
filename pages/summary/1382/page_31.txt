The paper provides a comprehensive review of deep semantic segmentation techniques applied to both natural and medical images. It highlights a variety of architectures and methods developed in recent years, emphasizing their evolution and effectiveness in image segmentation tasks. 

Key contributions discussed include the Recurrent Residual U-Net, which demonstrates significant potential in medical image segmentation, and the Gated Feedback Refinement Network, which enhances dense image labeling. The paper also references advancements such as SegNet and Mask R-CNN, which have been pivotal in addressing segmentation challenges in various domains.

The review covers biologically inspired learning alternatives to traditional backpropagation methods, particularly in recurrent neural networks, and explores the implications of these approaches for improving segmentation accuracy. Loss functions such as the Lovász-Softmax loss and IoU loss are examined for their roles in optimizing segmentation performance.

Overall, the authors compile a diverse range of methodologies that illustrate the progress and ongoing challenges in the field of deep semantic segmentation, with a focus on enhancing both natural and medical image analysis.
