The paper discusses the segmentation of images into nonoverlapping regions, a crucial aspect of medical image analysis. It highlights the significant advancements in deep learning techniques applied to various medical imaging modalities, including X-ray, MRI, PET, CT, and ultrasound. Researchers have focused on addressing challenges such as gradient issues in deep models and optimizing model performance through new functions.

The review categorizes existing literature on deep learning-based semantic segmentation into three main types: region-based, fully convolutional network-based, and weakly supervised methods. It also summarizes various RGB-D datasets and traditional machine learning techniques in the context of deep learning architectures. Several surveys, including those by Guo et al. and Hu et al., are mentioned for their contributions to understanding deep learning in both natural and medical image segmentation.

Key contributions of this review include a comprehensive analysis of semantic segmentation research, covering both 2D and volumetric medical images. The literature is organized into six categories based on contributions: architectural improvements, optimization function improvements, data synthesis improvements, weakly supervised models, sequenced models, and multi-task models. The paper also provides a timeline of influential papers in these areas, underscoring the evolution of methods and techniques in image segmentation.
