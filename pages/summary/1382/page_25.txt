The paper provides a comprehensive review of deep semantic segmentation techniques applied to both natural and medical images, highlighting various approaches and improvements reported in the literature. A significant part of the review includes a detailed summary table (Table 3) that categorizes several medical image segmentation studies based on their proposed improvements and the training data used.

The table outlines the authors, types of improvements—such as architecture optimization, multi-task models, and weakly supervised optimization—and specifies the training data modality, site, and patient count. For instance, Ronneberger et al. (2015) focused on architecture optimization with data from microscopy images of Drosophilia and HeLa cells. Other notable studies include Milletari et al. (2016), who also optimized architecture using MRI data for prostate segmentation, and Chaichulee et al. (2017), who implemented multi-task models on a large dataset of skin images.

The summary emphasizes the diversity in datasets and methodologies, ranging from conventional imaging techniques like MRI and CT to advanced methods such as synthesis-based and sequenced models. This highlights the ongoing evolution in the field, showcasing how different strategies and data types contribute to advancements in medical image segmentation. The review serves as a valuable resource for researchers seeking to understand the landscape of deep learning applications in semantic segmentation for medical imagery.
