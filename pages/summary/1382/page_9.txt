This review paper discusses advancements in deep semantic segmentation techniques for both natural and medical images, highlighting the effectiveness of the DeepLabV3+ architecture. The encoder module utilizes atrous (dilated) convolution to capture multi-scale contextual information, while the decoder enhances segmentation accuracy along object boundaries.

The paper also explores methods for reducing computational complexity in image segmentation networks. Various approaches have been proposed, including tensor factorization, channel pruning, and the application of sparsity to connections. Notably, strategies like spatial and context paths have been developed to manage the high computational costs associated with high-resolution feature maps in U-shaped architectures.

The review mentions innovative techniques for optimizing segmentation networks, such as hierarchical neural architecture search, which has shown to yield results comparable to state-of-the-art benchmarks on datasets like PASCAL VOC 2012 and Cityscapes. Additionally, depth-wise separable convolutions have been highlighted for their efficiency in reducing parameters in segmentation models.

Finally, the paper discusses the role of attention mechanisms in semantic image segmentation, emphasizing their capacity to select and localize the most salient features from the input data. Overall, the review encapsulates significant strides in the field, paving the way for more efficient and effective segmentation methodologies.
