The paper discusses advancements in medical image segmentation, highlighting trends in challenges since 2007 and the evolution of methodologies. It notes the rise in medical image segmentation challenges, with a focus on multi-modal data. 

The section on sequenced models emphasizes their application in segmenting temporal data like videos and 3D medical datasets. It argues that processing volumetric data with 3D convolutions may capture geometrical information better than using 2D sequenced models that analyze slices individually. This suggests a future exploration of the effectiveness of these two approaches.

The paper also critiques the optimization functions used in medical image segmentation. Researchers predominantly use cross-entropy loss functions alongside distance or overlap-based functions. It points out potential issues with using only overlap-based loss functions, especially in cases of class imbalance, which can lead to gradient vanishing and problematic optimization. The authors advocate for integrating cross-entropy with features that capture contour distance, suggesting a need for newer loss functions that combine these elements effectively. Additionally, they propose investigating automated loss function searches akin to neural architecture search, indicating a pathway for future research in this domain.
