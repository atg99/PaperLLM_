The paper discusses various loss functions used in segmentation tasks, emphasizing their mathematical formulations and applications. 

First, the Jaccard metric, also known as the Intersection over Union (IoU), is presented, defined by the formula IoU = TP / (TP + FP + FN), where TP represents true positives, FP false positives, and FN false negatives. The authors note that the Dice coefficient (DC) is always greater than or equal to IoU.

The Dice Loss (DL) is introduced as a way to utilize the DC as a loss function, expressed as DL(p, ˆp) = 2⟨p, ˆp⟩ / (∥p∥1 + ∥ˆp∥1), where p is the ground truth segmentation and ˆp is the predicted segmentation.

Next, the Tversky Loss (TL) is discussed, which generalizes DL by allowing for different weights for false positives and false negatives, defined as TL(p, ˆp) = ⟨p, ˆp⟩ / (⟨p, ˆp⟩ + β(1−p, ˆp) + (1−β)(p, 1−ˆp)). When β is set to 0.5, TL simplifies to DL.

Lastly, the Exponential Logarithmic Loss is introduced, proposed by Wong et al. to enhance segmentation accuracy for small structures. This loss function combines a weighted sum of exponential logarithmic Dice loss (Leld) and weighted exponential cross-entropy loss (Lwece). The formulations for Leld and Lwece are provided, highlighting the use of parameters γD and γCE to control the non-linear effects of the respective losses.

Overall, the paper provides a comprehensive overview of these loss functions, illustrating their significance in improving segmentation performance in various scenarios.
