The paper discusses advancements in deep semantic image segmentation, particularly focusing on the evolution of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) applied to both natural and medical images. It categorizes improvements into various sections, starting with network architectural advancements.

In Section 2, the authors highlight the transition from shallow networks and superpixel segmentation methods to end-to-end trainable deep neural networks. They emphasize the significance of fully convolutional networks (FCNs), introduced by Long et al. in 2015, which enabled pixel-wise labeling through up-sampling of output activation maps. A key innovation in FCNs is the fusion of deeper and shallower layer outputs, which helps maintain contextual spatial information as data progresses through the network.

The paper systematically reviews contributions to semantic segmentation, differentiating between non-medical and medical datasets, thus providing a comprehensive overview of the state-of-the-art methodologies and their applications.
