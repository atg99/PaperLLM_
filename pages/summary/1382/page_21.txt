The paper discusses the challenges and advancements in deep semantic segmentation of both natural and medical images, particularly focusing on weakly supervised methods applied to medical images due to the scarcity of richly annotated datasets. It highlights various strategies to leverage weak supervision, such as Kervadec et al.'s differentiable loss function that enhances segmentation performance for cardiac images with reduced computational demand. Other notable contributions include Afshari et al.'s use of bounding box annotations for segmenting lesions in PET scans, and Mirikharaji et al.'s development of spatially adaptive weight maps for noisy pixel-level annotations in skin lesion segmentation. Taghanaki et al. proposed learning spatial masks from image-level labels, while Peng et al. introduced a CNN training approach with discrete constraints. Furthermore, Perone and Cohen-Adad expanded the semi-supervised mean teacher approach for MRI segmentation, demonstrating its effectiveness in small data scenarios, and Perone et al. showed improvements in model generalization through unsupervised domain adaptation.

The paper also covers multi-task learning, which enhances machine learning efficiency by simultaneously addressing related tasks. It notes that multi-task models can significantly improve performance, as evidenced by Bischke et al.'s cascaded multi-task loss for building footprint segmentation and He et al.'s Mask R-CNN, which integrates object detection with segmentation tasks. Mask R-CNN has been widely adopted across various applications, including mapping, surgery robot detection, climate change analysis, satellite imagery conversion, image forgery detection, and tree canopy segmentation.
