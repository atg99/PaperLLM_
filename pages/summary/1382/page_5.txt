The paper provides a comprehensive review of deep semantic segmentation methods applied to both natural and medical images. It begins by discussing the efficiency of fully convolutional networks (FCNs) in making dense predictions for pixel-wise tasks such as semantic segmentation. The FCN architecture is highlighted for its ability to upsample and fuse predictions effectively, as demonstrated by the work of Long et al. in 2015.

The review then transitions to encoder-decoder segmentation networks, notably SegNet, introduced by Noh et al. in 2015. SegNet's unique approach involves using pooling indices from the encoder's max-pooling steps to achieve non-linear upsampling in the decoder, thereby mapping low-resolution features back to full input resolution for pixel classification.

Further advancements are discussed, including the U-Net architecture proposed by Ronneberger et al. in 2015, which combines a contracting path for context capture with a symmetric expanding path for precise localization. The inclusion of skip connections in U-Net addresses issues related to vanishing gradients and enhances model accuracy.

Milletari et al. in 2016 introduced V-Net, which incorporates residual connections and adapts the architecture for volumetric images by replacing 2D operations with 3D ones. They also focused on optimizing for the Dice segmentation metric.

Lastly, Jégou et al. in 2017 developed a segmentation version of DenseNet, adapting the U-Net-like encoder-decoder framework to improve segmentation performance. The paper emphasizes the evolution of these architectures and their contributions to the field of semantic segmentation.
