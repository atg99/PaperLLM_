The paper provides a comprehensive review of deep semantic segmentation methods applied to both natural and medical images. It highlights the diverse categories of contributions and various medical imaging modalities, emphasizing the challenges posed by smaller dataset sizes in medical imaging compared to natural images. This disparity complicates benchmarking efforts, as evidenced by the low representation of modalities such as PET, OCT, and topogram.

The review discusses the architectural trends in recent segmentation models, noting the prevalence of encoder-decoder networks that utilize richer feature representations, albeit with increased memory and computational demands. Future research directions include optimizing data transfer through skip connections and exploring architectural innovations such as atrous convolutions with feature pyramid pooling modules. The potential for developing new layers that extend beyond traditional convolutional methods is also suggested. Additionally, the paper points to the utility of neural architecture search techniques to identify optimal configurations for deep neural networks in segmentation tasks.
