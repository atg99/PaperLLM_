The focus is on unsupervised and self-supervised techniques that allow for the control of image attributes without the need for paired data. Härkönen et al. introduced a method using PCA to identify latent directions that correspond to specific attributes, enabling image synthesis control. In contrast, Jahanian et al. optimized trajectories in a self-supervised manner, learning to adjust latent codes based on the distance between generated images and target edits.

The paper also highlights closed-form solutions that directly obtain interpretable directions without the need for extensive training. The Semantic Factorization (SeFa) method leverages singular value decomposition of pre-trained GAN weights to discover significant latent directions that alter image output. 

Furthermore, the concept of disentangled directions is emphasized, where the goal is to manipulate multiple attributes independently. Shen et al. proposed a method for generating orthogonal directions to ensure that edits on one attribute do not interfere with others. This involves projecting directions in latent space to maintain independence among attributes. The paper notes that while methods based on StyleGAN and its variants have improved attribute separability, some attributes remain entangled, prompting the introduction of the style space (Sspace) by Wu et al., which allows for more precise local modifications.

The implications of solving the inversion problem are significant, as it enables various applications including image manipulation, interpolation, restoration, style transfer, and even adversarial defense, without reliance on dense-labeled datasets.
