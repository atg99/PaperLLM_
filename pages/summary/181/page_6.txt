The optimization process involves back-propagation and iterative updates to the latent code, allowing for fine-tuning until the generated image closely resembles the target image. 

In addition to pixel-wise accuracy, optimization-based methods often consider other factors such as perceptual quality and the ability to maintain semantic coherence in the generated image. This is critical for applications where image fidelity and interpretability are paramount. 

The paper also discusses hybrid approaches that combine learning-based and optimization-based methods. These methods typically start with an encoder to generate an initial latent code and then refine it using an optimization algorithm. This two-step process leverages the strengths of both methodologies, enhancing reconstruction quality and generalizability.

In summary, the paper explores the transformation from Pspace to PNspace using PCA whitening for better distribution normalization, the various methods of GAN inversion, and the distinct characteristics of learning-based and optimization-based approaches. The discussion emphasizes the importance of encoder design and the trade-offs involved in achieving high-quality image reconstruction from latent codes.
