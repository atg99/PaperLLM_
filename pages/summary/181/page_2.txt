This paper presents a comprehensive survey on GAN inversion methods, highlighting their algorithms and applications. It is the first of its kind, offering a detailed review of various GAN inversion techniques, comparing their properties and performance, and addressing challenges and future research directions.

The paper is structured to first define the problem of GAN inversion, emphasizing the need for the latent code derived from a given image to achieve two main objectives: accurately reconstructing the original image and enabling effective downstream tasks. The authors formulate the inversion problem mathematically, mapping real images back to a latent space of a pretrained GAN model. This involves finding a latent vector that minimizes the difference between the generated and original images, which is a nonconvex optimization problem.

Sections of the paper detail various pretrained GAN models, the evaluation of GAN inversion performance based on perceptual quality and inversion accuracy, and the characteristics of different latent spaces. The authors explore different approaches to solving the inversion problem, including learning-based, optimization-based, and hybrid methods. Learning-based methods often struggle with faithful image reconstruction, while optimization-based approaches provide better quality but at a higher computational cost. Hybrid methods attempt to balance these trade-offs.

The paper also discusses related tasks that involve learning inverse mappings of GAN models and differentiates them from traditional GAN inversion methods. Applications and future directions for GAN inversion research are addressed, underscoring the ongoing challenges in achieving both high reconstruction quality and efficient inference time.
