The paper discusses various methods for evaluating edited face images, focusing on the preservation of face identity when subjected to different permutations of edits. It highlights the limitations of these methods when applied beyond facial images. Subjective metrics, such as mean opinion score (MOS) and difference mean opinion score (DMOS), are also explored, emphasizing their reliance on human judgment, which can introduce bias and variance, along with high costs.

The section on GAN inversion methods introduces various latent spaces associated with GAN models, particularly StyleGANs, which have become prominent in image synthesis. The choice of latent space is crucial for effective image editing and reconstruction. It discusses several latent spaces including Zspace, Wspace, W+ space, Sspace, and Pspace, each with distinct properties and applications.

Zspace is the foundational latent space used across all GANs, but its constraints limit the representation capabilities. In contrast, Wspace and W+ space, derived from Zspace, offer greater expressiveness and disentanglement, though W+ sacrifices some editability for improved reconstruction. Sspace provides enhanced spatial disentanglement, while Pspace addresses issues related to high-dimensional Gaussian distributions in latent space. The paper concludes by emphasizing the ongoing exploration of these latent spaces to optimize image editing and generation tasks.
