The paper discusses the challenges and considerations surrounding transparency and fairness in AI research. Participants highlighted that while transparency is essential for publishing research, practical concerns such as costs, time, and the commercialization of data often hinder open practices. The use of proprietary data complicates reproducibility, as external researchers cannot access the original datasets. Clients may impose restrictions on research publications, creating a tension between openness and commercial interests.

To enhance reproducibility, using public or synthetic datasets was suggested. Participants emphasized the importance of client transparency regarding AI application claims, advocating for accurate representations of AI recommendations. Some participants argued that understanding risk is more crucial than transparency itself, suggesting that explainability becomes a concern primarily when failures occur.

Fairness in AI was interpreted in various ways, with some participants advocating for training models on diverse data to mitigate bias. Different methods were discussed, including downsampling over-represented groups and adjusting data weights, though these approaches have their limitations. Incomplete data availability and the costs associated with data acquisition were also noted as barriers to achieving fairness. 

The paper suggests that user judgment should play a role in interpreting AI results, particularly when biases may affect recommendations. Participants also pointed out the need for context understanding and the relevance of language used in historical data to ensure fairness in AI applications. Lastly, the distinction between non-discrimination and the removal of identifying information was acknowledged as a critical aspect of fairness in AI systems.
