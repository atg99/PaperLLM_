The collection of references highlights a range of studies and papers focused on the intersection of artificial intelligence (AI) and ethics. Key themes include the implementation of responsible AI, the ethical implications of AI technologies, and the challenges organizations face in aligning AI practices with ethical standards.

Ryan et al. (2021) contrast academic discussions on AI ethics with real-world organizational practices, suggesting a gap that needs addressing. Sanderson et al. (2023) and (2024) explore the tensions and trade-offs inherent in implementing ethical AI, emphasizing the complexity of ethical decision-making in AI applications. Schwartz's work on basic values provides a theoretical foundation for understanding individual values that inform ethical considerations in AI (Schwartz et al., 2012).

Seppälä (2021) addresses the transition from ethical principles to governance in AI, indicating a need for structured frameworks to ensure ethical compliance. Shneiderman (2020, 2021) advocates for guidelines that bridge ethics and practical applications, stressing the importance of human-centered design in AI systems.

The literature also touches on participatory design, community engagement, and the role of values in software engineering (Simonsen & Robertson, 2013; Whittle et al., 2021). Additionally, there is recognition of the limits of ethical principles in AI, prompting a focus on the tensions that arise in practice (Whittlestone et al., 2019).

Overall, this body of work underscores the critical need for ongoing dialogue between ethical theory and practical application in AI, advocating for collaborative approaches that consider diverse stakeholder perspectives.
