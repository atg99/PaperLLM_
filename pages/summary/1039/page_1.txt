The paper addresses the gap between high-level AI ethics principles and their practical application in the design and development of AI systems. It draws on semi-structured interviews with researchers and engineers at the Commonwealth Scientific and Industrial Research Organisation (CSIRO) in Australia. The study focuses on how their practices align with the Australian Government's AI ethics principles, which include privacy protection, reliability, transparency, fairness, accountability, and human well-being.

The authors highlight that while there is a growing consensus on AI ethics principles, there remains a discrepancy between these principles and actionable techniques for developers. They emphasize the need for governance mechanisms that complement ethics principles to ensure responsible AI development. The insights from the interviews reveal various tensions and trade-offs among the principles, providing a nuanced understanding of the challenges faced by practitioners.

Overall, the paper offers practical suggestions for implementing AI ethics principles and enhancing support structures, underscoring the importance of integrating ethical considerations into the development process of AI technologies.
