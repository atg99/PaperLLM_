The paper presents AraBERT, a model specifically designed for Arabic natural language processing tasks, demonstrating its superiority over mBERT and other state-of-the-art models across various datasets. The results indicate that both versions of AraBERT outperform mBERT in sentiment analysis, named entity recognition, and question answering tasks. Notably, AraBERT, despite being trained on Modern Standard Arabic (MSA), effectively handles dialects previously unseen during training.

In sentiment analysis, AraBERT achieved higher accuracy scores on multiple datasets, with notable results such as 96.2% on the HARD dataset and 92.6% on the ASTD dataset. In the named entity recognition task, AraBERTv0.1 improved the F1 score to 84.2, surpassing the previous Bi-LSTM-CRF model. The analysis suggests that the model's performance was influenced by the data size, vocabulary size, and pre-segmentation techniques that accommodate Arabic's complexities.

For question answering, AraBERT showed an increase in F1 score, although exact match scores were lower, with many errors being minor semantic variations. The findings support the assertion that monolingual models like AraBERT can achieve better performance than multilingual models trained on less targeted data, such as mBERT.

The paper concludes that AraBERT establishes a new benchmark for Arabic NLP tasks, is smaller in size than mBERT, and aims to serve as a baseline for future models. Future work includes the development of a version of AraBERT that does not rely on external tokenizers and further training for improved dialect understanding.
