The paper addresses the issue of vocabulary redundancy in tokenization for Arabic language processing. It demonstrates the use of Farasa for word segmentation into stems, prefixes, and suffixes, which allows for the creation of a more efficient subword vocabulary using SentencePiece. This method produced a vocabulary of approximately 60K tokens, with the final vocabulary size being 64K to accommodate further pre-training.

The paper details the fine-tuning process for AraBERT across three tasks: sequence classification, named entity recognition (NER), and question answering (QA). For sequence classification, the model uses the final hidden state of the special "[CLS]" token, combined with a feed-forward layer and Softmax for class probabilities. In NER, the IOB2 format is utilized to label tokens, treating it as a multi-class classification problem. For QA, the model predicts a "start" and "end" token to identify the answer span in a passage.

The evaluation of AraBERT includes its performance on three Arabic language understanding tasks: sentiment analysis, NER, and QA, compared against the multilingual version of BERT and other state-of-the-art models. The sentiment analysis component is evaluated on several datasets, including HARD, ASTD, ArSenTD-Lev, LABR, and AJGT, which encompass various genres and dialects. Previous sentiment analysis methods relied on lexicons and neural network architectures, with AraBERT aiming to improve upon these foundations.
