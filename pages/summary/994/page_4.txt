This paper discusses advancements in artificial intelligence (AI) applications across various medical fields, focusing on classification tasks for skin lesions, ophthalmology, and medical video analysis.

In dermatology, AI algorithms are being developed to differentiate between benign and malignant skin lesions, including melanomas and nevi, as well as carcinomas and seborrheic keratoses. This research has expanded to cover a wider range of skin conditions, including non-neoplastic lesions and incorporating metadata such as patient demographics. The integration of these AI systems into clinical workflows could enhance the detection of malignancies and monitor lesion changes over time.

Ophthalmology has seen significant AI advancements, particularly in diagnosing conditions like diabetic retinopathy and glaucoma through fundus imaging and optical coherence tomography. Convolutional neural networks (CNNs) have shown the capability to achieve diagnostic accuracy comparable to human physicians, with potential applications for screening both ocular and non-ocular diseases. This could revolutionize eye exams and enhance care delivery in underserved areas.

In the realm of medical video analysis, AI is being utilized to improve surgical procedures through real-time contextual awareness, skills assessment, and training. Early studies focus on detecting surgical tools and actions to evaluate surgeon performance and recognize surgical phases. Additionally, computer vision (CV) technologies are being explored for ambient intelligence applications in hospitals, enhancing patient monitoring, documentation, and workflow optimization. Outside clinical settings, CV can assist in monitoring daily activities for at-risk populations, such as the elderly, and provide tools for rehabilitation and remote physiological measurements.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the transformative potential of AI and computer vision in enhancing diagnostic capabilities, improving patient care, and optimizing healthcare workflows.
