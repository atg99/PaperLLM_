The paper discusses the integration of computer vision (CV) within various medical modalities, emphasizing its applications and benefits across different specialties. In otolaryngology, convolutional neural networks (CNNs) enhance patient management through smartphone-mounted devices. Hematology and serology benefit from AI-integrated microscopes for diagnosing conditions and counting blood cells. Gastroenterology showcases advanced video-based CNNs for endoscopic procedures, including cancer screening and infection detection, as well as innovative devices like smart toilets for at-home diagnostics.

In radiology, CV has evolved into a dedicated research field, particularly with chest X-ray analysis, where nearly one million annotated images have been compiled. This has facilitated rapid development of models for disease classification and detection, including COVID-19. The paper highlights ongoing advancements in areas such as image reconstruction and automated reporting.

Cardiology utilizes deep learning in cardiac imaging, particularly echocardiography, which is cost-effective and radiation-free. Deep learning models, such as EchoNet, have surpassed human performance in various diagnostic tasks and have led to the open-sourcing of annotated echocardiogram videos for broader research collaboration.

Pathology is also experiencing a transformation with the use of digital histopathology, where deep learning aids in diagnostics and prognostics. New imaging technologies allow for high-resolution tissue scanning, enabling more accurate analysis and the potential to combine various diagnostic modalities.

In dermatology, deep learning techniques have shown promise in differentiating between malignant and benign lesions, matching or exceeding the performance of certified dermatologists. Overall, the integration of CV into medical practices presents significant potential for improving diagnostic accuracy and efficiency across multiple fields.
