Hyperparameters for the model were tuned, with temperature values ranging from 1.0 to 10.0 and lambda values from 0 to 1.0.

The experimental design involved four male undergraduate subjects who recorded simultaneous speech and EEG signals while vocalizing English vowels and words, both in quiet and noisy environments. The EEG data was gathered using a 32-channel cap, and preprocessing included filtering and artifact removal using EEGLab's ICA toolbox. A total of 75 minutes of vowel data and 40 minutes of word data were collected, with recordings made on different days.

For feature extraction, EEG signals were sampled at 1000Hz, and five statistical features were derived, resulting in 155 features. Speech signals were sampled at 16KHz, with Mel-frequency cepstral coefficients (MFCCs) extracted to produce 39 features. Non-linear methods, specifically Kernel Principal Component Analysis, were employed for dimensionality reduction. The EEG features were reduced to 39 dimensions, with further delta features computed for enhanced representation.

Results indicated that recognition accuracy, defined as the ratio of correct predictions to total predictions, varied across different datasets. The performance of the models was evaluated based on validation and test accuracies, showing the effectiveness of combining acoustic and EEG features for improved automatic speech recognition.
