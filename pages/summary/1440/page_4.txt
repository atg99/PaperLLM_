This paper presents a novel approach to automatic speech recognition (ASR) using deep learning models that leverage only EEG features for character or word level prediction. The study demonstrates that EEG can effectively compensate for the performance loss of ASR systems caused by background noise. The validation accuracy for various datasets is reported, showing that the model achieves high test accuracy rates, notably 96.36% for vowels in the absence of noise when utilizing a full set of 31 EEG channels combined with MFCC features. A reduced feature set, using only 4 EEG channels, still yielded a commendable accuracy of 93%.

The research highlights the effectiveness of distillation training, which improved ASR accuracy when applied to EEG features, achieving optimal results with specific hyperparameters (temperature equal to 2 and lambda equal to 0.2). The paper further explores the contributions of individual EEG sensors, identifying channels T7, T8, Fc5, and P7 as having the most significant impact on test accuracy.

The findings suggest that EEG-based speech recognition has the potential to enhance technology accessibility for individuals with speech difficulties. Overall, this work represents a significant advancement in utilizing EEG for speech recognition, especially in challenging conditions with background noise.
