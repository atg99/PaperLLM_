The paper addresses the challenge of detecting small objects in object detection frameworks, specifically focusing on the limitations of output heatmaps in covering grid points. It identifies that simply enlarging the proposal area can lead to redundancy and decreased accuracy in small object detection. To overcome this, the authors propose an extended region mapping approach that allows for the redefinition of the output heatmap's representation area without enlarging the proposal area. This method ensures that target grid points of positive proposals are covered effectively.

The implementation details outline the network configuration, using ResNets as the backbone and a Region Proposal Network (RPN) to generate candidate regions. The training process involves specific settings for anchors, sampling, and optimization methods, with a focus on stability during multi-GPU training. The inference stage includes generating RoIs, classifying them, and applying non-maximum suppression to filter out duplicate detections.

Experiments were conducted on Pascal VOC and COCO datasets, with an ablation study highlighting the impact of grid point selection on detection accuracy. The results indicate that increasing the number of supervised grid points enhances detection performance, with a notable improvement observed when transitioning from 2 to 9 grid points.

In summary, the paper presents a novel approach to improve the accuracy of small object detection by modifying the relationship between output heatmaps and image regions, supported by empirical evidence demonstrating the effectiveness of this method.
