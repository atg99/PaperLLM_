This paper presents a detailed analysis of various feature fusion methods and their impact on object detection performance, specifically focusing on Average Precision (AP) metrics. The experiments demonstrate that different fusion techniques lead to notable improvements. Among these, second order feature fusion yields the highest performance, achieving a 0.7% increase in AP compared to the baseline without fusion.

The paper also introduces an extended region mapping strategy, which outperforms the traditional method of simply enlarging proposal boxes. The latter method shows an increase in performance for large objects but negatively impacts small object detection. In contrast, the extended mapping maintains performance across both large and small objects, resulting in a 1.2% improvement in overall AP.

Comparative experiments on the Pascal VOC dataset reveal that the proposed Grid R-CNN outperforms established two-stage detectors such as R-FCN and FPN by significant margins. Specifically, Grid R-CNN demonstrates a 3.6% improvement over FPN and 9.7% over R-FCN when evaluated using a COCO-style criterion.

Further validation on the COCO dataset shows consistent enhancements across various backbones, with Grid R-CNN improving AP by 2.1% over the Faster R-CNN baseline. Notably, the results indicate significant performance gains for medium and large objects.

Finally, evaluations on the COCO test-dev set show that Grid R-CNN achieves competitive results, outperforming Mask R-CNN without additional annotations. The paper concludes by noting potential for further improvements through integration with existing techniques like scaling and cascading methods, indicating that the Grid R-CNN framework has room for enhancement in future research.
