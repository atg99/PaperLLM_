The paper discusses an encoder-decoder architecture commonly used in sequence processing tasks, such as machine translation and time series prediction. The encoder takes an input sequence X and maps each element xt to a hidden state ht through a function f1, which can be an RNN or its variants. The decoder, initialized with the final hidden state hT of the encoder, generates an output sequence Y using a function f2, which also may be an RNN with additional layers tailored to the specific task.

A significant limitation of traditional encoder-decoder models is that they compress all input information into a fixed-length vector (hT), which can lead to performance degradation, especially with longer input sequences. To address this issue, the paper introduces an attention mechanism that allows the model to dynamically focus on relevant parts of the input sequence during decoding. This enhancement enables the model to generate a sequence of contextually relevant vectors rather than relying solely on a single fixed-length representation.

The encoder is further improved by implementing a bidirectional RNN that captures information from both preceding and following elements in the input sequence, enhancing the model's ability to understand long-term dependencies. The hidden states from the forward and backward passes are concatenated to form a comprehensive representation of the input sequence, enabling better performance in tasks requiring the modeling of complex relationships within data.
