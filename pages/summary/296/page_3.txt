The time evolution of RNNs is defined by equations that utilize weight matrices and activation functions to compute hidden states and outputs from inputs.

Long Short-Term Memory networks (LSTMs) enhance RNNs by incorporating gated units, which manage information flow through memory cells, input gates, output gates, and forget gates. This architecture allows LSTMs to maintain gradients and remember values across various time intervals, making them effective for processing sequential data.

Complex systems, characterized by numerous interacting variables leading to emergent behaviors such as adaptation and learning, are inherently challenging to model due to their intricate dependencies. Dynamical systems, a subset of complex systems, are defined by states represented in a state space and governed by evolution equations that predict future states. These systems can exhibit deterministic or non-deterministic behavior, depending on whether future states are uniquely defined by current conditions. The paper also discusses the application of attention mechanisms to effectively model sequential data from complex systems.
