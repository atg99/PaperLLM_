The paper discusses the modeling of dynamical systems using neural networks, particularly focusing on recurrent neural networks (RNNs). It highlights the importance of temporal dependence in these systems, which can be represented through differential or difference equations. Two primary approaches to incorporating temporal dependence in neural networks are identified: classic feedforward networks with time delays and RNNs, which are specifically designed for sequence modeling.

RNNs are recognized as suitable for modeling and predicting dynamical systems due to their temporal recurrence capability. Various network types, such as recurrent radial basis function networks and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks, have been employed to learn chaotic dynamics and perform complex tasks like model identification and time series prediction. However, traditional models face limitations, including a lack of explicit external memory, inability to perform sequential reasoning, and limited capabilities in achieving general intelligence.

To address these limitations, the paper introduces attention mechanisms, which are vital for enhancing reasoning processes in neural networks. Attention mechanisms allow models to focus on specific parts of the input or memory, thus facilitating a more interpretable and conscious approach to machine learning, akin to cognitive reasoning in the brain. The subsequent section elaborates on differentiable attention and its relevance in overcoming the constraints of classic deep learning models.
