This paper explores the capabilities of self-attention networks in recognizing and generating Dyckk;D, a formal language model that represents bounded recursion and captures the hierarchical structure of human language. The authors propose two distinct constructions for self-attention networks to handle Dyckk;D, illustrated in Figure 1. The first construction requires D + 1 layers and has a memory size of O(logk) per layer per token, focusing on parenthesis matching. The second construction operates with just two layers and also utilizes O(logk) memory, where the first layer calculates the depth of tokens and the second layer identifies the most recent unclosed open bracket based on this depth.

The paper emphasizes the importance of positional encodings in these models, noting that effective encodings must have logn bits to differentiate positions and facilitate position comparisons, which are crucial for formal language processing. The authors find that common positional encoding practices do not generalize well for Dyckk;D beyond training lengths, while a single fixed scalar monotonic positional encoding achieves high accuracy even for longer inputs.

Additionally, the paper compares the resource requirements of self-attention networks with recurrent networks, noting that while recurrent networks need at least O(D logk) memory for Dyckk;D, the proposed self-attention construction requires only O(logk) memory with O(logn) precision. Empirical results indicate that Transformers outperform LSTMs in syntactic tasks, highlighting fundamental differences between recurrent and non-recurrent sequence processing.

The study contributes to the theoretical understanding of self-attention networks and their applications in natural versus formal language processing, suggesting that different modeling choices may be necessary for various applications.
