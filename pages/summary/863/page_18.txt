The paper discusses a method for assigning matched brackets in a computational model, focusing on positions within a sequence and ensuring alignment with certain conditions. It outlines a systematic procedure for bracket assignment based on the current position's parity and existing assignments. If specific conditions are met, pairs of brackets are assigned to designated positions, and the process halts to allow for further computations.

The methodology guarantees that the number of assigned slots grows incrementally, ensuring that the total assigned positions remain within a bounded limit. The authors analyze the implications of these assignments on the output of the model, establishing that the output at a particular layer depends solely on specific input positions.

The proof of Theorem C.1 is presented, demonstrating that the number of restrictions needed to ensure the output's dependency on input is manageable. The results indicate that when certain parameters are minimized, the model's output can be determined by the partial assignments, leading to the conclusion that the model may not be capable of recognizing certain language structures, specifically the Dyckk;2 language.

Additionally, the paper details the experimental setup, including data generation for Dyckk;D languages, model specifications, and training metrics. The experiments utilize an LSTM model and Transformer configurations, with performance measured through accuracy in predicting closing brackets. The findings emphasize the importance of careful parameter tuning in achieving optimal model performance.
