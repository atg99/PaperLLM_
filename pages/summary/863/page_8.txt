The paper evaluates the performance of Transformers and LSTMs on the Dyck languages, particularly Dyck(8, 10), focusing on the impact of different positional encodings and memory sizes. The study compares various configurations of Transformers, including different numbers of layers and types of positional encodings—Fourier features, learnable features, and a novel scalar encoding. Results indicate that while all three encodings achieve high accuracy on the validation set, only the scalar positional encoding maintains near-perfect accuracy on the test set, demonstrating the importance of effective positional encoding for long sequences.

In comparing the two-layer Transformer with a one-layer LSTM, the Transformer consistently outperforms the LSTM across varying memory sizes. Both models achieve over 90% accuracy with specific memory sizes, but the Transformer exhibits a memory advantage, validating the theoretical framework of self-attention networks over recurrent models. 

Additionally, the paper explores a broader range of Dyck languages with different vocabulary sizes and recursion bounds, confirming the superior performance of the two-layer Transformer in various configurations while noting that LSTMs require only one layer for processing Dyck languages. Overall, the findings emphasize the necessity for distinct and systematic positional encodings to effectively process complex, order-sensitive sequences.
