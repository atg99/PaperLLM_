This paper explores the capabilities and limitations of self-attention networks, particularly in relation to formal hierarchical structures such as Dyck languages. Previous works, including Hahn (2020) and Bhattamishra et al. (2020a), have shown that hard-attention Transformers struggle to model Dyck languages, while soft-attention Transformers face challenges in achieving perfect performance with bounded Lipschitz continuity. Despite these theoretical limitations, empirical evidence suggests that Transformers can learn Dyck languages effectively and outperform recurrent models like LSTMs.

The paper also discusses a parallel line of research focused on recurrent networks and their ability to process hierarchical structures, demonstrating that RNNs and LSTMs can solve context-free languages under certain conditions, such as infinite precision or the use of external memory. However, it is noted that these networks generally perform poorly on Dyck languages without these assumptions.

Additionally, the authors highlight the ongoing debate in the NLP community regarding whether self-attention networks can effectively model hierarchical structures without recurrence. While Transformers excel in encoding syntactic and word order information, recurrent models have shown better performance in specific linguistically motivated tasks. The findings of this paper contribute to this discussion by confirming that both distributed and recurrent sequence processing methods can model hierarchical structures, albeit through different mechanisms.

The paper defines Dyck languages as formal languages consisting of well-nested brackets and introduces the concept of Dyckk;D, which restricts the depth of strings processed to a bounded size. It also outlines the structure of self-attention networks, specifically focusing on the encoder component of the Transformer model, which consists of self-attention and feed-forward layers. The integration of positional encodings with token embeddings is emphasized, as it plays a critical role in the model's ability to process sequences effectively.
