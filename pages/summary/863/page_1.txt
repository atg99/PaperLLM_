This paper investigates the capabilities of self-attention networks in processing bounded hierarchical languages, specifically Dyckk;D, a subset of Dyckk with a maximum nesting depth of D. While previous research indicated that self-attention networks struggle with formal languages that exhibit hierarchical structures, this study aims to clarify the implications of those limitations for understanding natural language.

The authors construct a hard-attention network with D + 1 layers and O(logk) memory size per token that successfully recognizes Dyckk;D. They also develop a soft-attention network with two layers and the same memory size that can generate this language. Experimental results demonstrate that self-attention networks trained on Dyckk;D can generalize to longer inputs with high accuracy, showcasing a theoretical memory advantage over recurrent networks.

The paper emphasizes that while self-attention networks may not handle unbounded hierarchical structures effectively, they can process bounded hierarchies, suggesting that the role of hierarchy and recursion in natural language may be more nuanced than previously thought. This finding contributes to the ongoing debate regarding the nature of natural language and the capabilities of current NLP models, particularly in relation to formal language theory and linguistic structures.
