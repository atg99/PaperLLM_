The paper discusses the performance of Transformers in processing Dyck languages, specifically Dyck(k, D), under various conditions. Unlike previous studies that reported mean length per layer, this work focuses on expected length to avoid skewing results due to randomness in longer sequences. The findings demonstrate that a two-layer Transformer can effectively process Dyck(k, D), with performance remaining stable even when additional layers are added, indicating that more complex architectures do not necessarily lead to overfitting.

Figures presented show validation performance across different positional encoding schemes and model architectures. Results indicate that all Transformer models achieve near-perfect accuracy with at least two layers. Additionally, a comparison between LSTM and Transformer models reveals that Transformers outperform LSTMs in various configurations of k and D, while maintaining consistent accuracy across different hidden dimensions. Overall, the study highlights the efficiency and capability of Transformers in handling structured languages like Dyck.
