This paper explores the understanding and characterization of Residual Networks (ResNets), which are prominent deep learning architectures used for various supervised learning tasks. The authors focus on the mapping aspect of ResNets, which transforms the embedding space into an unknown space where predictions or classifications can be made using linear criteria. They argue that this mapping can be viewed as the numerical representation of continuous flows of diffeomorphisms governed by ordinary differential equations. Specifically, ResNets with shared weights are described as numerical approximations of exponential diffeomorphic operators. The paper emphasizes both the theoretical and numerical significance of enforcing diffeomorphic properties and highlights the necessity of addressing numerical challenges to ensure consistency between the continuous formulation and the discrete implementation of ResNets. The authors also provide insights into the theoretical and computational implications for ResNet architectures.
