In this paper, the authors explore the relationship between Residual Networks (ResNets) and diﬀeomorphic mappings, proposing a framework that interprets ResNet architectures as numerical implementations of continuous diﬀeomorphic flows. It is established that the residual function F in ResNets can be viewed as a parameterization of an elementary deformation flow. The authors argue that this perspective constrains the design of residual unit architectures, leading to a more structured understanding of ResNets.

The proposed diﬀeomorphism-based learning framework is detailed in Section 3, where the authors highlight the connection between diﬀeomorphic transformations and ordinary differential equations (ODEs) in the context of learning. They emphasize the importance of diﬀeomorphic properties, particularly in medical imaging, as these ensure the invertibility of transformations while preserving topological features. The framework incorporates time-varying velocity vector fields, which govern the flows of diﬀeomorphisms and facilitate the numerical integration of ODEs to compute registration transformations.

The authors also introduce the concept of stationary velocity fields, which are independent of time, and discuss their significance in the context of diﬀeomorphic transformations. Overall, the paper provides a theoretical characterization of ResNet mapping blocks, analyzes deformation flows and Jacobian maps, and derives ResNet architectures with additional diﬀeomorphic constraints.
