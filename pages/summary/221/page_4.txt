The paper discusses the architecture of Residual Networks (ResNets) and focuses on the mapping block, which incrementally transforms an embedding space into a new space conducive for tasks like classification. This mapping block consists of residual units defined by the equation y = F(x; fWig) + x, where F represents the residual mapping to be learned. The authors highlight that the ResNet structure allows for the gradient of layers to remain non-vanishing, even with small weights, which enhances backward propagation.

The paper draws a connection between the incremental mappings in ResNets and di­eomorphic registration models, particularly Large Deformation Di­eomorphic Metric Mapping (LDDMM). These models address registration by composing a series of incremental di­eomorphic mappings, each close to the identity transformation. This relationship underscores the potential of ResNets in learning complex mappings through additive residual functions, thereby facilitating effective training and improved performance in machine learning tasks.
