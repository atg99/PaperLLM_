This paper explores four distinct ResNet architectures, focusing on their ability to model velocity fields in relation to data transformations. The architectures include a ResNet without shared weights for time-varying fields, a shared-weight ResNet for stationary fields, a data-driven symmetric ResNet that incorporates inverse consistency, and a domain-driven symmetric ResNet using random sampling for consistency across the entire domain.

The study emphasizes the characterization of ResNet properties through the lens of differential geometry, particularly examining the Jacobian of the transformations. The Jacobian provides insight into local volume variations induced by the transformations, with values indicating expansion, contraction, or preservation of volumes. The paper highlights the importance of maintaining a positive Jacobian to ensure the bijectivity of the transformation, which is critical for various registration methods.

Results indicate that classification accuracy alone is insufficient for evaluating the performance of these networks, suggesting a need for more comprehensive metrics to assess their effectiveness in classification tasks. This work contributes to understanding how residual-based approaches can be adapted for specific classification challenges by analyzing the deformation fields they estimate.
