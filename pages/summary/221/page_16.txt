The paper discusses the integration of residual networks within a framework of diffeomorphisms, focusing on the use of specific architectures for residual units. It highlights the advantages of higher-order explicit integration schemes and adaptive integration methods due to spatial variability in governing velocity fields. The authors employ tanh activation layers to ensure that the mapping block maintains diffeomorphic properties, contrasting this with the limitations of ReLU activation.

The paper references recent developments in reversible architectures and the connection between Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Spaces (RKHS) and deep networks, emphasizing the Lipschitz continuity of learned residual units. This continuity is significant for ensuring the RKHS nature of admissible velocity fields, suggesting further exploration into kernel-based parametrization of these fields.

The study finds that diffeomorphic mappings, defined as the exponential of velocity fields, enhance computational stability and reduce memory demands in ResNet architectures. The authors propose using Recurrent Neural Networks to implement these mappings. They note that the parametrization of the velocity field is crucial for achieving optimal prediction performance, suggesting that more complex architectures may be beneficial while preserving overall stability.

Training challenges are addressed with a focus on backpropagation and random initialization, framing the training process as a projection onto the manifold of acceptable solutions. The paper suggests that combining multiscale optimization strategies with backpropagation could enhance convergence and improve model performance.
