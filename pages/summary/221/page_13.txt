The paper discusses the effectiveness of a specific algorithm in achieving high classification rates, consistently exceeding 99%. It emphasizes the importance of visualizing decision boundaries to gain insights into the solution's regularity within the embedding space. The results indicate that incorporating constraints, such as stationary velocity fields and inverse consistency, results in smoother decision boundaries without compromising overall accuracy, which is vital for generalization and adversarial robustness.

Figures presented illustrate the decision boundaries for different ResNet architectures, highlighting the impact of shared weights on the smoothness of these boundaries. While decision boundaries provide some insights, they do not convey the topology of the manifold in the output space. The paper further examines the deformation flow through the spatial configuration of data points across network layers, revealing that networks with shared weights yield smoother transformations.

Additionally, the quality of the warping transformation is assessed through Jacobian maps, where local sign changes indicate potential issues with bijectivity. The findings suggest that applying constraints enhances the regularity of the deformed manifold, with domain-driven regularization on a ResNet with shared weights achieving the most consistent geometrical patterns. The introduction of symmetry consistency ensures positive Jacobian values across the domain, confirming the bijectivity of the estimated mapping.
