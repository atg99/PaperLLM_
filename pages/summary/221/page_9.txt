The paper discusses a method for achieving inverse consistency in neural networks by modifying the overall loss function. This is expressed through a specific equation that incorporates a regularization term, which aids in constraining the learning of the mapping block. The term is designed to be independent of the targeted task, allowing it to be applied to data points outside the learning set and thus enhancing the robustness of the model.

In the experimental section, the proposed architecture is evaluated using the CIFAR-10 image classification dataset and a synthetic 2D spiral dataset. The CIFAR-10 dataset consists of 60,000 images across 10 classes, with a focus on assessing the performance of the introduced residual blocks compared to other ResNet architectures. The architecture is structured into three main components: embedding, mapping, and prediction. The embedding includes a convolutional layer, batch normalization, and a tanh activation function to ensure bounded output. The mapping consists of multiple residual blocks, while the prediction phase employs additional convolutional layers, normalization, pooling, and a fully connected layer. Overall, the experiments aim to highlight the advantages of the proposed network design in terms of performance and geometric insights.
