This paper explores the theoretical underpinnings of Residual Networks (ResNets) and their relationship to diﬀeomorphic registration through the lens of ordinary and partial differential equations (ODE/PDE). Despite advancements in convolutional neural networks (CNNs), understanding ResNets remains a significant challenge. Recent studies have highlighted the importance of numerical stability in ResNet configurations, interpreting ResNets as depth-related transformations of input data into a new space where predictions are determined by linear operators.

The authors propose a deeper analogy between ResNets and diﬀeomorphic registration, specifically focusing on three main contributions. First, they redefine ResNet learning as the learning of a continuous and integral diﬀeomorphic operator, examining solutions that promote diﬀeomorphic properties. Second, they clarify that ResNets serve as numerical approximations of continuous diﬀeomorphic settings governed by ODEs. Lastly, they offer theoretical and computational insights into the specification and properties of ResNets.

The paper is structured to first link ResNets to diﬀeomorphic registrations, followed by the introduction of a diﬀeomorphism-based learning framework, and concludes with experimental results. The authors emphasize that ResNets have established themselves as leading architectures in deep learning, facilitating the training of very deep models without performance degradation.
