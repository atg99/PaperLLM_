In this section of the paper, the authors present results comparing a proposed network architecture to the established ResNet56v2, focusing on their performance with a constant embedding space dimension. The experiments reveal that both networks perform similarly when trained on the entire dataset, despite the ResNet architecture using three layers—convolution, batch normalization, and ReLU—where the latter is essential for efficient sparse parameterization of velocity fields. Notably, the authors find that batch normalization is not necessary for achieving satisfactory classification accuracy in their model, which instead employs a tanh activation layer to maintain an open and bounded embedding set.

The study also challenges the common belief that deep neural network performance relies on progressively removing uninformative variability through dimensional changes. Experimental results indicate that the proposed residual unit exhibits greater robustness to smaller training sets compared to the Keras ResNet. Furthermore, the impact of network depth on classification results is explored. While deeper networks typically risk overfitting with small datasets, the proposed architecture maintains accuracy even as depth increases, suggesting that this design leads to smoother variations in velocity fields without succumbing to overfitting.

In the subsequent section, the authors shift focus to the classification of 2-dimensional spiral data, building on previous work in differential geometry related to ResNet architectures. They indicate that no embedding layer is necessary in this scenario, as the mapping block's role is to warp input data points into a space where they become linearly separable, utilizing a binary cross-entropy loss function for evaluation.
