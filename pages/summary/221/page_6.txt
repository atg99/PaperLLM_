The paper discusses the mathematical framework of Residual Networks through the lens of flows of diffeomorphisms. It highlights how these diffeomorphisms form a subgroup in the underlying Lie group, leading to the definition of exponential operators. The authors explain that for a stationary velocity field, the diffeomorphisms exhibit a one-parameter subgroup property governed by an infinitesimal generator, allowing for computations of diffeomorphisms through repeated applications.

In the context of supervised learning, the paper frames the problem as learning diffeomorphisms based on a predefined loss function. It presents a typical classification scenario where the objective is to predict class labels from real-valued observations. The authors define a model comprising an embedding, a diffeomorphic mapping, and a linear classifier, which are jointly estimated to minimize the loss function, commonly using cross-entropy. The paper further explores the ODE-based parametrization of diffeomorphisms, leading to an equivalent estimation problem for a sequence of velocity fields, which are linked to the dynamics of the diffeomorphic transformations.

Overall, the work connects the mathematical properties of diffeomorphisms with practical applications in supervised learning, providing a robust framework for understanding the underlying dynamics in neural networks.
