The paper discusses the optimization of stationary velocity fields in the context of image registration, emphasizing a unique minimization approach that differs from traditional methods. Specifically, the loss function used here integrates a classification-based perspective rather than the standard prediction error minimization. The authors propose a derived neural network architecture to facilitate this optimization, which allows for the incorporation of additional priors on the velocity fields. 

The paper highlights the potential for including both integral norms of the gradient of the velocity fields and parametric representations to encourage small registration displacements. A discrete parametrization approach is introduced, where the learned velocity field is expressed as a linear combination of basis functions, with weights learned through convolutional layers. The discussion extends to various shortcut connection architectures and activation functions, noting their impact on the efficiency of the model. 

The authors illustrate the construction of diffeomorphisms through small perturbations of the identity, referencing established mathematical frameworks. This work aims to provide a methodological guide for developing effective residual units in image registration tasks, with implications for enhancing neural network designs in this domain.
