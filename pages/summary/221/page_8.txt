This paper introduces a novel residual block designed for constructing flows of diffeomorphisms, enhancing the capabilities of residual networks. The authors investigate two case studies: one involving the CIFAR-10 dataset, where images serve as inputs, and the other using spiral datasets with two-dimensional inputs. In the CIFAR-10 experiments, the basis functions are parameterized through a convolutional layer followed by a ReLU layer, while the spiral dataset employs a dense layer with a tanh activation.

Key aspects include the omission of biases in the convolutional layers and the introduction of a tanh layer along with a scaling layer to manage the velocity field's magnitude. The paper also incorporates a windowing layer to ensure that the velocity field and its derivative vanish at the boundaries and at infinity, guaranteeing Lipschitz continuity.

Additionally, the authors address a critical issue in registration-based frameworks, where transformations applied solely to the observation embedding can lead to optimization asymmetries. To mitigate this, they propose integrating both forwards and backwards, implementing a penalty term for inverse consistency, which was originally proposed by Thirion. This approach aims to enhance registration performance by ensuring a more symmetric optimization process. The proposed residual block is visually represented in the paper, highlighting its innovative structure.
