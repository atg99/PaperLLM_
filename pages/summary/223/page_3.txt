The paper discusses the training dynamics of residual networks (ResNets) and introduces a framework to analyze how these networks benefit from specific architectural choices, particularly the inclusion of shortcut connections and batch normalization. The authors argue that these elements help the training algorithm navigate towards global optima while avoiding spurious local optima.

In the first stage of training, the network is guided towards the basin of attraction of the global optimum, where the choice of step sizes is crucial for convergence. The analysis also relates to learning rate strategies, such as warm-up techniques, enhancing efficiency during training.

The paper presents a model that operates in a realizable setting, where labels are generated from a teacher network. The student network is defined in a way that it is not identifiably constrained, allowing for a more flexible representation of data. This leads to the formulation of a nonconvex optimization problem aimed at recovering the parameters of the teacher network from the student network.

The authors emphasize that the shortcut connections and the use of batch normalization within the network architecture contribute to improved training performance, enabling the network to learn effectively even from simple initialization strategies. The paper concludes with a theoretical framework that supports these observations, providing insights into the functioning of ResNets and their training methodologies.
