The paper discusses the behavior of gradient descent (GD) and its convergence properties, particularly in the context of training deep neural networks like ResNet. It highlights a two-stage optimization process where the step size is initially small to avoid local optima, followed by a gradual increase as the algorithm progresses towards the global optimum. The authors illustrate that GD without the proposed step size warmup can become trapped in spurious local optima.

The analysis emphasizes the significance of initialization, showing that GD can converge to the global optimum even when weights are initialized at zero. This aligns with empirical findings suggesting that deep ResNets perform well under such conditions. The paper also draws connections to existing literature on learning rate schedules, noting that the initial small step size aligns with techniques like learning rate warmup, which have been shown effective in training ResNets.

Furthermore, the authors propose investigating the properties of shallow networks within deeper architectures and the role of shortcut connections. They suggest that the findings regarding GD may extend to stochastic gradient descent (SGD) under varying batch sizes, with larger batches mimicking GD behavior and smaller batches introducing significant noise.

Overall, the paper contributes to understanding the optimization dynamics in training deep networks and proposes future avenues for research related to initialization strategies and the impact of network architecture on training efficacy.
