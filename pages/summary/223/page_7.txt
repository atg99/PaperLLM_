The paper presents a proof sketch for demonstrating the convergence of a gradient descent (GD) algorithm, focusing on the concept of partial dissipativity. The key lemma (Lemma 11) establishes that for any positive m, the function rwL satisfies a specific condition that ensures positive progress towards a target weight w*. This implies that the algorithm can employ a larger step size without the risk of divergence.

The subsequent lemma (Lemma 12) indicates that when the weight w is sufficiently accurate, the partial gradient with respect to a also exhibits partial dissipativity, which ensures convergence to a*.

The main theorem (Theorem 13) formalizes the convergence criteria, stipulating specific conditions on parameters a and m for all t > 0. It provides a method to choose appropriate step sizes, leading to convergence of both weights and parameters within polynomial time. The convergence is shown to hold even with arbitrary initialization, underscoring the significance of architectural elements like shortcuts in ResNet models.

The proof relies on establishing the partial dissipative regions for both w and a through a series of lemmas (Lemmas 14 and 15), which confirm that the algorithm consistently approaches the optimal values. Overall, the results contribute to understanding the efficiency of the GD algorithm in achieving global optimum solutions in neural network training.
