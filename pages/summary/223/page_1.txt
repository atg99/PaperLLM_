The paper "Towards Understanding the Importance of Shortcut Connections in Residual Networks" investigates the underlying reasons for the effective training of Residual Networks (ResNets), which are a significant advancement in deep learning due to their use of shortcut connections. The authors focus on a simplified two-layer non-overlapping convolutional ResNet, addressing the challenges of training such a network, which involves navigating a non-convex optimization landscape with potential spurious local optima.

The key findings show that applying gradient descent with appropriate normalization allows the training process to avoid getting stuck in these spurious local optima, ultimately facilitating convergence to a global optimum in polynomial time. This is particularly effective when the first layer's weights are initialized at zero and the second layer's weights are initialized within a certain range. The authors support their theoretical insights with numerical experiments.

The introduction contextualizes the importance of ResNets within the broader landscape of neural networks, highlighting their superior training efficiency compared to traditional feedforward Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), which struggle with deeper architectures. While existing literature suggests that deeper CNNs tend to underperform and are slower to train, ResNets can effectively utilize hundreds of layers, achieving notable performance improvements in various applications.

Despite the empirical success of ResNets, the paper emphasizes that a comprehensive understanding of their training dynamics remains elusive, motivating the authors' exploration into the role of shortcut connections. The findings contribute to the ongoing discourse about the mechanisms that enable deep learning models to perform effectively in practice.
