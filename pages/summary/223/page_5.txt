The paper presents a detailed analysis of the convergence of the Gradient Descent (GD) algorithm under specific conditions, structured into two main stages. 

In Stage I, the focus is on avoiding spurious local optima. Theorem 2 establishes that by initializing the algorithm with specific parameters (w0 = 0 and an arbitrary a0), and by selecting appropriate step sizes (with a proportional to O(1/k^2) and w proportional to eO(1/k^4)), the algorithm can avoid getting trapped in local optima. It defines a time T1, indicating the number of iterations needed to ensure certain bounds on the parameters m and M, which represent the lower and upper bounds of the local optimum.

Stage II transitions to the convergence towards the global optimum. After the initial phase, the algorithm restarts with adjusted parameters and a new step size that is again based on O(1/k^2). The results show that after T2 iterations, the algorithm can achieve a close proximity to the global optimum, specified by small values of ε, indicating that the parameters w and a are sufficiently close to their optimal values.

The paper also introduces the concept of partial dissipativity, which is essential for analyzing the convergence of the GD algorithm. This concept, adapted from dynamical systems, ensures that the gradients lead the algorithm towards optimal solutions. The authors reference previous studies that have utilized similar ideas, establishing a theoretical foundation for their results.

Theorem 4 further formalizes the conditions under which the GD algorithm can successfully navigate around spurious local optima, providing specific step size conditions that ensure convergence. The proof of these theorems and lemmas is provided in the appendix, indicating a rigorous mathematical underpinning to their claims.

Overall, the paper establishes a clear framework for understanding the behavior of the GD algorithm in complex optimization landscapes, emphasizing the importance of initialization and adaptive step sizes in achieving convergence.
