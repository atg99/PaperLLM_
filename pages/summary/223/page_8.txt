The paper presents numerical experiments aimed at demonstrating the convergence behavior of the Gradient Descent (GD) algorithm when training a two-layer non-overlapping convolutional ResNet. The experiments focus on the impact of step size warmup (SSW) on the convergence to the global optimum.

Key parameters include the choice of step sizes and initialization, which are crucial for ensuring convergence. The study utilizes parameters for a teacher network and a student network, with specific settings for the step sizes during training. The experiments consist of 5000 simulations for various combinations of parameters, specifically varying k and using a fixed p value of 8.

Results are summarized in Table 1 and Figure 4, showing the success rates of converging to the global optimum. The findings indicate that GD with SSW consistently achieves a 100% success rate across all simulations, while GD without SSW shows a significantly lower success rate, particularly as the dimension increases. The student network, when trained on the same teacher network, experiences difficulties, often getting trapped in spurious local optima.

Furthermore, the paper illustrates the algorithm's behavior through visual representations of the solution paths, emphasizing a phase transition in the GD process influenced by the choice of step sizes. The results underscore the effectiveness of SSW in enhancing the convergence properties of GD in complex neural network architectures.
