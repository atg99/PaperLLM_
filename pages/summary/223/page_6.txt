The paper discusses the optimization process under the shortcut prior assumption, focusing on the behavior of weight updates in the context of gradient descent (GD). It highlights the need for conservative updates to the weights, denoted as w, particularly when the initial weight, w0, is close to a target weight, w*. Lemma 6 establishes conditions under which the angle between the current weight and the target remains acute, ensuring w does not converge too closely to w* prematurely.

Lemma 7 provides a framework for understanding the behavior of the optimization algorithm within a defined region, ensuring that the loss function behaves predictably as updates are made. It emphasizes the importance of maintaining a certain distance from the target weight to avoid local optima. Lemma 8 further asserts that, even with arbitrary initialization of the parameters, the updates can be guided into a region where parameters remain positive and bounded.

In Theorem 4, the conservative step size for weight updates is justified, stating that such an approach prevents w from getting too close to w* too quickly, allowing for continuous updates towards the target parameter a*. This suggests that the algorithm can navigate around spurious local optima effectively during the initial training phase.

Stage II of the paper transitions to a discussion of accelerating convergence toward the global optimum after ensuring the solution is in a favorable region. Theorem 10 introduces larger step sizes for weight updates, which facilitate faster convergence while maintaining the previous guarantees about staying within the basin of attraction of the global optimum. The results collectively indicate that the proposed adaptive step sizes enable the algorithm to manage both exploration and convergence effectively throughout the training process.
