This paper investigates the optimization landscape of ResNet, focusing on a two-layer non-overlapping convolutional neural network that contains a spurious local optimum. Building on previous work, the authors analyze the impact of shortcut connections on the training convergence of ResNet. They establish a framework for the student network, defined by an optimization problem that aims to recover parameters from a teacher network.

The authors note that while prior research has shown that linear ResNet avoids spurious local optima, their study reveals that the presence of a shortcut connection can facilitate convergence to a global optimum when specific initial conditions are met. They demonstrate that with appropriate normalization and initialization, gradient descent can converge in polynomial time under the assumption that the output weight closely aligns with the average pooling shortcut connection.

Empirical evidence supports the assumption that the weights of well-trained deep ResNet have small magnitudes, which aligns with findings from previous studies regarding weight norms in deep networks. The analysis concludes that the convergence of gradient descent occurs in two stages, beginning with an initialization that keeps the weights sufficiently distant from spurious local optima.

Overall, the findings suggest that shortcut connections in ResNet not only mitigate the vanishing gradient problem but also enhance the training process, allowing for effective convergence in non-convex optimization scenarios.
