The paper discusses the significant public health initiative launched by the Egyptian government, the "100 Million Seha" campaign, aimed at screening for Hepatitis C virus (HCV) from October 2018 to April 2019, which resulted in approximately 35 million people being examined. It highlights the higher prevalence of primary hepatic malignancy in Southeast Asia and Africa compared to the United States, with a reported survival rate of around 18%, largely dependent on the disease stage at diagnosis.

Diagnosis of primary hepatic malignancy involves clinical, laboratory, and imaging tests, including ultrasound, magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), and computed tomography (CT) scans. CT scans are particularly noted for their ability to capture detailed images of organs and tissues, but they often require contrast material for better differentiation of malignant lesions from other conditions like infections or chronic inflammation.

The paper emphasizes the need for automated procedures for accurate detection and extraction of cancerous regions in CT scans, as the traditional manual and semi-manual interpretation methods are subjective, costly, and prone to errors. Image segmentation is identified as a crucial step in this process, enabling the partitioning of liver regions into meaningful segments for diagnosis and the development of computer-aided diagnosis (CAD) systems.

Challenges in liver and lesion segmentation include low contrast between the liver and adjacent organs, variations in tumor characteristics, and irregular tumor growth patterns due to treatment. The paper reviews recent advancements in image analysis for liver malignancy segmentation, focusing on supervised learning methods, particularly deep learning techniques such as convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and others. While deep learning models have shown improved accuracy, the paper notes the ongoing challenge of acquiring large, expert-prepared training datasets necessary for effective model training.
