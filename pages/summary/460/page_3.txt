The paper discusses the application of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) in medical image segmentation, specifically for liver cross-sectional CT scans. It highlights the effectiveness of CNNs as leading deep learning methods, citing Elshaer et al. who improved computation time by using two trained models for liver region extraction and lesion detection. Wen Li et al. employed image patches centered around pixels of interest, achieving an accuracy of 80.6% in classifying liver tissue as normal or tumorous. Other referenced works achieved over 94% accuracy in distinguishing normal from abnormal liver images.

Various CNN architectures such as AlexNet, VGG-Net, and ResNet are noted, with Bellver et al. utilizing the VGG-16 architecture. Additionally, two-dimensional U-Net models were mentioned for medical image segmentation. The primary goal of the paper is to introduce a novel segmentation technique using SegNet, which is noted for its memory and performance efficiency.

The paper is structured to first review recent segmentation methods, followed by the proposed method and experimental dataset, with results and conclusions presented in subsequent sections. It explains that CNNs consist of multiple layers, including convolutional and fully connected layers, which enhance accuracy but increase computational complexity. The VGG-16 architecture specifically includes 41 layers, with 16 having learnable weights. The encoder-decoder architecture is emphasized for pixel-wise classification, with U-Net being a notable example for effective segmentation.
