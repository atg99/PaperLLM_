The paper introduces a novel no-recurrence model, NRTR, designed to enhance the accuracy and speed of scene text recognition by addressing limitations in existing RNN/CNN-based systems. It emphasizes the use of a modality-transform block that integrates a two-layer convolutional structure with the encoder to extract more discriminative features. The study replaces traditional CNNs with CNNLSTM to better capture temporal information, leading to improved accuracy in the base model, although a minor decrease was observed in the larger model due to potential redundancy in feature extraction.

In comparative analyses against state-of-the-art methods, NRTR demonstrates superior accuracy across various benchmarks, outperforming previous best recognizers in both lexicon-free and lexicon-based scenarios. Specifically, it achieves notable gains on datasets such as IIIT5K, SVT, and IC03. The model's training speed is significantly faster, requiring only 5 hours per epoch compared to much longer times for other methods. Additionally, NRTR shows strong performance on irregular text benchmarks without specialized operations, indicating its robust text feature extraction capabilities.

Visualization results illustrate NRTR's ability to handle challenging images, while also identifying common failure types, such as occlusion and character confusion, which suggest future research directions. The conclusion reiterates the model's advancements in computational efficiency and performance, with aspirations to extend its application to end-to-end text spotting systems.
