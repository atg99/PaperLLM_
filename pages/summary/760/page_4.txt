The decoder is composed of multiple identical blocks that utilize masked multi-head attention to maintain the auto-regressive property, ensuring that predictions for a given position only depend on previous outputs. The attention mechanism incorporates keys and values from the encoder outputs, while the queries are derived from the previous decoder block outputs. The final output probabilities for character classes are generated through a linear projection followed by a softmax function.

The experimental section evaluates the model on several benchmark datasets, including IIIT5K, SVT, and various ICDAR datasets, detailing the number of text images and associated lexicons for each. The model, referred to as NRTR, is trained on Synth90k without fine-tuning on the benchmarks. The experiments reveal the impact of different configurations of the encoder and decoder on the model's performance, emphasizing that deeper models with more encoder blocks yield better accuracy.

Additionally, an ablation study investigates the effects of varying the number of encoder and decoder blocks as well as the dimensions of the fully connected layers. The results indicate that increasing the number of encoder blocks generally enhances accuracy, although there are diminishing returns related to time and memory costs. The study also examines the modality-transform block, finding that excessive convolutional layers can negatively affect performance due to the loss of detailed information.

Overall, the paper highlights the effectiveness of the NRTR architecture for text recognition tasks and provides insights into the design choices that influence model performance.
