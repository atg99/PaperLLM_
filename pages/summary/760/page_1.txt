The paper introduces NRTR, a novel no-recurrence sequence-to-sequence model designed for scene text recognition. Traditional methods rely on recurrent neural networks (RNNs) or convolutional neural networks (CNNs), which face limitations such as slow training speeds and high complexity due to their internal structures. NRTR addresses these issues by utilizing a fully self-attention mechanism within the encoder-decoder framework, allowing for more parallelization and reduced complexity during training.

The encoder employs stacked self-attention layers to extract features from 2D input images, while the decoder uses self-attention to recognize text based on the encoded features. To handle the variability in scene images, a modality-transform block is introduced to convert 2D images into 1D sequences, enhancing the extraction of discriminative features. 

NRTR demonstrates state-of-the-art performance on both regular and irregular text benchmarks and significantly outperforms existing models in terms of training speed, being at least eight times faster than the best competing methods. The study highlights the potential of self-attention mechanisms in overcoming the challenges of scene text recognition, showcasing NRTR as an efficient alternative to traditional methods.
