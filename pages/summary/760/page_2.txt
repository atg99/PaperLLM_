The paper introduces the No-Recurrence Text Recognition (NRTR) model, which operates on an encoder-decoder framework based solely on self-attention mechanisms. The encoder processes an input image sequence and transforms it into a hidden feature representation using stacked self-attention. The decoder then generates a character output sequence by leveraging the encoder's output. NRTR distinguishes itself from traditional methods by establishing global dependencies between input and output positions simultaneously, enhancing computational parallelization and efficiency compared to RNNs and CNNs.

A key innovation in NRTR is the modality-transform block, which preprocesses 2D images to convert them into a 1D sequence suitable for the encoder, addressing the challenges posed by variations in scales, aspect ratios, and backgrounds in scene text. Experimental results demonstrate that this block significantly enhances recognizer performance.

The model is evaluated on various benchmarks, achieving state-of-the-art performance without requiring a rectification module, and it boasts an eightfold increase in training speed compared to existing methods. The authors identify deficiencies in current RNN and CNN-based recognizers, advocating for their self-attention-based approach that allows for greater computational parallelization and reduced complexity.

The paper also contrasts NRTR with traditional text recognition methods, which typically rely on bottom-up or top-down schemes, and highlights its distinction from Transformer models used in NLP, noting that NRTR effectively handles 2D image input rather than 1D sentence input.

In summary, the contributions of this work include the introduction of a no-recurrence sequence-to-sequence model utilizing self-attention, the design of an effective modality-transform block, and the demonstration of superior performance across various datasets in both lexicon-free and lexicon-based scenarios.
