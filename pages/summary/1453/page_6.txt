This paper presents an evaluation of various models based on the BERT architecture on the GLUE benchmark, focusing on the performance and efficiency of TinyBERT, a distilled version of BERT. The study compares TinyBERT with other models such as BERT TINY, BERT SMALL, and several state-of-the-art knowledge distillation (KD) methods, including DistilBERT and MobileBERT.

Key findings from the experiments, summarized in Table 1, indicate that TinyBERT outperforms BERT TINY across all tasks while maintaining a significantly smaller model size and reduced parameters. Specifically, TinyBERT 4, which has 14.5M parameters and 1.2B FLOPs, achieves a speedup of 9.4x compared to the BERT BASE model. Additionally, TinyBERT 6 shows competitive performance with 67M parameters, demonstrating the effectiveness of both general and task-specific distillation methods.

The architecture of TinyBERT models involves a layer mapping function where TinyBERT 4 learns from every third layer of BERT BASE. The training process includes intermediate layer distillation and prediction layer distillation, tailored for different tasks within the GLUE benchmark.

The results highlight the trade-off between model size and performance, emphasizing that while smaller models like BERT TINY and BERT SMALL lag behind in accuracy, TinyBERT effectively bridges the gap by leveraging knowledge distillation techniques. Overall, the study concludes that TinyBERT offers a promising solution for deploying efficient and effective NLP models.
