The paper presents TinyBERT, a model designed to effectively distill knowledge from a larger BERT model through a novel Transformer distillation method. The authors highlight the challenges of distilling BERT, which is pre-trained on a large-scale unsupervised text corpus and then fine-tuned on specific datasets. To address these challenges, they propose a two-stage learning framework that includes general distillation and task-specific distillation.

In the first stage, general distillation, the original BERT model serves as the teacher, and TinyBERT learns from its representations across different layers, including the embedding layer, hidden states, attention matrices, and logits. The authors emphasize the importance of transferring the linguistic knowledge captured in the attention weights of BERT, as supported by prior research.

In the second stage, task-specific distillation, the authors utilize data augmentation to enhance the training dataset, followed by distillation from a fine-tuned BERT model. Both stages are deemed essential for improving TinyBERT's performance and generalization capabilities.

Key contributions of the paper include the introduction of a new Transformer distillation method, a comprehensive two-stage learning framework, and empirical results showing that TinyBERT achieves over 96.8% of the performance of the teacher BERT BASE on GLUE tasks while using significantly fewer parameters and requiring less inference time. The paper also demonstrates that a 6-layer TinyBERT can match the performance of the teacher model on the same tasks.

Overall, the proposed methods and findings in this study advance the field of model distillation, particularly for Transformer-based architectures.
