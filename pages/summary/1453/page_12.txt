The paper discusses the performance of BERT TINY compared to BERT BASE on various natural language processing tasks, particularly focusing on MRPC and CoLA. It posits that without mimicking the behavior of BERT BASE during pre-training, BERT TINY may develop mismatched intermediate representations, leading to suboptimal performance, especially on tasks with less data. However, for data-intensive tasks like MNLI, BERT TINY can still learn task-specific knowledge despite initial disturbances in pre-trained distributions.

The results indicate that knowledge distillation (GD) effectively transfers knowledge from the teacher model (BERT) to the student model (TinyBERT), achieving comparable performance even without masked language modeling (MLM) and next sentence prediction (NSP). Task-specific distillation further enhances TinyBERT's performance by allowing it to continue learning from the fine-tuned BERT BASE.

The paper also provides descriptions of various GLUE datasets, including MNLI, QQP, QNLI, SST-2, CoLA, STS-B, MRPC, and RTE, highlighting their respective tasks related to natural language inference, semantic equivalence, sentiment analysis, grammatical correctness, and paraphrase identification.
