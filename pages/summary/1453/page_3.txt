In this paper, the authors discuss the architecture of Transformer networks, specifically focusing on the Multi-Head Attention (MHA) mechanism and the Position-wise Feed-Forward Network (FFN). The MHA combines multiple attention heads to capture information from different subspaces, while the FFN applies two linear transformations with a ReLU activation function.

The paper then introduces the concept of Knowledge Distillation (KD), which aims to transfer knowledge from a larger teacher network, such as BERT, to a smaller student network, referred to as TinyBERT. The student network is trained to replicate the behavior of the teacher network by minimizing a loss function that evaluates the differences in their outputs.

The authors propose a two-stage learning framework for distilling knowledge from BERT to TinyBERT, emphasizing the importance of defining effective behavior functions and loss functions. They also present a unique Transformer distillation method that involves both attention-based and hidden states-based distillation.

The method is framed around a problem formulation where the student model has fewer layers than the teacher model. The authors define a mapping function that connects the layers of the student model to those of the teacher model, allowing the student to learn from selected layers of the teacher. The objective of the learning process is to minimize the overall loss across the selected layers.

Overall, the paper provides a comprehensive approach to distilling knowledge from large Transformer models to smaller ones, aiming to maintain performance while reducing model size.
