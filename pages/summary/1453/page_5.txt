The paper presents an algorithm for data augmentation aimed at enhancing task-specific distillation for TinyBERT, a smaller version of BERT. The proposed procedure involves replacing single-piece words with a mask and using BERT to predict likely word replacements, while for multi-piece words, GloVe embeddings are utilized to find similar words. The algorithm iterates until a specified number of samples is augmented, defined by parameters such as threshold probability and candidate set size.

The motivation behind this work is rooted in the observation that larger models like fine-tuned BERTs often suffer from over-parameterization on domain-specific tasks, which can hinder performance. The authors argue that by leveraging task-specific distillation methods and augmented datasets, smaller models like TinyBERT can achieve competitive performance. The augmentation process is integrated into a two-stage learning framework where general distillation provides a solid foundation and task-specific distillation refines the model by focusing on domain knowledge.

The effectiveness of TinyBERT is evaluated across various tasks using the GLUE benchmark, which includes a mix of single-sentence, sentence similarity, and natural language inference tasks. The model configuration for TinyBERT includes a reduced number of layers and parameters, specifically designed to maintain efficiency while still delivering competitive results in NLP tasks. Overall, the combination of data augmentation and targeted distillation significantly enhances the capabilities of TinyBERT despite its smaller size.
