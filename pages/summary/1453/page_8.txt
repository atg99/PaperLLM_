The paper investigates the effectiveness of various knowledge distillation techniques in compressing pre-trained language models (PLMs), particularly focusing on transformer-based models like TinyBERT. The authors present experiments that demonstrate the significance of different distillation strategies, revealing that the performance of the model drops substantially when not using transformer layer distillation, indicating its importance for effective initialization during pre-training. 

The study also analyzes the contributions of attention-based and hidden state-based distillation, concluding that attention distillation has a greater impact, though both methods are complementary. Additionally, the paper evaluates different mapping functions for layer selection in TinyBERT, showing that the uniform strategy outperforms both top and bottom strategies across various tasks, emphasizing the challenge of adaptively selecting layers for specific tasks.

The related work section highlights existing methods for compressing PLMs, including low-rank approximation, weight sharing, pruning, and quantization, with a particular focus on knowledge distillation. The authors contrast their proposed two-stage learning framework with other methods, such as DistilBERT and MobileBERT, which either distill knowledge at the pre-training stage or utilize specific architectures without a comprehensive distillation approach. Overall, the findings underscore the critical role of knowledge distillation in enhancing the performance of smaller transformer models.
