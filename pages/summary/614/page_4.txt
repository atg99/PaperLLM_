This paper discusses a novel approach to optimizing the architecture of recurrent neural networks (RNNs), particularly focusing on Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) units. The authors propose a methodology that treats the rows and columns of the weight matrices as discrete samples of time-evolving signals. By identifying and discarding signals that exhibit similar dynamics, the size and memory footprint of RNNs can be significantly reduced without compromising performance.

The architecture of LSTMs, which includes input, forget, output gates, and cell updates, is detailed, emphasizing the interdependencies among the weight matrices. The paper outlines how the removal of rows or columns from these matrices necessitates corresponding adjustments in related matrices to maintain the integrity of the network's operations.

A crucial aspect of this work is the development of an optimal weight pruning technique, which aims to minimize performance degradation while reducing network size. The authors employ a linear dynamical system (LDS) to model the sequences of weights, facilitating the identification of redundant weights that do not contribute significantly to network performance. This method, previously applied in video classification, is introduced here for the first time in the context of RNNs.

The paper also discusses the stacking of weight matrices into blocks to capture their inherent relationships, proposing various configurations for modeling the LSTM weight matrices collectively. The ultimate goal is to enhance the training and inference speed of LSTMs while reducing the number of weight parameters, thereby improving efficiency in practical applications.
