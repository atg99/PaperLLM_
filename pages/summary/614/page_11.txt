The paper evaluates the effectiveness of different fine-tuning approaches and pruning methods applied to the big PTB model in neural networks. The study compares single-step and iterative fine-tuning, with results indicating that iterative fine-tuning results in lower perplexity (73.73) compared to single-step (75.7). 

Further comparisons between baseline and pruned models reveal that the pruned model not only generalizes better, evidenced by a smaller perplexity of 73.73, but also has significantly reduced size, memory requirements, and inference time. Specifically, the pruned model has three times fewer parameters and memory size and nine times faster execution time than the baseline model, affirming the efficacy of the proposed pruning method in enhancing model performance by removing unnecessary weights.

The paper also contrasts the proposed method against state-of-the-art RNN pruning techniques. The results indicate that the proposed method achieves superior performance in reducing perplexity while pruning a larger percentage of network parameters compared to existing methods. Notably, it successfully prunes the embedding layer, which is often excluded in other studies, further validating its effectiveness.

Overall, the findings highlight the proposed method's capability to maintain or improve model performance while significantly reducing computational demands, thereby demonstrating its potential as a robust solution for model optimization in recurrent neural networks.
