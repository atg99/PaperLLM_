The paper presents a novel method for pruning Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) aimed at reducing memory requirements and increasing inference speed. The authors treat RNN weight matrices as collections of time-evolving signals, which can be modeled using Linear Dynamical Systems (LDSs). This approach allows for the pruning of weight vectors with similar temporal dynamics, as they have a minimal impact on model performance.

To enhance the fine-tuning process of the pruned model, the authors introduce a discrimination-aware variant of L2 regularization that penalizes weights with minimal output impact. Additionally, they propose an iterative fine-tuning approach that utilizes a larger model to guide the smaller, pruned version, preventing significant performance degradation that can occur with abrupt parameter reductions.

Extensive experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed method, showing improvements in perplexity by at least 0.62% on the Penn Treebank (PTB) dataset and a 1.39% increase in F1-score on the Stanford Question Answering Dataset (SQuAD), outperforming existing state-of-the-art pruning techniques that either marginally improve or worsen model performance.

The paper highlights the growing need for efficient RNN implementations in resource-constrained environments, especially with the increasing demand for sequence processing algorithms in various applications. Overall, it provides a significant contribution to the field of neural network optimization, particularly for RNNs.
