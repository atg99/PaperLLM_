The paper discusses a variation of L2 regularization aimed at reducing the magnitude of weights in recurrent neural networks (RNNs) that contribute minimally to the output. This method is complemented by a novel iterative fine-tuning scheme where larger models guide smaller ones, addressing the issue of performance degradation that often occurs during single-step network pruning.

The authors suggest future research directions, including applying the proposed pruning method to other deep learning architectures like convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and transformer networks. They also emphasize the need to evaluate the performance gains of pruned models in real-world mobile applications and to explore pruning techniques for models handling various types of sequential data, such as video and audio.
