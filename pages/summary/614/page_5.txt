The paper by Chatzikonstantinou et al. presents a technique for pruning Recurrent Neural Network (RNN) weight matrices through the use of Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) architectures. It emphasizes the interdependence of the weight matrices, indicating that the removal of rows or columns from one matrix necessitates corresponding adjustments in others. 

The authors model each row or column of the weight matrices as a temporal signal using an Autoregressive Moving Average (ARMA) model, which is formalized through Linear Dynamical Systems (LDS). The hidden state of the LDS is described by a pair of matrices that capture the dynamics and values of the signals represented in the weight matrices. 

To estimate the parameters of these LDS descriptors, the authors employ a principal component-based approach, constructing a matrix from the deviations of the weight elements from their average values. This matrix is then subjected to singular value decomposition, allowing for the calculation of the matrices that describe the system's dynamics. 

Furthermore, the paper introduces a k-medoid clustering algorithm to identify and retain only the representative signals from each cluster, thereby optimizing the pruning process by focusing on the most informative signals while discarding others. This method aims to enhance the efficiency of RNNs by reducing their complexity without significant loss of performance.
