This paper investigates the impact of various pruning strategies on the perplexities of the BigPTB and BiDAF models. The experiments focus on evaluating the effectiveness of different approaches to pruning recurrent neural networks (RNNs), particularly Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks in the BigPTB model and Bidirectional LSTMs (BLSTMs) in the BiDAF model.

The study reveals that stacking weight matrices into fewer blocks yields better performance than modeling each weight matrix individually. Specifically, for the BigPTB model, the optimal strategy involves using a single block of eight matrices, while for the BiDAF model, two blocks of four matrices are preferred. This suggests that the correlations among weight matrices are significant and should be considered in pruning strategies.

Furthermore, the paper evaluates specialized similarity metrics for modeling RNN weight matrices, finding that Martin's distance outperforms Grassmannian and Procrustes distances in terms of perplexity. The impact of pruning ratios is also examined, indicating that a decrease in the number of clusters leads to a linear reduction in model size and memory, but can negatively affect perplexity, highlighting the trade-off between model performance and sparsity.

Additionally, the effect of the Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss factor during fine-tuning is assessed, showing that an appropriate MSE loss factor significantly enhances model performance by reducing perplexity, although there is a threshold beyond which performance begins to decline.

In summary, this study underscores the importance of selecting optimal pruning strategies, similarity metrics, and hyperparameters in enhancing the performance of pruned neural network models while balancing efficiency and accuracy.
