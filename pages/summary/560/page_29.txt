This paper provides a survey on differentiable neural architecture search (NAS), focusing on the efficient automation of neural network design. It categorizes various works based on their applications across fields such as computer vision, natural language processing, medical applications, reinforcement learning, audio processing, and hardware optimization. 

Key findings highlight that many studies primarily address challenges associated with DARTS, particularly gradient approximation discrepancies and over-representations of skip connections. These issues lead to an optimization gap where the final architecture differs significantly from the proxy model used during the search process. While some approaches attempt to mitigate this gap by modifying the discretization process or proposing proxyless search methods, improvements in accuracy are often marginal, indicating a lack of consensus on effective solutions.

Additionally, the paper notes that many researchers recognize the bias introduced by non-parametric operations in DARTS, which can lead to architectural overfitting. Most studies suggest incorporating regularization techniques to counteract this effect. Overall, while advancements have been made in differentiable NAS, significant challenges remain, particularly in establishing a formal model that effectively addresses the optimization gap.
