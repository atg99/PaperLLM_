The paper discusses advancements in Neural Architecture Search (NAS), focusing on Differentiable Neural Architecture Search (DNAS). It highlights the NASNet approach, where the optimization of individual building blocks, or cells, leads to a stacked model, enhancing model transferability and reducing the search space. However, challenges arise from the disconnection between cell structures and the final multi-cell models.

The paper also explores Evolutionary Algorithms (EA) in NAS, where architectures are treated as genotypes evolving through generations. While EA can gradually improve architectures, it has significant computational costs, especially with large search spaces, leading to extensive GPU time requirements. Moreover, EA approaches often exhibit limited transferability.

The discussion then shifts to gradient descent, a foundational technique in machine learning that has evolved since the 19th century. The introduction of DNAS merges gradient descent with NAS, treating hyperparameter tuning as a continuous optimization problem. This method utilizes a supernetwork to evaluate multiple architectures simultaneously, significantly reducing computational demands compared to traditional methods like EA and reinforcement learning. DARTS, a notable method within DNAS, employs a cell-based paradigm, enhancing computational efficiency and contributing to the broader accessibility of NAS.
