The paper discusses advancements in neural architecture search (NAS) methods, specifically focusing on the P-DARTS and SDARTS approaches. P-DARTS utilizes a fixed 8-cell proxy network that evolves into a 20-cell final model, optimizing search efficiency by reducing candidate operations based on performance scores. This method significantly enhances performance by approximately 0.5% on CIFAR-10/CIFAR-100 while decreasing search costs from 1.5 GPU days to 0.3 GPU days compared to DARTS.

SDARTS, introduced by Chen et al., aims to stabilize the bi-level optimization process inherent in DARTS. The authors highlight the correlation between the optimization gap of the proxy model and the spectral norm of the Hessian matrix of validation loss. To address this, SDARTS employs two strategies: SDARTS-RS, which utilizes random smoothing to minimize validation loss under small perturbations, and SDARTS-ADV, which enhances adversarial robustness by minimizing worst-case loss through a multistep Projected Gradient Descent. While both methods improve the stability and generalizability of DARTS, SDARTS-ADV incurs higher computational costs, whereas SDARTS-RS sacrifices some accuracy. The paper illustrates these concepts and their implications for performance improvements in the context of NAS.
