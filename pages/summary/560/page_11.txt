This section discusses advancements in Neural Architecture Search (NAS), specifically focusing on differentiable approaches like DARTS and its variants, as well as BigNAS and UNAS. 

DARTS, along with its modified versions SDARTS-RS and SDARTS-ADV, demonstrates a landscape of validation accuracy in relation to architectural parameters, highlighting the efficacy of differentiable search methods. BigNAS improves upon traditional NAS by employing single-stage training methods that utilize techniques such as inplace distillation and exponential learning rate decay. It incorporates a coarse-to-fine architectural selection process, allowing for effective training of both large and small architectures, achieving a top-1 accuracy of 80.9% on ImageNet with a model of 9.5 million parameters.

Meanwhile, UNAS combines differentiable NAS with reinforcement learning, leveraging a corrected REINFORCE algorithm and a Gumbel-Softmax sampled DNAS approach. This hybrid method enables the search for both differentiable and non-differentiable objectives, addressing the variance issues associated with REINFORCE by introducing control variates. Furthermore, UNAS introduces a novel objective function to mitigate architectural overfitting by considering the generalization gap between training and validation losses. This method outperforms previous DNAS techniques on benchmark datasets like CIFAR-10/100 and ImageNet while maintaining a competitive search cost similar to DARTS.
