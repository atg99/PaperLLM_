The paper discusses advancements in Neural Architecture Search (NAS) methods, particularly focusing on HardCoRe-NAS and its performance compared to existing techniques like DARTS, FBNet, and ProxylessNAS. HardCoRe-NAS aims to find high-accuracy architectures while strictly adhering to latency constraints. It reformulates the bilevel optimization problem to include a hard latency constraint, allowing for a more efficient search process. Experimental results indicate that HardCoRe-NAS achieves lower latency and improved top-1 accuracy on ImageNet compared to previous methods, demonstrating its effectiveness.

Additionally, the paper highlights the role of reinforcement learning in RADARS, which aids the differentiable search process by reducing computational and memory costs. RADARS iteratively explores and exploits promising subsets of operations, enabling it to achieve competitive performance within constrained memory and time limits on ImageNet.

The paper also notes the challenges posed by search space restrictions in differentiable NAS methods, particularly with DARTS. ProxylessNAS attempts to circumvent these limitations by searching for entire architectures rather than relying on a cell-based approach, although this increases computational demands, which are mitigated through a binarization strategy. Overall, the research emphasizes the ongoing efforts to enhance NAS methodologies for better performance and resource efficiency.
