The paper provides a survey on Differentiable Neural Architecture Search (DNAS), focusing particularly on the DARTS (Differentiable ARchiTecture Search) family of approaches, which has gained prominence in the field. DARTS employs a cell-based architecture where the search space is modular, consisting of two types of cells: normal cells, which constitute the bulk of the architecture, and reduction cells, which handle dimensionality reduction. Each cell is structured as a directed acyclic graph, with nodes representing intermediary data representations and edges connecting these nodes through a combination of operations.

The paper also discusses various challenges associated with DARTS, including issues related to computational efficiency, over-representation of skip connections, and optimization inconsistencies. It highlights several DNAS methods that have emerged in response to these challenges, categorizing them into DARTS-based and non-DARTS-based approaches. The taxonomy presented illustrates the breadth of research in this area, indicating a growing interest in enhancing neural network design through efficient automation.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the significance of DARTS in advancing DNAS methodologies while acknowledging the need for ongoing improvements to address its limitations.
