The paper discusses advancements in Differentiable Neural Architecture Search (DNAS), focusing on methods like FBNet and its successor FBNetV2, as well as VIM-NAS. 

FBNet utilizes a masked output approach where the output of a layer is defined as a sum of sampled blocks, with the sampling probabilities determined by parameters Θ. The Gumbel-Softmax function is employed to convert discrete masks into a continuous distribution, facilitating a differentiable search process. Empirical results indicate that FBNet achieves superior accuracy on ImageNet compared to DARTS while maintaining a lower search cost. FBNet-A also demonstrates low latency on mobile devices.

FBNetV2 addresses the limitation of small search spaces found in its predecessor. It expands the search space significantly and introduces DMaskingNAS, which employs weight-sharing approximations to efficiently explore additional hyperparameters. The method retains the layer-wise DNAS paradigm but incorporates a channel-masking mechanism with Gumbel-Softmax. FBNetV2 can search for different input resolutions without incurring substantial computational costs, achieving a search space up to 10^14 times larger while maintaining a similar computational cost to FBNet.

VIM-NAS introduces a novel perspective by treating the architectural distribution as a latent representation of data points, aiming to maximize the mutual information between the architecture and the dataset. This method reformulates the DNAS objective using a Gaussian noise model, enhancing the speed of the architecture search process.

Overall, the paper highlights significant progress in automating neural network design through differentiable architecture search techniques, emphasizing efficiency and accuracy improvements over previous methods.
