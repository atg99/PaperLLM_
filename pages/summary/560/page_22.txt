The paper discusses advancements in neural architecture search (NAS) techniques, focusing on the efficiency and effectiveness of various methods. The One-Shot Neural Architecture Search (OFA) framework is highlighted for its novel training process that progressively fine-tunes sub-networks of decreasing sizes, achieving notable performance on ImageNet with significantly reduced GPU training hours compared to DARTS. 

The authors of DOTS also achieved a remarkable reduction in computational costs through a decoupled search process, which streamlined the topology and operation searches, allowing faster convergence. 

FBNet is introduced as a DNAS framework designed for low-power hardware, emphasizing latency and computational efficiency. Unlike DARTS, FBNet organizes its search around layers rather than cell blocks, allowing for a larger diversity of architectures. It employs a combined loss function that includes a hardware-aware latency component, which is differentiable thanks to the use of a latency lookup table. This approach facilitates a more efficient search through a stochastic supernet model, sampling one candidate block per layer based on a probability distribution.

Overall, these methods demonstrate significant progress in optimizing neural networks for practical applications, particularly in mobile settings.
