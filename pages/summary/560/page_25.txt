The paper discusses advancements in Differentiable Neural Architecture Search (D-NAS), specifically focusing on D-DARTS, an extension of the DARTS framework. D-DARTS maintains the cell-based design of DARTS but enhances it by allowing each cell to represent a distinct part of the overall architecture. This distributed approach enables the optimization of individual cells, which function as small independent neural networks.

A key innovation in D-DARTS is the introduction of the ablation loss function, L_AB, inspired by Game Theory and Shapley Values. This function assesses the marginal contribution of each cell to the overall validation loss, thereby quantifying individual cell performance relative to the average contribution across the architecture. This mechanism facilitates the optimization of cells by treating the architecture search as a cooperative game.

Moreover, D-DARTS allows for the incorporation of existing handcrafted architectures, such as ResNet50 and Xception, as starting points for the search process. By treating these architectures as local minima within the search space, D-DARTS significantly enhances the diversity of architectures discovered, achieving competitive performance on both small-scale datasets like CIFAR-10 and large-scale datasets such as ImageNet.

Overall, the D-DARTS framework demonstrates a substantial improvement in neural network design efficiency through its innovative search process and optimization strategies.
