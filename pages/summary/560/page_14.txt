The paper discusses advancements in neural architecture search (NAS) methods, focusing on DARTS (Differentiable Architecture Search) and its variants. It highlights the challenges associated with optimization gaps between proxy models and final models in DARTS. To address these issues, the authors propose a new approach called DOTS, which separates the topology and operation search into two stages. In the first stage, DOTS continuously relaxes the topology search space into weights associated with edge combinations, thereby reducing search costs. The aggregation of weights is facilitated by an architectural annealing scheme, which helps to bridge the optimization gap.

In the second stage, DOTS conducts an operation search to identify the optimal operation for each edge, similar to DARTS. To maintain the integrity of topology-related operations, DOTS employs a group strategy that divides the operation search space into subspaces, allowing independent searches within each. This approach successfully preserves both topology-oriented and agnostic operations, leading to improved accuracy scores over DARTS, specifically a +0.63% increase on CIFAR-10 and +2.7% on ImageNet.

Additionally, the paper introduces EnTranNAS, a different solution to the optimization gap problem. EnTranNAS consists of Engine-cells, which are differentiable cells similar to DARTS, and Transit-cells that handle the derived architecture. The authors argue that focusing on a single cell is sufficient for effective differentiable neural architecture search, contrasting with the broader approach of DARTS. The advancements presented indicate significant potential for improving NAS methodologies.
