The paper discusses the Differentiable Architecture Search (DARTS) method for searching convolutional neural network (CNN) architectures. DARTS operates within a search space defined by seven operations: skip connection, max pooling, average pooling, separable convolutions (3x3 and 5x5), and dilated convolutions (3x3 and 5x5). The search strategy involves progressively pruning edges from cell nodes until only two remain, utilizing a mixed output for each edge that is weighted by architectural parameters. These parameters are learned through gradient descent to minimize validation loss while a supernet, which represents potential architectures, is trained to minimize training loss.

DARTS formulates a bi-level optimization problem where the objective is to minimize validation loss subject to the optimal weights of the supernet. The gradient of the architectural parameters is computed using a specific formula that accounts for both the loss with respect to the parameters and the optimal weights. After the search phase, the final architecture is determined by discretizing the internal structure of each cell type based on edge selection.

The key advantage of DARTS is its efficiency; it avoids the need to fully train each candidate architecture, allowing it to derive performance feedback rapidly using the supernet as an approximator. This makes DARTS significantly faster than traditional reinforcement learning or evolutionary algorithm-based approaches.
