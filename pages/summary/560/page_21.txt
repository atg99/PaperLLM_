The paper discusses advancements in Differentiable Neural Architecture Search (DARTS) methods, focusing on strategies to enhance computational efficiency and latency reduction in neural network design. 

CDARTS addresses the over-representation issue in neural architecture by integrating an L1 regularization factor to the architectural weights of non-parametric operations. This adjustment successfully prevents certain operations from dominating the architecture.

Chen et al. define the problem of Neural Architecture Search (NAS) in practical scenarios as the ability to efficiently search architectures on a proxy dataset like CIFAR-10 and transfer them to more complex datasets like ImageNet. This contrasts with ProxylessNAS, which searches directly on the target dataset and incorporates latency constraints specific to hardware, achieving a notable accuracy improvement while maintaining mobile latency akin to MobileNetV2.

PC-DARTS aims to enhance computational efficiency by performing architecture search on a subset of randomly sampled channels, significantly reducing memory overhead and search costs. This method allows PC-DARTS to achieve better accuracy on ImageNet compared to ProxylessNAS while cutting search costs in half. To address inconsistencies in operation selection across sampled channels, PC-DARTS introduces shared learning parameters for edge normalization.

Lastly, the Once-for-All (OFA) approach decouples the training and search phases by utilizing a large supernet, allowing for configuration changes without further training. This method includes a training phase for optimizing subnetworks and a NAS phase that focuses on hardware-aware specialization, targeting specific latency requirements. However, optimizing numerous subnetworks simultaneously poses significant computational challenges.
