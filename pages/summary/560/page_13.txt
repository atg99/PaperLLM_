The paper discusses the challenges and advancements in Differentiable Neural Architecture Search (DARTS), particularly focusing on the optimization gap linked to architecture selection. Wang et al. highlight that the weight values associated with operations in DARTS, denoted as alpha (𝛼), do not always accurately reflect the strength of those operations. They introduce the concept of discretization accuracy at convergence, which measures the performance of an operation after fine-tuning the network. Their findings, illustrated in a bar chart, reveal discrepancies between 𝛼 values and discretization accuracy, emphasizing the complexities of architecture selection.

To address these issues, the authors propose a perturbation-based architecture selection method (PT), which involves masking operations to identify the most critical ones based on performance drops. This method preserves the supernet's accuracy and significantly enhances performance when used in conjunction with DARTS, achieving a notable increase in accuracy on CIFAR-10.

Additionally, the paper introduces 𝛽-DARTS, which employs a simple regularization technique called Beta-Decay, inspired by L2 regularization. This method imposes restrictions on the architectural parameters to mitigate optimization discrepancies, leading to improved generalization and robustness. The theoretical analysis indicates that 𝛽-DARTS achieves competitive results on both small-scale and large-scale datasets while conducting searches primarily on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100.

Gu et al. further critique DARTS, arguing that the ranking of operations does not accurately reflect their contribution to the final model performance, particularly for topology-related operations like skip connections.
