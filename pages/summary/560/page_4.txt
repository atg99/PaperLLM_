This survey article focuses on Differentiable Neural Architecture Search (DNAS), highlighting its significance in the rapidly evolving field of Neural Architecture Search (NAS). The authors review 26 papers published since 2019 in prominent machine learning and computer vision venues, aiming to provide a comprehensive overview of DNAS, particularly in computer vision tasks, while also touching upon its applications in other fields.

The paper aims to contribute to the understanding of DNAS by proposing a novel taxonomy that categorizes methods based on the specific challenges they address, rather than their origins. The identified challenges include bridging the optimization gap between proxy and final models, countering the over-representation of non-parametric operations, improving computational efficiency and reducing inference latency, and overcoming search space restrictions. 

The article is organized into several sections: it begins with a discussion on the black-box nature of DNAS and the emerging field of explainable AI. It then presents the proposed taxonomy, reviews recent works, and discusses the implications of DNAS for NAS and deep learning. The authors provide a tabular summary of the reviewed methods and conclude with insights on future research directions. Overall, this survey is positioned as a pioneering effort to consolidate and clarify the landscape of DNAS, emphasizing its challenges and contributions to the field.
