The paper discusses advancements in Differentiable Neural Architecture Search (DARTS) aimed at improving the efficiency and effectiveness of neural network design automation. 

FairDARTS addresses gradient approximation issues by substituting the softmax function with the sigmoid function. This change allows for independent adjustments of operation weights, enhancing fairness in selection and resulting in better gradient approximations. FairDARTS also introduces a novel zero-one loss function, L01, which encourages architectural weights to converge towards binary values, minimizing the discretization gap. The total loss combines L01 with a validation loss, enabling effective backpropagation for optimizing architectural weights.

ProxylessNAS takes a different approach by conducting searches directly on large-scale datasets, such as ImageNet, rather than relying on smaller proxy datasets. This method employs binary gates for one-hot vector outputs, significantly reducing memory consumption by instantiating only one path at a time during the search process. Although ProxylessNAS outperforms DARTS by 2% on ImageNet, it incurs a higher search cost, increasing from 1.5 GPU days for DARTS to 8.3 GPU days.

Progressive DARTS (P-DARTS) further aims to bridge the optimization gap by enhancing the proxy model during the search phase. It gradually increases the depth of the proxy network, improving the alignment between the search and final architectures.

Overall, these methods highlight ongoing efforts to refine neural architecture search processes while balancing performance and resource efficiency.
