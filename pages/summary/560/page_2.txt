This paper discusses the evolving field of Neural Architecture Search (NAS) and the associated challenges, such as memory efficiency, transferability between datasets, and computational efficiency. The authors highlight the integration of various methodologies to enhance NAS, particularly emphasizing the potential of parameter space differentiability with advanced optimizers for deep learning training.

A typical NAS framework comprises three essential components: a search space, a search strategy, and an evaluation strategy. The search space includes all possible hyperparameter configurations, typically represented as a discrete space, though it can be made continuous. The search strategy, or optimizer, explores this space and samples candidate architectures, while the evaluation strategy assesses the performance of these candidates, ideally without complete training to minimize computational demands.

The paper outlines the historical context of NAS, starting with heuristic-based methods for automating neural network design, such as random search and grid sampling. However, these approaches have been largely supplanted by machine learning-based methods, particularly Differentiable NAS. 

Reinforcement Learning (RL) is identified as one of the first ML-based NAS methods, where an RL agent selects hyperparameters based on past evaluations, with a reward system based on model performance. To address the time-intensive nature of training models, the authors mention the use of parallelism and asynchronous updates to expedite the process. The paper sets the stage for a detailed exploration of various NAS approaches and their organizational structure.
