The paper discusses advancements in Differentiable Neural Architecture Search (DARTS) with a focus on DARTS- and Path-Regularized DARTS (PR-DARTS). DARTS- enhances robustness and stability during the search process, achieving notable performance improvements of 0.5% on CIFAR-10 and 4.5% on ImageNet compared to standard DARTS, while also being more computationally efficient than previous methods like R-DARTS. 

PR-DARTS stands out as the first method to provide a theoretical analysis of the over-representation of skip connections in neural architectures, linking it to performance collapse. The authors introduce a convergence theorem that shows an increase in skip connections accelerates the supernet's convergence, which inadvertently biases the DARTS algorithm towards these connections. To address this, PR-DARTS incorporates stochastic binary gates to regulate operation selection and employs group-structured sparsity regularization to ensure balanced weight adjustments across operations.

The proposed regularization methods involve specific loss functions targeting skip and non-skip connections, along with path regularization to mitigate competition between deep and shallow cells. The overall optimization problem is reformulated to prioritize performance-oriented architectures, leading to empirical results that demonstrate PR-DARTS significantly outperforms standard DARTS and its predecessors on image classification tasks.
