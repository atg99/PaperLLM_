The paper discusses the applications of Differentiable Neural Architecture Search (DNAS), particularly highlighting its focus on improving Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) for computer vision tasks. Major DNAS frameworks like DARTS, ProxylessNAS, and FBNet have predominantly validated their methods on image classification and object detection datasets. However, the authors also explore various other applications of DNAS across different fields.

In remote sensing, DNAS has been applied to analyze radar images and scene classification. For instance, a DARTS-based method was utilized for optimizing CNN backbones for maritime radar image analysis. In natural language processing, DARTS and its variants have been employed to design Recurrent Neural Networks for language modeling. Additionally, AdaBERT leveraged DNAS to compress BERT models into smaller, more efficient versions for specific tasks.

Medical applications of DNAS include the analysis of MRI and volumetric images, where DNAS approaches are used for tasks like organ segmentation in cancer patients. In reinforcement learning, RL-DARTS applied DARTS to identify architectures suitable for both on-policy and off-policy algorithms, achieving significant performance improvements in various environments.

The paper also notes efforts in audio processing, where DNAS is aimed at enhancing speech recognition through the discovery of new CNN architectures. Furthermore, hardware optimization is a critical aspect of DNAS, with several studies focusing on minimizing computational costs and latency across different platforms, which is essential for real-world applications.

In the discussion, the authors review 26 recent DNAS approaches, noting that a significant majority are based on DARTS. They emphasize the ongoing relevance and popularity of DARTS since its introduction in 2019, and highlight a clear distinction between DARTS-based and non-DARTS-based methods in terms of the challenges they address. Overall, the paper underscores the versatility and growing impact of DNAS across various domains.
