The paper discusses the advancements in differentiable neural architecture search (DNAS) with a focus on FBNetV5, which improves upon previous FBNet models. FBNetV5 specifically targets enhancing the transferability of DNAS architectures across various computer vision tasks, including semantic segmentation, object detection, and image classification. This is achieved by integrating a differentiable NAS process with FBNetV3's unique approach, which aligns architectures with training hyperparameters to optimize performance.

FBNetV5 utilizes a supernet trained on a multitask dataset derived from ImageNet, enabling it to disentangle the architecture search from the training process. The method employs topology search to identify the best backbone architecture for each task simultaneously. The architecture search leverages a block-based search space, where architectures are represented as binary masks sampled from a Bernoulli distribution.

To optimize computational efficiency, the authors implement the REINFORCE algorithm along with importance sampling, reducing the number of required forward and backward passes in the search algorithm. The results demonstrate that FBNetV5 surpasses previous NAS methods across various datasets, achieving superior performance in the targeted tasks. The paper highlights the innovative search process of FBNetV5, which effectively balances computational cost and architectural optimization.
