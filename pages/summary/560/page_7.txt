The paper discusses the limitations and challenges of Differentiable Neural Architecture Search (DARTS) in the context of neural network design automation. Key issues identified include:

1. **Gradient Approximation Inconsistencies**: The use of gradient approximation methods can lead to inconsistencies in the optimization process, affecting architectural parameters. The softmax function's limited convergence ability results in a probability distribution where values are closely clustered, complicating the discretization process. This creates a challenge in selecting between operations with very similar probabilities.

2. **Overrepresentation of Skip Connections**: DARTS tends to favor skip connections, leading to architectures that resemble residual blocks. This is problematic as skip connections do not learn data representations effectively, potentially causing global performance collapse when they are selected inappropriately.

3. **Search Space Navigation**: Efficiently exploring the search space is a challenge, as every possible connection must be instantiated in memory. This is particularly problematic with large datasets like ImageNet, where computational resources become a concern. While techniques like Automatic Mixed Precision can help, they introduce risks of numerical instability.

4. **Restricted Search Space**: DARTS has a limited search space, focusing on only two building blocks, which simplifies the optimization problem but restricts the potential for discovering high-performing architectures.

The paper emphasizes the need for improved DNAS approaches that address these limitations rather than relying solely on existing methods like AMP.
