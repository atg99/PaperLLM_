The paper discusses advancements in architecture selection methods for deep learning networks, specifically focusing on DARTS (Differentiable Architecture Search) and its derivatives, including DARTS+PT and NoisyDARTS. The authors highlight that DARTS+PT effectively prevents skip connections from dominating the architecture selection process, referencing theoretical work by Greff et al. on the robustness of ResNet layers to reordering. This robustness implies that various edges in a cell aim to estimate the same optimal feature maps, which is crucial for the training phase.

Wang et al. established a formula for estimating optimal feature maps, emphasizing that a well-optimized supernet leads to a widening gap between architectural parameters associated with skip connections and convolutional layers. They noted that this issue arises when architecture selection relies too heavily on these parameters, but DARTS+PT mitigates this problem while retaining the original search algorithm.

NoisyDARTS introduces a novel approach by incorporating unbiased random noise during training, which helps to prevent excessive emphasis on skip connections. The addition of Gaussian noise improves generalization and smooths the loss landscape, resulting in better performance than prior DARTS variations across several datasets.

Further, Ye et al. introduced the Beta-Decay regularization method in their work, 𝛽-DARTS, which aims to reduce the optimization gap and address the over-representation of skip connections. Their theoretical framework suggests that the Beta-Decay mechanism fosters fair competition among operations and diminishes the dominance of skip connections, enhancing the overall effectiveness of the architecture search process.
