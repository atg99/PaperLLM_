The paper discusses advancements in Neural Architecture Search (NAS), particularly focusing on differentiable NAS (DNAS) techniques. It highlights the significance of regularization methods, applied either before or after softmax relaxation, in preventing dominance of skip connections and enhancing performance. This is crucial in addressing the unbalanced search space that arises from mixing parametric and non-parametric operations. Alternative methods such as early-stopping mechanisms are noted but are criticized for their arbitrary nature compared to formal regularization approaches.

The paper also emphasizes the reduction of search costs in certain DNAS methods, enabling their implementation on consumer-grade hardware, thus making DNAS more accessible. Some strategies prioritize computational efficiency over inference latency, allowing deployment on low-resource devices. It points out that many DNAS approaches have moved away from the traditional DARTS paradigm due to its inherent search space restrictions, leading to the development of novel search spaces like those used in FBNet and ProxylessNAS.

Despite significant progress, the paper notes that DNAS still faces limitations, as no single method has addressed all identified challenges simultaneously. This has contributed to the continued popularity of DARTS. The authors argue that DNAS is maturing, with some methods being integrated into major AutoML libraries, yet it remains an area ripe for further development and application beyond computer vision, including improvements in transformers and generative networks.

Finally, the paper underscores the importance of Explainable AI (XAI) in the deployment of deep learning systems, especially in high-stakes scenarios like automated decision-making.
