The paper presents a comparative analysis of various methods for story visualization using the CLEVR-SV and Pororo-SV datasets. It highlights the challenges faced by different models, such as ImageGAN, SVC, SVFN, and StoryGAN, in generating consistent and high-quality image sequences that accurately reflect the narrative described in the text inputs.

The Pororo dataset consists of 16,000 one-second video clips featuring 13 distinct characters, organized into 408 movie stories. Each video clip is accompanied by a manually written description averaging 13.6 words. The authors modified this dataset for story visualization by treating each description as text input for the model, ultimately creating 15,336 description-story pairs for training and testing.

Results demonstrate that ImageGAN struggles with generating coherent sequences, as it often switches settings and displays inconsistent character appearances. While SVC and SVFN show some improvement, they still fall short in quality, especially in the initial images produced. In contrast, StoryGAN excels due to its use of a Story Encoder, which enhances the quality and consistency of the generated images.

The paper also includes experiments that assess how different models handle variations in character names, further reinforcing the superior performance of StoryGAN in both image quality and narrative coherence. Overall, the findings underscore the importance of model architecture in achieving effective story visualization.
