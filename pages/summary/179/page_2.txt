The paper introduces StoryGAN, a framework designed for story visualization, which generates a sequence of static frames to accurately depict story plots where motion is less critical. Unlike traditional video clips, which often utilize simple sentence inputs and feature static backgrounds, StoryGAN addresses the complexities of narrative by capturing essential scene changes that align with plot developments.

Inspired by Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), StoryGAN operates as a two-player game involving a generator and a discriminator. It employs a sequential conditional GAN model, utilizing a recurrent neural network (RNN) to integrate previously generated images with the current sentence's image generation. The Context Encoder module plays a vital role, utilizing a stack of GRU cells and a novel Text2Gist cell to convert sentence descriptions into high-dimensional feature vectors that adapt as the story evolves.

To maintain consistency across the generated images, StoryGAN implements a two-level GAN framework. This includes an image-level discriminator for assessing the relevance of each sentence to its corresponding image and a story-level discriminator to evaluate the coherence of the entire image sequence with the overall narrative.

The paper develops two datasets, CLEVR-SV and Pororo-SV, for evaluating the story visualization task. Empirical results demonstrate that StoryGAN effectively captures the narrative's progression and surpasses existing models in generating coherent and high-quality image sequences, as confirmed by both quantitative metrics and human evaluations.

Additionally, the paper discusses related work in generative models, emphasizing the distinctions between story visualization and other tasks such as conditional text-to-image transformation, video generation, and visual storytelling. These comparisons highlight the unique challenges and methodologies employed by StoryGAN in visualizing complex narratives.
