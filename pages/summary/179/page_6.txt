The paper presents various baseline models for story visualization, including ImageGAN, SVC, and SVFN, each with distinct methodologies for generating images based on narrative descriptions. 

ImageGAN operates independently for each image generation without utilizing a story discriminator or encoder, relying instead on a concatenation of an encoded story and noise for input. This basic version serves as a foundation for comparison. 

SVC enhances ImageGAN by incorporating a story discriminator and replacing the Text2Gist cell with a simple concatenation of encoded story and description feature vectors. This model addresses some consistency issues but struggles with plausibility in outcomes, particularly in longer sequences.

SVFN further refines SVC's approach by employing a filter network instead of concatenation, transforming each sentence into a filter that convolves with the encoded story. This adjustment aims to improve the coherence of generated images.

The CLEVR-SV dataset is introduced, modified from the original CLEVR dataset for story visualization. It consists of 10,000 training and 3,000 testing image sequences, with strict rules governing the attributes of objects depicted in the stories. The study highlights how each model performs in maintaining story consistency and coherence, revealing that while ImageGAN struggles with attribute consistency, SVC improves on this but still generates implausible images. SVFN offers partial improvements.

In contrast, StoryGAN excels by effectively tracking narrative progression through its Text2Gist cell, maintaining object consistency with both story and image discriminators, and initializing the Text2Gist cell with the Story Encoder. This leads to superior image generation, as evidenced by the Structural Similarity Index (SSIM) comparisons, where StoryGAN outperforms all baseline models.

The paper also includes tests on the Cartoon dataset, demonstrating the robustness of StoryGAN in maintaining consistency across frames when altering initial object descriptions. Overall, StoryGAN is shown to be more effective in generating coherent and plausible images from narrative inputs compared to its predecessors.
