The paper discusses the challenges of generating sequential images where characters, motion, and backgrounds change significantly from image to image. It identifies two main problems: effectively updating contextual information to capture background changes, and combining new inputs with random noise to visualize character changes. To address these issues, the authors propose a deep recurrent neural network (RNN) based Context Encoder.

The Context Encoder uses a two-layer structure: the lower layer employs standard Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) cells, while the upper layer introduces a novel Text2Gist cell. The GRU layer processes concatenated inputs of the current sentence and Gaussian noise to produce an output vector. The Text2Gist cell integrates this output with the story context to generate a "Gist" vector, which encapsulates all necessary information for image generation at each time step. The update mechanism for the Text2Gist cell involves gates that control the retention and reset of information, allowing for the adaptation of context information during generation.

Additionally, the paper describes the use of two discriminators in the StoryGAN framework: an image discriminator that evaluates the consistency of generated images with the sentence and initial context, and a story discriminator that ensures global consistency across the generated image sequence. This dual-discriminator approach is crucial as the same sentence can lead to different images depending on the context, emphasizing the importance of contextual information for accurate generation.

The findings suggest that the Text2Gist cell is more effective than traditional RNNs for story visualization, and the discriminators play a significant role in maintaining both local and global coherence in the generated sequences.
