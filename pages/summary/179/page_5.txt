The paper discusses the architecture of a story discriminator within a generative model called StoryGAN, which is designed to create visual stories. The architecture consists of two main components: an image encoder that processes image sequences into feature vectors and a text encoder that converts multi-sentence stories into feature vectors. The image encoder operates as a deep convolutional network, while the text encoder functions as a multi-layer perceptron, both producing vectors of the same dimension.

The global consistency score between images and stories is calculated using a formula that incorporates an element-wise product of the encoded features, alongside learned weights and biases. This score is normalized with a sigmoid function, allowing the model to evaluate both local matching and overall consistency of the generated story with the images.

Training involves optimizing the parameters of the generator and discriminators through a defined objective function, which balances losses from image and story discriminators. The training procedure, detailed in a pseudo-code format, includes iterative updates for the discriminators and generator, utilizing mini-batches of story-sentence and story-image pairs.

The experimental section evaluates the StoryGAN model against existing methods in story visualization, particularly contrasting it with conditional video generation, which was found to produce inferior results. The comparisons focus on ablated versions of StoryGAN to ensure fair evaluation, with all models adhering to a consistent architecture for comparison.

In summary, the paper presents a novel approach to story visualization using a structured generative model that integrates image and text processing, demonstrating its effectiveness through empirical evaluation against alternative methods.
