The paper presents StoryGAN, a generative adversarial network designed to create coherent visual stories from textual narratives. The framework consists of multiple components: a Story Encoder, a Context Encoder, and an image generator, along with two discriminators for evaluating the generated images. 

The Story Encoder transforms the entire story into a low-dimensional embedding vector, which serves as the initial state for the Context Encoder. This encoder processes each sentence, incorporating contextual information to produce a vector for image generation. The image generator then creates images based on these vectors, ensuring that the generated image sequence is both locally consistent (each image aligns with its corresponding sentence) and globally consistent (the sequence of images coherently represents the full story).

The Story Encoder employs a stochastic mapping to encode the story, sampling from a normal distribution to facilitate randomness in the generation process. This approach addresses discontinuities in the story space, allowing for a more compact and meaningful representation. The parameters of the encoder are optimized alongside other components using backpropagation. Additionally, a regularization term based on Kullback-Leibler divergence is introduced to maintain smoothness in the latent semantic space.

The Context Encoder is designed to handle the dynamic aspects of story visualization, recognizing the need for a distinction between static and dynamic features in the generated content. Overall, StoryGAN aims to enhance the narrative visualization process by ensuring both semantic coherence and visual fidelity in the generated images.
