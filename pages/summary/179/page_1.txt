The paper presents StoryGAN, a novel approach for the task of Story Visualization, which involves generating a sequence of images that correspond to sentences in a multi-sentence paragraph. Unlike traditional video generation, which prioritizes continuity, Story Visualization emphasizes global consistency across scenes and characters, a challenge not effectively addressed by existing image or video generation techniques.

StoryGAN employs a sequential conditional GAN framework, featuring a deep Context Encoder that tracks the narrative flow, and two discriminators that operate at both story and image levels to improve image quality and sequence consistency. The authors created modified datasets, CLEVR-SV and Pororo-SV, to evaluate the model's performance. Results indicate that StoryGAN surpasses state-of-the-art models in image quality, contextual consistency, and human evaluation metrics.

The paper outlines two primary challenges in the Story Visualization task: ensuring that images coherently depict the entire story and maintaining logical progression in object appearance and background layout. The authors argue that sequentially applying text-to-image generation methods fails to produce coherent sequences necessary for effective storytelling. Thus, StoryGAN's design aims to address these challenges by enriching image generation with contextual information and guiding the process through its dual discriminator architecture.
