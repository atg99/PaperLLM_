The paper presents a novel 6-channel image representation for encoding event data, particularly focusing on negative polarity events. This representation integrates the spatial distribution of events through histograms of positive and negative events, alongside statistical information such as mean and standard deviation of event timestamps. The proposed methodology normalizes timestamp data over a specified time interval, enhancing the representation's ability to capture temporal dynamics.

The first two channels of the representation are dedicated to the histograms of positive and negative events, while the remaining four channels store the mean and standard deviation of normalized timestamps for both types of events. This dual-channel approach allows for a comprehensive understanding of how events are distributed over time at each pixel location.

The paper also explores semantic segmentation using a convolutional neural network (CNN) architecture based on Xception. The architecture is designed to handle the dense event data representations, maintaining the same resolution for input and output, thereby facilitating per-pixel classification. Key features of the architecture include skip connections to mitigate vanishing gradient issues and an auxiliary loss to enhance learning convergence.

Training is conducted using back-propagation with a soft-max cross entropy loss function, allowing for effective optimization of the model. Overall, the proposed representation and segmentation approach aim to leverage event-based data for improved performance in visual tasks.
