This paper presents the first results on semantic segmentation using data from event cameras, building an encoder-decoder architecture that learns segmentation solely from event data. The study proposes a novel representation for event camera data, which includes both event histograms and their temporal distribution. Experimental results indicate that this approach is effective for training semantic segmentation models, outperforming previous event data representations across various integration intervals.

The authors highlight two key complementary scenarios: event cameras struggle with stationary objects, as shown in an example of a car at a crosswalk, while they excel in low-light conditions where traditional cameras fail. The evaluation methodology involved generating approximate semantic segmentation labels from the DDD17 event-based dataset, as no benchmarks currently exist for this task.

The findings suggest that combining event camera data with conventional imagery yields better results, capitalizing on the strengths of both modalities. Future work will focus on improving the generation of semantic segmentation labels through domain adaptation techniques and event camera simulators, which currently lack such capabilities.
