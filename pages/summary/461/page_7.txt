The paper presents a comparison of semantic segmentation performance using different input representations on the Ev-Seg dataset, focusing on event-based and conventional cameras. Various encoding methods are analyzed, including basic dense encoding, temporal dense encoding, and a novel approach labeled "Ours." The results demonstrate that the proposed method achieves the highest accuracy and mean Intersection over Union (MIoU) across different time intervals of 50ms, 10ms, and 250ms.

Significantly, the combination of grayscale images with the proposed encoding method yields the best results, surpassing both event-based and grayscale-only approaches. The performance similarities across different time intervals are attributed to normalization of encodings and variability in camera speeds during training, which enhance generalization capabilities.

The paper underscores the advantages of event cameras, particularly their ability to capture motion information, which aids in segmenting moving objects more effectively compared to conventional cameras. While conventional cameras excel in capturing detailed appearance information, they struggle in extreme lighting conditions and with small object detection. Conversely, event cameras face challenges with static objects but excel in dynamic environments. Overall, the findings suggest that integrating event-based information with conventional methods can significantly enhance semantic segmentation outcomes.
