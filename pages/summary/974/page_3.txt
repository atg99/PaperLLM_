Dimitri Bertsekas is a distinguished academic with a background in Mechanical and Electrical Engineering from the National Technical University of Athens and a Ph.D. in system science from MIT. His career includes faculty roles at Stanford University and the University of Illinois before joining MIT's Electrical Engineering and Computer Science Department in 1979, where he served until his retirement in 2016. He currently holds the position of Fulton Professor of Computational Decision Making at Arizona State University.

Bertsekas' research and teaching encompass deterministic optimization, dynamic programming, stochastic control, large-scale computation, and data communication networks. He has authored numerous papers and eighteen influential books, some of which are standard textbooks at MIT and ASU.

His contributions have been recognized through several prestigious awards, including the INFORMS Prize for Research Excellence, the AACC John R. Ragazzini Education Award, and the IEEE Control Systems Award. He was elected to the National Academy of Engineering in 2001 for his pioneering work in optimization/control theory and its applications in data communications. Bertsekas, along with coauthor John Tsitsiklis, received the INFORMS John von Neumann Theory Prize in 2018 for their work on parallel and distributed computation and neuro-dynamic programming.
