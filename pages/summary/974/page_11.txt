The preface outlines the structure and approach of the book, focusing on the transition from exact dynamic programming (DP) to approximate methods. The authors emphasize the challenges of implementing exact DP algorithms and introduce approximations based on these foundations. The content is organized into four main directions:

1. **From exact to approximate DP**: The authors begin with a discussion of exact DP algorithms, highlighting their complexities before introducing approximation methods.
   
2. **From finite to infinite horizon problems**: Initial chapters deal with finite horizon problems, which are simpler and more intuitive, before advancing to infinite horizon scenarios.

3. **From deterministic to stochastic models**: The authors separately address deterministic and stochastic problems, noting that deterministic models are often simpler and beneficial for certain methods.

4. **From model-based to model-free implementations**: The book contrasts traditional model-based DP with reinforcement learning techniques that utilize simulators, allowing for greater flexibility and implementation ease.

Each section builds on previous methods, presenting more sophisticated variations while providing examples to illustrate concepts. The authors adopt a mathematical style that prioritizes intuitive understanding over rigorous proofs, maintaining modest mathematical prerequisites. This approach aims to make complex ideas more accessible while retaining the essential theoretical foundations of dynamic programming and approximation methods.
