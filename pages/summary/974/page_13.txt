In the preface, the author reflects on the interplay between human insight and computer interactions, emphasizing that mathematical reasoning with algorithmic models is crucial for developing human understanding. The author expresses gratitude to numerous students and colleagues, highlighting significant collaborations over 25 years, particularly with John Tsitsiklis, Janey Yu, and Mengdi Wang, as well as insights gained from Ben Van Roy and Ben Recht regarding policy gradient methods. The author notes that the book was shaped during a course taught at Arizona State University in early 2019, where the supportive environment fostered productivity. The preface also references Richard Bellman's contributions to dynamic programming (DP), illustrating his belief in the importance of approximate techniques and numerical algorithms in computational progress. The author acknowledges Bellman's autobiography and quotes that underline the historical context and challenges faced in the development of DP as a tool for obtaining numerical solutions.
