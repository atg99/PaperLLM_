The section discusses the concept of Euclidean distance as a measure of similarity between data points in two-dimensional, three-dimensional, and n-dimensional spaces. The Euclidean distance formulas are provided for both two and three dimensions, and a generalized formula for n-dimensional space is also introduced. The essence of the Euclidean distance is that a smaller distance indicates a higher similarity between data points. 

In the context of the K-Means clustering algorithm, the main objective is to minimize the total distance between data points and their respective cluster centroids through an iterative process. The algorithm begins by selecting K initial centroids and calculating the Euclidean distance from each data point to these centroids to assign points to the nearest cluster. After the initial assignment, the centroids are recalculated based on the newly formed clusters, and the process of assignment and recalculation continues iteratively until certain stopping criteria are met: no change in centroids, no change in data point assignments, or reaching a maximum iteration limit. The maximum number of iterations can be predefined, for instance, 200 iterations.
