Elbow Method

The Elbow Method is a technique used to determine the optimal number of clusters (K) in K-means clustering. The choice of K is critical as it influences the algorithm's performance, particularly regarding the initial selection of K points. To address this, the method evaluates the algorithm's performance across various K values by calculating the distance between each cluster's centroid and its data points, summing these distances to form a performance indicator.

As K increases, the objective function typically decreases; however, the Elbow Method is particularly effective for smaller K values. It assesses the squared differences for various K values, noting that as K rises, the average distortion decreases, leading to samples being closer to their centroids. The optimal K is identified at the point where further increases in K yield diminishing returns in distortion reduction, creating an "elbow" in the graph of WCSS (Within-Cluster Sum-of-Squares), which measures variance within clusters.

For instance, when K is set to 1, WCSS is high. As K is incremented to 2 and then 3, WCSS continues to decline. However, at K equal to 4, the decrease in WCSS becomes negligible. If K were allowed to equal the number of data points, WCSS would be zero, as each point would be its own cluster.

The formula for WCSS is presented as a summation of distances for each point within clusters. An example illustrates that when K equals 3, it represents an inflection point, suggesting that the optimal number of clusters in this case is three.
