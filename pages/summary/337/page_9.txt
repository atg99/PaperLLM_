The paper addresses the lack of strategies for reliably assessing the general and zero-shot performance of Generative Pre-trained Transformers (GPTs) on event stream datasets. It introduces Event Stream GPT (ESGPT), a tool designed to facilitate the evaluation of GPTs, particularly focusing on generative performance and the viability of these models in few and zero-shot tasks. ESGPT provides pre-built scripts for distributed Bayesian hyperparameter optimization using Weights and Biases (WandB), enhancing the efficiency of model evaluation.

ESGPT includes a PyTorch Lightning module that tracks various metrics for generative model performance, allowing for easy logging and visual assessment through WandB reports during hyperparameter tuning. The system is equipped to evaluate zero-shot performance, which has not been previously studied in this context. It requires a labeled dataset for evaluation, a method for unsupervised forecasting, and a function to translate generated samples into predictions.

To illustrate ESGPT's capabilities, the paper presents examples from the MIMIC-IV dataset focusing on two tasks: in-hospital mortality prediction and 30-day readmission prediction. ESGPT offers tools for hyperparameter tuning, traditional fine-tuning, and zero-shot evaluation. Results from the MIMIC-IV cohort demonstrate the effectiveness of hyperparameter tuning and the performance of neural network architectures, yielding AUROCs of 0.67 and 0.65 for traditional classifiers, and 0.79 and 0.57 for neural networks on the respective tasks. Overall, ESGPT fills a critical gap in evaluating the performance of GPTs on event stream datasets.
