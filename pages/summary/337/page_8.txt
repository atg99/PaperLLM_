The paper discusses the development of a structured event processing mode called NestedAttention, which focuses on modeling dependencies within medical event data, specifically in electronic health records (EHR). The architecture is designed to handle various measurements, starting with time-dependent factors such as age and time of day, followed by other demographic and clinical variables. The model ensures that internal covariates within a given inner list are conditionally independent when accounting for historical data and prior measurements.

The Event Stream GPT (ESGPT) library is introduced as a significant advancement in generating predictions for future events from EHR data. This library offers unique features, including complex emission distributions and the ability to generate events in a causal sequence, representing a novel approach in the field.

The evaluation of foundation models built on this architecture presents unique challenges, particularly in the context of medical datasets. The paper outlines critical metrics for assessing these models, such as generative performance, utility compared to existing machine learning systems, and considerations for privacy and equity across different population sub-groups. Furthermore, it highlights gaps in the evaluation strategies of current models, indicating the need for more comprehensive assessment techniques in this domain. Overall, the work emphasizes the potential of the NestedAttention architecture and ESGPT for advancing research in event stream data processing and modeling.
