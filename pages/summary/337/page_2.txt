This paper discusses the application of generative language modeling, specifically leveraging GPT-equivalent architectures, to the domain of continuous-time sequences of complex, multimodal events, with a focus on Electronic Health Record (EHR) datasets. The authors introduce Event Stream GPT (ESGPT), an open-source software package designed to simplify the process of building generative, auto-regressive transformers for event stream data. ESGPT addresses two main challenges in applying foundation model research to EHR data: the lack of a unified format for diverse datasets and the complexity of modeling continuous-time sequences of events.

ESGPT provides a unified representation of varied datasets, efficient preprocessing capabilities using the Polars library, and supports hyperparameter tuning, pre-training, fine-tuning, and zero-shot evaluation through a Hugging Face compatible API. The paper illustrates ESGPT's utility with a real-world example involving the MIMIC-IV EHR dataset, which includes comprehensive health data for approximately 300,000 patients. The modeling task involves constructing a GPT model to understand complex event sequences and their internal causal dependencies.

The authors emphasize that unlike traditional token sequences, the events in EHR data may have intricate causal relationships, necessitating a more sophisticated generative model. The paper outlines the methodology for utilizing ESGPT across different stages, including data preprocessing, model configuration, and evaluation, ultimately demonstrating its effectiveness in advancing research in this area. The work concludes with a discussion of related research and future directions.
