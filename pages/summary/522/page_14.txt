This paper discusses the theoretical foundations of contrastive unsupervised representation learning, specifically focusing on the performance bounds related to negative samples in the learning process. 

Section B presents Theorem B.1, which formalizes the results of Theorem 6.1. Key definitions are introduced, including the terms for classes and indices related to the sampling of data points. The notation Q(c+, c₁, ..., cₖ) is defined to represent the set of distinct classes, while I+(c₁, ..., cₖ) refers to indices where a specific class c+ appears in negative samples.

A new sampling approach for latent classes is proposed, which ensures that data points compared are from different classes, thereby enhancing the contrastive learning process. The generalization error is analyzed, leading to a bound on the expected performance of the learned representation.

Theorem B.1 asserts that under certain conditions, the expected error can be bounded by a combination of terms related to the unsupervised loss, the probabilities of class occurrences, and other constants. The proof is structured in three steps, leveraging properties of loss functions to relate supervised and unsupervised losses.

Overall, the paper provides a rigorous mathematical framework for understanding how negative samples impact the effectiveness of unsupervised learning methods in contrastive settings, ultimately contributing to the broader understanding of representation learning.
