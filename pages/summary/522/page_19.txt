The paper discusses the challenges of contrastive unsupervised representation learning, specifically how the selection of negative samples can influence the performance of the learning algorithm. It presents two examples illustrating that even with a large number of negative samples, the algorithm may choose suboptimal representations.

In the first example, the authors describe a scenario where a class consists of two functions, f0 and f1, with f1 mapping points to specific vectors while f0 is a zero vector. As the number of negative samples increases, the probability that the algorithm selects a suboptimal representation (f0) remains constant, leading to a situation where the supervised loss for f1 becomes less optimal.

The second example extends this idea by introducing clusters of classes. Here, the function f1 groups classes into clusters, making it effective for contrasting classes from different clusters. However, as the number of negative samples increases, the performance diminishes, and the algorithm may revert to selecting the suboptimal f0 again, even when more classes are present.

The experimental section details the construction of a dataset from Wikipedia articles filtered based on specific criteria, resulting in 3029 articles for training, validation, and testing. A bi-directional GRU model with a fixed input embedding initialized from pretrained GloVe vectors is employed for the experiments.

Overall, the findings suggest that while increasing negative samples can enhance representation learning, it can also lead to suboptimal outcomes if not managed carefully.
