The section discusses the implications of negative sampling in contrastive unsupervised representation learning, focusing on the concept of class collision. The unsupervised loss can be divided into two components: one when the similar pair and the negative sample are from the same class, and another when they are from different classes. The latter component, denoted as L6=un(f), can significantly affect the overall unsupervised loss, Lun(f). 

The authors establish that the magnitude of L6=un(f) can be influenced by the intraclass deviation of the function f, defined mathematically through the covariance matrix of f(x) within the same class. They introduce a bound (Lemma 4.4) that relates L6=un(f) to this intraclass deviation, suggesting that for a rich enough function class, it is possible to achieve good performance on supervised tasks.

The paper further critiques the guarantees provided by contrastive learning, highlighting that the bounds derived may not align with ideal expectations for supervised learning performance. The authors illustrate this discrepancy using examples where high values of L6=un(f) and intraclass deviation s(f) hinder the algorithm's effectiveness. They argue that while a strong guarantee on the mean classifier of the empirical minimizer bf can be established, achieving a direct competitive guarantee against the best f in terms of supervised performance remains challenging without additional assumptions. This underscores the limitations inherent in contrastive learning methodologies, particularly in relation to class collisions and intraclass variation.
