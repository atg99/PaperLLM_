The paper discusses contrastive unsupervised representation learning by addressing the decomposition of supervised loss and its implications for unsupervised learning. The authors decompose the supervised loss into an average loss over a distribution of supervised tasks, which leads to a clearer understanding of the unsupervised loss. This unsupervised loss is bounded by two components: one that evaluates the model's ability to contrast points from differing classes and another that penalizes intraclass deviation.

In the first step, the authors establish a convexity argument, demonstrating that the expected loss can be expressed in terms of the model's performance on contrasting samples drawn from different classes. This is formalized through Jensen's inequality, emphasizing that class distinctions need not be unique for the tuple to function as a task.

In the second step, the authors further refine the loss decomposition to manage instances of repeated classes, which aids in approximating an average supervised loss. This involves symmetrizing terms to facilitate the separation of classes and to derive a lower bound on the expected loss.

Overall, the paper presents a structured approach to understanding the relationship between supervised tasks and unsupervised learning, proposing a framework that could enhance the efficacy of contrastive methods in representation learning.
