This paper presents a numerical analysis of time-multiplexed reservoir computing using a simple nonlinear oscillator modeled by a Hopf normal form with delayed feedback. The authors explore the recall capabilities and computing performance of the system, calculating total memory capacity and contributions from both linear and nonlinear dynamics. 

The work begins with an explanation of time-multiplexed reservoir computing, where input is preprocessed with a mask to induce time-multiplexing before being injected into the system. This method allows for processing tasks such as time series predictions and equalization of nonlinearly distorted signals. The authors highlight the importance of the delay time in influencing performance and the different nonlinear recall contributions.

Reservoir computing traditionally involves a network of randomly connected nodes that transform inputs into a high-dimensional space for classification tasks. The paper describes how the system's performance is quantified using the normalized root mean square error (NRMSE), which measures the ability of the system to approximate target tasks. A successful operation requires a significantly larger training dataset compared to the output dimension to avoid overfitting.

The authors reference previous work that introduced a time-multiplexed scheme for applying reservoir computing to systems with delayed feedback. In this setup, system states for input-output pairs are recorded at different times, with a masking process applied to preprocess inputs. Overall, the paper aims to elucidate the computational capabilities of semiconductor lasers and nonlinear oscillators, contributing to the understanding of reservoir computing methodologies.
