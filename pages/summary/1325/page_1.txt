The paper examines the memory capacity of delay-based reservoir computing systems, specifically those utilizing a Hopf normal form as their nonlinearity. It explores the linear and higher-order recall capabilities of such systems, with a potential physical implementation being a laser with an external cavity where information is injected electrically. The study quantifies the computational capability of the reservoir through a complete orthonormal set of basis functions, revealing that the total memory capacity is influenced by the ratio of the information input period, or clock cycle, to the system's time delay. Optimal performance is observed when the time delay is approximately 1.6 times the clock cycle.

The introduction outlines reservoir computing as a machine learning approach inspired by the brain, emphasizing its efficiency in handling time-dependent tasks while minimizing training costs. The architecture consists of three components: the input layer, the dynamical processing system, and the output layer, which is trained to predict tasks. Various realizations of reservoir computing have emerged, including those using water, FPGAs, and neural cell cultures, with particular interest in opto-electronic and optical setups due to their speed and energy efficiency. The paper highlights the development of a time-multiplexed reservoir that balances information processing speed with hardware complexity.
