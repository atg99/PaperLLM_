This paper investigates the memory capacities and nonlinear transformation capabilities of a reservoir computer based on an oscillatory system with delayed feedback, particularly near a Hopf bifurcation. The study reveals that transformations of degree 4 are limited by the system's inherent constraints, as it cannot perform transformations beyond degree 3 due to the governing equations. 

The analysis shows that when both the delay time (τ) and the input period time (T) are sufficiently long, the total memory capacity (MC) can reach up to 50, though resonances between τ and T can lead to increased errors in tasks like NARMA10. A significant finding is the optimal ratio of τ to T, identified as 1.6, which enhances memory capacities and improves task performance. Additionally, high delay times restrict recallability to specific past inputs, limiting the system's applicability for certain tasks.

The paper concludes that understanding the parameter dependencies of memory capacities enables predictions about the reservoir's capabilities to approximate various tasks. It emphasizes the importance of avoiding resonances to fully leverage the computational potential of nonlinear oscillators. The results contribute to a broader understanding of reservoir computing systems, particularly those operating near Hopf bifurcations.
