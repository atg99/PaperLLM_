The paper presents a detailed analysis of memory capacities in a reservoir computer framework, focusing on the impact of delay time and input steps on memory recallability. Figure 3 illustrates the linear capacity C1 and quadratic pure capacity C2,p, plotted against delay time and input steps. The results highlight the effectiveness of nonlinear transformations using two Legendre polynomials, indicating that combinations of input steps can significantly influence memory performance. The heatmaps reveal areas of good and poor recallability, with yellow representing good performance and blue/black indicating poor recallability.

Figure 4 further explores total memory capacity MC and capacities of various polynomial degrees (1 to 4). It visualizes the resonance effects between clock cycles and delay times, particularly noting a loss in memory capacity at specific resonances, especially for degree 2. The analysis shows that higher-order transformations are more effective when the delay time is less than 1.5 times the input period. It concludes that the performance of the reservoir computer is highly dependent on the task, with certain input transformations being unapproximable due to memory limitations at specific steps. To mitigate these limitations, the authors suggest employing multiple systems with varying parameters.

The paper aims to characterize the computational capabilities of the reservoir computer through a comprehensive examination of memory capacities, with results demonstrating that total memory capacity is constrained by the number of read-out dimensions, which in this study is set to 50 virtual nodes. Initial findings show a low total memory capacity at short delays, attributed to high correlation effects.
