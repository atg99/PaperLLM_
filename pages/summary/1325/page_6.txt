The paper discusses the dynamics of delay-based reservoir computing systems, emphasizing the relationship between delay time (τ) and input period time (T). It establishes that when τ is less than 1.5T, the reservoir's computational capability is not fully utilized. Increasing τ enhances the total memory capacity (MC), peaking at around 1.5T. However, for τ greater than 1.5T, a significant drop in MC occurs at multiples of T, attributed to resonances that lead to sparse connections between virtual nodes, primarily affecting the quadratic memory capacity (MC2).

The authors highlight that while linear memory capacity (MC1) remains relatively stable during resonances, the trade-off between linear recall and nonlinear transformation capabilities becomes apparent when the total memory capacity reaches its theoretical limit. This suggests that a decrease in MC can be redistributed across different memory capacities, with MC2 experiencing the most significant reduction.

The study also examines the NARMA10 time series prediction task, revealing that resonances correlate with increased computation errors (NRMSE). A systematic analysis of memory capacities across varying τ and T shows that for short delays and input times, the memory capacities of degrees 1-3 fall below the theoretical limit, resulting in higher prediction errors. Overall, the findings indicate that to maximize computational efficiency in delay-based reservoir computing, systems should avoid operating at resonances between τ and T.
