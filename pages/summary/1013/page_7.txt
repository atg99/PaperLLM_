The paper discusses the role of various pattern recognition receptors (PRRs) in the immune response, focusing on Dectin-1, Dectin-2, AIM2-like receptors (ALRs), and extracellular soluble PRMs. Dectin-1, which contains an immunoreceptor tyrosine-based activation motif (ITAM), recognizes a variety of fungi through its ligand, β-1,3-glucan, activating downstream signaling pathways. Glycosylation, a critical post-translational modification, influences the immune system's regulation of biological activity, with abnormal glycosylation often linked to tumors.

Dectin-2, lacking an ITAM, primarily recognizes α-mannan in fungal cell walls and requires specific molecular structures for binding. ALRs, a new class of PRRs, recognize intracellular DNA and play a role in inflammasome formation and cytokine release, thus contributing to both innate immunity and apoptosis regulation.

The paper also highlights extracellular soluble PRMs, which play a non-specific role in humoral immunity by recognizing pathogen-associated molecular patterns (PAMPs). These include pentraxins, collectins, and ficolins, which facilitate pathogen elimination and enhance the function of cell-related PRRs. Pentraxins, such as C-reactive protein (CRP) and PTX3, respond to inflammation and can serve as biomarkers for various diseases, including COVID-19. Collectins, like mannose-binding lectin (MBL), recognize sugar structures on pathogens, while ficolins target bacterial carbohydrates.

Finally, the signaling pathways of PRRs involve protein kinases, adaptor proteins, and transcription factors, which mediate the immune response upon ligand binding. Overall, the paper emphasizes the significance of these receptors and their pathways in both health and disease contexts.
