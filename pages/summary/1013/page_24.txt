The paper discusses the interplay between the human gastrointestinal tract microbiome and the brain, highlighting the potential therapeutic roles of exosomes in regulating immune responses, particularly through Toll-like receptors (TLRs). Various studies are referenced, including those on exosome biology, their functions in disease treatment, and their applications in Alzheimer's disease and cancer immunotherapy. The authors emphasize the significance of TLRs in innate immune sensing and the emerging tools for predicting pathogen-associated molecular patterns and resistance proteins in plants. The article is open access, allowing for broad dissemination and adaptation of its content, provided proper attribution is given. Overall, it underscores the relevance of pattern recognition receptors in understanding health and disease dynamics.
