The paper discusses the role of the NF-κB signaling pathway in inflammation and immune responses, highlighting its activation through various pattern recognition receptors (PRRs). NF-κB, a heterodimer composed of p50 and p65, remains inactive when bound to the inhibitory protein IκB. When PRRs such as Toll-like receptors (TLRs), NOD-like receptors (NLRs), RIG-I-like receptors (RLRs), and C-type lectin receptors (CLRs) detect pathogen-associated molecular patterns (PAMPs) and damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs), they initiate signaling cascades that lead to the activation of NF-κB.

TLR signaling can be classified into MyD88-dependent and MyD88-independent pathways. The MyD88-dependent pathway, primarily responsible for producing pro-inflammatory cytokines, involves MyD88 recruiting IL-1R-related kinase 4 (IRAK4), which activates IRAK1 and IRAK2. This leads to the activation of the IκB kinase (IKK) complex, resulting in the phosphorylation and degradation of IκB, releasing NF-κB to translocate to the nucleus and regulate inflammatory gene transcription.

In contrast, NLRs like NOD1 and NOD2 recognize bacterial components and activate downstream signaling through receptor-interacting protein 2 (RIP2), while RLRs like RIG-I and MDA5 respond to viral RNA, activating the NF-κB pathway through mitochondrial antiviral signaling protein (MAVS). Additionally, CLRs utilize spleen tyrosine kinase (Syk) to activate NF-κB via the Dectin-1/Syk pathway.

The paper also addresses the mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) signaling pathway, which transmits signals related to various cellular processes, including inflammation and apoptosis. Overall, the study emphasizes the intricate interplay of these signaling pathways in regulating immune responses and their implications in health and disease.
