The referenced works collectively explore the structure, function, and signaling mechanisms of various pattern recognition receptors (PRRs), particularly Toll-like receptors (TLRs) and NOD-like receptors (NLRs), in innate immunity. Kaur et al. discuss TLR2 agonistic small molecules and their therapeutic potential, while studies by Omueti et al. and Jin et al. focus on the structural dynamics of TLRs and their ligand interactions, emphasizing the importance of domain exchanges and heterodimerization in lipopeptide recognition. 

Further insights into TLR signaling are provided by Su et al. and Liu et al., detailing the structural basis for receptor activation by various ligands, including double-stranded RNA and lipopolysaccharides. The role of NLRs in immune responses is highlighted by multiple authors, including Meunier and Broz, who discuss the versatility of NLRs as cytosolic sensors, and Girardin et al., who elucidate the mechanisms of peptidoglycan detection by NOD1 and NOD2.

The RIG-I-like receptors, crucial for sensing viral RNA, are also examined, with studies by Yoneyama et al. and Liu et al. detailing their regulatory functions and structural insights into RNA recognition. Overall, these works underscore the intricate interplay between PRRs and their ligands, shaping the host's immune responses to pathogens and influencing therapeutic strategies in infectious diseases.
