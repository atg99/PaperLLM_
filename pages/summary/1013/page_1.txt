This review article discusses pattern recognition receptors (PRRs), which play a crucial role in the immune system by recognizing specific molecular structures on pathogens and damaged cells. PRRs are integral to both nonspecific and specific immunity, facilitating immune responses such as anti-infection and antitumor effects. They can be categorized into five main types: Toll-like receptors (TLRs), nucleotide oligomerization domain-like receptors (NLRs), retinoic acid-inducible gene-I-like receptors (RLRs), C-type lectin receptors (CLRs), and absent in melanoma-2-like receptors (ALRs). 

The structure of PRRs typically includes ligand recognition, intermediate, and effector domains, which allow them to bind ligands and initiate downstream signaling pathways via adaptor molecules. The understanding of PRR signaling mechanisms has advanced significantly, leading to insights into the treatment of immune-related diseases and tumors. 

The article highlights the historical development of PRRs, particularly TLRs, which were first identified in Drosophila and later found to be critical in mammalian immunity. The discovery of TLRs has transformed research on innate immunity, demonstrating their role in linking innate and adaptive immune responses. 

Overall, the review emphasizes the importance of PRRs in immune function and their potential as therapeutic targets for treating various diseases.
