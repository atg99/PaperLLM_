The paper discusses the role of pattern recognition receptors (PRRs) in innate immunity, highlighting their function in recognizing pathogenic microorganisms and activating adaptive immunity through secondary signals. Since the identification of TLR4, various PRRs have been categorized into five types: Toll-like receptors (TLRs), nucleotide-binding oligomerization domain-like receptors (NLRs), retinoic acid-inducible gene-I-like receptors (RLRs), C-type lectin receptors (CLRs), and absent in melanoma-2-like receptors (ALRs). Each type of PRR has distinct protein domain structures and cellular distributions, enabling them to identify diverse pathogen-associated molecular patterns (PAMPs) from bacteria, fungi, and viruses.

Table 1 in the paper details the characteristics of common PRRs, including their domains, cellular locations, PAMP sources, and signaling pathways. For instance, TLRs are primarily involved in initiating immune responses through MyD88-dependent or TRIF-dependent pathways. NLRs and RLRs also play significant roles in immune signaling, while CLRs and ALRs contribute to recognizing specific microbial components and triggering immune responses.

The paper emphasizes the importance of these receptors in maintaining immune homeostasis and their implications in health and disease. Understanding the mechanisms of PRRs can provide insights into developing targeted therapies for various immunological disorders.
