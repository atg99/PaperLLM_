The cited works collectively explore the role of various pattern recognition receptors (PRRs) in innate immunity, inflammation, and disease processes. Ambati et al. demonstrate the enhanced efficacy of Dectin-2-targeted antifungal liposomes. Ritter et al. highlight how Schistosoma mansoni activates Dectin-2, influencing adaptive immune responses through the Nlrp3 inflammasome. Feinberg et al. elucidate the pathogen recognition mechanism of Dectin-2, which recognizes mannosylated O-antigens, as detailed by Wittmann et al.

Decout et al. investigate the molecular recognition of mycobacteria by Dectin-2, underscoring its significance in immune defense. The works of Feng et al., Zhang et al., and Liu et al. delve into the role of absent in melanoma 2 (AIM2) in inflammation, immunity, and cancer, with AIM2 recognized for its function in forming inflammasomes in response to cytosolic dsDNA.

The literature also discusses the pentraxin family, particularly PTX3, as a humoral pattern recognition molecule involved in innate immunity. Studies by Mantovani et al. and Garlanda et al. emphasize PTX3's roles in inflammation and tissue repair. C-reactive protein (CRP) and serum amyloid A (SAA) are identified as key players in inflammation, with implications for various diseases.

Furthermore, mannose-binding lectin (MBL) is highlighted for its clinical relevance in infections and autoimmune conditions, as explored by multiple authors, including Turner and Kilpatrick. The collective research underscores the complex interplay of PRRs in health and disease, pointing to their potential as therapeutic targets.
