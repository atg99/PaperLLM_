The paper discusses the role of various pattern recognition receptors (PRRs) in immune responses and their potential therapeutic applications in inflammatory diseases. Quinazoline compounds can inhibit the activation of TLR7, 8, and 9, while synthetic glycolipid 462FP7 serves as a TLR4 inhibitor for septic shock treatment. ODN1411, a phosphorothioate-modified oligodeoxynucleotide, targets TLR8 to interfere with its signaling.

The NLRP3 inflammasome is highlighted as a key target in inflammatory diseases, with MCC950 being a specific antagonist that inhibits NLRP3 activation. β-Carotene also blocks NLRP3 activation by binding to its PYD domain. Recent studies have identified small molecule inhibitors, such as tetrahydroquinoline derivatives and alkenyl sulfonylurea derivatives, demonstrating effective inhibition of NLRP3 and alleviating symptoms in various models.

The interaction between miRNA and PRR pathways is emphasized, particularly in rheumatoid arthritis, where miR-146a and miR-155 modulate TLR signaling to reduce inflammation. Other miRNAs, such as miR-19a/b and miR-20a, have been shown to promote inflammatory responses by targeting TLRs and key signaling components.

Exosomes, which contain RNA and proteins, are identified as important players in immune regulation, capable of modulating TLR signaling and influencing inflammatory responses. For instance, exosomes can activate TLRs and have been linked to Alzheimer's disease and multiple myeloma.

The conclusion notes significant advancements in understanding PRR-ligand interactions, highlighting the complexity of these mechanisms and the need for further research. Tools like RRGPredictor and machine learning methods are being developed to enhance PRR identification, which is crucial for understanding their roles in health and disease. Future studies on soluble PRRs and their mechanisms in immune defense and disorders are encouraged to deepen insights into PRR-mediated signaling pathways.
