The paper evaluates the performance of the FixMatch algorithm across various datasets, demonstrating its effectiveness in semi-supervised learning (SSL). In the CIFAR-10 dataset, FixMatch achieves 78% accuracy with as few as 10 labeled images. The STL-10 dataset, which includes 5,000 labeled and 100,000 unlabeled images, presents additional challenges due to out-of-distribution images. FixMatch outperforms the state-of-the-art ReMixMatch with a simpler approach on this dataset.

When tested on ImageNet, using 10% labeled data with a ResNet-50 architecture and RandAugment for strong augmentation, FixMatch achieves a top-1 error rate of 28.54%, outperforming UDA by 2.68%. Although S4L has a lower error rate of 26.79% after multiple training phases, FixMatch performs better than S4L after its first phase, suggesting potential for further improvement with additional techniques.

In exploring barely supervised learning, FixMatch is tested on CIFAR-10 with only one labeled example per class. The results show variability in accuracy based on the quality of labeled examples, with a median accuracy of 64.28% across multiple datasets. The most representative labeled examples yield a median accuracy of 78%, while outliers lead to significant performance drops. This indicates that the quality of labeled data is crucial for effective learning in low-label scenarios. The paper concludes that representative examples enhance model performance in SSL settings.
