The paper reviews various methods and frameworks in the domain of semi-supervised learning, emphasizing techniques such as temporal ensembling, pseudo-labeling, and virtual adversarial training. It references key studies that explore the effectiveness of these methods in enhancing learning from limited labeled data. Notable works include Laine and Aila's temporal ensembling, which improves model performance by leveraging both labeled and unlabeled data, and Lee's pseudo-labeling technique, which assigns labels to unlabeled data to aid training. The paper also highlights advancements in detecting out-of-distribution samples and adversarial attacks, crucial for improving model robustness. Furthermore, it discusses regularization techniques like decoupled weight decay and stochastic gradient descent with warm restarts, which contribute to better generalization in deep learning models. Overall, the paper synthesizes significant contributions to the field, providing insights into the state of semi-supervised learning and its ongoing challenges.
