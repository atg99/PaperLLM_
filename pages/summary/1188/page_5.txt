The paper discusses a comparison of various semi-supervised learning (SSL) algorithms that incorporate consistency regularization and optional post-processing methods for artificial labels. Table 1 summarizes the strengths and weaknesses of algorithms such as Temporal Ensembling, Mean Teacher, Virtual Adversarial Training, UDA, MixMatch, ReMixMatch, and FixMatch. The paper emphasizes the role of pseudo-labeling, where model predictions are converted into hard labels, often combined with confidence-based thresholding to enhance performance. Although some studies argue that pseudo-labeling alone may not be competitive, it has been effectively integrated into recent SSL methodologies.

The concept of consistency regularization, initially proposed in earlier works, is examined alongside various techniques for generating random perturbations, including data augmentation and adversarial methods. The paper highlights that strong data augmentation can yield superior results, even producing examples outside the original data distribution, which can be beneficial for SSL.

FixMatch is presented as a simplified combination of two established techniques: pseudo-labeling and consistency regularization, drawing parallels with UDA and ReMixMatch but omitting certain components like sharpening and training signal annealing. The authors assert that FixMatch demonstrates potential in scenarios with limited labeled data.

The experiments conducted evaluate FixMatch's effectiveness across multiple SSL benchmarks, including CIFAR-10/100, SVHN, STL-10, and ImageNet, adhering to standard evaluation protocols. The findings suggest that FixMatch performs well even with significantly reduced labeled data, indicating its robustness in label-scarce environments.
