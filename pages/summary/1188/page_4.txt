The paper discusses the dynamics of model confidence during training, particularly in the context of semi-supervised learning (SSL). Initially, the model's predictions are often low in confidence, indicated by max(qb) being less than a threshold. However, as training progresses, confidence increases, suggesting that pseudo-labeling can create an effective natural curriculum without additional effort. This aligns with previous findings in visual domain adaptation.

FixMatch employs two types of augmentations: weak and strong. Weak augmentation involves basic transformations like flipping and translating images, while strong augmentation utilizes methods based on AutoAugment followed by Cutout. AutoAugment relies on labeled data to establish augmentation strategies, which poses challenges in SSL scenarios with limited labels. Alternatives like RandAugment and CTAugment have been proposed, which randomly select transformations without prior learning from labeled data.

The paper emphasizes that semi-supervised performance is influenced by various factors beyond the SSL algorithm, including regularization, architecture, optimizer choice, and training schedule. Regularization, particularly weight decay, and the use of SGD with momentum are highlighted as critical for performance, while a cosine learning rate decay is employed for training. The paper also discusses extensions of FixMatch, such as Augmentation Anchoring and Distribution Alignment, which can enhance its effectiveness.

Finally, the authors place FixMatch within the broader context of SSL, noting its relationship to self-training methods that have been widely applied across different domains, including natural language processing and image classification.
