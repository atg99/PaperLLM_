The paper discusses the role of consistency regularization in semi-supervised learning (SSL) algorithms, highlighting its foundational principle that a model should yield similar predictions for perturbed versions of the same image. This approach, initially introduced in earlier works, has evolved with various modifications such as adversarial transformations and stronger augmentation techniques. Pseudo-labeling is also explored, where the model generates artificial labels for unlabeled data, which are then used in conjunction with a threshold to retain only high-confidence predictions.

The core of the paper introduces FixMatch, an algorithm that combines supervised and unsupervised loss functions. The supervised loss applies to labeled data through standard cross-entropy, while the unsupervised loss leverages pseudo-labels generated from weakly-augmented unlabeled images, enforcing a loss against strongly-augmented versions. The distinctiveness of FixMatch lies in its use of different augmentations to compute pseudo-labels and enforce consistency, a feature that is critical to its performance. The paper notes that unlike other SSL methods, FixMatch does not require an increase in the weight of the unlabeled loss during training, which may stem from its unique design.
