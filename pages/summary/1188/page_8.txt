The paper presents an extensive ablation study on FixMatch, a semi-supervised learning method, to understand its effectiveness in achieving state-of-the-art results. The study focuses on a single 250-label split from the CIFAR-10 dataset, reporting an error rate of 4.84% with default parameters. The authors investigate various factors including the confidence threshold for pseudo-labels and the impact of sharpening the predicted label distribution.

Key findings indicate that the optimal confidence threshold is 0.95, as higher thresholds improve the quality of pseudo-labels but reduce their quantity, ultimately enhancing accuracy. The introduction of sharpening as a hyperparameter did not yield better performance compared to using traditional pseudo-labeling.

Additionally, the study examines different data augmentation strategies, specifically comparing RandAugment and CTAugment. Results show comparable outcomes across CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and SVHN datasets, while CTAugment significantly outperformed in STL-10. The importance of Cutout, a data augmentation technique, was highlighted, revealing that both Cutout and CTAugment are essential for optimal performance. The authors also explored the effects of varying weak and strong augmentations for label generation, noting that using strong augmentation alone led to early model divergence, while weak augmentation helped stabilize training.

Overall, the ablation study underscores the significance of careful parameter tuning and augmentation strategies in enhancing the performance of FixMatch in semi-supervised learning contexts.
