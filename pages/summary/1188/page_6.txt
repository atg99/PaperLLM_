This paper evaluates the performance of the FixMatch method against various existing semi-supervised learning (SSL) frameworks on standard benchmarks including CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, SVHN, and STL-10. The study reports error rates based on a range of labeled examples, demonstrating FixMatch's effectiveness in scenarios with limited labeled data.

FixMatch is compared with several baseline methods: Model, Pseudo-Labeling, Mean Teacher, MixMatch, UDA, and ReMixMatch. All methods were implemented using a consistent codebase and network architecture, specifically Wide ResNet models tailored for each dataset. The experiments included settings with as few as four labeled images per class, a novel approach for CIFAR-100.

Results indicate that FixMatch outperforms other methods, achieving an average error rate of 11.39% on CIFAR-10 with only four labels per class, a significant improvement compared to previous methods achieving 13.13% error with 400 labels. However, ReMixMatch outperformed FixMatch on CIFAR-100, prompting further investigation into the contributing factors. The study revealed that Distribution Alignment is a critical component for enhancing performance, as evidenced by a combined FixMatch model with DA achieving a lower error rate than ReMixMatch.

The study also notes that performance variance is higher with fewer labeled examples, particularly evident in the CIFAR-10 results, where the error rate variance with four labels per class is significantly greater than with 25 labels. The findings underscore the potential of FixMatch in achieving state-of-the-art results in SSL tasks while emphasizing the importance of optimizing model components and hyperparameters.
