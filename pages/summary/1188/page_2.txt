The paper presents FixMatch, a novel approach to semi-supervised learning (SSL) that combines consistency regularization and pseudo-labeling in a simpler yet more effective manner than previous state-of-the-art methods. The methodology involves generating artificial labels from weakly-augmented unlabeled images. These labels are used as targets when the model processes strongly-augmented versions of the same images. The process begins with the model predicting class probabilities for weakly-augmented images; predictions exceeding a certain probability threshold are converted into one-hot pseudo-labels. The model is then trained to align its predictions on the strongly-augmented images with these pseudo-labels using a cross-entropy loss.

FixMatch leverages various strong augmentation techniques, including Cutout, CTAugment, and RandAugment, to create heavily distorted image versions. The results demonstrate that FixMatch achieves state-of-the-art performance on SSL benchmarks, such as 94.93% accuracy on CIFAR-10 with only 250 labeled examples, surpassing the previous best of 93.73%. It also performs well in scenarios with extremely limited labels, achieving 88.61% accuracy with just four labels per class.

The paper emphasizes the advantages of FixMatch's simplicity, which translates to fewer hyperparameters and a more manageable training process. An extensive ablation study is included to analyze the contributions of various components to FixMatch's success, alongside a review of essential experimental choices often overlooked in SSL research. The combination of weak and strong augmentations, along with effective regularization techniques, is highlighted as key to the algorithm's empirical performance.
