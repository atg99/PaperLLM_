The paper discusses significant advancements in convolutional neural networks (CNNs) through various architectures, particularly VGGNets, GoogLeNet, and ResNet. 

VGGNets, which won the ImageNet Challenge 2014, demonstrate that increasing network depth enhances performance. Key improvements over AlexNet include the removal of the Local Response Normalization (LRN) layer and the use of 3x3 convolution kernels instead of larger ones. This choice reduces the number of parameters significantly while maintaining effective feature extraction through multiple small kernels.

GoogLeNet, the winner of ILSVRC 2014, introduces the Inception module, allowing for a combination of different kernel sizes (1x1, 3x3, 5x5) to capture features at various scales. Inception v2 enhances this by implementing batch normalization and replacing larger kernels with smaller ones to streamline the network. Inception v3 builds upon these concepts, optimizing training and modifying input size, while Inception v4 and Inception-ResNet further refine the architecture and improve performance through additional Inception modules and residual connections.

ResNet addresses issues in deeper networks, such as gradient vanishing and exploding, by employing residual blocks with shortcut connections. The architecture allows for deeper networks (up to 152 layers) while maintaining performance, surpassing GoogLeNet in classification tasks.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the evolution of CNN architectures, highlighting how innovations in structure and training techniques contribute to improved image classification and object detection capabilities.
