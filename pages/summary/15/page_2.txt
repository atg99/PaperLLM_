This paper provides a comprehensive review of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN), focusing on their structure, key models, and future directions. The authors highlight several key contributions: 

1. An overview of CNN, detailing fundamental building blocks and innovative convolution structures.
2. A discussion of classic CNN-based models, including LeNet-5, AlexNet, MobileNet v3, and GhostNet, emphasizing their innovations.
3. An analysis of representative activation functions, loss functions, and optimizers, supported by experimental conclusions.
4. An exploration of applications beyond two-dimensional convolution, including one-dimensional and multi-dimensional scenarios.
5. Insights into future prospects for CNN, suggesting refinements of existing models and the creation of new architectures.

The paper is structured into sections that cover these topics in depth. It begins with a brief overview of CNN, explaining its advantages such as local connections, weight sharing, and dimensionality reduction through pooling. It also discusses the components necessary for building a CNN, including convolution, padding, stride, and pooling, as well as advanced techniques like dilated and deformable convolutions.

Overall, the review aims to provide a holistic perspective on CNN, addressing both its current state and future potential in the field of deep learning.
