This paper evaluates the performance of various activation functions in deep convolutional neural networks (CNNs) across different datasets. Key findings include:

1. **Activation Function Performance**: Tanh, PReLU, and ELU functions may lead to oscillations during training. Sigmoid and tanh can hinder convergence when using pre-trained weights. In contrast, Leaky ReLU and ELU generally yield better accuracy, although ELU can sometimes fail to facilitate learning. Leaky ReLU often outperforms in both accuracy and training speed.

2. **Selection Guidelines**: For binary classification, sigmoid is recommended for the output layer, while softmax is suitable for multi-class classification. It is advised to avoid sigmoid and tanh in certain scenarios.

3. **Comparative Results**: The paper provides a comparative analysis of different activation functions on datasets like MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, CIFAR-10, and CIFAR-100. Results indicate that ReLU, Leaky ReLU, and ELU consistently achieve higher validation accuracy compared to sigmoid and tanh across various models and datasets. 

The findings suggest that while traditional functions like sigmoid and tanh have their uses, modern alternatives like Leaky ReLU and ELU are generally more effective in training deep learning models.
