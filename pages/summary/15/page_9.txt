This paper presents experiments using pre-trained VGG-16 and LeNet-5 models on various datasets, specifically MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, CIFAR-10, and CIFAR-100, to evaluate the performance of different activation functions in deep neural networks. The experiments were conducted on an Intel Xeon E5-2640 v4 and NVIDIA TITAN RTX, utilizing Python 3.5.6 and Keras 2.2.4.

For the MNIST and Fashion-MNIST datasets, the findings indicate that linear activation functions yield the poorest performance, emphasizing the necessity of incorporating non-linear functions in deep networks. Among the tested activation functions, the sigmoid function exhibited the slowest convergence and overall performance. Conversely, the Exponential Linear Unit (ELU) demonstrated the highest accuracy but required more training time compared to ReLU and Leaky ReLU. Notably, ReLU and Leaky ReLU exhibited superior stability during training relative to PReLU and ELU.

The CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 datasets, which are more complex than the previous datasets, were also analyzed using the same models and activation functions. The results for these datasets are presented in corresponding figures and tables, further supporting the conclusions drawn from the MNIST and Fashion-MNIST experiments. These findings underline the critical role of activation functions in enhancing the performance and stability of deep learning models across various datasets.
