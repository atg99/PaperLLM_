The section on classic CNN models reviews significant architectures that have shaped the development of deep learning. LeNet-5, introduced by LeCun et al. in 1998, is recognized as a pioneering model for handwritten character recognition. It consists of seven layers, including convolutional and fully connected layers, and incorporates concepts like local receptive fields and shared weights, laying the groundwork for modern CNNs despite its limitations compared to traditional algorithms.

AlexNet, proposed by Alex et al. in 2012, marked a significant advancement in CNNs, winning the ImageNet competition. It features eight layers and introduces several key innovations: the use of the ReLU activation function to address gradient vanishing, dropout to prevent overfitting, overlapping max pooling to enhance feature richness, and local response normalization to improve generalization. Additionally, AlexNet utilizes two GPUs for training and employs data augmentation techniques to increase the training dataset, thus enhancing model performance.
