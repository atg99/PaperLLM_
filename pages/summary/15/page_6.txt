The paper discusses advancements in mobile neural network architectures, specifically focusing on MobileNet v3 and ShuffleNet models.

In MobileNet v3, the authors address the issue of information loss during channel compression when using the ReLU activation function in low-dimensional spaces. To mitigate this, they introduce the linear bottleneck module, which replaces ReLU with a linear transformation after the second 1 × 1 convolution in inverted residual blocks. MobileNet v3 incorporates three key improvements: platform-aware neural architecture search (NAS), a lightweight attention model based on squeeze and excitation (SE), and the h-swish activation function. Platform-aware NAS utilizes an RNN-based controller for block-wise search, while the NetAdapt algorithm fine-tunes layer-wise filter counts. The SE module reweights channels post-depth-wise convolution, enhancing accuracy without additional computational cost. The h-swish function, a computationally efficient variant of swish, is employed in the model's latter half, while hard sigmoid (h-sigmoid) replaces standard sigmoid.

ShuffleNets, developed by MEGVII, aim to optimize CNN structures for mobile devices with limited computing power. The paper outlines ShuffleNet v1, which introduces pointwise group convolution and channel shuffle to reduce computational load while maintaining accuracy. Pointwise group convolution limits computations to specific input channel groups, thereby decreasing complexity but hindering inter-group feature communication. To counter this, the channel shuffle operation facilitates random information exchange between groups. The ShuffleNet unit builds upon this concept, enhancing feature extraction capabilities.

Overall, the paper highlights the innovations in MobileNet v3 and ShuffleNet architectures, emphasizing their efficiency and effectiveness for resource-constrained environments.
