The paper discusses the use of various loss functions and optimization techniques in convolutional neural networks (CNNs). It highlights the application of softmax loss in tasks like face and emotion recognition, and speaker verification. The selection of loss functions is guided by the nature of the problem: L1 or L2 loss for regression, and cross-entropy loss for classification, with specific cases like contrastive and triplet loss for face recognition.

For optimization, the paper outlines the necessity of optimizers to handle the non-convex functions typical in CNN training. It describes three gradient descent methods: Batch Gradient Descent (BGD), which is slow but ensures convergence; Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD), which is faster but can oscillate and may not converge; and Mini-Batch Gradient Descent (MBGD), which combines the benefits of both BGD and SGD, making it the most popular choice in practice.

The paper also details various gradient descent optimization algorithms that enhance the training process. The Momentum algorithm helps achieve faster convergence by reducing oscillations, while the Nesterov Accelerated Gradient (NAG) provides predictability in updates. Adagrad adapts learning rates for parameters based on frequency, suitable for sparse data. Adadelta addresses the diminishing learning rate issue in Adagrad, and RMSprop is designed to overcome the same problem. Lastly, the Adam optimizer combines features of Momentum and RMSprop, proving effective across various CNN architectures, with its variant AdaMax simplifying the learning rate boundary.
