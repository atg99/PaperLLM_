This paper provides a comprehensive review of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), highlighting their significance in deep learning and various applications, particularly in computer vision and natural language processing. It begins with a historical overview of CNN development, tracing its roots from early artificial neural networks to the emergence of multi-layer networks and modern architectures.

The authors categorize their review into several key sections: an introduction to CNN history, an overview of CNN structures, and a discussion of classic and advanced CNN models that achieve state-of-the-art results. They also conduct experimental analyses to derive practical guidelines for function selection in CNN applications. The paper encompasses the use of one-dimensional, two-dimensional, and multi-dimensional convolutions, addressing both traditional and novel approaches.

Furthermore, the authors critique existing literature on CNNs, noting that previous reviews often lack a holistic perspective or fail to incorporate recent advancements. They conclude by discussing open issues and future directions for CNN research, aiming to guide further exploration in this rapidly evolving field. The work is supported by various grants and emphasizes the need for ongoing innovation within CNN architecture and application.
