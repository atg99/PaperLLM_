The paper discusses the selection rules for convolutional neural networks (CNN) and their various applications, particularly focusing on one-dimensional and two-dimensional CNNs. 

In the selection rules, it suggests using mini-batch processing to balance computing cost and update accuracy. The choice of optimizer should be based on data distribution, and if issues like oscillation or divergence arise, reducing the learning rate is recommended.

The applications of CNN are extensive, especially in the context of big data. One-dimensional CNNs are highlighted for their effectiveness in processing time series data and signal identification. Examples include predicting ECG data, weather forecasts, and traffic flow, where 1D CNNs extract relevant features for accurate predictions. Signal identification tasks, such as ECG signal classification and structural damage detection, are also cited as practical applications.

For two-dimensional CNNs, image classification is a primary application area. The paper traces the evolution of CNN architectures starting from LeNet-5, through AlexNet, to more complex structures like ResNet and DPN, which have significantly improved classification accuracy. Various medical and traffic-related classification tasks are also mentioned.

Object detection, a more advanced application of image classification, is discussed as well. The paper categorizes object detection methods into one-stage and two-stage approaches, with examples like YOLO and R-CNN. The progression of these methods reflects the advancements in deep learning techniques for detecting and classifying objects within images.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the versatility and power of CNNs in handling various data types and tasks, making them a fundamental component in the field of deep learning.
