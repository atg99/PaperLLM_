The paper discusses various optimization algorithms used in training convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and evaluates their performance on the CIFAR-10 dataset. Notably, it highlights Nesterov-accelerated Adaptive Moment Estimation (Nadam) as a combination of Adam and Nesterov's Accelerated Gradient (NAG), emphasizing its stronger learning rate constraints and effects on gradient updates. AMSGrad is introduced as an enhancement of Adagrad, addressing convergence issues in Adam by utilizing the maximum of past squared gradients for parameter updates.

The experimental evaluation tests ten optimizers, including mini-batch gradient descent, Momentum, Nesterov, Adagrad, Adadelta, RMSprop, Adam, Adamax, Nadam, and AMSGrad, using a pre-trained VGG-16 model without the last three layers on the ImageNet dataset. Key findings include:

- Most optimizers achieve convergence by the end of training.
- Mini-batch gradient descent shows the slowest convergence rate.
- Nesterov, Adagrad, RMSprop, Adamax, and Nadam exhibit oscillations, which can hinder convergence.
- The learning rate significantly affects convergence, with Nesterov, RMSprop, and Nadam potentially aiding in escaping local optima despite their oscillatory behavior.

The results are summarized in tables and figures, illustrating the accuracy and training loss associated with each optimizer.
