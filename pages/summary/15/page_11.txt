The paper discusses the selection of activation functions and loss functions in convolutional neural networks (CNNs). For activation functions, it suggests using ReLU or Leaky ReLU, particularly when many neurons become inactive during training. Leaky ReLU can be adjusted with a negative slope of 0.02 to enhance training speed.

Loss functions are critical for measuring the discrepancy between predicted and actual values. For regression tasks, Mean Absolute Error (MAE) and Mean Square Error (MSE) are commonly used. MAE is more robust against outliers, while MSE, being derivable, allows for better control of the update rate during optimization.

In classification tasks, cross-entropy loss is the standard, evaluating the difference between predicted probabilities and actual outcomes. However, it has limitations, as it does not account for the compactness within classes or the separation between classes. To address these issues, alternative loss functions have been developed. 

Contrastive loss increases the distance between different categories while minimizing distance within the same category, making it useful in tasks like face recognition. Triplet loss, introduced in FaceNet, focuses on refining embeddings by comparing an anchor image with a positive and a negative image to enhance classification accuracy at an individual level.

Center loss aims to maintain uniform distribution within classes, enhancing performance in tasks such as face recognition and image retrieval. Lastly, large-margin softmax loss promotes intra-class compression and inter-class separation by introducing a margin constraint.
