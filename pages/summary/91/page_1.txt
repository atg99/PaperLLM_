The paper discusses advancements in large-batch training methods for deep neural networks, particularly focusing on LSTM and CNN architectures. Large-batch training has significantly accelerated training times, exemplified by reducing the training duration of ResNet50 on ImageNet from 29 hours to 2.2 minutes by increasing the batch size from 256 to 32K. However, the authors identify three key challenges in the current landscape of large-batch training: 

1. Existing research primarily emphasizes CNNs, neglecting RNN methods such as LSTM, which are prevalent in real-world applications.
2. There is a lack of automated techniques to extend batch sizes beyond 8K, necessitating extensive hyper-parameter tuning.
3. Theoretical frameworks recommend a Sqrt Scaling approach to maintain gradient variance, yet practical applications of this method are limited.

To address these issues, the authors propose a novel method called linear-epoch gradual-warmup (LEGW). This approach facilitates large-batch training for both CNNs and RNNs while effectively implementing the Sqrt Scaling scheme. The results demonstrate that LEGW allows for a 64-fold increase in batch size for LSTM applications without accuracy loss or hyper-parameter tuning. For CNNs, accuracy is maintained even at a batch size of 32K. The method outperforms previous large-batch auto-tuning techniques, achieving an average speedup of 5.3 times over baseline models across four LSTM-based applications. The paper also provides theoretical justifications for the effectiveness of LEGW.
