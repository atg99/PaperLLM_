The paper discusses the use of learning rate decay strategies in large-batch training, focusing on the Linear Scaling and Sqrt Scaling schemes. While theoretical analyses suggest that Sqrt Scaling should maintain gradient variance, empirical evidence indicates that Linear Scaling often yields better performance, particularly when combined with a constant-epoch warmup strategy. For instance, Goyal et al. set a warmup period of five epochs, and although Linear Scaling is effective up to a batch size of 8K, it requires significant hyper-parameter tuning to scale to 32K.

The paper introduces the LEGW (Learning Rate and Epoch Gradient Weight) method, which enables Sqrt Scaling to perform effectively in practice, aligning with theoretical expectations. The authors illustrate LEGW using ImageNet training with the ResNet50 architecture. They demonstrate a multi-step learning rate decay approach where the learning rate is gradually increased and then reduced at specified epochs. For example, with a batch size of 1K, the learning rate is first ramped from 0 to 22.5 and then decreased at the 30th, 60th, and 80th epochs.

When scaling the batch size to 2K, LEGW adjusts the learning rate according to Sqrt Scaling principles and follows a similar gradual warmup and decay pattern. Additionally, the paper addresses polynomial decay methods, where the learning rate is adjusted based on the current iteration and total iterations. The results and detailed illustrations of these methods are provided in the figures referenced in the text. Overall, the paper emphasizes the flexibility and effectiveness of LEGW in optimizing learning rate strategies for large-batch training scenarios.
