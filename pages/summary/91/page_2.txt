The paper introduces the Linear-Epoch Gradual-Warmup approach, termed LEGW, which addresses challenges in large-batch training of deep neural networks. Traditional methods often rely on a linear scaling scheme for learning rates, which can lead to performance issues when batch sizes exceed 8K, necessitating manual hyper-parameter tuning. The authors argue that a Sqrt Scaling scheme is theoretically more effective for maintaining gradient variance but has seen limited practical application.

LEGW enables the implementation of Sqrt Scaling in practice, allowing for significant increases in batch size without the need for hyper-parameter adjustments. Specifically, the authors demonstrate that with LEGW, they can scale the batch size by 16 for GNMT applications and 32 for PTB datasets, all while maintaining accuracy. Additionally, in ImageNet training with ResNet50, LEGW achieves consistent accuracy at a batch size of 32K when combined with the LARS solver. 

The results indicate that LEGW outperforms previous large-batch tuning techniques, achieving an average speedup of 5.3 times over baseline methods across four LSTM-based applications. The paper also provides theoretical insights into the effectiveness of LEGW in large-batch training, emphasizing its potential to bridge the gap between theoretical and practical applications in deep learning.
