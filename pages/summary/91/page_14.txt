The referenced works focus on advancements in deep learning techniques and their implications for model performance. Key contributions include:

1. Smith et al. (2017) advocate for increasing batch size rather than decaying learning rates to enhance training efficiency.
2. Sutskever et al. (2013) emphasize the significance of initialization and momentum in optimizing deep learning models.
3. Wang and Tian (2016) introduce recurrent residual learning specifically for sequence classification tasks.
4. Wu et al. (2016) discuss Google's neural machine translation system, highlighting its effectiveness in bridging the gap between human and machine translation capabilities.
5. Ying et al. (2018) demonstrate image classification at supercomputer scale, showcasing the potential of large-scale computing resources.
6. You et al. (2017) explore scaling stochastic gradient descent (SGD) batch sizes to 32,000 for ImageNet training, while also presenting findings on achieving rapid training times.
7. Yu and Koltun (2015) propose multi-scale context aggregation via dilated convolutions to enhance feature extraction.
8. Zeiler (2012) presents Adadelta, an adaptive learning rate method that improves training dynamics.

In the appendix, it is noted that Adam outperforms Adadelta on the MNIST and PTB datasets, with LEGW also showing superior performance compared to a tuned Adam solver for specific applications. This underscores the importance of selecting optimal solvers for improving model performance across different tasks.
