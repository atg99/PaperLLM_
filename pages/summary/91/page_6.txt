The paper evaluates various models and their performance across different datasets, as summarized in Table 1. The models include a 1-layer LSTM on the MNIST dataset, PTB-small and PTB-large on the Penn Treebank, GNMT on WMT16, and ResNet50 on ImageNet, with respective metrics indicating high accuracy and low perplexity.

A key focus of the paper is on the LEGW method, which addresses the challenge of selecting an appropriate learning rate schedule. The authors argue that the local Lipschitz constant provides insight into why LEGW outperforms previous methods. The optimal step size is derived from a second-order approximation, suggesting that it should be inversely proportional to the local Lipschitz constant, L(x;g). The authors note that this constant typically peaks in early iterations, indicating that a smaller step size is necessary at the beginning of training, thus justifying the implementation of a linear warm-up strategy. The duration of the warm-up is suggested to increase with batch size to effectively cover the peak region.

The experimental section details the implementation of the LSTM model for handwritten digit recognition using the MNIST dataset. The authors describe the architecture, including input vector transformations and the dimensions of layers, achieving a notable accuracy improvement over existing state-of-the-art models. The paper emphasizes the importance of consistent hardware and epoch counts across different methods in their comparisons.
