The paper discusses the performance of various machine learning models after implementing hyper-parameter tuning. The first section highlights achieving 98.7% accuracy in 25 epochs of training with a momentum solver and a constant learning rate, aiming to scale the batch size to 8K without losing accuracy. It notes that increasing the batch size beyond 8K on a V100 GPU yields no further speedup.

In the language modeling section for the Penn Treebank (PTB) dataset, the authors detail their LSTM model configurations, including vocabulary size, input vector lengths, and hidden dimensions. They report achieving a perplexity of 1168 for the PTB-small model after 13 epochs and 789 for the PTB-large model after 55 epochs. The models utilize different solvers and learning rates, with a focus on scaling the batch size to 640 without increasing perplexity.

The Google Neural Machine Translation (GNMT) section describes the implementation of a state-of-the-art machine translation technique using a WMT16 English-German dataset. The encoder and decoder architectures are detailed, including the use of shared embeddings, LSTM layers, and a specific attention mechanism. The baseline BLEU score achieved is reported, with results demonstrating that the LEGW method allows for scaling without sacrificing performance.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the effectiveness of the LEGW method across different tasks, showcasing its advantages in scaling batch sizes while maintaining accuracy and efficiency.
