This paper discusses the optimization of large-batch training using the LEGW method, which allows for batch sizes up to 4K without compromising accuracy on TPU devices. It contrasts LEGW with various adaptive solvers, specifically Adam and Adadelta, which serve as baselines due to their minimal tuning requirements. The evaluation demonstrates that LEGW outperforms Adam in tasks involving the PTB and GNMT datasets, achieving higher accuracy and consistency, even after careful tuning of Adam's learning rate.

The paper also compares LEGW against comprehensive tuning methods, showing that LEGW consistently yields better accuracy across different datasets, including MNIST and PTB, even when other methods are allowed more training time. Notably, LEGW does not necessitate hyper-parameter tuning, enhancing its practicality.

Additionally, the robustness of LEGW is validated through its application in CNNs, specifically training ResNet-50 on ImageNet. Here, LEGW, in combination with the LARS optimizer, successfully handled a batch size of 32K while achieving the desired accuracy without hyper-parameter adjustments. The training was completed in a remarkably short duration of 7 minutes on a TPU-v2 Pod, indicating significant efficiency improvements over existing tuning strategies.
