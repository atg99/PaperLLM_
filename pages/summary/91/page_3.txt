The paper discusses the concept of Mini-Batch Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD) and the challenges associated with large-batch training in neural networks. It begins by defining the update rule for weights at each iteration using a mini-batch of samples, denoted as Bt, with size b. The weight update is based on the gradient estimator, which is calculated from the samples in the mini-batch.

The authors highlight that while increasing the batch size can enhance efficiency on modern architectures like TPUs, it can lead to slow convergence and lower test accuracy beyond a certain threshold. Previous studies indicate that large-batch training may suffer from generalization issues, with some suggesting that longer training times can improve accuracy, whereas others have successfully scaled batch sizes significantly without loss of performance.

To effectively manage large-batch training, the paper presents techniques for adjusting the initial learning rate (LR) in accordance with the batch size increase. Two main rules are proposed: the Sqrt Scaling Rule, which maintains gradient variance by adjusting LR based on the square root of the batch size increase, and the Linear Scaling Rule, which increases LR proportionally to the batch size increase.

Additionally, the Gradual Warmup Scheme is introduced to mitigate potential divergence during initial training phases by gradually increasing the LR from a smaller initial value over several epochs. The paper cites various studies where researchers have successfully scaled batch sizes to large numbers without significant accuracy loss, utilizing different algorithms and techniques.

Finally, the authors propose the Linear-Epoch Gradual Warmup (LEGW) scheme as an improvement for training with large batch sizes, extending the warmup period in proportion to the batch size increase to prevent divergence associated with higher learning rates. This approach aims to streamline the tuning process for developers working with deep neural network systems.
