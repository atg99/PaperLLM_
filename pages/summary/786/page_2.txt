The paper discusses the development of Deep Feature Aggregation Network (DFANet), which aims to enhance semantic segmentation performance while minimizing computational costs. Traditional methods often enrich feature maps from final outputs of single path architectures, leading to insufficient integration of high-level context with spatial details. To address this, the authors propose a lightweight approach that incorporates multi-level context through two main strategies: reusing high-level features from the backbone and combining features across different stages of the network.

DFANet consists of three components: lightweight backbones, sub-network aggregation, and sub-stage aggregation modules. The authors modify the Xception network to serve as the backbone, incorporating depthwise separable convolutions for efficiency and adding a fully-connected attention module to enhance the receptive field. Sub-network aggregation refines predictions by upsampling high-level feature maps, while sub-stage aggregation combines features of the same dimension to deliver detailed segmentation results.

The model is tested on Cityscapes and CamVid benchmarks, achieving impressive results with a Mean Intersection Over Union (IOU) of 71.3% at 3.4G FLOPs and 100 FPS on a powerful GPU. Even with smaller input sizes, the performance remains competitive compared to state-of-the-art methods.

Key contributions include establishing a new standard for real-time semantic segmentation with reduced FLOPs and improved speed, presenting a novel network structure for high-level context integration, and maximizing multi-scale receptive fields with minimal computational burden. The paper also highlights the efficiency of depthwise separable convolutions in maintaining performance while reducing computational demands. Overall, DFANet represents a significant advancement in the field of real-time semantic segmentation.
