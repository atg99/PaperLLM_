This paper discusses the development of two modified Xception networks, referred to as Xception A and Xception B, designed to reduce computational complexity while maintaining inference speed for semantic segmentation tasks. The architectures of these models are summarized in a table, highlighting the depthwise separable convolutions used in their construction.

Both models were pretrained on the ImageNet-1k dataset using a specific training protocol, including Nesterov momentum optimization and a defined learning rate strategy. The models were evaluated on the Cityscapes validation dataset, where the features were upsampled to match the original image resolution. 

The results indicated that while Xception A achieved a mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) of 67.1% with the Atrous Spatial Pyramid Pooling (ASPP) module, this was slightly lower than the 68.3% achieved by ResNet-50. However, Xception A demonstrated significantly lower computational complexity, making it favorable for resource-constrained environments. Xception B, while having even less complexity, showed less accuracy.

The paper also explored the impact of input image resolution on performance. Lower resolutions led to decreased accuracy, with a substantial drop in mIoU when the scaling ratio was reduced to 0.25. The results showed that Xception A outperformed ResNet-50 in terms of accuracy at a higher resolution and lower computational cost.

Furthermore, the introduction of a Fully Connected (FC) attention module provided notable accuracy improvements (4-6%) for both Xception models without significantly increasing computational demands. This suggests that high-dimensional context aggregation is beneficial for enhancing performance.

The feature aggregation strategies, including sub-network and sub-stage aggregation, were also evaluated. The findings revealed that a single application of aggregation improved accuracy, while further aggregation could introduce noise and decrease performance due to over-aggregation.

In conclusion, the paper supports the notion that lightweight models, when coupled with effective contextual modules like ASPP and FC attention, can achieve competitive performance in semantic segmentation tasks while reducing computational burdens.
