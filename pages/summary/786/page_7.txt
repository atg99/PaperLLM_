The paper presents a detailed performance comparison of various backbone architectures and aggregation strategies for a deep learning model, specifically focusing on improving segmentation accuracy as measured by mean Intersection over Union (mIoU). 

Table 3 outlines the performance metrics for different backbones, indicating that increasing the number of replicated backbones generally leads to improved mIoU, although diminishing returns are observed beyond a certain point, particularly with Backbone A x4 where mIoU drops significantly. The results suggest that overly small feature resolutions hinder classification accuracy.

Figure 4 illustrates the output progression through stacked backbones, demonstrating that initial predictions contain noise that becomes refined in subsequent stages. This refinement process highlights the benefit of introducing global context and improving detail through multi-scale information aggregation.

Table 4 further evaluates the proposed decoder module, which combines high-level and low-level features to enhance performance. The addition of high-level (HL) and low-level (LL) feature fusions consistently improves mIoU across various backbone configurations. Notably, the Backbone A x3 configuration, while initially yielding lower performance, benefits from the aggregation encoder's design, ultimately enhancing accuracy by effectively reducing noise and preserving detail.

The final architecture, termed DFA, achieves an impressive mIoU of 71.9% with only 3.4 GFLOPs, while a more efficient Backbone B variant maintains a competitive mIoU of 68.4% at just 2.1 GFLOPs. The paper emphasizes the balance between computational efficiency and accuracy, with speed comparisons presented in Table 5 showing the model's effectiveness in real-time scenarios, tested on a virtual machine setup.

Overall, the proposed methods demonstrate a robust approach to feature aggregation and decoding, leading to significant improvements in segmentation tasks while maintaining reasonable computational demands.
