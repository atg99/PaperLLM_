The paper introduces a Deep Feature Aggregation Network (DFANet) aimed at enhancing feature fusion through a structured approach involving sub-network and sub-stage aggregation. The architecture is designed to deliver low-level features and spatial information for improved semantic understanding. 

The sub-network aggregation operates at the network level, combining high-level features by stacking multiple backbone networks. Each encoder's output serves as the input for the next, allowing for a refinement process of the features. This method enhances the evaluation of spatial relationships by processing high-level features multiple times.

In contrast, sub-stage aggregation focuses on merging semantic and spatial information at the stage level across different networks. As networks deepen, they tend to lose spatial details, which traditional methods attempt to recover through skip connections. However, these methods often lack effective communication between parallel branches. The proposed sub-stage aggregation addresses this by integrating features during the encoding phase, where outputs from corresponding stages across networks are combined.

Formally, the sub-stage aggregation is defined by specific equations that describe how features are fused at each stage, aiming to learn a residual representation rather than a straightforward mapping. This innovative approach allows for the retention of resolution and improves the overall feature representation in the network.
