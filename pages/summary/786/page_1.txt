The paper presents DFANet, a highly efficient convolutional neural network (CNN) architecture designed for real-time semantic segmentation while addressing resource constraints. DFANet utilizes a lightweight backbone and employs a cascading approach to aggregate discriminative features through sub-networks and sub-stages. This multi-scale feature propagation allows DFANet to significantly reduce the number of parameters needed while maintaining a sufficient receptive field and enhancing model learning capabilities, thereby achieving a balance between speed and segmentation performance.

Experiments conducted on the Cityscapes and CamVid datasets showcase DFANet’s superior performance, demonstrating it is 8 times less computationally intensive in terms of floating-point operations (FLOPs) and twice as fast compared to existing state-of-the-art methods, all while achieving comparable accuracy. Specifically, DFANet achieves a Mean Intersection over Union (Mean IOU) of 70.3% on the Cityscapes test dataset with only 1.7 GFLOPs and a processing speed of 160 frames per second (FPS) on an NVIDIA Titan X card. When inferring on higher resolution images, it reaches a Mean IOU of 71.3% with 3.4 GFLOPs.

The introduction highlights the challenges faced by previous real-time semantic segmentation methods, which often compromise on detail preservation and computational efficiency. DFANet aims to overcome these limitations by leveraging a lightweight backbone and optimizing feature aggregation without sacrificing accuracy or speed, making it suitable for applications like autonomous driving and video surveillance.
