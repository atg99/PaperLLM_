This paper presents DFANet, a novel deep feature aggregation method aimed at achieving real-time semantic segmentation for high-resolution images. The proposed method demonstrates significant improvements in inference speed while maintaining competitive accuracy compared to state-of-the-art models.

In the analysis of various models, DFANet outperforms existing techniques in terms of speed, achieving up to 160 frames per second (FPS) at a mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) of 70.3% on the Cityscapes test set. This is notably faster than previous models, such as ENet, which operates at 135 FPS with an mIoU of 57%. The proposed method also exhibits efficient feature aggregation, allowing for a reduction in computational complexity (FLOPs) while still delivering strong segmentation performance.

The paper also evaluates DFANet on the CamVid dataset, where it achieves inference speeds of 120 FPS and 160 FPS, respectively, with mIoU scores of 64.7% and 59.3%. Although slightly lower than the best-performing methods, DFANet's speed is a significant advantage for real-time applications.

In conclusion, DFANet effectively combines high-level and low-level features through its aggregation strategy, achieving a balance of speed and accuracy. The results from both the Cityscapes and CamVid datasets validate the effectiveness of this approach. The research received support from the National Key R&D Program of China.
