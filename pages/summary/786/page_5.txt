The paper presents a semantic segmentation network that maintains a continuous flow of features from high-resolution to low-resolution images. The architecture is structured as an encoder-decoder model, utilizing three Xception backbones for encoding, which employ sub-network and sub-stage aggregation methods. The encoder is designed to preserve features from previous layers while enhancing semantic extraction through fully-connected layers retained from ImageNet pretraining.

The decoder is simplified for real-time inference, focusing on efficiently fusing high-level and low-level features. High-level features from the encoder are upsampled and combined with low-level features at the same spatial resolution, followed by another upsampling step to produce the final segmentation predictions. The implementation uses minimal convolution calculations to maintain efficiency.

Experiments were conducted on the challenging Cityscapes and CamVid datasets, with resolutions of 2048x1024 and 960x720, respectively. The training involved a mini-batch stochastic gradient descent strategy with specific parameters for optimization. The analysis includes both qualitative and quantitative evaluations, focusing on mean intersection over union (mIoU) and computation complexity through float-point operations (FLOPs).

The study highlights the importance of balancing backbone network size to avoid accuracy degradation while achieving model acceleration. The lightweight Xception backbone is noted for its favorable speed-accuracy trade-off in semantic segmentation tasks.
