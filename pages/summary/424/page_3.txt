Several studies have explored different model architectures and data augmentation strategies to improve detection results. Wang et al. proposed using Faster R-CNN with hyperparameter adjustments and various augmentations, while another study utilized YOLOv3 with different strategies, although augmentations proved largely ineffective. Kluger et al. employed Faster R-CNN, RetinaNet, and Random Forest, suggesting that ensemble methods could enhance predictions but also increase computational demands.

Maeda et al. implemented Progressive Growing GAN to generate synthetic pothole damage images, improving detection for this less common damage type in Japan. Arya et al. emphasized the need for models adaptable across countries to streamline data collection and training efforts. The consensus is that Faster R-CNN is effective for road damage tasks, particularly when focusing on damage types with limited occurrences.

The methodology outlined includes a data exploration phase, followed by splitting the training dataset into training and evaluation subsets to optimize hyperparameters. The training set comprises 21,041 images from Czech, India, and Japan, with varying damage type distributions, complicating the creation of a universal model. The dataset is divided to maintain a balance reflective of the original damage type distributions, ensuring that the evaluation set contains a sufficient number of images to validate model performance.
