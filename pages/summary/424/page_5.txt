This paper discusses the implementation of synthetic damage augmentation techniques to improve the training of models for damage detection. The authors propose using a fast color transfer technique to integrate synthetic damage patches into existing images, contrasting with traditional Poisson blending methods. They highlight the challenge of achieving natural-looking augmented data but emphasize the utility of quickly generating such data for preliminary exploration.

The augmentation strategy seeks to balance the representation of four damage types across different countries by assigning varying augmentation probabilities based on data exploration. Despite these efforts, the training results indicate that the augmented dataset does not significantly enhance model performance. The authors suggest that the combined dataset's diversity mitigates issues related to damage type imbalance.

Several additional augmentation methods, including random cutouts, brightness/contrast adjustments, and cropping, were tested but failed to improve validation accuracy. The paper attributes the ineffectiveness of these techniques to existing model configurations and the inherent variability in the training data.

Test time augmentation (TTA) was also examined, involving techniques like flipping and resizing, but it did not yield improvements in prediction accuracy. The authors further explore hyperparameter adjustments, specifically batch normalization and pixel mean adjustments, finding no performance enhancement from these changes.

The exploration of anchor box generation in Faster R-CNN is mentioned, with analysis of ground-truth data to determine optimal box sizes and ratios. Overall, the findings suggest that while augmentation strategies are essential for data diversity, their effectiveness is contingent on the existing data quality and model architecture.
