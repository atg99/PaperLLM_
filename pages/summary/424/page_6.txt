In summary, the study investigates the optimization of anchor box sizes and ratios for road damage detection using bounding boxes. The analysis reveals that the majority of bounding box areas are concentrated between 0 to 400 squared pixels, leading to the adjustment of anchor sizes to [[32;64;128]]. The aspect ratios were also refined to [[0:1;0:5;1:0;1:5]] based on the ground-truth bounding box distribution, facilitating faster convergence during training. Despite these optimizations, the performance of the EfﬁcientDet model was inferior to that of Faster R-CNN, indicating potential limitations of EfﬁcientDet for this dataset or the need for further parameter tuning.

Qualitative evaluations show that while predictions generally align well with ground-truth annotations, discrepancies exist, including missed annotations for detected damages. The study illustrates the model's ability to outperform human judgment in certain conditions, emphasizing the potential of machine learning models in detecting road damages under challenging visibility scenarios. Overall, the findings underscore the importance of precise anchor box configurations and the need for further experimentation with different models to enhance prediction accuracy.
