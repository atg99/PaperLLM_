The paper discusses the importance of road maintenance for safety and economic development, emphasizing the need for efficient detection and classification of road damages. It explores the use of deep learning, specifically Detectron2's implementation of Faster R-CNN, to automate these tasks. The authors conduct experiments using the Global Road Damage Detection Challenge 2020 dataset, which includes images from three countries and labels for four types of road damage: Longitudinal Crack, Transverse Crack, Aligator Crack, and Pothole.

The authors evaluate various base models and configurations, concluding that the X101-FPN base model with default settings is effective, achieving F1 scores of 51.0% and 51.4% on test sets 1 and 2, respectively. Despite good visual prediction results, the F1 scores were considered low, prompting an evaluation of the existing annotations and identification of discrepancies. The paper suggests improvements for the labeling process to enhance the dataset's quality.

Key contributions include the exploration of state-of-the-art object detection methods, experimentation with the dataset to find a transferable model, and qualitative evaluation of prediction results alongside recommendations for better labeling strategies. The evaluation method focuses on the F1 score to assess the balance between precision and recall.
