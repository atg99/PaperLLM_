The paper discusses the challenges of manually labeling road damage images for deep learning applications. It highlights instances where human annotations may be insufficient due to factors like varying lighting conditions and the difficulty in accurately setting bounding boxes. The authors illustrate these challenges with examples showing discrepancies between ground-truth and predicted bounding boxes, emphasizing that human labeling can lead to errors.

To address these issues, the authors propose a combination of human and machine learning efforts. They reference Kluger et al., who advocate for using a learned model to predict bounding boxes and classify damage types for unlabeled images, followed by human validation. However, the paper acknowledges that this process remains time-consuming and prone to error.

Further, the authors note that machine learning models can surpass human detection capabilities in some cases, as demonstrated by their model identifying damage types that are not visible to the naked eye until adjustments like brightness changes are made.

The paper also points out that some labels in the training set may have been generated without thorough human validation, leading to inaccuracies. To improve the labeling process, the authors suggest employing a self-learning technique inspired by recent advancements in image classification. This method involves training an initial model on existing labeled data, using it to generate annotations for unlabeled images, and iteratively refining the model with newly predicted annotations. This approach aims to incrementally enhance the labeled dataset, ultimately improving the model's performance.
