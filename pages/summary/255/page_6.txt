The paper discusses a modified approach to utilizing a decoder network in a four-cell DeepCaps model, particularly focusing on improving data generation capabilities. The model initially learns activity vectors constrained within an Rb space, where only a vector Pt, corresponding to the true label during training, is fed into the decoder. A notable limitation of traditional decoders is the lack of control over which physical parameters are represented by specific instantiation parameters, leading to challenges in generating data with consistent characteristics across different classes.

To address these challenges, the authors propose a new procedure where only the predicted class's activity vector Pt is sent to the decoder. This approach ensures that all instantiation parameters are drawn from the same joint distribution, allowing for consistent learning across different classes. Consequently, this method facilitates a better understanding of variations within the MNIST dataset, highlighting that parameters causing larger variations, such as rotation, exhibit higher variance compared to those leading to localized changes.

The paper emphasizes the advantages of this proposed decoder setup over previous methods, which relied on masking non-predicted class parameters, thereby enhancing the model's ability to generate data that meets specific requirements.
