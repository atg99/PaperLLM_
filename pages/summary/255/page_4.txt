The paper discusses a dynamic routing algorithm using 3D convolutions within a deep capsule architecture, specifically addressing the limitations of existing models on complex datasets such as CIFAR10. The routing mechanism involves each capsule tensor in layer l predicting cl+1 capsule tensors in layer l+1. In the initial iteration, predictions are equally weighted, and subsequent iterations adjust the coupling coefficients based on the agreement between predicted outputs and actual outputs, measured through their dot product. 

The proposed DeepCaps architecture consists of 16 convolutional capsule layers followed by a fully-connected capsule layer, designed to improve performance on datasets requiring a deeper understanding of features. The paper highlights the challenges of deep capsule networks, such as computational expense in early layers, and introduces customized layers like ConvCaps and ConvCaps3D to facilitate efficient stacking and maintain the capsule format of outputs. 

The algorithm iterates through routing, with the number of iterations set to three empirically, and adjusts the number of iterations in initial layers to one to manage computational load. The output of the routing process yields the final predictions for the next layer, which are squashed to represent the probability of entity existence. Overall, the paper proposes a novel approach to enhance the capabilities of capsule networks in handling complex image classification tasks.
