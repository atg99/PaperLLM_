The paper "Resurrecting Recurrent Neural Networks for Long Sequences" presents an exploration of various recurrent neural network (RNN) architectures and their performance on tasks from the Long Range Arena (LRA) benchmark. Key findings include the effectiveness of diagonal complex RNNs and their parameterizations, particularly using stable exponential representations which enhance performance across several tasks, including SCIFAR, LISTOPS, TEXT RETRIEVAL, PATHFINDER, and PATHX.

The results indicate that parameter tuning, specifically the initialization of eigenvalues and the use of normalization techniques, significantly impacts the performance of RNNs. The study highlights the superiority of certain configurations, such as the stable exponential parameterization with specific ring initializations, leading to improved accuracy metrics in comparison to traditional dense RNNs. 

The paper also emphasizes the robustness of hyper-parameter settings for the linear RNN units (LRUs) in relation to model width, depth, and learning rates, suggesting that default values suffice for most tasks, while specific adjustments are necessary for more challenging tasks like PATHFINDER and PATHX.

Theoretical insights are provided to justify the modeling capabilities of stacked linear RNNs interleaved with multi-layer perceptron (MLP) blocks. These insights include spectral and function-space perspectives that elucidate how linear RNNs can effectively capture complex nonlinear dynamics, supporting the observed performance improvements in the experiments.

Overall, the paper advocates for the resurgence of RNNs, particularly linear variants, in handling long sequences by leveraging advanced parameterization and initialization strategies.
