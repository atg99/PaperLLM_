The paper discusses advancements in recurrent neural networks (RNNs) for handling long sequences, focusing on credit assignment methods that improve training efficiency and biological plausibility. It reviews literature on alternatives to backpropagation, including Real-Time Recurrent Learning and sparse attention mechanisms, and suggests that insights from these approaches could enhance the training speed of linear RNNs. 

Furthermore, it highlights research on higher-order recurrent networks, particularly the Second-Order Tree Recurrent Network, proposing that simplified parallel implementations of these models could be beneficial. The paper also addresses the vanishing gradient problem through alternative models, including deep state-space models (SSMs) that utilize continuous-time representations.

Gu et al.'s work on SSMs reveals that structured state-space models can capture relevant information from previous inputs, leading to the development of the S4 model, which achieved state-of-the-art results on the Long Range Arena benchmark. Subsequent studies showed that much of S4's performance could be replicated using simplified diagonal versions of the state-space model, known as DSS and S4D.

The paper notes the rising interest in S4-like models and highlights the findings of Gupta et al. that suggest the success of S4 may be due to fundamental effects beyond the original HiPPO theory. It also introduces the S5 model, which processes input dimensions in parallel, setting new performance records and indicating a need for further exploration of its underlying mechanics. Additionally, the paper mentions novel variants of the S4 architecture, such as Liquid S4, which aim to simplify and enhance the original model's capabilities.
