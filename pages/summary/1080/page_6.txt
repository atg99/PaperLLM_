The paper "Resurrecting Recurrent Neural Networks for Long Sequences" explores the revival of recurrent neural networks (RNNs) for handling long sequences, focusing on the initialization and parameterization techniques that enhance their performance. The authors discuss the implications of Glorot initialization on the spectral properties of RNNs, highlighting that the eigenvalues of matrices initialized in this manner converge to the unit disk in the complex plane, which supports the stability of linear RNNs.

Key contributions include the introduction of a diagonal initialization scheme that samples eigenvalues uniformly from a specified range in the complex plane. This approach is shown to improve both the accuracy and computational efficiency of RNNs on tasks such as ListOps and sCIFAR, reducing training times to be comparable with state-of-the-art deep state space models (SSMs).

The paper also emphasizes the importance of stable parameterization for learning long-range dependencies, particularly through the use of exponential parameterization. This method decouples the eigenvalue magnitude from oscillation frequency, facilitating easier optimization. The authors demonstrate that this parameterization helps maintain stability during training, thereby preventing issues like vanishing gradients.

Overall, the findings suggest that with appropriate initialization and parameterization techniques, RNNs can effectively model long sequences, achieving competitive performance and efficiency.
