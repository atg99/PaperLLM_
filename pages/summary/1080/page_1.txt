The paper "Resurrecting Recurrent Neural Networks for Long Sequences" by Antonio Orvieto and colleagues addresses the challenges associated with Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) in modeling long sequences. While RNNs provide fast inference, they are difficult to optimize and slow to train due to issues like vanishing and exploding gradients. Although various techniques have been developed to mitigate these problems, RNNs still struggle with long-range dependencies and scalability.

The authors compare RNNs to recent advancements in deep state-space models (SSMs), which have shown superior performance on long sequence tasks while also enabling fast parallel training and RNN-like inference speeds. However, the differences between SSMs and RNNs raise questions about the source of SSMs' performance improvements.

To bridge this gap, the paper proposes enhancements to standard RNN architectures. The authors introduce a new RNN block called the Linear Recurrent Unit (LRU), which matches the performance of deep SSMs on the Long Range Arena benchmark while maintaining computational efficiency. Key modifications to RNNs include linearizing and diagonalizing recurrence relations, improving parameterization and initialization strategies, and implementing careful normalization during the forward pass.

Overall, the findings provide insights into the reasons behind SSMs' success and show that with thoughtful design, RNNs can achieve comparable performance on long-range reasoning tasks.
