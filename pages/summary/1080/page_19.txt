This paper explores the enhancement of recurrent neural networks (RNNs) for handling long sequences by enforcing stability and incorporating normalization techniques. The findings indicate that optimal performance is achieved when initializing the eigenvalues closer to the unit disk, with specific configurations of rmin and rmax yielding improved results. Notably, the best performance of 89.0% was observed with rmin=0.9 and rmax=0.999, demonstrating that stability and normalization contribute significantly to training stability and performance, particularly for learning long-range dependencies.

The paper expands on previous results, showing that vanilla dense RNNs benefit from dropping recurrent nonlinearities and that diagonalizing the linear RNN model can affect performance positively. The research highlights the advantages of initializing the diagonal RNN with a distribution close to the circle boundary, which helps mitigate vanishing gradients, thus facilitating long-range reasoning.

Additionally, the experimental setup involves using the S4 architecture with RNN layers, detailing the network's structure, which includes multiple residual blocks. The results suggest that the integration of stability and normalization techniques can close the accuracy gap with more advanced models, thereby enhancing the effectiveness of RNNs for long sequence tasks.
