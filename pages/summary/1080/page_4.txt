The paper "Resurrecting Recurrent Neural Networks for Long Sequences" investigates the potential of recurrent neural networks (RNNs) in handling long-range dependencies, particularly in comparison to state-space models (SSMs). The authors highlight the advantages of linear RNNs, which can be parallelized efficiently, unlike their nonlinear counterparts that require sequential computation. They emphasize that the parameterization of matrices A and B in their model is critical, as learning occurs on continuous-time parameters, and initialization plays a significant role in performance.

Using the Long Range Arena benchmark, the authors demonstrate that deep SSMs outperform transformers in long-range sequence tasks. They propose a modified architecture that replaces SSM layers with RNN layers while maintaining a similar parameter count. Their experimental setup involves a six-layer network with residual connections, trained using the AdamW optimizer, and explores various hyperparameters for optimal performance.

The paper outlines key steps for designing performant deep RNNs, beginning with the benefits of linear recurrences. The authors find that linear RNN blocks, when combined with nonlinear multi-layer perceptrons or gated linear units, can outperform tuned nonlinear RNN variants. They note that while linearity improves performance on many tasks, it still falls short of S4 on specific tasks. However, for text and retrieval tasks, vanilla RNNs achieve performance comparable to deep SSMs.

The authors argue against the necessity of recurrent nonlinearities, traditionally considered essential for RNN success, and present a deeper architecture with nonlinearity applied position-wise after RNN blocks. Through spectral analysis and theoretical considerations, they explore how these changes enhance expressivity and stability in long-range modeling, ultimately leading to a normalization strategy that aligns RNN performance with that of SSMs.
