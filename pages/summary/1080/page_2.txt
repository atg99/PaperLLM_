This paper explores the potential to enhance the performance of recurrent neural networks (RNNs) for long sequence tasks, particularly in light of recent advances in deep state space models (SSMs) like S4. The authors investigate whether the efficiency and effectiveness of these deep SSMs can be replicated using RNNs, ultimately introducing a new model referred to as the Linear Recurrent Unit (LRU).

The research emphasizes that while S4 models exhibit superior performance during training, their unique characteristics, such as specific initialization of state matrices, may not be as critical as previously thought. This raises questions about the underlying mechanisms that contribute to the efficacy of SSMs.

Key findings include:

1. **Linear Recurrences**: The authors demonstrate that removing nonlinearities from RNN recurrences (i.e., using linear recurrences) significantly enhances accuracy on the Long Range Arena (LRA) benchmark. This change allows the model to effectively capture complex sequence relationships without the pitfalls of gradient issues associated with nonlinear activations.

2. **Complex Diagonal Recurrent Matrices**: The paper discusses reparameterizing dense linear RNN layers into a complex diagonal form, which maintains expressivity while enabling efficient training through parallelization. This approach, also utilized by SSMs, further boosts training speed.

3. **Stable Exponential Parameterization**: The authors introduce stable exponential parameterization techniques that improve model stability and performance, particularly for long-range dependencies.

The results indicate that with these modifications, RNNs can achieve performance levels comparable to deep SSMs on LRA tasks, thereby resurrecting their utility in long sequence modeling. The paper provides a detailed analysis of the steps and methodologies employed, contributing valuable insights into the design of efficient RNN architectures.
