The paper "Resurrecting Recurrent Neural Networks for Long Sequences" explores the potential of recurrent neural networks (RNNs) in handling long sequences by investigating their expressivity and the impact of interleaving RNNs with multilayer perceptrons (MLPs). It presents a detailed table of hyperparameters for various tasks, including SCIFAR, LISTOPS, TEXT, RETRIEVAL, PATHFINDER, and PATHX, highlighting aspects such as task depth, hidden units, iterations, batch size, learning rate factor, weight decay, and dropout.

The authors argue that in a sequence-to-sequence context, RNNs should effectively map inputs to outputs using a flexible nonlinear transition map. They assert that a fully-expressive model can approximate any nonlinear mapping of inputs to outputs. The paper further delves into the spectral limitations of linear RNNs, which can approximate shift-invariant linear maps but struggle to retain information about frequencies not present in the input signal.

A key contribution is the analysis of the effects of combining RNNs with MLPs. The authors demonstrate that inserting an activation function and a linear layer after each RNN output leads to spectral leakage, allowing information to propagate across different frequency components. This is illustrated through a formal proposition showing how the ReLU activation function modifies the frequency representation of signals.

Overall, the paper emphasizes that interleaving linear RNNs with MLPs enhances their ability to approximate complex nonlinear relationships in sequential data, thus reviving their utility for long sequence tasks.
