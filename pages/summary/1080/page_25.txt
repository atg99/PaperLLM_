In the subsection on optimization of recurrent blocks, the authors validate their claims regarding the optimization of linear recurrent neural networks (RNNs) through experimental findings using simple toy examples. They highlight that recurrent nonlinearities, such as those produced by tanh or sigmoid activations, hinder the efficiency of gradient descent. Their results indicate that linear RNNs can serve as effective foundational blocks for modeling complex nonlinear dynamics when stacked in deep architectures. 

The authors reference previous work that suggests linear RNNs can perform comparably to more complex architectures when it comes to modeling tasks, particularly in terms of accuracy and convergence speed. They present an experiment where a single-layer RNN learns a one-dimensional convolution kernel, comparing performance between linear and tanh activations. The results show that linear RNNs outperform their nonlinear counterparts in terms of convergence speed, even when tuning the learning rate.

The authors further explore the benefits of exponential parameterization for learning linear RNN cores, which enhances learning efficiency by decoupling phase and magnitude parameters. They illustrate that as the target phase approaches the imaginary axis, standard parameterization can slow down learning due to the non-axis-aligned loss landscape, which complicates the optimization process. In contrast, exponential parameterization allows for a more effective learning process, particularly when the system needs to adjust its phase without altering its magnitude. 

Overall, the findings emphasize the advantages of linear RNNs and exponential parameterization in optimizing recurrent neural network architectures, supporting the idea that simplifying the recurrent core can lead to better performance in learning tasks.
