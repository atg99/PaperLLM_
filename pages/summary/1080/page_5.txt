The paper "Resurrecting Recurrent Neural Networks for Long Sequences" explores the effectiveness of recurrent neural networks (RNNs) for tasks involving long sequences. It presents empirical results demonstrating how removing nonlinearity from recurrent units impacts the performance on various tasks, such as sCIFAR, ListOps, Text, and Retrieval. The findings indicate that linear RNNs can effectively approximate complex nonlinear systems when interleaved with nonlinear feedforward blocks, enhancing the system's capacity in the spectral domain.

The authors argue that using complex-valued diagonal recurrent matrices significantly improves the computational efficiency of training and inference without sacrificing performance. They emphasize that diagonalization, combined with appropriate initialization, can mitigate common issues related to vanishing and exploding gradients, which plague traditional RNNs. The paper details the mathematical foundation for diagonalizing recurrent matrices, highlighting the necessity of complex numbers for an efficient representation of non-symmetric matrices.

The results show that diagonal linear RNNs can train approximately eight times faster than their dense counterparts while maintaining performance, mainly due to the computational advantages of handling diagonal matrices and the ability to parallelize operations. The authors propose a method for initializing diagonal systems to ensure that the eigenvalue spectrum remains consistent with dense linear RNNs, thereby isolating the effects of diagonalization from initialization.

Overall, the paper advocates for a renewed focus on linear RNN architectures, demonstrating their potential for efficiency and effectiveness in processing long sequences.
