The section discusses additional experimental results related to the training speed and stability effects of the Linear Recurrent Unit (LRU) compared to traditional Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) with tanh activations, as well as S4D and S5 models. The training speeds for various tasks are presented in Table 4, demonstrating that the LRU significantly outperforms the tanh RNN across all tasks, achieving speedups ranging from 1.9 to 29 times, depending on the specific task. 

The paper also illustrates an example of parallelization in the computation of hidden states in a linear recurrent model, showing how parallel computation can reduce the number of sequential steps needed from three to two, particularly when the matrix A is diagonal. This parallelization is especially beneficial for long sequences, minimizing computational overhead.

Furthermore, the section examines the impact of stability and normalization during training, specifically focusing on the parameters rmax and rmin in the sCIFAR experiment. It indicates that optimal performance occurs when rmax is set to rmin minus 0.9, with performance declining as rmax increases beyond this threshold. The learning rate was fixed at 0.004 for these experiments, which was determined to be optimal under a stable exponential parameterization.
