The paper discusses the resurgence of recurrent neural networks (RNNs) for handling long sequences, addressing challenges associated with training and performance. It highlights foundational works in RNNs, including the construction of deterministic finite-state automata and the difficulties in training RNNs due to issues like vanishing gradients. Key advancements in the field, such as the introduction of attention mechanisms and the development of generative models like WaveNet, are also reviewed. The authors emphasize continual learning techniques and propose novel architectures that enhance RNN capabilities, particularly in sequence modeling. The paper further explores the implications of nonlinearity and memory units in improving the approximation capacity of RNNs. Overall, it advocates for a renewed focus on RNNs, suggesting they remain a vital component in the landscape of neural network architectures for sequence data.
