The paper discusses the behavior of recurrent neural networks (RNNs) when processing long sequences, specifically addressing the phenomenon of "forward-pass blow-up." Proposition 3.3 establishes that for a diagonal matrix Λ with eigenvalues uniformly sampled on a circle in the complex plane, the squared norm of the state xk converges to a specific expected value as k approaches infinity. This expected value is derived under certain conditions, such as constant or white-noise input and Glorot input projection.

The proof begins by considering the challenging case where the input uk is constant. The squared norm is expressed as a double sum involving the entries of Λ and the input. The diagonal nature of Λ allows for simplification, leading to the evaluation of the expected value through a probability change of variables. This involves sampling the eigenvalues using polar coordinates and examining the resulting sums.

The expectation calculations reveal that non-zero contributions occur only when indices are equal, simplifying the analysis. The final result shows that the expected squared norm is linked to the logarithm of a ratio involving the minimum and maximum radii of the sampled eigenvalues. The paper also briefly addresses the case of white noise input, indicating that the proof follows similar reasoning to the constant input case.

Overall, the findings highlight the impact of the eigenvalue distribution on the stability of RNNs when dealing with long sequences, contributing to the understanding of RNN dynamics in such scenarios.
