The paper "Resurrecting Recurrent Neural Networks for Long Sequences" discusses advancements in recurrent neural networks (RNNs), particularly focusing on how matrix exponentials and structured initialization influence performance in long sequence tasks. It highlights the role of the Zero-Order Hold (ZOH) discretization, which leads to a recurrence relation that simplifies training by leveraging matrix exponentials. The authors argue that the success of their approach is rooted in a magnitude-phase decoupling within the recurrence, enhancing the training process, rather than solely in accurate integration.

The paper also challenges the necessity of structured initialization based on the HiPPO theory, demonstrating that simple uniform initialization can effectively address complex tasks such as PathX. Results show that uniform initialization, akin to diagonalized Glorot initialization, can match the performance of more intricate models.

Further, the authors explore how discretization affects the initialization spectrum, revealing that linking eigenvalue phases and magnitudes is not essential for achieving high accuracy in the Long Range Arena tasks. They suggest that a more natural magnitude-independent initialization can yield comparable results, even for long sequences.

The paper concludes that key factors contributing to the success of diagonal state-space models include the use of linear recurrences and complex diagonal exponential matrices, along with normalization and initialization techniques derived from discretization. Conversely, aspects like parameter sharing and continuous-time interpretations are deemed unnecessary for achieving optimal performance.
