The paper discusses the revival of recurrent neural networks (RNNs) for modeling long sequences, addressing the limitations of traditional architectures in handling extended time dependencies. It highlights various advancements in RNNs, including the Independently Recurrent Neural Network (IndRNN), which allows for deeper and longer networks. The authors explore the efficacy of different initialization techniques and activation functions, such as Mish, to enhance RNN performance. The integration of Koopman operator theory into neural networks is also examined, presenting a framework for better understanding dynamic systems. The paper emphasizes the importance of data-driven modeling approaches, such as dynamic mode decomposition, in improving RNN training and performance. Overall, it advocates for a renewed focus on RNNs, leveraging modern techniques to overcome historical challenges in sequence modeling.
