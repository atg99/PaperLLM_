The paper discusses the performance of recurrent neural networks (RNNs) in handling long sequences, presenting empirical results across various tasks including SCIFAR, LISTOPS, TEXT, RETRIEVAL, PATHFINDER, and PATHX. The authors compare various RNN architectures and configurations, highlighting the effectiveness of their S4D and S5 models, which achieve notable accuracy improvements over traditional RNN approaches.

Key findings include the performance metrics for different RNN types, such as RNN-L, RNN-R, and RNN-T, with S4D and S5 models showing superior results, particularly in SCIFAR and LISTOPS tasks. The paper also explores the impact of different nonlinearities on RNN accuracy, demonstrating that removing nonlinearity can affect performance.

The experimental setup employs AdamW as the optimizer with a learning rate warmup strategy, and various tuning parameters for model dimensions and initialization schemes are discussed. The authors emphasize the scalability of their architecture and the use of batch normalization, which contributes to improved performance.

The paper concludes that their RNN configurations, particularly S4D and S5, effectively address the limitations of traditional RNNs, making them suitable for long sequence tasks in various applications. The results indicate potential for future improvements and applications in the field of deep learning.
