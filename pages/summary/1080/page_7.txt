The paper discusses improvements in the performance of linear diagonal complex recurrent neural networks (RNNs) for long sequence tasks, particularly focusing on the parametrization of the transition matrix. It highlights the advantages of using an exponential representation for the eigenvalues of the transition matrix, demonstrating notable performance gains in tasks such as SCIFAR, ListOps, and Pathfinder. The stable parameterization of eigenvalues is emphasized, as it significantly enhances the ability of RNNs to handle long-range dependencies, especially in challenging datasets like Pathfinder.

The paper further elaborates on the initialization of eigenvalues on a ring close to the unit disk to promote long-range interactions and mitigate vanishing gradients. Results indicate that without enforcing stability, performance declines as the maximum eigenvalue approaches 0.9, while enforcing stability allows for higher values and improved performance. 

Additionally, the authors address normalization strategies to manage the training instability observed when eigenvalues are initialized close to the unit disk. They propose a normalization scheme for hidden activations that adaptively scales the input for each eigendirection, which leads to consistent performance improvements in long-range reasoning tasks. 

Overall, the modifications discussed contribute to the development of a more robust RNN model capable of effectively learning long-range dependencies in sequential data.
