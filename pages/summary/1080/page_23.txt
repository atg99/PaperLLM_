The paper discusses the revival of Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) for processing long sequences by analyzing the effects of ReLU activation and utilizing Fourier transforms. It establishes that the Fourier transform of a ReLU activated signal retains the original transform but is modified by a kernel that shifts information to higher frequencies. This capability allows RNNs to manage frequency scaling in a nonlinear fashion, overcoming limitations faced by linear RNNs, regardless of their width.

The authors introduce a proof demonstrating that multiplications in the time domain correspond to convolutions in the frequency domain. By defining specific functions and utilizing properties of Fourier transforms, they show that the ReLU function can be effectively represented in the frequency domain, enhancing the understanding of its dynamics.

Additionally, the paper explores insights from Koopman operator theory, which, in conjunction with recent developments in dynamic mode decomposition, provides a theoretical framework for understanding functions that can be approximated by linear RNNs interleaved with Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs). It highlights the role of the Koopman operator in evolving observables in nonlinear dynamical systems, illustrating its relevance in advancing the measurement of functions such as sine over time.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the potential of combining RNNs with MLPs to achieve complex nonlinear dynamics, leveraging mathematical foundations to enhance the capabilities of neural networks in handling long sequences.
