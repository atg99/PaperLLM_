The paper discusses the application of Koopman operator theory to recurrent neural networks (RNNs) for modeling long sequences in nonlinear dynamical systems. The Koopman operator is characterized by its linearity and boundedness, allowing for the representation of any observable function in terms of eigenfunctions. This property enables the transformation of complex nonlinear systems into a linear framework through a high-dimensional blow-up of the state space, akin to the function of a wide multi-layer perceptron (MLP) combined with a linear RNN.

The authors outline how the theory can be extended to nonlinear systems with inputs, demonstrating that the Koopman operator remains linear and bounded even in this more complex scenario. By defining the operator for observables that incorporate input sequences, the paper illustrates that the nonlinear dynamical system can be expressed in terms of a linear RNN framework.

Ultimately, the paper posits that Koopman operator theory provides a foundational basis for the representation of regular nonlinear dynamical systems using linear RNNs, facilitated by appropriate nonlinear reparameterization of inputs via MLPs. While the theoretical connection is established, the authors note that a detailed quantitative analysis would require further technical exploration, potentially drawing from recent advancements in the statistical learning community.
