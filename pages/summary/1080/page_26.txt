The paper "Resurrecting Recurrent Neural Networks for Long Sequences" discusses the challenges and advancements in training recurrent neural networks (RNNs) for long sequence tasks. It highlights the importance of parameterization techniques, specifically the use of complex eigenvalues and their impact on the optimization process.

The authors note that exponential parameterization significantly aids in learning complex eigenvalues, particularly as they approach purely imaginary settings. This approach aligns gradients better with phase parameters, facilitating more flexible learning. The findings are supported by experiments conducted on the Long Range Arena benchmark.

Additionally, the paper explores alternatives to complex number usage by presenting a derivation of the canonical real form for non-symmetric real-valued matrices. The authors explain that after diagonalization, one can obtain real eigenvalues and pairs of complex conjugate eigenvalues, emphasizing the existence of real eigenvectors corresponding to real eigenvalues.

The analysis includes a mathematical proof illustrating the relationship between eigenvalues and eigenvectors in real-valued matrices, demonstrating that complex eigenvalues come with complex conjugate counterparts. The discussion extends to the action of matrices on real eigenvectors and the implications for subspaces spanned by complex eigenvalues, providing a comprehensive understanding of the underlying linear algebra principles relevant to RNN training.

Overall, the paper presents a thorough examination of techniques and theoretical foundations necessary for improving RNN performance on long sequences, advocating for the revival of RNNs through advanced mathematical approaches.
