The paper investigates the performance of different graph partitioning strategies in the context of Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs), particularly focusing on the effectiveness of clustering partitioning over random partitioning. The results presented in Table 2 demonstrate that clustering partitioning yields higher test F1 scores across three public GCN datasets: Cora, Pubmed, and PPI. Clustering partitions minimize between-partition links, which contributes to better performance.

The authors further analyze label distribution within batches, showing that clustering partitions generally have lower entropy values, indicating a more skewed label distribution compared to random partitions. This skew can lead to biased gradient estimations during Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD) updates.

To address the identified issues of variance and biased estimation in clustering partitions, the authors propose a stochastic multiple clustering approach. This method involves partitioning the graph into a large number of clusters and randomly selecting a subset of these clusters for each batch during SGD updates. By incorporating between-cluster links, this approach reduces variance across batches and improves convergence, as demonstrated in experiments with the Reddit dataset.

The paper also discusses challenges in training deeper GCNs, noting that previous studies may have overemphasized the limitations of adding layers due to small dataset sizes. The authors suggest that deeper models can struggle with information transmission across layers, advocating for techniques like residual connections to facilitate better information flow.

Overall, the proposed methods aim to enhance the performance and efficiency of GCN training by leveraging advanced partitioning strategies and addressing the complexities associated with deeper network architectures.
