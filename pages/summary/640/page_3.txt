The paper discusses the time and space complexity of various Graph Convolutional Network (GCN) training algorithms, including GCN, GraphSAGE, FastGCN, VR-GCN, and Cluster-GCN. It emphasizes the limitations of full gradient descent in terms of computational and memory costs, particularly noting that it requires O(NFL) space for embedding matrices and a longer convergence time due to updates occurring only once per epoch.

Recent methods like mini-batch Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD) have been shown to enhance training speed and reduce memory usage by calculating gradients based on smaller batches. However, vanilla mini-batch SGD suffers from slow per-epoch times due to the need to aggregate embeddings from a large number of neighboring nodes, leading to a time complexity of O(bdLF2) for updates and O(NdLF2) per epoch.

The concept of "embedding utilization" is introduced as a measure of computational efficiency, indicating the reuse of embeddings across computations. Full-batch gradient descent achieves maximal embedding utilization, while mini-batch SGD tends to have lower utilization due to the sparsity of the graph.

The paper reviews attempts to improve mini-batch SGD through various approaches, such as GraphSAGE's fixed-size neighbor sampling and FastGCN's importance sampling. VR-GCN's strategy of storing previously computed embeddings is noted for its effectiveness, although it incurs high memory costs.

The authors propose the Cluster-GCN algorithm, which aims to maximize embedding utilization by designing batches to maximize the number of edges within the batch. This technique seeks to achieve the time efficiency of full gradient descent while maintaining the memory efficiency of vanilla SGD.

Overall, the paper illustrates the complexities and challenges in training GCNs and introduces a novel approach to enhance training efficiency by leveraging embedding utilization through clustering.
