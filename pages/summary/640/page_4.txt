The paper introduces Cluster-GCN, a novel approach to graph convolution that addresses the inefficiencies associated with traditional methods, particularly in terms of neighborhood expansion during stochastic gradient descent (SGD) updates. Traditional graph convolution methods face exponential neighborhood expansion, which can be computationally expensive. In contrast, Cluster-GCN partitions a graph into clusters, allowing for more efficient processing by focusing on intra-cluster connections.

The graph is divided into c groups, creating c subgraphs that simplify the adjacency matrix into a block-diagonal form. This structure enables the decomposition of the objective function, allowing for easier computation of embeddings and loss functions. The model updates are performed on these smaller subgraphs, leveraging only the relevant adjacency and feature matrices for each cluster during the SGD process.

Graph clustering algorithms like Metis and Graclus are utilized to form partitions that maximize within-cluster links while minimizing between-cluster connections. This clustering enhances the utilization of embeddings, as neighboring nodes are likely to reside within the same cluster, thereby reducing the need for extensive neighborhood searches.

Empirical results demonstrate that using clustering partitions results in higher accuracy compared to random partitions, highlighting the importance of effective graph partitioning. The paper also discusses the time and space complexity of Cluster-GCN, showing that it operates with linear complexity in terms of the number of nodes per batch, making it more efficient than previous methods. Overall, Cluster-GCN presents a significant advancement in graph convolutional networks, combining efficiency with improved accuracy through strategic graph clustering.
