The paper presents Cluster-GCN, an innovative algorithm designed to enhance the training efficiency of deep Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) on large-scale datasets. Traditional Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD) methods for GCNs face challenges due to their escalating computational demands and substantial memory requirements as the number of layers increases. Cluster-GCN addresses these issues by leveraging graph clustering to optimize the training process.

In this approach, the algorithm samples a subset of nodes that correspond to a dense subgraph identified through clustering. By limiting the neighborhood search to this subgraph, Cluster-GCN significantly improves both memory usage and computational efficiency, achieving test accuracies comparable to existing methods. The authors validate the scalability of their algorithm using a new dataset, Amazon2M, which contains 2 million nodes and 61 million edges, surpassing the previous largest dataset.

The results demonstrate that Cluster-GCN outperforms the previous state-of-the-art VR-GCN in terms of speed and memory usage, completing training of a 3-layer GCN in 1523 seconds with 2.2GB of memory compared to 1961 seconds and 11.2GB for VR-GCN. The algorithm also successfully trains deeper GCNs, achieving a state-of-the-art test F1 score of 99.36 on the PPI dataset, surpassing the previous best score of 98.71.

Overall, Cluster-GCN represents a significant advancement in GCN training, enabling the use of deeper architectures and providing improved efficiency for large-scale graph data.
