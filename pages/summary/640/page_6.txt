The paper presents Cluster GCN, a novel method aimed at enhancing the training of deep Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs). The method begins by partitioning graph nodes into clusters using the METIS algorithm. During each iteration, clusters are randomly selected to form a subgraph, and the loss on this subgraph is computed using a gradient estimator. 

A key contribution of the paper is the introduction of a "diagonal enhancement" technique to address the limitations of traditional GCN aggregation methods. In deep GCNs, the aggregation process tends to overlook the contribution of nearby nodes compared to distant ones. To mitigate this, the authors propose amplifying the diagonal components of the adjacency matrix, thereby giving greater weight to the previous layer's representations. This is executed through a modified normalization process that incorporates an identity matrix into the adjacency matrix and adjusts for numerical stability.

The method is evaluated across two tasks—multi-label and multi-class classification—on four public datasets, including the notably large Reddit and Amazon2M datasets. The experiments compare Cluster GCN against state-of-the-art GCN training algorithms, demonstrating its efficiency in terms of training speed and accuracy. The results indicate that Cluster GCN outperforms other methods, particularly for medium-sized datasets, validating its effectiveness in achieving state-of-the-art (SOTA) performance.

Overall, the paper underscores the importance of neighborhood information and numerical stability in deep GCN training, providing a comprehensive framework for future research in graph neural networks.
