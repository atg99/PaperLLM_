The paper presents a comparative analysis of memory usage and training efficiency of different Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs), specifically focusing on Cluster-GCN and VRGCN across various datasets. 

Table 5 illustrates memory usage across different models and datasets, with VRGCN consistently requiring more memory than Cluster-GCN. For instance, on the Reddit dataset with a 4-layer GCN and 512 hidden units, VRGCN requires 2064 MB compared to Cluster-GCN's 308 MB. This discrepancy arises because VRGCN saves historical embeddings for each layer, leading to increased memory demands, especially as layers increase.

Table 6 benchmarks sparse tensor operations in PyTorch and TensorFlow, highlighting that TensorFlow outperforms PyTorch significantly, particularly as the number of hidden units rises. This performance difference contributes to longer training times for Cluster-GCN on the Amazon dataset.

The paper introduces a new dataset, Amazon2M, which consists of over 2 million nodes and 61 million edges, constructed from Amazon co-purchasing networks. The dataset's features are derived from product descriptions, and the top-level categories serve as labels. 

In comparing training times, memory usage, and accuracy (F1 score) between VRGCN and Cluster-GCN on Amazon2M, it is observed that VRGCN performs faster with a 2-layer GCN but is slower with more layers. Notably, VRGCN experiences memory issues when training deeper networks, whereas Cluster-GCN maintains efficiency.

The findings suggest that while deeper GCNs can enhance accuracy, they also introduce complexity in training, particularly regarding neighborhood searching. The paper concludes that Cluster-GCN offers a more memory-efficient alternative for training deeper GCNs, facilitating better scalability for large graphs.
