The paper discusses the Multimodal Style Transfer (MST) method, which utilizes a whitening matrix (Wc) and a coloring matrix (Cs) derived from the covariance matrices of content and style features to facilitate feature transformation. The matrices Elkc and Dlkc, along with their style counterparts Elks and Dlks, are essential for transforming features while maintaining robustness and efficiency. Unlike previous methods that blend transferred features uniformly, MST allows for adaptive blending based on specific content-style pairs, enhancing flexibility in representation.

The implementation details reveal that MST employs a pre-trained VGG-19 model as the encoder and mirrors it for decoding, replacing pooling layers with up-scaling layers. The training process involves computing a perceptual loss that combines content loss and style loss, with specific formulations for each based on features extracted from the VGG-19 model.

The paper contrasts MST with other notable style transfer methods, highlighting differences in feature extraction, training requirements, and efficiency. MST's approach to clustering style features for matching with content features distinguishes it from methods like CNNMRF and MT-Net, while also offering advantages over WCT by using both content and additional style images for training.

The experiments outlined in the paper focus on validating the contributions of MST components, showcasing its effectiveness and user control flexibility through extensive evaluations.
