The paper discusses a method for style matching in images by utilizing a graph-based approach to align content features with style cluster centers at the pixel level. The authors calculate the cosine distance between content features and style cluster centers to assess the similarity. They introduce a data energy term to minimize disagreement between assigned labels and content features, while also addressing spatial continuity through a smooth term that encourages similar labeling within local regions.

The overall energy function combines both data and smoothness terms, making the problem of style matching a minimization challenge. Due to the complexity of this minimization, the authors propose using graph cuts to efficiently solve the problem by transforming it into a min-cut/max-flow problem. This approach allows for effective organization of content features based on their corresponding style labels.

Visualization results demonstrate the effectiveness of style feature clustering in capturing semantic information from style images and aligning it with content structures. The authors also outline a multimodal style transfer process that includes centering, whitening, and coloring of content and style feature pairs to achieve the final stylized output. This method aims to alleviate common artifacts in style transfer, particularly in cases with simple styles or large uniform backgrounds.
