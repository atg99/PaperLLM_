The paper discusses the limitations of existing style transfer methods, particularly in how they handle complex textures and spatial patterns in style images. Traditional methods often struggle to adaptively match style patterns to the content, leading to undesirable visual artifacts such as improper strokes in smooth areas and wash-out effects in clear spatial patterns. The authors note that these methods tend to represent style features as unimodal distributions, which is insufficient for capturing the diversity of spatially-distributed style patterns.

To address these challenges, the authors introduce a novel approach called multimodal style transfer (MST). This method combines the strengths of parametric and non-parametric techniques by proposing a multimodal style representation that consists of clusters representing different style patterns. MST formulates style-content matching as an energy minimization problem solved via graph cuts, allowing for adaptive matching of style clusters to content features based on spatial configuration.

The key contributions of the paper include the development of a multimodal style representation, the formulation of style-content matching as a graph-based optimization problem, and extensive comparisons demonstrating the robustness and flexibility of MST against state-of-the-art methods. The authors emphasize that their approach allows for mixing different styles, providing diverse stylized outputs.

In the related works section, the paper reviews the evolution of style transfer techniques, highlighting early formulations, improvements, and contemporary methods that focus on real-time processing and stability. The authors position their MST method as a significant advancement in the field, capable of enhancing existing stylization techniques while offering a more flexible framework for style transfer.
