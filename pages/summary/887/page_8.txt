The paper presents a novel approach to multi-style transfer (MST) that enhances the flexibility and effectiveness of style transfer methods. It investigates the impact of varying the number of style clusters (K) on stylization outcomes. With K set to 1, the MST approach results in either overly complex or simplistic stylizations that fail to align with content structures. In contrast, increasing K allows for a more nuanced and adaptive treatment of style patterns, leading to stylizations that better match content features and provide users with multiple stylistic options.

The authors differentiate their method from traditional style transfer techniques, which often blend styles using fixed weights. Instead, MST adaptively interpolates style patterns relative to the content, preserving essential structures while applying styles from multiple sources. This is illustrated through comparisons with AdaIN and WCT, which exhibit wash-out artifacts, whereas MST successfully transfers distinct style elements to appropriate content areas without requiring additional user input for spatial control.

Furthermore, the paper explores the generalization of MST to enhance existing methods like AdaIN by incorporating style clustering and graph-based matching. This integration mitigates issues such as distortion of content features typically seen in traditional approaches.

In conclusion, the paper argues that MST, through its multimodal style representation and adaptive matching, offers a significant improvement in style transfer techniques. Extensive experiments validate MST's effectiveness, robustness, and flexibility, suggesting its potential for broader application in style transfer methodologies.
