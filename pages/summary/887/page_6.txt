The paper investigates the effectiveness of a novel method called MST for style transfer, focusing on feature transformation and the preservation of discontinuity. It compares MST with existing methods, particularly emphasizing the role of distance metrics in feature matching. The study highlights that using Euclidean distance can lead to wash-out artifacts due to a lack of normalization, while cosine distance yields better results. 

The paper also addresses the importance of a smooth term in the energy function to avoid artifacts in facial areas during stylization. Empirical results suggest that a smoothness parameter (λ) of 0.1 balances smoothness and style diversity effectively. The comparison between MST and AdaIN reveals that MST achieves more natural results by employing a more optimized approach to feature transformation through whitening and coloring.

Qualitative comparisons with seven state-of-the-art methods demonstrate MST's superiority, as it avoids common issues such as distortion and halation, which are prevalent in other techniques. The user study conducted with 100 participants confirms MST's effectiveness, as it garnered 33.45% of the votes compared to other methods, including Gatys et al., which received 21.41%. Overall, the findings validate MST's ability to provide clearer, semantically matched stylizations while minimizing artifacts, showcasing its advancements in style transfer techniques.
