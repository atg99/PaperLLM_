This paper discusses advancements in skeleton-based action recognition and video summarization, focusing on the application of deep reinforcement learning techniques. In the realm of action recognition, a Deep Q-Network (DQN) model is employed to classify three actions: shifting left, staying the same, and shifting right. The model utilizes global video information and selected frames as states, with a reward system based on changes in categorical probabilities. A two-step network (FDNet) filters frames, while a Graph Convolutional Neural Network (GCNN) is used for labeling actions. This approach demonstrates superior performance on the NTU+RGBD dataset, SYSU-3D, and UT-Kinect datasets, surpassing other methods such as Dynamic Skeletons and Part-aware LSTM in cross-subject and cross-view metrics.

In video summarization, the same DQN framework is adapted to select and reject frames, employing a bidirectional LSTM to generate states based on input frame features. The reward mechanism focuses on diversity and representativeness. The integration of GoogLeNet enhances the summarization, achieving higher F-scores compared to traditional methods like uniform sampling and K-medoids. The results indicate significant improvements in both action recognition and video summarization tasks, showcasing the efficacy of the proposed models.
