The referenced papers cover a range of topics in computer vision and reinforcement learning, highlighting advancements in methodologies and applications. 

Toromano et al. focus on end-to-end model-free reinforcement learning for urban driving, utilizing implicit affordances to enhance navigation capabilities. Toshev and Szegedy introduce DeepPose, a framework for human pose estimation using deep neural networks, demonstrating significant improvements in accuracy. Tsai et al. present a video segmentation approach based on object flow, which enhances object tracking across frames.

Tsurumine et al. explore generative adversarial imitation learning for robotic cloth manipulation, while their later work discusses deep reinforcement learning with smooth policy updates, both contributing to advancements in robotic manipulation tasks. Uijlings et al. propose a selective search method for object recognition, enhancing detection efficiency. Uzkent et al. investigate efficient object detection in large images through deep reinforcement learning, showcasing practical applications of these techniques.

Valmadre et al. present an end-to-end representation learning approach for correlation filter-based tracking, which streamlines tracking processes. Van Hasselt and colleagues contribute to the field of reinforcement learning with their work on Double Q-learning, which improves learning stability. Finally, Van Hove discusses optimal coin and banknote denominations, advocating for the principle of least effort in economic design. 

These works collectively advance the fields of computer vision and reinforcement learning, providing foundational techniques and insights for future research and applications.
