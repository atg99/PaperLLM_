The paper discusses deep reinforcement learning (DRL) approaches, focusing on both planning and transition learning. It highlights the goal of training deep neural networks (DNNs) to approximate optimal policies and value functions. 

In the value function category, it introduces Deep Q-Networks (DQN), which utilize convolutional neural networks (CNNs) to handle high-dimensional state spaces, exemplified by successful applications in Atari games. The paper also covers Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS), which integrates a DNN-based transition model to predict future states and rewards based on previous frames and actions, aiding in planning optimal action sequences. Additionally, it mentions Value-Targeted Regression (UCRL-VTR), which constructs a set of consistent models to minimize regret and determines the value function through optimistic planning.

In the policy search section, it outlines methods that seek to directly find policies through either gradient-free or gradient-based approaches. Model-Ensemble Trust-Region Policy Optimization (ME-TRPO) builds on Trust Region Policy Optimization (TRPO) to stabilize learning with trust region constraints. Model-Based Meta-Policy-Optimization (MB-MPO) addresses the challenges of model-based DRL by learning an ensemble of dynamics models, enabling the policy to adapt rapidly without significant model bias.

Finally, the paper summarizes both model-based and model-free DRL algorithms, categorizing them into on-policy and off-policy techniques. On-policy methods utilize data from the current policy for updates, while off-policy methods leverage data from various policies to inform learning.
