The paper discusses the application of Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) in landmark detection and object detection, emphasizing the use of Euclidean distance to evaluate the accuracy of predicted landmark locations compared to ground truth. In landmark detection, the DRL agent adjusts a Region of Interest (ROI) around the landmark to enhance the alignment with the actual location, thereby maximizing the reward based on the distance improvement.

In the context of object detection, the paper reviews various methods for bounding box prediction. Early approaches involved extracting region proposals from images and classifying them using CNNs. Subsequent advancements included utilizing CNN features for proposing regions to accelerate detection and the introduction of Region Proposal Networks (RPN) for even faster results. Additional techniques like focal loss and Fast YOLO have been proposed to address challenges such as imbalanced data and real-time detection in video on embedded devices.

The framework of Markov Decision Processes (MDP) is highlighted in the work of [43], where DRL is employed for active object localization. The authors defined eight actions to refine bounding box adjustments and used a combination of feature vectors and action history to determine state, with the change in Intersection over Union (IOU) serving as the reward metric.
