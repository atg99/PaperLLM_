The paper discusses the differences between model-based and model-free reinforcement learning (RL), highlighting key factors such as iteration frequency, convergence speed, prior knowledge, and flexibility. Model-based RL requires fewer iterations and converges faster, utilizing prior knowledge of transitions, while model-free RL involves more iterations, slower convergence, and relies on trial and error without prior transition knowledge.

The paper also addresses the challenges in training evaluation functions in self-play games like chess, contrasting preference learning with least-squares temporal difference learning. It notes that value-based methods depend on optimal policies, complicating transfer learning.

Actor-critic methods, which combine policy gradient and value-based approaches, are presented as a significant advancement in RL. The paper details how these methods can be categorized based on reward settings into discounted and average return types.

In introducing deep reinforcement learning (DRL), the paper emphasizes its integration of reinforcement learning and deep learning, which enhances the representation of complex states and actions through neural networks. DRL is similarly divided into model-based and model-free algorithms, with the latter further categorized into value-based and policy gradient methods.
