A deep progressive reinforcement learning (DPRL) method was introduced to enhance action recognition by distilling informative frames while discarding ambiguous ones. The approach utilized a graph-based structure to represent the human body through joints and vertices. This consisted of two sub-networks: a frame distillation network (FDNet) for filtering frames and a graph-based convolutional neural network (GCNN) for action recognition. The problem was modeled as a Markov Decision Process (MDP), where the state combined global video information and filtered frames, with actions categorized into three types. The reward function was defined based on the probability of correct categorization.

The paper also discussed video summarization, highlighting a deep reinforcement learning (DRL) method for unsupervised video summarization. This involved a Diversity-Representativeness reward system and a deep summarization network (DSN) that predicted the likelihood of frame selection. An encode-decoder framework was employed, using GoogLeNet as the encoder and bidirectional RNNs as the decoder. The MDP framework was used to model the selection process, incorporating a novel reward function that balanced diversity and representativeness of the selected frames.

An improvement to the summarization network was proposed, utilizing Deep Q-learning (DQSN) and a trained classification network to provide rewards. This implementation involved bidirectional Gated Recurrent Units (Bi-GRU) for both classification and summarization, with the classification network being pre-trained and then applied to the generated summaries. The MDP framework continued to be utilized, with states representing video frame sequences and actions focused on retaining or discarding frames.
