The authors present various deep reinforcement learning (DRL) architectures, including V-DQN, M-DQN, and MS-DQN, to tackle crowd counting using ultrasound images. They highlight the complexity of crowd counting in computer vision, which they addressed through a DRL method that employs sequential decision-making. The proposed DQN agent, LibraNet, models crowd counting as a weighing scale, where the agent adds weights to one side to balance the crowded image on the other. The problem is formulated as a Markov Decision Process (MDP) with state variables comprising a weight vector and image feature vector. The action space mirrors a monetary system, and the agent receives two types of rewards: ending and intermediate rewards, calculated based on the accuracy of the crowd count.

Additionally, the authors discuss exposure bracketing in digital photography, where a DRL method, EBSNet, was developed for automated selection of optimal exposure bracketing to create high dynamic range (HDR) images. Without ground truth for bracketing, the EBSNet agent was trained using a novel dataset and received rewards based on the difference in peak signal-to-noise ratio between generated and reference HDR images. EBSNet features a semantic branch for feature extraction and an illumination branch to analyze lighting conditions, while the multi-exposure fusion network (MEFNet) minimizes Charbonnier loss to generate HDR images.

Lastly, they explore the challenges of autonomous driving in urban settings, proposing a novel end-to-end model-free RL method utilizing the CARLA Simulator. This approach introduces Implicit Affordances to navigate the complex environment, with rewards derived from the CARLA waypoint API. Overall, the paper emphasizes the innovative application of DRL across diverse domains, demonstrating its potential to address complex decision-making tasks.
