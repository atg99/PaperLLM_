The paper discusses the application of deep reinforcement learning (DRL) techniques in various domains, particularly focusing on two key areas: environment interaction through a modified Rainbow-IQN algorithm and the mitigation of racial bias in facial recognition systems.

In the first phase of training, a Resnet18 encoder predicts environmental variables such as traffic lights and pedestrian positions. The output features serve as states for the DRL agent, which operates in the CARLA simulator. To adapt to the simulator's continuous action space, steering and throttle values are discretized into multiple actions, resulting in a total of either 36 or 108 discrete actions.

The paper also addresses racial discrimination in facial recognition, highlighting a method that utilizes skewness-aware reinforcement learning. This approach recognizes that racial bias can stem from either data or algorithms, leading to the development of two ethnicity-aware datasets and a race-balanced network (RL-RBN). The authors formulated a Markov Decision Process (MDP) for adaptive margin policy learning, with states representing race groups and bias levels. A deep Q-network (DQN) is employed to adjust the adaptive margin based on rewards related to bias reduction.

Furthermore, the paper discusses the integration of attention mechanisms in convolutional neural networks (CNNs) to enhance image recognition. A proposed method, Deep REinforced Attention Learning (DREAL), utilizes deep Q-learning to optimize the attention module, addressing issues with sub-optimal weights from traditional training methods.

The paper concludes by summarizing various works and their comparisons, emphasizing the efficacy of DRL in computer vision applications such as landmark detection and video analysis. Future perspectives highlight the ongoing challenges and potential of DRL in these fields.
