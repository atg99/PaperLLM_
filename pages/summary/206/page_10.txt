The paper discusses the concepts of value functions and Q-functions in reinforcement learning (RL). The value function, denoted as V, aims to determine the maximum expected return from a given state, while the Q-function evaluates the expected return of taking a specific action in that state under a certain policy. The Q-function is crucial because it provides insights into the effectiveness of actions rather than just states.

Reinforcement learning is categorized into model-free and model-based methods. Model-based RL utilizes learned models, specifically the transition probability function and the reward function, to predict future actions. Within model-based RL, four main techniques are outlined:

1. **Value Function Methods**: These focus on maximizing value functions to derive the best policy, utilizing Bellman equations for optimal state-value functions. Techniques include Differential Dynamic Programming, Temporal Difference Learning, Policy Iteration, and Monte Carlo methods.

2. **Transition Models**: These models determine how an action in a current state leads to a new state, significantly influencing the performance of model-based RL algorithms. Transition models can be categorized into stochastic and deterministic approaches. Deterministic methods include decision trees and linear regression, while stochastic methods encompass Gaussian processes, Expectation-Maximization, and dynamic Bayesian networks.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of both value and Q-functions in developing effective RL strategies and highlights the distinctions between model-based and model-free approaches.
