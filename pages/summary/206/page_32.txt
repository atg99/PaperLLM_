The paper discusses the application of Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) in object detection and tracking. In object detection, the aim is to enhance the Intersection over Union (IOU) through iterative actions that adjust the bounding box of the detected object. The initial bounding box is predicted by an existing algorithm or refined using DRL iterations to maximize IOU improvement.

In the context of object tracking, the paper distinguishes between Single Object Tracking (SOT) and Multiple Object Tracking (MOT), highlighting their significance in various fields such as autonomous driving and security. SOT is further divided into active and passive tracking. Passive tracking assumes the object remains in the camera frame, while active tracking involves decisions about camera movement to keep the object in view. The paper references various studies addressing challenges like lighting changes and occlusion in both types of tracking.

Active tracking presents additional complexities due to the necessity of camera movement, making it more challenging than passive tracking. The paper cites efforts in active tracking that often separate detection and tracking tasks, which can lead to failures in noisy backgrounds. A notable approach mentioned is an end-to-end active object tracker utilizing DRL, combining Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) with Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks, and employing the actor-critic algorithm to optimize action selection and value estimation.
