The paper discusses various applications of reinforcement learning (RL) in diverse fields, highlighting distinct methodologies and their outcomes. 

In video summarization, different research works propose unique reward functions to include or discard frames for summarization networks, with some focusing on diversity and representativeness, while others combine multiple reward functions.

For object manipulation, a method utilizing a modified version of Deep Deterministic Policy Gradients (DDPG) is presented for deformable objects. This approach, tested in the Pybullet simulator, achieved a success rate of up to 90% for tasks like folding and hanging cloth, using a sparse reward system.

Visual perception-based control is explored through a virtual-to-real method employing semantic segmentation. The authors integrated modules for perception and control, leveraging pre-trained models like DeepLab and ICNet, and implemented the control policy using an actor-critic framework. Their approach resulted in enhanced success rates and reduced collision rates compared to other methods.

The paper also addresses automatic tracing of structures in biomedical imaging, proposing a deep RL method for sub-pixel neural tracking. This method utilizes 2D grayscale images and employs a reward system based on the intensity of the agent's movement, utilizing an actor-critic framework for value and policy estimation.

Finally, an RL method for diagnosing acute appendicitis in abdominal CT images is presented. This approach involves locating the appendix and employing a CNN classifier to assess the likelihood of appendicitis, ultimately defining a region of low-entropy for optimal diagnostic scores. 

Overall, the paper illustrates the versatility of RL applications across various domains, emphasizing the effectiveness of tailored reward functions and frameworks.
