The paper discusses advancements in multi-object tracking in video using deep reinforcement learning (DRL) techniques. It highlights two significant contributions from 2018 and 2019 that utilize different versions of the YOLO object detection framework and LSTM networks.

In the 2018 study, a Deep Q-Network (DQN) approach was implemented with six actions: moving right, left, up, down, scaling up, and scaling down. The state was defined as an image region represented by a bounding box, with rewards based on the Intersection over Union (IOU) exceeding a specified threshold. The use of YOLO-V2 for detection alongside VGG-16 architecture yielded competitive results in multiple object tracking accuracy (MOTA) and precision (MOTP) when compared to existing methods like RNN-LSTM, LP-SSVM, MDPSub-CNN, and SiameseCNN, particularly on the MOT15 dataset.

The 2019 study expanded on this by introducing a multi-agent DRL tracker (MADRL) framework, which used DQN with nine actions including movement in multiple directions, scaling, and stopping. The authors employed YOLO-V3 for detection, filtering multiple detections with IOU to select the results that served as input agents. These agents were processed through a pre-trained VGG-16 followed by an LSTM unit to enable information sharing among agents. While this approach exhibited higher running times, it maintained comparable or superior MOTA and MOTP metrics against similar methodologies on the MOT15 challenge benchmark.

Overall, the studies address challenges in existing tracking methods, such as handling varying numbers of targets and achieving real-time performance, demonstrating the effectiveness of DRL in enhancing multi-object tracking.
