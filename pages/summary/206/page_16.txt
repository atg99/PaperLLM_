The paper discusses the network structure of Dueling DQN, which combines the value function V(s) and the advantage function A(s;a) to predict action-value Q(s;a) for a given state. It then transitions to policy gradient deep reinforcement learning (DRL) methods, highlighting the Policy Gradient Theorem. Unlike value-based methods, policy gradient methods optimize the policy directly through an objective function G(θ), defined as a function of the policy parameters θ. The gradient of this objective is expressed using the policy gradient theorem, allowing for direct computation of the gradient.

The paper introduces the REINFORCE algorithm, which approximates the gradient using Monte-Carlo estimation. This reformulation allows for the calculation of the gradient by summing over trajectories and rewards, but the algorithm is criticized for being impractical due to its slow convergence and susceptibility to local optima. The discussion emphasizes the challenges faced in applying policy gradient methods effectively in practice.
