The paper discusses advancements in visual tracking and object tracking methodologies using reinforcement learning and deep learning techniques. Key contributions include:

1. **Actor-Critic Model (A3C)**: Introduced in 2019, this model employs four actions (4x, 4y, 4w, 4h) to manipulate an image region defined by a bounding box. The reward mechanism is based on the change in Intersection over Union (IOU). The model utilizes Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks and ResNet-18 architecture, achieving comparable success and precision scores against several benchmarks including LADCF, SiamRPN, and ECO across datasets like GOT-10k, LaSOT, UAV123, OTB-100, and VOT-2018/2019.

2. **TLD Tracker (2015)**: This model focuses on decision-making in object tracking, employing seven actions related to moving objects through states such as Active, Tracked, Lost, and Inactive. It addresses an inverse reinforcement learning problem, demonstrating competitive multiple object tracking accuracy (MOTA) and precision (MOTP) compared to various trackers like DPNMS, TCODAL, and SegTrack using the MOT15 dataset.

3. **Collaborative Multi-Object Tracker (2018)**: This approach utilizes a Deep Q-Network (DQN) with four actions similar to the A3C model. The system is structured with two networks: a prediction network and a decision network, employing a three-layer CNN and fully connected (FC) layer. It also measures the reward based on IOU exceeding a specified threshold. Performance metrics (MOTA and MOTP) indicate competitiveness with other models such as SCEA and MDP across MOT15 and MOT16 datasets.

The paper highlights the effectiveness of these models in enhancing tracking accuracy and precision while addressing the challenges posed by dynamic environments and multiple object scenarios.
