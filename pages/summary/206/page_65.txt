Imitation learning is a technique focused on training a policy to replicate an expert's behavior using samples from that expert. It serves as an alternative to reinforcement learning (RL) and deep reinforcement learning (DRL) for sequential decision-making tasks. Two notable approaches within imitation learning are behavior cloning, which employs supervised learning, and inverse deep reinforcement learning. Recent advancements include unsupervised third-person imitation learning, as proposed by Bradly et al., and behavior cloning loss enhancements by Nair et al. that leverage Deep Deterministic Policy Gradients and Hindsight Experience Replay. Additionally, Generative Adversarial Imitation Learning (P-GAIL) integrates imitation learning into the policy gradient framework, focusing on smoothness and causal entropy.

The paper presents a comprehensive survey of DRL applications in computer vision, emphasizing its growing popularity for enabling artificial agents to learn optimal strategies through experience. It begins with a structured overview of the theoretical foundations of deep learning, covering AutoEncoders, Multi-Layer Perceptrons, Convolutional Neural Networks, and Recurrent Neural Networks. It then discusses key RL techniques, differentiating between model-based and model-free methods. The survey categorizes main DRL techniques and explores their applications in various computer vision problems, such as landmark detection, object detection, and video analysis. The paper concludes by addressing the challenges that must be overcome to fully harness the potential of DRL in computer vision, highlighting recent advanced techniques in the field.
