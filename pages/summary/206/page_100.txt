The cited works cover a variety of topics within machine learning, computer vision, and reinforcement learning. Key concepts include the use of trajectory data to enhance Bayesian optimization in reinforcement learning, as discussed by Wilson et al., and the exploration of game annotations for learning purposes by Wirth and Fürnkranz. Wohlhart and Lepetit focus on learning descriptors for object recognition and pose estimation, while Woo et al. introduce the Convolutional Block Attention Module (CBAM) to improve neural network performance.

The field of object tracking is explored through benchmarks established by Wu et al., providing foundational metrics for evaluating tracking algorithms. Xia et al. propose a method for view-invariant human action recognition, leveraging histograms of 3D joint data. The effects of batch and weight normalization in generative adversarial networks are examined by Xiang and Li, highlighting their impact on model performance.

Xiang et al. discuss online multi-object tracking through decision-making frameworks, while Xiao and Lee present an unsupervised approach for video object proposals. Additionally, Xiao and Peng introduce a method for automatic tracing of 3D neuron morphology, showcasing applications in bioinformatics. Lastly, Xie et al. demonstrate that self-training with noisy student techniques can enhance image classification performance on large datasets like ImageNet. These works collectively contribute to advancements in machine learning methodologies and applications.
