The paper presents a comparison of various methods associated with video processing, categorized into three groups: video object segmentation, action recognition, and video summarization. 

In the first group, the approach for video object segmentation introduced in 2016 utilizes a Deep Belief Network, focusing on how actions change local values in sub-images. However, details regarding the backbone and performance metrics are not specified.

The second group discusses surgical gesture segmentation and classification from 2018, employing Trust Region Policy Optimization (TRPO) as a training technique. This method distinguishes between two types of actions: optimal step size and gesture class, with states derived from Temporal Convolutional Networks (TCN) and future frames. The reward system is designed to promote larger steps and minimize action errors, using a statistical language model for gesture probability. This approach demonstrates comparable accuracy and superior edit and F1 scores relative to several existing methods, including SD-SDL, Bidir LSTM, and Seg-ST-CNN, among others. The JIGSAWS benchmark dataset is utilized, and the code is available for further research.

Overall, the paper effectively highlights advancements in video processing methods, detailing their training techniques, performance, and dataset usage.
