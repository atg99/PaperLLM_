The paper discusses two primary approaches in reinforcement learning: policy search methods and model-free methods. 

1. **Policy Search**: This approach focuses on directly finding the optimal policy by adjusting its parameters, contrasting with value function methods that seek actions maximizing the value function at each state. Notable techniques include gradient-based, information theory, and sampling-based methods. 

2. **Return Functions**: These functions aggregate rewards or penalties throughout an episode, impacting both convergence and model feasibility. The two main types are discounted return functions, commonly used for reflecting future reward uncertainty, and averaged return functions. While discounted returns can lead to faster convergence with smaller discount factors, they may not yield optimal solutions. The challenges of modeling transition and reward functions are acknowledged, with comparative performance of model-based techniques evaluated across various benchmarking environments.

3. **Model-free Methods**: These methods rely on learning from interactions with the environment to estimate transition probabilities and reward functions for policy acquisition. They typically employ policy gradient and value-based algorithms. 

   - **Policy Gradient Methods**: This approach treats the RL task as a stochastic optimization problem, optimizing the expected reward directly without additional MDP information. It involves using approximate gradient estimations of policy parameters, with examples including Gibbs and Gaussian policies for discrete and continuous problems, respectively.

   - **Value-based Methods**: Here, the optimal policy is derived from approximating the optimal Q-function. Agents update the value function to learn an appropriate policy, with Q-learning as a prominent method characterized by its update rule based on learning rate and accumulated returns.

This summary encapsulates the key concepts of policy search, return functions, and model-free methods, highlighting their respective methodologies and challenges in reinforcement learning.
