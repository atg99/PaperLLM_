The paper discusses the implementation of Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) for video summarization, specifically highlighting two distinct approaches. The first approach utilizes a structure akin to Duel-DQN, where a DRL agent decides on the inclusion of consecutive video frames in a summary based on three types of rewards: a Global Recognizability reward, a Local Relative Importance Reward, and an Unsupervised Reward based on diversity-representativeness metrics. The networks are trained using features extracted from GoogLeNet.

The second approach focuses on ultrasound video summarization, employing an encoder-decoder architecture with a bidirectional LSTM for sequential modeling. This method also incorporates a DRL framework that uses latent scores from the Bi-LSTM to determine which frames to include in the summary. Three specific rewards are defined: Rdet for diagnostic likelihood, Rrep for representativeness, and Rdiv for diversity, which collectively evaluate the quality of the selected summary. Kernel temporal segmentation is applied for generalizing the video summary.

The paper includes a comparative summary of various video analysis works and illustrates a basic DRL implementation for video summarization, emphasizing the role of the agent in processing sequences of video frames.
