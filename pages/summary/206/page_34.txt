The paper discusses advancements in single object tracking (SOT) and multi-object tracking (MOT) using deep reinforcement learning (DRL) techniques. In SOT, the authors utilized relative translations and scale changes represented by 4x, 4y, and 4s, with a reward system based on Intersection over Union (IoU) thresholds. The proposed method involved offline training with a pre-trained VGG-M backbone and employed the Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (DDPG) approach.

An enhancement to existing SOT methods introduced by another study utilized DRL with an expert demonstrator, treating the task as a Markov Decision Process (MDP). Here, the state comprised two consecutive frames, and actions involved relative translations and scaling. The architecture featured a ResNet-18 backbone integrated with an LSTM unit for past information encoding, trained using the on-policy Asynchronous Actor-Critic (A3C) framework.

In the context of MOT, the paper highlights the challenges posed by environmental factors such as crowding and object overlap, categorizing techniques into offline and online methods. Offline methods use batch processing to create tracklets, while online methods rely on real-time frame processing. Traditional methods like Kalman and Particle Filtering are noted for their susceptibility to errors under these constraints.

To address these challenges, a new MDP-based approach for MOT was proposed, where object states include Active, Tracked, Lost, and Inactive, with a reward system derived from inverse reinforcement learning. The paper also critiques the tracking-by-detection paradigm, suggesting simultaneous detection and tracking using a collaborative Q-Network for improved performance. Another method combines LSTM and DRL, employing a YOLO V2 object detector for efficient multi-object tracking.
