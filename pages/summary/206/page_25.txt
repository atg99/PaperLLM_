The paper discusses various approaches to deep reinforcement learning (DRL) for landmark detection in medical imaging, particularly focusing on actor-critic and DQN methods. 

SLD (2019) employs an actor-critic framework with a binary reward function and a multi-layer perceptron (MLP) for both policy and value functions. This method demonstrates faster convergence and superior performance compared to conventional actor-critic and Q-learning techniques, specifically in detecting aortic valve and left atrial appendage (LAA) seed points in CT volumes.

MLD (2019) introduces a collaborative DQN approach, where each agent operates as a partially observable Markov decision process (POMDP) with its own reward based on relative improvements in Euclidean distance. This method effectively reduces computational load and outperforms supervised CNNs and standard DQNs in identifying brain MRI and cardiac MRI landmarks.

In a subsequent study (MLD, 2020), a DQN framework is proposed for multiple landmark detection, diverging from previous methods that focused on single landmarks. The state is defined as a 3D image patch, with a reward system based on changes in Euclidean distance. The action space remains consistent with earlier works, and the approach utilizes hard parameter sharing to enhance the detection process across multiple landmarks. 

Overall, the paper highlights the advancements in DRL techniques for landmark detection, emphasizing improvements in efficiency and robustness, particularly in the presence of missing data. A summary table compares different methodologies, and a figure illustrates a basic implementation of DRL in landmark detection, showcasing the role of the agent in shifting the region of interest (ROI) and the corresponding rewards.
