The paper discusses the training of Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) and introduces key concepts in Reinforcement Learning (RL). For RNNs, the gradient descent update rule is defined, where the weights are adjusted based on the error gradients using Stochastic Gradient Descent and the chain rule. The challenges of capturing long-term dependencies in RNNs have led to the development of Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks, which utilize a memory cell to manage information flow, and Gated Recurrent Units (GRUs), which adaptively capture dependencies without separate memory cells. These RNN variants have been successfully applied in various tasks, including natural language processing, speech recognition, and machine translation.

The paper then transitions to RL, explaining that it is a machine learning approach where agents learn optimal policies through trial and error, receiving feedback in the form of delayed scalar rewards. Unlike supervised learning, where data is provided, RL agents gather experiences by interacting with their environment to optimize cumulative rewards. The section highlights key aspects of RL, including the lack of a supervisor, the scalar nature of rewards, and the delayed feedback mechanism. The Markov Decision Process (MDP) is identified as a common framework for addressing RL problems, particularly those with discrete actions.
