The paper discusses advancements in video object segmentation using a cutting agent approach. It introduces a Deep Q-Network (DQN) framework with eight actions, including four translation actions (Up, Down, Left, Right) and four scaling actions (Horizontal shrink, Vertical shrink, Horizontal zoom, Vertical zoom), along with a terminal action (Stop). The states consist of the input frame, action history, and segmentation mask, with the reward based on the change in Intersection over Union (IOU). The proposed cutting-policy network is designed for box-context pairs, while the cutting-execution network focuses on mask generation, utilizing DenseNet architecture. This method shows improved performance in terms of mean region similarity, counter accuracy, and temporal stability compared to existing techniques like MSK, ARP, CTN, and VPN, evaluated on the DAVIS and YouTube Objects datasets.

Additionally, the paper addresses unsupervised video object segmentation through the MOREL framework, which employs an actor-critic algorithm. This method processes consecutive frames using optical flow with Spatial Transformer Networks and incorporates a reconstruction loss based on structural dissimilarity. The results indicate that this approach achieves a higher total episodic reward compared to traditional actor-critic methods, with experiments conducted on 59 Atari games. The code for the proposed methodologies is available for further research and implementation.
