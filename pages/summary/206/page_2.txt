Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) has found successful applications across various domains, including games, robotics, autonomous driving, healthcare, natural language processing, and computer vision. Unlike supervised learning, which relies on extensive labeled datasets, DRL derives training data through interactions with an environment, making it suitable for scenarios where labeled data is scarce.

DRL focuses on sequential decision-making, where the objective is to determine a series of actions based on experiences collected from the environment to achieve specific goals. In contrast to supervised learning, where feedback is immediate and can be quantitatively assessed, DRL often receives delayed scalar feedback, reflecting the success or failure of a sequence of actions. Furthermore, while supervised learning models are updated based on output errors, DRL employs policy gradients to guide learning without a differentiable loss function, encouraging exploration and refinement of actions.

Recent surveys in the DRL field have covered various aspects, including essential algorithms, models, and applications, particularly in healthcare. However, this paper emphasizes the implementation of DRL in computer vision tasks, such as landmark detection, object detection, object tracking, image registration, image segmentation, and video analysis.

The paper is structured to first introduce the fundamentals of Deep Learning, including Multi-Layer Perceptron, Autoencoder, Deep Belief Network, Convolutional Neural Networks, and Recurrent Neural Networks. It then explores Reinforcement Learning theories, starting with the Markov Decision Process and delving into value functions and Q-functions. Various RL techniques are categorized into model-based and model-free approaches. The discussion on DRL covers value-based methods, policy gradient methods, and actor-critic methods.

Subsequent sections detail the application of DRL in specific computer vision tasks, beginning with a problem overview and followed by a comparison of state-of-the-art approaches summarized in tables. This comprehensive survey aims to provide readers with a robust understanding of RL/DRL principles and their practical applications in computer vision.
