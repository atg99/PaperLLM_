The paper discusses advancements in image registration techniques utilizing deep reinforcement learning (DRL). It highlights various approaches and their effectiveness in different medical imaging scenarios. 

1. **Robust Image Registration (2017)**: This method employs a Deep Q-Network (DQN) with 12 actions corresponding to various transformations. The current transformation is considered as the state, and the reward is based on the distance error. The architecture includes 5 Conv3D layers and 3 fully connected (FC) layers, achieving a higher success rate than traditional methods like ITK.

2. **Multimodal Image Registration (2017)**: The authors introduced Duel-DQN, which updates transformations on a floating image. This approach utilizes a cropped 3D image as the state and employs batch normalization, 5 Conv3D layers, and 3 Maxpool layers. It demonstrated lower Euclidean distance error compared to methods such as Hausdorff and Iterative Closest Point (ICP).

3. **Robust Non-Rigid Agent-Based Registration (2017)**: This technique utilizes DQN with actions for n-dimensional vectors, taking fixed and moving images as states. The reward is based on changes in transformation error. The architecture incorporates a statistical deformation model with multi-layer CNNs. It outperformed methods like LCC-Demons and Elastix in terms of Mean Dice score and Hausdorff distance.

4. **Robust Multimodal Registration (2018)**: The Actor-Critic (A3C) method was employed, with 8 actions for different transformations. The state includes fixed and moving images, with rewards based on distance error. This method, using Monte Carlo techniques with LSTM and multi-layer CNNs, achieved comparable or lower target registration error than techniques like SIFT and Elastix.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the effectiveness of DRL-based methods in improving the accuracy and robustness of image registration across various medical imaging modalities.
