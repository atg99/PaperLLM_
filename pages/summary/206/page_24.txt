The paper discusses a partial policy approach aimed at developing multiple simple policies that can effectively project actions in the original action space for landmark detection in human anatomy. It is based on the premise that anatomical landmarks are interdependent, which allows one landmark's position to inform the locations of others. This interdependence enables agents to share knowledge during training, enhancing collective learning.

The state for each agent is defined as a region of interest (RoI) around its location, and the reward function is based on the improvement in Euclidean distance from the agent's current position to the target landmark. Each agent operates as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP), calculating individual rewards with disjoint policies to manage the complexity of locating multiple landmarks.

The proposed collaborative multi-agent framework, named Collab-DQN, utilizes a deep Q-network (DQN) architecture built on a convolutional neural network (CNN). While the CNN backbone is shared among all agents, the policy-making layers are distinct for each agent, allowing for both shared learning and specialized policy development.

The paper also reviews various deep reinforcement learning (DRL) methods for landmark detection, categorizing them into Single Landmark Detection (SLD) and Multiple Landmark Detection (MLD) approaches. It highlights performance metrics, training techniques, and the effectiveness of different models, noting that techniques such as Duel DQN and Duel DDQN show significant improvements in accuracy for specific landmark detection tasks compared to traditional methods.
