The paper discusses the architecture and functionality of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN), highlighting their significance in image classification tasks. CNNs are a specialized form of fully connected multilayer perceptrons (MLP) that utilize weight sharing to process data more efficiently. This architecture, inspired by the biological visual system, incorporates two key features: spatially shared weights and spatial pooling. These characteristics allow CNNs to learn shift-invariant features, making them particularly effective for image analysis due to the stationary nature of image statistics.

The typical structure of a CNN includes multiple stages, each producing a set of 2D arrays known as feature maps. These feature maps result from convolving the input image with filters, followed by the application of a point-wise non-linear activation function. Pooling layers further enhance the network's ability to manage slight input shifts and distortions while expanding the receptive field for subsequent layers.

Since 2012, CNNs have significantly advanced the field of Deep Learning, particularly in object recognition, as evidenced by their performance in the ImageNet classification challenge. The mathematical representation of a CNN is provided, emphasizing the transformation of input data through convolution and pooling operations across multiple layers.
