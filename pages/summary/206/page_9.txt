This paper discusses the concepts of trajectories and episodes within the framework of Reinforcement Learning (RL) as applied to Markov Decision Processes (MDPs). A trajectory, defined as a sequence of states, actions, and rewards, is theoretically infinite but is often treated as finite in practical applications, referred to as an episode. The trajectory distribution, denoted as Tπ, represents the probability of observing a specific trajectory given a policy π and is mathematically expressed using the product of action probabilities and state transition probabilities.

The primary objective in RL is to derive an optimal policy π* that maximizes the cumulative reward, known as the return. The return for each episode is calculated as a weighted sum of immediate rewards, and the expected reward maximization can be framed as an optimization problem. The paper defines the discounted expected reward G(π) as the expectation of the cumulative return, which the agent strives to maximize.

Additionally, the paper introduces value functions, which assess the efficacy of a policy π in terms of expected returns from a given state s. The value function, or state-value function Vπ(st), is calculated using the Bellman expectation equation, which incorporates the expected future rewards based on the selected actions and state transitions. The Bellman equation serves as a foundational principle in evaluating and optimizing policies in RL, particularly when aiming to determine the optimal policy π* that maximizes the value function.
