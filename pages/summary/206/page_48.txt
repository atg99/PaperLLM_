The paper discusses various deep reinforcement learning (DRL) methods for video object segmentation, focusing on unsupervised techniques. It highlights an approach named MOREL, which employs a two-step process: initially learning a representation of moving objects through unsupervised segmentation and then transferring these weights to a reinforcement learning framework. This method utilizes a modified actor-critic algorithm to predict segmentation masks and object translations from consecutive frames. The authors also incorporate optical flow estimation and structural dissimilarity for loss calculation.

Additionally, the paper presents a DRL method for dynamic semantic face video segmentation that utilizes Deep Feature Flow for feature propagation. This approach categorizes frames into key and non-key frames, employing a policy network to determine actions based on deviation and expert information. The network structure includes a convolution layer and fully connected layers, with rewards defined based on the intersection over union (IOU) for key and non-key actions, and an alternative reward based on segmentation accuracy when ground truth data is unavailable. Overall, the paper emphasizes the integration of reinforcement learning with video segmentation tasks to enhance efficiency and effectiveness.
