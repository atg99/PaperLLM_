Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) has emerged as an effective solution for complex optimization problems, including parameter tuning, selection of augmentation strategies, and neural architecture search. However, most reviewed approaches operate under the assumption of a stationary environment. In applications like landmark detection, the environment is based on the image with each state defined as an image patch containing the landmark location. This contrasts with the dynamic nature of real-world environments where both state and action can lead to evolving conditions. 

To fully leverage DRL in computer vision, several challenges must be addressed:

1. **Reward Function**: Defining a precise reward function in real-world applications is difficult due to the need for knowledge from various domains, which may not always be accessible. Intermediate rewards can be hard to compute, and delays in reward feedback complicate training. Manual design of reward systems is often necessary.

2. **Continuous State and Action Space**: Most RL algorithms, such as Q-learning, are suited for discrete states and actions, making training in continuous spaces challenging. Existing solutions often involve discretizing these spaces.

3. **High-dimensional State and Action Space**: Training Q-functions in high-dimensional spaces is complex. Many approaches resort to low-dimensional parameterization, typically limiting dimensions to less than ten, with few exceptions that utilize higher dimensions for specific applications.

4. **Complicated Environment**: Real-world systems are often partially observable and non-stationary, which contradicts the stationary assumptions of most current approaches. These systems are also characterized by stochastic and noisy conditions, such as action delays and sensor noise.

5. **Training Data Requirement**: DRL necessitates a substantial amount of training data or expert demonstrations, which can be challenging to obtain due to the high cost and difficulty of acquiring large annotated datasets.

The paper discusses these challenges in detail, highlighting their implications for deploying RL/DRL in real-world scenarios. Additionally, the authors present experimental designs and findings regarding common RL agents and introduce an open-source environmental suite, realworldrl-suite, to facilitate further research in this domain.
