The paper discusses advancements in dual-agent deformable face tracking, highlighting the use of deep reinforcement learning techniques such as DQN and actor-critic methods. The DQN model implements eight actions involving directional movements and scaling, utilizing a bounding box for state representation and measuring performance through distance error rewards. It demonstrates improved facial landmark detection and tracking accuracy, achieving lower point-to-point errors compared to methods like ICCR and MDM, validated on the 300-VW test set.

The actor-critic framework, explored in two variations, incorporates four actions related to tracking management. The first variant focuses on continuous updates and ignores certain frames, while the second emphasizes offline training with online tracking. Both models utilize bounding boxes as state representations and reward mechanisms based on Intersection over Union (IoU). The results indicate a higher area under the curve for success rates and precision compared to multiple existing tracking methodologies, with evaluations conducted on standard datasets such as OTB-2015 and VOT-2016.

Overall, the paper illustrates significant improvements in facial tracking performance through innovative use of deep learning frameworks, showcasing the effectiveness of reinforcement learning in complex visual tasks.
