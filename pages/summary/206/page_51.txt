The paper discusses advancements in video segmentation techniques, focusing on two key methods from 2020: Face Video Segmentation and Multi-agent Video Object Segmentation. 

In Face Video Segmentation, a multi-layer CNN is employed to categorize frames as key or non-key, utilizing state information such as deviation from previous decisions and expert decision history. The reward mechanism aims to enhance the mean Intersection over Union (IOU) and accuracy scores between segmentations of key and non-key frames. This method demonstrates superior performance compared to other techniques like DVSNet and DFF, validated using the 300VW and Cityscape datasets.

Multi-agent Video Object Segmentation leverages Deep Q-Network (DQN) with two action types: movement actions and set actions to place a location on a patch. The states include the input frame, optical flow from the previous frame, and action history, while the reward system is based on gamification through generated clicks. This approach incorporates down-sampling and up-sampling techniques akin to U-Net and DenseNet, achieving higher mean region similarity and contour accuracy compared to semi-supervised and weakly-supervised methods, as evidenced by results from the DAVIS-17 dataset.

Both methods illustrate significant improvements in video segmentation accuracy, contributing to the field's ongoing evolution.
