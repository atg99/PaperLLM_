The paper discusses a framework for object tracking that integrates a YOLO V2 object detector with a reinforcement learning agent comprising a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) and a Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) network. The YOLO V2 detects objects within a frame, while the agent processes the detected objects by encoding the current image patch and the history of the last ten actions. The actions include movements for the bounding box—right, left, up, down, scale-up, scale-down—and a stop action for terminal states. Rewards are assigned based on the Intersection over Union (IOU) metric, with a reward of 1 for IOU exceeding a set threshold, and 0 otherwise. The authors utilized VGG-16 as the CNN backbone and conducted experiments using the MOT benchmark for multi-object tracking.

The paper further categorizes various deep reinforcement learning (DRL) approaches for object tracking into two groups: single object tracking (SOT) and multi-object tracking (MOT). It compares different methods based on training techniques, actions, performance, and datasets used. Notable findings include the effectiveness of LSTM-based methods in achieving higher accumulated rewards and improved performance metrics compared to traditional tracking methods. The paper highlights the advancements in tracking accuracy and speed through the integration of DRL techniques, demonstrating their potential in enhancing object tracking systems.
