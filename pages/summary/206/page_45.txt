The paper discusses various deep learning approaches for image segmentation and anomaly detection, highlighting specific methods and their performance metrics across different datasets. 

1. **SeedNet (2018)**: Utilizes DQN variations (Double-DQN, Duel-DQN) for segmentation tasks with an action space of 800 actions. It employs image features and segmentation masks as states, with rewards based on changes in Intersection over Union (IOU). This method outperforms traditional models like FCN and iFCN on the MSRA10K saliency dataset.

2. **Iteratively Refined Multi-Agent Segmentation (2020)**: Implements an Actor-Critic framework (A3C) focusing on 3D image segmentation. It uses a single action per voxel to adjust segmentation probabilities, with states including segmentation probability and hint maps. The approach shows better performance than methods such as MinCut and DeepIGeoS on the BraTS 2015, MM-WHS, and NCI-ISBI 2013 datasets.

3. **Multi-Step Medical Image Segmentation (2020)**: Also based on the A3C framework, this model controls brush stroke positions and shapes to modify segmentations. It uses states comprising images, segmentation masks, and time steps, with rewards based on distance error changes. It achieves higher Mean Dice scores and lower Hausdorff distances compared to techniques like Grab-Cut and U-Net on datasets such as PROMISE12 and the REFUGE challenge.

4. **Anomaly Detection in Images (2020)**: Employs the REINFORCE algorithm with a focus on shifting the center of image patches. The method outperforms U-Net and unsupervised models in metrics like precision, recall, and F1 score on datasets including MVTec AD and NanoTWICE, although it only excels in recall against one baseline.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the effectiveness of reinforcement learning techniques in improving image segmentation and anomaly detection outcomes, showcasing their superiority over traditional methods across various benchmark datasets.
