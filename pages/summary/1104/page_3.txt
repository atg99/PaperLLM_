Dynamic Bayesian Networks (DBNs) integrate techniques from both static and dynamic networks, as demonstrated by Vlasselaer et al. in 2016. Uusitalo et al. (2018) employed DBNs with hidden variables to examine significant structural changes in the Baltic Sea food web, while Orphanou et al. (2014) utilized temporal Bayesian networks (TBNs) for analyzing temporal relationships in clinical data, aiding in medical diagnosis and prognosis. The hidden variables in these models captured unobserved processes affecting changes, aligning with known dynamics of the food web system.

Bayesian Decision Networks (BDNs) enhance Bayesian Networks (BNs) by incorporating decision and utility nodes, which facilitate the modeling of management decisions and the estimation of associated costs or benefits (Barton et al., 2008). BDNs are instrumental in risk analysis and management, exemplified by Loyd and DeVore (2010), who created a BDN for feral cat management in the U.S., and Catenacci and Guipponi (2013), who used a BDN for adaptation strategies to sea-level rise. Dynamic Decision Networks (DDNs) further evolve BDNs by integrating time-expanded dynamic networks, as developed by Murray et al. (2004) and Penman et al. (2015a), for applications such as guiding educational choices and wildfire risk reduction.

Additionally, the comparison between Bayesian Networks and Structural Equation Modeling (SEM) reveals that both can illustrate causal networks. SEM employs frequentist statistical tools primarily for hypothesis testing, while BNs leverage conditional probabilities and Bayes' theorem to explore causal relationships and the implications of specific conditions on outcomes. Recent work by Li et al. (2018) has explored the combination of BN and SEM methodologies to assess the combined effects of land use and climate on various outcomes. 

The paper also highlights an example of a DBN illustrating a dampened oscillating predator-prey system involving Canadian lynx and snowshoe hares, showcasing the model's ability to represent feedback loops and population interactions over time.
