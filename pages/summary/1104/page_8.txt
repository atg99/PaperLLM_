The collection of references presents a diverse range of studies focusing on Bayesian networks and their applications across various fields, including environmental management, education, and causal reasoning. Moreira and Wichert (2018) question the efficacy of quantum-like Bayesian networks compared to classical models. Murray et al. (2004) explore decision-theoretic approaches for selecting tutorial actions in educational contexts. Neil et al. (2007) discuss dynamic discretization methods for inference in hybrid Bayesian networks.

Further contributions include Nicholson and Flores (2011), who combine state and transition models with dynamic Bayesian networks for ecological modeling. Nielsen and Parsons (2007) apply formal argumentation to fuse Bayesian networks in multi-agent systems. O'Donoghue et al. (1997) investigate predator responses to prey population cycles, while Orphanou et al. (2014) survey the use of temporal Bayesian networks in clinical settings.

Phan et al. (2016) provide a systematic review of Bayesian belief networks in water resource management. Other notable studies include the validation of expert-elicited Bayesian networks (Pitchforth and Mengersen, 2013) and the assessment of habitat trends for terrestrial vertebrates (Raphael et al., 2001). Various papers address the integration of Bayesian networks with climate change assessments, risk management, and ecological service modeling.

The references highlight the versatility of Bayesian networks in addressing uncertainty and decision-making across multiple domains, emphasizing their growing importance in both theoretical and applied research contexts.
