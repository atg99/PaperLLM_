This paper presents a comparison of various models for handling missing data in the Healing MNIST dataset, SPRITES dataset, and a real medical time series dataset from the 2012 Physionet Challenge. The performance is evaluated using mean squared error (MSE) and classification performance metrics such as area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC).

In the Healing MNIST experiments, the proposed GP-VAE model consistently outperformed baseline models across different missingness mechanisms, achieving the lowest MSE values. The results indicate that GP-VAE not only provides stable reconstructions over time but also yields the most useful imputations for downstream classification tasks. The correlation between downstream classification performance and test likelihood suggests that the model is a reliable measure of imputation quality.

The SPRITES dataset, consisting of animated character sequences, was also evaluated, with the GP-VAE model again demonstrating superior reconstruction quality compared to other methods. 

For the real medical time series data, which is characterized by a significant amount of missingness, the paper employs AUROC as a proxy for imputation quality due to the lack of ground truth. The GP-VAE model was compared against several state-of-the-art approaches, including GRUI-GAN and BRITS, and showed promising results in mortality prediction tasks, highlighting its effectiveness in complex and sparse datasets.

Overall, the findings support the GP-VAE model's robustness and efficacy in addressing missing data across varied scenarios, making it a valuable contribution to the field.
