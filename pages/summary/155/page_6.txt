The paper presents the GP-VAE model, a novel approach for deep probabilistic multivariate time series imputation, demonstrating superior performance compared to various baseline methods. The evaluation is conducted on the Healing MNIST and SPRITES test sets, with results summarized in Table 1, showcasing metrics such as negative log likelihood (NLL), mean squared error (MSE), and area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC). The GP-VAE outperforms all baseline methods, including mean and forward imputation, VAE, and HI-VAE, achieving lower NLL and MSE while maintaining high AUROC scores.

The authors discuss baseline methods, which include single imputation techniques like mean and forward imputation that predict a single estimate for missing data, as well as more complex models like Gaussian processes, VAE, HI-VAE, GRUI-GAN, and BRITS. Each of these methods has its own limitations, primarily concerning the treatment of correlations between features or reliance on constant values for imputation.

The Healing MNIST dataset is highlighted for its relevance to medical data, featuring sequences of moving digits with significant missing values, analogous to patient health measurements. The dataset allows for a clear assessment of imputation accuracy against known ground truths. The results indicate that the GP-VAE model not only improves imputation accuracy but also enhances downstream classification tasks, reinforcing its utility in handling missing data in time series applications.
