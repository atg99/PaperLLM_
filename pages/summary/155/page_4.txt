The paper presents GP-VAE, a framework for deep probabilistic multivariate time series imputation specifically designed to address missing data in time series. The authors first establish the problem setting, where they assume a dataset composed of T data points, each with potentially missing features. They define observed and unobserved features, leading to the challenge of estimating missing values based on the available data. Unlike many methods that treat data points independently, the authors recognize the necessity of considering temporal correlations, which complicates the estimation of missing values.

The proposed generative model involves reducing the observed time series with missing data into a complete representation using a variational autoencoder (VAE). In this latent space, the authors model temporal dynamics with Gaussian processes (GPs), which are advantageous for time series modeling due to their data efficiency and calibrated posterior probabilities. However, they highlight challenges in directly applying GPs in the observed space, particularly due to the complexity of kernel matrix inversion and the difficulty of designing suitable kernel functions in the presence of missing data.

To overcome these challenges, the authors propose using a mixture of radial basis function (RBF) kernels to capture dynamics across multiple time scales, which is particularly relevant for medical time series data that exhibit varying dynamics. They employ a Gamma distribution over the length scale to compute an infinite mixture of RBF kernels, resulting in the Rational Quadratic kernel. This kernel choice is motivated by its effectiveness in modeling robust dynamic systems with multi-scale time dynamics.

Overall, the GP-VAE framework combines the strengths of VAEs and GPs to effectively handle missing data in multivariate time series while capturing the underlying temporal correlations and dynamics, making it a promising approach for imputation in various applications, including medical data analysis.
