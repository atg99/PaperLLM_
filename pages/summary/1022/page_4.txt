The paper discusses the design and evaluation of an audio recognition architecture called ERANNs, which avoids dimension reduction techniques like bottleneck blocks due to findings that ResNet38 outperforms ResNet54 on the AudioSet dataset. The architecture utilizes a modified approach with the first ARB lacking BatchNorm and Leaky ReLU, opting for batch normalization after the feature extraction phase to standardize data over the frequency dimension. 

Global pooling combines average and max pooling to leverage their strengths. The model incorporates two fully connected layers, applying softmax for sound classification and sigmoid for audio tagging. Leaky ReLU is preferred over ReLU to mitigate the dying ReLU problem. The system is designed to be input-length invariant due to the use of global pooling, allowing it to process audio signals of varying lengths after zero-padding.

Experiments were conducted across four datasets: AudioSet, ESC-50, UrbanSound8K, and RAVDESS, comparing performance and computational complexity with different hyperparameter settings. Training utilized the Adam optimizer and a one-cycle learning rate policy for AudioSet, while constant learning rates were employed for the other datasets. An exponential moving average technique was used for model evaluation to enhance stability.

For the AudioSet dataset, which contains over 2 million recordings across 527 classes, models were trained using a balanced sampling strategy to address class imbalance. The performance of the models was evaluated using mean average precision (mAP) as the metric. An ablation study on data augmentation techniques indicated that modified mixup and SpecAugment improved model performance, with the best results achieved using a cropping duration of 8 seconds. 

The findings highlight the effectiveness of the proposed techniques for audio pattern recognition tasks and establish a foundation for future research in this area.
