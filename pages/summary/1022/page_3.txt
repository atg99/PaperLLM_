The paper discusses various data augmentation techniques and the architecture of Enhanced Residual Audio Neural Networks (ERANNs) for training models on audio data. Key techniques include temporal cropping, where audio sections of a specified length are randomly selected for training, while full audio recordings are used for evaluation. The authors employ SpecAugment for frequency and time masking on spectrograms, which has shown effectiveness in previous audio processing research. They also utilize a modified mixup technique, which considers sound pressure levels, leading to improved performance compared to standard mixup methods on datasets like ESC-50 and UrbanSound8K.

Pitch shifting is applied during training with a 50% probability, particularly beneficial for speech data. The ERANNs architecture features a series of convolutional blocks designed to optimize processing speed by adjusting kernel sizes, strides, and padding based on a decreasing temporal size parameter. The architecture includes Audio Residual Blocks (ARB), which consist of multiple convolutional layers and batch normalization to enhance feature extraction while maintaining efficiency.

The proposed architecture is detailed in tables, showcasing various stages of output sizes and layer configurations, with specific parameters defined for strides and temporal sizes. The document emphasizes the systematic approach to model training and architecture design, aiming for improved performance in audio classification tasks.
