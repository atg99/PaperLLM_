The paper investigates the impact of hyperparameters on the performance, parameter count, and inference speed of Enhanced Recurrent Audio Neural Networks (ERANNs). Two hyperparameters are analyzed, with models evaluated at three values of W (with sm fixed at 4) and four values of sm (with W fixed at 6). The study aims to balance model complexity by limiting W to 6 and sm to 4. Inference speed is assessed by measuring the number of predictions made per second on 10-second audio recordings, utilizing an NVIDIA Tesla V100. 

Results show that increasing sm reduces FLOPs and enhances inference speed. For example, ERANN-1-6 and ERANN-2-6 are 1.68x and 1.46x faster, respectively, compared to ERANN-0-6 while also demonstrating improved performance. Although the state-of-the-art AST system outperforms ERANN-1-6 in terms of accuracy, ERANN-1-6 is significantly more efficient, having 1.62x fewer parameters and being 1.45x faster. The efficiency of ERANN-1-6 is particularly notable when compared to the AST (Ensemble-M) system, being 7.10x faster and 9.66x smaller.

Further comparisons with previous Audio Processing Recognition (APR) systems reveal that while ERANN-1-6 does not achieve state-of-the-art results on the AudioSet dataset, it is more efficient than the AST system. The proposed ERANN-2-5 model, with an mAP of 0.446, is 2.11x smaller than CNN14 and Wavegram-Logmel-CNN, while outperforming other prior systems.

The ESC-50 dataset is also examined, where various hyperparameter settings and pre-training impacts are evaluated. The results indicate that higher values of W lead to increased overfitting risks, particularly in small datasets. Notably, ERANN-1-3 demonstrates improved accuracy over ERANN-0-3 while being 1.38x faster.

Overall, the findings suggest that the proposed ERANNs not only enhance inference speed but also maintain or improve performance relative to existing models, highlighting their efficiency in audio classification tasks.
