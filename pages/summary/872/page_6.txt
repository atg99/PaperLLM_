This paper presents a comprehensive evaluation of a novel method for segmentation performance across multiple datasets, specifically focusing on cross-modal self-attention mechanisms. The results, summarized in Table 1, demonstrate that the proposed method consistently outperforms existing state-of-the-art approaches in terms of Intersection over Union (IoU), particularly excelling in challenging datasets like UNC+ and G-Ref. This suggests that the model effectively captures long-range dependencies and accurately identifies referred objects based on complex expressions.

In the ablation study, various attention mechanisms were assessed, revealing that the cross-modal self-attention method significantly outperforms alternatives such as word attention and pixel attention, as shown in Table 2. This indicates that the integration of language and vision features is enhanced by the proposed method.

Qualitative results illustrate the effectiveness of multi-level self-attentive features, with attention distributions varying across different levels of linguistic representation. Higher levels focus on object-referential words, while lower levels emphasize attributes and relationships.

Further experiments evaluated the effectiveness of multimodal feature representations, confirming that the proposed cross-modal self-attentive feature-based approaches yield superior performance compared to traditional methods. The paper also highlights the importance of the gated multi-level fusion module, which enhances feature integration compared to established fusion techniques.

Overall, the findings underscore the advantages of the proposed method in achieving robust segmentation performance through effective attention mechanisms and multimodal feature representations.
