The paper presents a novel approach to referring image segmentation that integrates multimodal features through a combination of cross-modal self-attention and gated multi-level fusion. The model comprises three main components: multimodal features derived from image, spatial coordinates, and language features; a cross-modal self-attention module to capture long-range dependencies between individual words and spatial regions; and a gated multi-level fusion module to synthesize features from different levels for final segmentation.

The motivation behind this approach stems from the limitations of previous methods that either used LSTM to encode entire referring expressions, which led to a loss of detailed information about individual words, or processed words sequentially, limiting the scope to local spatial regions. In contrast, the proposed model maintains individual word representations and leverages cross-modal self-attention to establish global contextual relationships.

For input, the model utilizes an image and a referring expression composed of N words. It employs a CNN to extract visual features, which are then combined with one-hot encoded word vectors and spatial coordinate features to create a comprehensive multimodal feature representation. The spatial coordinate features are defined in an 8-dimensional format that captures normalized positional and size information.

Overall, this method aims to enhance the understanding and segmentation of referred objects in images by effectively integrating detailed linguistic information with visual data through innovative attention mechanisms and fusion strategies.
