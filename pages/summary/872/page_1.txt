In this paper, the authors address the challenge of referring image segmentation, which involves segmenting an object in an image based on a natural language expression. Traditional methods do not adequately capture the long-range correlations between the image and the referring expression, treating them as separate entities. To overcome this limitation, the authors propose a Cross-Modal Self-Attention (CMSA) module that effectively integrates linguistic and visual features, allowing the model to focus on relevant words in the expression and significant regions in the image.

Additionally, the authors introduce a gated multi-level fusion module that selectively integrates self-attentive cross-modal features at different levels, thereby controlling the information flow of these features. The effectiveness of the proposed approach is validated through experiments on four evaluation datasets, where it consistently outperforms existing state-of-the-art methods. The research highlights the importance of capturing the interplay between language and visual data for improved segmentation accuracy, with potential applications in interactive photo editing and human-robot interaction.
