This paper addresses the limitations of existing multimodal interaction methods in referring image segmentation, which typically analyze linguistic and visual information separately within local contexts. The authors propose a cross-modal self-attention (CMSA) module that effectively learns long-range dependencies between visual and linguistic features, allowing the model to focus on significant regions in images and crucial keywords in text descriptions. Additionally, a gated multi-level fusion module is introduced to refine segmentation masks by selectively integrating multi-level self-attentive features, enhancing the precision of segmentation.

The contributions of this work are threefold: 
1. The introduction of a CMSA method that captures long-range dependencies, leading to robust multimodal feature representation.
2. The development of a gated multi-level fusion module that integrates features to capture fine details for accurate segmentation.
3. An extensive empirical evaluation across four benchmark datasets, showing superior performance compared to state-of-the-art methods.

The paper further reviews related research areas, including advancements in semantic segmentation, techniques in referring image localization and segmentation, and the application of attention mechanisms in deep learning. The authors highlight the novelty of their cross-modal self-attention approach, which connects linguistic and visual attention, differing from traditional methods that treat these modalities independently.
