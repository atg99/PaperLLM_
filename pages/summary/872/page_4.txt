This paper discusses the integration of multimodal features derived from visual and linguistic inputs for tasks such as referring image segmentation. The authors define a multimodal feature vector at a spatial location, incorporating a visual feature, a normalized word embedding, and spatial coordinates. This leads to a comprehensive feature representation that captures the relationship between specific locations in an image and corresponding words in a referring expression.

To address the challenge of redundant information in the large multimodal feature set, the authors propose a cross-modal self-attention module. This module efficiently captures long-range dependencies between words and spatial locations, allowing for a refined feature map that summarizes the multimodal features without being affected by the number of words in the language description. The self-attention mechanism involves transforming the multimodal features into query, key, and value pairs, computing attention scores, and generating a cross-modal self-attentive feature representation.

The final feature representation is obtained through average pooling, which consolidates information across all words in the referring expression. The paper also introduces a gated multi-level fusion technique to enhance segmentation performance by integrating features from multiple layers of the CNN backbone. This approach leverages the strengths of features at different scales to improve the overall segmentation output. The proposed methods are illustrated with a visual representation of the process, emphasizing the internal relationships between linguistic and spatial representations.
