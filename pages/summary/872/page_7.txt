The paper presents an ablation study on methods for multimodal feature representation and multi-level feature fusion in the context of referring image segmentation. The evaluation is conducted on the UNC validation set, comparing various techniques to enhance segmentation accuracy.

Key findings include:

1. **Performance Metrics**: The results are measured using precision at different thresholds (prec@0.5 to prec@0.9) and Intersection over Union (IoU). The proposed method, CMSA+GF, achieves the highest precision across all thresholds and an IoU of 58.32, indicating its effectiveness in the task.

2. **Comparison of Methods**: The study compares multiple approaches, including RMI-LSTM, RRN-CNN, and several configurations of the proposed CMSA (cross-modal self-attention) and GF (gated fusion) modules. The CMSA+GF method outperforms all other configurations in both multimodal representation and multi-level feature fusion.

3. **Visualization Techniques**: The paper employs visualization to analyze spatial feature representations corresponding to various query expressions. Heatmaps generated from the model highlight relevant objects in the images based on the queries, demonstrating the model's ability to understand different categories and relationships.

4. **Failure Cases**: The authors also discuss instances where the model fails, providing insights into potential limitations and areas for further improvement.

Overall, the results and analyses presented in the paper underscore the advantages of the proposed methods in enhancing the performance of referring image segmentation tasks.
