The paper presents the CAT framework, which utilizes a teacher-student model to improve classification in a domain adaptation context. The teacher classifier, an implicit ensemble of the student classifier, generates pseudo labels for unlabeled target data, which are then used in the loss functions Lc and La to drive the training process. This approach leverages mini-batch sampling and forward propagation to enhance training efficiency compared to previous methods.

A key component of the framework is the discriminative clustering mechanism. In the source domain, class-conditional structures are clearly defined due to labeled data, while in the target domain, these structures are less apparent. The introduction of the teacher classifier helps mitigate the error amplification typically associated with self-training by providing more reliable pseudo labels. The clustering loss Lc encourages features from the same class to cluster together while maintaining a predefined distance from features of different classes, thereby refining the representation space.

Concerns regarding the potential negative impact of incorrect teacher predictions on training dynamics are addressed by referencing prior research that indicates such training methods can still converge effectively. The teacher’s predictions, derived from a collective of instances, help maintain robust learning even in the presence of errors.

Additionally, the paper highlights the importance of aligning the feature spaces of the source and target domains to overcome geometrical mismatches that can occur due to their individual characteristics. This alignment is achieved by minimizing the divergence between corresponding clusters in both domains, thereby improving the overall performance of the classifier.
