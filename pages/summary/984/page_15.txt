These models often utilize sequence-to-sequence architectures, which have been shown to produce coherent and contextually relevant summaries. 

The paper highlights two primary methods of text summarization: extractive and abstractive. Extractive summarization focuses on selecting key sentences directly from the text, ensuring that the summary maintains the original author's voice. However, it can lead to lengthy and overlapping outputs. Conversely, abstractive summarization creates new sentences that may introduce novel vocabulary, resulting in concise summaries but posing challenges in training and quality control.

Numerous studies have advanced these techniques, with notable early contributions employing neural networks for extractive summarization. These initial frameworks utilized ranking methods to identify salient sentences, which were later refined with document-level encoders and classifiers. The paper indicates a significant body of research dedicated to enhancing both methodologies, underscoring their respective strengths and weaknesses in the landscape of natural language processing advancements through deep learning.
