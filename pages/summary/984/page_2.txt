The paper by Torfi et al. discusses advancements in natural language processing (NLP) through deep learning, emphasizing the importance of AI in managing the increasing volume of data that humans cannot process effectively. The authors define deep learning as the application of deep neural networks to large datasets, enabling the extraction of features and solutions to various tasks, from classification to complex reasoning. 

The paper outlines three primary deep learning architectures: 

1. **Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs)**: These are the simplest form of feed-forward neural networks, consisting of at least three layers where neurons in one layer connect to all neurons in the next. MLPs utilize nonlinear activation functions and are useful for straightforward tasks.

2. **Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs)**: Inspired by the human visual cortex, CNNs are particularly effective for processing 2D and 3D data, such as images. They perform feature extraction through convolutional layers, capturing essential information from the input data. CNNs have been successfully applied in image classification and various NLP tasks, where sentences are represented as matrices.

3. **Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs)**: RNNs are constructed by linking a series of feed-forward networks, allowing them to handle sequential data. They maintain a form of memory through their hidden layers, enabling them to remember past inputs, which is crucial for applications involving time-dependent information.

Overall, the paper highlights the intersection of deep learning and AI in enhancing NLP capabilities, focusing on how different architectures contribute to processing and understanding vast amounts of data.
