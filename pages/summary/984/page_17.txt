The paper discusses two main types of dialogue systems: task-oriented and non-task-based systems. Task-oriented systems focus on predefined tasks and are often developed using deep reinforcement learning, leading to improved performance and domain adaptation. These systems use end-to-end frameworks that simplify the engineering process but may struggle with interoperability with external databases. Various strategies, such as converting user input into internal representations and combining learning methods, have been proposed to address these challenges.

In contrast, non-task-based systems aim for more natural conversations, with chatbots typically classified into retrieval-based and generative methods. Retrieval-based models utilize existing information resources to provide accurate responses but are limited in variety, while generative models create responses from scratch, offering greater flexibility at the risk of potential errors. The retrieval-based methods can be further divided into single-turn and multi-turn response matching, with the latter considering context from previous interactions to maintain conversation consistency. Overall, the development of dialogue agents capable of human-like conversation remains a complex challenge, necessitating advanced deep learning techniques and extensive training on conversational data.
