The paper discusses the integration of artificial intelligence (AI) in law enforcement and its implications for users of law. It highlights the use of facial recognition technology by police departments to identify suspects by matching images with databases of individuals who have interacted with law enforcement. 

A significant focus is placed on the role of AI for users of law, which includes individuals, organizations, and businesses that operate under legal frameworks. Companies are increasingly utilizing business-logic policy systems—private expert systems that encapsulate rules for legal compliance—to navigate complex regulatory environments, such as import/export laws. These systems employ logic and knowledge representation to ensure that internal processes align with applicable regulations and avoid violations.

Additionally, the paper introduces the concept of computable contracts, which are legal agreements that can be processed and executed by computer systems. This innovation represents a growing intersection of technology and legal practice, emphasizing the evolving landscape of AI in law. Overall, the paper underscores the transformative potential of AI in enhancing legal compliance and operational efficiency for users of law.
