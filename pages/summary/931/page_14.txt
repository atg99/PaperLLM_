The paper discusses the application of artificial intelligence in the legal field, particularly through the development of expert systems for tasks such as tax preparation. It illustrates how tax laws can be translated into computer rules using an example where a marginal tax rate is applied to income exceeding a specific threshold. This process involves legal experts collaborating with software developers to encode tax provisions into formal rules that a computer can execute. 

The author contrasts this top-down approach, where programmers explicitly define all operational rules for the AI, with a bottom-up machine-learning approach, where algorithms learn and establish rules independently. The paper emphasizes the importance of this rule-based translation in enabling automated systems to make expert-level decisions in legal contexts.
