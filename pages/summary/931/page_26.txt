The paper discusses the potential for automation in legal tasks traditionally performed by lawyers, focusing specifically on litigation discovery and technology-assisted review. Litigation discovery involves obtaining evidence for lawsuits, often requiring the review of large volumes of documents from opposing counsel. Historically, attorneys manually reviewed these documents to determine relevance to legal issues. 

The introduction of electronic discovery in the mid-2000s allowed for predictive coding, a technology that uses computer-based techniques to classify documents as relevant or irrelevant. Recent advancements have incorporated artificial intelligence, particularly machine learning, which enables software to be trained on example documents to recognize patterns and improve document review efficiency. This development has become essential due to the increasing volume of documents associated with litigation, necessitating automated solutions to manage the workload effectively. 

The paper highlights the evolution of document review processes and the growing reliance on AI technologies in the legal domain, suggesting both opportunities and limitations for the future of legal practice.
