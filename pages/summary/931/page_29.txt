The paper discusses the integration of artificial intelligence (AI) in the legal field, highlighting its applications in predicting case outcomes and aiding in legal decision-making. Lawyers are increasingly using machine-learning systems to assess the likelihood of winning cases based on data rather than intuition. The text emphasizes that while AI can automate routine legal tasks, it struggles with more complex lawyerly functions that require abstract thinking, problem-solving, emotional intelligence, and strategic analysis.

Additionally, the paper examines the role of AI in the administration of law, particularly its use by judges and government officials in making legal and policy decisions. A key example is the use of AI in criminal justice for risk assessments, where judges utilize software to evaluate the likelihood of a defendant reoffending or fleeing when determining bail or sentencing. These systems leverage machine-learning algorithms to generate quantitative risk scores, reflecting a growing trend towards data-driven decision-making in the courtroom.
