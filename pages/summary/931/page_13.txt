The paper discusses the significance of data availability on the Internet and its impact on machine learning, particularly in the legal domain. It highlights that effective machine learning relies on large amounts of high-quality, structured, machine-processable data, which is currently scarce in law, except in specific niches. 

Furthermore, the paper explores the branch of artificial intelligence focused on logical rules and knowledge representation, which aims to model real-world phenomena for automation purposes. This involves programmers creating a set of rules that encapsulate the logic and knowledge of a specific activity. The structured knowledge allows computers to process and reason deductively about the information provided.

The paper also references the history of knowledge representation in AI research, emphasizing its role in developing expert systems. These systems translate expert knowledge into formal rules that computers can understand, facilitating automation in various fields, including medicine and potentially law.
