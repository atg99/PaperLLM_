The paper discusses the potential impacts of artificial intelligence (AI) on the legal field, emphasizing the need for a critical evaluation of where AI is likely to be effective and where it may fall short. It warns against the assumption that successful automation of one complex task, such as playing chess or driving, implies that AI can automate other complex tasks like legal argumentation. The author notes that current AI systems are typically "narrow" intelligence, designed for specific tasks and not easily transferable to unrelated activities. 

AI technology excels in environments with clear patterns, rules, and structured processes but struggles in domains that are abstract, value-laden, or require human judgment, intuition, or common sense. Understanding these distinctions is crucial for assessing AI's current and future role in law. The paper underscores the importance of recognizing the limitations of AI in areas that involve humanistic concepts and societal norms.
