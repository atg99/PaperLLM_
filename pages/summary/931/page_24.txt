The paper discusses the integration of artificial intelligence (AI) and law, tracing its origins back to the 1600s with Gottfried Leibniz, who explored the potential of mathematical techniques to enhance legal understanding. The evolution of AI in law gained momentum in the mid-twentieth century, aligning with broader AI research trends. Early efforts focused on knowledge representation and rule-based systems, primarily in European university laboratories, where researchers aimed to model legal arguments and rules in a computational format. Significant developments occurred from the 1970s to the 1990s, culminating in the establishment of the International Conference on Artificial Intelligence and Law (ICAIL) in 1987, which continues to showcase advancements in this interdisciplinary field. Pioneering figures in AI and law include Anne Gardner, L. Thorne McCarty, and Richard Susskind, among others, who have contributed to the foundational research and ongoing discourse in this area.
