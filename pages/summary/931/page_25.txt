The paper discusses the evolution of artificial intelligence (AI) in the legal field, highlighting a shift from knowledge-representation techniques to machine-learning-based approaches since around 2000. This transition has been driven by legal-technology startups and interdisciplinary research centers, such as Stanford University's CodeX Center for Legal Informatics, which have contributed to the integration of AI systems into various aspects of the legal system.

The authors categorize the use of AI in law into three main user groups: administrators of law (government officials like judges and legislators), practitioners of law (primarily attorneys), and those governed by law (individuals and organizations that interact with legal frameworks). 

The paper specifically focuses on the role of attorneys, who engage in a variety of tasks such as client counseling, risk assessment, contract drafting, and litigation. It emphasizes the increasing reliance on AI tools to enhance efficiency and effectiveness in these legal practices, reflecting the broader trend of AI adoption across multiple sectors.
