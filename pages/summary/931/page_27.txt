The paper discusses the role of automated predictive coding in legal document review, emphasizing its strengths and limitations. While automated systems can process thousands or millions of documents efficiently, the ultimate determination of relevance still rests with human attorneys. This is due to the complex nature of legal decisions, which require an understanding of law, facts, and strategic considerations that current AI technology cannot fully grasp. 

Automated predictive coding serves primarily to filter out documents that are likely irrelevant, allowing attorneys to focus their judgment on a smaller, more pertinent subset. This highlights the necessity of human involvement in sophisticated AI applications, particularly in legal contexts where judgment is essential. The paper also notes that certain tasks, like litigation discovery, can be partially automated due to identifiable patterns that algorithms can analyze. Overall, while AI can enhance efficiency in legal practices, human cognition remains irreplaceable in tasks requiring nuanced judgment.
