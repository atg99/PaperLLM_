The article aims to provide a clear understanding of the relationship between artificial intelligence (AI) and law. It argues that AI should not be perceived as magical or genuinely intelligent in a human sense. Instead, contemporary AI operates by identifying patterns and applying rules within narrow contexts, producing results that may appear intelligent without actual cognitive understanding.

The paper highlights the limitations of current AI technologies, which struggle with abstractions, meaning comprehension, knowledge transfer across different tasks, and managing unstructured or open-ended problems. Successful applications of AI, such as in chess or fraud detection, typically occur in structured environments with defined right or wrong answers and identifiable patterns.

Understanding these strengths and weaknesses is essential for grasping AI's potential impact on legal practice and administration. By recognizing where AI can be beneficial and where it falls short, stakeholders can make more informed decisions regarding its use in the legal field.
