This paper discusses significant issues surrounding the use of artificial intelligence (AI) in the legal system, particularly concerning predictive models used for assessing the risk of reoffending. It highlights the potential for bias in these systems, exemplified by how biased police arrest records can skew the data used for machine learning. Such biases may lead to the reinforcement of discriminatory patterns in AI predictions.

Another critical issue raised is the lack of interpretability and transparency in AI systems. Many models operate as "black boxes," making it difficult even for their creators to understand the decision-making processes. This has prompted calls for AI systems to provide explanations for their outcomes to ensure accountability and trust.

The paper also addresses concerns regarding the deference given to AI in automated decision-making within government. There is a risk that judges and other officials may overestimate the objectivity and accuracy of AI-generated assessments, potentially leading to unjust outcomes. The discussion emphasizes the necessity for caution in integrating AI into legal processes, ensuring that the technology is used responsibly and ethically.
