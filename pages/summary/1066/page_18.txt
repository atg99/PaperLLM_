The paper discusses the optimization of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) by analyzing the loss functions used for training the generator (G) and discriminator (D). It highlights the differences between KL divergence and reverse KL divergence in the context of GAN training. Specifically, it points out that using reverse KL divergence can lead to mode collapse, where the generator produces limited diversity in samples. This is illustrated through examples involving mixtures of Gaussians, where the generator tends to blur multiple modes into a single output, thereby reducing sample diversity.

The paper identifies two types of poor performance for G: generating a narrow range of samples with low penalization, and producing implausible samples with high penalization. This suggests that the design of the loss function significantly impacts GAN performance.

To address these issues, the paper introduces Wasserstein GAN (WGAN), which utilizes the Earth Mover's distance as a loss measure. This approach aims to enhance the stability of GAN training by providing a more robust optimization framework compared to traditional methods, thereby mitigating the problems associated with the original GAN loss functions. Consequently, the paper argues for the importance of innovating loss functions to improve GAN training stability and performance.
