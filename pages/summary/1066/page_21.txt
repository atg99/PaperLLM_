The paper provides a comprehensive survey and taxonomy of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) in computer vision, focusing on their various implementations and theoretical foundations. It discusses the decision boundaries of original GANs and Least Squares GANs (LSGANs), highlighting how LSGANs penalize generated samples that are far from the decision boundary, thereby improving the generator's output by pushing samples closer to the boundary.

The paper also introduces different f-divergence functions, which are crucial for the optimization of GANs. It lists various examples of f-divergence functions, including KL divergence and Pearson X2, and explains how these functions can be used to generalize the loss function of GANs. The authors emphasize that the f-divergence function must be tractable for practical computation, which is achieved through the use of the convex conjugate.

The optimization process for f-GANs is characterized by maximizing a lower bound on the f-divergence with respect to the discriminator while minimizing the f-divergence concerning the generator. This approach, termed variational divergence minimization (VDM), aims to bring the distribution of generated samples closer to the true data distribution.

Overall, the paper serves as a detailed reference for understanding the advancements and methodologies in GANs, particularly within the realm of computer vision.
