This paper provides a comprehensive survey of architecture-variant Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) in the context of computer vision, focusing on their potential to address three key challenges: image quality, mode diversity, and unstable training. The authors highlight the evolution of GAN architectures from 2014 to 2020, showcasing various interconnections among different GAN variants.

The paper discusses the architecture of MSG-GAN, which facilitates enhanced information sharing between the generator and discriminator through specific activation mappings. It presents the performance of MSG-GAN across multiple datasets, including CIFAR10 and CelebA-HQ, using consistent hyperparameters and the WGAN-GP loss function. While MSG-GAN shows promising results, its ability to generate diverse images remains untested, particularly in the CIFAR10 dataset, where performance was not as high as in other datasets. The authors speculate that the connection between G and D may limit diversity, suggesting that integrating a self-attention module could improve results.

The summary includes a visual representation of architecture-variant GANs and their performance relative to the three challenges, indicating that various models have made strides in improving image quality and addressing training instability. The authors encourage readers to consult original articles for a deeper understanding of each GAN variant's theoretical foundations and performance metrics, with quantitative results detailed in Table 2. Overall, the paper underscores the significance of architectural innovations in enhancing GAN capabilities in computer vision tasks.
