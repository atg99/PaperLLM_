The paper discusses the training dynamics of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) in computer vision, particularly focusing on the behavior of the Jensen-Shannon (JS) divergence used in the optimization process. It illustrates how the objective of the GAN is to update the generator (G) to align the generated distribution (pд) with the real distribution (pr). The figures demonstrate the training progression, indicating that while G is being optimized, the JS divergence remains constant at initial states when there is no overlap between pд and pr. 

The paper highlights a critical issue during GAN training, where the discriminator (D) can overpower G, leading to instability. This instability arises because the JS divergence does not provide sufficient gradient information when D is not optimal, resulting in a vanishing gradient problem for G. The authors emphasize that the training process is ineffective when the distributions do not overlap significantly, which is often the case in practice.

Furthermore, the original GAN framework aims to avoid this vanishing gradient issue by minimizing a different loss function. However, this strategy can lead to mode dropping, where G fails to capture the full diversity of the real data distribution. The paper proposes that understanding and addressing these challenges is crucial for improving GAN training and performance in computer vision tasks.
