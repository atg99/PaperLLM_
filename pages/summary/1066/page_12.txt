The paper discusses the implementation of a self-attention mechanism in Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), specifically in the context of the BigGAN architecture. The self-attention mechanism enhances the generator and discriminator's performance, particularly with large feature maps, as evidenced by improved FID and Inception scores. The authors emphasize that this approach is complementary to convolutional methods, recommending its use for generating diverse outputs in GANs.

BigGAN, building on the architecture of SAGAN, achieves state-of-the-art results on the ImageNet dataset by scaling up training parameters, such as increasing the number of channels and batch sizes. The training process involves specific settings, including halving the learning rate and employing a truncated Gaussian distribution during inference to balance image fidelity and variety. Key modifications to the BigGAN architecture include the integration of self-attention modules, hinge loss for stability, class-conditional batch normalization, and a strategy to update the discriminator more frequently than the generator. Additionally, the model utilizes a moving average of weights from prior iterations to enhance image generation quality. Overall, these strategies contribute to the effective scaling and performance of BigGAN in image generation tasks.
