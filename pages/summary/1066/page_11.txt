This paper surveys the use of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) in computer vision, focusing on key advancements and methodologies. A notable approach discussed is the Progressive Growing GAN (PROGAN), which begins training with low-resolution images (4x4 pixels) and incrementally increases the resolution by adding layers to both the generator and discriminator. This strategy enhances stability during training by allowing the networks to address simpler tasks before tackling the complexities of high-resolution image generation. The training process employs the Adam optimizer and specific normalization techniques, while leveraging datasets such as CIFAR10, LSUN, and CelebA-HQ.

Additionally, the paper highlights the Self-attention GAN (SAGAN), which incorporates a self-attention mechanism to overcome limitations of traditional CNNs that struggle with capturing global context in images. SAGAN effectively learns long-range dependencies, improving performance on multi-class datasets like ImageNet. The architecture utilizes spectral normalization and conditional batch normalization to optimize the training of both networks.

Overall, both PROGAN and SAGAN exemplify significant innovations in GAN architecture that contribute to producing high-quality, realistic images across various applications in computer vision.
