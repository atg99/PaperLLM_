The paper provides a comprehensive survey and taxonomy of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) in the context of computer vision. It highlights the evolution of architecture-variant GANs, as illustrated in a timeline, emphasizing their increasing complexity and diverse mechanisms. These architecture-variants are developed for specific applications such as image-to-image transfer, super-resolution, image completion, and text-to-image generation, aiming to enhance image diversity, quality, and training stability.

The discussion includes the Fully Connected GAN (FCGAN), which utilizes fully connected neural networks for both the generator and discriminator. It is noted that this approach was primarily tested on simpler datasets like MNIST and CIFAR-10. The authors suggest a training strategy involving multiple optimization steps for the discriminator and a single step for the generator to mitigate overfitting issues. However, the architecture faces challenges, such as the vanishing gradient problem and poor generalization for complex images. The paper aims to provide insights into various GAN architectures and their specific applications, contributing to the understanding and advancement of GAN technology in generating synthetic image data.
