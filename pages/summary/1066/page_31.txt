The paper provides a comprehensive survey and taxonomy of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) in the field of computer vision, highlighting their performance across various models and datasets including CIFAR10, ImageNet, LSUN, and CelebA. A performance summary table showcases the Inception Score (IS) and Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) for different GAN architectures. Notable findings indicate that models like BigGAN and PROGAN have achieved superior performance metrics, particularly in multi-class image generation.

The paper discusses advancements in GAN architecture, emphasizing the use of Wasserstein distance in models like BEGAN and PROGAN, and spectral normalization in SAGAN and BigGAN, which contribute to improved performance. The authors also identify future directions for GAN research beyond computer vision, such as in time-series generation and natural language processing. They note the challenges of applying GANs in these areas due to the discrete nature of non-image data and the need for efficient performance evaluation metrics.

Moreover, the paper acknowledges the societal implications of GANs, including potential misuse in generating misleading media content, which raises concerns about privacy and reputation. The authors call for responsible use and further research to harness the benefits of GANs while mitigating risks associated with their application. Overall, the paper underscores the transformative impact of GANs since their inception in 2014, while also highlighting the necessity for ongoing exploration and ethical considerations in their deployment.
