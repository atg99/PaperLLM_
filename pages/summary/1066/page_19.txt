The paper discusses the advancements in Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) specifically focusing on Wasserstein GAN (WGAN) and its variant WGAN with Gradient Penalty (WGAN-GP). It highlights the advantages of using the Wasserstein distance over traditional methods like KL and JS divergence, emphasizing its ability to provide a meaningful gradient for training the generator, even when distributions do not overlap. WGAN employs a regression-based approach for the discriminator, unlike the original GAN which uses it as a binary classifier, leading to a smoother gradient for generator training.

The paper also addresses the instability issues encountered in WGAN training when using certain optimizers, suggesting the use of RMSProp instead of Adam. WGAN-GP was introduced to enhance the generalization of WGAN, particularly for deeper models, by implementing a gradient penalty that restricts the Lipschitz constant of the discriminator. This modification improves the distribution of trained parameters, thus enhancing the model's capacity. The original loss function of WGAN is retained with additional terms for the gradient penalty, resulting in better performance compared to its predecessor. Overall, the developments in WGAN and WGAN-GP represent significant strides in stabilizing and improving GAN training in computer vision applications.
