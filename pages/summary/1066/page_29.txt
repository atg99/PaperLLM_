The paper discusses various applications of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) in computer vision, focusing on image completion, image matting, image-to-image translation, and video generation.

In image completion and repair, traditional algorithms rely on low-level cues to synthesize content for missing regions. However, these methods may struggle with complex patterns, such as in face images. The authors reference a method by Li et al. that employs an autoencoder with two discriminators to optimize the process through adversarial losses, represented by a specific loss function incorporating various components.

Image matting involves estimating the opacity of foreground objects in images or videos, which has applications in image editing and film production. The paper highlights the introduction of GANs into this field through AlphaGAN, which generates visually appealing compositions by training on images with both ground-truth and predicted alpha values.

For image-to-image translation, the paper mentions the use of Conditional GANs (CGANs) when paired training data is available. In cases of unpaired data, CycleGAN is introduced, utilizing cycle consistency loss to ensure mappings between domains are coherent. Other approaches like DiscoGAN and DualGAN also address the challenges of lacking paired training data.

In the realm of video generation, GANs have shown promise but face significant challenges due to the complexities of temporal modeling and increased resource demands. The paper notes that current research focuses on producing high-resolution videos, which remains a difficult task due to memory limitations and training stability issues.

Overall, the paper surveys the advancements and challenges of applying GANs across different aspects of computer vision, emphasizing their potential and ongoing research in the field.
