This paper presents a survey and taxonomy of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) in computer vision, focusing on various architectures including SGAN, CGAN, BiGAN, InfoGAN, BEGAN, and AC-GAN. One key aspect discussed is the enhancement of the discriminator's ability in CGAN through the introduction of encoded labels, although some labels may not fully correspond to the images.

The paper elaborates on InfoGAN, which advances CGAN by learning interpretable representations without supervision. It achieves this by maximizing the mutual information between conditional variables and the generated data. InfoGAN incorporates an additional classifier Q, which predicts the conditional variable y based on the generator G's output. This setup resembles an autoencoder, aimed at finding an embedding that minimizes the cross-entropy between the predicted and actual y values. The discriminator D retains its role in distinguishing real from generated data, similar to FCGAN. 

Overall, the paper provides a detailed examination of these GAN architectures and their functionalities, contributing to the understanding of their applications in computer vision.
