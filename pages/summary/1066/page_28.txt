The paper discusses loss-variant Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) and their applications, particularly in image synthesis. It categorizes various challenges faced by GANs and illustrates how different loss-variants address these challenges. The authors highlight that some loss-variants, like Spectral Normalization (SN-GAN) and Relativistic GAN (RGAN), exhibit strong generalization capabilities and can be integrated into other GAN architectures. They advocate for the widespread use of spectral normalization across all GAN applications.

In the context of image synthesis, the paper specifically focuses on image super-resolution as a key application area. It introduces the Super Resolution GAN (SRGAN) framework, which generates high-resolution images from low-resolution inputs through a combination of adversarial loss and content loss. The content loss in SRGAN can be defined in various ways, including pixel-wise mean squared error and feature map losses that capture different levels of image features. The authors demonstrate that SRGAN outperforms traditional methods in terms of perceptual quality, emphasizing the importance of selecting appropriate content losses for different applications.
