The paper discusses advancements in Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), focusing on mode collapse prevention and the introduction of various loss functions. A mode regularized GAN employs a loss function that combines a geometric measurement with traditional GAN components, effectively addressing mode collapse issues. The authors evaluate this approach on the MNIST and CelebA datasets.

Geometric GANs leverage hinge loss to improve stability and reduce mode collapse, especially in high-dimensional, low-sample-size scenarios. The authors detail the architecture and training methods, indicating the effectiveness of this approach on multiple datasets.

Relativistic GAN (RGAN) introduces a novel cost function that emphasizes the relative realism of generated samples compared to real ones. This approach modifies the original GAN's discriminator to enhance stability by focusing on the difference between real and generated data, moving the optimization goal towards a value of 0.5 rather than 1. The authors assert that RGAN can be generalized across various loss-variant GANs, contributing to the broader understanding of GAN optimization techniques.
