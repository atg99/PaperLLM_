This paper reviews advancements in Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), highlighting improvements in image quality, diversity, and training stability. It discusses various GAN architectures, noting that larger batch sizes enhance both image quality and diversity, exemplified by BigGAN. However, GPU memory limitations pose challenges for processing these large batches. The paper presents PROGAN's progressive training strategy as a viable alternative for maintaining high image quality without large batches. 

The addition of self-attention mechanisms in both the generator and discriminator, as seen in SAGAN, has yielded significant improvements in image diversity, particularly with the ImageNet dataset. Training stability is emphasized, with spectral normalization identified as an effective loss function due to its ease of implementation and low computational cost. 

While state-of-the-art GANs like BigGAN and PROGAN excel in producing high-quality images, their application in video generation and other domains such as time-series generation and natural language processing remains limited. The authors conclude that there are substantial opportunities for further research and application in these areas. The acknowledgment section credits anonymous reviewers for their contributions to improving the survey's quality.
