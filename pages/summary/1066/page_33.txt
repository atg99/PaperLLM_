This paper provides a comprehensive survey and taxonomy of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) in the field of computer vision. It highlights the evolution and various applications of GANs, categorizing them based on different methodologies and use cases. Key contributions from notable works are referenced, such as BEGAN, which introduces boundary equilibrium principles, and InfoGAN, which focuses on interpretable representation learning. The survey also discusses evaluation measures for GANs, emphasizing the pros and cons of current metrics.

The authors explore advanced techniques like mode regularization and self-supervised learning, which enhance the performance and applicability of GANs. The paper presents significant advancements in high-fidelity image synthesis, video generation, and multi-domain image translation, showcasing GANs' versatility in producing realistic outputs across different domains. Furthermore, the authors address challenges in GAN training and the need for improved evaluation strategies.

Overall, this survey serves as a foundational resource for understanding GANs' impact on computer vision, offering insights into their structure, functionality, and practical implementations.
