The paper presents a comprehensive survey and taxonomy of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) in the field of computer vision, focusing on developments from 2014 to 2020. It proposes a taxonomy that categorizes GANs into two main variants: architecture-variant and loss-variant. 

The architecture-variant is further divided into three categories: 
1. **Network Architecture**: This includes improvements made to the overall GAN architecture, exemplified by Progressive GAN (PROGAN).
2. **Latent Space**: This category emphasizes modifications based on different representations of latent space, such as Conditional GAN (CGAN), which incorporates label information for both the generator and discriminator.
3. **Application-Focused**: This pertains to architecture modifications tailored for specific applications, like CycleGAN, which is designed for image style transfer.

The loss-variant is split into two categories: 
1. **Loss Types**: Different loss functions optimized for GANs are discussed.
2. **Regularization**: This includes additional penalties integrated into the loss function or normalization operations applied to the network. The loss functions are categorized into integral probability metric (IPM)-based and non-IPM-based GANs, with IPM-based GANs having specific constraints on the discriminator, such as in Wasserstein GAN (WGAN).

The paper also references previous reviews of GANs, particularly those focused on performance evaluation across various types of GANs. Overall, the study aims to provide a structured overview of the advancements in GAN research, highlighting key concepts and classifications.
