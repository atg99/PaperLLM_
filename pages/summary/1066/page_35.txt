The paper presents a comprehensive survey and taxonomy of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) within the field of computer vision. It discusses the foundational concepts of GANs, their architecture, and various applications in image generation, translation, and enhancement. The authors categorize GANs based on their methodologies, highlighting advancements such as style-based generators and geometry-aware networks. Key studies are referenced, including those that address convergence, stability, and the integration of GANs with other machine learning techniques.

The survey emphasizes the evolution of GANs since their inception, showcasing significant contributions from various researchers. It covers applications ranging from image super-resolution to multivariate anomaly detection and person image generation. The paper also critiques existing GAN architectures, discussing challenges such as mode collapse and training instability. By providing a detailed overview of the current landscape and future directions for GAN research, the authors aim to facilitate understanding and inspire further innovations in the field.
