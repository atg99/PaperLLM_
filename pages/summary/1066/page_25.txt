The paper provides a comprehensive survey and taxonomy of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) in computer vision, highlighting advancements such as the Relativistic GAN (RGAN) and Spectral Normalization GAN (SN-GAN). RGAN improves upon standard GANs by utilizing a relativistic discriminator, demonstrating enhanced performance over previous methods when trained on datasets like CIFAR-10 and CAT at various image resolutions. The authors showcase the adaptability of RGAN, allowing it to be integrated with various GAN architectures.

SN-GAN introduces weight normalization to stabilize the training of the discriminator by controlling the Lipschitz constant through spectral normalization. This method is computationally efficient and enhances the stability of GAN training, as evidenced by its evaluation across multiple datasets including CIFAR-10, STL-10, and ImageNet. The results indicate improved diversity and quality in generated images compared to traditional normalization techniques.

Additionally, the paper discusses RealnessGAN, which diverges from the original GAN design by providing a continuous measure of realness instead of binary outputs. This approach aims to enhance the discriminator's capability to assess the authenticity of generated images more effectively. Overall, the survey emphasizes the evolution and effectiveness of various GAN architectures in the field of computer vision.
