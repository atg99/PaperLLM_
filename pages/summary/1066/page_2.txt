Generative adversarial networks (GANs) are a prominent focus in deep learning, with applications across various domains including computer vision and natural language processing. GANs stand out among generative models due to their ability to handle sharp density functions, generate samples efficiently, and eliminate deterministic bias, leading to significant success particularly in computer vision tasks like image generation and translation.

Despite their advantages, GANs face challenges, most notably in training and evaluation. Training issues include difficulty in achieving Nash equilibrium between the generator and discriminator, often resulting in mode collapse where the generator fails to capture the full dataset distribution. Evaluation challenges arise from the difficulty in accurately measuring the dissimilarity between real and generated distributions.

This paper aims to review existing GAN variants that address training challenges, focusing on improvements in image diversity, quality, and training stability. The review categorizes these improvements into architectural modifications and loss function adjustments. The article is structured to provide a comprehensive overview, including a discussion of related reviews, an introduction to GANs, a detailed examination of architecture-variant and loss-variant GANs, and a look at GAN-based applications in computer vision. It concludes with a summary of the reviewed variants and potential future research directions in the field.
