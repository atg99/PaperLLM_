The paper provides a comprehensive survey and taxonomy of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) within the field of computer vision. It discusses various GAN architectures, emphasizing their capabilities and limitations, particularly in handling label noise and improving performance in large-scale datasets.

One of the significant contributions discussed is the introduction of label-noise rGANs, which extend AC-GAN and CGAN to accommodate noisy training labels through a noise transition model. This model allows for learning a clean label conditional generative distribution, demonstrating improved performance on CIFAR-10 while showing robustness to label noise. However, the performance diminishes on more complex datasets like CIFAR-100 under high noise conditions, indicating a need for further research.

Additionally, the paper highlights the development of a new local sparse attention layer in Your Local GAN (YLG), which enhances the two-dimensional geometry and locality in GANs. This method, which replaces the dense attention layer in SAGAN, achieves superior performance and reduces training time significantly. The authors utilize an innovative approach called Enumerate, Shift, Apply (ESA) to adapt one-dimensional sparsifications to preserve two-dimensional locality.

Overall, the survey underscores the advancements in GAN architectures while identifying areas for future exploration, particularly in the context of noisy labels and complex datasets.
