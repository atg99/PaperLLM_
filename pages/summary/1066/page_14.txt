The paper discusses two advanced generative adversarial network (GAN) architectures: MSG-GAN and AutoGAN. MSG-GAN employs a progressive training approach akin to PROGAN, featuring connections between the intermediate layers of the generator and discriminator. It processes multi-scale images, concatenating them with corresponding main paths in the discriminator. This design aims for computational efficiency while maintaining effective information flow, although it faces the challenge of balancing these conflicting objectives.

AutoGAN introduces neural architecture search (NAS) to enhance GANs, utilizing an RNN to guide generator architectural variations. The training employs Inception score as a reward and implements a multi-level search strategy for progressive optimization. However, it presents challenges in integrating NAS with GANs, particularly due to the instability of GAN training and limitations in the generator search space. Furthermore, AutoGAN has not been tested on high-resolution datasets, leaving uncertainty about its broader applicability.

Overall, while both architectures show promising advancements in GAN technology, they also highlight critical areas needing further exploration, particularly regarding the limitations of their search spaces and the stability of training processes.
