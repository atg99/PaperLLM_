The Variational Autoencoder (VAE) generates a likelihood distribution p(x|z), parameterized by a neural network with weights denoted as θ, specifically in the decoder part represented as pθ(x|z). The encoder outputs parameters for the latent distribution, which is then sampled to serve as input for the decoder. 

The Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence between the approximate posterior distribution q(z|x) and the true posterior p(z|x) is defined mathematically. By employing Bayes' theorem, the KL divergence can be expressed in terms of the likelihood of the data given the latent variables and the prior distribution of the latent variables. 

This expression can be manipulated using logarithmic identities, breaking it down into two integrals. In the second integral, since log(p(xi)) is a constant, it can be factored out, leading to a simplification where the first integral represents the expected log likelihood term. The normalization property of the probability distribution q(z|x) allows for further simplification, ultimately demonstrating that the log likelihood of the observed data is bounded by the expected log likelihood of the model under the variational distribution. 

This framework illustrates the relationship between the variational approximation and the true posterior, highlighting the importance of the KL divergence in the optimization process of VAEs.
