The Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence is labeled a divergence rather than a metric due to its asymmetry, as metrics must be symmetric. Recent studies have introduced various symmetrization methods for KL divergence that enhance its generative performance. The KL divergence is inherently non-negative, which is mathematically demonstrated through its formulation. Specifically, the divergence DKL(q(x) || p(x)) is shown to be zero or positive, affirming its non-negativity.

The paper also discusses variational autoencoders (VAEs), which utilize the KL divergence in their framework. VAEs have numerous applications, including in the detailed analysis of diseases. In this context, the encoder generates an approximate posterior distribution q(z|x) and is represented by a neural network with specific weights. The discussion emphasizes the importance of the KL divergence in the functioning of VAEs and its implications for various applications.
