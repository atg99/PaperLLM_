The paper provides a glossary of key terms related to Bayesian statistics, including definitions for latent variables, evidence, prior and posterior probabilities, and likelihood probabilities. It introduces Bayes theorem, which relates conditional probabilities by dividing both sides of an equation by evidence probability.

The concept of Kullback-Leibler divergence is explored as a measure of similarity between two distributions in density estimation, a central task in generative models. It explains that information content is inversely related to the probability of an event; higher probabilities correspond to lower information content. This relationship is formalized through logarithmic expressions that quantify the information content of events with respect to different probability distributions.

The paper defines the Kullback-Leibler divergence as the expectation of the information difference between two distributions, providing mathematical formulations for both DKL(q(x) || p(x)) and DKL(p(x) || q(x)). It notes that this divergence is not symmetric, emphasizing that the expectation is taken with respect to different distributions in each case.
