The paper by Stephen G. Odaibo provides a tutorial on deriving the loss function for the standard Variational Autoencoder (VAE), emphasizing the role of variational inference in Bayesian machine learning. The tutorial highlights that due to the computational intractability of posterior distributions, maximizing an evidence lower bound on the log likelihood of data is essential during training.

The focus is on deriving the variational lower bound loss function under the assumptions of a Gaussian latent prior and a Gaussian approximate posterior. This specific scenario allows for a closed-form solution of the Kullback-Leibler divergence term in the variational lower bound. The paper methodically derives the necessary components, starting from Bayes' theorem to the Kullback-Leibler divergence, ensuring that all foundational elements are thoroughly explained.

Bayes' theorem serves as a critical framework for updating beliefs based on new evidence, with the paper detailing the mathematical formulation and implications of the theorem in a clear and structured manner. The author emphasizes the importance of understanding these derivations to grasp the underlying mechanics of VAEs and their optimization processes.
