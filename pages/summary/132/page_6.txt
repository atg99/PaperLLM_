The paper discusses the simplification of a logarithmic term in relation to the Kullback-Leibler divergence between two probability distributions, q and p. The derivation begins with a complex expression involving exponential functions and logarithms, which is progressively simplified through several equations.

Initially, the logarithmic term is expressed in terms of the variances and means of the distributions. The key transformation leads to the formulation of the Kullback-Leibler divergence, denoted as DKL(q || p), as an expectation. The expectation involves the log ratio of the two distributions and a term that incorporates the squared distances from their respective means.

The analysis reveals that the divergence can be expressed as the sum of a logarithmic term and a combination of expectations related to the variances of the distributions. Specifically, the final expression for the Kullback-Leibler divergence incorporates the variances and the expected squared distances from the means of both distributions.

The paper concludes by using the identity for the square of a sum to further clarify the relationship between the terms involved in the divergence calculation, emphasizing the role of the variances and the interplay between the means of the distributions. This structured approach highlights the mathematical intricacies of comparing probability distributions through the lens of information theory.
