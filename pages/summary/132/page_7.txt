The paper discusses the Kullback-Leibler divergence (DKL) between an approximate posterior distribution q(z|xi) and a prior distribution p(z). The derivation shows how DKL can be expressed in terms of the parameters of the distributions involved. The key expression derived is:

DKL(q(z|xi) || p(z)) = log(q) - (1/2) * (σ²_q + (μ_q - μ_p)²)

This equation emphasizes the relationship between the variances (σ²) and means (μ) of the distributions. The paper further connects this to the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO), derived from Equation (28):

log p(xi) = -DKL(q(z|xi) || p(z)) + E_q(z|xi)[log p(xi|z)]

The contribution of a single data point xi towards maximizing the objective function is then presented as:

(1/2) * [1 + log(σ²_j / (σ²_j - σ²_j))] + E_q(z|xi)[log p(xi|z)]

For a batch of data, the overall objective function G is formulated as:

G = Σ_j (1/2) * [1 + log(σ²_i / (σ²_i - σ²_i))] + (1/L) * Σ_l E_q(z|xi)[log p(xi|z(i;l))]

where J represents the dimension of the latent vector z, and L is the number of samples drawn using the reparameterization trick. This highlights the role of both the latent variables and the sampling process in optimizing the model.
