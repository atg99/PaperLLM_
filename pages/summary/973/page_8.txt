The paper "Quantifying Generalization in Reinforcement Learning" discusses the critical issue of generalization in the context of reinforcement learning (RL). It emphasizes the importance of understanding how RL agents perform on unseen tasks or states that differ from their training environment. The authors review a variety of strategies and frameworks aimed at enhancing generalization, including the use of procedural generation, domain randomization, and improved regularization techniques.

The paper highlights key methodologies for evaluating generalization, such as benchmarking against established environments like the Arcade Learning Environment, and the need for robust evaluation protocols to measure an agent's ability to adapt to new scenarios. It references several significant studies and frameworks that contribute to this discourse, including works on deep Q-networks, importance-weighted architectures, and the impact of determinism in learning.

The authors argue that a comprehensive understanding of generalization is essential for advancing RL research and developing agents that can perform reliably in diverse and dynamic environments. The paper concludes with a call for further investigation into the mechanisms of generalization, advocating for a systematic approach to quantifying and improving this aspect of RL.
