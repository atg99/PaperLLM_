The paper investigates the effects of introducing stochasticity into reinforcement learning environments to enhance generalization. It compares two approaches: epsilon-greedy action selection and policy entropy bonuses, against a baseline agent during training over 512 million timesteps. The results indicate that increasing stochasticity, either through the environment or the policy, significantly improves generalization performance. This improvement is more pronounced than traditional regularization methods, suggesting that stochasticity effectively counters overfitting.

Furthermore, the study explores the combination of various regularization techniques, such as data augmentation, batch normalization, and L2 regularization. While combining these methods yields slightly better test performance than using them individually, the overall impact is modest, indicating that they might be addressing similar issues related to generalization.

The paper also extends its findings to additional environments, specifically a variant of CoinRun called CoinRun-Platforms and a maze navigation task referred to as RandomMazes. The same experimental setup is applied to assess overfitting in these new contexts, using the IMPALA-CNN architecture along with LSTM for optimal exploration. Overall, the work highlights the importance of stochasticity in improving the robustness of reinforcement learning agents across different environments.
