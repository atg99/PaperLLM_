The paper explores the issue of generalization in reinforcement learning (RL) by analyzing agent performance in two environments: CoinRun-Platforms and RandomMazes. In CoinRun-Platforms, agents are trained over 2 billion timesteps, where they must collect coins while avoiding moving monsters. The findings illustrate that overfitting occurs with around 4000 training levels. Interestingly, after this point, agents' training performance improves with additional levels, contrary to the typical behavior observed in supervised learning. This improvement is attributed to an implicit curriculum in the level distribution, allowing agents to learn generalizable skills.

In RandomMazes, agents navigate randomly generated mazes, facing significant overfitting and a generalization gap even after training on 20,000 levels. The environment's partial observability and the agent's reliance on memory complicate generalization, raising questions about the effectiveness of LSTMs in such tasks. While agents eventually converge to an optimal policy with enough data, their generalization performance remains a concern, suggesting that alternative recurrent architectures may be more effective.

The conclusion emphasizes the importance of quantifying overfitting in RL and suggests that insights gained from these environments could inform the development of more generalizable agents in complex settings. The study advocates for the use of procedurally generated environments as benchmarks to refine RL methodologies.
