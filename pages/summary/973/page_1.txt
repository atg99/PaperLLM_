In summary, the paper "Quantifying Generalization in Reinforcement Learning" by Cobbe et al. tackles the issue of overfitting in deep reinforcement learning by introducing a new benchmark environment called CoinRun. The authors argue that traditional RL benchmarks often use the same environments for both training and testing, limiting insights into an agent's generalization capabilities. Their findings reveal that agents overfit significantly to training environments, underscoring the necessity for larger training sets to achieve better generalization.

The paper contributes by proposing a generalization metric based on CoinRun and demonstrating that deeper convolutional architectures and regularization techniques—such as L2 regularization, dropout, and data augmentation—can enhance generalization performance. The authors highlight the importance of separating training and test environments for a more accurate assessment of an agent's ability to generalize across tasks, aligning with the goals of previous benchmarks like the Sonic Benchmark. Overall, this work seeks to advance the understanding and evaluation of generalization in reinforcement learning.
