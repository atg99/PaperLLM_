This paper investigates the quantification of generalization in reinforcement learning (RL), focusing on various regularization techniques and their effects on performance. The authors analyze the impact of L2 weight penalty and dropout probability on training and test performance over 256 million and 512 million timesteps, respectively. Results indicate that both L2 regularization and dropout reduce the generalization gap, with optimal parameters identified as a dropout probability of 0.1 and an L2 weight of 0.0001.

Additionally, the paper explores data augmentation, employing a modified Cutout method to mask rectangular regions of input observations. This approach shows significant improvements in performance, particularly in the CoinRun environment. The effectiveness of data augmentation is acknowledged to vary across different datasets.

The study also examines batch normalization, which is integrated into the IMPALA-CNN architecture. Results demonstrate that batch normalization enhances generalization by normalizing the statistics of current batches during training and utilizing moving averages during testing.

Lastly, the authors evaluate the role of stochasticity in generalization by manipulating both environmental and policy stochasticity through epsilon-greedy action selection. This comprehensive analysis highlights the importance of regularization strategies in improving generalization in RL settings.
