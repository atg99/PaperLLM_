The study focuses on quantifying generalization in reinforcement learning by evaluating agent performance under varying training conditions. The researchers conducted experiments using both unbounded and fixed sets of training levels, revealing that the IMPALA-CNN architecture demonstrates significantly better sample efficiency and generalization capabilities.

While training on an unbounded set of levels prevents overfitting, it may not always correlate with improved generalization. Hence, the authors argue that fixed-level training offers a more reliable measure of generalization performance. Their findings indicate that the IMPALA-CNN outperforms other architectures in generalization tasks, and further improvements were observed with the development of the IMPALA-Large variant, which features a deeper structure and increased channel capacity.

The paper also explores the role of regularization techniques, which have traditionally been more relevant in supervised learning contexts. Given the established effectiveness of methods such as L2 regularization, dropout, data augmentation, and batch normalization in reducing generalization gaps, the authors aim to assess their impact in the reinforcement learning domain, specifically within the CoinRun environment using a consistent training set of 500 levels.

The overall conclusion emphasizes the importance of both architectural design and regularization in enhancing generalization performance in reinforcement learning, suggesting potential avenues for future research to optimize these elements further.
