The study investigates the generalization of reinforcement learning agents trained on varying levels of complexity in the CoinRun environment. The performance of agents is assessed based on their ability to handle unseen test levels after being trained on a set of training levels. This assessment highlights the importance of the number of training levels in reducing overfitting, with results showing that agents trained on fewer than 4,000 levels exhibit significant overfitting, while those trained on an unbounded set achieve optimal performance.

The agents are trained using the Proximal Policy Optimization algorithm for 256 million timesteps across multiple workers, and their performance is measured by averaging results over numerous episodes. The findings suggest that while increasing the number of training levels improves test performance, the architecture of the agent also plays a crucial role in its generalization ability. The IMPALA architecture demonstrates superior performance compared to the baseline Nature-CNN, underscoring the impact of algorithmic and architectural choices on reinforcement learning generalization.
