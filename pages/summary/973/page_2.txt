The paper discusses the quantification of generalization in reinforcement learning (RL) through the introduction of the CoinRun environment, designed to evaluate the performance of trained agents in collecting a coin while navigating obstacles. The research builds on previous works that highlight the issues of overfitting in RL agents, noting that performance can significantly degrade when agents are tested on environments that differ from their training distributions.

Prior studies, such as those by Zhang et al. and Justesen et al., have explored generalization by varying training and testing conditions, including the use of procedural generation to create diverse environments. These studies found that agents often overfit to specific training distributions, with their ability to generalize to new levels depending on the training conditions. Techniques to mitigate overfitting, such as sticky actions and random starts, were shown to be ineffective in many cases.

The CoinRun environment allows for a systematic investigation of generalization performance, utilizing a deterministic level generation approach that provides a large and quantifiable dataset for training. The design of CoinRun, with its varying level difficulties, creates a natural curriculum for agents, enabling the evaluation of their generalization capabilities. The paper emphasizes the necessity for experimental protocols that effectively address generalization issues in RL, and the findings suggest that injecting stochasticity and employing various methods could improve generalization outcomes. Overall, the work contributes to understanding the complexities of generalization in reinforcement learning and proposes a structured framework for future research in this area.
