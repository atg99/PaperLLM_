The paper presents an ablation study of FlexMatch, a method designed to improve the performance of semi-supervised learning. The authors compare FlexMatch with FixMatch, highlighting FlexMatch's superior convergence speed and overall accuracy on datasets like CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100. 

Key findings include that FlexMatch achieves a notable decrease in loss and higher accuracy at earlier training stages compared to FixMatch, demonstrating its effectiveness in learning difficult classes through a component called Class Proportional Learning (CPL). The paper also investigates critical components of FlexMatch, such as the optimal threshold τ and different mapping functions. Results indicate that an optimal τ value of around 0.95 maximizes performance, while the convex mapping function outperforms other types.

The authors conclude that FlexMatch not only accelerates convergence but also improves class learning, suggesting its potential for broader applications in semi-supervised learning contexts.
