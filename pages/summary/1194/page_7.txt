This paper presents a comparative analysis of two semi-supervised learning algorithms, FixMatch and FlexMatch, particularly focusing on their performance on various datasets including CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, STL-10, and ImageNet. The results indicate that FlexMatch significantly outperforms FixMatch across multiple metrics.

In CIFAR-100 with only 400 labeled samples, FlexMatch achieves an average error rate of 39.94%, compared to FixMatch's 46.42%. The analysis includes convergence graphs that illustrate loss and top-1 accuracy, demonstrating FlexMatch's superior performance in both metrics. Similarly, on the STL-10 dataset, FlexMatch reduces the error rate from 37.4% to 29.53% when combined with the proposed Class Pseudo-Labeling (CPL) technique, showcasing its effectiveness in leveraging unlabeled data.

The paper also discusses the performance of FlexMatch on the SVHN dataset and notes that its effectiveness is hindered by the dataset's simplicity and class imbalance, which leads to unreliable learning for certain classes. In contrast, FixMatch's fixed high threshold helps mitigate noise in this easier task.

Furthermore, the results on ImageNet show FlexMatch achieving an error rate of 42.02% for top-1 accuracy, outperforming FixMatch's 43.66%. The findings suggest that CPL not only enhances performance on existing SSL algorithms but does so without imposing additional computational costs, as indicated by the average running time analysis.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the advantages of FlexMatch and CPL in improving semi-supervised learning, particularly in challenging tasks and datasets, while also addressing the limitations encountered in simpler datasets.
