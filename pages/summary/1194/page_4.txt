In this paper, the authors introduce Curriculum Pseudo Labeling (CPL) for semi-supervised learning, aiming to improve the estimation of learning status without requiring additional inference processes or a validation set. The key premise is that a high threshold can filter out noisy pseudo labels, thus reducing confirmation bias. The learning effect of a class is assessed by the number of samples with predictions exceeding this threshold. This is mathematically represented as σt(c), which indicates the learning status of class c at time t based on the model's predictions for unlabeled data.

To normalize this learning effect between 0 and 1, the authors propose a scaling factor βt(c), which is then used to adjust the threshold Tt(c) for each class. This normalization allows the model to dynamically adjust thresholds based on the learning progress, encouraging further training on challenging classes while selecting high-quality samples from well-learned classes.

The flexible thresholds are incorporated into the unsupervised loss calculation in the FlexMatch framework. The overall loss function combines supervised and unsupervised losses, with the inclusion of CPL being resource-efficient since it does not require additional forward passes or parameters.

Additionally, the authors address early-stage training challenges, where models may exhibit confirmation bias by predicting most unlabeled samples into a single class. To mitigate this, they propose a warm-up process that modifies the normalization denominator, enhancing the reliability of the learning status estimation during initial training phases.
