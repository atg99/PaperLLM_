This paper addresses the limitations of existing semi-supervised learning (SSL) algorithms, such as FixMatch, Pseudo-Labeling, and Unsupervised Data Augmentation, which utilize a fixed threshold to select high-confidence predictions from unlabeled data. This approach can lead to the exclusion of valuable unlabeled data, particularly in the early stages of training, and does not account for the varying learning difficulties associated with different classes.

To overcome these challenges, the authors propose Curriculum Pseudo Labeling (CPL), a curriculum learning strategy that dynamically adjusts thresholds for each class based on its current learning status. Importantly, CPL does not require additional parameters or computational overhead, thus maintaining training efficiency. The integration of CPL with FixMatch results in a new algorithm called FlexMatch, which demonstrates significantly faster convergence and improved performance across various SSL image classification benchmarks.

FlexMatch shows marked enhancements over FixMatch, achieving performance gains of 18.96%, 16.11%, and 7.68% on the STL-10 dataset with label counts of 400, 2500, and 10000, respectively. Additionally, FlexMatch reaches its final accuracy in less than one-fifth of the training time compared to FixMatch. The authors also note that applying CPL to other modern SSL algorithms yields similar improvements in accuracy and convergence speed.

The paper concludes with three key contributions: the introduction of CPL as a cost-effective and easily integrable method for leveraging unlabeled data in SSL, the demonstration of significant performance enhancements in popular SSL algorithms, and the release of TorchSSL, a unified PyTorch-based codebase for SSL research that facilitates the study and customization of various algorithms.
