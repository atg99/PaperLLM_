Notably, FlexMatch shows superior performance particularly when labeled data is scarce, demonstrating its effectiveness in semi-supervised learning scenarios.

The paper evaluates FlexMatch alongside other algorithms such as Pseudo-Labeling, UDA, and FixMatch across multiple datasets, including CIFAR-10/100, SVHN, and STL-10. The results indicate that FlexMatch achieves state-of-the-art performance in most benchmarks, although UDA performs competitively on specific splits of the SVHN dataset. The experiments utilize a consistent methodology, including identical hyper-parameters and data augmentation techniques, to ensure fair comparisons.

The authors argue that traditional median error rates may misrepresent the performance of fast-converging algorithms, thus they also report the best error rates achieved during the training process. The findings highlight the advantages of FlexMatch, particularly in scenarios with limited labeled data, reinforcing its potential as a leading approach in semi-supervised learning. Detailed evaluations, including precision, recall, F1 score, and AUC metrics, are provided in the appendix, supporting the claims about the effectiveness of FlexMatch.
