The paper introduces FlexMatch, an enhancement of the FixMatch algorithm for semi-supervised learning (SSL). While FixMatch has achieved state-of-the-art results, it utilizes a fixed threshold for selecting informative unlabeled data across all classes, which does not account for the varying learning statuses and difficulties inherent to different classes. To address this limitation, the authors propose Curriculum Pseudo Labeling (CPL), a method that dynamically adjusts thresholds for each class based on the model's current learning state. This approach allows for more effective utilization of unlabeled data without adding extra parameters or computational demands.

FlexMatch, which integrates CPL with FixMatch, shows significant improvements in performance across various SSL benchmarks, particularly in scenarios with limited labeled data or challenging tasks. Notably, FlexMatch reduces error rates by 13.96% and 18.96% on CIFAR-100 and STL-10 datasets, respectively, when only four labels per class are used. Additionally, FlexMatch accelerates convergence, requiring only one-fifth of the training time compared to FixMatch to achieve superior results. The authors also demonstrate that CPL can be adapted to other SSL algorithms, enhancing their performance as well. The code for FlexMatch is available as open-source, promoting further research in this area.
