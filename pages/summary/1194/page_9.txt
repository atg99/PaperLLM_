The paper discusses the implementation of Curriculum Pseudo Labeling (CPL) to enhance the performance of semi-supervised learning (SSL) algorithms. A key aspect of CPL is the use of threshold warm-up, which mitigates noisy training by gradually increasing thresholds from zero. This approach leads to improved utilization of unlabeled data during the early stages of training, resulting in performance gains of 0.2% on CIFAR-10 and 1% on CIFAR-100 datasets.

The authors also compare CPL with class balancing objectives, demonstrating that while both methods aim to balance unlabeled samples across classes, CPL offers greater flexibility without the need for strict class distribution within batches. The paper highlights the effectiveness of CPL as a simple, cost-effective method that significantly enhances the performance and convergence speed of SSL algorithms.

In the context of related work, the authors reference various approaches in pseudo-labeling and curriculum learning, noting the reliance of previous methods on strong data augmentations and the challenges of dynamic thresholds in other domains. The conclusion emphasizes that FlexMatch, the improved version of FixMatch utilizing CPL, achieves state-of-the-art results across multiple SSL benchmarks. Future research directions include addressing challenges related to long-tail distributions of unlabeled data.
