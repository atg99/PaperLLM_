The references provided encompass a range of studies focused on human activity recognition (HAR) utilizing various technologies, particularly wearable sensors and smartphones. 

Foerster et al. (1999) conducted a validation study on detecting posture and motion through accelerometry, establishing foundational techniques in ambulatory monitoring. Mannini and Sabatini (2010) explored machine learning methods for classifying physical activities from on-body accelerometers, contributing to the understanding of activity recognition frameworks.

Yang et al. (2015) advanced the field by applying deep convolutional neural networks to multichannel time series data for HAR, showcasing the potential of deep learning in this domain. Rashidi and Cook (2009) emphasized user adaptation in smart homes, highlighting the need for integrating user feedback in automated systems.

Magherini et al. (2013) utilized temporal logic and model checking for recognizing human activities in ambient-assisted living, demonstrating a systematic approach to HAR. Cornacchia et al. (2017) provided a comprehensive survey on activity detection and classification using wearable sensors, summarizing key methodologies and findings.

Several studies focused on specific algorithms and techniques for HAR, including Anguita et al. (2012) who employed support vector machines for smartphone-based recognition, and Peterek et al. (2014) who compared classification algorithms for physical activity recognition.

The development of automatic annotation methods for free-living activity recognition was investigated by Cruciani et al. (2018), while Jetley et al. (2018) discussed attention mechanisms in learning processes. Witten et al. (1999) introduced Weka, a tool for practical machine learning applications, facilitating research in HAR.

Feature selection techniques were addressed by Pirttikangas et al. (2006), and combining classifiers was investigated by Todorovski and Dzeroski (2003). The application of cell phone accelerometers for activity recognition was showcased by Kwapisz et al. (2011), while portable activity detectors were developed by Kao et al. (2009).

Deep learning approaches were further explored by Ronao and Cho (2015), who applied convolutional neural networks for HAR using smartphone sensors, and by Jiang and Yin (2015), who also utilized deep learning methods. Ordóñez and Roggen (2016) combined deep convolutional and recurrent neural networks for multimodal activity recognition.

Shoaib et al. (2015) conducted a survey on online activity recognition using mobile phones, outlining the advancements in real-time recognition systems. Bahdanau et al. (2014) and Xu et al. (2015) contributed to the broader machine learning field with their work on neural translation and image captioning, respectively.

Lastly, Anguita et al. (2013) provided a public domain dataset for smartphone-based HAR, facilitating further research and development in the field. Overall, these studies collectively contribute to the understanding and advancement of human activity recognition technologies.
