The paper presents a comparison between two models, GF-VAE and a traditional VAE-based model, specifically focusing on their encoder and decoder configurations. In the analysis, the authors illustrate how GF-VAE, which employs a flow-based decoder, outperforms the standard model using a linear neural network decoder in terms of efficiency and performance.

The results indicate that GF-VAE consistently achieves better scores than MoFlow, a competing model, even with a smaller model size and reduced training time. For instance, with the combination of parameters (LB, LA) set to (6, 6), GF-VAE reaches a score of 90% in 35.9 seconds per epoch, while MoFlow achieves the same score with significantly longer training times and larger parameter sizes.

Further experiments using the ZINC-250K dataset reveal that GF-VAE excels in generating molecular graphs, outperforming MoFlow across various configurations. Specifically, GF-VAE achieves a score of 84.4% compared to MoFlow's maximum score of 56.4%, particularly during the early training epochs. These findings highlight the model's capability to effectively explore the latent space of molecular structures.

In conclusion, GF-VAE not only reconstructs input molecular graphs but also generates high-quality, unique molecular structures efficiently, demonstrating its superiority over traditional VAE models in terms of both performance and time efficiency.
