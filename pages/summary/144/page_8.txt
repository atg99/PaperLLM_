The paper compares two molecular graph encoders, pT and GF-VAE, in terms of their ability to learn atom embedding vectors. The authors trained both models on the QM9 dataset for 100 epochs and analyzed the cosine similarity of atom embeddings using heatmaps. For molecular graph (a), pT encodes a cluster of atoms too closely, failing to differentiate between unconnected atoms, while GF-VAE maintains a more appropriate representation by considering both graph connectivity and intrinsic features. In molecular graph (b), pT struggles to distinguish between different atoms within a ring structure, while GF-VAE effectively captures the relationships among ring atoms and nearby atoms.

The paper emphasizes the importance of a smooth latent space for biological applications, allowing for property-driven molecular generation. The authors demonstrate the smoothness of the latent space through grid searches and interpolation, showing that neighboring latent points correspond to similar molecules. Unlike other VAE models, GF-VAE's deterministic decoding allows for efficient mapping of latent points to molecular graphs.

The optimization of molecules is discussed, focusing on two branches: property optimization and constrained property optimization. The authors outline a three-step process involving training GF-VAE, developing a regressor for target chemical properties, and conducting a gradient ascent search for optimal molecules. They utilize metrics like Quantitative Estimate of Druglikeness (QED) and penalized logP (plogp) for property evaluation and measure molecular similarity using Tanimoto similarity of Morgan fingerprints. Overall, GF-VAE demonstrates superior performance in learning meaningful atom embeddings and optimizing molecular properties compared to pT.
