The paper presents an evaluation of various models for molecular generation and reconstruction performance, focusing on two datasets: QM9 and ZINC-250K. The results, summarized in Tables 1 and 2, indicate that flow-based models, particularly GF-VAE and MoFlow, achieve 100% reconstruction accuracy, outperforming VAE-based models. GF-VAE shows notable improvements over other models like GraphAF and one-shot models such as GraphNVP and GRF, particularly in terms of uniqueness ratio and reconstruction accuracy.

In terms of time efficiency, GF-VAE outperforms MoFlow, taking significantly less time to train on both datasets. Specifically, GF-VAE requires 2.3 hours for QM9 and 8.2 hours for ZINC-250K, compared to MoFlow's 3.3 hours and 22 hours, respectively. This efficiency is attributed to GF-VAE's design, which incorporates a VAE-encoder to minimize model size while maintaining performance.

The study also includes an analysis of model size versus performance, highlighting the advantages of GF-VAE's structure in achieving effective molecular generation with reduced computational demands. Overall, the findings suggest that GF-VAE is a competitive option for molecular generation tasks, offering both high performance and efficiency.
