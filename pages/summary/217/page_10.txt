The paper discusses the concept of invertible residual networks, which are a class of neural networks designed to facilitate efficient training and inference through invertibility. The authors highlight how these networks can be utilized in various applications, including generative modeling and semi-supervised learning. They draw on previous works that emphasize the importance of invertible architectures in enhancing model robustness and reducing adversarial vulnerabilities.

Key references include the introduction of generative flow models with invertible convolutions, normalization techniques for generative adversarial networks, and variational inference methods that leverage normalizing flows. The paper also cites studies that explore the relationship between deep learning and differential equations, showcasing the theoretical underpinnings that support the design of these networks.

The authors argue that excessive invariance can lead to adversarial vulnerabilities, suggesting that a balance between expressiveness and stability is crucial in network design. The paper concludes by emphasizing the potential of invertible residual networks to bridge the gap between deep architectures and robust learning, paving the way for future research in this area.
