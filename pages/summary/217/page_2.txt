The paper discusses Invertible Residual Networks (i-ResNets), highlighting their ability to leverage unconstrained architectures while maintaining invertibility by ensuring that the Lipschitz constant of each residual block is less than one. This condition allows i-ResNets to perform comparably to standard ResNets in image classification tasks across datasets such as MNIST, CIFAR10, and CIFAR100.

The authors introduce a method for training i-ResNets as maximum likelihood generative models on unlabeled data by approximating the Jacobian determinant of a residual block. This approach, similar to FFJORD, permits free-form Jacobians, enabling the networks to learn more complex transformations than those achievable by triangular mappings prevalent in other reversible models.

The paper establishes a theoretical foundation for the invertibility of ResNets, drawing parallels to Euler's method for ordinary differential equations (ODEs) and presenting a theorem that outlines a sufficient condition for invertibility. This condition requires the Lipschitz constant of the residual block to be less than one, allowing for the implementation of a fixed-point iteration to find the inverse. 

The authors also discuss the stability guarantees of both forward and inverse mappings due to the bi-Lipschitz nature of the residual layers when the Lipschitz condition is satisfied. They emphasize that the design of i-ResNets involves using contractive nonlinearities and linear mappings to meet the Lipschitz constraint, ensuring both performance and stability in the learning process.

Overall, the paper presents i-ResNets as a competitive architecture for both image classification and generative modeling, contributing to the ongoing development of general-purpose deep learning models.
