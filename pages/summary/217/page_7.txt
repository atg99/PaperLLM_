The paper discusses the development and evaluation of invertible Residual Networks (i-ResNets), particularly focusing on their classification and generative modeling capabilities. A key comparison is made with a baseline ResNet-164 architecture, examining the impact of varying Lipschitz constraints on performance metrics across datasets such as MNIST, CIFAR10, and CIFAR100. The results indicate that while invertibility without constraints is challenging, the proposed i-ResNet model can guarantee the existence of an inverse while maintaining competitive classification performance.

The authors also compare i-ResNets to the state-of-the-art flow-based model Glow, finding that i-ResNets outperform Glow in CIFAR10 classification tasks. This suggests that i-ResNets have a more suitable inductive bias for discriminative tasks. Additionally, i-ResNets are noted to be easier to train compared to Glow models, which require more complex training strategies.

In terms of generative modeling, the paper presents experiments with i-ResNets on simple two-dimensional datasets and images from MNIST and CIFAR10. The results show that i-ResNets can accurately fit densities and generate images effectively, leveraging their architecture to enhance performance. Overall, the findings demonstrate the potential of i-ResNets in both classification and generative tasks, highlighting their advantages over traditional models.
