The paper discusses the introduction of invertible Residual Networks (i-ResNets), which combine generative modeling with discriminative tasks, achieving competitive performance in both areas. The authors provide results comparing i-ResNets with various generative models on datasets like MNIST and CIFAR10, noting that while i-ResNets do not outperform models like Glow and FFJORD, they present a compelling alternative due to their simplicity and effectiveness.

Key findings include the performance metrics for MNIST and CIFAR10, where i-ResNets achieved bits/dim results of 1.06 and 3.45, respectively. The paper highlights that the performance gap is likely due to the use of a biased log-determinant estimator, suggesting that adopting an unbiased estimator could enhance results. 

The paper also outlines potential applications for i-ResNets, including joint classification and detection, unsupervised learning, semi-supervised learning, inverse problems, and adversarial robustness. The authors note that the Lipschitz bounds on i-ResNet layers could improve gradient stability and adversarial robustness.

In conclusion, the i-ResNet architecture allows for flexible layer designs while providing tractable density estimates. The authors acknowledge challenges, including the need for unbiased log-determinant estimators and the difficulty of enforcing Lipschitz constraints on network layers, indicating areas for future research.
