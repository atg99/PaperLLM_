The paper discusses the development and analysis of invertible residual networks, which are a class of neural network architectures designed to facilitate deep learning by enabling efficient training and inference without the need to store intermediate activations. The authors highlight the importance of these networks in solving inverse problems and improving stability in deep learning models. The work is supported by financial contributions from the German Science Foundation and involves various references to existing literature on neural networks, numerical methods, and approximation techniques. The paper acknowledges contributions from discussions within the research community, emphasizing collaborative efforts in advancing the understanding of invertible architectures.
