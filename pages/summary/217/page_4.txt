The paper discusses Invertible Residual Networks (i-ResNets) and their properties, particularly focusing on the computation of the log-determinant of the Jacobian associated with these networks. The authors utilize a power series approach to express the log-determinant, which converges under certain conditions related to the Lipschitz constant of the residual blocks. 

Key findings include that both the number of layers and the Lipschitz constant significantly impact the contraction and expansion bounds of i-ResNets. The paper outlines the computational challenges of calculating the log-determinant using the power series, including the high computational cost of exact Jacobian computations and the need for matrix powers.

To address these issues, the authors propose a stochastic approximation method for estimating the log-determinant. This involves using vector-Jacobian products computed efficiently through reverse-mode automatic differentiation and employing Hutchinson's trace estimator to estimate the trace of matrix powers.

The paper also examines the truncation of the power series to manage computational costs, noting that while truncation introduces bias, the bias can be bounded. The authors provide error bounds for both the log-determinant approximation and the gradient approximation, highlighting the relationship between the truncation error, the data dimension, the Lipschitz constant, and the number of terms in the series.

In summary, the work presents a framework for effectively calculating the log-determinant in i-ResNets, emphasizing the importance of Lipschitz constraints and offering methods to mitigate computational drawbacks while ensuring convergence and stability during optimization.
