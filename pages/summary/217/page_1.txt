The paper introduces Invertible Residual Networks (i-ResNets), which enhance standard ResNet architectures by making them invertible. This innovation allows a single model to effectively perform classification, density estimation, and generative tasks without the need for complex architectural modifications or dimension partitioning. The authors achieve invertibility by incorporating a straightforward normalization step during training, which is already part of existing frameworks.

To support training, the paper presents a tractable approximation for calculating the Jacobian log-determinant of a residual block, facilitating maximum likelihood training on unlabeled data. The empirical results demonstrate that i-ResNets can compete with leading image classifiers and flow-based generative models, a feat not previously accomplished with a single architecture.

The introduction highlights the challenge of selecting appropriate architectures for different tasks due to the increasing specialization of models in unsupervised learning and the divergence between architectures for discriminative and generative tasks. By focusing on reversible networks, the authors bridge this gap, showing that traditional fixed dimension splitting methods can hinder performance. They leverage the relationship between ResNets and ordinary differential equations to establish that invertible ResNets can be created simply by modifying the normalization scheme of standard ResNets.
