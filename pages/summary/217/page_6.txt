This paper presents a comparative study of invertible Residual Networks (i-ResNets) against various existing methods such as ResNet, NICE, Real-NVP, Glow, and FFJORD. The authors assess several characteristics of these networks, including their ability to be free-form, perform analytic forward and inverse mappings, maintain non-volume preserving properties, compute exact likelihood, and utilize unbiased stochastic log-determinant estimators.

The experimental section validates the invertibility of i-ResNets and evaluates their performance on image classification tasks using datasets like CIFAR10, CIFAR100, and MNIST. The authors meticulously train both i-ResNets and standard ResNets, ensuring bijectivity by employing "invertible downsampling" techniques. They find that i-ResNets demonstrate competitive classification performance, particularly with larger layer-wise Lipschitz constants, while maintaining provable invertibility.

The results highlight that standard ResNets are generally not invertible, as evidenced by reconstruction attempts that fail without Lipschitz constraints. Interestingly, some unconstrained ResNets become invertible after training on MNIST, but not on more complex datasets like CIFAR10 and CIFAR100. The paper also discusses the runtime efficiency of the models, indicating that the reconstruction process requires only a fraction of a second for practical applications.

Overall, the findings underscore the potential of i-ResNets for both classification and generative modeling, while emphasizing the significance of normalization techniques for maintaining invertibility in deep learning architectures.
