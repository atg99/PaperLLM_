The paper discusses a distributed reinforcement learning (RL) system implemented in Suphx for training agents to play Mahjong. It highlights the sensitivity of RL training to the entropy of the policy, noting that low entropy can lead to quick convergence without significant policy improvement, while high entropy can cause instability and high variance in the learned policy. To address this, the authors propose a regularization method for policy entropy during training. 

The entropy of the policy is adjusted dynamically based on its deviation from a target entropy, ensuring stable exploration. The system architecture features multiple self-play workers equipped with CPU-based Mahjong simulators and GPU-based inference engines to generate game trajectories. The policy updates are managed separately through a parameter server, which utilizes a replay buffer to maintain policy stability. Each simulator initializes games with the RL agent and sends state information to the inference engines to determine actions, ensuring that the self-play policy remains aligned with the latest updates from the parameter server.
