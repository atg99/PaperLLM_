The paper discusses the training of agents using reinforcement learning (RL) with a dataset of 1.5 million games. Each agent's training requires substantial computational resources, utilizing 44 GPUs over two days. The results indicate that the RL-basic approach outperforms supervised learning (SL), while RL-1 further improves upon RL-basic, and RL-2 provides additional enhancements over RL-1. This underscores the effectiveness of reinforcement learning, particularly with the incorporation of global reward prediction and oracle guidance.

The statistics of stable rank over one million games are illustrated, showing quartile values and extreme outliers. The global reward predictor allows the trained agent to focus on maximizing the overall game reward rather than just the immediate round score. An example is provided where the agent, despite having a strong position in the last round, opts for a conservative strategy to secure the highest overall rank rather than risking a loss by playing aggressively. In contrast, RL-basic's aggressive play leads to a potential drop in rank. The paper highlights the importance of understanding game dynamics and reward structures in training effective agents.
