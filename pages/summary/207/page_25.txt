The referenced works focus on advancements in artificial intelligence, particularly in reinforcement learning and its applications to strategic games. 

1. Silver et al. (2016) presented a method for mastering the game of Go using deep neural networks combined with tree search techniques, demonstrating the potential of AI in complex decision-making environments.

2. In 2018, Silver et al. introduced a general reinforcement learning algorithm capable of mastering chess, shogi, and Go through self-play, highlighting the versatility and effectiveness of self-learning in AI.

3. The 2017 study by Silver et al. showcased the ability to master Go without human knowledge, emphasizing the importance of self-play and exploration in developing advanced AI strategies.

4. Sutton et al. (2000) discussed policy gradient methods, which are crucial for reinforcement learning, particularly when function approximation is involved, laying foundational concepts for subsequent AI developments.

5. Tesauro (1995) explored temporal difference learning in the context of TD-Gammon, illustrating early applications of reinforcement learning in game environments.

6. Tsunoda's Tenhou and Dwango Media Village's NAGA project represent further applications of AI in games like Mahjong, showcasing the growing interest in AI-driven gameplay.

7. Vinyals et al. (2019) demonstrated the capability of AI to reach grandmaster levels in StarCraft II through multi-agent reinforcement learning, indicating the progression of AI in complex real-time strategy games.

These works collectively demonstrate the evolution of AI techniques, particularly reinforcement learning, and their successful application in mastering various strategic games, paving the way for future developments in AI.
