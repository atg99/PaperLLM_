Mahjong is a tile-based game originating from China, now enjoyed globally, particularly in its Japanese variant known as Riichi Mahjong. This version is notable for its professional league in Japan and has well-defined, accepted rules. The game comprises 136 tiles, categorized into three suits (Bamboo, Character, Dot) numbered 1 to 9 and seven Honour tiles. Each game involves multiple rounds, concluding when a player loses all points or meets a winning condition.

Players begin with 25,000 points, and one is designated as the dealer. Tiles are shuffled into four walls, and 52 tiles are dealt to players, with 14 forming a dead wall and the remaining 70 making up the live wall. Players draw and discard tiles in turns, aiming to create a complete hand consisting of four melds and a pair. Melds can be Pongs (three identical tiles), Kongs (four identical tiles), or Chows (three consecutive tiles of the same suit). A pair consists of two identical tiles and cannot be mixed with melds. 

A yaku, a specific tile pattern or condition, is crucial for determining the round score, with different yaku values based on the patterns achieved. Winning hands can feature multiple yaku, enhancing the scoring complexity.
