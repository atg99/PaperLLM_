The paper discusses the training and evaluation of various Mahjong agents using supervised and reinforcement learning techniques. It presents a comparison of different models based on their data size, test accuracy, and previous accuracy. The results indicate that the Chow model achieved the highest accuracy at 95.0%, while the Riichi model, with a smaller data size, recorded an accuracy of 85.7%.

In the reinforcement learning section, several agents were trained, including a supervised learning agent (SL) and its under-trained counterpart (SL-weak). The basic reinforcement learning agent (RL-basic) utilized the SL discard model and incorporated policy gradient methods for improvement. The RL-1 agent built upon RL-basic by adding global reward prediction, while RL-2 further enhanced it with oracle guiding.

To evaluate the agents, the study generated one million games, with each agent competing against SL-weak agents. The evaluation utilized stable ranks, computed from 800,000 sampled games over 1,000 iterations. The interquartile ranges of stable ranks were analyzed to assess the performance of the agents, ensuring a fair comparison by maintaining consistent models for the non-discard aspects.
