Recent studies in game AI have progressed from simple perfect-information games to more complex imperfect-information multi-player games. Mahjong, a popular tile-based game played by millions, presents significant challenges for AI development due to its intricate rules and mechanics.

Firstly, Mahjong features complicated scoring systems. With multiple rounds, the final score is derived from accumulated round scores, making it difficult to assess a player's performance based solely on individual round results. This complexity is heightened by the diverse winning hands, each yielding different scores, requiring players to strategically balance winning probability against potential scores.

Secondly, the game's structure includes hidden information, with each player holding up to 13 private tiles and additional tiles remaining concealed throughout the match. This creates an astronomical number of hidden states, complicating decision-making as players must infer the actions of others based on limited visible information. Consequently, connecting reward signals to observable actions is a challenge for AI.

Lastly, Mahjong's playing rules are complex, involving various actions and potential interruptions to the standard turn order. The unpredictability of player actions, influenced by private tiles, complicates the construction of a game tree, limiting the effectiveness of established techniques like Monte-Carlo tree search and counterfactual regret minimization.

In summary, the intricacies of Mahjong's scoring, hidden information, and complex rules pose significant obstacles for AI development, distinguishing it from previously studied games.
