This paper discusses the encoding of private tiles in a Mahjong game using a structured approach across four channels, where each channel represents a specific aspect of the player's hand. The encoding consists of four rows and 34 columns, with each row corresponding to a channel and each column indicating the presence of various tile types. The objective is to calculate look-ahead features that estimate the probability of winning a hand based on potential discards and draws from the wall.

In Japanese Mahjong, a winning hand comprises 14 tiles, including four melds and one pair, with numerous combinations leading to various winning hands and scores. Due to the complexity of these combinations, the authors implement simplifications: they utilize depth-first search to identify possible winning hands and focus solely on the actions of their own agent, disregarding opponents' moves. This results in over 100 look-ahead features, each represented as a 34-dimensional vector.

The architecture of the models used in this system (discard, Riichi, Chow, Pong, Kong) is consistent, differing primarily in the input and output dimensions. The discard model outputs 34 neurons for the unique tiles, while the other models have two output neurons indicating action decisions. Notably, the models lack pooling layers to preserve the semantic meaning of each column in the channel, as pooling could lead to the loss of critical information.
