In Mahjong, a game consists of multiple rounds, typically 8-12, where players are dealt 13 private tiles and take turns drawing and discarding tiles until a player completes a winning hand or the tile wall is exhausted. Each round concludes with players receiving scores based on their performance, contributing to an overall game reward determined by their accumulated scores across rounds. However, using round scores and game rewards as feedback signals for reinforcement learning (RL) training presents challenges.

Firstly, game rewards cannot effectively differentiate between well-played and poorly-played rounds since multiple rounds contribute to the same reward. Secondly, round scores may misrepresent the quality of actions, especially among skilled players who might adopt strategic conservatism in late rounds, potentially leading to negative scores despite good tactical decisions.

To address these issues, the paper proposes a global reward predictor designed to allocate the final game reward to individual rounds more effectively. This predictor, implemented as a recurrent neural network (specifically a two-layer gated recurrent unit followed by fully-connected layers), is trained on data from top human players in Tenhou. The training process minimizes the mean square error between the predicted rewards and the actual final game rewards, utilizing features from each round to enhance the accuracy of the predictions. This approach aims to provide a more nuanced signal for RL training, improving the understanding of player performance in Mahjong.
