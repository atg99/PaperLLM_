The paper discusses the implementation of a reward predictor using a GRU network to enhance the performance of an agent in the game of Mahjong. The reward for each round of K rounds is derived from the difference in scores between consecutive rounds, factoring in aspects such as the dealer position and various bet counters.

A significant challenge in Mahjong arises from the presence of hidden information, including the private tiles of other players and remaining wall tiles, making it difficult for agents to make informed decisions. To address this, the paper introduces an oracle agent that possesses complete information about the game state, including private tiles of all players and public scores. This oracle agent serves as a benchmark for training the normal agent, which lacks access to the complete information.

However, traditional knowledge distillation techniques prove ineffective due to the disparity in information access between the oracle and the normal agent. Therefore, the paper emphasizes the need for a more sophisticated approach to leverage the oracle agent’s capabilities to enhance the training of the normal agent, allowing for improved learning and decision-making in the context of Mahjong.
