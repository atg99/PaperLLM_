The paper discusses the performance evaluation of an AI agent, Suphx, which utilizes a policy gradient method and run-time policy adaptation in the context of Mahjong. The agent was tested against three opponents using an updated policy on a test set of 10,000 games, revealing a winning rate of 66% for the adapted version compared to its non-adapted counterpart. This indicates the effectiveness of the adaptation process, particularly in crucial final rounds of a game. 

Suphx's strategy, enhanced through simulations, enables it to make riskier plays to secure victories, avoiding lower placements. The AI was further evaluated on Tenhou.net, a prominent online platform for Japanese Mahjong, where it played over 5,000 games in the expert room and achieved a record rank of 10 dan, marking it as the first AI to reach this level. The paper highlights a comparison with other AI and human players, noting that Suphx, trained on 2.5 million games, did not implement run-time adaptation during these evaluations due to time constraints. The authors suggest that integrating this adaptation could further improve Suphx's performance.
