The paper outlines the decision-making process of a Mahjong AI model named Suphx, detailing its steps during gameplay. The process begins with the Riichi step, where Suphx evaluates whether it can declare Riichi with the drawn tile. If it can, it declares Riichi; if not, it proceeds to the Discard step.

In the Discard step, Suphx selects a tile to discard, after which the other players take their turns or the round concludes if no wall tiles remain. When an opponent discards a tile, Suphx checks if it can form a winning hand with that tile. If a win is possible, the winning model decides to declare victory, ending the round. If not, it assesses whether a Chow, Pong, or Kong can be formed.

If no actions are suggested by the Chow, Pong, or Kong models, the turn passes to other players, or the round ends if there are no wall tiles left. Conversely, if actions are suggested, Suphx proposes the one with the highest confidence score. If this action is not interrupted by higher-priority actions from other players, Suphx executes it and returns to the Discard step. If interrupted, the turn shifts back to the other players.

The decision flow illustrates the structured approach of Suphx in determining actions based on various game scenarios and player interactions, emphasizing the importance of evaluating winning conditions and potential actions in response to opponents.
