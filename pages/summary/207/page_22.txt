The paper discusses the decision-making process of the Suphx agent in a game setting, particularly regarding tile management for balancing future attack and defense strategies. It highlights a scenario where, despite the apparent safety of discarding a red-boxed tile—which human players might typically discard—Suphx retains this tile to maintain flexibility for future moves. This decision is critical when anticipating unexpected actions from opponents, such as a player declaring Riichi. By keeping the safe tile, Suphx can avoid breaking potential winning combinations, thereby enhancing its winning probability. The paper emphasizes the importance of state-level instant feedback over round-level feedback to improve the training of the policy function through optimal decision-making based on perfect information. Additionally, it notes that the current system of Suphx has undergone adaptations for real-time policy adjustments.
