The paper discusses a method for adapting a policy in a multi-round setting using a parametric approach called pMCPA. The adaptation of the policy is performed independently for each round, where the new policy parameters are determined by maximizing the expected reward based on limited simulations of trajectories. This approach allows for generalization to unseen states, as the updated policy can provide estimations for states not encountered during the simulations.

In the section on offline evaluation, the effectiveness of the Suphx framework is assessed through offline experiments. Five models were trained using supervised learning, where each model learned from state-action pairs observed from human professional players. The models were evaluated based on their training data sizes and test accuracies, with results showing a range of accuracies: 76.7% for the discard model, 85.7% for the Riichi model, 95.0% for the Chow model, 91.9% for the Pong model, and 94.0% for the Kong model. The paper notes that these results are not directly comparable to previous works due to differences in data and model structures.
