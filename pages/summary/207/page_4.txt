The paper discusses the functionality of the AI model Suphx, which is designed to navigate the complex rules of Mahjong through five specialized decision-making models: the discard model, Riichi model, Chow model, Pong model, and Kong model. In addition to these models, Suphx utilizes a rule-based winning model that determines whether to declare a winning hand. This model assesses the potential to form a winning hand based on tiles discarded by opponents or drawn from the wall, applying specific rules for decision-making. For instance, if it is not the last round, Suphx will declare a win. However, if it is the last round, it will only declare if the accumulated round score is not the lowest among all players.

The AI encounters two primary situations: the Draw situation and the subsequent decision-making steps. When Suphx draws a tile, it first checks if it can form a winning hand. If so, it declares a win. If not, it considers the Kong step, where it evaluates the potential to make a ClosedKong or AddKong with the drawn tile. Depending on the type of Kong, it may either return to the Draw situation or face the possibility of other players winning the round if an AddKong is made.

This structured approach allows Suphx to effectively manage the intricacies of Mahjong gameplay, ensuring that it can make informed decisions throughout the game.
