The paper discusses the development of a Mahjong agent using a two-phase training approach. Initially, an oracle agent is trained through reinforcement learning with both normal and perfect features. Gradually, the perfect features are dropped out using a dropout matrix, transitioning the model to a normal agent. The dropout process involves decaying the dropout rate from 1 to 0, at which point the training continues for several iterations with a reduced learning rate and selective rejection of state-action pairs based on importance weights to ensure stability and improvement in training.

Furthermore, the paper introduces a novel technique called parametric Monte-Carlo policy adaptation (pMCPA) to enhance the agent's performance during gameplay. Recognizing that player strategies in Mahjong vary significantly based on the initial hand, the approach involves three steps: simulating possible game scenarios by randomly generating opponent hands and employing the oﬄine-trained policy to create trajectories, fine-tuning the policy through gradient updates based on these simulations, and finally applying the updated policy in real-time against opponents.

This methodology aims to improve the adaptability and effectiveness of the Mahjong agent, distinguishing it from traditional game strategies used in other games like Go and StarCraft.
