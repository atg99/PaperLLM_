The paper discusses the architecture and training methodology of a model named Suphx, designed for playing Mahjong. It outlines the structure of various components within the model, including CNN architectures utilized for different functionalities such as discarding tiles and predicting actions based on game states. 

The model employs a three-step learning algorithm. Initially, it trains five models using supervised learning based on (state, action) pairs obtained from top players on the Tenhou platform. Subsequently, these supervised models are refined through self-play reinforcement learning, incorporating techniques such as policy gradient methods, global reward prediction, and oracle guiding to address the complexities of Mahjong. Finally, during actual gameplay, the model adapts its policy in real-time to enhance performance based on immediate observations.

The paper highlights the use of distributed reinforcement learning with entropy regularization to manage the challenges arising from asynchronous training. Key equations, such as the loss function incorporating importance sampling, are presented to illustrate the training process and the updating mechanism of the model’s policy. Overall, the work aims to advance the capabilities of AI in playing complex games like Mahjong through sophisticated learning techniques.
