This paper investigates the performance of Multi-Objective Evolutionary Algorithms (MOEAs) in solving various optimization tasks, focusing on the effectiveness of genetic transfer strategies. The study includes four cases, each highlighting different aspects of the algorithms' capabilities.

In Case 1, the convergence trends for the inverse trap-5 problem are analyzed, showing how the average objective values vary with different Bernoulli parameters. The results suggest that genetic transfers between the inverted trap-5 problem and a series of HDM problems are influenced by the learned relative mutation probabilities (rmp).

Case 2 explores the performance of MOEAs on K randomly generated HDM tasks, each aimed at minimizing the Hamming distance to a target string. The findings indicate that MFEA-II and the single-task CEA perform similarly, while MFEA-I suffers from detrimental genetic transfers due to a lack of an adaptive transfer mechanism. MFEA-II's ability to prevent harmful exchanges is identified as a key contribution of the paper.

In Case 3, the paper examines the LeadingOnes and TrailingOnes problems, which share a common global optimum but begin their searches from opposite ends of the solution space. MFEA-II effectively adjusts its rmp values, starting with low values to avoid early negative transfers, and gradually increasing them as the tasks converge. This adaptability allows both tasks to make progress towards the global optimum, contrasting with MFEA-I's less effective convergence trend.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of adaptive genetic transfer strategies in multi-task optimization, demonstrating that MFEA-II outperforms traditional approaches by mitigating the risks associated with harmful genetic transfers.
