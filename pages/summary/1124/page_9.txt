The paper presents an enhancement to the Multi-Objective Evolutionary Algorithm (MFEA) known as MFEA-II, which incorporates an online RMP (Resource Management Parameter) matrix learning scheme. The framework of MFEA-II retains the initial phases of the original MFEA but distinguishes itself with the addition of the RMP learning module. This module allows for the dynamic adjustment of genetic transfer across different tasks, enhancing the algorithm's adaptability.

The learning procedure for the RMP matrix involves building probabilistic models for each subpopulation and optimizing these models to maximize the likelihood of the data. The offspring population in MFEA-II is generated based on this learned RMP matrix, facilitating more effective inter-task crossover. The algorithm employs a parent-centric operator during crossover to maintain the theoretical underpinnings of the method.

The paper outlines the steps for inter-task crossover, which include sampling individuals from the population, checking the RMP condition, and performing either inter-task or intra-task crossover based on the learned parameters. If the RMP matrix contains only zeros, MFEA-II defaults to functioning similarly to standard evolutionary algorithms that operate on single tasks in parallel.

Overall, MFEA-II demonstrates improved capability in recognizing discrepancies and similarities across multiple tasks in real-time, as evidenced by subsequent experimental results. This advancement aims to optimize multi-objective problems more effectively by leveraging learned information during the evolutionary process.
