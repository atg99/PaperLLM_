This paper introduces a novel evolutionary multitasking approach aimed at enhancing online learning of latent similarities among optimization problems, facilitating effective knowledge exchange. The proposed method demonstrates versatility across various optimization problem types, as evidenced by experimental results.

The paper provides a comprehensive overview of probabilistic mixture models in multitask optimization. It emphasizes the necessity of a unified search space that integrates individual search spaces of different tasks, enabling seamless knowledge transfer. This unified space is defined with a dimensionality that accommodates the maximum dimensionality of the individual tasks, thereby addressing challenges associated with the curse of dimensionality and promoting efficient population-based search.

Knowledge sharing is facilitated through mixture distributions, which combine search distributions from multiple tasks. The offspring population for each task is generated from a mixture distribution that incorporates all available distributions, with mixture coefficients that dictate the degree of knowledge exchange. The paper cautions against blind mixing of distributions due to potential negative transfers and underscores the importance of learning optimal mixture coefficients online.

Furthermore, the study explores the implications of mixture distributions on global convergence in multitasking. It asserts that the asymptotic global convergence properties of evolutionary algorithms remain intact, particularly under the assumption of large population sizes. The paper utilizes the Glivenko-Cantelli theorem to support its claims regarding the convergence of empirical population density to true underlying distributions, laying the groundwork for practical applications of the theoretical findings, which will be further validated in subsequent experimental studies.
