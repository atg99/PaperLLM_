The authors argue that leveraging knowledge transfer across related optimization tasks can significantly improve convergence behavior and overall results. However, the risk of negative transfer, where unrelated tasks interfere with each other, necessitates a careful approach. MFEA-II employs a data-driven mechanism to identify and exploit both similarities and discrepancies among tasks, thus minimizing harmful interactions.

The study is grounded in theoretical principles and is validated through experiments on synthetic benchmarks and practical scenarios, demonstrating the framework's effectiveness in multitask settings. The findings suggest that MFEA-II can facilitate a more efficient optimization process by allowing tasks to mutually benefit from shared knowledge, ultimately leading to better performance in complex problem-solving environments.
