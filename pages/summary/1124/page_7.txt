The paper discusses the Multitasking Evolutionary Algorithm (MFEA) and its convergence properties in optimizing multiple tasks simultaneously. It establishes that the MFEA can asymptotically converge to global optimum solutions for each task, regardless of the random mixing parameter (rmp). Theorem 3 demonstrates that under the assumption of a large population, the MFEA maintains global convergence properties across all tasks if the ratio of selection parameters meets a specific condition.

However, the paper also highlights the potential negative effects of inter-task interactions in evolutionary multitasking. When tasks do not complement each other, tuning rmp can become challenging, leading to either detrimental interactions or lost opportunities for knowledge exchange. The authors analyze the rate of convergence of MFEA in scenarios where tasks are non-complementary, concluding that single-tasking may outperform multitasking under these circumstances.

To address these issues, the authors propose an online estimation technique for rmp that aims to minimize harmful interactions between tasks while still allowing for knowledge transfer. They argue that this approach, which involves dynamically learning optimal rmp values, can enhance the performance of MFEA by creating a balance between knowledge transfer and the mitigation of negative interactions. The paper emphasizes the importance of carefully managing the rmp to improve the efficacy of multitasking in evolutionary algorithms.
