The paper presents an experimental study demonstrating the effectiveness of the proposed online parameter estimation scheme of the multifactorial evolutionary algorithm II (MFEA-II). The experiments cover a range of optimization problems, including both discrete and continuous benchmarks, and a specific focus on the double-pole balancing controller design task. The performance of MFEA-II is compared against various baseline solvers, including MFEA-I, the canonical evolutionary algorithm (CEA), and two advanced natural evolutionary strategy variants, namely the separable natural evolutionary strategy (SNES) and the exponential natural evolutionary strategy (xNES).

The experimental configuration maintains a consistent population size across all algorithms, with specific settings for different problems, such as population sizes and maximum function evaluations. The study employs crossover and mutation operators aligned with theoretical assumptions, utilizing a probabilistic model for MFEA-II. Additionally, optimized parameters for the NES variants are specified, ensuring robust comparisons.

A pedagogical case study using binary optimization problems is also conducted. It explores three well-known problems: onemax, zeromax, and the deceptive trap-5 problem. The rationale for selecting these problems lies in their potential to highlight the advantages of the online parameter estimation scheme in MFEA-II. The study indicates that while onemax and trap-5 share a global optimum, the presence of numerous local optima in trap-5 can hinder convergence if inter-task crossovers are uninhibited. The zeromax problem, having conflicting solutions with onemax and trap-5, presents additional challenges. The findings suggest that concurrent task solving with MFEA-I, which uses a manually specified scalar for the parameter rmp, may suffer from negative transfer effects.
