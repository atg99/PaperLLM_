This study investigates the performance of multitasking evolutionary algorithms, specifically MFEA-I and MFEA-II, in solving three distinct problems simultaneously, compared to a single-task approach using CEA. The experiments focus on a dimensionality of D=200 and utilize the deceptive trap-5 problem as a primary example in analyzing convergence trends.

The results, averaged over 30 independent runs, reveal that both MFEA-I and MFEA-II outperform CEA, with MFEA-II demonstrating superior dominance. The left side of Figure 2 presents the learned pairwise relationship matrices (RMPs) for MFEA-II, which capture inter-task similarities across generations. The right side illustrates convergence trends, highlighting that MFEA-I struggles with genetic transfer issues, leading to suboptimal performance compared to MFEA-II.

MFEA-II effectively utilizes learned relationships to facilitate knowledge transfers at various optimization stages, especially when high-quality genetic material becomes available. This adaptability allows MFEA-II to guide the trap-5 problem towards the global optimum efficiently. In contrast, MFEA-I's uninhabited genetic exchanges make it susceptible to negative transfers, resulting in inferior convergence behavior.

Further analysis of MFEA-II's performance on synthetic continuous benchmark functions is presented, utilizing CEC composite minimization benchmarks with varying inter-task similarities. The study categorizes problem pairs based on Spearman's rank correlation, reflecting their inter-task relationships. Overall, the findings underscore the advantages of MFEA-II in leveraging genetic transfer for enhanced problem-solving in a multitasking context.
