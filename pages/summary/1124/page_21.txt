The figures presented in the paper illustrate the trends of learned relative mutation probability (rmp) and the convergence performance of two multi-faceted evolutionary algorithms (MFEA-I and MFEA-II) across different problem scenarios, specifically LeadingOnes and TrailingOnes. 

Figure 10 shows how the learned rmp evolves over generations, indicating a trend influenced by the specific problem being addressed. The shaded regions represent variability, denoting one-half standard deviation around the mean, which helps in understanding the stability of the learned rmp across generations.

Figure 11 compares the convergence trends of MFEA-I and MFEA-II for both LeadingOnes and TrailingOnes problems. The results suggest that MFEA-II generally outperforms MFEA-I in terms of achieving lower average objective values. However, the performance of these algorithms is negatively affected by uncontrolled inter-task genetic transfers, which can disrupt the evolutionary process.

Overall, the findings highlight the importance of regulating genetic transfers between tasks to enhance the effectiveness of multi-faceted evolutionary approaches in solving complex optimization problems.
