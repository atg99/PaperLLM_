The paper provides a proof for Lemma 2, discussing the probabilistic behavior of offspring solutions in a multitasking environment using Bayes' theorem. It establishes that an offspring solution \( x_a \) is assigned a skill factor \( k \), and may originate from one of several task densities. The uniform allocation of resources among \( K \) optimization tasks simplifies the probability calculations, leading to a formulation of the likelihood of \( x_a \) being drawn from a specific task density.

Three scenarios are presented to describe how \( x_a \) might inherit its skill factor through intra-task crossover, inter-task crossover, and local variation. The probabilities for each scenario are calculated, culminating in an overall expression for the mixture distribution of task densities.

Additionally, the paper visualizes two cases of global optima in a one-dimensional space: the complete intersection case, where the global optima of two tasks coincide at a single point, and the no intersection case, where the global optima are distinct and separated. This visualization aids in understanding the implications of task relationships within the optimization framework.
