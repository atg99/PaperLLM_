The paper discusses a theoretical framework for evolutionary multitasking algorithms, particularly focusing on a (λ, μ) selection strategy. This strategy selects the top λ individuals from μ offspring for each task, with the goal of converging the population distribution towards global optima over time. The authors define a parameter ρ = λ/μ, where 0 < ρ < 1, and model the selection process using a mixture distribution.

The central theorem states that if a certain condition (βk > ρ) is satisfied, the algorithm guarantees asymptotic convergence to the global optimum for each task as the number of iterations approaches infinity. The proof involves demonstrating that the infimum of objective values for each task decreases over time, indicating that the population is concentrating on more promising areas of the search space.

The paper further explores the implications of the multifactorial evolutionary algorithm (MFEA), which utilizes the parallelism of population-based search methods to tackle multiple optimization problems simultaneously. The MFEA employs parent-centric operators, such as crossover and mutation, which enhance the probability of offspring being similar to their parents, thereby facilitating knowledge transfer across tasks.

Overall, the research highlights the potential of evolutionary algorithms in multitasking optimization by establishing conditions for convergence and presenting practical algorithmic strategies.
