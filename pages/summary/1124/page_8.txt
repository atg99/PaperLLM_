The paper discusses a method for optimizing a K-matrix of pairwise values, specifically focusing on the relationships between different optimization tasks. The matrix, denoted as RMP, captures the synergy between tasks, with the constraint that the diagonal elements are equal to one, reflecting self-synergy. This approach allows the model to account for non-uniform inter-task synergies prevalent in practical scenarios.

At a given generation, the authors introduce gk(x;t) as a probabilistic model for estimating the true distribution pk(x;t) of each task, built from a dataset of subpopulation Pk(t). They reformulate the probabilistic models by incorporating the RMP matrix instead of using a scalar representation. This leads to a new probabilistic mixture model, gk_c(x;t), which aims to approximate the distribution of offspring populations closely.

The method for learning the RMP matrix online involves maximizing a log-likelihood function based on the probabilistic models of each subpopulation. The authors argue that this data-driven learning paradigm minimizes the gap between the estimated and true probability densities using Kullback-Leibler divergence as a measure of the information loss in approximating one distribution with another.

Theorem 4 presents the equivalence between maximizing the log-likelihood function and minimizing the KL divergence across all tasks, suggesting that the learning of the RMP matrix effectively enables tasks to influence one another while reducing negative transfer effects. The authors emphasize the importance of selecting suitable probabilistic models to avoid overfitting, proposing the use of simpler models to ensure effective inter-task transfers.

Overall, the paper presents a theoretically grounded approach to online learning in a multitask setting, aiming to enhance the performance and transferability of optimization tasks through a well-structured probabilistic framework.
