This is particularly evident in the CI+LS scenario, where MFEA-II mitigates negative transfer effects by dynamically adjusting genetic material transfers, unlike MFEA-I, which suffers from these negative interactions.

The effectiveness of the online RMP (Reinforcement Multi-Parameter) learning module is further demonstrated through the learned pairwise RMP curves, which indicate that MFEA-II accurately captures the inter-task similarities. High RMP values correlate with high similarity tasks, while lower values are seen for tasks with less similarity, suggesting that MFEA-II adeptly identifies synergies and differences across tasks.

In terms of convergence behavior, MFEA-II shows significant improvements over MFEA-I and CEA, reaching convergence faster in the CI+HS case. The analysis of the learned RMP trends supports this accelerated convergence.

The paper also discusses a practical case study applying MFEA-II in neuroevolution for a double-pole balancing controller design, indicating its versatility and effectiveness in real-world multitasking environments. Overall, MFEA-II demonstrates superior performance and adaptability, making it a promising approach for tackling complex multitasking problems.
