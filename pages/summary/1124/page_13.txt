This paper explores the benefits of multitasking in reinforcement learning, specifically through the omnidirectional knowledge transfer exhibited by the MFEA-II method. It highlights how complex tasks can enhance the learning of simpler ones and vice versa, a dynamic not present in sequential transfer methods. The study focuses on the double-pole balancing problem, a common benchmark in artificial learning, where balancing two poles of varying lengths on a cart presents increasing difficulty as the lengths become similar.

The authors demonstrate that solving multiple tasks simultaneously accelerates the acquisition of knowledge, allowing simpler tasks to be solved unintentionally while attempting harder ones. The experimental setup involves a simulation of the double-pole task using a feedforward neural network controller, with various pole lengths tested to assess performance.

Results indicate that multitasking with MFEA-II significantly improves the success rates of both simple and complex tasks compared to single-task approaches. For instance, while tackling tasks separately yielded low success rates, combining tasks led to notable improvements, with the percentage of successful runs for simpler tasks benefiting from the complexity of harder tasks.

Furthermore, the study expands to six different tasks with varying pole lengths and compares MFEA-II against other state-of-the-art solvers. The findings consistently show that MFEA-II outperforms other methods, especially as task difficulty increases, underscoring the effectiveness of multitasking in knowledge transfer within reinforcement learning systems.
