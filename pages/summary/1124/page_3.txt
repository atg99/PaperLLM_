Our approach builds on the multifactorial evolutionary algorithm (MFEA) framework but enhances it by introducing a symmetric matrix to control the extent of knowledge transfers, allowing effective multitasking across multiple tasks with varying inter-task relationships. This parameter matrix is learned and adapted continuously during the multitasking process, reducing the need for extensive manual tuning.

The paper is structured as follows: Section II reviews notable multitask optimization methodologies, particularly focusing on Bayesian and evolutionary approaches. Section III discusses mixture models and their relevance to multitasking. Section IV details the MFEA framework, highlighting its theoretical foundations and limitations. Section V presents the online parameter estimation strategy for MFEA-II, followed by experimental results in Section VI, which includes a case study on neuroevolution-based controller design. Finally, Section VII concludes with a discussion on future research directions.

Our findings indicate that MFEA-II effectively addresses the challenges associated with negative transfer and enhances performance in multitasking optimization, demonstrating its applicability in both continuous and combinatorial search spaces.
