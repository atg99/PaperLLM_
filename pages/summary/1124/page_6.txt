The paper discusses the dynamics of real-coded evolutionary algorithms (EAs), focusing on the behavior of offspring populations in relation to parent populations. It highlights common evolutionary operators like simulated binary crossover, polynomial mutation, and Gaussian mutation, emphasizing that under certain conditions, the empirical density of the offspring can closely approximate that of the parents. This approximation simplifies theoretical analysis, particularly in the context of maintaining parent distribution through parent-centric evolutionary operators.

The analysis assumes that the parent population follows a multivariate Gaussian distribution. It notes that crossover operators maintain the mean of the parents in the offspring, and under independent variable treatment, the covariance of the offspring remains largely unchanged. This indicates that the distributions of parent and offspring populations are preserved through variable-wise operations.

The paper introduces the Multi-Objective Evolutionary Algorithm (MFEA), which tackles multiple optimization tasks simultaneously. Each individual in the population is associated with a specific task, termed the skill factor. The MFEA employs a collaborative framework where individuals can undergo both intra-task and inter-task crossover, allowing the transfer of genetic material and knowledge across different tasks. The random mating probability (rmp) governs the extent of this knowledge transfer, where a higher rmp facilitates more frequent inter-task interactions.

Additionally, the paper establishes theoretical foundations for the MFEA, noting that the overall distribution of the parent population is a mixture of distributions corresponding to each optimization task. While offspring distributions may align with the parent distribution under specific evolutionary assumptions, the mixture components for different tasks may not necessarily be equivalent. This highlights the complexity and adaptability of the MFEA in addressing multiple optimization challenges.
