This paper presents an automated brain tumor segmentation method using a SegNet architecture applied to multi-modal MRI images. The SegNet model is chosen for its efficiency, requiring fewer parameters and less computational power compared to other deep learning networks like DeconvNet. It allows for simpler end-to-end training and lower memory usage by reusing pooling indices instead of transferring entire feature maps, as done in U-Net.

The architecture of SegNet includes a down-sampling (encoding) path with 13 convolutional layers that correspond to the first 13 layers of the VGG-16 network, and a matching up-sampling (decoding) path. This structure facilitates the retention of high-resolution feature maps while significantly reducing the number of parameters from 134 million to 14.7 million. The encoder features five convolution blocks followed by max-pooling, while the decoder mirrors this with upsampling operations. The final output is processed through a multi-class soft-max classifier to generate pixel-wise class probabilities.

The paper also details the post-processing step, where four separate SegNet models are trained to identify different tumor-related features, such as background, necrosis, edema, and enhancing tumor. The model's deeper layers learn complex features, resulting in score maps that inform the classification of each pixel in relation to the defined tumor classes.
