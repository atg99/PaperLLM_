The paper discusses an automated approach for brain tumor segmentation using a deep learning model called SegNet, applied to multi-modal MRI images. The dataset comprised 75% of patients for training (158 high-grade gliomas and 57 low-grade gliomas) and 25% for testing (52 high-grade gliomas and 18 low-grade gliomas). Each patient’s MRI was segmented manually into three labels: necrotic and non-enhancing tumor, peritumoral edema, and enhancing tumor, with ground truth verified by neuro-radiologists.

The segmentation focused on three tumor regions: the complete tumor region, the core tumor region (excluding edema), and the enhancing tumor region. The model's performance was quantitatively assessed using the F-measure, which combines precision and recall to evaluate the intersection between manual definitions and automated predictions.

Preliminary results indicated that the 3D model effectively detected brain tumors, even when trained on individual MRI modalities rather than combined inputs. This success is attributed to the model's architecture, which captures fine details from adjacent MRI slices. The segmentation was enhanced by extracting the four highest feature maps alongside pixel intensity values from the original images, with accurate results obtained from the last deconvolution layer of the SegNet model.

Evaluation results from the BraTS 2017 Training dataset showed that the SegNet Max DT method outperformed individual SegNet models in F-measure across different tumor regions. The results highlight the effectiveness of the approach in accurately segmenting brain tumors, as evidenced by comparison tables included in the study. Overall, the paper emphasizes the potential of the SegNet model for clinical applications in brain tumor segmentation.
