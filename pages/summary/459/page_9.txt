The paper discusses an automated method for brain tumor segmentation using multi-modal MRI images and the SegNet architecture. The approach combines maximum feature maps from various SegNet models to ensure that only the strongest and most relevant features are presented to the classifier. Training is conducted separately for each MRI modality due to the distinct features associated with each type, which facilitates faster training with simpler models. This strategy also allows for the extraction of specific features directly related to each modality, providing clinicians with targeted information.

However, the training process is noted to be time-consuming, although the prediction phase is efficient, enabling rapid processing of testing datasets for semantic segmentation and classification. While the proposed method excels at segmenting core and enhanced tumors compared to current state-of-the-art techniques, it falls short in accurately segmenting complete tumors. The paper suggests that the accuracy of the segmentation could be enhanced through post-processing techniques and by refining the SegNet models with additional training datasets. Future work is recommended to explore different fully convolutional network (FCN) and convolutional neural network (CNN) architectures over time to further improve system performance.
