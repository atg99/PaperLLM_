The paper discusses an automated brain tumor segmentation method utilizing multi-modal MRI images through the SegNet architecture. The approach focuses on learning complex features directly from data to enhance segmentation accuracy. Previous studies have highlighted the effectiveness of CNNs in distinguishing between low-grade and high-grade gliomas, employing dual pathways to capture both local and global information for improved segmentation.

The proposed method consists of four key steps: preprocessing to construct 3D MRI datasets, training a fine-tuned SegNet model for each MRI modality, post-processing to extract significant feature maps, and classification to segment the tumor into four distinct parts. Preprocessing involves applying N4ITK bias field correction to mitigate artifacts and normalizing intensity values across different modalities to ensure consistency.

The authors emphasize the importance of utilizing correlations between different MRI modalities, which is often overlooked in existing methods. The paper showcases the potential of their approach in accurately locating and segmenting tumor volumes, thereby contributing to advancements in medical imaging and diagnosis.
