The paper discusses a method for classifying MRI images into tumor and sub-tumor parts using a combination of high activation feature maps from a SegNet architecture and pixel intensity values. The highest activation feature maps capture strong hierarchical features, thereby enhancing classification performance. A feature vector is generated from these maps and the original MRI data, which is then classified using a Decision Tree (DT) classifier. The choice of DT is justified by its proven effectiveness in brain tumor segmentation.

The methodology includes combining the four highest score maps with pixel intensities, followed by tuning the DT classifier's depth to optimize performance, with a maximum depth of 15 yielding the best results. The classification accuracy was evaluated using 5-fold cross-validation.

The implementation was carried out in MATLAB 2018a on a system with an Intel Core i7 CPU and 16 GB RAM, utilizing the deep learning toolbox for semantic segmentation and the classification learner toolbox for the DT classifier training. The training process took about three days on a single NVIDIA GPU Titan XP, employing stochastic gradient descent for updating the loss function with specified parameters.

The study utilized the BraTS 2017 dataset, which included 285 patient subjects with high-grade glioma (HGG) and low-grade glioma (LGG), to validate the proposed algorithm.
