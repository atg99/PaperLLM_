The proposed method addresses the limitations of traditional offline clustering approaches by enabling online learning, which is more efficient for large datasets. By maintaining soft assignments through the Sinkhorn-Knopp algorithm, the method avoids the pitfalls of hard assignments that can lead to loss of information. This approach is particularly beneficial for handling dynamic datasets where the size and content may vary over time.

The multi-crop strategy used in the method enhances the learning process by extracting diverse features from different parts of the image. This is in contrast to previous methods that relied on predefined tasks or specific augmentations. The focus on consistent mapping between different augmentations aligns with principles of contrastive learning, reinforcing the idea that similar images should yield similar representations.

Overall, the research presents a novel online clustering-based self-supervised learning framework that effectively learns visual features by leveraging the relationships between multiple augmented views of images, providing a scalable solution suitable for various dataset sizes without requiring extensive offline processing.
