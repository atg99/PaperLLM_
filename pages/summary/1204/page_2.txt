The paper discusses advancements in contrastive loss methods for self-supervised learning, particularly focusing on the challenges of computing pairwise comparisons of image representations in large datasets. Traditional approaches often rely on approximating the loss through random subsets or clustering methods, which can be inefficient and not scalable. This work introduces a novel online clustering-based method, Swapping Assignments between multiple Views of the same image (SwA V), which computes cluster codes dynamically while maintaining consistency between different views of the same image. This approach allows for effective contrast without explicit pairwise comparisons, making it scalable for extensive datasets.

Additionally, the paper proposes a multi-crop strategy that enhances the number of image views during training using smaller-sized images, which does not impose additional memory or computational burdens. This method has shown to improve performance significantly, as it allows for a richer set of comparisons without introducing bias through the use of varied image sizes.

The authors validate their contributions through extensive evaluation on standard self-supervised benchmarks, achieving notable improvements in accuracy on the ImageNet dataset. Specifically, they report a top-1 accuracy of 75.3% with a standard ResNet-50 and 78.5% with a wider model, alongside consistent gains of 2% to 4% across various self-supervised methods. Overall, the paper presents two main contributions: a scalable online clustering loss that enhances performance without requiring large memory banks or momentum encoders, and an effective multi-crop strategy that boosts the number of views with no added computational cost. Combining these innovations results in a 4.2% performance increase on ImageNet, surpassing traditional supervised pretraining methods without the need for fine-tuning features.
