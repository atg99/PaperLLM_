The paper presents a comparison of self-supervised learning methods for image classification on the ImageNet dataset, specifically focusing on the performance of clustering-based approaches versus contrastive instance learning methods. The results indicate that methods like SwA V and DeepCluster-v2 outperform SimCLR, particularly when employing a multi-crop strategy, which enhances accuracy by 2% without multi-crop and up to 3.5% with multi-crop.

DeepCluster-v2 introduces improvements over its predecessor by incorporating explicit comparisons to k-means centroids, addressing the instability of cluster assignments that disrupts training. This adaptation allows for better performance but requires multiple dataset passes, making it less practical for large-scale datasets. In contrast, SwA V operates online by swapping assignments at the batch level, facilitating training efficiency.

The study also highlights the effectiveness of the multi-crop strategy across various methods, noting that it significantly boosts performance, especially for clustering-based techniques, while not benefiting supervised models. Overall, this work emphasizes the advantages of clustering methods in self-supervised learning and the potential of multi-crop strategies in enhancing model accuracy.
