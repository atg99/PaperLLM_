The paper presents a novel approach called UNIQ (Uniform Noise Injection for Quantization) aimed at enhancing the quantization process of neural networks. It introduces a method for injecting uniform noise during inference, which simplifies noise modeling by removing the need for complex and bin-dependent distributions. The authors focus on k-quantile quantization but note that this method can be adapted for any threshold configuration.

UNIQ improves upon previous noise modeling techniques by providing a more accurate representation of quantization noise, particularly in low bit regimes. This advancement leads to a better performance in the accuracy versus complexity tradeoff compared to existing methods like distillation and XNOR. The strategy is also effective for smaller models, such as MobileNet, which are typically more challenging to quantize due to their lower parameter redundancy.

While the non-uniform nature of the proposed quantization may complicate hardware implementation, the authors suggest that using look-up tables can alleviate these challenges. They emphasize the importance of exploring innovative solutions for network quantization beyond traditional uniform strategies.

The paper also reviews related work in the field, highlighting previous efforts to quantize network weights and activations to very low bit representations and the accuracy challenges that arise from such reductions. Some earlier studies have proposed wider networks to counteract these accuracy losses, further underscoring the significance of the UNIQ approach in advancing quantization methodologies.
