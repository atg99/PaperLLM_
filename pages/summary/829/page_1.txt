The paper presents a new method for quantizing neural networks, termed U N I Q, which emulates a non-uniform k-quantile quantizer. This approach addresses the limitations of traditional uniform quantization techniques by adapting to the distribution of quantized parameters. The authors highlight the significance of comparing performance based on bit-operations (BOPS), particularly in low computational budget scenarios, and propose that their method, while more complex to implement in hardware, offers a foundation for innovative quantization strategies.

The introduction discusses the widespread use of deep neural networks and the associated challenges of high storage and computational costs, which can hinder deployment on resource-constrained devices. Quantization is identified as a viable solution to reduce both computational demands and memory footprint, allowing larger networks to fit into smaller devices. The paper notes that while uniform quantization can be effective, it is often suboptimal due to the non-uniform distribution of weights in neural networks.

The authors propose a k-quantile quantization method that uses balanced bins to better represent the actual distribution of weights, which is typically bell-shaped. They also introduce a training technique that incorporates noise to simulate quantization effects, enhancing the performance of networks with quantized weights. Overall, the study aims to provide a more efficient and effective framework for neural network quantization, particularly for applications requiring reduced complexity during inference.
