The paper "UNIQ: Uniform Noise Injection for Quantization" discusses the benefits of both weights and activations quantization in neural networks. While the primary focus is on weights quantization, the authors emphasize that quantizing activations can also reduce arithmetic complexity and communication overhead, especially in distributed models. It is noted that training with noisy weights can make the model less sensitive to quantization of activations, allowing for effective quantization during training.

The experimental evaluation compares the proposed UNIQ scheme against leading quantization methods using the ImageNet-1K dataset for visual classification tasks, with the CIFAR-10 dataset utilized for testing various design choices. The study employs MobileNet and ResNet architectures as baselines, highlighting MobileNet's suitability for limited hardware environments and ResNet's popularity and performance.

The metric used for quantifying network complexity is the number of bit operations (BOPs), which is particularly relevant for hardware implementations. The training process involves fine-tuning pre-trained models with specific hyperparameters, including a learning rate and momentum.

Results indicate that UNIQ performs competitively with both ResNet and MobileNet across various quantization levels, achieving lower complexity while maintaining accuracy. The paper also discusses the trade-off between accuracy and complexity, noting that the non-linear relationship between activation and weight bitwidth affects the overall complexity measured in BOPs. This relationship highlights the importance of optimizing bitwidths in convolutional layers to manage computational efficiency effectively.
