this paper, we introduce UNIQ, a training scheme aimed at improving the accuracy of quantized neural networks. Our approach involves uniformizing the distribution of quantized parameters and injecting additive uniform noise, followed by a process of de-uniformization. We found that injecting noise into all layers simultaneously is ineffective for deeper networks. Instead, we recommend splitting the training into N stages, where noise is injected into a subset of layers at each stage. 

Through experiments with ResNet-18 on CIFAR-10, we determined that injecting noise into a single layer per stage yields the best accuracy, while for MobileNet, which has 28 layers, injecting noise into two consecutive layers at each stage is more effective. Our analysis of weight distributions revealed that they generally follow a Gaussian distribution.

The UNIQ scheme is designed for efficient training using back propagation in full precision arithmetic and achieves optimal performance with the k-quantile quantizer. We benchmarked our method based on BOPs, demonstrating improved accuracy for quantized neural networks on ImageNet, especially in the low computational range of less than 400 GBOPs. Notably, our MobileNet implementation achieved 66% accuracy, outperforming significantly quantized networks.

While this paper focuses on a uniform bit allocation across parameters, future work will explore more complex bit distributions. The proposed method is versatile and can be applied to various quantizers, with the injected noise being uniform but varying in variance according to bin width.
