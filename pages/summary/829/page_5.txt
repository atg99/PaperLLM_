The paper introduces UNIQ, a method for quantizing neural network weights through uniform noise injection. The authors observe that weights in neural networks typically exhibit a normal distribution, enabling the use of the cumulative distribution function (CDF) to transform weights into a uniform distribution. This transformation, referred to as the uniformization trick, facilitates the application of a uniform quantizer.

During training, the authors propose replacing the quantization process with the injection of random additive noise, which maintains the model's differentiability and allows for more effective gradient updates. Instead of using quantized weights directly, the method uses a representation that incorporates noise drawn from a non-uniform distribution, which adapts as the network parameters change.

For deeper networks, the authors suggest a gradual quantization approach, dividing the network into smaller blocks and applying noise incrementally. This strategy enhances convergence by allowing deeper layers to adapt to quantization errors from preceding layers. The approach is effective for both training from scratch and post-training fine-tuning, with the best results achieved by reducing the learning rate as noise is introduced.

The paper emphasizes that this iterative process can further improve accuracy, as earlier blocks can adjust to changes in subsequent layers. Empirical results and analyses support the proposed methods, demonstrating their efficacy in enhancing neural network quantization.
