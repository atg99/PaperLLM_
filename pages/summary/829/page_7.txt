The paper discusses UNIQ, a method for uniform noise injection aimed at improving quantization in neural networks, particularly focusing on the trade-offs between performance and computational complexity. The authors highlight the importance of including the cost of fetching parameters from external memory in the Bits Operations Per Second (BOPs) calculation. They make two key assumptions: each parameter is fetched once and the cost for a b-bit parameter is b BOPs, leading to an access cost of nb for a network with n parameters.

The results presented in Figure 1 and Table 1 demonstrate that networks trained with UNIQ, such as ResNet-34 and ResNet-50, achieve better computational complexity and accuracy compared to competing networks, particularly when quantizing the first and last layers.

An ablation study examines the impact of quantization levels on accuracy, revealing that quantization can help prevent overfitting in smaller datasets like CIFAR-10, with quantized models nearly matching full-precision performance. Different quantization methods, including k-quantile, k-means, and uniform quantizers, were compared. The k-quantile method showed superior performance in terms of accuracy and training efficiency, requiring 60% more training time compared to a baseline, while k-means incurred a 280% increase.

Furthermore, the paper contrasts training from scratch with fine-tuning a pre-trained model. Both approaches yielded comparable accuracy with slight variations across different quantization setups, indicating their effectiveness in producing quantized models close to the full-precision baseline. The findings are summarized in several tables detailing accuracy results across different configurations.
