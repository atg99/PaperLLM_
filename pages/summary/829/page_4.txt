The paper discusses various quantization methods for neural networks, highlighting the limitations of traditional techniques and proposing a novel approach called Uniform Noise Injection for Quantization (UNIQ). 

UNIQ aims to enhance the performance of quantized neural networks by training them with uniform random additive noise, simulating quantization errors. This method is designed to improve inference accuracy when applying standard deterministic quantization. 

The authors begin by exploring existing quantization schemes, such as k-means and uniform quantizers, noting that while k-means is optimal for uniformly distributed data, neural network weights often follow a bell-shaped distribution. Consequently, traditional k-means may not yield the best results for classification tasks due to its sensitivity to distribution tails, which do not significantly impact classification performance.

In response, the paper introduces the k-quantile quantizer, which creates equiprobable bins by determining thresholds based on the cumulative distribution function. This approach allows for better representation of the underlying data distribution without being overly influenced by outliers.

Overall, the proposed UNIQ method addresses the challenges of neural network quantization by incorporating noise during training, which can lead to improved model performance during inference, particularly in classification scenarios.
