4.1 Comparison Methods
In the absence of specific knowledge distillation methods for GCN models, we implement three existing methods: KD, FitNet, and attention transfer (AT). Neuron selectivity transfer is excluded due to its requirement for matching feature map sizes. We also establish a baseline by training the student model with the original loss. 

- The KD method distills knowledge by using softened labels from the teacher network to convey class similarity.
- The FitNet method incorporates intermediate feature maps, aiming to align the student’s features with the teacher’s through a mapping function.
- The AT method focuses on aligning attention maps between models, encouraging the student to concentrate on similar spatial areas as the teacher.

4.2 Node Classification
For the node classification task, we use the PPI dataset, which consists of 24 graphs representing various human tissues. The dataset is split into 20 training graphs, 2 validation graphs, and 2 testing graphs. Each graph has an average of 2372 nodes and 50 input features, with 121 classes. Both the teacher and student models are GAT-based, with the student model being deeper but having fewer hidden features. The binary cross-entropy loss is applied given the multi-label nature of the task.

The results indicate that while other knowledge distillation methods resulted in performance drops for the student model, our method positively influences the student’s performance, achieving the best results.
