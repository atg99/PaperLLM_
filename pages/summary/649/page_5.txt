The paper discusses the challenges and solutions associated with applying graph convolutional networks (GCNs) to dynamic graphs, particularly in the context of knowledge distillation from a teacher model to a student model. The authors introduce a method for handling dynamic graphs by adding virtual edges based on the union of edges from both the student and teacher models. This approach allows for the preservation of local structure while accommodating changes in node features and connections.

Three common kernel functions are explored: linear, polynomial, and radial basis function (RBF) kernels, with specific parameters set for the polynomial and RBF cases. The dynamic graph model, exemplified by the Dynamic Graph CNN (DGCNN), enables the graph to reflect topological connections in a learned feature space rather than the initial feature space. However, the variability in node connections complicates the computation of local structure vectors.

To address this, the authors propose a strategy that leverages the union of edges from both graphs, allowing for the generation of local structure vectors that maintain a consistent distribution of nodes. This facilitates the transfer of topological relationships from the teacher to the student model, even when the models have different neighborhood sizes.

Experiments are conducted using two datasets: the protein-protein interaction (PPI) dataset for node classification and the ModelNet40 dataset for 3D object analysis. The performance of the proposed knowledge distillation method is evaluated against various GCN architectures, demonstrating state-of-the-art results in distillation performance across different setups. The primary goal is to maximize the information transfer from the teacher model to the student model during the training process.
