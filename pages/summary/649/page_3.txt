The paper presents a novel knowledge distillation method for Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs), highlighting the local structure preserving (LSP) module as its core component. The proposed framework aims to optimize a student model by minimizing the distribution differences of local structures between a teacher and a student GCN. This approach builds upon the concept of knowledge amalgamation, where a student learns from multiple teachers across different domains, enabling it to handle various tasks simultaneously.

The paper emphasizes the importance of GCNs for processing non-grid data, particularly in applications like 3D object recognition. Unlike traditional convolutional networks, GCNs operate on graph-structured data, allowing them to effectively utilize the relationships between nodes. The authors detail the mechanics of GCNs, explaining how features are aggregated from neighboring nodes to produce new feature representations.

In addition, the paper explores different distance functions within the LSP module, aiming to enhance knowledge transfer between GCN models. The authors also propose extending the LSP approach to dynamic graph models, suggesting a pathway for future research in effectively distilling knowledge across various graph-based architectures.
