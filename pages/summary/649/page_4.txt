The paper discusses the implementation of a graph attention network (GAT) and the motivation behind knowledge distillation in this context. The GAT utilizes a multilayer perceptron for both the aggregation of features and the attention mechanism, where the aggregation is based on the normalized attention weights assigned to neighboring nodes.

The authors emphasize that the aggregation strategy is crucial for embedding node features and is learned during training. They propose a method to transfer knowledge from a teacher network to a student network by distilling the embedded topological structure rather than the aggregation function directly. This involves matching the local structure representations derived from both networks.

Local structure is defined using a graph representation, where the similarity between nodes is quantified using the Euclidean distance, and the local structure vector for each node is normalized. The paper outlines a strategy to compute the similarity of local structures between the teacher and student networks using Kullback-Leibler divergence, leading to a local structure preserving loss.

The total loss function combines the cross-entropy loss from the student's predictions and the local structure preserving loss, with a hyperparameter to balance their contributions. The authors also address limitations in the similarity measurement by proposing the use of kernel functions, which allow for mapping feature vectors to higher dimensions and improving similarity computation.

Overall, the paper presents a framework for knowledge distillation in graph neural networks, focusing on preserving local structures to enhance the learning process in student networks.
