This paper presents a novel knowledge distillation approach specifically designed for graph convolutional networks (GCNs), marking the first dedicated effort in this area. Knowledge distillation traditionally focuses on transferring knowledge from a larger, pre-trained teacher model to a smaller student model, primarily within convolutional neural networks (CNNs). However, existing methods do not adequately address the unique topological characteristics of non-grid data, such as graphs, which are handled by GCNs.

The authors propose a Local Structure Preserving (LSP) module that captures the topological differences between the teacher and student models. This module allows the student to learn a similar local structure by minimizing the distance between the distributions of local structures from both models. The approach is adaptable to dynamic graph models, where the graphs evolve during training.

The effectiveness of the proposed method is demonstrated through evaluations on two tasks: node classification using a Protein-Protein Interaction dataset and 3D object recognition with ModelNet40. The results indicate that this method consistently outperforms existing knowledge distillation techniques across different architectures and domains.

Key contributions of the paper include:
1. Introduction of a dedicated knowledge distillation method for GCNs.
2. Development of the LSP module to assess local topological structure similarity.
3. Comprehensive evaluation on varied tasks, showcasing superior performance compared to other methods.

In summary, this work addresses a significant gap in knowledge distillation for GCNs, providing a framework that effectively incorporates topological information for enhanced model training.
