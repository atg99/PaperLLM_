The paper presents a novel approach to knowledge distillation from Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs), specifically targeting the preservation of local structures during training. The study evaluates a teacher-student model setup using the ModelNet40 dataset, where the teacher model is a deep GCN with five layers and the student model is a simplified version with four layers. The experiments demonstrate that the proposed method enhances the student model's performance, achieving comparable accuracy to the teacher model despite having significantly fewer parameters.

Key findings include that both the Knowledge Distillation (KD) and Attention Transfer (AT) methods improve student performance, with the proposed method yielding the best results due to its ability to learn structural information from dynamic graphs. The results show that the student model can achieve an accuracy of 91.9% and a mean class accuracy of 88.6%, underscoring its generalization capability across various tasks and architectures.

The paper further explores the impact of different kernel functions on model performance, concluding that the Radial Basis Function (RBF) kernel yields the best results among various tested functions. Additionally, the study assesses the trade-off between model complexity and performance by varying the number of channels and layers in the student model, revealing that a model with more channels closely matches the teacher's performance while maintaining a lower parameter count.

In conclusion, this work represents a significant advancement in knowledge distillation from GCNs, demonstrating state-of-the-art performance and the potential for extension to dynamic graph models. The research is supported by various Australian Research Council projects and highlights the importance of preserving local structures in knowledge distillation processes.
