In this section of the paper by Townes et al., the authors focus on the implementation of Poisson Generalized Linear Model Principal Component Analysis (GLM-PCA) compared to negative binomial models for analyzing single-cell RNA sequencing data. They emphasize that using a Poisson model assumes that biological variability is encapsulated by the factor model, with minimal unwanted variability relative to sampling variability. The model allows adjustments for gene-specific or cell-specific covariates, such as batch effects.

The authors applied Poisson GLM-PCA to both technical and biological negative control datasets, successfully eliminating spurious correlations between the first dimension and total unique molecular identifiers (UMIs) and the fraction of zeros in the data. They evaluated GLM-PCA as a visualization tool against other methods, demonstrating that it effectively distinguished between T cell subtypes in various datasets, a separation not achieved with traditional PCA methods.

Despite its advantages, GLM-PCA has the drawback of being computationally slower than PCA, as it relies on an iterative algorithm to estimate latent factors. This section also highlights the efficiency of using deviance residuals for fast approximations in GLM-PCA, underscoring the balance between computational speed and the effectiveness of data representation. Overall, the findings illustrate the utility of GLM-PCA in enhancing the analysis of single-cell RNA sequencing data while addressing challenges related to noise and variability.
