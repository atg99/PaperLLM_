In the paper by Townes et al., the authors critique current normalization and transformation methods in single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-Seq) that increase variability in the fraction of zeros across cells. This variability is identified as a significant source of distortion in clustering algorithms, leading to false-positive results influenced by biased latent factors. They illustrate this point with principal component analysis (PCA) results from both technical and biological replicate datasets, demonstrating that total unique molecular identifiers (UMIs) per cell affect the observed latent factors.

To address the limitations of traditional PCA, which is based on Euclidean distance and Gaussian likelihood, the authors propose the use of generalized linear models (GLMs) within a PCA framework (GLM-PCA). This approach offers a more suitable alternative for analyzing non-normally distributed count data. The authors highlight that while the multinomial likelihood is effective for modeling technical variability, biological variability—such as that arising from cell cycle differences—requires alternative modeling approaches like the Dirichlet-multinomial likelihood. Although both likelihoods are complex, they can be approximated using Poisson and negative binomial distributions, respectively, providing a more robust analysis of scRNA-Seq data.
