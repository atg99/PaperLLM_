The study by Townes et al. investigates the impact of normalization and log transformation on single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) data, particularly focusing on unique molecular identifier (UMI) counts. The authors assert that traditional normalization methods, such as counts per million (CPM), distort UMI data by creating an artificial inflation of zero counts. This inflation arises because log transformation cannot appropriately handle exact zeros, leading to exaggerated differences between zero and nonzero counts.

Through the analysis of negative control datasets, the authors demonstrate that applying principal component analysis (PCA) to log-CPM values reveals a strong correlation with the fraction of zeros, which diminishes significantly when using raw CPM values without log transformation. They argue that this distortion complicates downstream analyses by confounding biological variability with technical noise.

The paper provides a mathematical justification for their claims and illustrates the issue of zero inflation with a specific example of UMI counts modeled as multinomial distributions. The authors conclude that log transformation is unnecessary and detrimental for UMI count analysis. They suggest that when normalization is unavoidable, using approximate multinomial deviance residuals could be a preferable alternative to log-transformed CPM.
