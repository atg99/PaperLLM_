This paper by Townes et al. discusses the analysis of single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-Seq) datasets, focusing on various datasets and the implications of unique molecular identifiers (UMIs) versus read counts. The authors provide a detailed overview of several scRNA-Seq datasets, including those from different tissues and cell types, highlighting the number of cells, UMIs, and the nature of each dataset, including technical and biological controls.

The authors emphasize the difference between UMI counts and read counts, illustrating that UMI counts are computationally de-duplicated and follow a discrete distribution without zero inflation, unlike read counts which are zero-inflated due to PCR duplicates. They explain the process of generating UMI counts from mRNA transcripts, noting that various stages of the scRNA-Seq protocol lead to losses of transcripts, resulting in UMI counts that are significantly lower than the original mRNA transcript count.

Furthermore, the paper introduces a multinomial sampling model for UMI counts, where the relative abundance of transcripts influences the likelihood of being counted as UMIs. The authors note that genes with higher relative abundance are more likely to be represented in UMI counts, while those with lower abundance may not be detected at all. They also mention that the multinomial distribution can be approximated by independent Poisson distributions, which aids in computational analysis.

Overall, the findings underscore the complexity of interpreting scRNA-Seq data and the importance of understanding the limitations and characteristics of UMI counts in capturing gene expression patterns in single cells.
