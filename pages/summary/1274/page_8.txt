TOWNES et al. (2019) present a fast approximation to GLM-PCA tailored for single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-Seq) data. The authors argue that traditional PCA, which involves centering and scaling data as z-scores, is inadequate due to the skewed, discrete nature of scRNA-Seq data that often includes many zeros. They propose replacing the normal null model with a multinomial null model, which better reflects the data-generating process. This new approach uses deviance and Pearson residuals as alternatives to z-scores, facilitating a rapid transformation that directly models counts and allows for adjustments for covariates like cell cycle signatures or batch labels.

The computational efficiency of the multinomial models is highlighted, with GLM-PCA demonstrating a significant speed advantage over ZINB-WAVE, processing 68,000 cells in under 7 minutes. The deviance and Pearson residual methods also showed comparable speeds to PCA. Feature selection is enhanced through the use of deviance, which effectively identifies informative genes without the biases associated with normalization, as it operates directly on raw UMI counts. The study finds a strong correlation between high deviance and high expression genes, while highly variable genes show weaker correlations, underscoring the effectiveness of deviance in selecting truly informative genes.

In terms of clustering performance, dimension reduction using GLM-PCA and its multinomial residuals outperformed other methods, with multinomial deviance-based feature selection yielding better results than traditional highly variable gene methods. The authors conducted systematic comparisons across various normalization, feature selection, and dimension reduction methods, assessing clustering accuracy using adjusted Rand index and silhouette coefficient. Overall, their findings support the use of multinomial models in enhancing analysis of scRNA-Seq data.
