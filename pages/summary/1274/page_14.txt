The paper by Townes et al. (2019) discusses a feature selection method utilizing deviance to identify informative genes in single-cell RNA sequencing data. The authors argue that genes with constant expression across cells are not informative and can be modeled using a multinomial null model. They introduce the binomial deviance as a more practical approximation for screening genes, where a high deviance indicates poor fit to the null model, highlighting genes with biological variation.

The study systematically compares various feature selection methods, including highly expressed, highly variable, and highly deviant genes, while excluding highly dropout genes due to their poor performance in clustering. Different normalization, transformation, and dimension reduction techniques such as PCA and ZINB-WAVE are evaluated across multiple parameter settings and clustering algorithms.

The authors acknowledge contributions from colleagues and provide a detailed funding section, indicating their financial support from various grants. They also emphasize the availability of their methods and datasets for public use. Overall, the paper presents a robust framework for identifying informative genes in single-cell transcriptomics, enhancing downstream analysis and clustering performance.
