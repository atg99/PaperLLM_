The paper by Townes et al. introduces a novel approach called GLM-PCA, which integrates generalized linear models into principal component analysis for analyzing high-dimensional count data, such as single-cell RNA sequencing. The method optimizes a generalized version of mean squared error known as deviance, allowing for various model features, including intercept terms and overdispersion. Unlike traditional PCA, which minimizes MSE, GLM-PCA minimizes deviance, providing a more coherent framework that accounts for data-generating mechanisms.

The authors detail the mathematical formulation of the objective function, emphasizing the use of total unique molecular identifiers (UMIs) as an offset for normalization. The optimization process involves iterative Fisher scoring, which is more numerically stable when updating latent dimensions rather than rows or columns. The method also allows for the visualization of biological variability through the projection of latent factors.

Additionally, the paper discusses the residuals derived from GLM-PCA, specifically deviance and Pearson residuals, and their relationship to the underlying statistical models. Deviance residuals are shown to offer a better symmetry compared to Pearson residuals, which can also be adapted for a negative binomial null model. The authors validate their approach by simulating different clusters of cells and comparing the accuracy of various residual approximations through PCA.

Overall, GLM-PCA presents a significant advancement in the analysis of count data, enabling researchers to better capture biological variability and interpret complex datasets.
