In this paper, Townes et al. present a critical examination of the standard pipeline used in single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-Seq) analysis, particularly focusing on unique molecular identifier (UMI) counts. They highlight that many methods currently employed for scRNA-Seq analysis, including normalization and dimension reduction, are adapted from bulk RNA-Seq techniques, which may not be suitable for the sparse and discrete nature of UMI counts.

The authors detail the typical steps in the scRNA-Seq analysis pipeline, starting with the normalization of raw counts, followed by feature selection of highly variable or highly expressed genes, and concluding with principal component analysis (PCA) for dimension reduction. They point out that the normalization methods often struggle with the high number of zeros in UMI data, and the introduction of pseudocounts can lead to biases. They argue that PCA, while popular, may not adequately handle the characteristics of UMI counts, leading to distorted results.

To address these issues, the authors propose a unifying statistical framework based on the multinomial distribution, which avoids the complications of zero inflation and more accurately reflects the underlying data generating mechanism of UMI counts. They introduce a novel approach called GLM-PCA, which generalizes PCA to work with exponential family likelihoods and operates directly on raw counts, thus circumventing normalization pitfalls. Additionally, they present a method for feature selection using closed-form deviance statistics.

The paper concludes with a comparison of various methods using publicly available UMI count datasets, establishing best practices for scRNA-Seq analysis. The authors emphasize the need for rigorous statistical justification in the methods used for analyzing UMI counts, advocating for approaches that are tailored to the specific characteristics of scRNA-Seq data.
