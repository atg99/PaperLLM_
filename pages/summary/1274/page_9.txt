In the study by Townes et al., the authors investigate the effectiveness of different dimension reduction and feature selection methods in enhancing the performance of Seurat clustering. They focus on GLM-PCA, which utilizes a Poisson approximation to multinomial distribution, and compare it against traditional PCA methods applied to deviance residuals and log-transformed counts per million (CPM). Their findings indicate that both GLM-PCA and the residuals method yield similar results on one dataset, but GLM-PCA demonstrates superior performance on another dataset. Notably, the performance of ZINB-WAVE factors declines as the number of latent dimensions increases, while GLM-PCA maintains robustness under these conditions.

Additionally, the authors emphasize the efficacy of the deviance feature selection method, which outperforms the highly variable gene approach in terms of accuracy across both datasets and dimension reduction methods. Overall, the study highlights the advantages of GLM-PCA and deviance-based feature selection in achieving better cluster separation and maintaining performance consistency.
