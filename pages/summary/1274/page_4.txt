In the paper by Townes et al., the authors investigate the relationship between zero counts and unique molecular identifiers (UMIs) in single-cell RNA sequencing data. They demonstrate that the number of zeros in a sample is inversely related to the total number of UMIs, and they validate this using negative control data. Specifically, they find that the empirical probability of a gene having zero counts aligns well with theoretical predictions based on a multinomial model, confirming that UMI counts are not zero-inflated.

To further assess the validity of the multinomial model, the authors evaluate the goodness-of-fit of several null distributions against two negative control datasets. They find that the multinomial, Dirichlet-multinomial, and Poisson distributions fit the UMI counts best, while the zero-inflated lognormal and Dirichlet-multinomial distributions are more suitable for read counts. These findings are consistent with previous studies indicating that the relationship between average expression and zero probability follows the predicted theoretical curve.

Although there is a debate regarding the appropriateness of the Poisson versus negative binomial models for high count data, the authors find that the Poisson model provides a better fit for their high-count dataset, without ruling out the negative binomial as a feasible alternative. Overall, their results indicate that the multinomial model effectively characterizes the sampling distributions of both technical and biological replicates in their analysis of negative control data.
