The paper discusses various approaches to utilizing machine learning (ML) in quantum applications, particularly focusing on the enhancement of Monte Carlo sampling methods for classical and quantum systems. Early applications employed energy-based generative models for classical systems, while advancements have led to "self-learning" Monte Carlo techniques being adapted for fermionic systems, effectively reducing autocorrelation times compared to conventional Markov Chain Monte Carlo methods.

Recent developments have seen the integration of state-of-the-art generative ML models to improve sampling efficiency in challenging classical problems, such as spin glasses. This transition involves reformulating sampling tasks into finding efficient autoregressive deep network representations, which has also been extended to quantum systems, allowing for a normalized autoregressive wave-function representation that bypasses traditional Monte Carlo methods.

The paper highlights the challenges posed by the sign problem in quantum Monte Carlo techniques, particularly in fermionic models and frustrated spin Hamiltonians. It suggests that ML approaches may offer solutions to mitigate the sign problem and enhance the inference of fermionic phases through Green’s function analysis. Additionally, ML techniques are proposed as alternatives to conventional methods for reconstructing spectral functions from imaginary-time correlations.

The discussion also covers the classification of many-body quantum phases, emphasizing the complexity of identifying elusive phases in both numerical simulations and experiments. ML schemes have gained traction for phase identification, particularly in synthetic data derived from quantum system simulations. Notable challenges in this context include quantum many-body localization and topological phases of matter. The paper details various studies that have effectively utilized ML for phase transitions, highlighting the need for tailored training strategies to address the unique characteristics of these complex phases.
