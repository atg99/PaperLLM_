The paper discusses the transformative impact of machine learning (ML) on various fields, particularly in physics, likening its significance to that of computers in previous decades. ML's primary objective is to identify patterns in data to inform decision-making, exemplified by applications such as self-driving cars where data from sensors is processed to recognize potential dangers. Recent advancements, particularly through deep learning and reinforcement learning, have demonstrated ML's capabilities in previously inaccessible areas, raising expectations for its role in achieving general artificial intelligence.

The authors highlight the growing interest among physicists in leveraging ML for fundamental research, noting the shared goals of both fields in data analysis and modeling complex systems. However, a fundamental difference exists: physicists rely on their expertise to inform models, while ML approaches are data-driven and often opaque, leading to mixed reactions among scientists.

The review aims to provide a comprehensive overview of the intersections between ML and various physics domains, including statistical physics, high-energy physics, cosmology, many-body quantum systems, quantum computation, and computational chemistry. Each section explores the applications and challenges posed by ML within these contexts, alongside advancements in instrumentation suited for ML tasks.

Additionally, the paper introduces key ML concepts, categorizing learning problems into supervised, unsupervised, and reinforcement learning. It references various resources, including textbooks and online courses, to support further understanding for physicists interested in ML. Overall, the review presents a balanced account of the potential of ML in enhancing research within physics while acknowledging the complexities and reservations associated with its application.
