This paper discusses the advancements in machine learning (ML) techniques applied to electronic structure calculations, particularly in the context of density functional theory (DFT). It highlights the use of kernel ridge regression to address issues with noisy derivatives by projecting energies back onto the learned space through principal component analysis (PCA). The paper also explores the application of neural networks (NN) in learning the exchange-correlation potential and generating ground state electron densities corresponding to nuclear potentials, which enhances the prediction of molecular energies and allows for reactive molecular dynamics simulations.

Notably, the paper mentions successful implementations of approximate electron densities, like those derived from isolated atoms, to predict molecular energies. Additionally, it describes how local electron densities from embedded atom methods have been employed to train Bayesian ML models for calculating total system energies in periodic crystalline solids. The scalability of these methods is demonstrated through a neural network model designed for large DFT simulations of 2D porous graphene sheets.

The paper concludes that while ML presents new opportunities for learning electron densities and corresponding system energies, incorporating physically-motivated constraints into ML-based methods has yielded mixed results. Some improvements have been observed when multiple constraints are applied, suggesting a complex interplay between ML techniques and traditional physical principles in achieving accurate electronic structure predictions.
