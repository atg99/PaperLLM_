The paper discusses the evolution of machine learning techniques in particle physics, highlighting the transition from simple feed-forward neural networks to more complex architectures such as boosted decision trees and deep learning methods. The introduction of deep learning around 2014 marked a significant advancement, particularly in event selection and jet tagging tasks at hadron colliders like the LHC. Deep learning's ability to handle high-dimensional data without the need for engineered features has proven advantageous, especially in classifying boosted jets and identifying their progenitors.

In jet physics, the challenge of varying particle numbers in jets has led to the exploration of recurrent neural networks and other advanced architectures that accommodate this variability. Research has also focused on hybrid approaches that integrate domain knowledge, such as tree-structured networks inspired by natural language processing techniques.

Moreover, machine learning has been applied beyond classification and regression, extending to density estimation and modeling complex scenarios in particle physics. The use of Gaussian processes for modeling inhomogeneous Poisson point processes highlights the interpretability of these methods, connecting them to physicists' causal models.

In neutrino physics, the application of convolutional neural networks to analyze energy deposit patterns in large detector volumes demonstrates the effectiveness of deep learning in extracting meaningful insights from complex data. The NOνA experiment serves as a prime example of how deep learning can enhance the analysis of neutrino interactions, improving detection capabilities and overall experimental outcomes.
