In supervised learning, the training process involves adjusting the weights of the connections between neurons to minimize the empirical risk, which is quantified by a loss function. The optimization is commonly performed using gradient descent techniques, including variants like stochastic gradient descent (SGD), which utilizes mini-batches of data to enhance efficiency.

Key tasks in supervised learning include classification, where the model predicts discrete labels, and regression, which involves estimating continuous values. Regularization techniques, such as ridge regression and support vector machines, are employed to prevent overfitting and improve model generalization.

Overall, supervised learning encompasses a range of methods, and the choice of algorithm, initialization, and hyperparameters significantly impacts performance. Neural networks, particularly multi-layer feed-forward architectures, have emerged as powerful tools in this domain, enabling advancements in various applications.
