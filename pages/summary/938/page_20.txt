The paper discusses the application of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) in solving inverse problems, particularly in astrophysics for tasks like image denoising and recovery. It highlights the challenges of defining physically motivated priors in scenarios such as gravitational lensing. Recent advancements include recurrent inference machines that have shown success in such applications.

The paper further delves into the complexities of statistical inference in high-dimensional spaces, particularly how traditional methods like Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) face difficulties when the likelihood function is intractable. In particle physics and cosmology, simulations often yield complex latent variables, making direct likelihood estimation impractical.

To address these challenges, likelihood-free inference methods, such as Approximate Bayesian Computation (ABC), have been developed. ABC approximates likelihoods through distance metrics between simulated outputs and observed data, though it struggles with high-dimensional data due to the need for effective summary statistics. Innovations like inference compilation using neural networks have emerged to enhance this process.

The paper also notes that while ABC is often interchangeably used with the broader concept of likelihood-free inference, various other techniques exist for estimating approximate likelihoods or likelihood ratios, including neural density estimation and classifier training. These methods are particularly relevant in particle physics, where numerous studies have employed surrogate likelihoods derived from simulated datasets to achieve significant discoveries, such as the Higgs boson.
