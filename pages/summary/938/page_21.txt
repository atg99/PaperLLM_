The paper discusses the application of machine learning techniques in likelihood-free inference, particularly in the context of experimental particle physics and cosmology. Likelihood-free inference is essential for situations where the likelihood function is intractable, which is common in complex models like effective field theory (EFT) used in particle physics.

Recent advancements include the development of neural network-based approaches that leverage simulations to provide training data for surrogate models, improving sample efficiency in inference processes. The paper highlights the joint likelihood ratio and joint score as tractable components that can enhance training data, as visualized in the accompanying schematic.

Specific techniques such as adversarial variational optimization (AVO) are introduced, which adapt generative adversarial networks to optimize simulation parameters instead of neural network weights. This is particularly challenging due to the non-differentiable nature of many scientific simulators, but variational optimization offers a solution through a differentiable surrogate loss function.

In cosmology, the paper cites early applications of Approximate Bayesian Computation (ABC) in studying galaxy formation and the evolution of the Hubble parameter. The development of tools like CosmoABC aimed to streamline the application of ABC methodologies. More recent efforts involve learning summary statistics that maximize Fisher information to effectively summarize high-dimensional cosmological data, with ongoing work to make these statistics robust against systematic uncertainties.

Overall, the paper illustrates the intersection of machine learning and statistical inference in addressing complex modeling challenges in physics and cosmology.
