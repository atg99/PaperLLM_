The paper discusses the application of neural quantum states (NQS) in reconstructing quantum states from measurements, emphasizing the effectiveness of unsupervised and variational learning techniques. In simpler scenarios where measurements are based on Born's rule, standard learning methods can suffice to reconstruct the quantum state. However, for more complex cases involving mixed states or requiring information from multiple measurement bases, quantum state tomography becomes necessary, leading to the development of specialized NQS approaches.

Variational learning is highlighted as a prominent application of NQS, particularly for solving many-body quantum problems. This involves using NQS representations to approximate solutions to the Schrödinger equation through variational methods. The paper notes that while these methods generally involve greater computational demands than traditional learning schemes, they have shown competitive results across various quantum models. Notably, advancements have been made in two-dimensional lattice models and topological phases of matter.

In addition, the paper covers the use of NQS in solving the time-dependent Schrödinger equation and the application of advanced learning techniques, such as stochastic reconfiguration, which is adapted for variational learning in quantum contexts. The ongoing challenge of balancing the use of deeper networks with efficient learning techniques is also addressed.

Overall, the integration of machine learning into quantum many-body simulations, particularly through NQS and Monte Carlo methods, represents a significant advancement in computational techniques for studying complex quantum systems.
