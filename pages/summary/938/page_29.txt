The paper discusses the integration of machine learning (ML) with quantum computing, particularly focusing on how ML can aid in the development and understanding of quantum systems. It highlights three primary problems where ML techniques have been applied: quantum state tomography, quantum control, and quantum error correction. Quantum state tomography (QST) aims to reconstruct the density matrix of a quantum state using measurements, which is crucial for assessing the performance of quantum hardware. Traditional QST methods require a prohibitive number of measurements, but ML strategies, such as compressed sensing and deep learning, have shown promise in reducing these requirements.

ML approaches can exploit structural properties of the density matrix to enhance the efficiency of QST. For instance, compressed sensing can significantly reduce measurement needs from an exponential scale to a logarithmic scale based on the rank of the density matrix. Recent developments include utilizing neural networks to optimize measurement bases and reconstruct density matrices from measurement outcomes. Additionally, tensor-network parameterizations have emerged as a method to tackle large and low-entangled quantum systems effectively.

Overall, the intersection of quantum computing and ML presents a fertile ground for innovation, with ML likely becoming an essential tool alongside traditional quantum methods as research progresses.
