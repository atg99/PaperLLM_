In this paper, the authors present a novel approach to enhance Off-Policy Actor-Critic (OffP-AC) learning through the introduction of a meta-critic network. This meta-critic serves as an augmentation to the traditional critic by providing an additional loss that facilitates the actor's learning process. Unlike the vanilla critic, which primarily estimates the action-value function, the meta-critic is designed to expedite learning by maximizing the actor's learning progress.

The framework operates by training the actor using losses from both the critic and the meta-critic, while the critic continues to be trained through standard temporal-difference methods. This dual training approach leverages randomly sampled transitions, resulting in improved sample efficiency compared to existing on-policy meta-learners. A key advantage of the meta-critic is its ability to be learned online within a single task, contrasting with traditional meta-learning paradigms that require data from entire task families.

The authors formalize their learning process as a bi-level optimization problem, where the upper level involves meta-critic learning and the lower level concerns conventional learning. This joint optimization is executed iteratively online, enhancing the actor’s performance more effectively than prior methods such as LIRPG, which only provides a scalar intrinsic reward for on-policy optimization.

The paper evaluates the proposed method against several continuous control benchmarks, demonstrating significant improvements in contemporary OffP-AC algorithms, including DDPG, TD3, and SAC. The results highlight the effectiveness of the online meta-critic in fostering superior learning efficiency and performance in reinforcement learning tasks.
