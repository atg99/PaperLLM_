The paper discusses the development of a meta-critic framework designed to enhance the performance of reinforcement learning (RL) algorithms, specifically in the context of off-policy actor-critic methods. The actor is updated using a loss derived from the critic's evaluations to increase the likelihood of actions that yield higher Q-values. The critic employs Q-learning updates to refine its action-value function.

The authors highlight the resurgence of interest in meta-learning within RL, as it has the potential to improve learning performance and sample efficiency. They differentiate their approach from existing methods by emphasizing the ability to learn the loss function online, without requiring extensive offline learning, which is a common limitation in prior studies. The proposed meta-critic operates as a differentiable loss function tailored for RL, contrasting with traditional methods that often focus on multi-task scenarios or robustness against out-of-distribution samples.

The methodology involves training a meta-critic loss that augments the standard critic loss, which is aimed at accelerating the learning process of the actor. This is framed as a bi-level optimization problem where the lower-level optimization minimizes both the standard and meta-critic losses, while the upper-level optimization ensures that the actor exhibits improved performance on validation data after training with both losses.

The paper suggests that combining the losses enhances stability and speeds up learning, indicating that successful training of the actor by the meta-critic leads to improved performance on the designated tasks. The findings contribute to advancing single-task off-policy actor-critic RL by integrating online meta-learning techniques.
