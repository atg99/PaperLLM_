The paper presents an ablation study on the performance of the Meta-Critic (MC) module in the Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) framework applied to various environments, particularly focusing on the Walker2d task. The study compares the Max and Sum Average Returns from different configurations of the Meta-Critic against baseline methods. It was found that the Meta-Critic improved performance by 15-30% in compute cost and 10% in parameter count, which is relatively minimal compared to the replay buffer’s memory requirements.

To determine if the benefits of the Meta-Critic could be replicated by simply increasing compute or model size, control experiments were conducted by adjusting the baseline's compute budget and parameter count. These experiments demonstrated that merely increasing updates or parameters did not yield consistent performance improvements and sometimes resulted in lower returns. This indicates that the advantages of the Meta-Critic cannot be solely attributed to increased compute or parameter size.

The paper discusses the introduction of an auxiliary Meta-Critic that leverages actor learning progress to enhance the learning process in off-policy actor-critic reinforcement learning methods. This approach does not require task families and can be applied to single tasks, providing a minor overhead during training and none during testing. While the method currently employs a myopic approach with a single inner step per outer step, the authors suggest that future work could explore longer look-ahead strategies, despite potential challenges such as high-variance gradients. 

In conclusion, the Meta-Critic module offers a promising direction for improving continuous control in reinforcement learning and has the potential for broader applications in multi-task and multi-domain settings.
