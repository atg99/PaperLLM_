The paper discusses the update process for a meta-critic in a reinforcement learning framework, specifically focusing on the comparison between two parameter updates, ϴ_new and ϴ_old. The objective is to determine if the meta-critic provides a beneficial source of loss that leads to improved parameter performance. The meta-critic is trained using a validation batch to prevent overfitting, ensuring sample efficiency in an off-policy setting.

The meta-loss L_meta is defined as the validation performance of the updated actor, measured by a normal critic. To enhance optimization efficiency and stability, an alternative clipped loss, L_meta_clip, is introduced, which captures the difference between actor updates with and without the meta-critic's input. This approach allows the meta-critic to assess whether its learning has improved validation performance, facilitating adjustments to its parameters.

The design of the meta-critic, denoted as h(θ), is crucial. It must depend on policy parameters and be permutation invariant to transition samples. The paper critiques a naive approach that directly uses parameter regularization, suggesting a more effective design that incorporates state information. The proposed meta-critic architecture involves a three-layer multi-layer perceptron that processes features extracted from the policy network, with two design options: one using joint features and another augmenting these features with state and action information. The final layer employs a softplus activation to mitigate overestimation typical in vanilla critics.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of the meta-critic in improving actor performance through effective loss definitions and careful design considerations, ultimately contributing to more stable and efficient reinforcement learning processes.
