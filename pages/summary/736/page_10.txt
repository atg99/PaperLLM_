The paper presents a framework for meta reinforcement learning (RL) that enhances learning efficiency by incorporating an auxiliary meta-critic trained online to maximize learning progress. This advancement could significantly benefit various applications of reinforcement learning, particularly in areas like game playing and robot control, where the speed of learning and overall performance can be improved. 

The proposed meta-critic approach also aims to reduce the energy demands associated with training agents, addressing environmental concerns. Furthermore, it seeks to facilitate learning-based control in physical robots, which currently face challenges due to sample inefficiencies in RL algorithms and the vulnerability of robots to wear and tear during extended operation.

A notable contribution of this work is the introduction of learnable reward functions, moving away from reliance on fixed, manually defined rewards. However, this complexity raises concerns regarding the interpretability of these learned rewards and the risk of agents developing undesired behaviors. Thus, the authors suggest that future research should focus on developing explainable AI techniques for analyzing reward functions to ensure they align with intended outcomes.
