In this section of the paper by Alzubaidi et al., the authors discuss the challenges of training deep learning (DL) models, particularly deep convolutional neural networks (DCNNs), which require large datasets to achieve high performance in image classification and recognition tasks. They highlight that while DCNNs can outperform human performance given sufficient data, the risk of overfitting necessitates large datasets and effective generalization of models. 

The authors point out the difficulty in acquiring labeled datasets, especially in fields like medical imaging and environmental science, where the labeling process is both costly and labor-intensive. They note that many crowdsourcing workers lack the expertise to accurately label complex medical images, leading researchers to rely on domain experts, which further complicates and slows down the data collection process.

To address these challenges, transfer learning (TL) has gained popularity, as it can improve accuracy in various tasks. However, the authors caution that TL's effectiveness can be limited when the source dataset (e.g., ImageNet with natural images) differs significantly from the target dataset (e.g., medical images). They reference studies indicating that models trained from scratch can perform comparably to those using TL in certain medical imaging contexts. 

The authors then introduce the concept of same-domain TL, where training utilizes images similar to the target dataset, exemplified by using chest X-ray images of different diseases to improve COVID-19 diagnosis accuracy. 

Finally, they propose data augmentation techniques as a viable solution to enhance the training dataset size and mitigate overfitting, emphasizing that these methods can effectively address limited data challenges.
