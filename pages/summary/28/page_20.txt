The paper discusses various loss functions used in Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) and the challenge of overfitting in model training. 

1. **Loss Functions**:
   - **Cross-Entropy or Softmax Loss Function**: This function, also known as log loss, is primarily used for evaluating CNN performance in multi-class classification problems. It outputs probabilities using softmax activations, converting non-normalized outputs from the previous layer into a probability distribution. The mathematical representation includes the output class probability and the cross-entropy loss formula.
   - **Euclidean Loss Function**: Commonly referred to as mean square error, this function is utilized in regression problems. Its mathematical expression quantifies the estimated loss based on the differences between predicted and actual values.
   - **Hinge Loss Function**: This loss function is employed in binary classification tasks, particularly with Support Vector Machines (SVMs). It focuses on maximizing the margin between classes, with a typical margin set to 1.

2. **Regularization in CNNs**: The paper highlights the issue of overfitting in CNN models, where the model performs well on training data but poorly on unseen test data. Conversely, an under-fitted model fails to learn adequately from training data. A model that performs well on both is termed "just-fitted." The paper suggests the importance of regularization techniques to mitigate overfitting and provides further insights into the concepts of overfitting and underfitting in subsequent sections. 

Overall, the discussion emphasizes the significance of selecting appropriate loss functions and implementing regularization strategies to enhance the generalization capability of CNN models.
