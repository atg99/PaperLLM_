In the paper by Alzubaidi et al., various methodologies for enhancing model interpretability and addressing challenges in deep learning (DL) are discussed. The authors explore back-propagation-based and perturbation-based techniques for interpreting model outputs. Perturbation-based approaches involve altering parts of the input to observe changes in output, which, while computationally complex, are straightforward. Conversely, back-propagation techniques assess the importance of input features by propagating output signals back to the input layer, demonstrating their utility in various scenarios.

The paper emphasizes the importance of confidence scores in DL predictions, which indicate the model's certainty about its predictions. These scores are crucial in applications like healthcare, where they help mitigate the risks of unreliable predictions. The authors note that confidence scores often require post-scaling due to potential overconfidence in predictions. Several methods, including Bayesian Binning into Quantiles, isotonic regression, and temperature scaling, are discussed as techniques for achieving reliable probability scores.

Additionally, the concept of catastrophic forgetting is introduced, which occurs when a DL model learns new information at the expense of previously acquired knowledge. This is particularly relevant in dynamic fields where data is continuously updated. The authors highlight that while retraining a model from scratch is an option, it is resource-intensive and may destabilize prior learning. To combat catastrophic forgetting, the paper mentions three categories of machine learning techniques: those based on regularization, rehearsal training, and dynamic neural networks, all aimed at preserving learned information while integrating new data.
