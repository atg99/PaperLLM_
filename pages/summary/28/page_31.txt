The paper discusses the architecture of GoogLeNet, highlighting its innovative approach to convolutional neural networks (CNNs) through the use of small blocks inspired by the network-in-network (NIN) architecture. This design allows for capturing both channel and spatial information at various resolutions using convolutional filters of different sizes (5x5, 3x3, and 1x1). The paper emphasizes how GoogLeNet improves CNN efficiency and learning capacity by replacing traditional convolutional layers with micro-neural networks and introducing a bottleneck layer with 1x1 convolutions to regulate computation.

Additionally, GoogLeNet's use of sparse connections helps mitigate redundant information, which reduces the overall parameter count from 40 million to 5 million. The architecture also incorporates a Global Average Pooling (GAP) layer instead of a fully connected (FC) layer, further decreasing connection density. The paper notes the implementation of RmsProp as an optimizer and batch normalization to enhance performance. Despite these advancements, the paper acknowledges shortcomings in GoogLeNet's heterogeneous topology, which necessitates adaptation between modules, and other representation issues.
