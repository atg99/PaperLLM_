In the paper by Alzubaidi et al., the authors discuss the role of non-linear activation layers in Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs). These layers, which follow learnable layers such as fully connected (FC) and convolutional layers, introduce non-linearity to the input-output mapping, enabling CNNs to learn complex patterns. The ability of activation functions to be differentiable is crucial for error back-propagation during network training.

The paper highlights several commonly used activation functions:

1. **Sigmoid**: Outputs values between 0 and 1, characterized by an S-shaped curve.
2. **Tanh**: Similar to sigmoid but outputs values between -1 and 1.
3. **ReLU (Rectified Linear Unit)**: The most widely used function in CNNs, converting inputs to positive values and offering lower computational load. However, it can encounter issues like the "Dying ReLU," where neurons become inactive due to large gradients.

To address the Dying ReLU problem, the authors introduce alternatives:

1. **Leaky ReLU**: Allows a small, non-zero gradient for negative inputs, represented by a leak factor, commonly set to a small value like 0.001.
2. **Noisy ReLU**: Adds Gaussian noise to the ReLU function, enhancing its robustness.

These activation functions are essential for improving the performance and training efficiency of CNNs.
