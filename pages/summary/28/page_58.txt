The paper discusses the computational challenges associated with training deep neural networks (DNNs), particularly focusing on the use of GPUs, CPUs, and FPGAs. It highlights that training on large datasets, such as those exceeding 14 million images, requires significant computational resources, with predictions of over 1020 FLOPS needed as DNN complexity increases. Historically, GPU utilization has improved training times, yet sessions can still take days or weeks, necessitating optimization strategies to reduce learning time.

The authors emphasize the impact of memory bandwidth and capacity on training performance, noting that DNNs often experience a high computation-to-bandwidth ratio. A comparative analysis presented in Table 3 outlines the strengths and weaknesses of different processing devices. For instance, while CPUs are easier to program and have stable architectures, FPGAs offer greater customization and lower latency. GPUs excel in processing capabilities but often do not reach their maximum performance due to bandwidth limitations.

The paper concludes that there is no one-size-fits-all solution for DNN training, as each device type has unique advantages and trade-offs that must be considered based on specific application needs.
