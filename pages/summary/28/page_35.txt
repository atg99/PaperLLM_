In the paper by Alzubaidi et al., DenseNet is highlighted for its unique architecture that enhances the network's ability to differentiate between newly added and preserved information. This is achieved through the concatenation of features from previous layers, which facilitates a more efficient gradient flow throughout the network. As a result, DenseNet not only improves information transfer but also acts as a regularizer, helping to mitigate overfitting, especially with smaller datasets.

On the other hand, ResNext is introduced as an advanced version of the Inception Network, also referred to as the Aggregated Residual Transform Network. It incorporates the concept of cardinality, which adds an extra dimension to the transformation set, enhancing the network's resource management and learning capabilities. Unlike the Inception network that requires separate customization for each layer, ResNext integrates features from ResNet, VGG, and Inception, using a VGG-like topology with 3×3 filters in its architecture. The incorporation of multi-transformations within the split, transform, and merge blocks further boosts performance, particularly as cardinality increases, as demonstrated by Xie et al.
