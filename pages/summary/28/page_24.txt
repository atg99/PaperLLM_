The paper discusses the optimization of learning algorithms in deep neural networks, focusing on the Adam optimization strategy. Adam is highlighted for its efficiency in memory usage and computational power, particularly in training deep networks. It combines the advantages of Momentum and RMSprop by calculating an adaptive learning rate for each parameter, utilizing squared gradients to adjust the learning rate akin to RMSprop, while also employing a moving average of the gradient similar to Momentum.

The section further elaborates on the design of algorithms, specifically backpropagation. It introduces a notation for weights within the network, defining weights as connections between neurons across different layers. For instance, the weight from the first neuron in the first layer to the first neuron in the second layer is denoted as w2_11. The paper also emphasizes the importance of biases for each neuron, indicating that each layer can have its own distinct biases. Additionally, it discusses the parameters of the network, including the number of layers, neurons, and connections, providing a framework for understanding the structure of multi-layer perceptrons (MLPs).
