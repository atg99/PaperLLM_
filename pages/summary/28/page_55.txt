The paper discusses advancements in brain tumor segmentation techniques, emphasizing the importance of accurate segmentation in surgical preparation to minimize neurological damage. Traditional manual segmentation methods are time-consuming and prone to error, prompting the need for automated solutions. Various studies are reviewed, highlighting the use of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and other machine learning techniques for improved segmentation accuracy.

Wadhwa et al. provided an overview of MRI brain tumor segmentation, while Akkus et al. reviewed different metrics and CNN architectures, detailing several relevant competitions and datasets. Chen et al. proposed a CNN approach incorporating the DeepMedic model and a novel training scheme, achieving significant results on the BRATS datasets. Hu et al. introduced a multi-cascaded CNN combined with conditional random fields, yielding excellent outcomes compared to existing methods.

Moeskops et al. utilized three parallel CNNs with varying input patch sizes to classify MRI brain images, achieving satisfactory accuracy. While most studies focus on 2D slices, Milletare et al. implemented a 3D CNN for prostate MRI segmentation, inspired by the U-Net architecture, achieving competitive results. Pereira et al. developed a deeper CNN model that performed well in BRATS challenges, while Havaei et al. explored 2D CNN architectures, significantly reducing execution time compared to previous winners. Overall, these advancements signify a shift towards more efficient and accurate brain tumor segmentation methodologies.
