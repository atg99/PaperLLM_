The paper discusses the vanishing gradient problem encountered during the training of artificial neural networks (ANNs) using backpropagation and gradient-based learning techniques. This issue arises when the gradients become exceedingly small, preventing effective weight updates and halting further training. The sigmoid activation function, which compresses a large input space into a smaller output range, exacerbates this problem by producing minimal derivatives for large input variations. In shallow networks, this may not be significant, but deeper networks suffer as the gradients diminish exponentially through multiple layers, particularly affecting the early layers that are crucial for recognizing key input features.

To mitigate the vanishing gradient problem, the paper suggests using activation functions that do not compress input space excessively, such as the ReLU function, which maintains larger derivatives. Additionally, implementing batch normalization can help by normalizing inputs and preventing squashing into a small output range, thereby maintaining sufficient gradient flow. The paper also notes that advancements in hardware, particularly GPUs, facilitate the training of deeper networks by allowing standard backpropagation techniques to be applied more efficiently, thus addressing the challenges posed by the vanishing gradient problem.
