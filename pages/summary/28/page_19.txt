The paper discusses key components of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), focusing on the Parametric Linear Units, Fully Connected Layers, and Loss Functions. 

Parametric Linear Units function similarly to Leaky ReLU, with the distinction that the leak factor is adjustable during model training, represented mathematically with a learnable weight denoted as 'a'. 

The Fully Connected Layer, typically positioned at the end of CNN architectures, connects every neuron to all neurons in the preceding layer, functioning as the CNN classifier. It operates on a vector input derived from the flattened feature maps of the last pooling or convolutional layer, with its output serving as the final result of the CNN.

Loss Functions are critical for determining the prediction error during training. They calculate the difference between the CNN's estimated output (prediction) and the actual output (label), guiding the optimization process. Various types of loss functions are applied depending on the specific problem being addressed.
