The referenced studies highlight the application of deep learning, particularly convolutional neural networks (CNNs), in medical imaging for various diseases. Zeleznik et al. focus on predicting cardiovascular risk from computed tomography, while Wang et al. utilize CNNs for detecting lymph node metastases in breast cancer. Yu et al. provide a comprehensive overview of CNNs in medical image analysis, emphasizing their state-of-the-art capabilities and future directions.

Several studies, including those by Bharati et al. and Dong et al., explore the use of hybrid deep learning approaches for lung disease detection and chest X-ray analysis. Rajpurkar et al. introduce Chexnet, which achieves radiologist-level pneumonia detection. Other notable contributions include the ChestX-ray8 database by Wang et al., aimed at weakly-supervised classification of thoracic diseases.

In Alzheimer's disease diagnostics, multiple studies, such as those by Wen et al. and Mehmood et al., utilize CNNs for classification, showcasing the potential of deep learning in neuroimaging. The development of networks for diabetic foot ulcer classification by Alzubaidi et al. and Goyal et al. illustrates the versatility of CNNs in dermatological applications.

Furthermore, the application of deep learning extends to cancer detection, with frameworks proposed by Khan et al. and Alzubaidi et al. for breast cancer classification. Skin cancer classification efforts by Hosny et al. and Kassem et al. demonstrate the effectiveness of CNNs in analyzing histopathology images.

Lastly, the use of deep learning in the context of COVID-19 detection from chest X-ray images is explored by Heidari et al. and Al-Timemy et al., reflecting the growing importance of machine learning in addressing public health challenges. Overall, these studies collectively underscore the transformative impact of deep learning technologies in medical diagnostics and imaging.
