The paper discusses various applications of deep learning (DL) and convolutional neural networks (CNNs) in image registration, particularly in the context of medical imaging and 3D reconstruction. It highlights the work of Alzubaidi et al., Mahadevan, Wang et al., and others who have contributed to this field. Notably, Mahadevan's combination of conceptual models aims to enhance artificial intelligence systems through unlabelled training schemes, while Wang et al. utilized adversarial methods with CNNs for 3D object reconstruction from 2D images.

In medical imaging, Yang et al. implemented an encoder-decoder model using MRI scans, achieving significant improvements in computation time with their Large Deformation Diffeomorphic Metric Mapping model. Miao et al. trained a CNN on synthetic X-ray images, demonstrating rapid execution times and high accuracy in registering 3D models with 2D images. Li et al. proposed a neural network for non-rigid registration between lateral cephalograms and volumetric cone-beam CT images.

The paper also emphasizes the growing complexity and capability of machine learning (ML) and DL approaches, noting the rapid increase in deep neural network (DNN) layers, which has outpaced traditional growth metrics like Moore's Law. This advancement enables more effective execution of applications that were previously challenging due to computational limitations. The paper concludes that substantial computational resources are essential for training large models on extensive datasets, such as ImageNet, which contains millions of images.
