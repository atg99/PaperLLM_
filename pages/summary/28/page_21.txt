The paper discusses several techniques for improving model performance and preventing overfitting in neural networks. 

1. **Dropout**: This technique involves randomly dropping neurons during training epochs to promote feature selection across the entire neuron group. It ensures that the model learns independent features, as dropped neurons do not participate in forward or back-propagation during training. The full network is used for predictions during testing.

2. **Drop-Weights**: Similar to dropout, this method drops the connections (weights) between neurons instead of the neurons themselves during training epochs, maintaining the same principle of promoting feature learning.

3. **Data Augmentation**: To combat overfitting, data augmentation artificially increases the training dataset size using various techniques. This approach allows the model to learn from a more extensive range of data.

4. **Batch Normalization**: This method normalizes output activations to follow a unit Gaussian distribution by subtracting the mean and dividing by the standard deviation at each layer. It addresses the internal covariance shift caused by continuous weight updates during training, which can slow convergence. Advantages of batch normalization include preventing vanishing gradients, improving weight initialization, reducing convergence time, and decreasing training dependency on hyper-parameters.

Overall, these techniques contribute to enhancing model robustness and efficiency in training.
