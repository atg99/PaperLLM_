In this section, the authors discuss the mechanics of backpropagation in a neural network with two layers. They define the error associated with an individual input as the difference between the label (d) and the output (y). The process of backpropagation involves adjusting the weights and biases based on the cost function, represented by the error. This is achieved by computing the partial derivatives of the error with respect to the weights and biases, denoted as ∂E/∂wh_ij and ∂E/∂bh_j. 

To facilitate this computation, a local error variable, δ1_j, is introduced for each neuron in the hidden layer. The output error for δ1_j is calculated for all neurons in the output layer, with the error of the epoch represented as e(k). The derivative of the activation function at the output, ϑ′(vj(k)), is also utilized in this calculation.
