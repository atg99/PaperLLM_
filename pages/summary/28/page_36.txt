The paper discusses advancements in deep learning architectures, particularly focusing on ResNext and WideResNet. ResNext employs 1x1 filters to regulate the length of features before applying a 3x3 convolution, utilizing skipping connections for optimized training. Conversely, WideResNet addresses the feature reuse problem inherent in deep residual networks by widening the network instead of deepening it. This approach, proposed by Zagoruyko and Komodakis, enhances performance by increasing the network's width through an additional factor, k, while maintaining the core learning ability of residual units.

WideResNet's design leads to better representational capacity but also presents challenges such as exploding and vanishing gradients and time-consuming training processes. He et al. tackled the feature reuse issue by incorporating dropout in residual blocks, while Huang et al. introduced the stochastic depth concept to mitigate slow learning and gradient issues. The paper notes that WideResNet has double the parameters of ResNet but offers improved training efficiency. It also highlights that many architectures prior to residual networks were wider than ResNet, leading to the establishment of wider residual networks. Additionally, placing dropout between convolutional layers rather than within residual blocks enhances learning effectiveness in WideResNet. The discussion concludes with a mention of the increasing depth of feature maps in multi-convolutional layers, referencing previous deep CNN architectures.
