The paper discusses advancements in deep learning (DL) models, particularly focusing on the use of 1×1 filters in convolution, which enhances nonlinearity and allows for deeper network architectures that can be regularized using dropout. A significant innovation mentioned is the Global Average Pooling (GAP) layer, which substitutes fully connected layers and reduces model parameters while maintaining feature map dimensions. 

The paper also introduces ZefNet, developed from DeconvNet by Zeiler and Fergus in 2013, which aims to visualize CNN performance and neuron activation. This visualization helps in understanding the internal feature representation at each layer, allowing for optimization of deep belief networks and auto-encoders. ZefNet's reverse mapping of convolutional outputs aids in monitoring learning and identifying performance issues in CNNs, as demonstrated experimentally with AlexNet. The findings led to adjustments in CNN topology and parameter optimization, enhancing overall performance by refining the first two convolutional layers.
