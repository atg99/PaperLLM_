The introduction highlights the rapid proliferation of machine learning (ML) across various domains, notably deep learning (DL), which has become essential due to advancements in data acquisition and hardware, particularly High Performance Computing (HPC). DL, an evolution of traditional neural networks, excels in performance through its multi-layer learning models that simultaneously utilize transformations and graph technologies. The paper emphasizes the importance of data representation in ML, noting that effective representation significantly enhances algorithm performance. 

Feature engineering has historically been a labor-intensive process, requiring domain expertise to construct features from raw data. In contrast, DL automates feature extraction, minimizing human intervention and enabling the extraction of both low-level and high-level features through its layered architecture. This approach mirrors the human brain's ability to process and classify information from sensory input.

The paper aims to provide a comprehensive overview of DL, covering its core concepts, architectures, challenges, applications, computational tools, and evolution. Among the various DL architectures, Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) are highlighted for their ability to autonomously identify significant features, contributing to the widespread adoption of DL techniques in contemporary research.
