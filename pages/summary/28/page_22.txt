This section of the paper by Alzubaidi et al. discusses the learning process in Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), focusing on optimizer selection and the associated enhancements. It highlights two critical aspects: the choice of learning algorithm (optimizer) and the integration of enhancements such as AdaDelta, Adagrad, and momentum to improve output performance.

The core objective of supervised learning algorithms, including CNNs, is to minimize error through loss functions that depend on learnable parameters like biases and weights. Gradient-based learning techniques are standard for CNNs, necessitating continuous updates of network parameters across training epochs while searching for locally optimized solutions to minimize error.

The learning rate, a hyper-parameter, defines the step size for parameter updates and must be chosen carefully to avoid negatively impacting the learning process. The Gradient Descent algorithm is emphasized for its role in minimizing training error by repetitively updating network parameters based on the computed gradient of the objective function.

The paper outlines the parameter updating process through back-propagation, where gradients are propagated backward through the network layers. The mathematical representation of this process is provided, detailing the relationships between current and previous weights, learning rate, and prediction error.

Different variants of the gradient-based learning algorithm are discussed, including Batch Gradient Descent (BGD), which updates network parameters only once per training epoch by considering the entire training dataset. While BGD can lead to faster convergence and more stable gradients for smaller datasets, it requires significant computational resources and may be less efficient for larger datasets.
