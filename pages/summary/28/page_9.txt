Deep Learning (DL) techniques exhibit several key characteristics, including robustness, generalization, and scalability. Robustness ensures that DL models can handle variations in input data effectively. Generalization, often achieved through transfer learning, allows the same DL techniques to be applied across different data types and applications, particularly beneficial in scenarios with limited data. Scalability is highlighted by the ability of models like ResNet, which has 1202 layers, to operate efficiently on supercomputing scales, as demonstrated by implementations at institutions like Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory.

DL approaches are categorized into three primary types: unsupervised, semi-supervised (partially supervised), and supervised learning. Deep supervised learning focuses on labeled datasets, enabling models such as recurrent neural networks (RNNs), convolutional neural networks (CNNs), and deep neural networks (DNNs) to learn effectively from input-output pairs. While this method can leverage prior knowledge for improved data output, it risks overfitting if the training set lacks representative samples.

Deep semi-supervised learning utilizes semi-labeled datasets and often incorporates techniques like generative adversarial networks (GANs) and deep reinforcement learning (DRL). This approach reduces the dependency on labeled data but may suffer from inaccuracies due to irrelevant features in the training data. An example application of this technique is in text document classification.
