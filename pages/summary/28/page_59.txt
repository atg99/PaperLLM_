The paper discusses the performance characteristics of various computational approaches—CPU, GPU, and FPGA—used in deep learning (DL) tasks. 

The CPU-based approach highlights the robust network connectivity and storage capabilities of CPU nodes, which are versatile but fall short in raw computational power compared to GPUs and FPGAs. This limitation is primarily due to their need for enhanced network and memory capacities for unprocessed computations.

The GPU-based approach emphasizes the efficiency of GPUs in executing parallel computing operations essential for DL, such as matrix multiplication and convolutions. The integration of high-bandwidth memory (HBM) in modern GPUs significantly boosts performance, achieving a performance ratio of 10-20 times compared to CPUs for dense linear algebra tasks. GPUs can contain up to sixty-four computational units, maximizing parallel processing, with peak performances reaching up to 25 TFLOPS for half-precision calculations. They are particularly optimized for deep neural network (DNN) training and can also enhance inference operations.

The FPGA-based approach notes the widespread use of FPGAs in deep learning tasks, especially as inference accelerators. FPGAs can be dynamically reconfigured to optimize performance and reduce overhead compared to GPUs. While they may have lower floating-point performance, their efficiency in power consumption and latency is notable. Factors contributing to FPGA efficiency include custom hardware implementations, pruned networks, and reduced arithmetic precision. FPGAs can achieve over 80% efficiency and 15 TOPs peak performance for convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and can significantly reduce model sizes through pruning techniques, providing a substantial advantage in optimizing solutions for multilayer perceptron (MLP) neural processing.

Overall, each computational approach has its strengths and weaknesses, making them suitable for different aspects of deep learning applications.
