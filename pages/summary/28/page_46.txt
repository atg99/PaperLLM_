The paper discusses various challenges and techniques related to positional biases, imbalanced data, and interpretability in deep learning (DL) models. 

Positional biases can arise when training data is not representative of testing data, particularly in facial recognition tasks where faces need to be centered in frames. Geometric transformations, such as rotation and horizontal flipping, are effective in mitigating these biases due to their straightforward implementation. However, they require additional training time, computational resources, and careful monitoring to ensure that image labels remain unchanged. The complexities of biases that separate testing from training data extend beyond simple transformations, making it challenging to determine when these techniques should be applied.

Imbalanced data is another significant issue, especially in biological datasets where negative samples often outnumber positive ones, such as in the case of COVID-19 X-ray images. Training DL models on imbalanced data can yield poor results. To address this, the paper suggests using appropriate evaluation criteria, such as the area under the curve (AUC), and employing weighted cross-entropy loss to improve model performance on smaller classes. Techniques like down-sampling larger classes or up-sampling smaller ones can also help achieve balance. Additionally, constructing models at various hierarchical levels can accommodate the inherent structure of biological data.

Lastly, the interpretability of DL models is emphasized, as they are often viewed as black boxes. Understanding the patterns and motifs recognized by these models is crucial, particularly in fields like bioinformatics where disease diagnosis is involved. It is essential to not only know the predictions made by the model but also to understand the rationale behind them to enhance the reliability of outcomes. This can be achieved by assigning importance scores to the features used in the model's decision-making process.
