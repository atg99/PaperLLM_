The paper discusses advancements in Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN) topology, specifically focusing on the Visual Geometry Group (VGG) and GoogleNet architectures. VGG, developed by Simonyan and Zisserman, enhances image recognition capabilities by employing a multilayer model with nineteen layers more than previous networks like ZefNet and AlexNet. It utilizes small 3x3 filters instead of larger ones, proving that these can achieve similar performance while reducing computational complexity and the number of parameters. Despite not winning the 2014-ILSVRC competition, VGG gained recognition for its depth and simplicity, although its high computational cost, with around 140 million parameters, remains a drawback.

GoogleNet, which won the 2014-ILSVRC competition, aims to balance high accuracy with lower computational costs. It introduces the inception block, which integrates multiple-scale convolutional transformations, enhancing feature extraction through a combination of merging, transforming, and splitting functions. This innovative design further advances the efficiency and effectiveness of CNN architectures in image classification tasks.
