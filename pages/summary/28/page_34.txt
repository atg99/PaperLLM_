In the paper, Alzubaidi et al. discuss advancements in convolutional neural networks (CNNs), particularly focusing on Inception-ResNet and DenseNet architectures. They highlight the innovative use of 1×1 convolutions in the Inception-V3 model, which allows for the mapping of input data into smaller, isolated spaces before applying larger convolutions, enhancing correlation detection. Inception-ResNet merges inception blocks with residual learning, demonstrating that incorporating residual connections accelerates training and maintains generalization capabilities similar to Inception-V4.

Additionally, the authors address the vanishing gradient problem through DenseNet, which improves upon ResNet by establishing cross-layer connectivity. Unlike ResNet, where each layer has isolated weights and limited information contribution, DenseNet connects every layer to all preceding layers, resulting in a significantly higher number of direct connections. This architecture facilitates better information flow and feature reuse, thereby enhancing the model's performance. The paper emphasizes the importance of these architectural innovations in improving CNN efficiency and effectiveness.
