This paper justifies the use of weight decay via the L2 norm of weights as a regularization method in training deep neural networks. It is structured as follows: Section 2 reviews existing literature on the generalization ability of deep neural networks, both theoretically and empirically. Section 3 offers necessary preliminaries, while Section 4 summarizes the notation for deep networks with residual connections, referred to as the stem-vine framework. Section 5 presents the main results, which include covering bounds for deep networks and ResNet, a generalization bound for ResNet, and a practical implementation based on these theoretical findings. Section 6 contains all proofs, and Section 7 concludes the paper.

In the related works section, the importance of understanding the generalization ability of deep neural networks is highlighted. Zhang et al. demonstrate that neural networks can fit training data nearly perfectly, even with random labels, prompting further investigation into the theoretical foundations of deep learning success. Kawaguchi et al. address open problems related to generalization ability, while Harvey et al. establish upper and lower bounds on the VC-dimension of networks with ReLU activation functions, leading to generalization bounds dependent on VC-dimension and sample size. Golowich et al. improve upon previous work by presenting upper bounds on the Rademacher complexity, emphasizing the impact of network depth and providing bounds on generalization error. Neyshabur et al. investigate various methods to explain the generalization ability of deep networks, including norm-based control and robustness.
