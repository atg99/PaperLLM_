The paper "Why ResNet Works? Residuals Generalize" by Fengxiang He, Tongliang Liu, and Dacheng Tao investigates the impact of residual connections on the hypothesis complexity and generalization ability of deep neural networks. The authors establish that the upper bound of the covering number, which measures the complexity of the hypothesis space, remains the same for networks with residual connections as it does for chain-like networks, provided the total number of weight matrices and nonlinearities is fixed. This finding suggests that residual connections do not inherently increase the hypothesis complexity of neural networks.

Additionally, the authors derive a margin-based multi-class generalization bound for ResNet, illustrating that similar bounds can be applied to other advanced architectures like DenseNet and ResNeXt. The paper emphasizes the importance of regularization techniques, such as weight decay, to maintain optimal generalization capabilities by controlling the norms of weight matrices. This work contributes theoretical insights into the functioning of residual networks and offers practical guidelines for enhancing their performance.
