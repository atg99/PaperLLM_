The paper discusses the role of transformations in neural networks, specifically focusing on affine and nonlinear transformations. It highlights the use of spectrum norms of weight matrices and Lipschitz constants to quantify the effects of these transformations. A function is defined as Lipschitz continuous if it satisfies a specific condition involving its outputs and inputs, which is commonly applicable to various nonlinearities used in neural networks such as ReLU and sigmoid functions.

The paper further categorizes important tasks for deep neural networks into multi-class classification. It describes a scenario where input examples are represented in a matrix format, with each row corresponding to a data point. Neural networks are trained using methods like stochastic gradient descent to fit training data and make predictions on test data. The hypothesis function computed by a trained neural network is then transformed into a multi-class classifier by selecting the class with the highest output magnitude.

To formally express this process, the paper introduces functions computed by the stem and vines of the network. The function computed by a vine is denoted as F_V, while the stem function is denoted as F_S. The output from the stem at a specific vertex is also defined, and the final hypothesis function at that vertex incorporates outputs from both the stem and the vine through residual connections. This framework is referred to as the stem-vine framework, providing a structured approach to understanding the computations within the neural network.
