This paper discusses the performance of stochastic gradient descent (SGD) on the CIFAR-10 dataset, specifically examining the impact of different batch sizes (128 and 8192) on test errors. The findings indicate that employing weight decay allows SGD to discover flatter minima in the loss surface, which correlates with lower test errors. The paper also includes technical advancements and empirical analyses, referencing other works for broader context.

In the appendix, the authors provide proofs for several concepts omitted from the main text. The first proof establishes a covering bound for the hypothesis space induced by a single weight matrix, which is essential for subsequent proofs. This analysis utilizes the Maurey sparsification lemma. The authors introduce the covering number to quantify the complexity of output values generated by a data matrix when processed by a weight matrix. The paper concludes with proofs of covering bounds for deep neural networks and ResNet, as well as a generalization bound for ResNet, thereby reinforcing the theoretical underpinnings of their empirical findings.
