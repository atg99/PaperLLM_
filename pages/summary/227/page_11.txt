The paper discusses the hypothesis space of neural networks, specifically focusing on chain-like neural networks referred to as "vines." Each vine is constructed using multiple weight matrices and nonlinearities, forming a hypothesis space denoted as H. The authors define a covering number for this hypothesis space, which helps in analyzing its capacity. 

The text establishes that for any weight matrix, there exists a corresponding bound, and all nonlinear functions exhibit Lipschitz continuity. The covering number of the hypothesis space is influenced by the number of weight matrices and the covering numbers associated with each vine. The authors present an inequality that relates the covering number of the overall hypothesis space H to the supremum of covering numbers from the individual components.

A lemma is introduced that provides an upper bound for the covering number of chain-like deep neural networks. This lemma states that if the weight matrices lie within certain bounded balls and the input data is also constrained, the covering number can be expressed in terms of the individual covering numbers of the weight matrices.

The findings emphasize how the structure of the neural network impacts its hypothesis capacity, providing a mathematical framework for understanding the complexity of neural networks through covering numbers. A detailed proof of the results is promised in a later section.
