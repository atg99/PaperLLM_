The paper presents Lemma 1, which establishes a generalization bound for a function set H mapping from Rn0 to RnL. Given a margin, the lemma defines a modified hypothesis set Hϵ and states that for any ε in the interval (0,1), with high probability over a dataset D of size n, every function F in H satisfies a specific generalization bound. This bound is expressed in terms of the empirical Rademacher complexity of the modified hypothesis set, suggesting that finding an upper bound for this complexity directly leads to an upper bound on generalization error.

The paper references previous work that connects the Rademacher complexity with covering numbers, particularly through Lemma 2, which provides a way to bound empirical Rademacher complexity using covering numbers of the hypothesis set. The covering number is defined as the minimum number of balls of a given radius required to cover the hypothesis space.

The combination of Lemmas 1 and 2 allows for a relationship between the covering bounds of an algorithm and its generalization bounds. The subsequent sections of the paper focus on developing generalization bounds specifically for deep neural networks that incorporate residual connections, employing the covering number approach.

Additionally, the paper introduces the "stem-vine framework" as a notation system for deep neural networks with residual connections, emphasizing the topological structure these networks exhibit. The discussion includes the construction of neural networks through the integration of weight matrices and nonlinear activation functions such as ReLU, sigmoid, and max-pooling. The analysis aims to clarify the generalization capabilities of these deep neural networks.
