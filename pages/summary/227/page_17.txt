The paper discusses the impact of residual connections on the generalization capability of neural networks, particularly within the framework of ResNet. It presents two main insights: first, the influence of weight matrices on generalization is invariant regardless of their position in the network; second, the addition of an identity vine does not affect generalization. These findings provide a theoretical rationale for ResNet's competitive generalization performance compared to traditional chain-like neural networks.

The expected risk, as indicated, is the sum of empirical risk and generalization error, highlighting that residual connections help reduce training errors significantly across various tasks. The authors also explore practical implications, noting that the generalization bound is positively correlated with the norms of weight matrices. Higher norms can lead to poorer generalization, which justifies the use of weight decay—a technique that regularizes weights by incorporating their L2 norm into the objective function. This regularization helps control weight magnitudes, ultimately improving generalization ability.

The paper references a study by Li et al. that further investigates the effects of weight decay on the loss surface of deep neural networks, reinforcing the theoretical insights presented. Overall, the work provides a comprehensive analysis of how residual connections and weight decay contribute to the generalization performance of deep neural networks.
