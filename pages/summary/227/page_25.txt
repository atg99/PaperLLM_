This paper discusses the application of specific equations to prove a previous equation, focusing on the formulation of radii for hypothesis space covers using an inductive method. The proof proceeds through three steps:

1. The first step establishes a relationship between the radius of the cover computed by weight matrix A1 and nonlinearity ϵ1, showing that the radius is related to parameters from subsequent layers.
  
2. The second step extends this relationship to a general case, considering the radius computed by weight matrix Aj-1 and nonlinearity ϵj-1, demonstrating how the radius changes in the absence of connections (vines).

3. The third step incorporates the presence of a vine linking specific vertices, leading to a more complex relationship involving multiple parameters.

The results of these steps culminate in a comprehensive equation that defines the overall radius in terms of the parameters from different layers and connections. The final proof asserts that the initial radius can be expressed in relation to a defined variable, completing the argument.
