In this section, the authors examine the generalization capabilities of deep neural networks featuring residual connections, specifically focusing on ResNet as a case study. They derive a generalization bound based on a margin-based multi-class approach, utilizing the covering number of the hypothesis space. Initially, they propose an upper bound for the covering number applicable to any deep neural network within the stem-vine framework. This is followed by a specific covering bound for ResNet, leading to a generalization bound supported by previous lemmas.

The authors emphasize the necessity of preventing overfitting when introducing new structures to improve training performance. ResNet's incorporation of residual connections introduces complexity to the model, yet empirical findings show that these connections effectively reduce training error and enhance training speed without compromising generalization capability. However, prior to this work, theoretical backing for these empirical observations was lacking.

The covering bound indicates that regardless of the arrangement of weight matrices—whether in the stem or vines—the complexities of the hypothesis spaces remain unchanged when the total number of weight matrices is fixed. This suggests that the generalization performance of deep neural networks with residual connections can be as robust as that of traditional architectures, especially in worst-case scenarios. This theoretical insight helps explain the competitive generalization capabilities of residual networks compared to their chain-like counterparts while maintaining effective training performance.

The subsection concludes with Theorem 1, which presents a general covering bound for deep neural networks with residual connections, outlining the conditions and structure necessary for its application.
