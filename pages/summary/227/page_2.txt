The introduction discusses the significant advancements in deep neural networks, particularly highlighting the impact of residual connections since the introduction of ResNet. These connections have facilitated improved training and generalization abilities in various applications, including computer vision and data mining. However, the paper notes a lack of theoretical analysis regarding the relationship between residual connections and the generalization ability of deep neural networks.

The authors argue that residual connections introduce complexity by creating loops in the network architecture, which could potentially worsen generalization according to Occam's razor. This concern is particularly relevant for safety-critical applications, where errors could have severe consequences.

To address this issue, the paper investigates the hypothesis complexity associated with residual connections through the concept of covering numbers. The authors propose an upper bound for the covering number, demonstrating that this bound remains constant regardless of whether weight matrices are used in residual connections or in the traditional chain structure, as long as the total number of weight matrices is fixed. This suggests that residual connections do not inherently increase the complexity of the hypothesis space.

Furthermore, the paper establishes a generalization bound for ResNet, showing that generalization is inversely related to the product of the norms of all weight matrices. This finding leads to a practical recommendation: to enhance generalization ability, regularization techniques should be employed to manage the norms of the weight matrices effectively. The framework and results presented offer insights into the implications of residual connections on neural network performance.
