The paper introduces a novel neural network architecture characterized by a "chain-like" structure, which is referred to as the stem, and incorporates residual connections termed vines. The stem consists of a series of weight matrices and nonlinearities organized sequentially. Each weight matrix is denoted as Ai, where i ranges from 1 to L, and the corresponding nonlinearities are denoted as ϵj. The output dimensions of the weight matrices are defined by ni, with n0 representing the input data dimension and nL being the output dimension of the network. 

For simplicity, the paper assumes an equal number of weight matrices and nonlinearities (LN = L), and expresses the stem as a vector S that includes both weight matrices and nonlinearities. Vertices between these components are indexed, with specific labels assigned to facilitate reference.

The vines serve to connect the stem at various vertices, with the potential for multiple vines linking the same vertex pair. Each vine is indexed by a triple vector (s, t, i), representing the connection between vertices N(s) and N(t). Similar to the stem, each vine comprises its own set of weight matrices and nonlinearities, with varying counts for each.

The architecture allows for complex transformations through affine transformations by weight matrices and nonlinear transformations induced by nonlinearities. The paper notes that while the number of weight matrices and nonlinearities is typically equal, there are instances, such as in ResNet architectures, where this is not the case due to the specific connections between components. Overall, this framework aims to enhance the flexibility and capability of neural network designs.
