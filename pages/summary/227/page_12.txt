In this paper, the authors discuss the implications of residual connections in deep neural networks, particularly focusing on their impact on hypothesis complexity and generalization capabilities. They highlight that residual connections introduce parallel structures, which complicate the mapping representation as a series of affine or nonlinear transformations. To tackle this complexity, the authors provide a novel proof in Section 6.3 that addresses the covering bounds for networks with residual connections.

The paper establishes that the hypothesis complexity influenced by weight matrices remains consistent across both the stem and the vines of the network. Specifically, adding an identity vine does not alter the hypothesis complexity of the deep neural network. The authors derive that the covering number of a chain-like neural network, which includes both the stem and the vines, is upper bounded by the product of the covering numbers of individual layers.

In their analysis, they demonstrate that the contributions from weight matrices in both the stem and vines can be expressed similarly, suggesting that residual connections do not undermine the generalization capabilities of deep neural networks. Notably, when a vine is an identity mapping, it contributes a value of one to the covering number, indicating that it lacks tunable parameters.

However, the authors also note that while identity vines do not affect hypothesis complexity, the presence of vines can influence parts of the stem in the covering bound, posing an open question regarding the cross-influence mechanisms between the stem and vines. The paper concludes with an example analysis of a 34-layer ResNet, illustrating the stem-vine framework and its implications for understanding the generalization capability of deep neural networks.
