The references listed provide a comprehensive overview of significant contributions to the field of reinforcement learning and its applications. Key works include Dayan and Hinton's exploration of "Feudal reinforcement learning," which proposes a hierarchical approach to learning. Dearden et al. contribute to the understanding of "Model-based Bayesian exploration" and "Bayesian Q-learning," highlighting the integration of Bayesian methods in reinforcement learning. Degrave et al. present a "differentiable physics engine" for robotics, facilitating deep learning applications. Deisenroth and Rasmussen introduce PILCO, emphasizing data efficiency in policy search. Depeweg et al. explore learning in stochastic systems using Bayesian neural networks. The discussion by Der Kiureghian and Ditlevsen on the distinction between aleatory and epistemic uncertainty is crucial for risk assessment in modeling. Dilokthanakul et al. focus on intrinsic motivation in hierarchical reinforcement learning, while Diuk et al. propose an object-oriented representation for efficient learning. Lastly, Doll et al. and Doya et al. discuss the prevalence and advantages of model-based approaches in reinforcement learning. This collection underscores the evolution and interdisciplinary nature of reinforcement learning research.
