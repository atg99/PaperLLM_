This paper discusses implicit model-based reinforcement learning, focusing on several key areas. It begins with value equivalent models and progresses to learning to plan, highlighting the combined learning of models and planning. The benefits of model-based reinforcement learning are explored through various dimensions, including data efficiency, exploration, optimality, transfer, safety, and explainability. The paper also addresses potential disbenefits of this approach. 

The theoretical framework underlying model-based reinforcement learning is examined, alongside a review of related work in the field. The discussion section synthesizes the findings and emphasizes the implications for future research and applications. The paper concludes with a summary that encapsulates the main points, supporting the argument for the advantages and challenges inherent in model-based methods.
