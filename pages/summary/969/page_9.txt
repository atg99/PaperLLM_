This paper presents a comprehensive survey on model-based reinforcement learning (RL), an integration of reinforcement learning and planning approaches for sequential decision making, typically framed as Markov Decision Processes (MDPs). The authors, Thomas M. Moerland and colleagues, systematically explore two main components of model-based RL. 

First, they examine the learning of dynamics models, addressing challenges such as handling stochasticity, uncertainty, partial observability, and temporal abstraction. Second, they categorize the integration of planning and learning, discussing crucial aspects such as initiation of planning, allocation of resources for planning versus real data collection, methods of planning, and the incorporation of planning within the learning and acting cycle.

Additionally, the survey introduces implicit model-based RL as a cohesive alternative to traditional model learning and planning. The authors highlight the potential advantages of adopting model-based RL and draw connections to related fields, including hierarchical RL and transfer learning. The survey serves as a foundational resource for understanding the landscape of model-based RL, detailing both theoretical frameworks and practical considerations.
