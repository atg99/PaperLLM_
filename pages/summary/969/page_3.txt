This paper presents a comprehensive survey of model-based reinforcement learning (MBRL), a key area of research in artificial intelligence that combines reinforcement learning with the use of models to improve decision-making processes. The authors, Moerland, Broekens, Plaat, and Jonker, explore the theoretical foundations of MBRL, its various methodologies, and the practical implications of these approaches.

The survey categorizes existing MBRL techniques into different frameworks, highlighting the strengths and weaknesses of each method. The authors discuss how model-based approaches can significantly enhance learning efficiency by enabling agents to simulate outcomes and plan actions based on learned models of the environment. They emphasize the importance of accuracy in models, as it directly influences the performance of the reinforcement learning agent.

Additionally, the paper addresses the challenges associated with MBRL, such as model inaccuracies and computational complexity, and reviews various strategies to mitigate these issues. The authors also explore future directions for research in MBRL, suggesting areas where further investigation could lead to advancements in the field.

Overall, the survey serves as a valuable resource for researchers and practitioners interested in the developments and applications of model-based reinforcement learning, providing insights into both theoretical and practical aspects of the discipline.
