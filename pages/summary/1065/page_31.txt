The paper provides a comprehensive survey of the use of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) in enhancing privacy and security across various applications. It discusses the potential of GANs in malware detection, emphasizing their ability to generate unknown malware based on known attacks, although manual feature extraction by experts remains necessary. The paper highlights the advantages of dynamic analysis over statistical methods, suggesting that GANs can improve malware detection by generating adversarial samples, especially in the context of virtualization and side-channel attacks.

The paper also addresses the challenges of white-box attacks, which require greater transparency and expert involvement, noting a scarcity of datasets for such attacks that could benefit from GAN applications. In the realm of bioinformatics, the authors point out the limited focus on certain recognition systems, advocating for the development of GAN-based methods for diverse bioinformatics applications beyond fingerprint and face recognition.

Industrial security is identified as a complex field where GANs can generate adversarial data from limited datasets, although concerns about unbalanced data distributions are raised. The conclusion summarizes the review of state-of-the-art GAN approaches across various fields, including image and video processing, malware and fraud detection, and outlines unresolved challenges and future research directions related to application scenarios, model design, and data utilization.
