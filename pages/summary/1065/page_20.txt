The paper reviews various methods aimed at enhancing the privacy of generative models, particularly in the context of differential privacy. Zhang et al. and Xu et al. proposed methods that add differential noise solely to the discriminator in Wasserstein GAN (WGAN) to stabilize training. However, these methods resulted in low-quality generated data and slower convergence, leading to excessive privacy loss. To address these issues, three solutions were suggested: 

1. Parameter grouping, which balances convergence rate and privacy cost through careful grouping of training parameters.
2. Adaptive clipping, which utilizes a public dataset to dynamically adjust clipping bounds for improved convergence and privacy.
3. Warm starting, where a public dataset initializes models to save privacy budget during critical iterations.

Despite these advancements, the reliance on a public dataset is a significant limitation in private data release scenarios, and the focus has primarily been on unlabeled data.

Triastcyn and Faltings introduced a conditional GAN (cGAN) framework for generating labeled data that maintains differential privacy by injecting noise into the discriminator. This framework improves data quality with minimal privacy loss but raises concerns regarding potential information leakage when label information is involved.

In distributed GAN settings, preserving generator privacy is crucial. Existing studies have proposed adding differential privacy during local GAN training, using noise mechanisms similar to previous methods. The balance between utility and privacy remains a challenge, with variations in privacy loss metrics being explored to optimize this trade-off.

Lu et al. highlighted that traditional quality metrics for private data release may not be suitable, as strict privacy guarantees can compromise data utility. Many users prefer informal privacy measures that provide adequate protection while maintaining usability.

The paper concludes with a comparison of the reviewed methods, emphasizing the need for further research into the trade-offs between privacy efficacy and data utility in generative models.
