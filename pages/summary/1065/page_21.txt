The section discusses security issues related to Generative Adversarial Networks (GAN), focusing on model robustness and adversarial sample attacks. Adversarial attacks pose significant threats to machine learning models, where attackers manipulate input data to mislead classification outcomes. This is mathematically framed as an optimization problem that seeks to minimize the distance between original and adversarial data while ensuring incorrect classification.

Two primary types of adversarial attacks are identified: non-targeted attacks that only require a misclassification and targeted attacks that aim for a specific misclassification. The exploration of adversarial sample generation using GANs is highlighted, particularly the work by Baluja and Fischer, which introduced a method to generate targeted adversarial samples directly from original data rather than through noise manipulation.

Subsequent advancements include Zhao et al.'s method that combines a GAN model with a data inverter to enhance attack capabilities, allowing for the transformation of normal data into adversarial representations. Additionally, Xiao et al. proposed a model integrating a target classifier with GAN for black-box attacks, which optimizes both classification and noise scale.

Overall, while GANs present innovative approaches for generating adversarial samples, challenges remain in terms of model dependency and effectiveness in black-box scenarios.
