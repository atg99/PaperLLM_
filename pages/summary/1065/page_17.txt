The paper discusses the privacy challenges in distributed learning systems, particularly in the context of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs). It highlights that centralized machine learning systems are vulnerable to privacy issues such as membership and preimage privacy due to their reliance on a single server. In contrast, decentralized learning approaches, like distributed selective SGD (DSSGD) and federated learning, allow for local training without sharing sensitive data, thus enhancing privacy.

However, the paper notes that these distributed systems are not impervious to privacy attacks. It describes how attackers can exploit the capabilities of GANs to mimic data distributions, leading to potential privacy breaches. For instance, a method introduced by Hitaj et al. uses GANs to recover sensitive information from distributed systems without compromising the central server or local models. The attacker can adaptively update the GAN based on shared parameters to extract more information.

Furthermore, the paper presents the concept of mGAN-AI, where a malicious server could leverage a multi-task GAN to reveal user-level privacy by generating fake data and identifying users participating in the training process. This underscores the need for robust privacy protection mechanisms in distributed learning environments to safeguard against such sophisticated attacks. Overall, while distributed learning offers some privacy benefits, significant vulnerabilities remain that need to be addressed.
