The paper discusses various challenges and future directions in privacy-preserving data generation, particularly focusing on Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs). 

In section 6.1.2, it highlights the inadequacy of existing methods that treat public records as discrete data, emphasizing the need to consider sequential relationships in text for improved performance in privacy protection.

Section 6.1.3 addresses the oversight in current privacy protection methods for videos and speeches, which often ignore spatio-temporal relations. The paper argues that these relations can be exploited to extract private information, thus necessitating their incorporation into privacy protection strategies.

The authors also note in section 6.1.4 that current GAN-based models obscure sensitive information by adding noise but lack clarity on what information is hidden and where noise is applied. This lack of transparency can undermine privacy protection, especially against advanced privacy detectors.

In section 6.1.5, the paper points out that existing GAN methods do not provide a theoretical guarantee of privacy protection due to the black-box nature of the generator and the instability of adversarial training. It calls for breakthroughs to establish such theoretical guarantees.

Section 6.1.6 discusses the computational challenges posed by the increasing data sharing through IoT devices, advocating for lightweight GAN models that balance performance with cost-effectiveness.

Finally, section 6.2.1 suggests that future research should focus on improving GAN models to enhance their convergence rates and address mode collapse, which will bolster their effectiveness in both offensive and defensive applications.
