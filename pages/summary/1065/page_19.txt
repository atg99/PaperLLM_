The paper presents a survey on Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) with a focus on private and secure applications, highlighting various mechanisms for model privacy protection. It categorizes existing literature based on their purpose—whether for protection or attack—and the type of scenario (white/black box). The comparison table illustrates the requirements and privacy implications of different GAN models, such as their ability to protect against membership inference or preimage attacks.

The authors discuss the integration of privacy-preserving techniques, particularly differential privacy, with GANs. They note that differential privacy can help mitigate overfitting and privacy leakage by introducing noise into model parameters. Key contributions include the first attempt to create private generative models using GANs, which allows for unlimited data generation while ensuring privacy. This method utilizes a differentially private k-means algorithm to partition the training dataset into disjoint subsets for local model training.

The paper also introduces the PATE-GAN model, which employs a teacher-student framework to generate a differentially private dataset for training. The effectiveness of these approaches is contingent on selecting an appropriate value for k, as improper choices can lead to privacy risks. Additionally, the DPGAN model is proposed, which enhances privacy by adding noise to gradients and utilizing a loss function that helps balance privacy and utility.

Overall, the paper emphasizes the significance of developing GAN mechanisms that prioritize data privacy while maintaining effective model performance.
