The paper discusses the application of Generative Adversarial Networks (GAN) in malware detection and bioinformatics recognition systems. It highlights the challenges posed by black-box malware detection, where attackers manipulate malware to evade detection without knowing the internal workings of the detection model. A substituted detector is used to approximate the black-box detector, utilizing adversarial malware examples generated by GAN alongside benign samples. The loss function for adversarial example generation balances the similarity to benign samples and the misclassification rate of adversarial samples.

Additionally, the paper references the use of GAN for analyzing malware across different platforms, including Android and Windows. It notes the transformation of malware binaries into images for training purposes and the effectiveness of federated GAN in enhancing security and communication between devices. The difficulty of detecting zero-day malware is addressed through a transferred deep-convolutional GAN (tDCGAN) that learns malware characteristics and generates adversarial samples.

In the realm of bioinformatics, GAN is employed to create synthetic fingerprints for verification systems. The paper describes two methods based on GAN for fingerprint generation, emphasizing the potential of GAN to construct bio-information systems that are efficient and secure while addressing privacy concerns associated with real bio-data. Overall, the integration of GAN in these fields demonstrates its versatility and effectiveness in overcoming traditional detection and recognition challenges.
