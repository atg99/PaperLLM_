The paper discusses methods of membership inference attacks on generative adversarial networks (GANs) and proposes strategies for protecting membership privacy. It outlines two main types of attacks: black-box and co-membership attacks. In black-box attacks, an attacker can exploit the discriminator of a GAN by generating data labeled as "real" to train a local discriminator for inferring membership without auxiliary knowledge. If auxiliary knowledge is available, the attack can be more effective, but the assumptions required for black-box attacks may limit their practicality.

The co-membership attack, introduced by Sume et al., aims to determine whether multiple data points are part of the training dataset simultaneously. This involves training an attacker’s neural network to minimize the distance between original and generated data. However, this method has flaws, such as decreased accuracy with larger datasets and the need for a unique attack network for each victim model, making it less efficient and applicable primarily in white-box scenarios.

To counter these membership inference attacks, Nasr et al. proposed a privacy-preserving method that uses adversarial regularization in training machine learning models. This method involves two parties: an attacker and a defensive classifier. The attacker seeks to maximize their privacy gain from the victim model, while the defender aims to minimize their loss function, which balances normal classification loss with privacy considerations. This approach allows for training models on machine learning as a service platforms while safeguarding membership privacy.
