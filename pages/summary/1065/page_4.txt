The paper provides a comprehensive overview of Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) and their variants. It begins by explaining the fundamental structure of GANs, initially introduced by Goodfellow et al. The GAN framework consists of two primary components: a generator (G) that produces fake data from a latent space and a discriminator (D) that distinguishes between real and fake data. The training process continues until the discriminator can no longer differentiate between the two, modeled as a min-max game with a specific objective function.

The paper also highlights various GAN variants that have emerged since the original design to address different applications and scenarios. These variants expand upon the foundational concepts of GANs, demonstrating their versatility and adaptability in various fields.
