The paper provides a comprehensive survey on Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) with a focus on their applications in privacy and security. It highlights the evolution and current trends in GAN research, which can be categorized into application-oriented and theory-oriented studies. The applications of GANs span various data formats including image generation, natural language processing, time series data generation, and semantic segmentation, with numerous subcategories such as image super-resolution and medical data generation.

The paper also discusses ongoing theoretical advancements aimed at addressing challenges like unstable training and mode collapse. Solutions proposed include feature matching, mini-batch discrimination, and the introduction of novel GAN variants such as Self-Attention GAN.

In terms of privacy and security, the paper emphasizes the relevance of GANs in addressing increasing privacy concerns and regulatory requirements. The adversarial training inherent in GANs allows for a nuanced exploration of security threats, facilitating both the modeling of attacks and the development of defense mechanisms. Various models are presented, including Generative Adversarial Privacy and Privacy Preserving Adversarial Networks, which illustrate the diverse configurations and loss function formulations in current GAN-based methods.

The survey outlines the organization of the content, detailing sections on GAN fundamentals, applications for data and model privacy, security applications, and future research directions. The conclusion reiterates the potential for further exploration in leveraging GANs for enhanced privacy and security applications.
