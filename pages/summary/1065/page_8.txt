The paper discusses various methods for synthesizing face images while preserving user identity and privacy. It introduces a VGAN model that utilizes three discriminators for different functionalities: ensuring image quality, identity recognition, and expression recognition. This model aims to balance privacy and utility in the synthesized images. Additionally, a targeted identity-protection iterative method (TIP-IM) is mentioned, which generates adversarial identity masks to prevent identity leakage without compromising visual quality.

Cao et al. present a DCGAN-based approach for multi-view identity-preserving face image synthesis, enhancing pose-invariant recognition and preventing identification leakage. The importance of high-resolution images in real-world recognition systems is highlighted, with Mirjalili et al. proposing an AC-GAN-based model to hide gender information for privacy protection. Their model uses an autoencoder as the generator and includes classifiers to ensure that the gender attribute is confounded while maintaining the realism of face images.

Wu et al. introduce Privacy-Protective-GAN (PP-GAN) to preserve soft-biometric attributes beyond gender, while Mirjalili et al. also propose PrivacyNet, allowing users to obfuscate specific soft-biometric attributes while retaining recognition capabilities.

The paper also addresses medical image privacy concerns, with Kim et al. proposing a DCGAN-based framework that includes a deep encoder to remove identity features while maintaining segmentation performance. Lastly, it discusses the privacy issues associated with street-view images and the need for solutions to protect individual identities in such datasets.
