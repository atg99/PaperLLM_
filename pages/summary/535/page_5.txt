The paper discusses an unsupervised clustering method for video representations using Bayes' rule to establish the probability of a video belonging to a particular cluster. It highlights that the probability can be expressed via a softmax function based on squared distances from cluster centers, noting that activity classes in large datasets like AVA and Kinetics follow a Zipf distribution. This observation allows for a prior constraint on the distribution of videos in clusters, leading to the formulation of prior probabilities for each class.

The authors define a fitness metric based on minimizing the Kullback-Leibler divergence between the learned distribution and the Zipf distribution, which does not require labeled data, hence the method is termed 'ELo.' Additionally, a weakly-supervised baseline called ELo-weak is introduced for comparison, which utilizes a limited number of class labels and k-means clustering for evaluation.

The paper also outlines the evolution of loss functions through mutations in an evolutionary strategy, comparing tournament selection and CMA-ES for optimizing these functions. The authors detail their approach to self-supervised learning tasks, emphasizing the flexibility of their evolved loss function to determine the significance of various tasks automatically. Tasks include reconstruction and prediction, using a simple decoder architecture to minimize memory and training time while ensuring that representations retain necessary temporal information.

Overall, the work presents a novel unsupervised approach to video representation learning that integrates probabilistic modeling, evolutionary strategies, and self-supervised tasks without relying on labeled data.
