The paper presents a novel framework for multi-task, multi-modal unsupervised representation learning, focusing on the integration of various data modalities such as RGB, optical flow, and audio. The core idea is to leverage synchronized multi-modal data to enhance representation learning, using distillation losses to facilitate information sharing among different modalities. This approach eliminates the need for human annotations, positioning it as a fully unsupervised learning method.

A significant challenge addressed in the study is the effective combination of diverse self-supervised and distillation loss functions, as their relevance and scaling can vary. To tackle this, the authors introduce an evolutionary algorithm that optimizes the multi-modal, multi-task loss function, enhancing the training of the network. Instead of relying on labeled data to measure the algorithm's fitness, the study proposes an innovative unsupervised alternative based on power law distribution matching using KL divergence constraints.

The main contributions of the work include the formulation of unsupervised learning as a multi-modal, multi-task process, the evolutionary search for an effective loss function, and the introduction of an unsupervised evaluation metric that performs comparably to label-guided approaches. The findings reveal that large amounts of unlabeled data, coupled with self-supervised tasks and power law distribution matching, yield powerful feature representations that rival those obtained from extensive labeled datasets. The proposed model, termed 'ELo', demonstrates superior performance over traditional pre-training methods, including ImageNet and smaller labeled video datasets, and only falls short compared to Kinetics pre-training with full human annotations.
