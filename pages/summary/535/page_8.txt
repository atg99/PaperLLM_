The paper presents a unified framework for multi-task, multi-modal unsupervised video representation learning, demonstrating its effectiveness in improving recognition tasks. It evaluates the performance of different methods using varying amounts of labeled samples from the Kinetics-400 dataset, revealing that using only 25k labeled samples results in reasonable performance, only slightly lower than fully-supervised models. The study finds that using the entire dataset enhances performance due to better initializations and the integration of multiple modalities into the RGB stream.

Additionally, the research explores the benefits of incorporating unlabeled data, showing that performance improves as more diverse data is added, even with fewer sample exposures. The analysis of evolved loss functions highlights the importance of certain tasks, such as audio alignment, while indicating that tasks like RGB frame shuffling are less beneficial for action recognition.

The comparison of evolutionary strategies reveals that CMA-ES is the most effective method for quickly converging to optimal solutions. Strong correlations between different fitness measures suggest that Zipf matching is a suitable criterion for evaluating unsupervised representations. Overall, the findings indicate that the proposed approach yields powerful video representations that can match or surpass the performance of supervised models.
