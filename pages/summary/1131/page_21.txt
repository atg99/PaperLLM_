The paper discusses the integration of cyber-physical systems (CPS) and digital assistants into human networks, highlighting their dual role as both integral components of these networks and as services that can be traded and negotiated. Current digital assistants like Alexa and Siri exemplify advanced CPSs that interact with humans, are mobile, and increasingly autonomous, capable of decision-making and continuous learning. The anticipated future involves delegating numerous tasks to these digital assistants, effectively creating a parallel digital society that engages in information gathering, connection establishment, and trading.

The paper introduces a CPS swarm approach, which manages large-scale systems where agents (digital assistants) operate autonomously based on simple behavioral rules. These agents interact locally while sharing global information in dynamic environments, which can be instrumental in scenarios such as epidemic tracking and emergency management. The potential of CPS swarms extends to combating fake news through collaborative information gathering and analysis, as well as negotiating and trading resources among swarms for various tasks.

Additionally, the paper highlights the growing trend of sensing-as-a-service (SaaS) in the Internet of Things (IoT), which could be enhanced by CPS swarms that possess mobility and autonomy. This would enable complex interactions among multiple levels of CPS swarms, facilitating tasks like environmental monitoring and transportation. Blockchain technology is noted as crucial for ensuring secure transactions within this framework, establishing a trust system among swarm members.

The paper concludes by identifying significant open research challenges in the field of swarm intelligence, emphasizing the need for further exploration in domain-specific topics related to CPS and digital assistants.
