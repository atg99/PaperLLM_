The paper discusses the emerging field of Cyber-Physical Systems (CPSs) and their role in pervasive computing, highlighting several fundamental challenges that remain in the design, computation, prediction, and control of large systems composed of autonomous components. CPSs are defined by the National Science Foundation as engineered systems that integrate computational and physical components, allowing for their interaction through sensing, computation, control, and networking. This interdisciplinary approach encompasses various fields such as embedded systems, robotics, and networking.

CPSs are increasingly relevant as they contribute to the creation of cyber-physical-social systems, where multiple interconnected CPSs interact within social networks. The concept of Cyber-Physical System of Systems (CPSoS) is introduced, describing a distributed ecosystem of CPSs that face unique challenges related to autonomy, integration, cybersecurity, and dynamic operation in unpredictable environments.

The paper emphasizes that traditional centralized control methods are inadequate for managing these complex systems. Instead, it advocates for strategies that promote distributed control, local coordination, partial subsystem autonomy, dynamic reconfiguration, and the emergence of useful behaviors. It acknowledges that no single framework can address all challenges and suggests a "systems-of-solutions" approach, with swarm intelligence identified as a potentially effective strategy for tackling the complexities inherent in CPSs and CPSoS.
