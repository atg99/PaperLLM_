The section on Human-in-the-Loop discusses the benefits of integrating human interactions within swarm systems, particularly in Cyber-Physical Systems (CPS). This integration allows CPS to leverage human cognitive and sensory-motor capabilities, while humans can utilize CPS as an enhanced sensor-actuator system. The design of these mixed initiative systems presents various technical challenges, including the physical interaction methods, the frequency and clarity of state information presented to humans, and the dissemination of human commands to CPS swarms.

Research efforts have focused on developing multi-modal interfaces that facilitate bi-directional communication between humans and swarm systems. However, allowing human operators to interact directly with these systems introduces new risks related to security, safety, stability, and reliability, especially if human actions are irresponsible or malicious. These concerns, particularly regarding security and privacy, require more attention in future research.

In the conclusions, the paper emphasizes the significance of Swarm Intelligence (SI) within the context of CPS, which connects the physical world with digital representations. The complexity of CPS interactions necessitates a framework capable of addressing emergent behaviors, particularly in scenarios where centralized solutions are infeasible due to information scarcity, time constraints, or dynamic environments. The paper suggests that ongoing research should focus on drawing inspiration from various disciplines, developing innovative applications, and advancing the understanding of SI through modeling, design, verification, and the human-in-the-loop paradigm.

The research was supported by the European Union Horizon 2020 program and the Qatar National Research Fund, highlighting the collaborative nature of this investigation into CPS and SI.
