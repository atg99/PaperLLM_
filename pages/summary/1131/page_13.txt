To address the limitations of current automatic generation systems for CPS swarms, it is crucial to incorporate model-checking methods with rule synthesis and verification. This integration can ensure that the design and control stages of CPS swarms are robust and reliable, providing necessary guarantees for their operation.

In terms of real-world deployment, while swarm robotics has mostly focused on simple robots in controlled environments, there is a need to transition towards more complex systems that can perform meaningful tasks in real-world scenarios. This requires a shift from simplistic robot designs to more sophisticated systems that effectively utilize sensor data and information sharing. The example of Intel’s Shooting Star drones illustrates that achieving practical applications may necessitate a balance between swarm intelligence and centralized control.

Maintenance of deployed swarm systems poses another challenge. The longevity of such systems depends on their ability to self-monitor and self-repair, which could potentially simplify maintenance tasks compared to traditional systems. However, the complexity of maintaining numerous autonomous components remains a critical question that warrants further investigation.

In summary, to advance the field of swarm robotics and CPS swarms, researchers must embrace a more integrated approach that combines formal analysis, advanced modeling techniques, and practical deployment strategies. This will enable the transition from theoretical studies to the realization of effective and reliable swarm systems capable of addressing complex real-world challenges.
