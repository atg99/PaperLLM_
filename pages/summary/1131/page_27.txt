The paper provides a comprehensive overview of various studies and developments in swarm robotics, emphasizing cooperative navigation, foraging algorithms, and decentralized control mechanisms. It highlights the importance of local communication among robots for effective task execution, such as area coverage and spatial tasks. Key contributions include work on self-organizing behaviors, game-theoretic approaches, and the evolution of swarm systems utilizing neural network controllers and grammatical evolution for behavior synthesis. The review also discusses the design of low-cost robots like Kilobot and Spiderino, which are tailored for swarm research and educational applications. The advancements in swarm robotics are presented through various conference proceedings and journals, underscoring the collaborative and interdisciplinary nature of this field.
