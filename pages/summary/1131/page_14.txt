The paper emphasizes the importance of characterizing the time-evolution and stability properties of complex systems while analyzing their structural and topological characteristics. It highlights the growing significance of information flows in self-organization and emergence, noting the potential benefits of incorporating concepts from game theory and multi-agent systems into the study of SI. 

The authors argue that the distinction between swarms and multi-agent decentralized systems is often blurred, suggesting that insights from multi-robot systems can greatly inform SI research. They advocate for a closer alignment between physical swarms and multi-agent systems, focusing on self-organization and scalability.

Looking towards the future, the paper proposes that SI will play a crucial role in addressing challenges posed by complex cyber-physical systems (CPS). It suggests that novel swarm models may emerge from a broader range of inspirations, including organic, inorganic, and social systems, which have not been fully explored. 

Specifically, the authors call for increased consideration of non-collaborative systems, such as predator-prey dynamics and conflicting scenarios, to enhance SI frameworks. They contend that integrating concepts from population dynamics, game theory, and evolutionary game theory could lead to advancements in SI design, particularly in contexts involving cyber threats and competing CPS. Overall, the paper outlines a vision for future research that seeks to harness diverse inspirations to tackle complex real-world problems through the development of innovative SI models.
