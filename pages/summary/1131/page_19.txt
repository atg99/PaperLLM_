4.2.4 Electric Energy Grids

Electric energy grids, characterized by their large and interconnected nature, are suitable for swarm-based solutions. Venayagamoorthy identifies various applications of computational intelligence within these grids, such as energy management algorithms, voltage control, dynamic load forecasting, and vehicle-to-grid systems. Although swarm algorithms are not explicitly mentioned, many coordination tasks align with swarm intelligence (SI) applications.

Several approaches model parts of the electrical grid as swarms. For instance, a demand-side management approach coordinates households in a neighborhood using geographically close coupling to promote fairness among consumers. Additionally, a virtual mass storage system integrates small distributed battery units in homes with rooftop photovoltaic systems. The SmartGRID concept introduces a decentralized grid scheduling framework based on SI, while a hybrid swarm/immune-based auction system coordinates generation and consumption in smart microgrids. Swarm electrification, focusing on integrating small off-grid systems into connected microgrids, is particularly relevant for rural areas.

The shift toward swarm-based systems is prompted by the increasing number of renewable distributed energy resources, the inability of renewable plants to adjust output to meet demand, and the rise in power consumption due to electric transportation. This transition advocates for a self-organized, distributed control system rather than a centralized model.

However, the real-world implementation of CPS swarm models in energy grids requires significant research advancements. Key challenges include validating and testing systems before deployment, as new control strategies can destabilize critical infrastructure. Solutions may involve simulations or applying CPS swarm models in sufficiently large smart microgrids to mitigate risks associated with larger national grids.

4.2.5 Space Missions

Swarms of Cyber-Physical Systems (CPSs) offer promising applications for space missions. They could be employed to inspect communication satellites for damage or to coordinate space debris disposal. For instance, reflective spheres could fly past an asteroid to measure its gravitational pull. Initial missions may focus on exploring water, raw materials, and potential life on exoplanets, with swarms providing low-cost, fault-tolerant solutions for autonomous exploration.

Data collected by these swarms would be transmitted back to Earth, with examples like NASA's Marsbees utilizing a rover as a base and charging station. The Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency envisions swarms of autonomous machinery for ground preparation and infrastructure development on the Moon and Mars. 

The feasibility of using CPS swarms in these scenarios hinges on the ability of individual CPSs to follow local rules for autonomous behavior, exchange local information among themselves and with Earth stations, and operate effectively in the challenging environments of space.
