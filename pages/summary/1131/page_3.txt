This paper explores the intersection of swarm intelligence (SI) and cyber-physical systems (CPSs) to address challenges in designing and managing complex systems. SI draws inspiration from natural swarm systems, such as insect colonies, which exhibit self-organization and coordination in dynamic environments. Engineered swarm systems consist of autonomous agents, ranging from simple to complex, that collaborate to achieve a common goal through locally defined rules. Key characteristics of SI systems include parallel processing, distributed control, locality of interactions, scalability, adaptability, and resilience.

The authors argue for a formalization of CPSs through the lens of SI, identifying two primary issues: the need for common properties across diverse CPSs and the necessity to classify open research topics within SI relevant to CPSs. The paper aims to bridge the gap between SI and CPS communities by reviewing SI concepts and their application to CPS design, with a focus on swarm robotics as a foundational element.

The paper is structured into several sections: Section 2 defines SI and its relevance to CPS swarms, outlining properties and inspirational sources. Section 3 discusses challenges in applying SI to real-world scenarios, highlighting why SI algorithms are not widely implemented. Section 4 presents future research directions and potential applications of SI in CPS swarms. The authors emphasize the importance of developing new approaches that enable self-organization of agents rather than relying on traditional control methods.

In conclusion, the paper motivates researchers to explore the integration of SI into CPS design, highlighting both opportunities and limitations, and sets the stage for future research endeavors in this evolving field.
