The paper discusses the potential and challenges of deploying heterogeneous swarms of autonomous cyber-physical systems (CPS) for applications such as search and rescue (SAR) and environmental monitoring. Key features of effective swarm operation include local interaction among swarm members and human teammates, cooperative behaviors at the individual level, and the emergence of effective global behaviors. Despite advancements, significant progress is still needed in autonomous decision-making, data exchange, and functioning in complex environments.

In SAR applications, swarms composed of drones and ground rovers can assist in delivering aid or guiding victims. However, existing projects have not yet yielded practical results for real-world missions. Critical considerations for deploying these swarms include the number, type, and initial placement of robots, alongside the need for intuitive interfaces for human interaction.

In the context of environmental monitoring, CPS swarms can act as self-reconfiguring sensor networks, monitoring ecosystems with minimal disturbance. They operate based on local rules, exchanging information to adapt their behavior. The autonomous nature of these swarms is crucial, especially in dynamic environments where human interference should be minimized. Efficient communication protocols are essential to reduce disruption to natural ecosystems, allowing for long-term monitoring with sustainable energy use.

The paper highlights successful real-world projects, such as subCULTron and CORATAM, which illustrate the viability of CPS swarms in environmental monitoring and emphasize the need for intelligent energy management to support extended observation periods. Overall, the effective deployment of CPS swarms hinges on overcoming current limitations in technology and ensuring harmonious interaction with natural environments.
