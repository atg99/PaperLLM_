The paper discusses the existing gap between the analysis of formal models in disembodied swarm intelligence (SI) and the challenges faced in embodied SI, particularly in the context of cyber-physical systems (CPSs). In disembodied scenarios, such as optimization problems like Ant Colony Optimization (ACO) and Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO), much of the formal analysis focuses on stability and convergence. However, this analysis often relies on simplified models or specific assumptions, which can hinder understanding more complex aspects like the speed of convergence. As a result, empirical procedures are frequently used for performance analysis and parameter tuning, limiting the applicability of theoretical properties.

Conversely, embodied SI presents additional challenges due to the unique characteristics of real-world scenarios and the variability in the behaviors of individual CPSs. This complexity makes it difficult to abstract properties and apply results across different studies. Despite these challenges, the paper highlights that mathematical abstraction can still facilitate the identification and analysis of typical swarm behaviors, such as foraging, coverage, aggregation, and cooperative tracking.

In addressing the design of local rules for SI, the paper notes that while defining rules for disembodied SI is relatively straightforward, it becomes significantly more complex in CPS swarms due to unpredictable environments. The work on automatic synthesis of behavioral rules in swarm robotics is discussed, showcasing various approaches, including artificial evolution techniques and supervisory control theory. However, the paper acknowledges that these contributions have primarily been validated in simpler scenarios, which limits their applicability in more complex environments. 

Overall, the paper emphasizes the need for more robust theoretical frameworks and methodologies to address the complexities of embodied SI in practical applications.
