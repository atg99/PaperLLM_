Spatial constraints refer to the physical limitations of the CPSs, such as their operational range and the proximity required for effective interaction and communication with other components. 

Essentially, swarm models of Cyber-Physical Systems (CPS) integrate multiple components that operate autonomously, adapt to their environment, and communicate locally to achieve effective system-level behaviors. This complexity is amplified when considering ensembles of CPSs, or Cyber-Physical Systems of Systems (CPSoS), which operate at varying spatial and temporal scales. 

A practical example is the autonomous car, where each component functions as an individual CPS, while a fleet of such cars represents a swarm. These swarms can interact with surrounding CPSs, creating a larger network of interconnected systems. The anticipated scale and diversity of these CPS swarms will require a systematic approach to categorize their capabilities—such as mobility, communication, sensing, actuation, and processing—and constraints related to time and space. 

Understanding these dimensions is crucial for developing solutions that effectively address the complexities inherent in CPS swarms.
