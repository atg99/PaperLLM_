Collective motion in active solids and active crystals utilizes elasticity-based mechanisms to enable self-organization, particularly relevant for the collective behavior of cyber-physical systems (CPS) swarms. These models facilitate phenomena such as pattern formation and morphogenesis, showing resilience to heterogeneity, which can be analytically derived. 

The paper highlights promising future applications of swarm intelligence (SI), emphasizing its potential across diverse practical scenarios. Despite limited real-world implementations thus far, SI concepts are poised for broader application, particularly when traditional centralized approaches are infeasible or insufficient due to complexity or real-time constraints. This is especially pertinent in complex CPSs or scenarios lacking established infrastructure, such as post-disaster situations.

The authors foresee significant potential for swarm applications in more intricate environments beyond controlled settings, such as traffic systems and urban landscapes. They discuss specific application scenarios that remain largely unexplored but could benefit from swarm characteristics, pointing out ongoing research needs for effective implementation.

A notable example is autonomous driving, which requires complex computations in dynamic environments. Here, SI can enhance traffic management by enabling cooperation among autonomous vehicles and infrastructure. Benefits include reduced congestion, improved traffic flow, and lower emissions. Modeling these entities as a swarm allows for effective local information exchange and decision-making, yielding emergent behaviors that a centralized system could not achieve. 

The authors suggest that while transport logistics have seen some application of swarm algorithms, smart traffic management remains relatively untapped, indicating a significant area for future research and development in CPS swarm applications.
