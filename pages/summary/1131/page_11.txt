The paper discusses the processing power and communication limitations that define the operational capabilities of Cyber-Physical Systems (CPSs), particularly in swarm configurations. It highlights spatial constraints such as maneuverability, communication range, and physical dimensions that can restrict CPSs from operating effectively in confined spaces. 

Key features of CPS swarms are categorized into swarm composition and network topology. Swarm composition includes parameters like swarm size, spatial re-configurability, and diversity of CPSs. Network topology is influenced by the communication capabilities of individual CPSs, with mesh networks often being advantageous for facilitating communication among swarm members. However, hierarchical communication structures may be more suitable in certain scenarios, such as sensor networks where data consolidation is necessary.

The paper also addresses the challenges of employing swarm intelligence (SI) in CPSs, noting potential advantages in adaptability, robustness, and scalability. However, it emphasizes the difficulties in achieving predictability and controllability in complex systems designed through a bottom-up approach. The interaction between the digital and physical components of CPSs complicates the derivation of formal properties and makes model checking problematic. Additionally, CPSs are vulnerable to external disturbances, necessitating adaptive models for resilience.

The challenges of obtaining formal guarantees from both SI and CPS perspectives limit the application of SI in critical systems, such as aviation control, where predictable behavior is essential. The paper concludes by suggesting that overcoming these challenges is crucial for the effective design and implementation of CPS swarm systems.
