The paper discusses the formulation of Continuous-Time Recurrent Neural Networks (CT-RNNs) as systems of input-driven ordinary differential equations (ODEs). The output of these networks is a linear mapping of hidden states governed by an ODE that ensures the existence and uniqueness of solutions. A stochastic variant, Continuous-Time Noisy Recurrent Neural Networks (CT-NRNNs), is introduced by replacing the ODE with an Itô Stochastic Differential Equation (SDE), incorporating noise as a perturbation to the deterministic model. The existence of unique solutions to the SDE is ensured under certain conditions regarding the drift and diffusion coefficients.

The paper emphasizes a specific choice of drift function, which is a Lipschitz continuous scalar activation function extended to vectors, and discusses the parameterization of the diffusion coefficient that allows for both additive and multiplicative noise. These noise injections are framed as stochastic learning strategies, drawing connections to regularization mechanisms in neural networks, including additive Gaussian noise and multiplicative noise akin to dropout strategies.

Additionally, the paper addresses the challenge of numerical simulations of the SDE, opting for explicit Euler-Maruyama integrators as a practical solution for numerical discretization. The theoretical and empirical analyses presented aim to enhance understanding and application of these noisy recurrent networks in machine learning contexts.
