The paper "Noisy Recurrent Neural Networks" by Soon Hoe Lim and colleagues presents a framework for analyzing recurrent neural networks (RNNs) that incorporate noise into their hidden states. The authors conceptualize RNNs as discretizations of stochastic differential equations influenced by input data. This perspective allows for the exploration of the implicit regularization effects of noise injection, particularly in the context of small noise levels.

The findings indicate that noise injection promotes flatter minima, encourages models with more stable dynamics, and enhances classification margins in tasks. The study also establishes sufficient conditions for global stability, introducing the concept of stochastic stabilization, which suggests that noise can improve stability during training. Empirical results support these theoretical claims, demonstrating that RNNs exhibit increased robustness to various input perturbations.

The introduction highlights the growing interest in viewing RNNs as dynamical systems, which aids in developing novel architectures and improving reliability. The paper emphasizes the role of noise as a natural regularizer, contributing to implicit regularization that favors solutions in favorable regions of the loss landscape, thereby enhancing generalization and robustness. This work sheds light on the mechanisms behind the success of stochastic optimization in neural networks, linking implicit regularization to wider minima and improved classification performance.
