In this section, the robustness of neural recurrent networks (NRNNs) is evaluated against various perturbations, comparing their performance to other state-of-the-art models, particularly in the context of clean and corrupted data. The study focuses on different types of input perturbations, including additive white noise, multiplicative white noise, salt and pepper noise, and adversarial perturbations. 

The perturbation strategies are detailed as follows: additive white noise simulates measurement errors, multiplicative white noise reflects variations in data acquisition, salt and pepper noise represents defective pixels, and adversarial perturbations aim to maximize the predictive loss under certain constraints.

The evaluation includes NRNNs and three other RNN models derived from continuous-time frameworks, as well as an exponential RNN. Each model is trained under specific conditions for the ordered MNIST classification task, where input images are processed pixel-by-pixel. The results indicate that while the Lipschitz RNN performs best on clean inputs, NRNNs demonstrate greater resilience to input perturbations, particularly under certain noise configurations that balance accuracy and robustness.

The findings are summarized in a table showing average test accuracies under various noise levels for different RNN models. The NRNN configurations exhibit high accuracy even with added noise, suggesting their effectiveness in handling corrupted data. As the noise level increases, the predictive accuracy for all models tends to decline, emphasizing the importance of robustness in neural network performance.
