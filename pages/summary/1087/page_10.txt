This paper presents a comprehensive theoretical analysis of Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) that are trained using noise injection in hidden states. The authors investigate the regularizing effects of various noise injection schemes within the framework of Stochastic Differential Equations (SDEs). Experimental findings support the theoretical claims, demonstrating that Noisy RNNs exhibit enhanced robustness to input perturbations while achieving state-of-the-art generalization performance.

The paper suggests that this framework can inform the design of reliable RNN classifiers. It also highlights potential future research avenues, such as exploring the learning dynamics of deterministic RNNs near stability edges, and addressing the trade-off between noise-induced stabilization and potential negative impacts on long-term memory capacity. Acknowledgments are extended to various funding sources, including Amazon AWS and IARPA, while clarifying that the conclusions do not necessarily reflect the views of the sponsors. 

Overall, the study contributes to the understanding of noise in RNN training and opens pathways for further investigation into optimizing RNN performance.
