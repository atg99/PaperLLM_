The paper investigates the robustness of various Recurrent Neural Network (RNN) models, specifically focusing on their performance under different types of input perturbations. The study evaluates the performance of Noisy RNN (NRNN), Antisymmetric RNN, Exponential RNN, Lipschitz RNN, and CoRNN on two tasks: ordered MNIST and Electrocardiogram (ECG) classification.

For the ordered MNIST task, results indicate that NRNNs demonstrate superior resilience to both white noise and salt-and-pepper perturbations compared to the other models. As shown in Figure 1, NRNN maintains high accuracy under varying noise levels, while the Antisymmetric and Exponential RNNs show significant sensitivity to even small perturbations. Table 2 presents average test accuracies under adversarial conditions, highlighting the NRNN's robustness, particularly with large perturbations, achieving up to 98.8% accuracy.

In the ECG classification task, aimed at distinguishing between normal and abnormal heartbeats, the NRNN again outperformed other models under both additive and multiplicative noise conditions. With a training dataset consisting of 500 sequences, the NRNN achieved optimal performance with noise levels set at 0.03 for multiplicative and 0.06 for additive noise, as detailed in Table 3. Overall, the NRNN consistently exhibited less sensitivity to perturbations while maintaining state-of-the-art performance on clean inputs, confirming its effectiveness across different tasks and noise types.
