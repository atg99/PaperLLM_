The paper discusses the Euler-Maruyama (E-M) scheme for approximating solutions to stochastic differential equations (SDEs) through a specific partition of the interval [0, T]. The method introduces a family of approximations denoted by (hε_m), which converge strongly to an Itô process satisfying the SDE with a specified rate when using uniform step sizes.

A critical focus of the paper is on the implicit regularization provided by Nonlinear Recurrent Neural Networks (NRNNs), distinguishing them from deterministic models. The authors argue that NRNNs, under certain reasonable conditions, exhibit implicit regularization due to their stochastic learning approach, which can lead to flatter minima without the need for explicit loss modifications. This insight is derived through a perturbation analysis in a small noise context, enhancing the understanding of noise as a regularization mechanism.

The paper outlines assumptions for the drift and diffusion coefficients of the SDE, emphasizing the need for Lipschitz continuity of their derivatives and boundedness over the interval [0, T]. The analysis primarily addresses discrete-time NRNNs, although continuous-time aspects are also explored for broader applicability.

The first main result of the paper establishes a relationship between the loss function averaged over noise realizations for NRNNs and that of deterministic RNNs, revealing that the expected loss for NRNNs includes a term reflecting the injected noise and its effects, thus reinforcing the concept of implicit regularization.
