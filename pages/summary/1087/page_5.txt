The paper discusses the implications of injecting noise into the hidden states of deterministic recurrent neural networks (RNNs), specifically how this process can serve as a form of regularization. It presents two main functions, ^Q and ^R, which are defined in terms of the dynamics of the RNN and the loss function's Hessian. The findings suggest that while the regularization term ^R is non-negative when the loss is convex, the term ^Q may not be, though it can be made negligible by choosing a sufficiently small noise level.

Theorem 1 indicates that this noise introduction results in an approximate equivalence to a regularized objective functional. The regularization effect is determined by the Jacobians of the RNN's state transitions, the diffusion coefficients, and the Hessian of the loss function, evaluated along the deterministic RNN's dynamics. The presence of noise is shown to smooth the Hessian landscape, which is often associated with wider minima and increased robustness against perturbations in hidden states.

The paper emphasizes that the inclusion of noise leads to less sensitivity to input perturbations and a preference for stable dynamics within the model. This characteristic is particularly relevant for classification tasks, where the noise-induced regularization enhances the tendency for the model to achieve larger classification margins. The authors explore the performance of noise-regularized RNNs in comparison to deterministic RNNs, concluding that the noise contributes to improved robustness and performance in classification tasks by effectively regularizing the model's dynamics. 

In summary, the introduction of noise in RNNs provides a mechanism for implicit regularization that can lead to more stable and less sensitive models, particularly beneficial in classification settings.
