The paper discusses the generalization error of deterministic recurrent neural networks (RNNs) and their classification margins. The generalization error is defined as the difference between empirical risk on the training set and true risk, quantified by GE(g) = |R(g) - RN(g)|. The classifier operates based on the output of the RNN, which is formulated through an Euler discretization of an ordinary differential equation with step sizes.

Two key concepts are defined: classification margin and score. The classification margin indicates the robustness of a classifier by measuring the largest ball around an input point that remains within the decision region for its class label. A larger margin implies greater robustness against input perturbations. The score for a sample measures the separation between classes at the network output, with a positive score indicating a degree of separation but not necessarily a large margin.

The paper presents bounds on the classification margin of RNN classifiers, assuming certain conditions are met. The results hinge on the relationship between the score and the classification margin, showing that the classification margin can be bounded by a function of the score under specific assumptions. Additionally, the paper discusses the loss minimized by the RNN classifier, which incorporates noise injection that may enhance generalization performance.

Overall, the findings suggest that careful consideration of classification margins and scores can lead to more robust RNN classifiers, with implications for their performance in practice.
