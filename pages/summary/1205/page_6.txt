The paper discusses methods to evaluate clustering algorithms through cophenetic correlation coefficients and the Hubert statistic, both of which measure the similarity between distance matrices derived from clustering results. The cophenetic correlation coefficient (CPCC) ranges from -1 to 1, with higher values indicating better clustering performance. It is calculated using the average of cophenetic distances in the cophenetic matrix. The Hubert statistic also ranges from -1 to 1, assessing concordance and discordance among pairs of examples, where a higher value suggests improved clustering.

External validation methods are introduced as a means of assessing clustering when external class labels are available. These methods involve comparing clustering results from an algorithm with external partitions, which may represent expert insights or results from other algorithms. Various external validation metrics, such as purity, precision, recall, and mutual information, can be employed to evaluate the clustering effectiveness.

To facilitate this comparison, a contingency matrix is constructed, comprising true positives (TP) and false positives (FP), among other terms. This matrix aids in quantifying the overlap between clusters generated by the algorithm and those defined by external sources, thereby providing insights into the clustering quality.
