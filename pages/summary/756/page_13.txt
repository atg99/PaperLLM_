The paper provides an overview of various sequence-to-sequence (seq2seq) models developed for neural abstractive text summarization, highlighting significant contributions made up to 2018. It categorizes different models based on their frameworks, training methods, optimizers, datasets used, and evaluation metrics like ROUGE and human assessments.

Key models include:

- Celikyilmaz et al., which introduced deep communicating agents and a semantic cohesion loss using LSTM architectures.
- Chen and Bansal's approach that combined an LSTM encoder with reinforcement learning for sentence rewriting.
- Hsu et al.'s model that employed both abstraction and extraction with an inconsistency loss mechanism.
- Li et al. introduced actor-critic methods using GRU networks in a reinforcement learning context.

The paper also discusses advancements in addressing the repetition problem in summarization. Temporal attention, initially proposed for neural machine translation, was adapted to mitigate repetition in multi-sentence summaries by monitoring past attention weights.

Overall, the paper encapsulates a variety of methodologies and innovations in the field, emphasizing the evolution of techniques aimed at improving the quality and coherence of generated summaries.
