The paper discusses the text generation process and training methodologies for models used in natural language processing. It begins by outlining the generation process, which uses a special start token "SOS" to initiate the generation of tokens one at a time, based on the conditional probability of the next token given all previous tokens and the input data. The generation continues until an end token "EOS" is produced or a maximum length is reached.

The authors review different approaches for learning model parameters, focusing first on cross-entropy training (XENT). This method maximizes the log-likelihood of observed sequences by minimizing the cross-entropy loss. It employs the teacher-forcing algorithm, which uses ground-truth tokens during training but relies on predicted tokens during testing. This discrepancy leads to exposure bias, where the model's outputs during testing differ significantly from the training data.

To address exposure bias, the paper introduces scheduled sampling, also known as Data As Demonstrator (DAD). In this approach, during training, the input at each decoding step can either be a model-generated token or an observed token from the training data. The choice is determined by a Bernoulli distribution, with a probability that varies over time, allowing the model to gradually adapt to using its own predictions rather than solely relying on ground-truth tokens. This method aims to reduce the gap between training and testing phases, improving the model's performance in generating coherent text.
