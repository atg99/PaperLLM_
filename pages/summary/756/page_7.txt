This paper provides a comprehensive overview of sequence-to-sequence (seq2seq) models specifically for neural abstractive text summarization. The authors conduct a literature survey and implement several methods within an open-source library called NATS. Key contributions of the paper include:

1. A thorough review of current advancements in seq2seq models focused on abstractive text summarization.
2. An examination of techniques addressing challenges in recurrent neural network (RNN) encoder-decoder architectures.
3. A discussion on various training strategies for seq2seq models and methods for generating summaries.
4. An open-source library that implements certain models and systematically investigates the impact of different network components on summarization performance.

The paper is structured as follows: it presents a taxonomy of seq2seq models, a detailed introduction to the basic framework and its extensions, various training strategies including word-level and sentence-level methods, and summary generation techniques such as beam search. Experimental results are provided based on datasets like CNN/Daily Mail, Newsroom, and Bytecup. The paper concludes by summarizing the findings and implications of the study.
