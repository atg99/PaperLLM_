The paper discusses the task of text summarization, which aims to condense lengthy documents into concise summaries while retaining essential information and meaning. There are two primary approaches to text summarization: extractive and abstractive. Extractive summarization selects sentences or phrases directly from the source text, resulting in grammatically correct summaries that maintain key information. In contrast, abstractive summarization generates new language using models that understand the content of the source documents, allowing for innovative summaries that can incorporate external knowledge.

The focus of the paper is on recent advancements in recurrent neural network (RNN)-based sequence-to-sequence (seq2seq) models for abstractive summarization. Seq2seq models have been effectively utilized in various natural language processing tasks, including text summarization. Initial improvements in this area were made through the introduction of attention-based encoders and neural network language model decoders, leading to significant performance gains over traditional methods.

The paper also highlights the development of the pointer-generator network, which combines abstraction and extraction techniques. This model allows for the copying of words from the source text and the generation of new words, effectively addressing challenges such as out-of-vocabulary words and the accurate reproduction of factual information. Subsequent studies have demonstrated the effectiveness of this mechanism, which also helps mitigate issues related to repetition and unnatural summary generation.
