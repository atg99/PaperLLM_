The paper discusses advancements in training methods for pointer-generator networks and the evolution of sequence-to-sequence (seq2seq) models, particularly in the context of natural language processing. T. Shi et al. introduced an algorithm that incorporates novel rewards, including saliency and entailment, alongside traditional ROUGE metrics to improve summary quality. Lietal proposed an actor-critic training framework utilizing an attention-based seq2seq model and a binary classifier to evaluate summary quality. Chen et al. presented a multi-step compression-paraphrase method for abstractive summarization, optimizing sentence extraction strategies through an advantage actor-critic algorithm. Keneshloo et al. offered a comprehensive overview of various reinforcement learning (RL) methods for seq2seq training, implementing these approaches in an open-source library.

The paper also highlights the limitations of traditional RNN-based seq2seq models, particularly issues related to vanishing and exploding gradients, as well as computational constraints when processing long sequences. It emphasizes the advantages of convolutional neural network (CNN)-based models, which allow for parallelization, linear computational complexity, and efficient gradient signal propagation. Notable models like ByteNet and quasi-recurrent neural networks (QRNN) are discussed, illustrating their effectiveness in outperforming LSTM-based architectures in tasks such as character-level machine translation while improving computational speed. The paper ultimately underscores the shift towards CNN-based approaches in seq2seq modeling for enhanced performance and efficiency.
