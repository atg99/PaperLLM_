The paper discusses the development of two sequence-to-sequence (seq2seq) models for text summarization, focusing on generating diverse outputs through modified beam search algorithms. The first model, Pθ1(y|x), generates sequences based on input x, while the second model, Pθ2(x|y), does the reverse. A key innovation is the introduction of a diversity rate parameter, γ, which encourages the generation of hypotheses from different parent sequences, thereby enhancing diversity in the output.

Additionally, the paper presents a re-ranking mechanism for the N-best list of generated sequences, combining scores from both models along with a task-specific auxiliary term. This is expressed as a scoring function that incorporates learned parameters to optimize performance.

The paper also introduces Diverse Beam Search (DBS), which partitions hypotheses into groups and applies a scoring function that augments dissimilarity. This approach ensures that generated sequences from different groups remain distinct, further promoting diversity.

The authors developed an open-source library, NATS, based on the RNN seq2seq framework for abstractive text summarization, which incorporates features like an attention-based model and a pointer-generator network. They conducted systematic experiments on three publicly available datasets—CNN/DailyMail, Newsroom, and Bytecup—to evaluate the effectiveness of their implementations and various hyperparameters.
