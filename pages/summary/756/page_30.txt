This paper surveys recent advancements in sequence-to-sequence (seq2seq) models for neural abstractive text summarization, highlighting the challenges related to model architectures, parameter inference mechanisms, and summary generation processes. It presents a taxonomy of these topics and reviews various seq2seq models utilized in the field. The authors developed an open-source toolkit called NATS, which includes features such as attention mechanisms and beam search.

Experimental results using the CNN/Daily Mail dataset are summarized, and benchmarks are established for two newer datasets: Newsroom and Bytecup. The findings indicate that model C10110 outperforms G11110 in terms of ROUGE scores across different summarization tasks.

The paper identifies several future research directions, including:
1. **Large Transformers**: These models, pre-trained on extensive text corpora, have shown exceptional performance and potential for zero-shot and low-resource summarization.
2. **Reinforcement Learning (RL)**: RL strategies can utilize user-defined metrics for model training, enhancing summarization quality by leveraging external resources.
3. **Summary Generation**: Moving from beam search to sampling-based approaches may increase the diversity and novelty of generated summaries.
4. **Datasets**: The reliance on news corpora often leads to extraction bias, necessitating the development of diverse datasets that encourage true abstraction in summarization.

Overall, the paper underscores the growing importance of seq2seq models in summarization tasks and outlines key areas for future exploration.
