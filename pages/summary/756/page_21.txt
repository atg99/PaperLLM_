The paper discusses neural abstractive text summarization using sequence-to-sequence models, focusing on training strategies and reinforcement learning (RL) techniques. It highlights the use of curriculum learning algorithms that adjust the probability of using ground-truth tokens as training progresses, allowing the model to gradually rely on its own predictions. The End-To-End Backpropagation (E2E) method is also introduced, which incorporates a fusion of top-k predicted tokens to enhance model training.

Additionally, the paper emphasizes the rising popularity of sequence-level training with deep RL algorithms, which facilitate the incorporation of user-defined metrics like ROUGE scores into the training process. The encoder-decoder model is framed as an agent that makes sequential decisions to generate summaries. The RL framework updates the model based on the comparison between the generated summary and the ground-truth summary. The paper mentions the REINFORCE algorithm as a common method for training sequence-to-sequence models and introduces the MIXER algorithm, which aims to improve convergence rates.
