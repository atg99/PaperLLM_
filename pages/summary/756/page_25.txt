The paper discusses a framework for neural abstractive text summarization using sequence-to-sequence (seq2seq) models, with a focus on the decoding process facilitated by the beam search algorithm. The algorithm initializes various sequences and probabilities, and iteratively computes candidate words and their associated probabilities through a decoder LSTM cell. The process includes selecting the top candidates, updating state variables, and choosing the best hypotheses based on accumulated probabilities.

Additionally, the paper introduces Maximum Mutual Information (MMI) as a method to enhance the learning process in neural models. MMI emphasizes the importance of both the dependency of the target summary on the source article and the likelihood of the source given a target summary. The training objective is reformulated to maximize mutual information, although calculating the likelihood of the target summary directly is challenging. The authors note that various approximation methods have been proposed to address this issue, which can be summarized in three steps.

Overall, the paper presents a comprehensive approach to improving abstractive summarization through advanced decoding strategies and training methodologies.
