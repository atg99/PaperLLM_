The paper provides an overview of various sequence-to-sequence (seq2seq) models developed for neural abstractive text summarization between 2015 and 2017. It categorizes the models by year, reference, highlights, frameworks, training methodologies, optimizers, datasets used, and evaluation metrics. 

Key highlights include:

- The introduction of attention mechanisms, with models such as Attention Based Summarization by Rush et al. (2015) utilizing a combination of bag-of-words, convolution, and attention in neural networks.
- The evolution of architectures, including LSTM and GRU models, with advancements in techniques like pointer networks and reinforcement learning.
- Diverse training strategies, including sequence-level training and multi-task learning approaches, as seen in models by Ranzato et al. (2015) and Pasunuru et al. (2017).
- A variety of datasets employed for training and evaluation, such as Gigaword, DUC, and CNN/DailyMail, alongside metrics like ROUGE, BLEU, and human evaluations for assessing performance.

The table encapsulates the significant advancements in neural summarization techniques, highlighting the transition from basic models to more complex architectures that incorporate attention and reinforcement learning to improve summarization quality.
