The paper discusses advancements in long document summarization using sequence-to-sequence (seq2seq) models, particularly focusing on hierarchical attention mechanisms to enhance performance. It outlines the architecture of seq2seq models, which typically consist of a hierarchical encoder designed to capture the structure of source documents. The encoder processes both chunks of text—representing top-level salient information such as important sentences, sections, and paragraphs—and keywords as lower-level salient information.

The hierarchical encoder operates by first encoding tokens within a chunk to create chunk representations, followed by encoding different chunks to form a comprehensive document representation. The paper employs a single-layer forward Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) for both word and chunk encoding.

Attention weights are calculated at each decoding step, allowing the model to focus on relevant parts of the document. Four models utilizing hierarchical encoding for summarization are reviewed:

1. **Hierarchical Attention**: This model emphasizes that words in less important chunks should receive less attention. It introduces a re-scaled word-level attention distribution that combines chunk-level and word-level attention to compute context vectors.

2. **Discourse-Aware Attention**: Similar to hierarchical attention, this approach modifies the re-scaled attention distribution by incorporating alignment scores instead of attention weights, refining how attention is distributed across chunks and words.

3. **Coarse-to-Fine Attention**: Aimed at improving computational efficiency, this model also features chunk-level and word-level attention. However, it samples word-level hidden states from chunks to compute context vectors, optimizing the summarization process.

The paper provides insight into how these hierarchical attention mechanisms contribute to generating coherent and contextually relevant summaries from long documents.
