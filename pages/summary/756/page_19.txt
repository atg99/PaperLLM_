The paper discusses various models and strategies for neural abstractive text summarization using sequence-to-sequence (seq2seq) frameworks. It emphasizes the significance of aligning word-level and sentence-level attention in models, where high word-level attention correlates with high sentence-level attention.

One notable method introduced is the Key-Information Guide Network (KIGN), which employs a two-step process to enhance summary generation by leveraging salient keywords extracted from source articles using the TextRank algorithm. The KIGN utilizes a bi-directional LSTM to encode key information and incorporates it into the decoder, modifying the alignment mechanism to improve output quality.

Another model discussed is the Reinforce-Selected Sentence Rewriting model, which consists of two seq2seq networks: an extractive model that identifies salient sentences and an abstractive model that paraphrases these sentences into a concise summary. This model has shown superior performance on datasets like CNN/Daily Mail and is noted for its computational efficiency compared to the pointer-generator network.

The paper also reviews training strategies for seq2seq models, distinguishing between word-level and sequence-level training methodologies. Word-level training focuses on optimizing the prediction of the next token, while sequence-level training often employs reinforcement learning (RL) techniques. The discussion includes various training algorithms and their applications to enhance the performance of seq2seq models in text summarization tasks.
