The paper discusses neural abstractive text summarization utilizing sequence-to-sequence (seq2seq) models, focusing on various training strategies and algorithms to enhance model performance. It highlights the limitations of traditional REINFORCE methods, such as slow convergence and challenges with large action spaces. To address these issues, the MIXER algorithm is introduced, which employs a curriculum learning strategy by initially training the seq2seq model using cross-entropy loss before transitioning to REINFORCE. This approach has shown improved performance in generating summaries compared to existing methods.

Additionally, the Self-Critic Sequence Training (SCST) method is presented, which utilizes a testing-time inference algorithm as a baseline in REINFORCE. SCST generates two action sequences during training: one from greedy search and another sampled from a distribution. The model's gradient is adjusted based on the rewards of both sequences, leading to lower variance and better optimization compared to standard REINFORCE. The authors propose a mixed loss function combining reinforcement learning and cross-entropy loss, improving both ROUGE scores and human readability of the generated summaries. The paper concludes by summarizing various RNN encoder-decoder architectures and training strategies, setting the stage for generating summaries from source articles.
