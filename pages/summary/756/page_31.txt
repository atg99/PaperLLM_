The paper discusses advancements in neural abstractive text summarization using sequence-to-sequence models, highlighting the need for new datasets to enhance summarization systems. It emphasizes that current automatic evaluation protocols, such as ROUGE and BERTScore, are inadequate for assessing the overall quality of generated summaries. Key features like factual correctness, fluency, and relevance should be evaluated by human experts. Therefore, a critical research direction is the development of improved evaluation systems that go beyond existing metrics to align more closely with human judgment. The paper references various studies and methodologies that contribute to this field, underlining the ongoing evolution and challenges in achieving effective abstractive summarization.
