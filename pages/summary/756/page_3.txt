The paper discusses neural abstractive text summarization using sequence-to-sequence (seq2seq) models, focusing on challenges such as exposure bias and inconsistencies between training and testing metrics. Exposure bias arises when the model is trained with ground-truth tokens but generates its own tokens during testing, leading to cumulative decoding errors. The mismatch between training (using cross-entropy loss) and evaluation metrics (like ROUGE and BLEU) further complicates performance assessment.

To address these issues, the authors explore training strategies including curriculum learning and reinforcement learning (RL). Curriculum learning, exemplified by scheduled sampling, gradually transitions the decoder's input from ground-truth to generated tokens, mitigating exposure bias. The MIXER approach combines cross-entropy training, REINFORCE, and curriculum learning, tackling both exposure bias and evaluation metric inconsistencies, although REINFORCE has high gradient variance.

The paper also highlights actor-critic methods, which involve a critic network estimating value functions to improve the stability of training. Self-critical sequence training (SCST) is noted for its lower variance compared to REINFORCE without requiring a second critic network.

In terms of applications to abstractive text summarization, various RL algorithms have shown success across language generation tasks. Notable contributions include Ling and Rush's coarse-to-fine attention framework and Paul et al.'s implementation of self-critic policy gradient methods, which combine RL with traditional loss functions to enhance performance and readability. Overall, the paper presents a comprehensive overview of advancements and methodologies in neural abstractive summarization, emphasizing the integration of RL to improve the effectiveness of seq2seq models.
