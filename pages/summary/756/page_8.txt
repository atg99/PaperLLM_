The paper discusses the basic framework of a sequence-to-sequence (seq2seq) model for abstractive text summarization, which consists of an encoder and a decoder. The encoder processes a source article, converting it into hidden states, while the decoder generates a summary based on these hidden states. The model uses one-hot representations for tokens in both the source article and the summary. The paper highlights the use of recurrent neural networks (RNNs), particularly long short-term memory (LSTM) and gated recurrent units (GRU), as the preferred architectures for seq2seq models, emphasizing the advantages of bi-directional LSTMs for better document representation.

The encoder reads the input tokens and produces sequences of hidden states that capture both forward and backward temporal dependencies. The decoder then utilizes these encoded representations to generate the summary. The paper details how the hidden and cell states of the LSTM decoder can be initialized using the encoded vectors from the encoder, and describes the process of updating the hidden states during decoding. The vocabulary distribution for generating target tokens is computed using a softmax function, which is central to determining the probability of selecting each token from the vocabulary. Overall, the paper aims to address existing challenges in text summarization, such as repetition and out-of-vocabulary words, while proposing potential solutions.
