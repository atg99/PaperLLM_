The paper discusses neural abstractive text summarization using sequence-to-sequence (seq2seq) models, highlighting the foundational role of LSTM-based encoder-decoder frameworks. It identifies challenges within these models, such as ineffective training of the encoder through backpropagation, leading to low accuracy and human-readability in generated summaries, often characterized by out-of-vocabulary (OOV) words and repetitions.

To address these issues, the paper introduces the attention mechanism, which has proven successful in various natural language processing tasks including machine translation and image captioning. In an attention-based encoder-decoder architecture, the decoder utilizes encoded representations and selectively focuses on relevant parts of the source text during each decoding step. For example, when summarizing a lengthy sentence about a soccer match, the decoder can concentrate on specific phrases to generate a concise summary.

The attention distribution over source tokens is calculated using an alignment mechanism, which helps the decoder determine where to focus to produce each target token. This is achieved through a content-based scoring function that computes alignment scores, facilitating the selection of pertinent information from the source text. The paper sets the stage for further exploration of models designed to enhance the quality of summaries produced through neural abstractive summarization.
