The paper discusses advancements in attention mechanisms for text summarization, focusing on hierarchical and graph-based attention models. The hierarchical attention mechanism captures chunk-level salient information based on attention weights, while the graph-based attention framework explicitly calculates saliency scores using the PageRank algorithm, which considers the similarities between chunks of text. This method allows for a more efficient selection of salient information during decoding by focusing on chunks that rank higher than those in previous steps.

Furthermore, the paper explores the combination of extractive and abstractive summarization approaches. It highlights that extractive methods typically outperform abstractive ones in terms of ROUGE measures, largely due to their ability to directly extract important snippets from source documents. The proposed Extractor + Pointer-Generator Network model integrates salient information from extractive models into an abstractive framework, enhancing performance. It incorporates various loss functions, including extractor loss and inconsistency loss, to optimize both the extractor and the pointer-generator network, ensuring consistency in attention across sentences and improving overall summarization quality.
