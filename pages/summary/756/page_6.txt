The paper discusses advancements in neural abstractive text summarization, focusing on various methodologies and challenges related to long documents. A guide network is utilized to encode key information representations that assist in the summary generation process. Long documents present unique challenges, such as identifying salient information, which Nallapati et al. addressed with a hierarchical attention model. Ling and Rush proposed a coarse-to-fine attention mechanism to handle very long sequences efficiently by splitting documents into manageable chunks. Cohan et al. introduced a discourse-aware attention model for large-scale scientific datasets, while Tan et al. developed a graph-based attention model leveraging a hierarchical encoder-decoder framework.

The paper also explores multi-task learning as a promising approach, where models can simultaneously tackle different tasks, enhancing their summarization capabilities. Pasunuru et al. integrated entailment generation into their summarization framework, and McCann et al. proposed a Natural Language Decathlon to address various tasks in a unified manner.

Additionally, the paper highlights the limitations of traditional beam search algorithms in generating diverse candidate sequences. To overcome this, several methods have been proposed, including the use of Maximum Mutual Information (MMI) and the Diverse Beam Search (DBS) algorithm, which aims to enhance output diversity in various language generation tasks.

Despite significant research in neural abstractive summarization, the authors note a scarcity of comprehensive survey papers on the subject. This article aims to systematically review the current advancements in sequence-to-sequence models for abstractive text summarization, covering aspects such as network structures and training strategies.
