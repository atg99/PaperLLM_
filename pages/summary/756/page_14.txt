The paper discusses advanced techniques for addressing the challenges of repetition and attention in sequence generation for tasks like text summarization. 

Firstly, a temporal attention score is defined, which normalizes attention based on previous decoding steps to prevent the decoder from repeatedly focusing on the same input tokens. This mechanism is crucial for ensuring varied and coherent outputs.

Secondly, intra-decoder attention is introduced as a method to track previously decoded tokens in the summary, thereby allowing the decoder to avoid redundancy by not re-encoding the same information. This is achieved through calculated attention weights that incorporate both source article tokens and previously generated summary tokens.

Additionally, the coverage mechanism is highlighted as a solution to the limitations of standard attention models, which often overlook past alignment data. The coverage vector accumulates attention distributions from previous decoding steps, informing current attention scores. A coverage loss is also defined to penalize repeated attention on the same source locations, promoting diversity in multi-sentence summaries.

Overall, these techniques aim to enhance the efficiency and quality of abstractive summarization by improving how attention is managed throughout the decoding process.
