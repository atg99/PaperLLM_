The paper titled "Neural Abstractive Text Summarization with Sequence-to-Sequence Models" by Tianshi, Yaser Keneshloo, Naren Ramakrishnan, and Chandan K. Reddy provides a comprehensive survey of neural abstractive text summarization using sequence-to-sequence (seq2seq) models. The authors highlight the growing popularity of these models and discuss various techniques aimed at enhancing their performance in terms of saliency, fluency, and human readability.

The survey categorizes the techniques into three main areas: network structure, parameter inference, and decoding/generation, while also addressing concerns like efficiency and training parallelism. The authors review several models initially developed for tasks such as language modeling and machine translation, which have been adapted for summarization.

In addition, the paper introduces the Neural Abstractive Text Summarizer (NATS) toolkit, an open-source library designed for abstractive text summarization. The authors conducted extensive experiments on the CNN/Daily Mail dataset to evaluate the effectiveness of different neural network components. They also benchmarked two models from the NATS toolkit on the recently released Newsroom and Bytecup datasets.

The paper emphasizes the significance of automatic text summarization in an era of information overload, offering insights into the methodologies and advancements in seq2seq models for generating high-quality summaries.
