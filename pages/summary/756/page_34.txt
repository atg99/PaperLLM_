This paper references various significant works in the field of natural language processing, particularly focusing on text summarization and related neural network approaches. Key contributions include the foundational work by Krizhevsky et al. on deep convolutional neural networks for image classification, which laid the groundwork for similar architectures in text processing. Kryscinski et al. critically evaluate neural text summarization techniques, highlighting advancements made in abstraction methods.

The paper discusses various models and frameworks, such as BART for natural language generation, and examines the role of reinforcement learning in dialogue generation. It also explores the use of generative adversarial networks for summarization tasks and the effectiveness of multi-task neural networks for natural language understanding.

Significant methodologies like ROUGE for evaluating summaries are mentioned, alongside advancements in attention mechanisms and the use of pretrained encoders for improved summarization outcomes. The paper emphasizes the importance of faithfulness and factuality in abstractive summarization, reflecting ongoing challenges in the field.

Overall, the references collectively illustrate the evolution of techniques in text summarization and the broader implications of neural network architectures in enhancing natural language processing capabilities.
