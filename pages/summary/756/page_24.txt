The paper discusses the process of summary generation, focusing on finding an optimal sequence for summarization. The objective is to maximize the log probability of a sequence given an input, which is often computationally complex due to the vast number of possible output sequences. The paper reviews the beam search algorithm as a practical approach to approximate exact inference.

The greedy search is introduced as a simpler method that generates sub-optimal sequences by selecting the best word at each decoding step. However, this method often results in less human-readable summaries. Beam search, on the other hand, balances efficiency and quality by retaining only the top scoring sequence fragments at each step, allowing for a more refined generation process.

The paper also addresses the issue of diversity in generated sequences, noting that traditional beam search can produce similar hypotheses, limiting the effectiveness of summarization systems. To enhance diversity, the authors reference research that explores various post-training decoding algorithms and techniques like oversampling, which can improve the variety of generated summaries without compromising overall performance. 

Overall, the paper emphasizes the importance of both optimizing summary generation and ensuring diverse outputs in the context of abstract summarization models.
