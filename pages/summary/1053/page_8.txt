The paper discusses the importance of explanations in artificial intelligence, particularly in enhancing understanding of classifiers and their predictions through positive and negative evidence. It highlights various goals for using explanations, such as improving models through regularization, enabling model compression, and addressing certification and legal considerations, although these applications are identified as areas for future work.

In the section on methods of explainable AI, the authors categorize approaches into model-agnostic techniques, local perturbation methods, propagation-based techniques, and meta-explanation methods. The discussion begins with explaining predictions using simple surrogate functions, which are easier to interpret than complex models like deep neural networks. Local Interpretable Model-agnostic Explanations (LIME) is presented as a key technique in this category, where it approximates complex models by sampling around the input of interest to fit an interpretable surrogate function. While LIME is versatile and applicable to any classifier, its high computational complexity is noted as a significant limitation, particularly for advanced models like GoogleNet, which can require considerable time to generate explanations for individual predictions.
