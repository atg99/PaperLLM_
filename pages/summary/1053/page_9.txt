The paper discusses various methods for explaining artificial intelligence models, particularly focusing on local perturbation techniques. It highlights the SmoothGrad method, which approximates the gradient by sampling the input's neighborhood, classifying it as a gradient-based explanation method. 

The paper categorizes explanation methods into those utilizing gradient information and perturbation-based approaches. Sensitivity Analysis (SA) is mentioned as a traditional method that explains prediction changes rather than the predictions themselves. However, SA faces limitations such as gradient shattering, making it less optimal for modern AI models. Variants of SA attempt to address these issues through techniques like local gradient averaging and path integration.

Perturbation-based methods, such as the occlusion method and Prediction Difference Analysis (PDA), evaluate the model's response to local changes by masking input dimensions or conditional sampling, respectively. While model-agnostic, these methods are computationally intensive due to the need for extensive evaluations of the model.

Additionally, the meaningful perturbation method is introduced as a meta prediction task that employs optimization to generate informative explanations. This approach aligns with other techniques that interpret learned concepts by creating prototypes using activation maximization. While these methods can yield insightful explanations, their computational demands can be significant.
