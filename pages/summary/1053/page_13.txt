This paper discusses the advancement of explainable artificial intelligence (AI), emphasizing the need for human-centered explanations in human-machine interactions. Recent approaches aim to aggregate low-level explanations and quantify neural representation semantics, signaling a shift towards developing more complex meta-explanations. The authors highlight that understanding user needs and optimizing explanations for effective human usage remains a significant challenge requiring further research.

The paper points out the absence of a formal, universally accepted theory of explainable AI, although some studies have begun to address this by creating mathematically grounded explanation methods, such as integrating explanations within the framework of Taylor decomposition. Additionally, axiomatic approaches are recognized as promising avenues for establishing a general theory of explainable AI.

The authors also note the challenge of utilizing explanations beyond mere visualization, advocating for their integration into broader optimization processes to enhance model performance and reduce complexity.
