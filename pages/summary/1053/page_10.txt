Propagation-based approaches to explanation utilize the internal structure of models to enhance the explanation process. One prominent method is Layer-wise Relevance Propagation (LRP), which applies to various neural network architectures including deep neural networks and LSTMs. LRP explains model decisions by redistributing the prediction from the output back to the input through local redistribution rules. This method can be integrated into the deep Taylor decomposition framework and has been adapted for other machine learning models by transforming them into a neural network format. The efficiency of LRP lies in its low computational cost, requiring only one forward and one backward pass, and it avoids issues like gradient shattering since it does not depend on gradients.

Other methods, such as Deconvolution and Guided Backpropagation, focus on identifying input patterns related to the network output rather than quantifying input feature contributions. Various heuristic and optimization-driven techniques also fall into the "leveraging structure" category, with implementations available in tools like the iNNvestigate toolbox.

Meta-explanations aggregate individual explanations to discern broader patterns in classifier behavior. Spectral relevance analysis (SpRAy) clusters individual heatmaps to automate the investigation of classifier strategies across datasets, identifying model weaknesses. Another approach, network dissection, assesses the semantics of hidden units to determine the concepts encoded by neurons, while recent studies explore explanations based on user-defined concepts and their relevance to predictions.
