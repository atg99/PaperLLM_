The paper discusses the importance of explainability in artificial intelligence (AI) systems, emphasizing that explanations are crucial for deriving new insights from data patterns that may not be evident to human experts. For instance, in fields like the game of Go or scientific research, AI can uncover novel strategies or associations that can lead to significant scientific discoveries. Historically, linear models dominated scientific communities due to their interpretability, but the emergence of explainable non-linear models, such as deep neural networks, has opened new avenues for research across various domains like neuroscience, health, and autonomous driving.

Furthermore, the integration of AI into daily life raises legal and ethical challenges, particularly concerning accountability and fairness. The EU's General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) introduces the right to explanation, mandating that individuals understand the rationale behind AI-driven decisions, such as loan rejections, to ensure compliance with anti-discrimination laws. The paper underscores that as AI's influence grows, the demand for transparency will become increasingly critical.

Lastly, the paper outlines various explanation techniques that provide insights into AI systems' decision-making processes. These techniques differ in their content, intended audience, and purpose, highlighting their practical utility in understanding AI operations.
