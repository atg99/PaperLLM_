The paper discusses various methods for evaluating the quality of explanations in explainable AI (XAI). One approach involves using word-level explanations to create document-level representations, where the effectiveness of these representations in tasks such as classification reflects the quality of the original explanations. Additionally, explanations are utilized in reinforcement learning and can also serve functional purposes like compressing or pruning neural networks.

Another evaluation method focuses on the fulfillment of specific axioms that define necessary properties of explanations, such as relevance conservation, explanation continuity, sensitivity, and implementation invariance. Unlike other quality measures that require empirical testing, the fulfillment of these axioms can often be demonstrated analytically.

Despite advancements in XAI, challenges remain in both methodological and theoretical aspects, particularly concerning the objective evaluation of explanation quality and the application of explanations beyond mere visualization. Current explanation methods, such as heatmaps, primarily provide first-order information, indicating which input features are relevant but failing to clarify the relationships between these features. This limitation is significant in fields like neuroscience, where understanding the interplay of multiple features is crucial.

Moreover, the explanations generated are often at a low abstraction level, making it difficult for humans to interpret them in relation to broader concepts. This necessitates a further interpretation step, which can lead to misunderstandings. The paper highlights the potential for meta-explanations that aggregate low-level evidence to provide insights into the model's behavior at a more abstract level, thereby enhancing human understanding.
