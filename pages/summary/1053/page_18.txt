The references listed in this section encompass a range of studies and methodologies related to explainable artificial intelligence, deep learning, and neural network visualization. Key contributions include the work by Ross et al. on training differentiable models with explanation constraints, which emphasizes the importance of understanding model behavior. Samek et al. have provided significant insights into evaluating visualizations of deep learning models, highlighting the need for interpretability in AI systems. Other notable works include the development of techniques such as Grad-CAM for visual explanations from deep networks, and the application of recurrent deep learning models to neuroimaging data. Additionally, concepts such as Axiomatic Attribution and the use of Fisher vectors for image classification are discussed, reflecting diverse approaches to enhancing model transparency and performance. The references collectively underscore the critical intersection of AI, interpretability, and practical applications in various domains, including neuroscience and computer vision.
