The paper discusses the importance of transparency and explainability in artificial intelligence (AI), particularly in safety-critical applications. While black box AI systems are widely accepted in consumer electronics and online services due to their sufficient performance, the lack of transparency becomes a significant concern in areas where incorrect decisions could jeopardize human life or lead to substantial financial losses. This is evident in fields such as autonomous driving and healthcare, where trust in AI systems is paramount.

The authors emphasize that the ability to explain AI decision-making processes is crucial for establishing this trust. They draw parallels to the historical example of Clever Hans, a horse that seemingly performed arithmetic by responding to subtle cues from its handler, rather than understanding the math itself. This phenomenon mirrors issues present in modern AI systems, where models may rely on spurious correlations in data rather than genuine understanding. For instance, a machine learning model might correctly identify objects in images not by recognizing them, but by detecting unrelated context clues, such as water for boats or rails for trains, demonstrating the potential pitfalls of unexamined datasets.

The paper suggests that these "Clever Hans" predictors highlight the necessity for thorough scrutiny of AI systems to ensure reliable and valid decision-making, thus reinforcing the call for greater transparency in AI technologies.
