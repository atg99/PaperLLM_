The paper discusses the limitations of current deep neural networks, highlighting their tendency to rely on superficial features, such as environmental context, rather than genuine understanding of the concepts they are meant to classify. Examples include models that distinguish between "Wolf" and "Husky" based on snow presence, or those that associate dumbbells with the arms lifting them. Such models, labeled "Clever Hans" predictors, may perform well on test sets but are likely to fail in real-world scenarios.

To address these issues, the authors advocate for the importance of explainability in AI systems. Explainability aids in identifying biases and weaknesses in models, allowing for better understanding and improvement of AI systems. It is particularly crucial in contexts such as medical diagnosis or autonomous driving, where trust and verification of decisions are essential. Explanations can provide medical experts with comprehensive insights, similar to a detailed radiology report, and help ensure that AI systems operate in a robust and safe manner.

Furthermore, the paper emphasizes the social dimension of explanations in human-machine interactions. Just as explanations foster trust in human relationships, they are vital for human-AI relationships. They enhance understanding and acceptance of decisions, making individuals feel more involved in the decision-making process. Consequently, the authors argue that explainability should be a fundamental aspect of AI systems to promote trust and improve user experience.
