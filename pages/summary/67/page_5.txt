The paper discusses the performance of three recurrent neural network (RNN) architectures: bidirectional RNN, bidirectional LSTM, and bidirectional GRU, focusing on their application in speech recognition tasks. It highlights the efficiency of GRUs, which have a simpler structure than LSTMs, making them equally capable of handling long-term dependencies. 

The architecture employs spectrograms generated from audio waveforms as input features, producing English text as output. The model consists of an input layer, five hidden layers (including three feedforward layers and a bidirectional recurrent layer), and an output layer using a softmax function to predict character probabilities. The bidirectional layer enhances context processing by considering both past and future information.

The study implements a connectionist temporal classification (CTC) loss function to evaluate the model's performance, allowing for the integration of all possible character alignments. This facilitates the backpropagation of loss gradients for weight updates during training, contributing to the overall effectiveness of the proposed architectures in speech recognition.
