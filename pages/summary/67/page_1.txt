The paper titled "Performance Evaluation of Deep Neural Networks Applied to Speech Recognition: RNN, LSTM and GRU" by Apeksha Shewalkar, Deepika Nyavanandi, and Simone A. Ludwig investigates the effectiveness of various deep neural network architectures in automatic speech recognition tasks.

The authors explain that Deep Neural Networks (DNNs), characterized by multiple hidden layers, have gained traction in this field due to their ability to integrate acoustic and language models. They note that standard feedforward neural networks are inadequate for processing speech data because they lack the capability to handle temporal dependencies. To address this limitation, Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) were developed, which can process sequences by maintaining information over time.

However, RNNs struggle with long-term dependencies due to the vanishing and exploding gradient problems. To overcome this, Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks were introduced, which effectively manage both long-term and short-term dependencies in speech data. The Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) is another advancement that simplifies LSTM while still addressing long-term dependencies.

The study evaluates the performance of RNN, LSTM, and GRU on a reduced TED-LIUM speech dataset. Results indicate that LSTM achieves the lowest word error rates, showcasing its superior performance in speech recognition tasks. In contrast, GRU demonstrates faster optimization while producing word error rates comparable to those of LSTM.

The paper emphasizes the significance of these findings in enhancing speech recognition systems, providing insights into the strengths and weaknesses of each neural network architecture.
