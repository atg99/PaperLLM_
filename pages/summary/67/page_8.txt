The paper presents a comparative analysis of three neural network models: RNN, LSTM, and GRU, using two different layer architectures of 500 nodes and 1,000 nodes. The results indicate that LSTM consistently outperforms the other two models in terms of word error rate (WER), loss, and mean edit distance across both architectures. 

In the 500-node architecture, the WER for LSTM is 77.55%, significantly lower than RNN's 87.02% and GRU's 79.39%. The loss values also reflect this trend, with LSTM at 160.51, compared to RNN's 186.61 and GRU's 162.22. Similarly, for mean edit distance, LSTM shows the best performance at 0.3853.

In the 1,000-node architecture, LSTM further improves its performance, achieving a WER of 65.04%, while RNN and GRU have WERs of 78.66% and 67.42%, respectively. The loss for LSTM is 134.35, indicating better accuracy, while the mean edit distance for LSTM is 0.3222, again lower than the other models.

Figures included in the paper illustrate the WER results and the progression of WER values per epoch for all models, emphasizing the superior performance of the LSTM model throughout the training process. Overall, the findings suggest that LSTM is the most effective model for the given tasks, with improvements observed as the architecture scales from 500 to 1,000 nodes.
