This paper evaluates the performance of RNNs, LSTMs, and GRUs for speech recognition, utilizing the reduced TED-LIUM data set. The authors implement an end-to-end training approach with Connectionist Temporal Classification (CTC) for labeling sequence data.

The paper begins by discussing the limitations of feedforward neural networks in speech recognition, particularly their inability to manage temporal dependencies and varying speaking rates. RNNs are introduced as a solution due to their ability to retain information from previous time steps. However, RNNs face challenges with long-term dependencies and require pre-segmented training data for optimal performance. This led to the development of LSTMs, which effectively manage long-term dependencies through specialized memory cells.

The authors review related work, noting the shift from traditional generative models to sequence-to-sequence discriminative models in speech recognition. They highlight the advantages of LSTMs and GRUs over standard RNN architectures, particularly in language modeling and various speech processing tasks. The paper outlines the structure of the experiments conducted, which compare the efficacy of RNNs, LSTMs, and GRUs on the TED-LIUM data set.

The findings are discussed in detail, with results indicating that LSTMs and GRUs outperform traditional RNNs in speech recognition tasks. The paper concludes with suggestions for future work, emphasizing the need for continued exploration of deep learning architectures in improving speech recognition accuracy.
