The paper evaluates the performance of Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs), Long Short-Term Memory networks (LSTMs), and Gated Recurrent Units (GRUs) on a reduced TED-LIUM speech dataset, focusing on two architectures with 500 and 1,000 nodes per layer. Key evaluation metrics include Word Error Rate (WER), loss, mean edit distance, and running time. 

Results indicate that LSTMs achieve a lower WER than GRUs in both architectures, with the 1,000-node architecture yielding a WER of 65.04% for LSTM and 67.42% for GRU. However, LSTMs require more running time compared to GRUs, which is a critical consideration for practical applications. The paper recommends using GRUs for the reduced dataset as they provide competitive WER values with a more efficient running time.

The authors note that standard feedforward neural networks struggle with speech data due to their inability to capture temporal dependencies, leading to the development of RNNs and subsequently LSTMs and GRUs to address these challenges, particularly the vanishing and exploding gradient problems associated with long-term dependencies.

Future work will focus on optimizing parameters, including learning rates and dropout rates, and exploring the impact of increasing neuron counts in hidden layers. The research acknowledges support from the Extreme Science and Engineering Discovery Environment and NVIDIA Corporation.
