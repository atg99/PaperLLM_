The paper presents an evaluation of deep neural networks, specifically RNN, LSTM, and GRU models, for speech recognition tasks using a subset of the TED-LIUM release 2 corpus. The study employs the ADAM optimization algorithm for backpropagation due to its flexibility with learning rates and training parameters.

The dataset consists of 378 audio talks and their corresponding transcripts, totaling 11.7 GB after reduction. Evaluation measures focus on accuracy and speed, with key metrics including Word Error Rate (WER), loss, and mean edit distance. WER, calculated from substitutions, insertions, deletions, and the total number of words, serves as the primary indicator of performance, with lower values indicating better recognition.

Parameters for the experiments included a dropout rate of 30%, 10 epochs, a training batch size of 16, and various neuron counts in hidden layers. Results showed that LSTM outperformed both RNN and GRU in WER and loss metrics across both 500-node and 1,000-node architectures. Specifically, LSTM achieved WER values of 77.55% and 65.04% for the respective architectures, while RNN recorded the highest WER at 87.02% and 78.66%. The mean edit distance was also lowest for LSTM.

In terms of execution time, RNN was the fastest, but its performance was inferior to LSTM and GRU. For the 500-node architecture, LSTM took over 2 days to run, while GRU took 1.5 days. The running time increased significantly for the 1,000-node architecture, with LSTM taking over 7 days compared to GRU's 5 days and 5 hours. Overall, the results indicate that while LSTM is slower, it offers superior performance in speech recognition tasks compared to RNN and GRU.
