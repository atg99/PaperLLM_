The paper discusses the interaction dynamics between operators and two types of robotic systems: Automated Guided Vehicles (AGVs) and collaborative robots. It highlights that interactions with AGVs primarily involve issuing start/stop commands and ensuring accurate positioning, whereas interactions with collaborative robots require timely feedback on their status and progress in tasks. The research utilizes UX-based tools to map these interactions, which aids in identifying user needs and potential error scenarios. 

The study emphasizes the importance of a User/Task Matrix to establish a general understanding of workstation dynamics. A subsequent detailed analysis focuses on determining the optimal interface type based on interaction challenges, interface characteristics, workplace positioning, and preferred access methods, including device suitability. This structured approach aims to enhance interface design guidelines tailored to specific robotic interactions, ultimately improving user experience in robotic environments.
