The paper discusses the design of interfaces in Human-Robot Collaboration (HRC), emphasizing the importance of human factors in interface design. It highlights a UX design cycle methodology aimed at understanding human needs during interactions with robots, particularly in industrial settings. The research identifies various interface types suitable for different working conditions, noting that while gesture and speech interfaces are viable for simple communications, more complex interactions necessitate different approaches, such as physical buttons or visual cues.

Key findings include the recommendation of using bidirectional communication with medium frequency and low complexity, supporting the use of ubiquitous and wearable interfaces. The paper suggests that multiple feedback types can enhance user comprehension, and it outlines the necessity of distinct communication strategies for critical situations, emphasizing the need for easily accessible commands.

The analysis utilizes UX tools, such as the User/Task Matrix and Experience Maps, to guide interface design and validate user experience. The approach demonstrates the importance of integrating UX considerations into HRC design, showcasing its effectiveness through a case study involving assembly operations with anthropomorphic robots and Automated Guided Vehicles (AGVs). The authors conclude that this methodology can lead to more usable and effective interfaces in collaborative environments.
