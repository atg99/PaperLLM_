The paper discusses the importance of incorporating User Experience (UX) into the design of Human-Robot Interaction (HRI) within the context of Human-Robot Collaboration (HRC) in modern manufacturing. While prior research has predominantly focused on technological advancements, this work emphasizes the necessity of considering human factors during the design process. The authors propose a structured UX-oriented method to analyze human-robot dialogues and map interactions during shared tasks, ultimately identifying requirements for effective HRI design.

The research was applied to an industrial case involving assembly operations supported by collaborative robots and Automated Guided Vehicles (AGVs). A multidisciplinary team utilized the proposed method to define system interface requirements by focusing on UX and interaction design tools rather than solely on technological aspects. The results demonstrated the effectiveness of this approach in recognizing interaction needs and guiding interface design.

In the broader context of Industry 4.0, the paper highlights the evolving role of robots in smart factories, emphasizing that while robots enhance process efficiency, human involvement remains critical for flexibility and responsiveness to market demands. The paper advocates for a holistic understanding of HRI, drawing from various disciplines such as engineering, psychology, and sociology, to ensure that robots not only operate efficiently but also meet the social and emotional needs of human users.
