The paper discusses the need to reorganize tasks in human-robot collaboration (HRC) by assigning cognitive and control tasks to humans while relegating repetitive and precision-oriented tasks to robots. It emphasizes that current research in human-robot interaction (HRI) tends to focus on technical aspects, often neglecting the human perspective. Although some studies address safety and the design of collaborative robot applications, this paper advocates for a human-centered design (HCD) approach that places human needs and experiences at the forefront of HRI design.

The research outlines a structured methodology aimed at enhancing HRI through a detailed understanding of communication exchanges between humans and robots. It seeks to establish guidelines for designing human-robot interfaces, particularly in manufacturing contexts, where effective collaboration is crucial. The paper defines HRI as all forms of interaction between humans and robots, while HRC is a subset focused on shared goals and physical interaction within a defined workspace.

The authors categorize HRI into three areas: coexistence, cooperation, and collaboration, each distinguished by the level of shared goals and physical contact between humans and robots. They stress that successful HRC relies on intuitive user interfaces that facilitate clear communication, allowing both parties to leverage their respective strengths—robots for accuracy and speed, and humans for cognitive problem-solving.

In summary, the paper advocates for a shift towards a human-centered approach in the design of HRI, emphasizing the importance of understanding user needs and behaviors to enhance the overall user experience and improve collaboration between humans and robots in various applications.
