The paper discusses the integration of three perspectives in industrial human-robot interaction (HRI): the robot-centred view, the robot cognition-centred view, and the human-centred view. The authors emphasize the importance of merging the human-centred view with traditional robot-centred approaches to incorporate human factors and principles from Human-Computer Interaction (HCI) and Human-Machine Interaction (HMI). This integration is crucial as it ensures that technological advancements benefit human interaction, addressing physical, cognitive, and psychosocial aspects as highlighted by the International Ergonomics Association.

The authors argue that enhancing human factors in HRI can reduce physical fatigue and cognitive load, ultimately improving user experience (UX). They define UX as the perceptions and responses resulting from the interaction with a product or system. A focus on UX design is essential for fostering positive interactions with robots, as negative experiences can hinder the effective use of robotic features.

The paper identifies the current state of UX consideration in industrial robot design as relatively low, suggesting a need for a structured human-centred methodology that prioritizes user needs. This involves employing UX-based techniques to evaluate interactions, primarily through usability assessments that measure effectiveness, efficiency, and user satisfaction.

Different methodologies for HRI evaluation are discussed, with a distinction between those focusing on robot performance and those examining user experiences. The authors advocate for user testing in real-world conditions and the use of both quantitative and qualitative evaluation techniques to gather comprehensive data on user interactions.

In conclusion, the paper calls for a systematic approach to HRI design that prioritizes human needs and communication requirements from the outset. This approach is essential for ensuring the successful integration of robots in industrial settings and realizing their full potential benefits. The proposed UX-based approach involves forming multidisciplinary teams that engage end-users throughout the design process, ensuring that user experience is thoughtfully designed rather than assumed.
