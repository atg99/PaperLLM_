The paper classifies Human-Robot Interaction (HRI) interfaces into four main categories: visual displays, gestures, speech and natural language, and physical and haptic interactions. These interfaces correspond to three levels of interaction: coexistence, cooperation, and collaboration. Visual interfaces dominate the industrial sector due to their ability to present complex information clearly to operators. In contrast, natural language interfaces are underexplored in industrial settings but have seen applications in social robotics.

The paper emphasizes the importance of intuitive interfaces that facilitate easy command input and feedback comprehension, especially for users with limited technological knowledge. It notes that current research on industrial HRI has largely overlooked a human-centered approach, primarily focusing on safety and side-by-side collaboration rather than genuine interaction. Emerging research directions include gesture recognition and affective robotics, which adapt robot behavior based on human physiological signals.

Additionally, the paper discusses the use of Virtual Reality technology for designing and testing HRI scenarios, which can enhance safety and effectiveness. It highlights the need for compliance with ISO safety standards for collaborative robots, which dictate operational behaviors based on human proximity but do not address interface design. The paper argues for incorporating human factors in interface design to enhance communication and understanding between humans and robots, thereby improving cooperation and collaboration.
