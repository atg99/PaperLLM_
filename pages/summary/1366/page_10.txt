The paper presents a detailed analysis of a robotic assembly process involving two collaborative robots (Robot R1 and Robot R2) and two Automated Guided Vehicles (AGVs), focusing on user experience (UX) and task interactions. The process is structured around a User/Task Matrix, which outlines the sequence of tasks performed by different actors, including the operator and the robots, at various positions during the assembly.

Key points include:

1. **Task Coordination**: The User/Task Matrix identifies all tasks, their temporal sequences, and the actors involved, highlighting simultaneous tasks and their interactions. This analysis aids in understanding the complexity of the activity and the potential for confusion among operators due to overlapping tasks.

2. **Communication Needs**: The analysis reveals the frequency and complexity of human-robot interactions, emphasizing the need for effective communication to minimize operator stress and enhance efficiency.

3. **Experience Map**: Developed from the User/Task Matrix and focus group discussions, the Experience Map illustrates the operator's journey through the process. It maps out the tasks and interaction points, although it does not include emotional responses, which can vary by individual and are difficult to predict early in the project.

4. **UX Scenarios**: The global user experience is summarized in five steps under regular conditions, with additional maps illustrating scenarios in case of robot or AGV errors. These maps clarify the interaction types and communication needs, indicating that while the operator primarily interacts with AGVs, awareness of the robots’ operations is also crucial.

Overall, the analysis provides insights into the complexity of the assembly process and the importance of understanding both task coordination and user experience to optimize interactions between human operators and robotic systems.
