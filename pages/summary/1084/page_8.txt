The paper introduces POPQORN, a novel framework for quantifying the robustness of recurrent neural networks (RNNs). This method evaluates various RNN architectures, including vanilla RNNs, LSTMs, and GRUs, by providing a certified lower bound on the minimum distortion that RNN adversaries can cause, contrasting with the upper bounds typically provided by adversarial attacks.

In Experiment I, the paper presents a comparison of POPQORN with baseline methods (CLEVER-RNN and C&W-Ada) on the MNIST dataset, showing averaged bounds and standard deviations for various network configurations. Results indicate that POPQORN achieves competitive certified robustness across different architectures and configurations.

Experiment II utilizes heat maps to illustrate the certified 2-norm bounds for the digit "1" and "4." The findings reveal that points later in the stroke sequence tend to have larger bounds, indicating they are less influential on the overall classification. This suggests that later points can endure greater perturbations without affecting the classification outcome.

In Experiment III, POPQORN is applied to LSTM classifiers for question classification tasks using the UIUC CogComp QC dataset. The analysis highlights the importance of individual words in sentences, with the method identifying the most sensitive words that significantly impact classification decisions.

The conclusion emphasizes POPQORN's capability to compute meaningful certified bounds and its effectiveness in revealing the sensitivity of individual frames in sequential data, thereby enhancing understanding of RNN robustness.
