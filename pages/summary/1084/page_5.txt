The paper "POPQORN: Quantifying Robustness of Recurrent Neural Networks" focuses on establishing bounds for the robustness of Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks by deriving upper and lower bounds for hidden and cell states. The authors emphasize the importance of knowing these bounds, which can be achieved through similar derivations as those used for vanilla Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) and Gated Recurrent Units (GRUs). The process involves recursively propagating linear bounds from the output layer back to the first layer, while addressing the complexities introduced by the non-linearities in LSTMs, specifically the functions related to the gates of the network.

To tackle these non-linearities, the authors propose using bounding planes rather than simple lines, which allows for a more accurate representation of the relationships between the variables involved in the gates. The bounding planes are defined based on the maximum and minimum values of the non-linear functions and are formulated as a constrained optimization problem, solved using gradient descent methods.

The paper provides a detailed derivation for a 1-layer LSTM, illustrating how to bound the output function by applying the bounding planes to the components of the LSTM. By defining specific parameters related to the weights and states, the authors derive inequalities that help in quantifying the robustness of the LSTM network. Overall, the work contributes to a deeper understanding of LSTM behavior under varying conditions and offers a methodological approach to assess their robustness.
