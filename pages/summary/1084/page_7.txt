The paper presents POPQORN, a method for quantifying the robustness of recurrent neural networks (RNNs). The approach involves initializing a distortion parameter and performing a binary search to compute upper and lower bounds of adversarial distortion based on given thresholds. The process continues until stopping criteria are met, either through a maximum number of iterations or when the search interval falls below a specified tolerance.

The authors highlight that their work represents a novel contribution to the field, as no previous methods have provided guarantees for RNN robustness quantification. They compare POPQORN with existing methods, including the CLEVER score, which estimates minimum adversarial distortion, and the C&W attack, which provides an upper bound. The adaptations made for sequential input to these methods are also detailed.

Experiments conducted include evaluations of POPQORN on vanilla RNNs and LSTMs trained on the MNIST dataset. Results indicate that bounds computed by POPQORN correlate with the architecture of the networks, where increasing hidden neurons generally leads to higher bounds, while more layers tend to decrease them. Additionally, the paper discusses the identification of sensitive frames in input sequences and provides supplementary comparisons of bounding techniques.

Overall, POPQORN demonstrates effective quantification of robustness for RNNs, offering insights into the characteristics of certified bounds and their implications for adversarial robustness. The implementation details and experimental setup are also documented, contributing to the reproducibility of the results.
