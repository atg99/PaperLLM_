The paper discusses a proof that combines the descent property with a specific inequality to analyze the convergence behavior of a sequence generated by an optimization algorithm. The descent property ensures that the function value decreases at each iteration, leading to a relationship between the function values at consecutive iterations. 

By defining a variable related to the difference in function values and applying a telescoping sum, the authors derive bounds on the differences across iterations. They utilize a lemma from previous work to establish a relationship involving the ratio of consecutive differences, which helps in analyzing the convergence rate.

The proof further distinguishes between two cases based on the frequency of a specific ratio being above a threshold in the iterations. These cases yield different upper bounds on the sequence's convergence behavior. Ultimately, the authors conclude that the convergence rate can be characterized by a minimum of two expressions, which can be adjusted by selecting appropriate constants.

The results culminate in specific convergence bounds, noting that setting particular values for the constants simplifies the expressions, leading to the desired conclusions about the sequence's behavior in optimization contexts.
