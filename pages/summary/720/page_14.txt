The paper discusses Exact Policy Mirror Descent (PMD) methods, a framework for optimization algorithms based on mirror descent. This framework utilizes Bregman divergence, a distance-like function defined for strictly convex and continuously differentiable functions. Two notable examples of Bregman divergence are the squared Euclidean distance and Kullback-Leibler divergence. The paper emphasizes the importance of ensuring that the second argument in KL divergence lies within the relative interior of the feasible set.

The set of feasible policies is defined as the Cartesian product of the feasible action sets, and a weighted divergence function is introduced. This function retains the properties of Bregman divergence, specifically being nonnegative and equating to zero only when the two policies are identical.

The PMD method is formulated with dynamically weighted divergences, where the next policy is obtained by minimizing a combination of the policy gradient and the divergence term. This approach allows for the incorporation of a state distribution and the discounted state-visitation distribution under the current policy. The paper concludes by presenting the mathematical formulation that leads to the update of the policy in the PMD framework.
