The paper discusses the convergence rates of policy gradient methods, focusing on the proximal gradient method's convergence analysis. It highlights a descent property that allows for the derivation of convergence rates, specifically showing that the method achieves a rate of O(1/sqrt(k)) under certain conditions. The authors introduce the concept of weak gradient-mapping dominance, which is a relaxed version of the classical Kurdyka-Lojasiewicz condition, facilitating faster sublinear convergence rates. 

The paper defines weak gradient-mapping dominance with an exponent ranging between 1/2 and 1, establishing that if a function F is weakly gradient-mapping dominant, the proximal gradient method can generate sequences that converge to the minimum of F at a specified rate. The authors present Theorem 4, which states that under weak gradient-mapping dominance, the proximal gradient method with a fixed step size achieves a convergence rate that depends on the initial function value and the parameters of the dominance condition. 

In particular, for the case where the exponent is equal to 1, the convergence rate is notably improved, demonstrating that the method can effectively minimize the function F while maintaining computational efficiency. Overall, the paper extends classical results on convergence rates to the context of policy gradient methods, providing a theoretical foundation for their application in optimization problems.
