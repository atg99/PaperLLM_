The paper discusses the convergence rates of policy gradient methods in the context of Markov Decision Processes (MDPs). Traditional algorithms like value iteration and Q-learning rely on dynamic programming and the contraction property of the Bellman operator, which face challenges with nonlinear function approximation. In contrast, policy gradient methods focus on optimizing expected value functions and are compatible with differentiable policy parametrization, particularly with modern deep neural networks.

Recent research has begun to clarify the convergence properties of these methods. While it was previously believed that policy gradient methods converge only to local minima due to nonconvexity, findings by Fazel et al. demonstrate that they can converge to global optima under certain conditions in linear quadratic control problems. Agarwal et al. further show that the projected policy gradient method achieves a sublinear convergence rate of O(1/√k), with iteration complexity influenced by state-visitation distributions and Lipschitz constants.

Zhang et al. propose a variational policy gradient framework that improves convergence to O(1/k). The natural policy gradient method also shows an O(1/√k) convergence rate, with Agarwal et al. enhancing this to O(1/k) and indicating that the step sizes can be chosen independently of the Lipschitz constant.

Overall, the paper highlights significant advancements in understanding the convergence rates of policy gradient methods, emphasizing their potential for achieving global optimization efficiently.
