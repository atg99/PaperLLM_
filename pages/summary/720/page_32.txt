The paper discusses a composite optimization problem characterized by minimizing a function F, which is the sum of an L-smooth function f and a proper, convex, and closed function . The proximal gradient method is shown to converge geometrically to a global minimum under the strong gradient-mapping domination condition. This is demonstrated by combining the descent property with the strong gradient-mapping dominance condition, resulting in a geometric recursion that establishes a relationship between the function values at iterations.

The paper also draws connections between the strong gradient-mapping domination condition and established concepts like the Kurdyka-Lojasiewicz (KL) condition and the proximal Polyak-Lojasiewicz (PL) condition. The KL condition, with a specific exponent, provides a bound on the subgradients of F, while the PL condition involves a different formulation but is shown to be equivalent to the KL condition under certain constants. An interlacing relationship between the gradient-mapping domination and the proximal PL condition is proposed, suggesting that these conditions may imply each other, thus enhancing the understanding of convergence in optimization problems.
