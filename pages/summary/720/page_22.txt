The paper discusses linear and superlinear convergence in standard discounted Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) without the need for entropy regularization. It highlights that linear convergence can be achieved despite the potential mismatch in the support of the policy distribution. The convergence rate is characterized by the distribution mismatch coefficient, which is a measure of how closely the policy aligns with the optimal policy.

The work references a previous study by Lan (2021) that achieved a slower linear convergence rate using an approximate policy mirror descent method, which involved more complex technicalities due to its use of diminishing regularization and varying step sizes.

For superlinear convergence, the paper outlines conditions under which this can occur, particularly focusing on the step sizes and their relationship to the convergence of the policy. It emphasizes that if certain conditions are met, including the convergence of the transition probability matrix of the policy, then the policy will converge to the optimal policy at a superlinear rate. The discussion includes specific conditions that are sufficient for establishing superlinear convergence, which are less stringent than directly assuming convergence to the optimal policy. These conditions have also been validated in the context of classical Policy Iteration algorithms.
