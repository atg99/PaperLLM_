The paper discusses the properties of a row stochastic matrix P(π) and its implications on the spectral radius, which is shown to be strictly less than one according to the Perron-Frobenius theorem. This leads to the conclusion that the matrix is always invertible. The weighted value function Vπ is defined in terms of the matrix, indicating its dependence on the policy π and the reward vector r(π).

The paper introduces the concept of the distribution mismatch coefficient, which quantifies the difference between two distributions under a given policy. The state-visitation distribution ds, s0(π) is formulated, ensuring that it sums to one across all states. The coefficients are derived from the inverse of the matrix (I - P(π)), reflecting the probability of transitioning between states.

The distribution mismatch between two distributions π and ν is defined as the maximum ratio of their components, providing an asymmetric measure of their divergence. This mismatch is finite if the support of ν contains that of π, and it is bounded when ν represents a uniform distribution.

The paper highlights the importance of these distribution mismatch coefficients in the convergence properties of policy gradient methods, referencing previous works. The focus is particularly on the case where the distributions are equal and the policies are optimal, leading to a specific formulation for the mismatch coefficient C*π.
