The paper discusses the convergence rates of policy gradient methods in the context of Markov Decision Processes (MDPs). It emphasizes the importance of the distribution mismatch coefficient, denoted as C?γ, which is closely related to concentrability coefficients used in approximate dynamic programming. The paper asserts that C?γ is advantageous because it imposes no restrictions on MDP dynamics and can be finite even when other concentrability coefficients are infinite.

When γ is chosen as the stationary distribution of the MDP under the optimal policy π*, it results in C?γ = 1, simplifying the analysis of minimizing the value function Vπ. The paper also notes that for discounted MDPs with entropy regularization, the stationary distribution typically has full support over the state space, although this is not guaranteed unless the MDP is ergodic.

The paper further delves into the definitions of Q-functions and their role in policy gradient methods. It defines the state-action value function Qs,a and establishes relationships between Q-functions and value functions through equations that show how Q-values can be computed. The gradients of the value functions, known as policy gradients, are derived and expressed in terms of weighted Q-functions.

A key result discussed is the performance difference lemma, which is essential for analyzing discounted MDPs and related algorithms. The paper provides an alternative proof for a variant of this lemma, which has been referenced in previous works, highlighting its relevance in the analysis presented. Overall, the paper focuses on establishing formal frameworks for understanding the convergence properties of policy gradient methods in reinforcement learning.
