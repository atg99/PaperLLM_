The paper discusses convergence rates of policy gradient methods, particularly focusing on the Policy Mirror Descent (PMD) method. It highlights the challenges in estimating the exact value of the step size parameter, proposing a more aggressive increasing rule that ensures convergence. Theorem 10 establishes that to ensure a specific performance gap between the estimated and optimal value functions, a certain number of iterations is required, with iteration complexity being dimension-independent under specific conditions.

The paper also examines a special case where the performance evaluation distribution is the stationary distribution of the Markov Decision Process (MDP) under the optimal policy. In this scenario, the iteration complexity is shown to be logarithmic in terms of the desired accuracy.

Additionally, it references recent studies on policy gradient methods with entropy regularization, noting that such approaches guarantee ergodicity and cover the entire state space. The analysis presented in the paper aligns with previous work while highlighting key differences, particularly in the assumptions and methods used to derive convergence rates.
