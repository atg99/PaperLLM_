The paper discusses the convergence rates of projected policy gradient methods in reinforcement learning. The method generates a sequence of policies starting from an initial policy, utilizing a projection step defined in the Euclidean norm. The projection is performed separately for each state within the state space.

Agarwal et al. established that with a constant step size, the projected policy gradient method converges at a rate of O(1/√k). The convergence is characterized by the minimization of the difference between the value of the policy at step k and the optimal value. Two key ingredients underpinning this analysis are the smoothness condition, which bounds the difference in gradients of the value function, and the variational gradient domination condition, which relates the value function of the policy to the optimal value function.

The paper extends these results to show that the projected policy gradient method can achieve a faster convergence rate of O(1/k) under specific conditions. The theorem presented applies when the parameters of the method are equivalent, with an additional consideration for cases where they differ, incorporating a distribution mismatch coefficient.

Overall, the findings highlight the efficiency of projected policy gradient methods in optimizing policies within reinforcement learning frameworks, emphasizing the importance of the chosen step sizes and conditions for achieving rapid convergence.
