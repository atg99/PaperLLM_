The paper discusses the sample complexity of the inexact Projected Mirror Descent (PMD) method using a simple Q-estimator. It establishes conditions under which the difference between the estimated and true Q-values remains bounded with high probability. Specifically, it states that if the batch sizes are sufficiently large, the estimation error can be made small. Theorem 16 formalizes this by providing bounds on the value function's difference after K iterations, given certain step size constraints and batch size requirements. Additionally, it outlines conditions for achieving a desired accuracy level with respect to the sample complexity of state-action pairs. The complexity is expressed in terms of the number of states and actions, along with logarithmic factors. The proof involves applying the union bound across iterations to ensure the error bounds hold throughout the process.
