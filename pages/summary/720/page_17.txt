The paper discusses the convergence rates of policy gradient methods, particularly focusing on the policy mirror descent method and its performance in relation to general Bregman divergence. It builds upon previous work by Agarwal et al. and Lan, extending their results to a broader context. The authors derive a convergence rate of O(1/k) for the policy mirror descent method, which applies to any state-visitation distribution.

The paper employs the performance difference lemma to analyze the convergence, demonstrating that the difference in value functions at successive iterations is non-negative. The authors introduce specific notation to simplify the presentation of their results, particularly concerning the state-visitation distribution under the optimal policy.

The main theorem establishes that the gap between the value function at the k-th iteration and the optimal value function decreases at a rate dependent on the Bregman divergence, providing a clear framework for understanding the convergence behavior of policy gradient methods. This work contributes to the theoretical foundation of reinforcement learning algorithms by clarifying their convergence properties in a more generalized setting.
