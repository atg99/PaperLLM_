The paper discusses inexact Policy Mirror Descent (PMD) methods for discounted Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) with large state and action spaces, where computing exact policy gradients or Q-functions is impractical. It introduces an inexact PMD method that minimizes a specific objective involving an inexact evaluation of the Q-function.

The convergence properties of this method are analyzed under an assumption that bounds the evaluation error of the Q-function. Specifically, it is assumed that the difference between the inexact Q-function and the exact Q-function is limited by a constant. The paper derives results showing that the policy updates in the inexact PMD method lead to non-increasing values of the expected value function, although the objectives are no longer strictly decreasing due to the inexact evaluations.

The proof of the convergence results relies on the performance difference lemma and uses Hölder's inequality to relate the inexact evaluations to the updates. The findings indicate that while the inexact evaluations introduce some challenges, the method still maintains convergence properties under the specified assumptions.
