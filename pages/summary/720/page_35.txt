The paper discusses the convergence rates of policy gradient methods, particularly focusing on softmax policy gradient techniques. It highlights the importance of understanding global convergence rates in the context of reinforcement learning, where these methods are commonly applied. The authors reference several key works in the field, including foundational texts on optimization and Markov Decision Processes, which provide a theoretical backdrop for their analysis.

The authors build on previous research, such as error bounds for policy iteration and value iteration, to establish a framework for evaluating the performance of policy gradient methods. They emphasize the role of first-order optimization techniques and the significance of non-uniformity in enhancing convergence rates.

The study also references various algorithms, including Trust Region Policy Optimization and Proximal Policy Optimization, discussing their implications for achieving faster convergence in regularized Markov Decision Processes. The paper concludes by suggesting that adaptive strategies can lead to improved convergence rates, thereby enhancing the efficiency of policy gradient methods in practical applications.
