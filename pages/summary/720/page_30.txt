The paper presents a theory of weak gradient-mapping dominance to enhance the sublinear convergence rate of projected policy gradient methods in discounted Markov Decision Processes (MDPs). By using a simple, non-adaptive rule for geometrically increasing step sizes, the authors demonstrate that policy mirror descent methods can achieve linear convergence without the need for entropy or strong convex regularizations. The results indicate that the convergence rates with strong convex regularizations do not outperform the proposed method, regardless of the regularization strength.

The study emphasizes the importance of dynamic preconditioning based on discounted state-visitation distributions for achieving fast convergence rates that are largely independent of problem dimensions. It notes that the choice of local Bregman divergence, whether KL-divergence or squared Euclidean distance, does not significantly impact the results. As step sizes approach infinity, the preconditioned policy mirror descent methods converge to the classical Policy Iteration algorithm, suggesting that these methods can be viewed as inexact Policy Iteration techniques.

A key limitation identified is the focus on direct policy parametrization. The authors propose two potential extensions: one being the treatment of policy parametrization as a nonlinear optimization problem, which may complicate convergence guarantees, and the other following the framework of compatible function approximation. The latter approach allows the extension of the results to general policy parametrization while maintaining stability with geometrically increasing step sizes.

The paper concludes with acknowledgments to contributors and mentions that some findings were developed during a tutorial preparation at a conference. The authors express a desire to further investigate reducing sample complexity dependence on certain coefficients.
