The paper discusses the convergence rates of policy gradient methods, addressing a mistake in a previous proof regarding quasi-concavity. The authors provide a counter-example to clarify that the function in question is neither quasi-convex nor quasi-concave, but affirm that this error does not impact the remaining results.

In the appendix, the authors derive the policy gradient formula using matrix calculus, detailing the components of the value function and the application of matrix calculus techniques to arrive at the policy gradient expression. They introduce a component-wise expression leading to an aggregated form of the policy gradient.

Additionally, the authors define a stronger notion of gradient-mapping domination, which they argue results in geometric convergence to a global optimum. This is characterized by a condition involving a smooth function and its properties, specifically relating to the strong gradient-mapping dominance condition. The paper concludes with this refined understanding of convergence rates in policy gradient methods.
