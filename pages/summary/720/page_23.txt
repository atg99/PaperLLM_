The paper discusses the convergence rates of policy gradient methods, focusing on the conditions that lead to linear and local quadratic convergence. A key finding is that a finite constant condition allows for stronger convergence guarantees compared to previous conditions. The authors reference Khodadadian et al. (2021), who demonstrated that the Natural Policy Gradient (NPG) method can achieve superlinear convergence under specific conditions, particularly with entropy regularization ensuring a unique optimal policy. 

The paper also connects the analysis of the Policy Mirror Descent (PMD) method to classical Policy Iteration, noting that PMD does not impose restrictions on step sizes, allowing for large or increasing step sizes. Under certain limits, the PMD method aligns with the classical Policy Iteration method, maintaining similar convergence rates. The authors highlight that their results provide a convergence rate comparable to those established for various policy gradient methods, with implications for adaptive step size rules and non-adaptive schedules. Overall, the findings contribute to understanding the efficiency and robustness of policy gradient methods in reinforcement learning contexts.
