The paper discusses the convergence rates of policy gradient methods, focusing on the construction of the Q-estimator bQs;a((k)) using trajectories T(k;i). The Q-estimator is defined as the average of individual Q-estimators bQ(i)s;a((k)), which are computed over time steps and rewards. A lemma provides a high-probability bound on the error between the estimator bQ((k)) and the true Q-value Q((k)). It establishes that if the number of trajectories Mk satisfies a specific growth condition, the estimation error can be bounded with high probability. The proof involves defining the expectation of the Q-estimator and applying Hoeffding's inequality to show that the estimation error remains within a specified range across all state-action pairs. The results indicate that the convergence of policy gradient methods can be achieved under certain conditions, contributing to the understanding of their efficiency and reliability in reinforcement learning contexts.
