The paper discusses a framework for Markov Decision Problems (MDPs) with a focus on minimizing regret rather than maximizing reward. It defines a stationary policy that depends only on the current state, with the value of such a policy calculated as the expected sum of discounted regret over time. The authors propose transforming the reward matrix into a regret matrix to align with optimization conventions. They consider an initial state distribution and aim to minimize the expected value function associated with this distribution.

The paper emphasizes policy gradient methods for minimizing the weighted value function. These methods iteratively generate a sequence of policies through the evaluation of the policy gradient. The authors introduce a projected policy gradient method and a more general form based on mirror descent, which can incorporate various distance-like functions. They highlight the natural policy gradient method as a specific case when using Kullback-Leibler divergence. The primary contribution of the paper is the analysis of convergence rates for these policy gradient methods.
