The paper discusses the convergence rates of policy gradient methods, specifically focusing on the policy mirror descent methods. It establishes that these methods can achieve linear convergence without the need for regularization when geometrically increasing step sizes are utilized. The iteration complexity for achieving a specified accuracy is derived, and when the stationary distribution under the optimal policy is used, the results align with existing theoretical bounds. 

The authors also address conditions for superlinear convergence and relate their findings to the classical Policy Iteration method. They explore the iteration complexity of inexact policy mirror descent methods, demonstrating that the use of geometrically increasing step sizes remains stable despite potential errors in policy gradient or Q-function evaluations. The paper presents a sample complexity bound that incorporates factors related to the state and action spaces.

In the preliminary section, critical definitions and properties of infinite-horizon discounted Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) are outlined, including the probability simplices over state and action spaces and the formulation of the value function. The value function is expressed in terms of the transition and reward functions, laying the groundwork for subsequent analysis. 

Overall, the paper contributes to the understanding of convergence behavior in policy gradient methods, providing both theoretical insights and practical implications for their implementation.
