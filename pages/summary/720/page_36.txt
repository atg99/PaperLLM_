The provided references encompass significant contributions to the field of reinforcement learning. Sutton and Barto's "Reinforcement Learning: An Introduction" serves as a foundational text, outlining core concepts and methodologies in reinforcement learning. Sutton et al. discuss policy gradient methods, highlighting their effectiveness in scenarios with function approximation. Szepesvári and Munos focus on finite time bounds for fitted value iteration, providing theoretical insights into value-based methods.

Wainwright presents a minimax optimal approach to variance-reduced Q-learning, emphasizing its robustness. Williams introduces simple gradient-following algorithms that enhance connectionist reinforcement learning, while Ye establishes strong polynomiality for the simplex and policy-iteration methods within Markov decision processes.

Yuan et al. analyze sample complexity in vanilla policy gradient methods, contributing to understanding the efficiency of these approaches. Zhan et al. propose a generalized framework for policy mirror descent in regularized reinforcement learning, demonstrating linear convergence. Lastly, Zhang et al. introduce a variational policy gradient method tailored for reinforcement learning with general utilities, expanding the applicability of policy gradient techniques.

Collectively, these works advance theoretical foundations and practical algorithms in reinforcement learning, addressing key challenges such as convergence, efficiency, and applicability across diverse scenarios.
