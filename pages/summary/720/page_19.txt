The paper discusses the convergence rates of policy gradient methods, specifically focusing on the projected Q-descent and exponentiated Q-descent methods. It establishes that the projected Q-descent method has a broader range of step sizes that can ensure convergence compared to the exponentiated update variant, particularly when the initial policy is uniform. The analysis shows that for both methods, the step size can be arbitrarily large, which is a departure from classical optimization where step sizes are typically bounded.

The paper also introduces the concept of linear convergence in the policy mirror descent algorithm. It defines the optimality gap and the per-iteration distribution mismatch coefficient, which are crucial for understanding the convergence behavior. A key result is presented that establishes a relationship between these quantities, suggesting that under certain conditions, the algorithm exhibits linear convergence.

Overall, the findings highlight the unique properties of discounted Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) that allow for larger step sizes and provide insights into the convergence dynamics of policy gradient methods.
