The paper discusses the convergence rates of Policy Gradient Methods (PGM) with a focus on the use of Proximal Policy Optimization based on Bregman divergences. It introduces an update rule for policy parameters that is independent of certain preconditioning choices, utilizing adaptive preconditioning with dynamically weighted divergences. Two specific methods are highlighted: Projected Q-descent, which uses squared Euclidean distance, and Exponentiated Q-descent, which employs KL-divergence, both resulting in closed-form update rules. 

The analysis reveals that with a constant step size, the convergence rate is O(1/k), while an exponentially increasing step size leads to linear convergence, influenced by the distribution mismatch coefficient. The paper asserts that these results apply to PMD methods with general Bregman divergences, achieving convergence rates that match or exceed existing benchmarks. The key factor for rapid convergence is identified as the adaptive preconditioning through weighted divergence functions, with the choice of local divergence (KL or Euclidean) being less critical. 

The analysis relies on performance difference lemmas and proximal optimization techniques, ensuring rigor in covering both divergence types under specified technical conditions.
