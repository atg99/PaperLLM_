The paper discusses the convergence rates of policy gradient methods, particularly focusing on the sample complexity required to achieve a desired accuracy. It establishes conditions under which the value function converges within a specified number of iterations, leading to estimates for batch sizes and total state-action samples needed for convergence. The results indicate that the sample complexity is O(ε²), which is an improvement over previous works that reported complexities of O(ε⁴) and O(ε³). The paper also contrasts its findings with those of other researchers, noting that while some approaches achieve similar complexities, they may include additional factors that affect performance.

The authors reference several studies that have contributed to the understanding of sample complexities in discounted Markov Decision Processes (MDPs), highlighting the advances made in the tabular setting. They note that while their results have a worse dependency on the distribution mismatch coefficient compared to established lower bounds, there remains potential for further improvement in the sample complexity of stochastic policy gradient methods. The paper emphasizes the importance of refining these methods to enhance their efficiency in practical applications.
