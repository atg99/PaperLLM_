The paper discusses the convergence rates of policy gradient methods in reinforcement learning. The authors analyze the optimality and approximation properties of these methods, particularly in the context of distribution shifts. The study builds on previous work that established bounds on sample complexity and global optimality guarantees. Key contributions include demonstrating linear convergence for finite Markov decision processes and examining the effects of entropy regularization on convergence rates. The paper synthesizes findings from various foundational texts on dynamic programming and optimization, highlighting the importance of first-order methods in achieving efficient convergence. Overall, the research provides insights into improving the performance of policy gradient methods by addressing convergence challenges in practical applications.
