The paper introduces the performance difference lemma, which states that the difference in value functions, Vs(π) - Vs(π̃), can be expressed in terms of the expected value of the Q-functions and their respective policies. This relationship is formalized in Equation (19), linking the value functions to the Q-function differences and the state transition probabilities.

The proof utilizes the definition of Q-functions and manipulates the equations to derive the performance difference, leading to a representation that shows how changes in policies affect performance. The lemma also extends to a weighted version (Equation (20)), emphasizing its role in analyzing policy gradients.

The significance of this lemma is highlighted in its implications for variational gradient domination and descent methods, which are crucial for optimizing policies with potentially large step sizes.
