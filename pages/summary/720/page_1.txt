This paper examines the convergence rates of policy gradient methods in infinite-horizon discounted Markov decision problems with finite state and action spaces. The author develops a theory of weak gradient-mapping dominance to demonstrate a sharp sublinear convergence rate for the projected policy gradient method. Additionally, it is shown that a class of policy mirror descent methods, including the natural policy gradient and projected Q-descent methods, can achieve a linear convergence rate with geometrically increasing step sizes, without requiring regularization techniques like entropy. The paper also analyzes an inexact policy mirror descent method and estimates its sample complexity under a simple generative model. The study contributes to understanding the efficiency of reinforcement learning algorithms in optimizing policies for sequential decision-making.
