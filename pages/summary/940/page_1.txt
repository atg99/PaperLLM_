This paper discusses the roles of resolution and statistical uncertainty in hierarchical bandits-based black-box optimization algorithms, proposing a framework termed optimum-statistical collaboration. This framework manages the interaction between optimization error and statistical error during the optimization process. The authors provide a general analysis applicable to a wide range of functions and local smoothness assumptions, which is broader than previous studies.

The framework leads to the development of a variance-adaptive algorithm, VHCT, which is shown to achieve rate-optimal regret bounds theoretically under various local smoothness conditions. Experimental results demonstrate that VHCT outperforms existing algorithms in several scenarios. The paper emphasizes the importance of balancing resolution and uncertainty in creating efficient black-box optimization algorithms, addressing practical challenges faced in optimizing complex systems.
