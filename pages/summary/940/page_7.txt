The paper discusses an algorithmic framework for optimizing functions using a well-defined resolution descriptor and uncertainty quantifier. It emphasizes the importance of selecting a good node in the optimization process, which is reflected in the mild requirement that the function value should be close to the optimum. The framework allows for the reduction of certain subsets of choices, leading to bounded regret in the optimization process.

The implementation section introduces a novel VHCT algorithm that utilizes a variance-adaptive choice of statistical uncertainty quantifier. It critiques existing local smoothness assumptions used in prior works, which can be overly restrictive. The paper proposes a generalized local smoothness condition, termed ϕ(h)-local smoothness, which broadens the analysis to include functions that may not conform to previous assumptions. This new condition allows for better handling of complex functions by adjusting the resolution descriptor accordingly, ultimately leading to valid regret bounds for a wider array of functions. The examples provided illustrate the flexibility and applicability of the proposed framework in optimizing various types of objective functions.
