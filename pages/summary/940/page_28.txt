The paper discusses the derivation of an upper bound for a specific expectation related to the performance of algorithms in a Bayesian optimization context. By equating the dominating terms in the inequalities derived from two expressions (a) and (b), the authors simplify the analysis to focus on the leading terms, leading to a refined upper bound expression. The final result incorporates constants and logarithmic terms, establishing a relationship between different algorithm performances.

In the experimental section, the authors detail their implementation of various algorithms, including POO and HOO, using publicly available code. They highlight the efficiency of their algorithms compared to Bayesian Optimization (BO), noting that BO is significantly more computationally expensive and yields inferior performance in terms of cumulative regret.

Synthetic experiments are presented to compare the performance of different algorithms under varying smoothness parameters. The results indicate optimal parameter settings for VHCT and HCT, and the authors choose these settings for subsequent real-life experiments. The paper emphasizes the advantages of their proposed methods over traditional Bayesian optimization approaches.
