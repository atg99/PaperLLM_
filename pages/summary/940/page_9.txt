The VHCT Algorithm is a variant of the HCT algorithm designed to optimize decision-making in a structured environment using a known smoothness function and a specific partition. The algorithm incorporates an upper-bound for each node, which is based on the average observed reward and adjusted for exploration and exploitation through two key terms: the observed error (OEh) and the stochastic error (SEh,i). The upper-bound is defined as Uh,i(t) and is infinite for unvisited nodes, allowing the algorithm to prioritize exploration of less explored areas.

To enhance the tree structure utilization, a tighter upper bound Bh,i(t) is introduced, ensuring that no node's upper bound exceeds its children's maximum upper bound. The selection policy of the VHCT algorithm favors nodes with the highest Bh,i(t), ensuring efficient exploration.

A threshold τh,i(t) is defined to determine when a node can explore its children, which is contingent on the relationship between SEh,i and OEh. This threshold is lower in VHCT compared to HCT, facilitating more efficient exploration in low noise scenarios.

The paper also discusses the expected cumulative regret associated with VHCT, providing upper bounds that depend on the variance of the noise encountered during exploration. The bounds are refined using knowledge of noise variance, which can lead to improved accuracy in decision-making compared to naive estimates. Overall, VHCT is positioned as a more effective approach for exploration in noisy environments.
