The paper discusses an optimum-statistical collaboration framework for node selection in an optimization process. It outlines a selection policy that searches nodes from the root to leaves, pulling nodes based on the relationship between observed estimates (OE) and statistical estimates (SE). The goal is to ensure that the relationship OE1≥SE1>OE2≥SE2≥... is maintained along the path to the global maximum.

The framework emphasizes a dynamic process where both OE and SE decrease as the exploration algorithm progresses deeper into the tree. The authors propose a theorem providing a general regret upper bound for any selection policy adhering to this framework. The theorem states that if the policy ensures the pulled node meets a specific condition, a bound on cumulative regret can be established with a high probability.

The results are applicable across various functions and do not depend on the specific forms of OE and SE, making the findings broadly relevant for optimization tasks.
