The paper discusses the performance of various synthetic objective functions in optimization experiments, specifically focusing on the VHCT algorithm. Four functions are analyzed: 

1. **DoubleSine**: A one-dimensional function with multiple sharp local minima and one global minimum, originally proposed by Grill et al. It is characterized by its complex landscape, making optimization challenging.

2. **Counter Example**: This function decreases rapidly around zero, complicating smoothness measurement. Despite this, it is continuously differentiable and monotonic, making it easier to optimize.

3. **Himmelblau**: A two-dimensional function featuring four flat global minima. The study uses the negative of this function for maximization within a defined range, thereby simplifying the optimization task.

4. **Rastrigin**: A multi-dimensional function known for its numerous sharp local minima and a single global minimum. The negative version is also used for maximization in a 10-dimensional space.

The results indicate that VHCT outperforms other algorithms, particularly in the smoother Himmelblau function compared to the more complex Rastrigin and DoubleSine functions. The paper also notes that the performance of VHCT is examined under high-noise conditions, with noise uniformly distributed in a specified range. Overall, the findings support the theoretical claims about VHCT's efficiency in optimization tasks.
