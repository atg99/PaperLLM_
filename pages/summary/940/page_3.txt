This paper introduces a framework for optimizing black-box functions using hierarchical bandits, termed optimum-statistical collaboration. It comprises two components: a resolution descriptor and an uncertainty quantifier. The framework is analyzed through a unified theoretical approach that accommodates various forms of these components, covering all black-box functions meeting the general local smoothness assumption and having a finite near-optimality dimension, which previous studies did not address.

A novel uncertainty quantifier, the variance-adaptive quantifier (VHCT), is proposed, enhancing exploration by leveraging variance information from past samples. The framework demonstrates different regret guarantees based on smoothness assumptions, with VHCT showing improved convergence in scenarios with minimal reward noise. Experimental results indicate that the variance-adaptive quantifier outperforms existing algorithms across diverse objectives.

In related work, the paper critiques earlier bandit-based optimization methods that impose complex assumptions, such as weak Lipschitz conditions and predefined budget constraints. Recent approaches like POO and others have simplified these assumptions but still require fixed budgets, limiting their applicability as anytime algorithms.

The problem is framed as optimizing an implicit objective function within a constrained evaluation budget, with stochastic feedback modeled by a noise term. The aim is to identify a point close to the global maximum within the given budget. Performance is evaluated using cumulative regret, defined as the difference between the optimal value and the sum of observed rewards over time, alongside the alternative measure of simple regret.
