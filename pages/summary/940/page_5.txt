The paper presents the Optimum-Statistical Collaboration (OSC) algorithm, which aims to optimize decision-making in a statistical context. The algorithm incorporates two key components: the Resolution Descriptor (OE) and the Uncertainty Quantifier (SE). 

The Resolution Descriptor, OE, quantifies the optimization error by bounding the difference between the function value at any point in the partition and the global optimum. It ensures that the optimization process remains focused on areas close to the global optimum.

The Uncertainty Quantifier, SE, assesses the statistical estimation error for a given node, providing a measure of uncertainty based on the number of pulls from that node. It is designed to ensure that the probability of significant estimation errors remains below a specified threshold.

Algorithm 1 outlines the OSC process, which iteratively selects nodes based on the defined OE and SE values. The algorithm initializes a set of partitions and proceeds through a loop, updating the selected nodes and pulling values until the optimization criteria are met.

Overall, the OSC framework is designed to improve optimization efficiency by balancing exploration and exploitation based on statistical measures of uncertainty and resolution. The theoretical analysis provided in the paper supports the effectiveness of the proposed algorithm in optimizing decision-making processes.
