This paper focuses on measuring algorithm performance through cumulative regret, particularly relevant in scenarios like medical trials where the cost of suboptimal decisions must be accounted for. The study employs hierarchical partitioning to discretize the parameter space, allowing for a structured exploration of nodes based on their rewards.

The concept of near-optimality dimension is introduced as a generalized definition that extends previous work. It quantifies the number of near-optimal regions within the hierarchical partition, enabling a better understanding of the function's behavior and its optimization challenges. The definition is flexible, accommodating various local smoothness properties of functions, which enhances the framework's applicability to a broader range of optimization problems.

The paper establishes a general regret bound based on this near-optimality dimension, highlighting that a slowly decreasing function used in the analysis does not necessarily simplify the optimization task. Instead, it can indicate a more complex landscape, underscoring the intricacies involved in algorithm performance evaluation and optimization strategies.
