The paper focuses on the performance of the VHCT algorithm in blackbox optimization, particularly under the local smoothness conditions specified by Condition (GLS). It examines two smoothness functions: ϕ(h) = ν1ρh and ϕ(h) = 2/h, deriving corresponding near-optimal regret bounds for each case. 

Theorem 4.1 establishes an upper bound on the expected cumulative regret for the first smoothness function, indicating that the regret is influenced by variance, which suggests that VHCT can outperform non-adaptive algorithms in scenarios with low to moderate noise. Theorem 4.2 provides a similar analysis for the second smoothness function, noting that the regret bounds are also dependent on the number of near-optimal regions.

The authors highlight that previous works have shown regret bounds for specific smoothness functions, but their results extend the analysis to different smoothness assumptions. The paper emphasizes the importance of variance in regret analysis and suggests that VHCT's adaptive nature allows for faster convergence compared to traditional methods.

The experimental section compares VHCT against other algorithms, including T-HOO, HCT, POO, PCT, and Bayesian Optimization, to validate its effectiveness. The findings support the theoretical claims regarding VHCT's performance in various optimization scenarios.
