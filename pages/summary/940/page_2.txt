The paper addresses limitations in existing black-box optimization algorithms that rely on specific smoothness assumptions, particularly the exponential local-smoothness condition. Previous studies, such as those by Auer et al. and Grill et al., have focused on functions that satisfy this condition, which restricts their applicability to a narrow class of functions. The authors highlight the inadequacy of current methods in analyzing functions that do not meet these smoothness requirements, exemplified by a proposed function that lacks local optima yet is not exponentially smooth.

The authors propose a more unified theoretical framework that allows for the exploration of a broader range of functions and algorithms without being constrained by local smoothness assumptions. This framework emphasizes the collaboration between optimization error and statistical error in the exploration process. They introduce a new variance-adaptive black-box algorithm, VHCT, which can handle functions with varying levels of smoothness and different uncertainty quantification methods.

Key contributions of the paper include the identification of two critical components in the exploration mechanism and the development of an algorithm that operates under mild assumptions, thus expanding the scope of black-box optimization.
