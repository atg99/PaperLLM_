The paper discusses a bound on the cumulative regret in a decision-making process under certain conditions. It establishes that the difference in the regret, denoted as ∆ ht,it, is less than or equal to a maximum value derived from two functions, OE and SE, which represent optimal and suboptimal estimates respectively. 

The first term in the bound is expressed in a series of inequalities, leading to an upper limit for the regret. The analysis involves a binary tree structure where nodes represent decisions, and the number of children nodes is not restricted, allowing for a flexible K-nary tree representation. The paper emphasizes that nodes are selected based on their parent's selection frequency, ensuring that certain optimality conditions are met.

The final cumulative regret is upper bounded by a combination of logarithmic and summation terms, indicating the relationship between regret and the number of selections made, as well as the near-optimality dimension related to the parameters a, C, and OE. The results are presented with a high probability assurance, specifically at least 1−δ/4n², ensuring robustness in the findings.

Additionally, the paper includes preliminary notations necessary for understanding the proof of the main theorem, aligning with previous works by Shang et al. and Azar et al. on similar topics.
