This paper discusses the effectiveness of SpotTune, a fine-tuning method, compared to other fine-tuning variants like Fine-tuned ResNet-101 and L2-SP. The findings indicate that while Fine-tuned ResNet-101 has a higher capacity and performs well, it underperforms against SpotTune across most datasets, except for WikiArt, which has a larger training dataset. Experiments showed that when reduced training data from WikiArt was used, SpotTune still achieved higher accuracy than Fine-tuned ResNet-101, supporting the hypothesis that dataset size affects performance.

SpotTune is distinguished by its specialized fine-tuning policy for each instance, taking into account the similarities between the target and source datasets. It also reduces overfitting by sharing layers with the source task without parameter refinement. The paper evaluates three variants of SpotTune: one that routes all images through fine-tuned blocks, a global variant that uses a fixed number of layers, and a random policy that fine-tunes a set number of layers randomly. Results demonstrate that an optimized policy is superior to a random one.

Visualization of the learned policies reveals that different datasets require varying fine-tuning strategies, and the number of fine-tuned blocks utilized differs significantly across datasets. This emphasizes the advantage of an image-specific fine-tuning approach, which allows SpotTune to adaptively select the best fine-tuning strategy for individual examples, enhancing overall performance and efficiency.
