The paper presents a method called SpotTune, which focuses on fine-tuning specific blocks of a pre-trained neural network to enhance classification accuracy on target datasets. The methodology introduces a loss function that includes a term to control the number of fine-tuned blocks, denoted as \( l_k \), and a deterministic policy loss, \( l_e \), which encourages the policy to assign values of either 0 or 1 to blocks, thus creating a global policy applicable to all images. The overall loss function combines the classification loss \( l_c \) with the additional losses, allowing the network to learn the optimal blocks for fine-tuning without manual selection.

The experiments conducted compare SpotTune against various fine-tuning and regularization techniques across multiple datasets, including fine-grained classification benchmarks and datasets with significant domain mismatches from ImageNet. The datasets used in the evaluation include CUBS, Stanford Cars, Flowers, Sketches, and WikiArt, among others. Performance is evaluated based on classification accuracy, and results from the Visual Decathlon Challenge are also reported, emphasizing the robustness of SpotTune across different visual domains.

The paper's comparison with baselines such as standard fine-tuning, feature extraction, stochastic fine-tuning, and fine-tuning specific layers demonstrates the effectiveness of SpotTune in achieving higher accuracy. The approach is particularly advantageous as it learns the best blocks to fine-tune, providing a more efficient and accurate model adaptation compared to traditional methods. Future work will explore the optimal number of blocks to fine-tune, potentially leveraging reinforcement learning for improved performance.
