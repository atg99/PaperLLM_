The paper presents a comparison of SpotTune, a fine-tuning method, against various baseline models across multiple datasets, including CUBS, Stanford Cars, Flowers, WikiArt, and Sketches. The results, summarized in Table 2, indicate that SpotTune consistently outperforms other methods in classification accuracy.

Key findings include the effectiveness of different fine-tuning strategies. The Standard Fine-tuning method achieves high accuracy, while stochastic variants and specific layer fine-tuning (last-k layers) show slightly lower performance. Notably, the Random Policy, which randomly decides layer fine-tuning, performs competitively but does not surpass SpotTune.

The paper also discusses several baseline methods, including L2-SP, which uses regularization to maintain a balance between new task learning and pre-trained model performance, and Progressive Neural Networks, which focus on lifelong learning by combining shared and task-specific representations.

SpotTune employs a ResNet-50 pre-trained model on ImageNet, optimized using SGD with momentum. The architecture of the policy network consists of multiple blocks with varying channel sizes, and it is designed to adaptively fine-tune specific layers while freezing others to reduce parameters and enhance regularization.

Overall, the research emphasizes the superiority of SpotTune in achieving higher accuracy compared to both traditional fine-tuning methods and recent state-of-the-art techniques, highlighting its potential for effective transfer learning in various visual classification tasks.
