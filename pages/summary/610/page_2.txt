The paper introduces SpotTune, a novel approach for input-dependent fine-tuning in deep neural networks, addressing inefficiencies in current strategies that generally apply a uniform decision for all examples in a target task. Traditional methods typically fine-tune the last few layers of a network, which may not be optimal for all instances, especially when the target task has limited data or varying similarities with the source task. SpotTune proposes a mechanism to learn a decision policy for fine-tuning on a per-instance basis, determining which layers of a pre-trained model should be fine-tuned or kept frozen based on the input data.

The decision policy is generated from a lightweight neural network and utilizes Gumbel Softmax sampling to manage the discrete and non-differentiable nature of the decisions. Additionally, the authors present a global variant of SpotTune that allows a consistent fine-tuning strategy across all instances, potentially reducing the number of parameters in the final model.

Through empirical evaluations, SpotTune demonstrates superior performance, outperforming standard fine-tuning techniques on 12 out of 14 datasets and achieving the highest score in the Visual Decathlon Challenge, a benchmark for multi-domain learning algorithms. The paper also reviews related work in transfer learning and feature sharing across tasks, highlighting the unique contributions of SpotTune in automatically determining optimal fine-tuning strategies tailored to individual examples, contrasting with existing methods that lack this adaptability.
