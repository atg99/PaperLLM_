The paper presents a novel approach to dynamic routing in neural networks, particularly focusing on adaptive fine-tuning strategies for pre-trained models. Unlike existing conditional computation methods that prioritize computational efficiency by dropping layers, the proposed method aims to enhance accuracy by selectively freezing or fine-tuning layers based on individual training examples.

The approach, termed SpotTune, utilizes a pre-trained Residual Network (ResNet) model and aims to determine which layers should be adapted for a target task while keeping others fixed. This is achieved by introducing a binary random variable that indicates whether a specific residual block should be frozen or fine-tuned, allowing for dynamic adjustments during training. The architecture is designed to enable each training image to follow its unique fine-tuning policy, enhancing adaptability.

To optimize the decision-making process for layer adjustments, the paper employs Gumbel Softmax sampling, which facilitates backpropagation through discrete nodes, thus allowing the policy network to be trained concurrently with the classification task. The lightweight policy network outputs routing decisions that dictate whether an input image will utilize the frozen or fine-tuned blocks, ensuring that the overall computational burden remains manageable.

Overall, the proposed SpotTune method effectively combines the benefits of pre-trained models with dynamic routing mechanisms, aiming to improve the performance of deep neural networks on target tasks while maintaining the integrity and efficiency of the original architecture.
