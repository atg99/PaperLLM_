The paper discusses the advancements in end-to-end autonomous driving systems utilizing deep learning, specifically focusing on the modular pipeline structure. Each module's output is designed to align with the subsequent module's input to facilitate effective information transfer throughout the system.

The sensory data input is derived from a multi-sensor array, including LiDAR, RADAR, cameras, GPS, and odometers, which are essential for semantic mapping and depth analysis. The perception module processes this raw data to assist in the vehicle's localization, which involves estimating the vehicle's position using parameters such as orientation, speed, and acceleration. Localization techniques integrate various objects and their relationships to create a detailed environmental representation.

Path planning is divided into local and global planners, where the local planner executes trajectories that avoid obstacles based on goals set by the global planner. It predicts future states of traffic actors, such as vehicles and pedestrians, using historical trajectory data. The control module receives trajectory commands in two forms: as a series of commands or states, which are processed through either direct hardware actuation methods like PID control or path tracking methods such as Model Predictive Control (MPC).

The modular system architecture consists of discrete components that connect sensory inputs to motor outputs, encompassing core processes like perception, localization, mapping, planning, and vehicle control. This structured approach enhances the overall functionality and efficiency of autonomous driving systems.
