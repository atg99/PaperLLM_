The paper surveys recent advancements in end-to-end autonomous driving using deep learning, highlighting the simplicity of this approach compared to conventional modular systems. It develops a taxonomy based on modalities, learning, and training methodologies, emphasizing the potential of domain adaptation to optimize training processes. 

A comprehensive evaluation framework that includes both open and closed-loop assessments is proposed to analyze system performance. The paper compiles a list of advancements, publicly available datasets, and prominent simulators used to create virtual environments for end-to-end systems, such as MATLAB, CarSim, PreScan, CARLA, and LGSVL. These simulators support various features like sensor integration, weather conditions, camera calibration, and system scalability.

Furthermore, the paper discusses cooperative perception methods, including vehicle-to-vehicle, vehicle-to-infrastructure, and vehicle-to-everything communication, stressing the importance of enhancing transmission efficiency while maintaining data privacy. It also highlights the emergence of large language and vision models, underscoring their potential in optimizing visual recognition systems and the need for tailored models specific to autonomous driving.

In conclusion, while end-to-end autonomous driving approaches show remarkable performance, the paper calls for ongoing efforts to improve safety and interpretability to foster broader technology acceptance.
