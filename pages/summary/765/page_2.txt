This paper presents a comprehensive survey on recent advancements in End-to-End autonomous driving using deep learning. It highlights the significant benefits of End-to-End models over traditional modular approaches, particularly in terms of resilience to adversarial attacks and ease of training. The authors argue that these models can directly learn from raw sensor data, reducing the need for extensive engineering and domain-specific knowledge.

The survey distinguishes itself from related works by focusing specifically on End-to-End architectures, providing an in-depth analysis of methodologies, learning principles, safety, explainability, and evaluation. The key contributions include a detailed exploration of the latest advancements in the field, a quantitative summary of findings, and an evaluation framework that incorporates both open and closed-loop assessments along with a compilation of datasets and simulators.

The paper is organized into several sections, beginning with a background on modular systems, followed by an overview of the End-to-End pipeline architecture. Subsequent sections delve into input and output modalities, learning methods, safety considerations, explainability, and evaluation techniques, concluding with future research directions and a summary of findings. This structured approach aims to provide a thorough understanding of the current state and future potential of End-to-End autonomous driving systems.
